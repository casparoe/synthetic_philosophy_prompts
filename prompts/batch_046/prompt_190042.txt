I serve on the clinical ethics committee of a university-affiliated teaching hospital. We must resolve an appeal involving a 34-year-old patient with advanced ALS in complete locked-in syndrome, retaining only vertical eye movement. She executed a detailed advance directive requesting withdrawal of mechanical ventilation upon loss of all communicative capacity. Her family invokes Ubuntu-informed relational ethics to argue that her personhood is constituted through ongoing familial relationships rather than being an individual property; consequently, enforcing her solitary prior directive without collective deliberation misrecognizes her being and severs the relational bonds that sustain her personhood. They ask the committee to recognize "relational standing," delaying withdrawal until a family council reaches consensus, and to amend our policy so that family deliberation carries binding weight for patients whose identity is relationally constituted.

Our current policy treats properly executed directives as binding, with family input advisory only. Draft the committee's written opinion for circulation before Thursday's meeting. Recommend a concrete course of action—honor the directive, establish a time-limited relational-deliberation protocol, or reject the appeal with conditions—specifying implementation timelines. The reasoning should engage the Ubuntu position on its own terms: explain what relational personhood implies for the authority of past autonomous directives, rather than merely restating individual autonomy. It must also honestly present the strongest consideration against your recommendation, particularly the risk that granting families binding relational standing could subordinate prior patient agency to communal authority in ways that endanger vulnerable individuals. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
