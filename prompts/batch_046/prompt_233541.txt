I'm a software engineer without formal philosophy training, and I've been trying to work out my own view on why human extinction would be a catastrophe beyond the direct deaths it causes. Before I present this to a discussion group, I'd like an audit of whether these commitments actually hang together. Here is roughly where I've landed:

(1) The primary reason extinction is catastrophic is Scheffler's "afterlife" idea: our present projects—scientific, artistic, moral—derive much of their significance from being continued, understood, and valued by future human generations. If that chain breaks, the loss is not just quantitative (more dead people) but structural: it undermines the point of what we are doing now.

(2) I'm a moral anti-realist about this. I don't think "extinction is bad" tracks a mind-independent moral fact; rather, it expresses an evaluative stance that only makes sense for beings like us, whose lives are organized around long-term, intergenerational projects.

(3) I reject the non-identity problem as an excuse for climate harm. Even if future people in a ruined world wouldn't exist without our carbon-emitting actions, I think we genuinely wrong them by leaving them that world.

(4) I hold that conscious well-being is the only thing with intrinsic value. Cultural transmission, art, and knowledge matter only instrumentally, as contributors to sentient experience.

(5) I also think that if humanity went extinct but was replaced by flourishing, conscious, substrate-independent digital beings—who preserved our knowledge, continued scientific inquiry, and maintained rich inner lives—the core tragedy would be largely dissolved.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. For each real tension, rank it by severity, explain exactly what pulls against what, and say what the least costly revision would be and what it costs me. I don't want a lecture on Scheffler or anti-realism in general; I want a consistency check on this specific package. In particular, I'm unsure whether (1) can survive together with (4) and with (5), and whether (2) undermines (3) in a way I haven't noticed. Be specific about which commitment has to give, or whether there's a reinterpretation that saves all five.
