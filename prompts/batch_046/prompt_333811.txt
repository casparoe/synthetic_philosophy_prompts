Consider the long-standing debate over Frege's puzzle about identity statements—specifically, why 'a = a' and 'a = b' can differ in cognitive value when 'a' and 'b' are co-referential. In a seminar I have been convening, three positions have stabilized without convergence over the course of a year. The first holds that the puzzle is fully resolved by distinguishing sense from reference, with informativeness explained entirely by differences in mode of presentation. The second holds that this semantic answer is incomplete: the real explanatory work concerns how rational agents update mental representations upon discovering an identity, so the puzzle belongs primarily to cognitive psychology or the theory of belief dynamics rather than to semantics proper. The third—less commonly foregrounded in standard treatments—maintains that both preceding views miss the core issue, which is normative and assertoric: an identity statement can be informative not because of how it represents the world or updates a mental file, but because of the conditions that entitle a speaker to assert sameness and the communicative norms governing disclosure of identity. All three camps accept the same canonical cases—the Hesperus/Phosphorus discovery, failures of substitutivity salva veritate in belief reports, and scenarios of unexpected identification—yet cross-camp arguments have become circular, with each side treating the others' central concerns as peripheral or as answering a different question. I want a diagnosis of this particular stall, not a survey of interpretations of Frege. Is the disagreement substantive—about what counts as an adequate explanation of informativeness—or is it verbal, with each camp using 'cognitive value' to mark a different phenomenon? Are they answering three distinct questions—semantic, psychological, and pragmatic—under a single heading? Or is there a hidden formal or empirical question, perhaps about the structure of mental representation or the logic of assertion, that could in principle settle it? Given your diagnosis, specify what would count as progress: a conceptual distinction that cleanly separates the questions, an empirical result about belief revision, a formal result in the logic of identity, or an argument that the core question is ill-posed and should be dissolved rather than solved.
