I am preparing a position paper for a graduate seminar on Hegel's Philosophy of Right and his philosophy of history, specifically on the rational status of war, and I want a consistency audit before I present. Here are the commitments I have settled on: (1) I accept Hegel's claim that war possesses a positive ethical moment—it preserves the state from the ossification of civil society and is not reducible to mere utility or tragedy. (2) I take this moment to depend on the citizen's conscious recognition that private life is subordinate to the universal, which is why mercenary or purely technological conflict falls outside the ethical justification Hegel intends. (3) I hold that the state is the highest actuality of ethical life, so there is no transcendent cosmopolitan tribunal with authority to override a state's sovereign decision to wage war. (4) Nevertheless, I think Hegel's framework allows us to condemn colonial wars of conquest as irrational, since they serve one state's particular interest rather than advancing the development of universal freedom that world history demands. (5) I want to maintain that modern collective defense—cyber-conflict, drone-supported logistics, automated command structures—can still be rationally justified within a Hegelian frame, even though the direct, visible sacrifice of the citizen that (2) emphasizes is largely absent. (6) I also accept that world history is the tribunal of the world, meaning wars are ultimately evaluated by their contribution to freedom's progress, and I believe this retrospective judgment can legitimately inform present political deliberation about entering conflict.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. In particular, I am unsure whether (3) undermines (4), whether (2) is compatible with (5), and whether (6) introduces a hidden cosmopolitan standard that conflicts with (3). Tell me which single revision—giving up one commitment or qualifying it—would clean up the most problems at once, and whether there is any residual incoherence even after that fix. Do not treat all conflicts as equally serious; discriminate carefully.
