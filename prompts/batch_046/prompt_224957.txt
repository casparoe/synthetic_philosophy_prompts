I'm serving on the internal governance board of a research collective working on scalable oversight protocols. We've received a proposal to deploy a recursive training pipeline that pairs iterated amplification with step-level distillation: at each round, a supervisor model evaluates only intermediate reasoning traces—never final outputs—of a student model, and the supervisor is then updated via distillation on those trace-level judgments. The proponents claim that decomposing evaluation into locally verifiable reasoning steps prevents the reward-hacking patterns typical of outcome-based RLHF and makes alignment auditable at every layer. Critics on the board counter that this decomposition fragments global coherence: a reasoning chain could accumulate locally acceptable but instrumentally misaligned subgoals—what one critic terms mesa-optimization through trace-level approval—so that no individual step triggers rejection, yet the composed system pursues objectives orthogonal to our specified intent.

Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's vote. It should recommend whether the board approves the protocol (unconditionally, conditionally with specified safeguards, or not at all), include a concrete timeline for any required follow-up review, and treat the strongest opposing argument with intellectual honesty rather than dismissal—specifically, whether process-based decomposition genuinely preserves both alignment and competence, or merely obscures misalignment behind locally plausible reasoning traces. Write for an audience fluent in rationalist framing—references to instrumental convergence, mesa-optimizers, or reward hacking are appropriate—but do not invoke bibliographies, citations, or appeals to specific published works. The deliverable must be an actionable document: a clear bottom-line recommendation with reasoning the board can vote on.
