I'm presenting my working paper, titled "Reserve Prices, Not Veto Points: A Two-Stage Bargaining Model for Moral Compromise Between Lexically Incommensurable Frameworks," at next Thursday's interdisciplinary ethics colloquium. The thesis is that when two moral communities—say, one operating with a deontological rights-based framework that treats certain protections as lexically prior, and another operating with a relational care-ethics framework that treats contextual responsiveness as non-negotiable—negotiate institutional compromise, the standard "gains from trade" analogy fails because it implicitly treats moral commitments as fungible preferences. Instead, I argue that stable, Pareto-improving compromise is possible only if each side temporarily reframes its lexical commitments not as absolute veto conditions but as reserve-price constraints within a two-stage bargaining protocol: first, a framing stage where each side specifies which normative claims are genuinely non-tradable versus which are protectable only at a cost they are willing to bear; second, an exchange stage where trade-offs occur only within the protected reserve zones.
