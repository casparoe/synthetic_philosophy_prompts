Next month I present at a workshop on formal epistemology and embedded rationality. My thesis is that embedded agency dissolves the standard evidential-versus-causal decision-theoretic distinction from within: because an embedded agent's model of its own decision procedure is physically instantiated in the world-model it updates, treating a future action as a pure evidential update is structurally unstable. The agent cannot maintain a clean separation between updating on its action and causing its action without either failing logical omniscience or smuggling in an external-observer viewpoint. The audience is half formal epistemologists with strong Bayesian commitments, two logicians who specialize in self-reference and logical omniscience, one computer scientist working on self-modifying AI architectures, and a philosopher of science who treats decision-theoretic distinctions as purely formal coherence conditions that should never be collapsed into causal claims. They are skeptical of metaphysical revisionism and will defend standard EDT/CDT splits as belief-level formalisms, not world-level structures. Sketch of my argument: (1) embed the agent's reasoning process in its world-model as a causal node; (2) show that any update rule treating the agent's own output as evidence must either assume a non-physical access point or generate a fixed-point instability; (3) conclude that the evidential/causal split is an artifact of an external framing that embedded agents cannot consistently adopt. Give me my Q&A prep sheet: the five objections they will actually raise, ordered by likely damage, each phrased in the vocabulary of the relevant sub-group (Bayesian coherence, fixed-point logic, code-level self-modification, formalist separation, hidden Cartesian dualism). For each, state what I should concede up front, what I must hold, a reply I can deliver in about two minutes, and the softest point in that reply. Include the one question I am hoping nobody asks—whether this view implies that embedded agents are globally unable to plan—and draft how to answer it if pressed. Finally, flag the weakest point in my own position that I should actively defend rather than hope they miss: whether my argument tacitly presupposes a particular resolution to the logical omniscience problem rather than deriving it from embeddedness alone.
