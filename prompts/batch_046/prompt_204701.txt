I'm writing a feature on how social scientists actually explain collective events — financial panics, viral norms, that sort of thing — and my editor and I got into a argument about methodological individualism versus holism. The way I understand it, methodological individualism forces you to reduce every social fact to mental states inside individual brains, which seems obviously wrong for phenomena like inflation or institutional bias, since no single person 'contains' those things. So doesn't that mean holism wins by default, with society exercising downward causation that literally overrides individual choice?

I keep seeing references to 'structural individualism' and agent-based modeling as some kind of compromise, but to me that sounds like definatly admitting individualism can't do the work on its own. Is there a version of individualism that allows structures and relations to do real explanatory labor without collapsing into full-blown ontological holism, or am I just misunderstanding what the debate is actually about? Would love some clarity before I file the draft.
