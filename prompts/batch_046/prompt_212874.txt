so ive been rereading the benacerraf stuff and its wild how the same stalemate keeps replaying every few years. platonists say we grasp structures directly, nominalists say math is just syntax with rules, and the causal-knowledge crowd keeps insisting knowledge needs interaction with objects but never actually specifies what interaction with a non-physical thing even looks like. my working theory is that this isnt really one debate but two different questions being run together under the same name: one about epistemic justification for beliefs like 7+5=12, and one about how a physical brain could represent an abstract object at all. if thats right, then of course nobody moves — theyre answering different prompts with the same vocabulary. but heres a wrinkle i dont see discussed: what if theres a middle kind of access thats neither causal contact nor pure intuition, more like algorithmic tractability? you dont need to 'interact' with the number 7 to run a proof that refers to it, but youre also not just staring at a platonic form. is the stall actually a hidden disagreement about whether mathematical cognition is best modeled as logical derivation, empirical pattern-matching, or this third thing? diagnose the disagreement for me: verbal, substantive, or two seperate disciplines talking past each other? and if the deadlock is real, what kind of result — formal, empirical, or conceptual — would actually shift anyone?
