I'm working through a consistency check for a discussion on rational decision-making under branching-world quantum mechanics, and I want to make sure my package of views doesn't collapse under inspection. Here is roughly where I've landed:

1. I accept the Everett interpretation: measurements produce genuinely branching worlds with no collapse, and all branches are ontologically on a par.

2. I hold a reductionist view of personal identity across branching: there is no hidden further fact that singles out one post-measurement copy as the true continuation of me; every branch containing a psychologically continuous functional successor instantiates a person who stands in the "me" relation with equal legitimacy.

3. Despite 2, I reject quantum immortality. I do not think subjective experience is selectively restricted to survival-branches, and I do not expect that repeated quantum-risk exposure will leave me observing extreme old age.

4. To explain 3 without abandoning 2, I adopt the view that phenomenal consciousness requires a threshold of integrated information, and that branches in which my brain is destroyed fall below this threshold, so there is genuinely no experiencer there—without this implying that experience "jumps" to the surviving branches.

5. I endorse updateless decision theory (UDT) and think rational choice should maximize expected utility summed across all worlds weighted by Born-rule measure, with no updating on purely indexical self-locating information.

6. I accept anthropic reasoning for standard self-locating puzzles (e.g., Sleeping Beauty) but deny it applies to post-branching identity: branching produces actual duplicates, not epistemic possibilities, so there is no reasoning-from-ignorance about which copy I am.

7. I maintain that quantum Russian roulette—arranging a quantum event that kills me in half the branches—is rationally impermissible, and that this judgment must be formulation-independent: it should not depend on whether I first frame the decision problem before or after the quantum event has occurred.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it. For each real tension, rank its severity and say what the cheapest revision would be (and what it costs). In particular, I'm worried that 2 and 5 together might commit me to caring about the total measure of my copies in a way that makes 7 unstable, or that 4 and 6 are pulling in opposite directions about how to treat self-location. Also tell me which single change to this set would clean up the most conflicts without destroying the overall shape of the view.
