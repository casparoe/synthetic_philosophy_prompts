I am a PhD student in philosophy of biology serving on my university's institutional animal-use review board, and I need a reusable decision procedure for proposals involving non-mammalian species—cuttlefish, decapods, bees, and similar—where the binary vertebrate exemption is inadequate but no operational framework exists. The board has roughly twenty minutes per proposal, members lack neuroscience doctorates, and any step that turns on values rather than evidence must flag explicit sign-off by the designated ethicist and veterinarian.

Please operationalize the following synthesized principle into a step-by-step procedure: phenomenal consciousness should be presumed to the extent that an animal's nervous system exhibits (a) functionally integrated information processing that is not reducible to feedforward reflex arcs and that serves a unified behavioral economy rather than modular isolation; (b) evolutionary convergence with architectures associated with conscious processing in other lineages; and (c) flexible behavioral adjustment to novel conditions that cannot be parsed into isolated reflex pathways. Crucially, the absence of mammalian-style thalamocortical loops should be treated as an evidential gap requiring stronger positive indicators, not as disconfirmation.

The deliverable should be ordered questions with sufficient-answer criteria, explicit stop points (waive protective protocols, recommend mandatory analgesia and enrichment, or escalate to value judgment), and clear markers separating evidential steps from unavoidable judgment calls. Include observable proxy indicators that non-specialists can check. Then apply the procedure to three proposals: a cuttlefish behavioral-learning study, a decapod shrimp nociception assay, and a honeybee navigational-disruption experiment. Ensure one case yields an answer the board will find unwelcome, and specify which one and why. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure cannot determine and where it will systematically misfire.
