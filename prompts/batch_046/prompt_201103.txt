Write an essay addressing the controversy over whether an open-borders immigration policy can be sustained alongside a robust, redistributive welfare state. Rather than framing integration as assimilation or legal compliance alone, analyze the tension through the lens of reciprocal transparency: the idea that high-trust redistribution depends not merely on objective fiscal contribution but on citizens' ability to perceive that contribution as part of a shared, mutual scheme. Distinguish between procedural integration (access to rights and labor markets) and normative integration (participation in the shared expectations that make common-pool institutions feel legitimate to contributors). Weigh the claim that without normative integration, open borders erode the social conditions for redistribution, against the claim that conditioning mobility on normative assimilation violates basic cosmopolitan equality and that welfare-state design can be decoupled from cultural homogeneity. Reach and defend a specific conclusion—for instance, that open borders are compatible with the welfare state only if integration is reconceived as mutual institutional adaptation rather than one-sided assimilation, or that the reciprocity-transparency problem is fatal regardless of integration design. Ground your argument in at least one concrete mechanism, such as fiscal-externality dynamics, democratic legitimacy, or the stability of social-solidarity norms, and explain how your distinction between procedural and normative integration reshapes the operation of that mechanism. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; the argument should stand entirely on its conceptual merits.
