Write a dialogue between a neopragmatist committed to Rorty's antirepresentationalism and a scientific realist who maintains that predictive success is inexplicable without representational accuracy. Frame their exchange around a hypothetical case: imagine a scientific community that systematically reassigns every observational term to refer to a physically distinct kind—calling electrons "protons" and vice versa, mapping temperature to entropy—while preserving every predictive and practical success of the original vocabulary. Have each interlocutor address whether this inversion shows that vocabulary is merely a tool with no representational aim, or whether it instead reveals that representational content is preserved beneath the linguistic shuffle. They should respond directly to each other's arguments: the realist should press whether the pragmatist can distinguish genuine scientific progress from arbitrary relabeling without covertly appealing to correspondence, and the pragmatist should challenge whether the realist can explain why predictive success should count as evidence for representation rather than for practical coping. Both characters should be equally competent and neither should be a straw figure. The dialogue should take a definite line—either defending the coherence of antirepresentationalism in the face of inversion, or arguing that inversion collapses the distinction the pragmatist needs—rather than merely surveying options. Present everything as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as one might write in a letter.
