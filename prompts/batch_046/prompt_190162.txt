Consider this argument, which I have been turning over, about whether pure time preference can be sustained as a rational principle. (1) A stationary, time-separable utility function discounts future utility exponentially with factor δ ∈ (0,1). (2) If δ < 1 is defended on non-epistemic grounds, it must be because temporal proximity to the present confers intrinsic value—goods are more valuable simply by being nearer in time to the evaluator. (3) This makes the value of a state of affairs partly indexical: its weight depends on the temporal location of the agent doing the evaluating. (4) Apply the principle consistently to moral evaluation: from my present standpoint in 2025, the welfare of people in 2325 is weighted by δ^300, essentially zero; but from the standpoint of an evaluator in 2275, the very same welfare receives weight δ^50, which may be substantial. (5) Hence, whether the suffering of those future people constitutes a grave moral harm varies with the evaluator's temporal position. (6) But moral reasons are generally taken to be agent-neutral and timeless: if a harm is grave, it is grave regardless of when or by whom it is assessed. (7) Therefore, pure time preference, interpreted as a value principle, contradicts the agent-neutrality of moral reasons. (8) One might try to confine pure time preference to prudential reasoning alone, but then one owes an account of why temporal proximity has intrinsic value for self-regarding choice yet none for other-regarding choice—a boundary that looks ad hoc. (9) If no such account is available, pure time preference either undermines moral universality or collapses into an unmotivated asymmetry between prudence and morality. I feel that some step between (3) and (8) illicitly extends an indexical feature of first-personal planning into a domain where it does not belong, but I cannot pinpoint whether the error is in treating value as indexical, in assuming agent-neutrality, or in the inference from one to the other. Which step should be rejected, and what does rejecting it commit us to?
