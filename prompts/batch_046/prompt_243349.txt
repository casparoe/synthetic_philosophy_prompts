I am preparing an essay for my advanced epistemology course on the bootstrapping problem and the puzzle of easy knowledge. Please compare two conceptual responses to epistemic circularity: first, the view that validating a belief-forming process requires an independent, non-circular evidential source; second, the view that a cognitive competence can be self-certifying when reflective awareness of its own reliability is generated by the very exercise of that competence, provided that awareness does not function as a premise in the original justification. Which framework better explains how basic perceptual knowledge extends to knowledge that skeptical alternatives are false, without either collapsing into vicious circularity or conceding too much to skepticism? In your answer, address whether the second view genuinely avoids circularity or merely relocates it within the structure of the competence itself.
