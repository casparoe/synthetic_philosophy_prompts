Draft a final-exam essay question for an upper-level undergraduate seminar on free will and moral responsibility. Constraints: 75 minutes; 25 marks total. The topic must be Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against moral responsibility, but the question should be impossible to answer by mere summary. Require the student to reconstruct the argument precisely: the premise that true moral responsibility requires being causa sui, the infinite regress generated by any attempt to be the ultimate source of one's own character, and the distinction between genuine moral responsibility and derivative functional accountability. Then require application to an invented scenario—the "Architect of Character" case—in which one agent's complete motivational structure was deliberately engineered by a prior agent who allegedly possessed full self-creative powers. The model answer should determine whether Strawson's argument extends to the engineered agent, whether delegating origins breaks the regress or merely relocates it, and whether accepting this scenario forces one to abandon the universal causal determination premise that underwrites the argument. Provide the marking scheme as five must-hit points with individual weights that sum to 25, and for each point include a one-line note on the characteristic error that typically costs marks there. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and do not invoke obscure secondary literature that students could not be expected to know without specialized access.
