I am organizing a 90-minute workshop for a philosophy of education reading group of about sixteen graduate students, all of whom have read Whitehead’s essay on the aims of education and are familiar with his rhythm of romance, precision, and generalization, yet tend to treat that sequence as a benign, almost inevitable maturation rather than as a fragile dynamic that institutions routinely fracture. To push them past that complacency, I want them to experience from the inside what I will call pedagogical lock-in: a defensive institutional posture in which the disciplined methods acquired during the precision stage are systematically redeployed to suppress the curiosity that originally justified them, so that generalization either never arrives or arrives only as inert, dead knowledge. The workshop should make this failure visceral, not merely conceptual.

Please design a structured simulation or role-play with explicit rules, assigned roles, material constraints if needed, and a clear timeline that fits within 90 minutes. The incentives and evaluation criteria should be arranged so that participants first encounter an open, exploratory problem that rewards imaginative engagement (romance), then are required to adopt rigorous, standardized procedures for solving it (precision), and finally face a hidden institutional pressure—embedded, for example, in how points or resources are allocated—that rewards them for using those very procedures to shut down further questioning rather than to extend their findings into broader application (the blocked generalization). Include a debrief that maps specific turning points in the activity onto Whitehead’s three stages, explains what will predictably go wrong the first time the activity is run—such as participants mistaking strategic compliance for genuine understanding, or the facilitator accidentally naming the paradox before the group has felt it—and states frankly which feature of Whitehead’s view the simulation cannot reproduce, likely the long temporal span over which the rhythm unfolds across an actual life or curriculum, and how you as facilitator must supply that absence verbally or structurally. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; the design should be fully usable by someone working without library or web access.
