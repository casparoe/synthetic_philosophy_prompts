I spent much of last night arguing with a close friend about whether theoretical physics has lost its empirical anchor, and I have been trying to distill where I actually stand before we continue. Here is the package of commitments I have arrived at; I want an audit of whether they hang together, because I have the sense that I am borrowing premises that may cancel one another out.

First, I am a scientific realist about theoretical entities—fields, particles, spacetime curvature—even when they are not directly observable, provided they sit inside theories that generate risky, indirect empirical consequences. Second, I think eternal inflation’s multiverse of causally disconnected pocket universes crosses the line from science into metaphysical speculation, not merely because the entities are unobservable in principle, but because the framework produces no risky predictions that could be undermined by any observation we might actually make. Third, I take the string landscape—the enormous space of metastable vacua—seriously as a legitimate theoretical result rather than an embarrassment for physics, though I find the swampland conjectures far more promising because they issue negative, falsifiable constraints on low-energy effective theories rather than accommodating virtually every possibility. Fourth, I accept that non-empirical theory confirmation—mathematical consistency, explanatory coherence, and what I would call predictive compression, the degree to which a theory constrains the space of possible observations relative to its free parameters—is legitimate in fundamental physics when direct experimental access is permanently blocked. Fifth, I maintain that any theory capable of accommodating every possible observational outcome without constraining the data space is degenerate and should not be accepted, even on purely structural or aesthetic grounds; predictive compression remains a necessary condition for rational acceptance. Sixth, I think anthropic reasoning is valid in cosmology, but only when it selects among genuinely distinct theoretical predictions, not when it retrofits a parameter space—such as selecting a cosmological constant—to match a single observed value.

After writing these out, I can see at least one tension that may dissolve on inspection: my acceptance of non-empirical confirmation for quantum-gravity research might seem to conflict with my dismissal of the multiverse on observational grounds, and I am unsure whether that conflict is real or only apparent. But I suspect there are deeper inconsistencies I am not seeing—particularly between my defense of the landscape and my insistence on predictive compression, and between my scientific realism and my denial of scientific status to entities predicted by the very inflationary dynamics I otherwise treat seriously.
