Construct a decision-tree taxonomy for a philosophy-of-science seminar that sorts commitments in the debate between Humeanism and anti-Humeanism about laws of nature. The tree should be designed so that a respondent answers concrete metaphysical questions without knowing in advance which named position each answer leads to. Begin at a root node asking whether the same total physical state of affairs could obtain under different laws. From there, branch through the following diagnostic questions in order: (a) whether observed regularities require explanation by something over and above the particular matters of fact that instantiate them; (b) whether counterfactual support for laws is primitive in nature or derived from the actual distribution of properties; (c) whether the best-system analysis captures all nomic facts, or whether there remains an unexplained governing relation between universals and their instances. Each terminal leaf should correspond to one of these five positions: (i) canonical best-system Humeanism; (ii) Mill-Ramsey-Lewis regularism with no primitive modal facts; (iii) dispositional essentialism treating laws as necessary connections among properties; (iv) governing-law necessitarianism with a primitive nomic relation; and (v) a deliberately constructed, currently unoccupied position—call it nomic selectionism—that accepts full Humean supervenience but rejects best-system analysis in favor of a primitive, non-explanatory selection principle that picks out one regularity-set among equally simple competitors. For each leaf, state in one sentence the view's most awkward commitment regarding scientific explanation. Then, in a separate section, diagnose the empty nomic selectionism branch: argue whether it is incoherent, redundant with an existing view, or genuinely unexplored, and identify which of the tree's prior nodes a defender of this view would have to answer differently. Structure your response as follows: (1) The Tree, presenting each node as a direct question and each leaf with its label, approximately 350 words; (2) Branch Justifications, explaining why each split genuinely separates the logical space rather than merely labeling pre-existing camps, approximately 250 words; (3) Leaf Profiles, describing all five positions including the invented one, with their awkward explanatory commitments, approximately 300 words; (4) Diagnosis of the Unoccupied Node, assessing the status of nomic selectionism, approximately 200 words. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; do not invoke highly specialized technical results that depend on precise recent literature; and keep the voice appropriate to a graduate seminar handout.
