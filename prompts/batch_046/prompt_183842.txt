I have been discussing with colleagues whether prioritarianism genuinely escapes the leveling-down objection or merely displaces it into less visible dimensions of distributive choice. The standard dialectic is familiar enough: prioritarian weights favour benefiting the worse off, yet critics charge that this can justify reducing the well-being of the better off without improving anyone's condition, which seems perverse. What strikes me as under-examined is how the objection behaves when we distinguish synchronic leveling — equalising welfare within a single distribution — from diachronic leveling, in which priority weights applied across generations or successive life-stages might systematically retard improvement for those temporarily better off. This produces not a static equality of misery but a path-dependent stagnation. I am also struck by the possibility that prioritarianism avoids intrinsic leveling only to invite instrumental leveling: if the social and epistemic mechanisms required to identify and weight the worst off generate bureaucratic costs that fall disproportionately upon the prosperous, we may have relocated rather than resolved the objection.

Assuming this framing is coherent, I want to construct a research agenda rather than a survey. If you were advising a small conceptual philosophy group with roughly three years of focused work — not empirical social science, but normative and formal analysis — which open questions in this vicinity deserve priority? I am particularly interested in whether the core crux lies in refining the formal structure of prioritarian weights (for instance, whether continuous versus threshold-sensitive weighting alters the objection's force), in clarifying the moral status of path-dependent welfare losses, or in examining whether the identification conditions for being worst off implicitly carry leveling effects of their own. Please defend a clear ordering: which line is upstream of the others, which is tractable with current philosophical tools without requiring novel formal apparatus, and what kind of intermediate result over the next few years would cause you to revise the ranking? I do not want bibliographies, citation lists, or reading recommendations; I want a reasoned judgment about where limited conceptual labour should go, and why.
