I'm writing the final exam for my upper-level seminar in social ontology. I want a single 90-minute essay question on the social construction of race, gender, or money that students cannot answer by summarizing standard conferralist or status-function accounts. The question should center on what I call the "double-structure problem": institutional kinds are constituted by collective acceptance of a status function, yet in practice they are routinely deployed as if they tracked an intrinsic, pre-social property—consider administrative gender classifications that presuppose biological binaries, or fiat currencies treated as possessing value independent of collective agreement. Ask students to choose one kind and defend either that this tension is a stable feature of institutional reality or that it is an internal contradiction undermining the kind's coherence. Please provide: (1) the exam question, specifying a target of about 1,000 words; (2) a model answer of roughly 700 words that introduces at least one original conceptual distinction rather than rehearsing named theorists; (3) a marking scheme presented as a table with columns: Criterion (the conceptual move required), Marks (out of 20), A-level execution, and Typical B-level failure. The scheme must distinguish structural understanding from mere labeling of positions. Do not ask students for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists, and do not frame the task as requiring knowledge of obscure recent literature; a student working only from general philosophical training should be able to engage the core issue.
