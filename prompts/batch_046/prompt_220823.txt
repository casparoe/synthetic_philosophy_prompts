I'm part of a facilitation collective that runs governance circles for cooperative housing, and lately we've been hitting this wall where a proposal looks like it makes everyone better off or at least doesn't hurt anyone, but people still balk because it feels like it's stepping on someone's personal turf. I was skimming something about Sen's liberal paradox in a social-choice course and realized this is basically it — you can't guarantee both minimal liberty over your own sphere and Pareto efficiency — but the formal version is useless in a meeting with tired people and snacks on the table. So I need a reusable audit procedure, like an ordered checklist my team can actually run, that takes a house proposal and checks whether it stays in the collective-resource zone or spills into an individual's minimal-liberty boundary, then checks for Pareto improvement in a practical non-mathy way, and clearly marks the spot where the answer stops being factual and becomes a values call we have to vote on or table.

The procedure needs to handle the hard case where there's a real Pareto gain but also a real liberty intrusion — like a smell or noise rule that helps one resident's health but controls what another does in their own unit — and it should tell us exactly when to stop with a hard no versus when we hand it to a values vote. Please apply it to three proposals we actually faced: (1) installing soundproofing in shared walls using collective funds to reduce noise spillover, (2) banning strong-smelling cooking after 9pm in private kitchens because one resident gets migraines, and (3) requiring every unit to display a specific solidarity poster in shared hallways because the majority says it makes them feel safer. Make sure one of these gives an answer that feels wrong on intuition — probably saying no to something that looks like a harmless accommodation — and defend that result firmly rather than just listing "on the one hand, on the other hand." Take a definite position on whether minimal liberty should ever be overridden for Pareto gains in these micro-settings, with your reasons, don't leave me to pick.
