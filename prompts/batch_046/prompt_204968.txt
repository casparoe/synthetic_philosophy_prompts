I'm a senior evaluator at an agency that certifies adaptive decision systems—clinical support algorithms, animal-training protocols, and educational software—before deployment in high-stakes environments. We keep misapplying evaluation frameworks: demanding internal-model transparency from Skinnerian reward learners, or treating Gregorian language tools as automatic proof of Popperian reasoning. I need a reusable, thirty-minute certification procedure grounded in the four-part taxonomy of cognitive architectures—Darwinian (hardwired, genetically fixed responses), Skinnerian (trial-and-error reinforcement within a lifetime), Popperian (internal generation and rejection of counterfactual hypotheses), and Gregorian (symbolic and cultural tools that restructure cognition itself)—operationalized for reviewers with no philosophy background.

The deliverable should be a step-by-step procedure with these components:

(1) An ordered diagnostic checklist that classifies the baseline architecture, with explicit failure conditions that halt evaluation and require redesign.

(2) A scaffold-stripping sub-procedure—my own framing, not standard in the literature—to distinguish genuine Gregorian architecture from mere Gregorian behavior. Define precisely what counts as passing (the agent retains functional restructuring when external symbol systems, databases, or tools are removed) and what counts as dangerous cognitive debt (dependency that masks an underlying Skinnerian or Darwinian core). Specify exactly how this test should be weighted in certification.

(3) Decision rules mapping each architecture, including hybrid states, to permitted deployment conditions, with mandatory rejection conditions that apply regardless of performance metrics.

(4) Explicit hand-off nodes where the decision is one of values rather than architecture—e.g., whether high efficiency justifies accepting cognitive debt—and where the evaluator must escalate rather than sign off.

(5) Worked applications to three cases: (a) a reinforcement-learning clinical triage bot that queries a large language model for post-hoc explanations; (b) a genetically modified agricultural pest with a fixed olfactory-response circuit; (c) a wild corvid population observed using stick tools to extract larvae, with controlled absence of suitable tool material. Include at least one case—likely the triage bot—where the procedure yields a restrictive recommendation you'll find counterintuitive given its accuracy, and defend that recommendation with definite reasons rather than presenting it as one option.

The procedure must be honest about which steps are mechanistic and which are judgment calls disguised as flowchart nodes. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Take a definite, committed position on how scaffold-stripping should be weighted, defend that weighting, and state clearly where the procedure will systematically misfire—particularly with hybrid architectures—and what it cannot do.
