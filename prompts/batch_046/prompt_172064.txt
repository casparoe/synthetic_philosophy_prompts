I sit on the review board for this tiny residency program that pairs one artist with one scientist for three months and gives them shared funding if we think their collaboration will actually produce something intrinsically good. Lately weve been fighting constantly because half of us want to fund deep friendships even if the output is boring, and the other half only cares about the final object being beautiful, plus theres that old idea about organic unity—where the value of the whole is more than the sum of the parts—that nobody can explain in practical terms. We have to vote every other friday, none of us studied philosophy, and we need a reusable decision procedure: the exact questions to ask in order, what counts as enough of an answer to move on, where we stop with a hard no, and where the question is just values and we have to vote like humans. It needs to handle the messy cases: two people who genuinely like each other but make ugly functional stuff, a beautiful result produced by people who hated each other the whole time, and a duo where one person is clearly carrying all the weight. Please apply it to three real-style proposals: ceramicist best friends proposing decorative sinks, a physicist and poet who fought constantly but built a stunning sound installation, and a partnership where one partner is basically a passenger. Make sure you flag the cases where the procedure will misfire, include at least one result the board probably wont like, and tell us plainly what this cant settle—especially where friendship and beauty pull in opposite directions. Keep it grounded in what Moore actually said about these goods being fundamental and about wholes exceeding parts, but dont make us read books; we need something we can run in fifteen minutes with a checklist.
