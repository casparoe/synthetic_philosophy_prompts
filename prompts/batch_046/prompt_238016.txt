Question: Is there a morally relevant difference between lying and misleading? Discuss with reference to assertoric commitment, and mention paltering.

Student answer:
Lying is asserting something you believe is false, which directly violates assertoric commitment because youre putting your authority behind a falsehood. Misleading is different: you say only true things but arrange them so the listener draws a false conclusion, like paltering -- saying "i didnt take the money from the jar" when you took bills from the drawer. Here every individual claim is literally true, so you never break assertoric commitment or the sincerity condition; theyre basically the same thing here. So misleading is morally lighter because it doesnt corrupt the shared ground of assertion, it just exploits the listeners reasoning. The wrong is mostly on them for not being careful. Even in paltering, since no false sentence was uttered, theres no direct betrayal of conversational sincerity. Special obligations like being a doctor might require more transparency, but thats an added duty, not proof that misleading equals lying in basic moral weight. So yes, the difference is real and big.
