A colleague in my philosophy of social science seminar offered the following summary of moral foundations theory after reading Haidt's popular writings and a few of the original empirical papers. She said: "The theory demonstrates that political liberals and conservatives do not merely disagree about morality; they operate with different moral architectures. Liberals draw almost exclusively on the care and fairness foundations, whereas conservatives distribute moral weight across all six—adding loyalty, authority, and sanctity. Because evolutionary psychology treats these six as distinct adaptive solutions, having access to only two means liberal moral cognition is structurally incomplete, like color vision missing several primaries. Haidt's data therefore imply that conservative morality is closer to the full human evolutionary endowment, and that liberalism represents a kind of developmental truncation—particularly in WEIRD societies, where the binding foundations atrophy after adolescence because cultural selection suppresses them. This explains why partisan disagreement is so intractable: the two sides are not weighing the same values differently, but perceiving entirely different moral realities."

I suspect this blends descriptive psychology with normative conclusions in ways the original theory avoids, but I am not sure exactly where the seams are. Could you audit the summary claim by claim? For each part, tell me whether a careful defender of moral foundations theory would accept it as accurate, whether it overstates or subtly misrepresents the research—especially the move from "different weightings" to "structural incompleteness," the evolutionary argument that conservatism is "closer to human nature," and the idea of developmental truncation in WEIRD populations—or whether it is an outright distortion. I also want to know whether any corrected version of this summary actually supports the normative conclusion that one side is closer to being right about morality, or whether that inference is entirely external to the theory. Please treat this as an accuracy audit I can use in conversation, not as a grading exercise; I am trying to decide what to trust when I encounter this kind of framing in public discourse.
