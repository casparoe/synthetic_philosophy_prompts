In debates over figures like Jefferson, Churchill, or Columbus, one often hears the dismissal that 'he was a man of his time,' or that 'we must not judge the past by present standards.' I am not asking for a biography or a final moral verdict on anyone. Instead, treat the slogan as a piece of language being made to do specific argumentative work — in this case, to close down moral criticism of historical wrongs. Disambiguate it into the precise philosophical claims it might carry: that moral norms are historically indexed; that retrospective blame violates conditions of fairness; that an agent is only responsible for wrongs they could have conceptually recognized; that present observers suffer unavoidable epistemic bias; or something else entirely. Lay these out as a menu. For each, say whether it is tautological, a substantive and defensible thesis (and of what kind — relativist, epistemic, or about moral psychology), plainly false, or a category mistake that does not bear on blame at all. Then identify which reading, if any, actually licenses using the slogan to block criticism of a specific act — for instance, slaveholding or imperial violence — and whether any consistent version can apply selectively, excusing Jefferson's slaveholding while still permitting condemnation of Columbus's enslavement of Taíno peoples, without collapsing into special pleading. Keep the analysis conceptual and do not provide bibliographies or reading lists.
