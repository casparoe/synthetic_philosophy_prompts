I'm preparing an interview for my philosophy-of-science podcast with a philosopher who defends what she calls "scale-bound determinism": the claim that deterministic dynamics hold locally within precisely specified mechanistic descriptions, but that chaos necessarily fragments mechanism identity across scales, so that global determinism is not merely epistemically inaccessible but structurally incoherent. I want eight to ten questions that do not ask her to restate the view. I need at least one question pressing on how she distinguishes her position from standard epistemic accounts of chaos—specifically, why mechanism individuation rather than measurement error is doing the work—with a follow-up anticipating her reply that cross-scale divergence undermines the very conditions for counting two states as belonging to the same system. I need at least one question on her response to a Laplace-style demon restricted to a single descriptive scale, with a follow-up keyed to her likely claim that the demon succeeds only by presupposing the scale-bound identity she denies. I need at least one question on what her view implies for scientific practice, particularly whether climate or neuroscience modeling should abandon determinism as a regulative ideal, with a follow-up addressing her probable move toward local, scale-specific validation criteria rather than global predictive convergence. The remaining questions should probe related pressure points: the modal status of deterministic laws under her account; whether quantum indeterminacy is redundant or complementary to her chaos-based argument; how she avoids collapsing into instrumentalism about macro-level explanations; and whether her view entails that there is no fact of the matter about long-term macro-trajectory identity. Each question should be specific enough that she cannot answer with a generic defense of determinism, and each should be paired with a follow-up tailored to the answer she is most likely to give.
