I am a retired molecular biologist who has only recently begun serious study of philosophy of medicine, and I would like a logical audit of the position I have sketched below. I want to know whether my commitments hang together or whether I am helping myself to incompatible premises.

My view comprises the following six points:

(1) For somatic conditions I accept a naturalist framework: a state is a disease when an internal part functions below age- and sex-stratified statistical norms for the species in a way that reduces evolutionary fitness.

(2) For psychiatric conditions I reject evolutionary dysfunction as either necessary or sufficient; instead, a mental state is a disorder when it systematically blocks an individual from achieving minimal vital goals, along broadly normativist lines.

(3) I reject the WHO definition of health as complete physical, mental, and social well-being, because it collapses health into general flourishing and would improperly medicalize ordinary grief and aging.

(4) I hold that senescence—the universal, gradual physiological decline—is not a disease, even though it involves widespread functional deterioration relative to youthful norms.

(5) I maintain that evolutionary dysfunction is necessary for any genuine disease, but that normative evaluation enters only in the psychiatric domain, leaving somatic medicine purely descriptive.

(6) To clarify my thinking I have introduced a distinction between etiological pathology—deviation from evolutionary-functional design that causes harm—and teleonomic mismatch—statistically normal states that are maladaptive in an organism's actual environment or life-plan. I take aging to be pure mismatch, and I take some psychiatric conditions, such as severe social anxiety that prevents fulfilling unavoidable social roles, to be mismatch without underlying pathology.

Please examine these for consistency. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and what is the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity, specify exactly which commitment must give and what that costs me, and identify the single revision that would clean up the most problems. I do not want citations or a reading list—only conceptual analysis.
