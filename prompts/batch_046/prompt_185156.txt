I have recently been appointed senior deliberation officer in a mid-sized municipality's Office of Civic Participation, where my team has moved beyond one-off public-comment periods to structured, recurring consultations on zoning, infrastructure, and budget proposals. After working carefully through John Dewey's writings on democracy and inquiry—especially his claim that democratic life is best understood as a cooperative experiment in which citizens form hypotheses about shared conditions, test them against experienced consequences, and revise both means and ends in light of those consequences—I want to translate this philosophy into a reusable operational procedure. You should act as an expert fluent in both Deweyan experimentalism and modern participatory governance practice. Before constructing anything, please think carefully and briefly outline which conceptual elements—fallibilism, the social and cooperative character of inquiry, the distinction between technical administration and genuinely democratic participation, and the revision of norms through experienced consequences—must survive simplification and which can be pragmatically streamlined without betraying the theory.

Then deliver the procedure itself: an ordered flowchart in words that my facilitators can run on any concrete proposal entering our pipeline. Specify the questions to ask in sequence, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, the explicit stop-points where the procedure must halt with a determination of "no," "redesign," or "return to problem-definition," and the points—clearly flagged—where judgment about values cannot be eliminated and where a small, well-meaning team is most likely to smuggle in unexamined assumptions. Fix the practical constraints: facilitators have limited technical expertise, decisions must be documented for an elected board, and the process must conclude within a defined timeline rather than running indefinitely. Apply the procedure to three cases: (a) selecting a route for a bus rapid-transit line where engineering efficiency conflicts with dense neighborhood disruption; (b) siting a composting facility near a low-income residential area, where the risks and benefits of the proposed "experiment" are asymmetrically distributed across populations; and (c) a scenario in which the procedure outputs a recommendation for participatory redesign rather than approval or rejection, but that redesign conflicts with the mayor's office pre-committed political timeline—specify exactly what the procedure directs the facilitator to do when democratic inquiry clashes with hierarchical authority, and include at least one case where the result is politically unwelcome to my office. For each case, show the step-by-step run.

Finally, please be explicit about where your reasoning is most vulnerable: identify the weakest point in your translation, specify which alternative interpretation of Dewey's experimentalism or which practical failure mode would cause you to revise the procedure's structure, and state clearly what argument or evidence would change your mind about whether this procedure remains faithful to Dewey rather than a distortion of it.
