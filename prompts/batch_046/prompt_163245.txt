I am taking an advanced seminar in philosophy of mind and writing a paper on the conceptual aftermath of Dennett's brain-transplant thought experiment, in which a brain is extracted, duplicated, and the original destroyed, generating the familiar where-am-I problem. To avoid writing yet another survey of psychological versus bodily continuity, I want to identify which open questions in this neighborhood actually deserve concentrated philosophical effort—say, the kind of work that could be done by a small research group over three years without requiring new neuroscience.

To ground the question, consider a variant I have been developing: rather than an abrupt transplant or copy, imagine that over a decade a subject's neural tissue is replaced incrementally with synthetic substrates that preserve functional architecture, so that there is never a discrete moment of transfer, only a gradual shift. This raises questions that the standard binary version obscures.

Given that framing, I am weighing three potential research priorities. First, whether psychological continuity theories need a threshold condition—some specific degree of functional preservation—below which identity is lost, and whether such a threshold could be non-arbitrary. Second, whether the where-am-I framing itself rests on a mistaken assumption that personal identity requires a single, localized point of view, and if so, whether abandoning that assumption dissolves the puzzle or merely relocates it. Third, whether resolving these conceptual issues has any downstream implications for practical ethics—specifically, for how we conceptualize agency and responsibility in patients undergoing prolonged deep brain stimulation or partial neural prosthesis.

Which of these should be prioritized, and in what order? I am less interested in a balanced literature review than in a defended agenda: for each line, explain why it is upstream or downstream of the others, what makes it tractable for philosophers rather than empirical scientists, and what kind of result—conceptual, not experimental—would cause you to reorder the priorities. Also, are there other questions in this space that I am neglecting which should displace one of these three?
