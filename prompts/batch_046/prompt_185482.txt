Read Spike Jonze's Being John Malkovich (1999) through the debate over the Cartesian theater and Dennett's multiple drafts model of consciousness. The film literalizes an inner audience: through a hidden portal, people enter John Malkovich's head and observe the world through his eyes, seated like spectators in a private auditorium with no control over the action. Does this literalization endorse the Cartesian theater—an inner observer watching the show of experience—or does it satirize it by revealing the absurdity of an audience that lacks agency? Pay particular attention to the shift when Craig Schwartz moves from observing to possessing Malkovich: he eventually controls Malkovich's speech, relationships, and career, as if one draft among many has achieved temporary dominance. Does this possession support Dennett's claim that there is no central author of consciousness, only competing drafts that seize the substrate? Or does the film still presuppose a singular stage—Malkovich's body—where exactly one draft must take over at a time, thereby smuggling a theater back in under a different name? I want a reading that takes both the narrative logic and the philosophical critique seriously: where does the metaphor of "occupancy" illuminate distributed processing, and where does the need for a singular bodily vessel resist it? Which scenes should a philosophy of mind class examine, and what does the film show about the horror of being reduced to a substrate for others' experience—something that pure argument about the absence of a central observer tends to miss?
