Design a decision-tree taxonomy—built from logical commitments rather than from named schools—classifying philosophical positions on whether synthetic minimal cells, seeds capable of centuries-long dormancy, and trained artificial neural networks count as alive. The tree should use exactly four diagnostic splits that a respondent can answer without knowing theory labels: (i) whether evolutionary lineage is metaphysically necessary for life or whether de novo engineered functional organization suffices; (ii) whether suspended metabolism is absence of life, paused life, or irrelevant to status; (iii) whether information-processing without biochemical metabolism can ground life-status; and (iv) whether causal origin (engineered, evolved, or emergent) makes a difference independent of present function. From these, derive the branching structure: start with (i), then branch on (ii), then on (iii) and (iv) as needed, allowing every combination of yes/no/unclear answers. At each leaf, place one coherent position—strict evolutionary essentialism, metabolic functionalism, organizational pluralism, historical-continuity functionalism, and informational-process vitalism—marking any leaf that lacks a prominent defender as "unnamed" and giving a one-sentence description of its most awkward commitment regarding at least one of the three test cases (e.g., that a synthetic cell is alive despite lacking ancestors, or that a dormant seed is dead despite retaining repair mechanisms). Identify all unoccupied combinations in the logical space and diagnose each in one sentence: is it incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored? If two positions collapse onto the same branch and cannot be separated by these four questions, flag the overlap explicitly rather than inventing a distinction. The audience is an upper-level philosophy-of-biology seminar familiar with standard definitions (autopoiesis, metabolism-reproduction-evolution) but not with niche taxonomies; keep the instrument to six branching nodes and no more than eight leaves. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or requests for further reading—the deliverable must be answerable by reasoning about the commitments and borderline cases alone.
