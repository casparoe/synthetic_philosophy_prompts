I have recently been appointed legal guardian for an elderly relative now in the advanced stages of dementia, and I am simultaneously navigating the adoption of a child raised in a communal-care tradition rather than an exclusive nuclear-family structure. These circumstances have made the question of personhood practically urgent rather than merely theoretical. I want to understand what a genuinely adequate relational theory of personhood—one that takes seriously the Ubuntu-inspired claim that personhood is constituted through community rather than possessed prior to it—would actually have to deliver.

Write the full list of desiderata: the phenomena it must explain, the distinctions it must preserve, the results it must not contradict, and the boundary cases it must adjudicate. Specifically, address:

- The core explanatory targets: why someone who no longer recognizes family members retains full moral and legal status; how a child can maintain a unified identity across multiple legitimate caregivers without fragmentation; and whether a person who is permanently excommunicated or lives in complete isolation thereby loses, retains, or never possessed personhood in the first place.

- The distinctions that must not be collapsed: between being a person and being recognized as one; between relations that genuinely constitute personhood and those that merely express, sustain, or instrumentally support it; between moral status and social role; and between obligations owed to persons and conditions necessary for being one.

- The non-philosophical constraints: consistency with developmental psychology on the social origins of self-awareness, with legal frameworks that treat personhood as non-contingent on current cognitive capacity, and with anthropological evidence that some societies do not sharply distinguish individual identity from communal membership.

- Which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable for any relational theory and which are preferences of particular camps—for instance, whether active reciprocity is required, or whether asymmetrical, sustained care obligations suffice to maintain personhood.

- Any subset of these desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable, particularly any tension between the claim that social relations genuinely constitute personhood and the claim that personhood is inalienable regardless of social abandonment.

Then briefly indicate how three broad approaches fare against the list: a liberal individualist account, a strict Ubuntu relational account, and a care-ethics relational account—focusing especially on the dementia and adoption cases, but without turning this into an extended comparative essay. A table is acceptable. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citation recommendations; I need conceptual architecture, not references.
