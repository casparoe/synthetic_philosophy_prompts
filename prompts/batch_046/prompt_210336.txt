I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of science, currently writing my dissertation on the structure of conceptual change in formal and empirical disciplines. In a seminar last week on self-reference and axiomatic limits, we spent considerable time on Douglas Hofstadter's notion of jumping out of the system—JOOTSing—and on the classic MU-puzzle drawn from formal string-manipulation systems, in which the unsolvability of deriving MU is visible only from a meta-level that recognizes structural invariants (like the preservation of the number of I's) that the system's internal rules never mention. I've become convinced that treating scientific paradigm shifts as instances of JOOTSing rather than as gradual, anomaly-driven adjustments captures something important about incommensurability, but I want to test whether this analogy actually holds water or collapses into mere metaphor. Could you describe the most important implications of applying the JOOTSing framework—specifically the kind of meta-level structural recognition required to resolve the MU-puzzle—to our understanding of rational comparison between incommensurable scientific frameworks? I am particularly interested in whether this view forces us into epistemic relativism, whether it provides a principled criterion for distinguishing legitimate framework-transcendence from arbitrary abandonment, and what it implies about the possibility of a complete scientific explanation that never needs to step outside its own formal apparatus.
