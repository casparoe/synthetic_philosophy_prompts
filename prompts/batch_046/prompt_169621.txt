I'm putting together a handout for a philosophy of politics workshop and I want a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually think freedom is, not by which label they like. Start with a concrete case: a worker whose boss never gives a direct order but runs an algorithim that adjusts schedules and pay in real time, so the worker can technicly quit but never knows what quitting would cost them. From there build branches that ask about the things that actually split positions: does arbitary power count as unfreedom even when it is never exercised? does a tax that funds universal education reduce negative liberty or expand positive liberty, or is that distinction missing something? does structural poverty that blocks university access count as a constraint, or only when an agent deliberately causes it? The tree should cover negative, positive and republican liberty, but push into the weeds — include a branch on whether algorithmic choice-architecture that shapes behaviour counts as domination when no human is directly interfering, and another on whether benevolent interference that removes risky options is still interference. Each leaf should be a named position if one exists (Berlin-style negative, Berlin-style positive, Pettit-style republican, maybe an unnamed hybrid), plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Also map the empty combinations — for instance, someone who says domination matters but capacity deficits don't, or the reverse — and say briefly whether they seem incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Flag any pairs your tree can't really seperate. Write it casually, in British English, with a typo or two if that feels right.
