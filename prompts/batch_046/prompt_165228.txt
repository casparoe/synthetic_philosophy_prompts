I serve on the Intergenerational Resource Governance Council, a treaty body tasked with deciding whether to authorize Project Continuum: a self-replicating robotic extraction program in the outer solar system that would generate material resources for an indefinite, possibly infinite, sequence of future generations, but only by irreversibly altering a presently protected celestial region. The proposal's ethical defense relies on what its authors term asymptotic intergenerational parity — a constraint requiring that the per-capita welfare of each successive generation be no less than 99 percent of that of the preceding one, thereby producing an unbounded total of future benefit while keeping the present sacrifice finite, though never achieving full equality with current populations. The council is divided. One faction holds that obligations extending infinitely forward dilute present duties to practical negligibility, and that the non-identity problem undermines any claim that future people are harmed by non-authorization; the other faction holds that an infinite welfare stream, however slightly attenuated, morally outweighs any bounded present loss, and that parity is sufficient to prevent exploitation across time. Draft the council's formal decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or approval with binding conditions. The memo should present an actionable bottom line, give reasoning that the losing faction could recognize as addressing their strongest point, and specify any treaty provisions necessary to enforce the parity constraint without allowing present generations to treat future ones as mere instruments.
