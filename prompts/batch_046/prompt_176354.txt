Write a deadpan Lonely Planet-style travel guide entry for Tranquilia, a fictional nation that has enshrined Tranquilism — the philosophical view that only the absence of craving and suffering has value, while pleasure and achievement are at best irrelevant — into law. Around 500 words, cover local customs (how citizens greet one another by declaring wants they have successfully eliminated), the national holiday Null Day marking the census of unmet needs, and a practical sidebar for visitors on navigating restaurants where enjoyment is technically legal but socially suspicious. The satire should land the standard objections — that absence is nearly impossible to measure, that removing craving might remove the drive to build or create, and that suffering and deep care are often intertwined — but only through deadpan travel advice, never by stating them outright. Keep the voice casual and deadpan; a typo or two is perfectly fine.
