hey, working on a study-group handout about the cogito and i'm tired of the standard flowcharts. want a decision tree that actually sorts people by what they think Descartes's I-think-therefore-I-am line gets them, not by which label they slap on it.

build a taxonomy / tree. start commitment: you accept the argument proves at least something about the present moment. then branch on concrete questions that split interpretations for real.

node questions (phrase so someone can answer without knowing the jargon):
A. is the "i" a persisting substance, a bare particular, or just the grammatical slot of an occurrent mental event? include the Lichtenberg-style "it thinks like it rains" path where there's no owner at all.
B. does the proof say anything about persistance over time, or is it strictly momentary? if over time, is it direct intuition or via a hidden continuity premise?
C. is the inference direct/performatative (you can't think and be wrong that it's you) or does it rely on a suppressed major premise linking mental activity to existence?
D. injection / niche split: is the certainty indexical security (like knowing "here" points to wherever you stand) versus metaphysical security (like discovering a substance in the world)? this should cross-cut A-C, not line up neatly.

each leaf: named position if there's a real occupant (Cartesian substance, Humean bundle, no-owner, etc.) or "unnamed". one sentence on its most awkward commitment, e.g. if you take indexical-only plus momentary-only, can you ever say "i existed yesterday"?

flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually seperate. flag answer combos that look incoherent rather than just unpopular, and combos that are empty but conceptually possible.

format:
1. The tree (~350 words): nodes as answerable questions, then path labels.
2. The leaves (~300 words): path, name/unnamed, awkward commitment sentence.
3. Empty / incoherent branches (~150 words): unoccupied combos and diagnosis.
4. Unseparable pairs (~100 words): where the tree fails.

write casual, rationalist-blog voice. no citations, no bibliography, no "read Descartes Meditations II" stuff - a model without web should be able to build this from logic alone. avoid asking for obscure 19th-century texts by heart; stick to the conceptual space. and if you want, you can let "seperate" slip in naturally, not required.
