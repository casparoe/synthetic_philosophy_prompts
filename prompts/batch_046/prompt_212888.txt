ok so i'm trying to map out my commitments on just war stuff for a discussion group and want to check if they hang together or if i'm smuggling in contradictions. here's where i've landed:

1. i buy walzer-style moral equality of combatents — uniformed fighters on both sides have the same rights/liabilities regardless of whether their cause is just.
2. but i also think the separation between jus ad bellum and jus in bello is mostly an instutitional fiction, not a deep moral fact; the reason soldiers don't get to kill civilians is tied to the same rights-structure that makes unjust aggression wrong.
3. if a soldier genuinely knows (high credence) that their side is unjust, they're personally culpable for killing enemy soldiers, even though they technically retain the right to do it.
4. collective blame for an unjust war should sit with leaders and the voting public, not with rank-and-file soldiers who don't control policy.
5. drone operators ought to face stricter epistemic obligations than infantry — they have time, distance, and data access, so they can't lean on the fog of war excuse as easily.
6. in practice i think epistemic access is so bad that point 3 applies to basically nobody, so equality holds in almost all real cases.
7. i think soldiers should use expected-value reasoning about justice: if they assign >50% credence to injustice, they shouldn't fight; below that, they're clear.

so my questions: which of these are genuinely in tension vs just looking weird? specifically, does 2 undermine 1? does 5 violate the separation thesis in 2? is 7 compatible with equality at all? and is there a hidden tension between 4 and 3 about where culpability actually lives? rank the tensions by severity and tell me the cheapest fix for each — but don't just say drop equality because i think there's something salvageable there. also, is there an apparent contradiction that actually dissolves once you distinguish liability from culpability?

write it like an audit, not a lecture. and please don't tell me to read walzer or cite page numbers — i don't have the book in front of me and i want to think this through myself.
