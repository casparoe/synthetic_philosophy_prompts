two people in my study group, jake and mira, got stuck arguing about whether hume's standard of taste actually saves objectivity about beauty. jake said hume gives this checklist for the true judge — delicacy of taste, practice comparing works, being free of prejudice, and having good mental faculties — and if you meet those conditions your judgments converge, so beauty isn't just "whatever i like," there's a real standard out there.

mira said thats circular. you identify the good judges because they agree with each other, then you use them to prove agreement exists. she also said hume still locates beauty in the sentiment, so all he's done is filter out noisy feelings, not show beauty is in the object. then she brought up aesthetic supervenience — that if beauty tracks physical properties like symmetry or color balance, then either it's reducible to those properties (which kills the sentiment part) or it's causally inert. she said hume's standard never addresses that, it just tells you whose feelings are calibrated to the physical features.

jake came back that mira's smuggling in metaphysics hume rejects. for hume beauty IS the sentiment, and the correlation with physical traits is just constant conjunction, not supervenience. the standard is functional — like testing eyesight — not circular. he said even if calibrated judges disagree on some weird contemporary sculpture, that just shows cultural variance within a shared framework, not that there's no standard at all.

mira said okay but then "objective" is doing too much work. if the best you can say is "well-trained people tend to agree," thats intersubjective consensus, not objectivity. and she pointed out that hume's own examples — the same poem that pleases a young person disgusts an older one — show that even the "true judge" might shift over time, so the standard is more like a statistical average than a fact of the matter.

they kept going in circles and neither would budge. can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall? also tell me what both are missing, maybe something about how hume's standard relates to modern cognitive aesthetics or the supervenience problem mira mentioned. keep it casual, i dont need a formal essay.
