I've come around to the view that career politicians should be replaced by a hybrid system: strict term limits plus legislative sortition from a qualified pool, with permanent civil servents handling the actual drafting. The claim is that this kills careerism without sacrificing competence, since expertise lives in the staff, not the legislators. What would count against it? I want the serious defeaters, not just "people like voting for faces." Specifically: does this just create a shadow class of permanent staff who become the real lawmakers? Is there an accountability gap where randomly selected citizens can't be recalled? And has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability—like, if a sortition experiment fails, defenders just say "the pool wasn't right" or "the staff had too much power"? Rank what's fatal vs. awkward, and say what defenders of the model would honestly concede counts against them. Be honest if the position is actually well-insulated and thats a problem.
