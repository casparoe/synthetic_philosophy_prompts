ok so i'm writing up my philosophical profile for a grad seminar on practical reason and want to check consistency before i present. here's roughly where i've landed: (1) i buy Bernard Williams's internalism about reasons — a reason for A to φ exists only if there's a sound deliberative path from A's actual motives to being motivated to φ; (2) i also think moral requirements are catagorical in the Kantian sense, binding regardless of what you happen to care about; (3) i think that 'sound deliberative path' can include rationally acquiring new motives, so internalism doesn't collapse into just 'follow your presant desires'; (4) i hold that so-called 'external reasons' are at best descriptions of what a fully rational agent would do, but they lack genuine normative pull if you don't have the matching motive; (5) i think it's rational to treat moral reasons as decisive in deliberation even when they clash with my strongest current inclinations.

my worry is that (1) and (2) pull in opposite directions, and (4) seems to undermine (5) if moral reasons turn out external. but maybe (3) saves the package? also (5) feels like it needs (2) to be real, yet (4) denies that kind of reality. can you audit this set? sepcifically: which pairs are genuinely in tension vs only apparently, rank them by severity, and for the worst tell me the least costly revision and what it costs. don't just flag every combo — i want to know what actually has to give. also, am i sneaking an externalist commitment in through the backdoor via (3) or (5) without noticing?
