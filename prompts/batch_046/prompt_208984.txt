I retired five years ago from a career in experimental physics and have since taken up philosophy with some seriousness, most recently through a continuing-education course in metaphysics at my local university. Next month I am to moderate a colloquium Q&A for a visiting scholar—an unnamed philosopher who defends what she calls 'contextual profile nominalism', a reconstructed nominalist view according to which universals are neither Platonic forms nor Aristotelian immanent entities, but rather 'modal profiles': structured dispositions of particular objects that become metaphysically salient only relative to a comparison class fixed by perceptual or practical context. On this account, resemblance is not grounded in shared properties but is treated as a derivative, three-place relation between two particulars and a contextual frame, and instantiation is rejected altogether as a primitive metaphysical relation.

I should like you to draft a sequence of eight to ten interview questions suitable for a philosophically literate but non-specialist audience—fellow retirees, science graduates, and advanced undergraduates—questions that go well beyond inviting the guest to restate her position. Specifically, I want at least one question addressing whether the classic 'one-over-many' argument is genuinely dissolved by this view or merely relocated into the prior selection of comparison classes; at least one probing the circularity risk if resemblance itself must be analysed without any appeal to shared universals; and at least one pressing on how higher-order properties—properties of properties—are accommodated without smuggling universals back in through a structural back door. For each of these three, please include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to offer: for instance, that comparison classes are pragmatically rather than metaphysically fixed, that resemblance is primitive at the level of frames, or that higher-order talk reduces to patterns among first-order profiles. The questions should anticipate these standard replies and push further.

Finally, please conclude with one question asking what empirical finding or conceptual result could in principle count against the view, as opposed to merely prompting an adjustment of the contextual frame. Avoid generic biographical inquiries, requests for bibliographies or reading lists, and appeals to highly technical apparatus—such as the fine structure of modal logic or recent specialist debates in analytic ontology—that would leave my audience behind.
