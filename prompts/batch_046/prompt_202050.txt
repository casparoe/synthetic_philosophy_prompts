My friend serves on a municipal board proposing a voluntary "life-coordination" program in which an AI system manages residents' daily choices—diet, social commitments, work priorities—using continuous biometric and psychological data to maximize long-term reported well-being. The board now considers making enrollment the default, with opt-out permitted only after mandatory counseling. My friend defends this as genuine liberation: people chronically mispredict what will satisfy them, and freeing them from the burden of choice is the highest form of respect. I find the default-opt-out structure troubling, but I struggle to articulate whether my resistance is merely sentimental attachment to choice, or whether there is a principled reason that trading decision-making authority for guaranteed contentment corrupts something essential about human flourishing. I would value help clarifying the philosophical structure of this disagreement and identifying the strongest objection to my friend's position.
