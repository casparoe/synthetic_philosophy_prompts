I'm trying to map my gut feelings about demandingness, going case by case. Saving a kid from drowning even if it ruins your shoes: required, no debate. Donating 10% of income to malaria nets: also required, feels wrong not to. Donating 50% and living near poverty: heroic but not required. Picking a career you hate just to earn more and donate: same, supererogatory, not mandatory. Now the tension. If I skip a $5 coffee every day, that adds up to lives saved over years, but demanding I do that feels crazy. Yet refusing to donate 10% also feels wrong. What's the difference? Helping my friend move on saturday instead of working overtime to donate: I think I should help my friend, even though the donation saves lives. But if I skip the move to binge TV, thats fine. So it's not just about lost donations. The case I keep flip-flopping on: parents with small kids -- should they be held to the same donation standards, or is parenting already a kind of contribution? Is there one principle that gets all of this -- maybe about roles, or thresholds, or not using people as means -- and if not, which verdict should I drop? And what does it predict for someone who takes a low-paying meaningful job instead of earning-to-give?
