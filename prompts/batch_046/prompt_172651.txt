hey, writing up my view on introspection for a phil of mind / clinical psych seminar and want to check if it actually cohere. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) occurrent phenomenal states — sharp pain, color experience — are accessed more or less directly and are definately reliable, though fallible.
(2) for standing propositional attitudes i mostly buy the transparency thesis: knowing i believe p is world-directed reasoning, not scanning some inner file.
(3) but emotional introspection strikes me as genuinely unreliable — confabulation, affective forecasting errors, etc. — so first-person reports there need real skepticism.
(4) despite that, i think clinicians diagnosing mood disorders should weight patient self-reports more heavily than behavioral or biomarker data.
(5) there's also an observer-effect: attending to an affective state changes its phenomenal quality, but this doesn't happen with sensory experience or belief.
(6) i reject the unitary-faculty model — introspection is just a cluster: direct access for qualia, inferential for attitudes, interpretive for affect.
(7) and i think emotional introspection reliability varies with linguistic resources: richer emotion vocabularies yield more accurate reports, but this cultural factor doesn't touch sensory introspection at all.

my worry is that (3) and (4) pull in opposite directions, and that (7) might actually undermine (3) by showing emotional introspection can be accurate. does (5) threaten (1) if some pains are affective? is (6) just an ad hoc escape hatch? can you audit the package — rank tensions by severity, flag which ones dissolve on inspection, and say what's the least costly revision for each? i don't want to just drop (4) without knowing exactly what it costs me elsewhere.
