You chair a laboratory-ethics review board that must assign welfare-protection tiers—Tier 0 (none), Tier 1 (minimal harm avoidance), Tier 2 (active welfare optimization), Tier 3 (full moral-patient protections)—to every entity used in experiments, ranging from social insects to adaptive climate controllers. The governing principle is that moral status is graded continuously along three dimensions—phenomenological integration, preference-satisfaction potential, and ecological-interdependence—with no single dimension providing a sharp threshold, and you must personally sign each assignment after a twenty-minute review with no specialist on call. Design a reusable decision procedure—ordered tests or a flowchart in words—that translates this three-dimensional grading into an operational checklist for a non-philosopher reviewer; mark explicitly where judgment calls are unavoidable and where the procedure will misfire. Apply it to three cases: (1) a forty-subject study stressing bumblebee colonies to measure pollination-cognitive trade-offs; (2) an LLM-driven adaptive thermostat controlling an artificial-ecosystem simulation via real-time feedback loops that modify habitat parameters; and (3) a garden-snail nociception trial using chemical stimuli. One case must yield a tier you find personally unwelcome, and you must explain why the procedure forces that result despite your intuition. Present the three applications in a table with columns: Case | Tier Assigned | Critical Judgment Call | Procedure Limitation or Conflict | Signature Justification. Then provide a JSON summary with fields: procedure_name (string), ordered_steps (array of strings), unavoidable_judgment_points (array of strings), misfire_conditions (array of strings), case_results (array of objects with keys case, tier, unwelcome_result, reason_forced). Write in American English, include no bibliographies or citations, and state what the procedure cannot do.
