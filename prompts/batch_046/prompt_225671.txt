For a graduate seminar on collective ontology, draft the full specification of desiderata that an adequate resolution to the sorites paradox must satisfy when applied specifically to collective or distributed attributions—e.g., whether a decentralized institution counts as democratic, whether a population-level behavioral pattern counts as cooperative, or whether an ecological network counts as degraded—rather than to the standard individual predicates such as baldness, tallness, or heap-hood. For each desideratum, provide the conceptual pressure or concrete case that motivates it; distinguish non-negotiable constraints (such as avoiding arbitrary sharp boundaries in collective ascriptions) from camp-specific preferences (such as preserving bivalence for legal or administrative purposes); and identify any subset of requirements that appears jointly unsatisfiable, with particular attention to tensions between gradualist metaphysics and the practical need for discrete regulatory thresholds in collective governance. Keep the primary weight on the criteria themselves: which demands are genuine adequacy conditions and which are prejudices of particular theoretical camps, whether the list contains an internal contradiction that makes perfect satisfaction impossible, and which single requirement does the most work in excluding popular candidates. Conclude with a brief scorecard—no more than a short paragraph—indicating how epistemicism, supervaluationism, and a contextualist degree-theoretic approach each fare against the non-negotiable items, noting specifically where each collapses.
