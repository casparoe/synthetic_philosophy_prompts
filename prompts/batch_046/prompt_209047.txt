I am preparing to close a session of our clinical ethics seminar on disclosure and deception, but I need something more private first: my family recently faced a situation in which a physician, at my request, delivered a technically true statement about a tumor's partial response—omitting that the response was minimal and temporary—thereby allowing relatives to form a hopeful belief without any falsehood being uttered. I want a villanelle, nineteen lines in five tercets and a closing quatrain with an ABA rhyme scheme, in the voice of the person who arranged this disclosure. The two refrains should be "No word I spoke was false" and "But truth was used like a blade that keeps its edge"; they should alternate through the tercets and finally appear together in the closing couplet. The poem should take seriously the philosophical distinction between lying—asserting falsehood with intent to deceive—and misleading—causing false belief through truthful utterance that violates conversational expectation—so that the form itself tracks whether the speaker's defense of mere misleading and the accusation of moral continuity with lying actually converge or only keep circulating past each other. The conceptual rendering should be precise enough that someone familiar with the Gricean analysis of implicature recognizes the structure without the poem naming Grice, and the poem should leave the tension open rather than resolving whether the moral difference survives.
