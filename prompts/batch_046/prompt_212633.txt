I've been working through the tension between Halbwachs's claim that collective memory is reconstructed for present social solidarity and Stoler's argument that colonial archives function not as neutral repositories but as active technologies of governance. If both a community's living memory and the documentary record are therefore socially mediated constructions—each shaped by present needs or structures of power—does asking which should give way when they conflict presuppose an impossible epistemic neutrality? Should we reject that framing entirely and ask instead whose mnemonic authority is being marginalized? Or can source criticism still provide non-arbitrary criteria for adjudicating between them without pretending to stand outside all social mediation?
