I'm trying to nail down my actual view on translation indeterminicy, going by gut reactions. Field linguist hears "gavagai" when rabbits run past: i think it means rabbit, not "undetached rabbit part" or "rabbit stage" -- the hypotheses that save those seem too ad-hoc. Whole community's "Madagascar" drifts from mainland to island: i think reference genuinely shifts, no hidden anchor. One speaker consistently uses "elm" for beeches, never corrected: i still think "elm" in his mouth means elm, he's just wrong about the trees. But when an LLM maps a word in language L onto English "justice" and we can't tell if L-speakers mean justice, fairness, or social-balance, i feel there's genuinely no fact of the matter, stronger than ordinary error. And if we found every "gavagai"-speaker had a hidden mental file labeled RABBIT activated only for whole rabbits, i'd still say that doesn't settle translation, since Quine's challenge is about observable behavior, not inner files. So is there one principle behind all five? Or do i have to drop one? What does it say about a bilingual kid who uses "perro" only for brown dogs -- does "perro" in her mouth mean dog, or brown-dog?
