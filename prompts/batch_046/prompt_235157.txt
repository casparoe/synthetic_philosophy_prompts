For a philosophy-of-war reading group I am writing out my positions on intention, institutional agency, and strategic bombing, and I want a consistency audit before I present. Here is my current package:

(1) I accept the doctrine of double effect in roughly the form Anscombe defended: there is a morally decisive distinction between harm that is intended as an end or as a means, and harm that is merely foreseen but not intended.

(2) I agree with Anscombe's judgment that the atomic bombing of Hiroshima was murder, because the civilian deaths were intended as a means to force Japanese surrender.

(3) I think that in large-scale state decision-making—strategic bombing campaigns included—the mental states of leaders are distributed, reconstructed after the fact, and often opaque even to the agents themselves, so attributing a singular, clean intention to kill civilians is psychologically unrealistic and should not carry decisive normative weight.

(4) I hold that the bombing was wrong regardless, but I want the wrongness grounded in a rights-based constraint: non-combatants possess a right not to be treated as parameters in a lethal risk-calculation, independently of what attackers intend.

(5) I reject consequentialist aggregation in warfare: that more lives might be saved does not license killing innocents as a means.

(6) I think just-war theory should maintain an absolute prohibition on targeting civilians, justified not by the metaphysics of the agent's intention but by the victims' rights.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision in each case? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each say exactly what has to give. I am especially unsure whether (3) undermines the basis for (2), whether (1) can survive alongside (4) and (6), and whether my rights-based framing in (4) conflicts with my rejection of consequentialism in (5). Do not treat every pairing as problematic; identify the real fault lines, explain why any apparent conflicts dissolve, and tell me which single change would clean up the most inconsistencies without collapsing the package.
