As a journalist preparing a feature on the philosophical afterlife of quantum mechanics, I have drafted the following interpretive notes for an essay on Erwin Schrödinger's engagement with Vedanta and the problem of consciousness. I argue that his well-known advocacy for the unity of consciousness is usually misread as a mystical or pantheistic doctrine, when in fact it functions as a structural claim about the limits of individuation. On my reading, Schrödinger treats the apparent multiplicity of minds—the Vedantic Maya—not as a false appearance of reality, but as an indexical boundary that arises whenever a conscious system attempts to describe itself from within. I draw a parallel with the quantum formalism: just as the wave function of an entangled system refuses to factorize into independent, observer-independent subsystems, consciousness refuses to factorize into discrete, self-contained subjects. The result is what I term a perspectival monism: there is one reality and one consciousness, but every empirical access to it necessarily introduces a perspectival cut, and that cut is not an illusion to be dispelled but a structural feature of embedded observation.

I then pose what I call the boundary problem: if individuation is purely indexical and never ontologically fundamental, what explains the specificity and stability of each perspectival cut? Why does one embedded system experience itself as this particular observer rather than that one, without reintroducing the very multiplicity that the monism denies? I suggest that Schrödinger's view avoids the standard combination problem faced by contemporary panpsychism—because he denies that consciousness is composed of micro-subjects—but only at the cost of leaving the boundary problem unresolved.

I would appreciate your comments on this draft. Specifically: first, is my characterization of Maya as an indexical limitation rather than an ontological illusion textually defensible given Schrödinger's own writings? Second, is the boundary problem I have identified genuinely distinct from the combination problem, or does it collapse back into it? Third, does the analogy between quantum non-factorizability and Vedantic multiplicity hold philosophical water, or does it conflate an epistemological feature of measurement with an ontological claim about consciousness? I am particularly interested in whether this framing makes Schrödinger's view more coherent, or whether it exposes a deeper tension between his scientific objectivism and his metaphysical monism. Please do not provide a bibliography; I am asking for conceptual assessment only.
