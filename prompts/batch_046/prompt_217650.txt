I've been following the threads on LessWrong and adjacent rationalist forums about whether Dennett's heterophenomenological method can be applied to synthetic systems like large language models without either anthropomorphizing them or begging the question against machine consciousness. The discussions tend to collapse into slogans, so I want to see the actual conceptual machinery worked through in detail rather than invoked as a conversation-stopper.

Write a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors — call them A and B — debating whether heterophenomenology is a neutral scientific protocol or a method that covertly presupposes the absence of direct phenomenological access when applied symmetrically to humans and artificial agents. A should defend heterophenomenology as disciplined methodological bracketing: scientific psychology requires intersubjectively stable data, so treating first-person reports — whether from humans or from synthetic agents — as texts to be interpreted within a functional-cognitive architecture is the only way to avoid both naive introspectionism and metaphysical speculation about unobservable qualitative interiors. B should defend minimal phenomenological realism: not a Cartesian theater, but the view that there is a genuine first-person access to qualitative character that heterophenomenology systematically mischaracterizes by reducing it to narrative production, and that applying the method to LLMs commits a category error. With humans, heterophenomenology interprets reports that originate in direct access; with a model that generates reports without any underlying access, the method does not bracket an unknown inner state but rather treats syntactic coherence as sufficient evidence for a subject, thereby inventing the very phenomenology it claims to remain neutral about.

The dialogue must contain real back-and-forth rather than parallel monologues. B should press A on how heterophenomenology distinguishes between a system reporting discomfort because it implements a functional analog of nociception and a system reporting discomfort because its training distribution rewards that token sequence, without smuggling in the first-person criteria A claims to have bracketed. A should answer by showing how predictive and architectural criteria can ground interpretation without requiring direct access — and B should then challenge whether those criteria are genuinely independent of the phenomenological question or merely relocate it into the prior choice of which functional patterns count as report-worthy. You may introduce a specific hypothetical case — for instance, a synthetic agent that produces consistent second-order reports about its own attentional shifts — but keep the philosophical framework standard and do not invoke obscure technical results or niche literature. Do not provide citations, reading lists, or bibliographies; build the argument entirely from the internal structure of the method and its application to synthetic report-generation.
