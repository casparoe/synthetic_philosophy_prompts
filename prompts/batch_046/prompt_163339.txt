You are acting as an expert consultant in moral psychology and comparative metaethics, tasked with helping a doctoral researcher design a conceptual instrument. I am a PhD student in philosophy writing my dissertation on whether empirical findings in cross-cultural moral psychology—specifically the documented divergences between WEIRD and non-WEIRD populations in weighting harm, fairness, loyalty, authority, and purity—pose a genuine challenge to moral objectivity, or whether they can be accommodated within objectivist frameworks. I need you to construct a rigorous taxonomy presented as a decision tree that classifies metaethical positions according to how they respond to these divergence patterns, rather than according to the philosophical labels people self-apply.

Please begin by carefully mapping the logical space of possible responses before attaching any position names. Start from the shared empirical premise that there exist robust, replicable cross-cultural differences in moral judgment. The first branches should distinguish whether such divergence is best interpreted as a difference in underlying moral competence (analogous to grammatical variation) or in applied norms; if the latter, whether local social-ecological conditions provide a rational basis for the divergence or whether arbitrary cultural transmission is the better explanation. Subsequent nodes should ask whether any universal core survives—restricted perhaps to harm and fairness, or extending further—whether objectivity can tolerate parameterized application conditions (where universal principles have triggering conditions indexed to local social ecology), and whether moral cognition is better modeled as tracking mind-independent norms, as expressing response-dependent sentiments, or as a form of constructive sentimentalism in which shared affective practices generate locally valid norms. Each internal node must be phrased as a concrete claim or question that a researcher could answer without already knowing which leaf it produces.

The tree should terminate in clearly defined leaves that include, at minimum: strong moral universalism; domain-restricted universalism; parameterized universalism (defined as above); constructive sentimentalism; deep pluralism or relativism; and error theory restricted to contested domains such as purity and loyalty. Wherever a logically possible combination of answers is not occupied by any prominent view, flag the empty space and briefly diagnose whether it is likely unoccupied due to incoherence, theoretical unattractiveness, or simple neglect. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for further citations, and do not ask me to look up specific studies; the instrument must be fully usable by someone with general knowledge of the WEIRD literature but no web access. Present your answer as a clean branching tree with labeled nodes, followed by a brief scoring guide that explains how a reader’s pattern of answers maps onto the corresponding positions.
