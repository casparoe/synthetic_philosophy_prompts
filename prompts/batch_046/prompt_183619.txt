You advise the programming committee of an experimental concert series that wants to organize performances around Schopenhauer's metaphysics of music. The committee needs a reusable decision procedure to classify proposed works as belonging either to the core program—pieces Schopenhauer would treat as direct expressions of the Will, independent of representational content—or to the peripheral program—works that remain within the realm of representation by depicting Ideas, serving narrative functions, or relying on non-musical media. Translate Schopenhauer's distinction between music and the other arts into an ordered checklist of questions that non-philosophers can apply, specifying at each step what answer pushes a work toward the core and what pushes it toward the periphery. The checklist must handle program music, opera, sound art using environmental data, and purely instrumental absolute music. Apply the procedure to three proposals: a late Beethoven string quartet, an immersive installation built from field recordings of melting glaciers with no score, and a live performance of a film-score suite projected with its original images. For each case, state the procedural outcome and identify the exact step where judgment cannot be eliminated. Finally, flag one borderline category—such as instrumental music with a concealed program—that the procedure cannot resolve mechanically, and instruct the committee on how to decide those cases without appealing to external scholarship. Keep the response concise; do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
