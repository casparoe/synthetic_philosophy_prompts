Compose a dialogue between two equally competent interlocutors—one defending the view that money is a socially constructed kind sustained by collective intentionality, the other defending the view that money is a functional kind defined by its roles in exchange, accounting, and value storage regardless of collective belief. To push the debate beyond standard examples, have them examine a hypothetical silent currency: in a small community, polished shells circulate with fixed exchange rates and operate as a unit of account, yet every resident sincerely denies that the shells are money, describing them solely as ornamental tokens. Have the speakers dispute whether this case undermines constructivism, vindicates functionalism, or exposes a reflexive feedback between hidden economic practice and explicit denial. Present exactly five numbered exchanges; in each exchange, each speaker should reply in one or two sentences that directly address the other’s prior point. Omit any introduction or conclusion outside the numbered list.
