ok so ive been noticing this pattern in opinion writing and interviews that i dont have a word for. a journalist writes "the election was stolen — not that i personally believe that, but thats what my sources claim" or a doctor on tv says "this treatment saves lives, though i wouldnt pick it for my own family." theyre presenting the claim as objective fact or expert consensus, sometimes even pushing action based on it, but then immediately marking their own belief-state as separate or absent. its not hypocrisy since theyre transparent about the gap. and it feels adjacent to that moore-paradox thing about saying its raining but i dont believe it, but these cases feel different: the speaker isnt contradicting their own direct observation, theyre outsourcing belief to an institution or data-set and disclaiming personal ownership. does this specific move — asserting a proposition while explicitly flagging that your mental state is decoupled from it, especially when the belief is attributed to some external system — have an established name? or is it just a social variant of moores paradox? i want to know where the edges are: does it still apply if they say "the jury was right but i disagree" versus "the science says X but im skeptical", and how is this different from ordinary hedging or just playing it safe? i wont be satisfied with just a label — i want the carving, what counts and what doesnt.
