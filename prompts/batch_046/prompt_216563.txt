I've been working on a long-form piece about why we still don't really know what makes an "a" an "a," even though every design app and image generator on earth can spit out a thousand variants in seconds. I was chatting with a type designer friend last week and she brought up Hofstadter's old Letter Spirit project — the one where a program was supposed to invent letterforms in different "styles" — and it made me realize that most of the conceptual puzzles there never actually got settled; they just got hidden behind better rendering tech. So I'm trying to figure out where the actual live questions are now, especially for people who care about cognition and design but don't have massive GPU clusters.

Here are four lines that keep nagging at me, and I can't tell which ones are dead ends versus upstream of everything else. First, there's what I'm calling "counter-essentialism": is the enclosed negative space inside an "a" actually part of its categorical identity, or is it just an accident of certain typefaces? Second, the graded-boundary problem — if I stretch an "a"'s tail until it touches the bowl, or remove the bowl entirely, is there a principled non-arbitrary point where it stops being an "a" and becomes noise or another letter? Third, with diffusion models now generating infinite letterform variations, should we be modeling the category as a continuous manifold in style-space rather than around a single prototype, and does that shift actually help recognition science or is it just math decoration? Fourth, there's this idea of "negative letterforms" — the spaces between and inside letters — and whether a letter's identity depends partly on how it carves up surrounding white space, not just on its own strokes.

Of these directions — counter-essentialism, the graded style-to-misrecognition boundary, the manifold-modeling approach for generative systems, and the negative-space framing — which ones deserve real research effort over the next few years, and which are overrated philosophical decoration? I'm specifically asking for someone advising a small group with about three years, limited compute, and an interest in both vision science and design theory: what's tractable without giant training runs, what's upstream of the other questions, and what evidence in the next couple years would move one up or down? Definately lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then give the reasoning right after with no preamble and don't restate the question back to me.
