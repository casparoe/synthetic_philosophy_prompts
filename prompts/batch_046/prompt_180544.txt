Please argue that a non-zero pure rate of time preference—discounting a future good solely in virtue of its being future, without appeal to risk, uncertainty, or shifting preferences—is rationally permissible, and in fact required if we reject a particular form of diachronic neutrality. Frame the defense around the following conceptual move, which I want treated seriously rather than dismissed out of hand: if rational agents may weight goods by their modal proximity, giving greater practical weight to actual outcomes than to merely possible ones without being charged with arbitrary discrimination, then by structural parity they may weight goods by their temporal proximity relative to the present indexical standpoint, treating future goods as analogously “merely possible” from the agent’s present point of view. Build the argument by showing that standard objections—especially those that analogize temporal location to spatial location and charge pure discounting with arbitrariness—fail because they tacitly presuppose an eternalist or B-theoretic conception of time that collapses the indexical present into a bare coordinate, thereby erasing the very feature that makes modal proximity non-arbitrary. At the same time, acknowledge precisely where the modal-temporal analogy is weakest: for instance, that future goods will become actual, whereas merely possible goods need not, and that the agent’s future self may be continuous with her present self in ways that possible-world counterparts are not. Conclude by specifying exactly what ontological commitment about time—whether a tensed view, a presentist ontology, or a specific indexicalist semantics—your defense requires, and whether accepting that commitment is more or less costly than accepting universal diachronic neutrality. In constructing this argument, be explicit about where you are speculating versus where the reasoning is rigorous, and invite pushback if the modal analogy rests on a category mistake—say, conflating ontological status with epistemic accessibility—or if the task as posed forces an implausible synthesis that you should not be expected to defend.
