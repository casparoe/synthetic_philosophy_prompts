Design a single 90-minute essay question for a graduate seminar on interpretation theory that applies Davidson’s radical interpretation framework to a deliberately non-standard case: an interpreter attempts to assign beliefs and meanings to outputs produced by a synthetic, non-biological system that operates in a closed environment without evolutionary history, biological needs, or shared human form of life. The question must require students to do more than recite the principle of charity, the holism of belief and desire, or Quinean indeterminacy; it should force them to confront whether charity is a normative constraint that can survive the absence of shared human practical rationality, and whether holism becomes unstable when the interpreter cannot anchor attributions to observable behavior tied to survival or preference satisfaction. Provide the exam question itself, a model answer of approximately 700 words, and a marking scheme that awards specific points for distinct conceptual moves—such as recognizing charity as normative rather than empirical, explaining why holism does not collapse into arbitrary assignment without shared form of life, distinguishing interpretive indeterminacy from mere linguistic ambiguity, and addressing whether the synthetic case reveals a hidden anthropocentric premise in Davidson’s argument. The scheme should separate an A response from a B response by naming the precise arguments or distinctions each tier must contain, with point allocations that sum to 20. Write in a formal, polished register suitable for a doctoral comprehensive examination, and do not ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
