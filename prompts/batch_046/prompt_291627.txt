Write a short story centered on Molyneux's problem, but transpose the classic scenario into a near-future clinical setting. The protagonist was born blind and has spent decades identifying geometric solids—specifically a dodecahedron and an icosahedron—through meticulous tactile exploration. She now receives an experimental cortical prosthesis that does not restore biological vision; instead, it renders spatial geometry as structured auditory textures, with edges translated into sustained tones, vertices into brief percussive clicks, and curvature mapped onto harmonic density. Immediately after activation, she is presented with the same two solids she knows intimately by touch, now perceptible only through this auditory-spatial translation. Narrate her internal attempt to determine whether she genuinely recognizes the objects across modalities, focusing on the tension between authentic cross-modal identification and the projection of tactile memory onto an unfamiliar acoustic landscape. Avoid delivering a definitive philosophical verdict; conclude with her suspended between the conviction that she has finally seen what she once touched and the suspicion that she has merely constructed a new metaphor for an old familiarity.
