I'm assembling a set of conceptual writing prompts that should be answerable from internal knowledge yet specific enough to avoid generic pretraining responses. I was recently discussing how the legal framing of genocide—particularly the intent-based definition that descends from Lemkin's original formulation—creates a peculiar bureaucratic theater: tribunals and governments expend enormous energy deciding whether a precise word applies, as though the moral reality of mass violence were suspended until the correct taxonomy was certified. The politics of that naming—who controls the label, which events are upgraded or downgraded for diplomatic reasons, and how legal precision can obscure the scale of harm—strikes me as material for sustained, deadpan satire.

Please write a parody of an internal "Field Classification Manual" issued by a fictional international bureaucracy—perhaps the Bureau of Atrocity Nomenclature—for researchers tasked with labeling episodes of mass violence. Format it as a numbered procedural document of approximately 650 to 800 words, written in the flat, technical voice of an administrative handbook. The target should be the proceduralism and political calculus that surrounds the genocide category itself: the obsessive requirement to demonstrate specific intent, the diplomatic incentives to avoid the term, and the way competing classifications—crimes against humanity, ethnic cleansing, complex emergency—operate as rhetorical escape hatches. The humor should arise from exaggerating genuine features of this politics rather than inventing absurdities out of whole cloth, so that a specialist recognizes the fairness of the exaggeration, while remaining fully legible to a reader unfamiliar with the literature.

To keep the exercise off the beaten path, inject at least one invented but plausible-sounding bureaucratic mechanism—such as a "Semantic Impact Assessment" that weighs the geopolitical fallout of each label, or a "Retroactive Renaming Protocol" allowing classifications to be revised downward after funding cycles conclude—so that the satire turns on a concrete, niche procedural detail rather than broad mockery. Include within the manual a brief sidebar or appendix listing "common errors in intent verification," framing obvious moral failures as dry methodological mistakes. The irony should reside entirely in the gap between clinical language and the horror it describes; the piece should never wink or break character. Do not include bibliographies, citations, requests for further reading, or references to any named living public figure.
