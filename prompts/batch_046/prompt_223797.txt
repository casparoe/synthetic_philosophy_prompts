In my philosophy of knowledge seminar, discussion of Gettier-style counterexamples has stalled in a way that feels structurally different from ordinary theoretical disagreement. We all accept the canonical cases—the false barn facade, the stopped clock, the Smith-Jones derivation through a false disjunction—and we all reject the analysis of knowledge as justified true belief. Yet for two years three camps have remained fixed: one holds that justification is transmitted through an intermediate false premise, so the proper remedy is a no-false-lemma constraint on the reasoning chain; another, working within virtue epistemology, insists that the agent fails to deserve credit for the true belief because the correct outcome is environmentally lucky rather than competence-driven; and a third argues that we misapply closure principles, treating knowledge as closed under entailment from premises the agent does not know. We share identical case judgments, we agree JTB is insufficient, and none of us denies the others' descriptions of the examples, yet no position has yielded ground.

What kind of disagreement is this, actually? Do the camps differ about a hidden empirical claim—perhaps about how human cognition attributes epistemic credit or tracks modal stability across nearby worlds—or is the dispute normative, about which standard governs the concept of knowledge? Is there an unacknowledged formal question doing the work, such as whether justification transmission behaves like a modal robustness relation that must be invariant across worlds where the false lemma is false, and could a formal result about closure in non-classical logics dissolve one camp? Or are we facing bedrock intuitions about the disvalue of epistemic luck that no argument can shift? Given your diagnosis, what would count as genuine progress: a distinction that dissolves one position, an empirical finding about reasoning, a formal result, a change of question away from counterexample-driven analysis, or an argument that no further convergence should be expected and why?
