"Etiological accounts (Millikan, Neander) say a trait's function is determined by its evolutionary selection history—hearts pump because ancestors that pumped were selected. Causal-role accounts (Cummins) say function is just the current causal contribution a part makes to a system capacity, with no need for history. On dysfunction: etiological views say a heart is broken if it fails to do what it was selected for, even if it accidentally aids respiration. Causal-role views treat dysfunction as deviation from the statistical norm for parts in that system, so a non-pumping heart is abnormal relative to the population. One objection to etiological theories is that they can't assign functions to synthetic organisms with no evolutionary past. One objection to causal-role theories is that they're too permissive—heart sounds or heat count as functions—so they have to borrow historical norms to fix the problem, which undermines the view."
