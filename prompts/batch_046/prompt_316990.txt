Construct a thought experiment, set in a realistic institutional context (for example, a hospital triage board or a research-grant committee), in which two rational agents begin with an identically specified common prior over all relevant states, share every piece of evidence they possess without restriction, and yet reach permanently divergent posteriors about the correct decision—without either agent being deceived, bounded, or privately informed. The divergence must be driven by a single structural feature that you explicitly inject and define; this feature must not be a well-known counterexample such as self-locating uncertainty, quantum branching, or ambiguous language. The scenario should occupy one paragraph. After presenting it, demonstrate that the case meets its specifications: (1) state the shared prior and the two posteriors precisely enough to show they differ on the actionable decision; (2) identify exactly which premise of Aumann’s agreement result is violated and argue whether that premise is a necessary condition for rational belief revision; (3) show what happens to the disagreement when the injected feature is removed—does convergence restore, and why?; (4) present the strongest counter-argument available to a defender of the strict common-prior assumption, explaining how they would diagnose the violated premise as either illegitimate or misapplied; and (5) state clearly which feature of your design does the substantive work and what would collapse if that feature were weakened. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific texts; build all reasoning from the concepts as defined in the prompt.
