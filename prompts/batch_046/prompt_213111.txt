for a seminar on archives and power, lay out the desiderata a theory of archival silence actually has to meet if its going to do more than repeat "winners write history". it needs to distinguish active erasure from passive omission, account for self-censorship versus outside suppression, and explain how some voices still leak through despite systematic exclusion. it also needs to handle what you might call "generative silence" — the idea that archives dont just store absences but actively shape whats thinkable later through whats missing. for each requirement say whether its a hard constraint or just one camps preference, and flag whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, maybe giving agency to self-censorship conflicts with a fully structural account of record-keeping. keep it tight, no bibliographies.
