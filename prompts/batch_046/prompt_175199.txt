Moore's Principia Ethica for an independent study on intrinsic value, and I've found myself stuck on a structural feature of his ideal that doesn't seem to match the standard vocabulary. Moore famously ranks personal affection and the appreciation of beautiful objects among the highest goods, treating them as distinct states of consciousness that contribute to an organic whole. Yet in the way these goods are usually discussed—both in Moore's own examples and in common expositions—I keep noticing a particular circular dependency. The value of aesthetic contemplation appears to reach its peak only when it is shared with a friend, while the value of friendship appears to reach its peak only when it involves joint attention to something beautiful. This is not merely the claim that the two goods often co-occur, or that together they form a larger good; rather, each seems to require the presence of the other in order to achieve its own fully realized form. I have tried describing this as an instance of Moore's "organic unity," but that concept seems designed for parts that combine into a single experiential whole, not for two goods that remain conceptually distinct while being mutually necessary for each other's completeness.

Is there an established term in value theory or in Moore scholarship for this specific configuration—where two intrinsically valuable states depend on each other for their ideal realization without collapsing into a single compound state?
