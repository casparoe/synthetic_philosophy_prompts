As a novelist who occasionally runs workshops on narrative and existence, I want a structured in-session game for twelve participants—writers and philosophy students together—in a single seventy-five-minute meeting, designed so that they feel, before anyone names the concepts, the structural tension that absurdism exposes and that contemporary hybrid accounts of meaning in life attempt to resolve. The idea must emerge from the rules, not from a facilitator’s explanation. Please design the phases with precise timing, a mechanism of small but real stakes (not monetary—perhaps public commitment to a constructed life-project, or the loss of a collaboratively built artifact), and roles that force each participant first to defend an assigned human life-project using appeals to objective or cosmic endorsement, then to watch every such appeal systematically nullified by a hidden rule representing the universe’s silence. After that collapse, they must reconstruct a defense using only subjective attachment plus internal coherence, but under a constraint that forbids them from saying “it simply feels right”; they must instead invoke a standard of appropriateness between the agent and the project. Inject into the design a niche conceptual framing—call it the fittingness gap—to name the precise moment they discover that neither objective worth nor raw subjective desire can validate the life without a third, ungrounded criterion telling them whether their attachment is fitting to its object. Include a debrief that maps the first collapse onto the absurdist recognition of cosmic indifference, maps the second reconstruction onto hybrid theories that require both subjective attraction and objective conditions without cosmic validation, and is frank about which feature of the experience—the phenomenological vertigo of groundlessness—the rules alone cannot reproduce and the facilitator must supply through framing. Also tell me exactly what will predictably go wrong the first time this is run—likely participants smuggling hidden validators back into Phase Two, or refusing to accept the nullification as genuine rather than procedural—and how to handle it without breaking the spell. Do not request citations, names of specific thinkers, or a bibliography; the activity should stand on its mechanics alone.
