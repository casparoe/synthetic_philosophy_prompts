Having spent my career in experimental biology before turning to philosophy, I keep returning to the contrast between sphexish behavior—those mechanically rigid, ritualized action sequences philosophers cite as antithetical to free will—and phenotypic plasticity, in which an organism adjusts development and behavior to environmental cues within a fixed genotype. Could you compare these two notions as models of flexibility? I am particularly interested in whether phenotypic plasticity offers a genuinely different kind of adaptability from sphexish rigidity, or whether it merely distributes the same deterministic causation across a longer feedback loop. If the latter, then what conceptual work does the rigidity-plasticity distinction actually perform in debates about free will, and can it be maintained without smuggling in a non-physical capacity for choice?
