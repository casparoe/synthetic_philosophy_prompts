For my next novel, I am constructing a protagonist who is a retired metaphysician turned fiction writer, and I want an audit of her philosophical voice before I finalize it. She holds: (a) that classic ontological debates—over universals, numbers, and composite objects—are largely verbal disputes, in that opponents share the same world but deploy "exists" with different implicit rules; (b) that Carnapian tolerance is the right response to these cases, meaning every internally coherent ontological framework is equally legitimate and there is no framework-neutral fact about what "really" exists; (c) that within a single, fixed narrative framework—such as the world of her own novel—there are nevertheless objective, non-linguistic truths about which entities exist, so her verdict that a doppelgänger is unreal inside the story is correct or incorrect, not merely a linguistic convention; (d) that some metaphysical disagreements are not verbal but involve what I term "framework leakage," where one disputant unknowingly imports the existence-criteria of a rival framework, producing a substantive contradiction that clarification alone cannot resolve. Do (a) through (d) form a jointly tenable set? Specifically, does (c) genuinely conflict with (b), does (d) undermine (a), and is there an apparent tension—perhaps between (b) and (d)—that dissolves once we distinguish internal questions from external ones? Rank any real tensions by severity, indicate the least costly revision, and say whether the core idea that metaphysics is mostly verbal can survive intact.
