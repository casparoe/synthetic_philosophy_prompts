Longtime reader of rationalist forums here. I'm trying to pin down a conceptual knot in Rawlsian public reason that doesn't come up in the standard framing of whether religious citizens should vote for theocracy.

Give an example of a public-policy dispute—preferably one not already overanalyzed in introductory literature—where a religious citizen advances a secular, publicly accessible justification for a coercive measure (say, a restriction on some form of commerce or a positive duty of care) that genuinely satisfies Rawls's proviso: the secular argument is complete, stands on its own, and does not invoke revelation. However, the citizen's actual epistemic commitment to that secular argument is parasitic on a specifically religious eschatology or soteriology—without the religious backing, they wouldn't hold the secular premise with sufficient conviction to defend it politically, and the secular translation feels to them like a strategic costume rather than a genuinely shared basis.

The conceptual issue I want exposed is whether public reason, on this reading, demands only that the right kind of reason be present in the public square (a content condition), or whether it implicitly demands a kind of psychological independence of secular justification from comprehensive doctrine (a motivational condition), or even a social condition about which doctrines can stably support liberal institutions across generations. In other words: if the proviso is technically met but the secular reason is motivationally hollow for the speaker and wouldn't survive the removal of the religious scaffold, does public reason have any further objection? And does this reveal that the distinction between political and comprehensive doctrines tracks something real about the arguments themselves, or merely tracks which metaphysical commitments have already been culturally domesticated?

Please construct the example so that it turns on this structural feature rather than on obvious biblical citation or standard establishment-clause jurisprudence, and lay out precisely where the conceptual pressure falls—on content, on psychology, or on long-run stability.
