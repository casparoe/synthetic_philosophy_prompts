ok so me and my friend got into it about this burial co-op that offers "transformation plots" - they dont embalm, just wrap the body in linen and bury it in a wildflower meadow with no headstone, only a gps pin in a shared field. The contract literally tells families not to visit a grave because theres no individual site, but then they make you write a "life narrative" for a digital archive. My friend said this is pure Zhuangzi - death is just qi transforming, so mourning the spot is stupid and the body is just borrowed form. But I think the practice is actually schizofrenic: it treats the physical body as totally disposable nature-stuff but still clings to narrative identity in a database. So what view of death and personal continuity does this setup actually presuppose? Is it genuinely Zhuangist, or does it smuggle in a completely different metaphysics about what survives? And if we took Zhuangzis view seriously - like the story where he drums on a tub for his dead wife because shes just transformed - what parts of this co-op model would have to change?
