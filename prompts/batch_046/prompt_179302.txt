Question: ‘Infinitism, as defended by Peter Klein, holds that justification consists in an infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons, thereby escaping both the arbitrariness of foundational basic beliefs and the circularity of holistic coherentism. Critically evaluate this claim, paying particular attention to how infinitists respond to the “finite mind” objection and to the “modal compression” worry—the idea that an infinite linear dependency chain, when subjected to modal evaluation of its support relations, structurally collapses into a form of circularity.’

Student’s answer:

Infinitism solves the regress problem by denying that justification ever terminates. For Peter Klein, every belief requires a further reason, producing an infinite chain that never repeats. This avoids foundationalism’s arbitrary stopping points—beliefs justified without further reason—and also avoids coherentism’s circular mutual support.

On the finite mind objection, critics say humans cannot actually possess infinite beliefs. Klein answers by distinguishing occurrent belief from dispositional availability: one need not hold every reason in mind, but must be able to produce a further justification whenever challenged. This is analogous to a mathematician’s implicit grasp of an endless sequence.

The modal compression worry claims that under modal analysis the linear chain folds back on itself. However, this confuses representational mapping with epistemic dependency. Just because a model can represent the chain circularly does not make it circular in reality. Moreover, as Laurence BonJour’s foundherentist critique demonstrates, any linear account eventually requires a foundational anchor, which infinitism correctly rejects.

Thus infinitism offers a viable middle path. It honours the intuition that reasons must continue indefinitely, without accepting the foundationalist’s unjustified stoppers or the coherentist’s closed loops. The finite mind objection is met by the availability distinction, and modal compression misunderstands the difference between structural representation and actual justification.
