Compare the knowledge norm of assertion (that one should assert p only if one knows p) with the belief norm (that one should assert p only if one believes p) in cases where a speaker’s reasoning to p contains a false intermediate lemma that does not affect p’s truth. Construct three scenarios for a policy analyst delivering a forecast: (1) sound reasoning yielding a true conclusion the analyst knows; (2) reasoning with one false but causally irrelevant intermediate step yielding a true conclusion the analyst believes but fails to know because of the internal flaw; (3) reasoning with a false intermediate step that propagates error, yielding a false conclusion the analyst mistakenly believes. For each scenario, state precisely whether each norm permits or prohibits the assertion, and pinpoint where the two norms diverge. Then argue whether scenario (2) shows the knowledge norm is overly restrictive—penalizing a speaker whose belief and the conclusion’s truth remain intact—or whether the belief norm is too permissive, licensing assertions from epistemically defective reasoning positions. Work out the answer from the internal logic of the norms alone; do not cite authors, texts, or external authorities.
