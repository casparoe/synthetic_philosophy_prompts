A genetics educator on a podcast summarizes quantitative heritability like this: the coefficient h² is the ratio of genetic variance to total phenotypic variance in a given population, so an h² of 0.75 for IQ means 75 percent of individual differences are caused by genes. Because it is a variance ratio, shared environment is already excluded by definition. From this she infers that if a child is moved from a poor environment to a rich one, the expected IQ gain should be roughly (1 − h²) times the environmental gap, which with high heritability leaves only a small fraction closable by intervention. She also claims that since twin studies compute h² from monozygotic versus dizygotic correlation differences, the method is automatically robust to gene-environment correlation and indirect genetic effects, so recent findings about genetic nurture do not threaten standard estimates.

Please audit this summary. Separate what is accurate, what is an acceptable simplification, and what is a misrepresentation of quantitative genetics. Specifically, does heritability license the (1 − h²) formula for individual environmental gains, and are twin-study estimates genuinely immune to genetic nurture? Finally, say whether the educator's pessimism about environmental intervention survives once the errors are corrected.
