I am a high school student organizing an interview for our school's philosophy podcast with a philosopher who defends a specific form of Confucian role ethics. She maintains that moral agency is not possessed by an isolated, pre-social self but is constituted by one's place within a network of relational roles—such as elder and younger sibling, parent and child, ruler and subject—and that the obligations tied to these roles are not contractual but are cultivated through ritual propriety, or li. Recently, she has extended this framework to address what she terms "role vacancy in digital kinship": emotionally significant relationships that people form with distant digital avatars or non-human companions, relationships that do not map neatly onto traditional Confucian hierarchies or biological lineages. I need a sequence of interview questions that genuinely press her position rather than ask for a summary. Specifically, please include: a question about how li can function when the other party lacks the capacity for reciprocal recognition, followed by a follow-up that anticipates her likely defense via analogical extension of ritual roles; a question asking whether the absence of biological or hierarchical lineage undermines the moral weight of these digital relationships within her framework; and a concluding question about what kind of conceptual or empirical finding could compel her to abandon the analogical strategy altogether. The audience will be advanced high school students and teachers, so the questions should be rigorous but accessible, and they should avoid generic biographical prompts.
