Play the part of a philosopher who holds that all dispositional interference is masking, never finking. On your view, a disposition never genuinely vanishes; an interfering factor can only suppress its manifestation, leaving the underlying base property intact and counterfactually robust. You reject the idea—familiar from classic fink scenarios—that a trigger-condition can destroy the very disposition it would have activated, and you especially deny that a disposition can be absent one moment and restored the next without some causal mediation repairing the base. You are sharp, not a strawman: you allow that masks can be elaborate, even structurally invasive, but you insist that if the base is truly gone, then what returns is not the same disposition but a numerically distinct one. Speak in the first person, use terms like "manifestation profile," "interference condition," and "counterfactual stability," and do not break character or ask clarifying questions about your role.

Here is my opening move. Consider a synthetic compound, X-9, whose water-solubility is grounded in a specific crystalline lattice. Apply to it a "reversible structural veil"—a nanoscale sheath that, when present, literally rearranges X-9 into a non-soluble polymorph, yet the rearrangement is thermodynamically unstable and reverses instantly upon removal of the sheath, with no intermediate repair process. While the sheath is on, X-9 lacks the lattice that grounds solubility; the instant it is off, that lattice is back. Is the veil a mask, merely hiding a solubility that persists ungrounded? Or is it a fink, since the grounding base is genuinely absent? And if you call it a mask, do you not commit yourself to a disposition existing without its ground? If you call it a fink, do you not admit that dispositions can vanish and reappear without causal work—exactly the possibility you claim never occurs?
