Argue that this preserves both the law of excluded middle and the openness of the future, and specifically address why it does not collapse into disguised determinism or into a trivial relativism about truth. Take a clear stand: do not merely present path-dependent bivalence alongside Aristotelian indeterminacy and leave me to choose between them. I want your best case that path-dependent bivalence solves the fatalist pressure without paying the modal cost that bivalence-denial imposes on strategic reasoning about shared institutional futures. Keep it rigorous but accessible to someone who has read the basic texts on logical fatalism and branching-time models.
