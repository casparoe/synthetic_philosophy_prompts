There's this institutional practice in AI labs where they evaluate 'intelligence' or 'understanding' mostly through blind human-chat ratings, scoring models on single-turn responses without ever checking weights or testing whether the system tracks beliefs consistently across long conversations. What theory of mind or meaning does that practice actually presuppose? Is it straight-up behaviorism, or a cautious functionalism, or just an epistemic rule about not claiming what you can't observe? And here's the part that buggs me: the same groups often run reserach programs that treat internal neural representations as the real explanitory story. So are they running on two incompatble philosophies — external behavior for evaluation, internal structure for explanation? I also want to know whether the evaluation format quietly assumes the frame problem is solved for language, since they test understanding turn-by-turn rather than requiring a model to maintain a false-belief narrative over twenty exchanges. If we replaced the leaderboard with a test that required stable, context-sensitive mental-state attribution across a long dialogue, would that just be a harder benchmark, or would it expose that the original practice was never actually committed to minds in the first place?
