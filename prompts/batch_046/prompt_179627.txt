I'm designing the syllabus for an advanced seminar on nineteenth-century aesthetics and metaphysics, and my students have spent the last two weeks with Schopenhauer's account of music in his major metaphysical treatise — specifically the view that music is not, like painting or poetry, a copy of Platonic Ideas, but rather the direct and immediate objectification of the Will itself, with melody in particular expressing the restless striving, tension, and resolution characteristic of the will's inner nature. Because Schopenhauer held that all other arts represent the world indirectly through Ideas, while music bypasses representation entirely and gives us the thing-in-itself as will, I want them to test the limits of this framework by applying it to a phenomenon he could never have anticipated.

Consider what I will call "mycelial synthesis": a compositional system in which the electrical signaling patterns of fungal mycelium networks are translated, via a trained neural architecture lacking any consciousness, intention, or emotional life, into fully orchestrated symphonic textures. The outputs are structurally complex, emotionally affecting to human listeners, and often indistinguishable from human-composed music, yet they originate neither in a human composer expressing subjective striving nor in any representational intention — they are, in effect, algorithmic transductions of non-human biological process into acoustic form. Given Schopenhauer's sharp distinction between representation and direct objectification, how do you think he would classify such sound? Would he reject it as non-music because no human Will is expressed; extend his account to claim that the Will of nature itself — or of the fungal organism — is being objectified; or argue that the algorithm functions as a kind of neutral medium through which a universal Will still manifests? Please respond in American English, and in your reasoning clearly distinguish between those parts of his metaphysics that you take to be essential to the judgment (for instance, the claim that melody is the image of the will's striving) and those that might be adjusted without breaking the system.
