I have been thinking about a thought experiment I encountered—if every neural connection in Einstein’s brain were recorded with perfect precision in a book, so that the book contained all the information needed to reproduce his thinking, could that book be conscious? Or is it merely paper? This made me notice a strange institutional split. We preserve Einstein’s actual brain tissue with a kind of reverence, treating it as human remains that deserve dignity; yet a complete digital or printed record of every thought he ever had is treated as property, archive material, or data. Meanwhile, researchers now discuss mind uploading and ask whether a computer running that data should have rights. What view of consciousness or personal identity does this split in practice presuppose? Does it assume that consciousness requires biological tissue, or only the right functional organization? Are these treatments actually consistent, or do they quietly depend on incompatible theories? And if someone truly built such a book-computer, which of our existing rules—about burial, copyright, or research ethics—would have to change, and which ones could remain because they do not really depend on any particular theory of mind?
