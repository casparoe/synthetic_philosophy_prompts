Write a dialogue between a Theravada reductionist and a Madhyamaka philosopher about anatta and whether it actually eliminates the self or just renames it. The reductionist should defend the view that only momentary citta and cetasika exist, and that rebirth is nothing but kamma-vipaka carrying forward with no transmigrator at all. The Madhyamaka should accept emptiness of svabhava but insist that the conventional person is dependently designated in a way that cannot be dissolved into a heap without losing moral accountability.

Make them wrestle with a wierd injected case: after death, one mental continuum splits into two continuous branches, each inheriting part of the kamma. The reductionist should argue this is unproblematic — continuity is one-to-many, just divided causal lines, so no single subject was ever needed. The Madhyamaka should push back that without a non-arbitrary criterion for which branch counts as "the same person," the conventional designation collapses into arbitrariness, and if moral responsibility tracks that designation then it gets split arbitrarily too. They need to respond directly to each other’s actual points, not talk past one another.

Keep it focused and in the weeds, avoid generic textbook summaries, write in British English, and keep it fairly short. At the end, say how confident you are in what you’ve claimed, and flag the specific point you feel least sure about.
