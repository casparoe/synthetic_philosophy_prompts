For a philosophical evaluation of whether we live in a "hinge of history" — an era with disproportionate, long-term influence over civilization's trajectory — I want the full specification of what a theory identifying such hinges must achieve. Write the desiderata: the explanatory targets it must hit (distinguishing genuine hinge dynamics from mere turbulence or rapid change), the classification cases it must get right (e.g., the development of nuclear deterrence versus the spread of the printing press), the distinctions it must preserve without collapse (causal hinge versus normative hinge, where present agents choose among mutually exclusive value-lock-in regimes; objective structural hinge versus epistemic hinge; global versus regional hinge; and hinge identification versus ordinary risk assessment), and the consistency conditions it must satisfy (with path-dependence models in economics and history, and with the constraint that hinge status not depend on outcomes causally inaccessible to present agents). For each, state the motivating consideration, and flag which are non-negotiable versus camp-specific preferences. Add one further, self-injected constraint: the theory must supply a criterion for "counterfactual hinge stability" — whether the hinge status persists across nearby possible worlds in which the triggering technological or political event fails to occur — and must not conflate this with forecasting confidence. Identify any subset of these desiderata that appears jointly unsatisfiable, especially whether requiring both objective hinge status and full epistemic transparency to present decision-makers can be co-satisfied. Briefly score the pure statistical-duration objection (that any fixed epoch is a priori unlikely to be the maximally influential one) against the list, and say which single desideratum does the most to exclude it without circularity. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than on a full survey of existing views.
