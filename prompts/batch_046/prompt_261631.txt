My study group tried to apply global workspace theory to split-brain cases and ended up with something abserd. (1) In classic split-brain patients the corpus callosum is cut, so left and right hemispheres can't broadcast to each other—each becomes its own workspace. (2) If that's right, then unity of consciousness just is continuous global broadcasting across the cortex. (3) But broadcasting breaks all the time: during sleep spindles, under anesthesia, in attentional blinks, even in momentry working-memory consolidation failures. (4) So by the same logic we should be constantly splitting into temporary sub-persons or blanking out entirely, which doesn't happen—at least not in the way we experience it. (5) Either global workspace theory can't ground the split-brain conclusion, or we're actually a flickering swarm of micro-minds and the feeling of continuous selfhood is just the left hemisphere making up a story. (6) That feels like it proves too much. Which step should I reject, and what does giving it up do to either the split-brain interpretation or the workspce model?
