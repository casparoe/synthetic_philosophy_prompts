Write an essay assessing whether the dogmatism paradox genuinely threatens rational belief revision, with particular attention to whether a structurally analogous problem arises in distributed, non-symbolic architectures such as neural networks. Outline the paradox: if rational agents are required to be closed under logical consequence, then discovering that one belief is false can force the abandonment of arbitrarily distant other beliefs merely to preserve consistency, which seems irrational; yet denying closure appears to license logical blindness. Weigh the principal symbolic responses—rejecting logical omniscience, adopting non-prioritized or selective revision strategies, or tolerating limited dogmatism—before examining the claim that connectionist systems escape the difficulty. In neural networks, parameter updates that accommodate new evidence in one domain implicitly shift dispositions across unrelated domains without explicit logical ordering; determine whether this distributed revision avoids the paradox because there is no explicit belief set, or whether it reproduces the same problem in an obscured form, substituting opaque weight drift for transparent logical conflict. Reach and defend a clear verdict: does the paradox identify a universal constraint on rational updating that applies across representational architectures, or is it merely an artifact of the symbolic belief-set model?
