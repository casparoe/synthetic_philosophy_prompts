I chair the allocation committee for the Mid-Atlantic Regional Mental Health Trust. At next quarter's vote we must decide whether to pilot the "Discontinuity Protocol," a funding framework proposed by our clinical-ethics working group that applies a lexical-threshold form of negative utilitarianism. Under the Protocol, any intervention reducing suffering above a defined "autonomy-erosion threshold" receives absolute budgetary priority; suffering below that threshold, together with all positive welfare and pleasure, is treated as incommensurable with severe-suffering abatement, so no cross-domain resource trade-offs are permitted. Some committee members object that this assigns zero rather than merely lower weight to mild suffering and pleasure, and that using an autonomy-based metric to fix the lexical boundary smuggles in a non-utilitarian conception of personhood, undermining the framework's internal coherence. Proponents counter that without such lexical incommensurability, marginal funds will always drift toward high-volume, low-intensity wellness programs that yield measurable but ethically insignificant gains. Please draft the committee's decision memo recommending whether to approve the pilot, reject it, or approve it with conditions; include a concrete implementation timeline or sunset clause, and provide an honest, substantive account of the strongest consideration favoring the position you do not recommend.
