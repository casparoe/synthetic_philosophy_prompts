I take the view that the dollar auction and organizational escalation of commitment are not distinct failures of rationality but expressions of a single structure: an agent rationally continues when the expected cost of exit—including damage to a maintained strategic identity or credible posture—exceeds the expected cost of further investment, even knowing recovery of the original goal is impossible. On this account, labeling escalation a "sunk-cost fallacy" mistakes a commitment-preservation mechanism for an error in expected-value reasoning. What would count decisively against this unification? I want the empirical cases, formal conditions, or conceptual distinctions that genuinely separate auction-style bidding wars from organizational lock-in—particularly where one occurs without the other, where the rationality conditions apply in one domain but fail in the other, and whether the view can be qualified to absorb every counterexample without becoming unfalsifiable. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a defender of the unified view should themselves concede as damaging.
