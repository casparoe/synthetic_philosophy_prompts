I serve as the semantic-reconciliation lead for a federated institutional knowledge graph that aggregates autonomous records—legal registries, clinical files, and civic databases—into a shared ontology. Because nodes are contributed without central vetting, we routinely encounter direct contradictions: the same entity is marked both bankrupt and solvent, both citizen and non-citizen, or a single statute is listed as both repealed and in force by different authoritative sources. Our governance charter forbids silent deletion of conflicting entries, since that erases institutional provenance, but it also forbids allowing arbitrary inferences—what paraconsistent logic calls explosion—to propagate through the graph from a localized contradiction. I need a reusable decision procedure, grounded specifically in the core commitments of dialetheism and paraconsistent logic, that a reconciliation team with no formal logic training can run in roughly fifteen minutes per conflict cluster.

The procedure should take the form of ordered questions or tests, each with a criterion for what counts as a sufficient answer, and it should specify the exact point at which the process stops with a technical resolution—merge, isolate-and-flag, reject, or escalate—versus the point at which the question becomes one of values or liability and must be handed to a human signatory. It must distinguish contradictions that are genuinely dialetheic (both true, or at least both institutionally authoritative) from those that dissolve under disambiguation, temporal indexing, or category error. It must also be explicit about where judgment calls are disguised as procedural steps, and it must flag the kinds of misfire it is likely to produce—for example, retaining a contradiction that creates downstream legal exposure.

Please construct the procedure, then apply it to the following three conflict clusters:

1. A clinical graph contains two authoritative nodes for the same patient: one records a severe penicillin allergy, the other records an active penicillin prescription from a different hospital system. The contradiction is direct, the sources are both accredited, and patient safety protocols depend on the graph output.

2. A corporate entity is registered as dissolved in Jurisdiction A and as actively trading in Jurisdiction B, with no clear conflict-of-laws resolution available in the metadata. Retaining both statuses exposes the graph to liability if contracts are executed against the dissolved entry, yet deleting either erases a valid legal record.

3. A physical-asset node is labeled liquid in a financial-liquidity module and liquid in a materials-state module, with the contradiction arising only because the predicate is homonymous across domains.

In your applications, include at least one case where the procedure yields an answer that the reconciliation lead will find unwelcome—specifically, a case where logical isolation is technically correct but operationally costly. Finally, state precisely what the procedure cannot do: which categories of contradiction it will systematically misclassify, which institutional risks it cannot internalize, and where it must always defer regardless of the logical form.
