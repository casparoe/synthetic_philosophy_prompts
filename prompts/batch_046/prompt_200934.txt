As a retired physicist who has recently begun studying philosophy of mind in earnest, I have been trying to articulate my own view of selfhood without merely adopting a standard textbook position. Below is the package of commitments I have settled on; I would like a careful consistency audit before I present it to a discussion group.

I take Hofstadter's strange loop seriously: the self is not a substance but a symbol—specifically, the pronoun I—that becomes genuinely self-referential when a representational system of sufficient complexity directs its capacities back upon itself, and I treat this as describing an actual emergent pattern rather than a metaphor. That loop is causally efficacious—disrupt the dynamics that constitute it and the system's behavior changes—so it is not an epiphenomenal gloss. I am a physicalist about the substrate: the loop supervenes on neural dynamics, with no non-physical ego or soul. I also think the loop denotes something genuinely real, ontologically on a par with a vortex or hurricane, rather than a useful fiction. For the reference to be genuinely self-referential rather than merely self-describing, I hold that the system must represent its own representational acts, which requires a second-order loop. I accept that conceivability is an unreliable guide to possibility for emergent properties, so the conceivability of zombies or disembodied selves does not by itself threaten physicalism. At the same time, I maintain that the qualitative what-it-is-like aspect of the loop—the first-person feel—is irreducible to any functional or structural description; it is essential and not capturable by any third-person account. I further insist that physicalism requires supervenience, so a brain physically identical to mine in every detectable respect could not lack my loop. Finally, I believe that closing the loop requires continuous, analog integration in real time; discrete, step-wise computation, however complex, cannot achieve the seamless feedback necessary for genuine self-reference.

Please audit this set for me. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each conflict? For each real tension, indicate the least costly revision and what it would cost me. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging, and tell me whether there is a single revision that would clean up the most problems at once. I ask that the answer be written in American English and be kept formal and polished.
