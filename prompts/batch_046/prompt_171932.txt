hey so im stuck on this thing with my repair cafe and i need someone to poke holes in my reasoning. ive been running a small community repair space out of a rented storefront for about a year now — people bring broken toasters, bikes, old laptops, we fix them for free or on donation. last month we found out our electricity is being pulled from the buildings shared meter through an old wiring mistake from before we moved in. not dangerous, just unaccounted for. the landlord found out during an audit and said if we come forward he has to disconnect us immediately until we get our own dedicated meter installed, which takes like 3-4 months and costs around 4k that we absolutely dont have. in the meantime we serve maybe 80 to 100 people a week, and for a lot of them this is the only way they can keep essential stuff running — one guy needs a working fridge for insulin, another uses a repaired bike for delivery work, etc.

my co-founder wants us to just stay quiet, keep running, and settle the unpaid portion quietly later if anyone asks. his reasoning is that the harm of shutting down is huge and spread across dozens of families, while the harm of keeping the lights on is tiny and falls entirely on one property owner who probably wont notice a small spike. he also says theres basically no real difference between me not preventing all that suffering and me actively causing it by locking the doors — either way im the one making the choice that leads to people going without.

but i keep feeling like theres something categorically wrong with deliberately keeping power i know isnt mine, regardless of how the numbers shake out. like, failing to save everyone is one thing, but actively doing something that violates someone else's rights to do it feels like a different kind of wrong. at the same time, if i shut us down just to keep my own hands clean, im basically choosing a worse outcome for a lot of vulnerable people over a minor inconvenience to a landlord. is there actually a reason the rule should hold even when breaking it prevents way more damage than it causes? and does it matter that the people hurt by closing are totally different people from the one hurt by staying open?
