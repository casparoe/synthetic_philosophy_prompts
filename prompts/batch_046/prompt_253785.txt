I am constructing a novel in which a philosopher-character becomes obsessed with Borges's metaphysics of time. She keeps repeating the idea that the present is a fiction we agree to maintain, attributing the thought to Borges. Before I put these words in her mouth, I need to understand exactly what philosophical machinery this line might conceal. Could you lay out the precise claims this slogan could stand for, separating at least the following readings: first, an ontological denial of temporal becoming, in the vein of Borges's arguments against the reality of succession and his engagement with Schopenhauer and Berkeley; second, a psychological thesis about the specious present as a constructed artifact of memory and anticipation; third, a linguistic-fictionalist claim that temporal indexicals lack genuine referents; and fourth, a social-coordination thesis that the present is a pragmatic fiction communities maintain to enable collective action. For each reading, say whether it amounts to a platitude, a substantive and defensible position, or something false; indicate which, if any, corresponds to the argument Borges actually advances in his essays on time; and identify the feature—such as the denial of causal relations between discrete moments—that does the philosophical work. Then explain what happens if that feature is removed: does the slogan collapse into ordinary psychological presentism, or does it retain independent force? Finally, advise me which reading would make my character sound rigorously philosophical rather than merely lyrical, and how a defender of the weakest reading would attack the stronger ones. Please provide your answer in American English.
