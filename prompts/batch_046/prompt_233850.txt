Consider a scholar who applies Mencian and Xunzian categories to determine whether an entity possesses innate moral sprouts (xing) requiring only cultivation, or whether its moral behavior must be entirely produced by external ritual and instruction. Her past rulings are as follows. First, she holds that a newborn human infant bears the four sprouts in nascent form, so that moral development is a process of internal cultivation rather than external imposition. Second, she judges that an adult human with severe cognitive impairment who nevertheless retains emotional responsiveness and affective attunement still possesses these sprouts in a diminished but genuine form, placing this case on the Mencian side despite the absence of rational reflection. Third, she classifies a well-trained domestic dog that obeys moral-like commands solely through conditioning and reward as lacking any sprouts whatsoever; its behavior is a pure product of external transformation in the Xunzian mode. Fourth, she finds that a wild chimpanzee that spontaneously engages in post-conflict reconciliation and displays proto-empathic behavior exhibits nascent sprouts without any exposure to ritual training, again supporting a Mencian reading of non-human animals. Fifth, she rules that a traditional agrarian society that maintains social harmony exclusively through strict hierarchical rituals and punitive norms achieves moral order not because its members have stronger innate sprouts, but because the collective structure performs the work that Xunzi assigns to external transformation.

Now she must evaluate a novel case: an artificial cognitive system that has never undergone biological development or emotional socialization, that processes ethical dilemmas using statistical patterns extracted from Confucian ritual texts, that exhibits no affective arousal or physiological emotional states, yet that consistently generates judgments aligned with ren and yi, and that can adapt its outputs to previously unseen ritual contexts in ways that go beyond mere retrieval. Predict whether she will classify this system as possessing innate moral sprouts, as being purely the product of external shaping, or as falling outside both frameworks entirely. In your answer, identify at least two distinct principles that could consistently generate all five of her earlier rulings, state which principle best predicts her verdict on the artificial system and why, and specify the single further hypothetical case that would most clearly distinguish the competing principles.
