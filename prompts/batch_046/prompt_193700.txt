Please provide the complete desiderata: the empirical patterns from moral psychology and evolutionary biology the theory must accommodate without contradiction; the distinctions it must preserve, including between an individual's preference profile and its normative weight, between organically evolved dispositions and engineered institutional tilts, and between collective decision structures and synthetic agents whose value architectures are designed rather than evolved; the boundary cases and logical constraints it must survive, particularly avoiding circular identification of bias through normative outcomes alone. If any subset of these desiderata proves jointly unsatisfiable given known logical or empirical results, present that tension explicitly; it is the central finding I want to highlight. Finally, identify which single requirement eliminates the largest share of current popular accounts, and indicate whether that exclusion should be treated as a feature or a bug.
