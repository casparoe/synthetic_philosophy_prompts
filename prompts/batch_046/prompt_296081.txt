hey, i'm a retired microbiologist who got into philosophy late in life, mostly because i wanted to understand why people care so much about things that don't replicate in a petri dish. lately i've been chewing on G.E. Moore's Principia Ethica — specifically his claim that friendship and beauty are among the greatest intrinsc goods. i jotted some notes for a local reading group and they feel wobbly, especially where i try to mix in biology. here's the gist:

1. Moore says a beautiful thing is valuable because it produces a certain conscious state, but the value belongs to the whole (object plus appreciation), not to the parts separately. he calls this organic unity.

2. Friendship, for Moore, is also an intrinsic good, but it's relational — it involves two people across time. so i started wondering: is a friendship an organic unity in the same sense? if you remove one friend, the remaining half isn't just a diminished friendship; it's categorically different, like cutting a plant in half and expecting both pieces to stay the same organism.

3. here's where i get into the weeds. in ecology there's obligate mutualizm — two species that can't survive apart. i think Moore might be treating friendship like obligate mutualism, but actually most human friendships are facultive: you can survive without them, they just improve fitness. if friendship is facultative, then maybe its value is more instrumental than Moore admits, which conflicts with calling it an intrinsc good.

4. but then i pushed further. if long-term friends form a coupled system — like two neurons firing in sync — the organic whole might not be inside either person's head but in the interaction pattern itself. so maybe Moore was right that value lives in the whole, but wrong to locate it inside individual consciousness. it sits in the space between them, like a magnetic field.

5. i also wonder if this makes friendship closer to beauty than Moore says. a sunset needs an observer; without one it's just photons. similarly friendship needs two observers to maintain the relational field. if both vanish, the friendship disappears entirely, not just weakens.

my questions: am i misrepresenting Moore's organic unity? is there a standard objection that friendship can't be intrinsic because it's relational? and does my biology analogy clarify anything, or am i just smuggling in metaphors? i'd especially like comments on point 4 — does it make philosophical sense, or have i invented a problem that doesn't exist? please don't worry about giving me a bibliography; i just want to know if the argument holds water. also i wrote "principia" with an extra e in one spot in my draft, but that's not important. thanks!
