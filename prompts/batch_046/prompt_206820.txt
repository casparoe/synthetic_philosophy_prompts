I've mostly bought into a causal-role account of biolgoical function, but with a specific twist: the system boundary that defines the role isn't arbitrary or evolutionary, its fixed by organizational closure (autopoiesis). So the heart's function is pumping blood because thats the contribution that sustains the closure right now; malfunction is just deviation from that sustaining role, and evolutionary history is at best a heuristic for finding roles, not what makes them functions. This lets me avoid proper-function talk entirely.

What would count against this? I don't care about the alien-heart objection. I care about: (1) synthetic biology cases where a part clearly has a designed function that doesn't currently contribute to closure, (2) whether closure is just smuggling etiological history in through the back door, and (3) whether malfunction really makes sense without some normative baseline that my view denies.
