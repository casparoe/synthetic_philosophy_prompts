Write a short dialogue between two equally competent philosophers, Alia and Bren, on where meaning resides in a tripartite message structure consisting of a frame message (the interpretive protocol), an outer message (the decoding instructions or symbolic pattern), and an inner message (the intended semantic content). Alia argues for frame primacy: she holds that the frame is the authoritative semantic key, not a mere syntactic scaffold, and that if the outer message attempts to override or recursively invert the frame, the result is not deeper meaning but a collapse of interpretability. Bren argues for distributed, recursive meaning: he holds that the frame, outer, and inner layers can correct one another, and he introduces a specific hypothetical artifact—a self-inverting triplex in which the frame mandates literal symbol-by-symbol decoding, the outer message commands metaphorical inversion of that very literalism, and the inner message describes the act of inversion itself—to show that fixing meaning solely in the frame renders the inner content unintelligible. The dialogue should exhibit real back-and-forth: Alia must directly address Bren's triplex and explain why recursive correction does not produce richer meaning but semantic nihilism; Bren must respond to Alia's charge of nihilism by showing how frame-fixation itself produces an empty formalism. Both should concede partial points without becoming straw figures.
