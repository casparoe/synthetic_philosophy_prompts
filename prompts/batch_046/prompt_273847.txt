I am a high school student preparing an independent philosophy project on practical rationality. Please construct a decision-tree taxonomy that sorts positions on whether honoring sunk costs is always irrational. The tree should be built from the logical space of answers, not from a list of philosopher names: begin with concrete cases—such as whether one should finish an expensive, half-completed artistic project solely because of effort already invested, whether collective sunk costs (e.g., a publicly funded infrastructure plan) generate different obligations than private ones, and whether loyalty to one's past commitments can ever justify continuing a failing course of action. Then map those answer-combinations onto named positions where they exist (strict instrumental rationalism, commitment-device compatibilism, narrative-identity views) and mark unoccupied combinations as unnamed, with one sentence diagnosing whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Crucially, include a branching node that separates time-neutral evaluation—judging each choice only by its future prospects—from evaluation that weights diachronic practical identity: does preserving continuity with one's past self ever provide a non-instrumental reason to honor a sunk cost? Every internal node must be phrased as a question answerable without knowing philosophical labels. The tree should have six to eight leaves. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or philosopher-name quiz; the instrument should classify someone purely by what they say about the cases.
