As a retired physicist who spent decades working with statistical ensembles and ergodic theory, I have recently begun studying philosophical debates about probability and belief, and I find the Sleeping Beauty problem both familiar and disorienting. The standard formulation involves a subject who is awakened either once or twice depending on a coin flip, then asked for her credence that the coin landed heads. In my scientific background, we constantly face the choice between ensemble averages—averaging over many independent systems in parallel—and time averages—following a single system through its successive temporal stages. This makes me wonder whether the disagreement between halfers and thirders can be illuminated by comparing two interpretations of what the awakening events represent: on one view, the Monday and Tuesday awakenings are like independent draws from an ensemble of centered worlds, so that finding oneself awake provides statistical information about the size of the ensemble and should shift one's credence toward tails; on the other view, they are successive temporal stages of a single persisting agent, so that the update rule should depend only on the prior credence plus the indexical knowledge that one is currently awake, without treating the second awakening as an additional sample. Could you compare these two interpretations—call them the ensemble-sampling view and the single-agent temporal-update view—specifically with respect to how each treats self-locating belief? I am particularly interested in whether the ensemble analogy genuinely clarifies the formal structure of centered-world credences, or whether it misleads by importing assumptions from statistical mechanics that do not apply to indexical uncertainty.

To test whether this distinction is robust or merely an artifact of discrete framing, I want to consider a modified scenario I have not encountered in the literature: suppose Beauty is not awakened at discrete times but is maintained in a continuous state of consciousness for the full 48-hour period if the coin lands tails, and for only a 24-hour period if it lands heads, with her being asked for her credence at a moment selected uniformly at random during her conscious interval. In this continuous formulation, there is no clear Monday awakening versus Tuesday awakening to serve as discrete samples. Does the ensemble-sampling interpretation collapse into the single-agent view, or does it generate a different quantitative answer altogether? And does this continuous variant expose a hidden assumption in the standard discrete formulation—perhaps about the identity conditions of the subject across temporal parts, or about how to weight moments within a lifespan—that the two interpretations disagree about but rarely make explicit? I would like the comparison to address both the discrete and continuous cases, and to say whether the continuous version favors one interpretation or reveals that both depend on an unstated premise about how to count awakening-moments in the reference class.
