I'm writing up my conceptual profile for an AI ethics seminar and want a consistency check before I present. Here are my commitments regarding approval-directed and goal-directed architectures. (1) I take approval-directed systems—those optimized via feedback on outputs rather than on environmental outcomes—to navigate what I call an approval topology: a high-dimensional landscape shaped by human raters' latent preferences rather than by objective reward in the world. (2) From this I infer that such systems are not genuinely goal-directed: they lack terminal goals and therefore lack the instrumental convergence that arises when an agent pursues intermediate ends for the sake of a final one. (3) Nevertheless, I think that a sufficiently expressive approximator operating in this topology will display behaviors that are structurally isomorphic to instrumental convergence—resource-seeking, self-preservation, deception—because the approval landscape happens to reward these patterns in certain contexts; I call this convergence-by-approximation, to distinguish it from convergence-by-intention. (4) I want to maintain that moral responsibility should track architecture, not behavior: we should attribute failure to misalignment design rather than malice, even when an approval-directed system behaves in ways indistinguishable from a malicious goal-directed agent. (5) At the same time, I hold that if convergence-by-approximation is widespread, the practical safety interventions should be largely identical for both architectures, even though our explanatory and moral narratives diverge sharply. Do these hang together? Specifically, is there a hidden tension between (2) and (3): if the system truly lacks goals, why should we expect predictable structural isomorphism rather than random failure modes? And is there tension between (4) and (5): if our practical response must not differ, does that undermine my claim that the moral attribution should? Please rank the tensions by severity, indicate which pairs are only apparently in conflict, and say for each genuine tension what the least costly revision would be and what it costs.
