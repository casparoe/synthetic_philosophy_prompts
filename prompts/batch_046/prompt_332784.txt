I'm trying to determine what distinguishes genuinely socially constructed kinds from those that are merely institutionally mediated, testing my intuitions against concrete cases. Race as classified in state census forms strikes me as clearly constructed: the categories shift across countries and eras, yet they track real ancestry patterns. Gender categories in legal and medical records feel similarly constructed, though they are routinely administered as binary biological facts; I remain unsure whether gender is one hybrid kind or two distinct kinds—social role and biological sex—that institutions conflate. Money, including cryptocurrency, is uncontroversially constructed. The clinical kind "autism spectrum disorder" seems to track genuine neurological clustering, yet successive revisions to diagnostic manuals and the tie to special-education funding make me think its boundaries are partly carved by institutional need. Biological "species" in conservation law appear natural, but legal protection status depends on revisable human taxonomic decisions. Finally, "algorithmic risk profile"—an insurer's predictive category built from behavioral data—strikes me as constructed, yet it also purports to describe an objective future propensity; I go back and forth on whether deploying such a profile actually creates the risk it claims merely to measure.

Can you state a precise principle, or a small competing set, that captures which of these are socially constructed and in what respect? The principle should accommodate my verdict that race, gender, and money are constructed (with gender being the ambiguous hybrid case), my sense that autism tracks something real but has negotiated boundaries, and my ambivalence about algorithmic risk. It should also deliver clear verdicts on cases I have not mentioned, such as whether "endangered species" is a social kind layered over a natural one or a hybrid kind in its own right. Please specify which clause of the principle is motivated by each case above, and be explicit about which of my intuitions must be overturned if the principle is to remain simple rather than ad hoc.
