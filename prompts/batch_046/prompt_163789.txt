For an upcoming podcast episode, I will interview a philosopher who advances what she calls the “opacity-lien” account of promissory obligation: promises bind not through the promisee’s reliance or the promisor’s autonomy, but by generating a normative debt whose strength is indexed to how opaque the promisor’s own reasons were to herself at the moment of utterance. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate the view. Include at least one question pressing whether this account collapses into a form of evidentialism about practical reason, one on what it implies for promises made under conditions of complete self-knowledge, and one on whether a broken promise can be “discharged” in something like the way a financial debt is discharged. For each question, provide a follow-up keyed specifically to the reply she is most likely to offer—especially replies that appeal to a distinction between first-order and second-order opacity.
