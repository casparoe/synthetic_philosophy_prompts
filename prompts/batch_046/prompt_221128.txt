I'm working through Lakatos's distinction between progressive and degenerating problem shifts, and I seem to have hit an internal contradiction in his framework. Lakatos holds that a programme is progressive when modifications to its protective belt generate genuinely novel predictions, and degenerating when they merely accommodate anomalies already observed. At the same time, he insists that the hard core is protected by a conventional methodological decision and is never directly refuted; all empirical pressure is deflected onto auxiliary assumptions in the protective belt. Consider how contemporary cosmology handles recalcitrant data: flat galactic rotation curves are addressed not by revising the gravitational hard core—whether Newtonian or Einsteinian field equations—but by postulating dark matter in the protective belt, while the accelerating expansion revealed by Type Ia supernovae is accommodated by reviving a cosmological constant term, again leaving the core equations intact. In both cases, the adjustment appears tailored specifically to absorb a known anomaly. By Lakatos's own lights, hasn't this programme executed precisely the immunizing maneuver that makes a shift degenerating? If every long-running physical theory survives only by shielding its hard core in this way, then either Lakatos must classify modern cosmology as degenerating, or there is some hidden feature of these adjustments—perhaps in their predictive structure—that distinguishes them from the ad hoc epicycles he criticizes. But I cannot see what that feature could be, since the hard core remains untouched either way, and the anomalies themselves prompted the adjustments. So what distinguishes a legitimate protective-belt revision from a merely ad hoc rescue, and does that distinction allow Lakatos to maintain that programmes like general relativity remain progressive despite relying on exactly this protective mechanism?
