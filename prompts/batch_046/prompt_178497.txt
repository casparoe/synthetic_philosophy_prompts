Compare tranquilism—the view that only the absence of craving and suffering possesses intrinsic value, with positive pleasures carrying at most instrumental worth—against asymmetrical hedonism, which treats suffering as intrinsically weightier than equivalent pleasure is intrinsically good. Focus specifically on whether tranquilism can consistently characterize the complete cessation of craving as an active achievement rather than a neutral default state, without implicitly relying on a positively valued condition of contentment that its official axiological framework denies. Then evaluate which position provides the more coherent justification for preferring a life of sustained, low-intensity calm over a life marked by intense but intermittent joy, and whether that justification can be sustained within tranquilism's own restrictive commitments.
