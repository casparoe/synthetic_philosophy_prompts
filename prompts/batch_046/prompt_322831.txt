Compare the pessimistic meta-induction's conventional picture of scientific theory change—where past successful theories are largely false and replaced wholesale—with a "referential drift" model in which key theoretical terms do not vanish but gradually shift their referential content across revolutions. Concentrate on what each model implies for structural realism's defense that mathematical structures survive change even when ontologies do not: does drift preserve enough continuity to save the realist, or does it erode structural retention itself? In answering, clearly separate what is widely acknowledged about the historical record (for example, that some now-rejected theories yielded accurate predictions) from what is genuinely disputed (for example, whether structural continuity is robust enough to defeat the induction, or whether drift undermines even structural preservation). Avoid presenting any single assessment of structural realism's success against the pessimistic meta-induction as if it were the settled consensus among philosophers of science.
