I'm presenting a reconstructed interpretation of Weber's Protestant Ethic thesis to an interdisciplinary workshop on historical explanation and the philosophy of social science. My thesis is that Weber should be read not as claiming that Calvinist theology directly caused capitalist rationalization, but as identifying what I call a normative ratchet: in specific confessional communities, economic practices compatible with systematic accumulation became self-reinforcing because economic failure was interpreted as potential evidence of spiritual reprobation. This did not create capitalism ex nihilo, but functioned as a constraint-shaping mechanism — it filtered out alternative economically rational practices (such as consumption-oriented merchant banking or aristocratic rent-seeking) that were available in materially comparable Catholic regions, thereby explaining the differential persistence and intensification of accumulation rather than its origin.

The audience is precisely this: three economic historians of early modern Europe who are committed to materialist explanations (they think in terms of resource endowments, trade networks, and state formation); two quantitative sociologists who study cultural values through survey instruments and think "culture" is either unmeasurable or endogenous to institutional incentives; and one philosopher of social science who specializes in Weber's methodology and is skeptical of importing causal language from the natural sciences into interpretive sociology. They care about archival specificity, operationalization, and whether an explanation respects the logical structure of interpretive understanding.

Give me the objections in roughly the order they will be raised, ranked by combined likelihood and damage. Conclude with the question I am hoping nobody asks — the comparative challenge that takes my mechanism outside the European confessional context — together with the reply and its softest point. This is preparation for spoken Q&A, not a written defense: do not provide a bibliography, do not ask for sources I should cite, and do not include verbatim quotations from Weber or any other author.
