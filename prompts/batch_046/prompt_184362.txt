As a retired biologist who has recently begun studying philosophy of biology, I am struck by how quickly the standard examples in the function debate—hearts, kidneys, flagella—become familiar. To push past that, I want a dialogue that applies the core disagreement to a genuinely novel case. Please write a formal, focused dialogue between Dr. Elara Voss, defending an etiological or selected-effects account of biological function, and Dr. Marcus Reed, defending a causal-role or systemic-capacity account. Set their discussion around a hypothetical engineered structure: a synthetic membrane-bound vesicle, de novo expressed in a modified bacterium, that fixes carbon using an artificial chromophore and is sustained by cellular feedback loops. It has no evolutionary history—no ancestral variants were ever selected for carbon fixation—yet it clearly contributes to the organism's energy economy. Voss should argue that attributing a proper biological function without a selection history collapses design intent into natural function, and she should address Reed's likely reply that organizational maintenance and contribution to system viability are sufficient. Reed should argue that function is fixed by present causal role within the system's organizational closure, pressing Voss on how her view handles evolutionary novelties and synthetic biology, and he should respond specifically to her worry that causal-role accounts cannot distinguish functional contributions from accidental byproducts. Both should be equally competent; neither should talk past the other. Keep the exchange polished, avoid generic biographical questions, and do not include requests for citations, bibliographies, or further reading.
