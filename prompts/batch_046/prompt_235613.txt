Here's the essay question we gave: In moral particularism, the idea is that moral reasons are "holistic"—their weight or even direction can flip with context, a bit like how gene expression depends on epigenetic environment rather than being hard-wired. Explain what particularists mean by this holism about reasons, drawing on Jonathan Dancy's view that moral reasoning has a distinctive shape that isn't governed by invariant principles. Then evaluate whether particularism can avoid collapsing into a hidden generalism about "context-rules," and whether it can still account for our practice of justifying judgments with general features—like saying that was cruel, therefore wrong—without treating those features as carrying fixed moral weight.

Here is one student answer, about 400 words:

Particularism is the view that there are no moral principles that tell us what to do in every case. Jonathan Dancy argues that moral reasons are holistic: a feature that counts in favor of an action in one situation can count against it in another. For example, causing pain is usually a reason not to act, but in dental surgery it is part of the reason to act. This shows that the moral relevance of a feature depends on the whole context, not on a universal rule.

Dancy thinks this means moral reasoning doesn't have the law-like structure we see in physics. Instead, it's more like perception: we just see what matters in each case. Because of this, particularists think moral judgments are basically intuitive or emotional responses to the situation, since there are no principles to guide us.

This also means particularists must reject any general talk. If someone says cruelty is wrong, a particularist should say that's false, because cruelty might be justified in some extreme context. So particularism denies that features like cruelty have any stable moral valence at all.

The epigenetics analogy helps here: just as genes don't determine outcomes because the environment changes everything, moral features don't determine outcomes because context changes everything. So particularism is basically the idea that moral properties aren't real determinants, just like some people think genes aren't real determinants.

Overall, particularism captures the messiness of real moral life, but it makes reasoning impossible because there are no rules.
