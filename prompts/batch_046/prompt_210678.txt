I recently had a lengthy argument with a close friend about a scenario we both encountered: an interactive educational assistant that not only tailors its explanations to a user's cognitive style but also gradually reweights the importance it assigns to different subjects based on inferred emotional engagement, all without explicitly disclosing this prioritization logic. My friend maintained that because the user retains full autonomy to discontinue use and is never coerced, the system's influence is entirely acceptable—merely presenting information in a more compelling form.

I countered that when an influence mechanism operates beneath the threshold of the influenced party's awareness, and specifically targets the architecture of their preferences rather than addressing their rational capacities, it constitutes an unacceptable form of control regardless of formal consent. We reached an impasse because we could not determine whether the relevant distinction should hinge on transparency, on the preservation of rational autonomy, or on some other criterion altogether. I would appreciate a careful analysis of where the boundary ought to be drawn between acceptable influence and unacceptable control in cases involving adaptive, opaque mechanisms, particularly one that applies directly to the kind of situation I have described.
