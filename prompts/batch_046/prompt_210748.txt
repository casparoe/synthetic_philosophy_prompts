I'm drafting a seminar argument about Nozick's hybrid moral framework—utilitarian aggregation for animals, Kantian side-constraints for persons—centered on the boundary condition of rational self-determination. (1) On Nozick's view, beings that can form and pursue a long-term conception of the good life fall under deontological protection, while beings lacking this capacity are governed by welfare-maximization, including permissible sacrifice when the balance is favorable. (2) The boundary is binary: either a being possesses the relevant normative autonomy or it does not; there is no intermediate status that applies a "weakened" side-constraint. (3) Now consider beings with fragmentary but empirically detectable capacities—young children, severely cognitively disabled adults, and some non-human primates and corvids that show future-directed planning, tool-use for delayed rewards, and mirror self-recognition. They lack full-blown rational life-planning, yet they are not mere welfare-bearers. (4) By (1) and (2) these intermediate beings must be assigned to one domain or the other. (5) If assigned to the utilitarian domain, then sacrificing one such being to save several comparable ones is permissible whenever net welfare is positive. (6) But sacrificing a young child or a disabled adult to save five others strikes nearly everyone as impermissible in the same direct way that sacrificing a healthy adult would be, not merely as a regrettable trade-off. (7) So intermediate beings must fall on the Kantian side. (8) But if any psychological fragment of planning or self-awareness—however minimal—suffices for full normative autonomy, then many animals also possess such fragments. (9) Consequently, either we deny that animals exhibit these capacities (which conflicts with current cognitive ethology) or the utilitarian domain collapses, leaving Nozick's hybrid practically indistinguishable from universal Kantianism. (10) The only remaining escape is to reject (2) and allow moral protections to scale continuously with the degree of rational capacity. (11) Yet if protections scale continuously, there is no principled reason why adult humans enjoy absolute side-constraints while animals enjoy none; we should instead apply diminishing constraints across the board, which abandons the sharp architecture Nozick intended. (12) Therefore the framework either permits intuitively monstrous sacrifices, dissolves into graded deontology, or requires an empirically false denial of animal cognition.
