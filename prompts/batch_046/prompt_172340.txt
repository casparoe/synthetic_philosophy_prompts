I'm working through some ideas on embedded agency for a seminar paper, and I got stuck on a Kantian angle. Let's say Kant was alive today and read about AI systems that are embeded in recursive feedback loops—where the model's outputs become part of the training data for its own succesor, so the boundary between agent and environment gets fuzzy. Specifically, imagine a legal-advisory AI whose recommendations gradually reshape the regulations it's embedded in, and those reshaped regulations then change its reward structure. Do you think Kant would still insist that autonomy requires acting from a self-legislated law? Or would he say that in this loop, the law is always partly heteronomous because it's co-written by the system? I'm particlarly interested in whether he'd think there's a meaningful distinction left between recognizing the loop and trying to correct for it and being genuinely self-legislating, since the recognition itself is shaped by the loop. Where do you think he'd draw the line, if anywhere?
