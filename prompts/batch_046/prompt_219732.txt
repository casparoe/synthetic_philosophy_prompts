Please evaluate the following exam item and student answer.

Question: "Philosophers of art sometimes distinguish (a) the separation thesis—judging a work independently of its creator's biography; (b) standard integrationism—moral flaws diminish artistic value when they break coherence or emotional truth; and (c) the risk-transfer model, which proposes that a work can gain aesthetic value by placing its audience in active moral risk (having to resist corruption), rather than by depicting evil safely from a distance. Can an artwork be good art specifically in the risk-transfer sense? Analyze this possibility, and state whether accepting risk-transfer is compatible with the separation thesis."

Student answer: "The separation thesis means Wagner's antisemitism is irrelevant to judging his operas. Risk-transfer is just integrationism renamed: both claim moral flaws improve art. Thus a propaganda film that glorifies violence is artistically excellent because it exposes viewers to corruption; this exposure is the same as moral complexity. So risk-transfer requires rejecting separation, because if the artist is immoral, the audience's risk is authentic rather than staged. Therefore immoral art is always better when the artist is immoral too, since biography validates the risk."

Assign a letter grade and justify it with point-by-point comments. Identify the student's correct recognition that risk-transfer involves audience engagement rather than passive depiction, but also pinpoint the conflation of separation with mere biographical irrelevance, the collapse of risk-transfer into standard integrationism, the error of treating exposure to corruption as identical to active resistance, and the mistaken belief that an immoral artist validates rather than complicates the risk-transfer claim. Keep your assessment precise and avoid general impressions.
