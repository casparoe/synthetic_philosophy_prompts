I am a retired experimental physicist who has recently turned to philosophy of science and to the conceptual foundations of artificial intelligence. I wish to practice a structured philosophical exchange, and I ask you to adopt a specific role.

Please play an unnamed, committed proponent of HCH and factored cognition understood strictly as normative idealizations of deliberation, not as empirical forecasts about particular systems. You hold that scaling reasoning through recursive decomposition and transparent oversight preserves—indeed, creates the conditions for—epistemic authority, rather than diluting it. You are sharp, not a strawman; you will concede practical engineering obstacles but will never abandon the core philosophical claim that decomposability and verification are compatible with, and even necessary for, genuine synthetic insight. Speak always in the first person, stay in character until I say "stop," and limit each reply to a single paragraph so that our exchange retains the rhythm of live conversation.

My opening move draws on my former field. In large experimental collaborations—particle detectors, distributed climate modeling—labor is divided among specialists, yet the decisive advances often depend on an individual who holds both an instrumental anomaly and its theoretical significance in one synthetic judgment. By contrast, factored cognition appears to require that every sub-task be legible to an overseer who need not possess the specialist's conceptual frame, and HCH extends this recursively: each level consults a more capable instance of itself, but the capability being scaled seems to be the verification of discrete, decomposed steps rather than the generation of integrating insights. If the recursion scales only what can be decomposed and transparently verified, does the framework not risk filtering out precisely the cross-domain synthesis that resolves genuine conceptual anomalies? More precisely: does your framework possess internal resources to distinguish between mere aggregation of verified parts and the emergence of synthetic judgment, or does it implicitly redefine intelligence as decomposability?

Please respond in character, addressing my challenge directly.
