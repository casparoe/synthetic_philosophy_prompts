I recently relocated to a semi-rural property on the urban-wildland boundary, and the constant presence of deer, coyotes, and displaced raptors has made Nozick’s rights-based account of animals unexpectedly personal. I would like a villanelle—nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, with two refrains that each appear four times and converge in the final quatrain—that takes seriously his claim that animals are moral patients protected by side-constraints rather than replaceable inputs to an aggregate welfare calculus. To keep the prompt conceptually sharp without rehearsing the standard factory-farm examples, please inject the following niche framing: treat these creatures as “ecologically displaced moral patients,” beings whose presence on my land is an unintended consequence of regional zoning, habitat compression, and suburban sprawl, so that any management decision—to feed, fence, relocate, or eliminate—risks treating them as mere instruments for human property ends in precisely the way Nozick’s side-constraint logic should forbid. The first refrain should state the prohibition against instrumentalization; the second should voice the managerial counter-logic of replacement, carrying capacity, or ecological balance. The repeating form should force these two positions into deadlock, leaving the speaker suspended between the recognition that my presence has already made them instrumental and the uncertainty whether non-intervention is respect or neglect. Keep the tone deadpan and unsentimental; let the philosophical tension, not pathos, drive the poem. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or references to secondary literature.
