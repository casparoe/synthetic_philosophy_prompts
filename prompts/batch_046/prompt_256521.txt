I am a novelist drafting a philosophical essay to accompany my forthcoming novel, which imagines a settlement where violence gradually becomes not merely forbidden but conceptually unavailable — not because citizens hold pacifist convictions, but because the settlement's ritual architecture renders aggressive action incoherent with the practices that sustain collective life. I wish to argue that the coherence operating here functions as a free-floating rationale: a normative reason against violence that resides in the structural relations of the practices themselves, rather than in any individual or collective mental state. I draw an explicit analogy with coherence arguments for expected utility maximisation, which demonstrate that rationality can be attributed to patterns of preference and choice without requiring a unified reasoning agent behind them; I extend this to propose that normative force might similarly float free of minds, embedded in the architecture of social practice rather than in belief or intention. I contrast this with communitarian defences of pacifism, which locate the commitment in shared identity, and with liberal accounts, which require explicit individual consent. The essay's more speculative claim is that such rationales are not invented or adopted by the community but are discovered within the practice-structure itself — a claim I fear may collapse into mysticism on one side or into mere causal explanation on the other. I am not seeking bibliographic references, reading lists, or citations; the argument should stand or fall on its conceptual coherence alone. Could you comment, first, on whether the analogy between decision-theoretic coherence and social-practical coherence holds, or whether it illicitly imports psychological language into structural description? Second, is the notion of a rationale that floats free of any reasoner philosophically defensible, or does it necessarily presuppose a hidden agent? Third, does my framing of these rationales as 'discovered' rather than constructed survive scrutiny, and if not, what adjustment would preserve the insight without metaphysical excess? Finally, I should welcome your view on whether this sounds like an unacknowledged variant of a familiar position in collective intentionality, though I ask only for conceptual comparison, not for attributions or sources.
