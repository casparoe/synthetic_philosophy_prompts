Consider my verdicts on gendered career patterns across societies of differing equality. (1) In Nordic countries with strong gender-equality policies, occupational segregation is often larger than in less egalitarian societies; I read this as largely reflecting freely expressed preferences once economic necessity and legal barriers are removed, so I do not treat it as unjust. (2) Norway's board quotas, however, seem to me a justified intervention to correct historical exclusion in corporate leadership, even though they override current market distributions—so I accept coercive correction at the top. (3) Where a generous welfare state makes part-time nursing or teaching attractive and continuous engineering careers less so, I regard the resulting gender gap as acceptable, even though state architecture shaped the options. (4) But if girls in the same egalitarian system are subtly steered from physics by classroom dynamics despite equal aptitude, I judge that unjust and correctable. (5) I also think a persistent female majority in primary teaching, given equal opportunity and informed choice, is not unfair. (6) Yet I am pulled toward saying that if gender differences in occupational preference widen precisely as societies become more egalitarian—suggesting biological expression—justice may still require counteracting them when they reproduce status or income hierarchies.

These sit uneasily: I want Nordic segregation to be benign because preferences are free, but I want to correct hidden steering in physics and possibly override even biologically rooted preferences that create hierarchy. Is there a precise principle—perhaps tracking the distinction between coerced and uncoerced preference formation, or between benign and hierarchical outcomes—that accommodates (1) through (5) without ad hoc complexity? State the principle, identify which clause responds to which case, apply it to an unconsidered scenario (e.g., a society where gender-neutral career counseling eliminates segregation but overall job satisfaction falls for both sexes), and tell me honestly which of my six verdicts it must reject and why.
