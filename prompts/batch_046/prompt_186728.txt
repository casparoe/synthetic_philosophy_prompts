Quick conceptual check for my grad seminar prep. We teach Sen on Bengal, the Irish famine and teh Great Leap Forward, yet international aid protocols still file famine under 'natural disaster'—same beurocratic bucket as earthquakes. I'm trying to get students to see this isn't just sloppy taxonomy. What theory of harm and moral agency does that practice actually presuppose? Does it need a hard nature/politics split, or is it really just about tractability and donor politics? I've also been playing with a hypothetical: if we replaced that classification with an 'entitlement-liability' regime—states pay into a pool when entitlement collapse is demonstratable—what philosophical commitments about collective causation and responsibility would that smuggle in? I want them to map the implicit theory behind both the old framework and my imagined one, not just parrot Sen. Keep it focused—no need for bibliographies, just the conceptual architecture.
