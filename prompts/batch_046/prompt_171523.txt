Please construct a realistic, one-paragraph scenario—set in a professional guild or municipal cooperative, not a trolley problem and without exotic physics—featuring a member subject to an unfair internal rule, such as a mandatory contribution to a fund she never uses. Engineer the case so that a fair-play theorist concludes she still owes compliance because she receives non-excludable protections she cannot waive, while an associative-obligation theorist concludes she owes nothing because she has formally waived all excludable benefits and the remaining protections are bare legal statuses rather than cooperative goods. After presenting the scenario, show the reasoning each theory gives, identify the structural feature—specifically the combination of excludable benefits with an available waiver mechanism—that forces the split, explain whether making all benefits genuinely non-excludable collapses the disagreement, and note how a defender of the losing theory would criticize the case as rigged.
