Write an essay addressing the following controversy. In The Mind's I, edited by Douglas Hofstadter and Daniel Dennett, a dialogue considers whether a book that encodes every informational pattern of Einstein's brain could be conscious. Extend this scenario to what we might call a participatory text: an open manuscript continuously rewritten in real time by thousands of readers, such that the text's present state causally determines the rules governing its next revision, producing a sustained feedback loop. Assess whether this distributed, collectively authored system could satisfy the conditions for consciousness—particularly the demand for a unified, self-referential structure—raised in that dialogue. Weigh the claim that consciousness is substrate-independent and requires only appropriate functional organization against the counterclaim that it demands localized causal integration and an intrinsic point of view that no dispersed textual network can possess. Reach and justify a specific verdict: either that a participatory text of this kind could in principle be conscious, or that consciousness is necessarily tied to a singular, embodied substrate with properties that distributed books cannot replicate. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
