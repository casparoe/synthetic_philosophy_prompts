I live in a residential cooperative governed by a board of five. Over the past four years they have eliminated maintenance backlogs, kept fees below inflation, and installed security that actually works. This quarter they enacted a charter amendment they call a performance covenant: residents may vote to remove board members only if service-delivery metrics fall below targets for two consecutive quarters; otherwise appointments are made by the board itself. They expelled a resident who circulated an unaudited alternative budget, and several neighbors defend the change by pointing to the results. I feel uneasy accepting authority that is justified solely by quarterly outcomes, but I cannot tell whether my discomfort reflects a real philosophical problem or mere nostalgia for voting rituals. Please advise me in American English, treating my situation as an instance of the broader question of whether prosperity and orderly administration can justify removing democratic accountability. Do not offer legal steps or a reading list; I want to know whether there is something conceptually wrong with letting delivery scores replace consent as the source of legitimate authority.
