Compare endurantism and perdurantism as ontologies of persistence through time, but do not restrict yourself to the familiar temporary-intrinsics objection. Instead, examine both views through the following framing: consider a concrete particular that undergoes total qualitative change—losing every intrinsic property it originally possessed—while retaining exactly one relational property, namely being the continuous object of attention for a single observing subject whose own diachronic identity is itself philosophically contested.

First, reconstruct the endurantist claim that the observed particular is literally identical across all stages despite sharing no qualitative features, and explain whether this claim can be sustained without covertly appealing to spatiotemporal continuity conditions or relational structures that are more naturally accommodated within a four-dimensionalist framework. Second, present the perdurantist account in terms of temporal parts, and evaluate whether the relational anchor of continuous observation supplies any non-circular criterion for unifying those parts into one persisting entity, or whether it merely relocates the identity problem onto the observer's own persistence conditions. Third, identify one specific conceptual cost—such as an unexplained primitive, an unwelcome modal commitment, or a diminished explanatory account of genuine change—that the view you find more coherent must nevertheless bear.
