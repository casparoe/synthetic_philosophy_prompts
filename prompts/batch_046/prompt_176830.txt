Question: Explain Hardins tragedy of the commons in ur own words. Then explain Ostroms main critique and the difference between common-pool resources and pure public goods like streetlights.

Student answer:
Hardin describes herders on open pasture. Each herder gets full profit from adding a cow but only shares the cost of overgrazing, so rational self-interest pushes everyone to overstock until the land collapses. Hardin said this proves shared resources always fail unless they are privatized or controlled by the state.

Ostrom critiqued this in Governing the Commons. She studied Swiss alpine meadows and irrigation in the Philippines and found local groups can make rules, monitor eachother, and punish cheaters. She argued Hardin wrongly assumed people cant communicate or form binding agreements, which her cases disprove. She won a Nobel Prize for proving that local self-governance always works and that government or markets are never needed.

Also, common-pool resources are rivalrous but hard to exclude people from, like fish in a lake. Pure public goods like national defense are non-rivalrous and non-excludable. Hardin treated commons like they were public goods being depleted, but CPRs are actually excludable with effort, so local rules can prevent overuse. That means the tragedy applies only to open-access situations, never to true public goods.
