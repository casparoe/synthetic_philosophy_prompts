In other words, does a second-order version of the paradox arise—one in which not just my mental state but my explicit formulation of the rule collapses into interpretation—such that any attempt to ground meaning in a stated rule begs the question in a structurally distinct way?

Please write the proposal with the following parts. Second, explain why the standard responses do not automatically dissolve this version: show conceptually why dispositional accounts, communal-check accounts, and primitivist accounts might still face a regress or circularity specifically at the level of rule-articulation rather than rule-application. Fourth, specify what a successful result would look like—perhaps demonstrating that the second-order collapse is genuinely distinct and requires a separate solution, or showing that it reduces to the first-order problem and therefore does not advance the debate—and state clearly what would count as failure, such as discovering that the framing is merely notational. I will supply all citations and references myself; please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or specific textual quotations. Write in a formal academic register suitable for submission to my instructor, and keep the focus on conceptual structure rather than on biographical or historical details about the original argument’s publication.
