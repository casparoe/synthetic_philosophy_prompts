I am designing a PhD comprehensive exam in metaphysics and need an essay question that avoids the standard statue-and-lump rehearsal by working through a graded-constitution framework I have developed in my recent work. In this framework, constitution is not binary but mediated by functional-modal profiles—the range of modal properties, persistence conditions, and kind-typical capacities that an object can bear across nearby worlds without forfeiting its categorical identity. Present the following case: in 2023 a museum conservator restores a fifth-century BCE bronze statue of Athena by replacing roughly thirty-five percent of the original bronze with atomically identical bronze recovered from the same ancient smelting deposit, then replicates the original surface oxidation through controlled chemical treatment. The museum director contends that the result is not the original statue but a new object that merely inherits its aesthetic-functional role, while the conservator maintains that diachronic continuity is intact because the substrate's geological origin is irrelevant to the statue's functional-modal profile. The exam—forty-five minutes, approximately eight hundred words—should ask students whether the restored object is materially constituted by the original statue, requiring them to address three specific issues: (i) whether four-dimensionalist temporal-parthood can accommodate synthetic-origin parts without breaking spatiotemporal continuity; (ii) whether artistic intention can function as an external fixer of the functional-modal profile that determines constitution; and (iii) whether the bronze substrate now simultaneously instantiates two competing profiles, generating an indeterminacy in constitution rather than a simple failure of identity. Please provide the exam question, a model answer of about seven hundred words that applies the graded-constitution framework correctly, and a marking scheme that distinguishes an A answer—which resolves all three issues within the framework, clearly separates constitution from mereological identity, and rejects binary treatment of constitution—from a B answer—which applies standard binary constitution but misses the graded profile—and from a failing answer—which collapses constitution into part-identity or relies on unexamined intuition. Do not include requests for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
