If semantic externalism is true, then Oscar on Earth and his physically identical twin on Twin Earth share every internal property yet think different thoughts—one about H₂O, the other about XYZ—because their causal environments differ. Now consider Oscar gradually relocated to Twin Earth, who years later sincerely remembers “that clear liquid I drank as a child.” His brain states are now continuous with his twin’s, and he currently interacts only with XYZ. Does the indexical reference in that memory fix its content to H₂O via his original acquisition chain, stranding the thought in an absent chemical world, or does it shift to XYZ, allowing silent revision of past mental content without any internal or phenomenological change? It seems the externalist must either disconnect mental content from present cognitive life or permit undetectable content-shifts. How should this be resolved without giving up the core claim that meaning is not fully “in the head”?
