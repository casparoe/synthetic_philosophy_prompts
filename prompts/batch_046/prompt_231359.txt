I have been trying to understand why certain canonical thought experiments in physics—Galileo's reasoning about bodies falling in a perfect vacuum, Einstein's equivalence principle imagined inside a sealed elevator, or the twin-paradox deduction about differential aging—are routinely presented in textbooks and peer-reviewed conceptual papers as yielding direct knowledge about nature, despite never having been performed under the conditions they specify and despite relying on idealizations that are physically impossible to realize. By contrast, in fields such as epidemiology or behavioral ecology, constructing an elaborate hypothetical scenario about pathogen transmission or evolutionary strategy without supporting observational data is classified as speculative modeling, not as an evidential contribution in its own right. What philosophical commitments about scientific knowledge, idealization, and the epistemic authority of reason does this institutional asymmetry presuppose?

Must the practice depend on the claim that fundamental physical laws are rationally transparent—accessible through conceptual analysis alone—whereas biological and social mechanisms are irreducibly contingent and empirically opaque? Or can the distinction be reconstructed in purely methodological terms, appealing to tractability, mathematical structure, or the role of counterfactual isolation, without committing to any deep divide between the knowability of physics and that of other sciences? Reconstruct the implicit epistemology that licenses treating mental simulation as knowledge-yielding in one domain but merely heuristic in another, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it only contingently accompanies, and indicate where the practice is philosophically underdetermined—compatible with rival views about the relationship between conceivability, possibility, and empirical warrant. Finally, say whether adopting a uniformly probabilistic or empiricist stance would force a revision of how these disciplines treat conceptual reasoning, or whether the asymmetry can survive even if we reject any special rational access to physical law.
