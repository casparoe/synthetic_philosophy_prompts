You know the standard story — ontologists fight over whether numbers exist, Carnap says they're just picking different languages, end of story. But now there's this 'ontological pluralism' view where people claim different domains literally have different existance conditions, not just different vocabularies. I want to see if that actually solves Carnap's problem or just relocates it one level up.

So here's the niche case i cooked up for class: take the social kind 'corporation'. In legal ontology it exists iff it's registered; in moral ontology maybe only if it has interests. The pluralist says both are right relative to their framework. But then the old dispute — do corporations really exist? — becomes a dispute about which framework applies. So aren't we just back to a substantive disagreement, except now it's about logic and framework choice rather than about the corporation itself?

My question: does pluralism genuinely dissolve verbal disputes, or does it push them up into meta-level fights about framework selection? And when the competing frameworks have incompatible logical structures like this, is the choice between them still just pragmatic, or have we smuggled in a real ontological commitment after all?
