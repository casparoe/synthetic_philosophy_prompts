I have recently been asked to design an epistemology module for surgical residents, and I have found myself caught between two interpretations of what Wittgenstein calls hinge commitments—those background certainties, such as that one’s own hands exist or that the earth has long existed, that seem to lie outside ordinary justification. To clarify the stakes for the curriculum, I need an adversarial-collaboration document drafted by two opposing camps: Camp One, the hinge cognitivists, who maintain that these commitments are a distinctive, practice-embedded form of epistemic entitlement; and Camp Two, the hinge pragmatists, who maintain that they are not beliefs or knowledge-claims at all but non-epistemic framing conditions that make reasoning possible. Please produce the joint statement they could both sign. It should include: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims—about the structure of On Certainty, about the failure of both skeptical doubt and empirical verification to apply to hinges, and about the shared rejection of treating hinges as either subjective whims or empirically falsifiable hypotheses—together with an explicit acknowledgment of the analogy I have been using, in which hinges operate like boundary conditions in a physical model rather than like variables within it; (2) a disagreements table stating each camp’s precise position in wording it would accept, specifically on whether saying that one knows one has hands expresses a cognitive state or functions as a grammatical rule, and on whether the form of life supplies normative justification or only causal explanation; (3) the cruxes—particularly whether a hinge can be rationally criticized without performative contradiction, and whether the loss of a hinge entails epistemic failure or merely practical disorientation; and (4) for each crux, a concrete clinical case both sides agree is relevant, for instance a surgeon experiencing transient depersonalization who briefly doubts bodily continuity mid-operation, with each camp’s predicted assessment of the surgeon’s epistemic status recorded in advance. The document should avoid bibliographic demands, should not ask for citations, and should be written so that neither camp can dismiss the other’s formulation as a straw man.
