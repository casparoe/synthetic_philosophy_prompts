I hold a specific variant of illusionism about phenomenal consciousness that I will call predictive-error illusionism. On this view, there are no primitive qualitative properties instantiated in experience; instead, first-order perceptual states track environmental features through predictive-coding mechanisms, and second-order meta-cognitive monitors register the resolution or suppression of prediction-error signals. The illusion consists in framing these confidence-weighted informational updates as if they carried an intrinsic, non-functional phenomenal character—so that reporting "a reddish qualitative feel" is actually reporting the brain's own successful error-minimization in the red-spectrum range, misinterpreted by the meta-cognitive system as the presence of a sui generis quale. The appearance of phenomenology is therefore an epistemic artifact of monitoring, not an additional feature of consciousness.

What would have to be true for this position to be wrong? I want an honest map of its exposure, not a rehearsal of generic anti-illusionist objections. Specifically: which results in cognitive neuroscience or clinical psychology—concerning, for instance, blindsight with preserved affect, masked priming without meta-cognitive access, or dissociations between predictive-error magnitude and reported confidence—would genuinely undermine predictive-error illusionism rather than being absorbed by redefining the scope of "meta-cognitive framing"? Which philosophical arguments—drawn from the transparency of experience, the knowledge argument, or the structure of phenomenal concepts—would be decisive if they succeeded, and which ones does this version already anticipate and neutralize by locating the illusion at the monitoring level rather than in first-order representation? Furthermore, does the view risk unfalsifiability: if every report of qualitative character can be redescribed as a misinterpreted predictive-confidence signal, has it insulated itself too thoroughly? If so, is that insulation a theoretical virtue or a fatal defect for a theory that aims to explain why phenomenal consciousness seems undeniable? Please distinguish between defeaters that would damage illusionism generally and those that specifically threaten predictive-error illusionism, and rank them by how decisive they should be for a committed defender—identifying, in particular, the objections that such a defender should concede as genuinely damaging without abandoning the core view.
