Write a deadpan parody of a corporate End-User License Agreement (EULA) titled “Anthropocene Epoch v1.0 — Terms of Planetary Use,” issued by a fictional International Commission on Stratigraphy. Adopt the voice of dry software-license legalese—sections such as Acceptance of Epoch Terms, Grant of Geological License, System Requirements (stratigraphic markers), Data Collection and Monitoring (the “golden spike” authentication server), Patch History (competing release builds: the 1950 Great Acceleration / nuclear-fallout release, the 1610 Orbis-Columbian-Exchange patch, and earlier industrial beta versions), Termination and Epoch Rollback Policy, and Limitation of Liability. Treat geological deep time as a versioned product deployed by an unelected technical committee. The satire should target, without ever naming them explicitly, the bureaucratic claim that a stratigraphic vote can unilaterally declare a universal historical boundary; the demographic asymmetry of whose activities are coded as “system-wide” changes; and the arbitrariness of selecting one “golden spike” (GSSP) boundary over others. Include at least one invented niche mechanism—e.g., a “Golden Spike Licensing Server” that authenticates boundary validity, or a user-feedback clause allowing future committees to roll back to the Holocene if adoption metrics are poor. Keep the irony sustained and legible: a reader should recognize that the form mocks the practice of treating planetary history like a software update with terms of service, but the text itself should never break character to explain the joke. Aim for roughly 500–600 words. I come from a software-engineering background with no formal philosophy training, so I want the conceptual criticism to emerge entirely from the license form and its absurd application to geological time, not from explicit philosophical terminology.
