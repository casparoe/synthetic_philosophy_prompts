I have observed a pattern in mature scientific disciplines that I cannot place within the usual philosophy-of-science taxonomy. Consider two illustrative cases. In one, physicists continue to write grant proposals and review articles using the canonical vocabulary of elementary particles and continuous fields, yet in their day-to-day modeling they treat these entities as emergent approximations of an underlying information-theoretic structure, applying evaluation criteria—such as entanglement entropy rather than scattering amplitudes—that the canonical framework explicitly does not recognize. In another, biologists maintain the standard adaptationist lexicon—fitness landscapes, optimal phenotypes, gene-for-trait mappings—in publications and textbooks, while in their actual experimental design they rely on developmental plasticity and niche construction, measuring success by ecological resilience metrics that have no direct translation into the adaptationist framework.

What is striking is not merely theoretical pluralism. The practitioners are fluent in both registers, switch between them without explicit comment, and never declare that the old vocabulary has been superseded or that the two are incommensurable. The traditional language is preserved institutionally—used for peer review, funding, and pedagogy—while its conceptual content has been quietly evacuated from practical work. There is no acknowledged crisis, no rival school openly defending the new ontology, and no attempt to demonstrate that one framework reduces to the other.

I am familiar with Kuhn's account of paradigm shifts and with the problem of incommensurability, and I know Lakatos's distinction between progressive and degenerating research programmes. But this seems structurally different: the old framework is not abandoned, falsified, or shown to be degenerate; it is maintained as a kind of institutional shell while an incompatible ontology operates inside it. Does this configuration have an established name? If not, how should it be distinguished precisely from Kuhnian incommensurability, from Lakatosian degeneration, and from ordinary theoretical pluralism? And what exactly marks its boundaries—must there always be an institutional divide between the vocabulary used for funding and the vocabulary used for experiment, or can the bifurcation occur within a single practitioner's reasoning without any social separation?
