As a journalist covering the philosophy of science and ideas for a British quarterly, I have been investigating how Wittgensteinian hinge epistemology—those propositions discussed in On Certainty that stand fast and form the scaffolding of inquiry—might survive translation into technologically mediated contexts. In the course of my research I spoke with colleagues who insisted the framework collapses outside ordinary biological life, and I now find myself attempting to map the implicit logic of a particular philosopher of science, Dr. Elena Voss, whose public lectures over the past year have delivered a consistent but unexplained pattern of verdicts. She holds that there are enduring physical objects functions as a hinge; that I have never visited the moon operates as a personal hinge contingent upon biography; that elementary arithmetic truths such as two and two make four are hinges within mathematical practice but lose that status if the practice itself is abandoned; that empirical generalisations like all metals expand when heated are never hinges; that my sensory apparatus is broadly trustworthy in ordinary conditions is a hinge; and that global sceptical hypotheses—such as the world having been created five minutes ago—are not hinges but parasitic fantasies that borrow their sense from the very framework they deny.

I am preparing my feature and need to anticipate her position on a case that has not yet reached her desk: a subject who has lived for eighteen months in a fully immersive virtual environment, whose physical body remains in stasis, and for whom my body is currently moving through space has become a fixed point of practical reasoning despite being physically false. Predict Dr. Voss's verdict on whether this proposition qualifies as a hinge. Identify at least two competing interpretive rules—perhaps one tying hinge status to the form of life or language-game in which the belief operates, and another tying it to biological or physical reality-conditions—that are both consistent with her earlier rulings yet diverge on this virtual-reality case. State which rule you think she implicitly favours, with reasons drawn from her track record, and describe the single further hypothetical case that would definitively distinguish the two rules.
