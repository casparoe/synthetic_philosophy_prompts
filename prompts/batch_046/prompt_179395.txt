For a philosophy-of-computation seminar, write a decision tree that classifies interpretations of Von Neumann’s comparison between neural and digital architecture. Start with the shared premise that both brain and computer perform logical operations. First branch: does the brain’s analog, noisy, parallel structure undermine the analogy or refine it? Second branch (for the “refine” path): is logical reliability in neural tissue achieved through redundancy analogous to error-correcting codes, or through logical primitives that are fundamentally non-arithmetic? Third branch (cross-cutting): is the capacity for self-replication and growth an essential feature of the brain’s logic, or merely a biological contingency? Each leaf should carry a label—standard if one exists (e.g., strong computationalist, weak analogist, logical pluralist), otherwise “unnamed”—plus one sentence stating its most awkward commitment (for instance, being forced to deny that digital systems could ever replicate cognition, or being forced to treat all mental computation as arithmetic despite chemical noise). Note any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate. Do not include bibliographies, citations, or requests for further reading; the instrument should work for someone reasoning from the concepts alone.
