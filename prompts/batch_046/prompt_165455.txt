I work for a regional biosafety board and we keep recieving proposals that affect enormous insect populations -- new pesticides, genetic suppression drives, lighting overhauls that crash moth counts. There's this scale argument that since insects outnumber us by orders of magnitude, even a tiny probablity they're sentient means total welfare impacts could swamp human ones. I want a decision procedure my reviewers can run in about 30 mins per proposal, with questions in order, what counts as passing each gate, where it stops with a hard no, and where it just hands the call to a person because its values not facts. The procedure needs to deal with the 'shadow sentience' problem I just made up: cases where a proposal kills few insects but might change the phenomenal state of survivors in some unknown direction, so we're trading dead ones for possibly suffering ones. Apply it to two cases: a fungal spray that kills ~10^11 beetles to prevent crop failure, and a mosquito gene drive that suppresses reproduction over 20 years but leaves survivors with altered neural pathways. Make sure one of them produces an answer I probably wont like. Also flag where the procedure misfires or relies on a guess.
