Write a villanelle of nineteen lines with two refrains on the Euthyphro dilemma. One refrain should voice Socrates's recognition horn—that the pious is loved by the gods because it is pious—while the other should voice the command horn—that it is pious because it is loved. Let the form do the philosophical work: have the refrains take turns through the tercets so that each explanation keeps returning only to be displaced, and let them stand together in the final quatrain, unresolved, to show that the two directions of explanation pass each other without intersection. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader who knows Plato's dialogue can identify which line is which and can see that the circularity is built into the structure rather than solved by it. In the middle stanzas, extend the problem beyond theology: ask whether merit in a secular institution is recognized because it is genuinely deserving or is made deserving by the very act of institutional recognition, so the dilemma becomes a question about the direction of normative explanation itself. Present the poem as continuous verse, without annotations, headings, or explanatory notes.
