You serve as a senior advisor to the University Committee on Student Wellness and Curriculum Integration, with expertise in ancient philosophy, applied ethics, and institutional governance. Our committee must render a binding decision on whether to approve, reject, or conditionally approve a pilot “Stoic Resilience Module” proposed for first-year orientation. At our last session, the proposal’s architects presented a curriculum grounded in the modern revival of Stoic practice, organizing exercises around a taxonomy of control that divides student experience into “direct control” (judgments and intentions), “indirect influence” (outcomes partly shaped by effort but not fully determined by it), and “complete externality” (institutional structures, other people’s actions, and systemic conditions). Several members defended this three-tier framework as a legitimate philosophical development of the classical Stoic distinction between what is in our power and what is not; others argued it is a dangerous revision that lets students classify systemic campus issues—housing policy, mental-health funding, administrative transparency—as “completely external,” thereby dissolving collective responsibility and misrepresenting the classical binary. We need a formal memo that recommends a specific, actionable decision to the full committee, treats the strongest philosophical objection with intellectual honesty, and supplies criteria reviewers could actually apply, all without relying on outside sources.

Please begin by briefly outlining the classical Stoic dichotomy of control as it appears in the tradition associated with Epictetus, then analyze whether the proposed three-tier taxonomy is defensible as an extension or problematic as a distortion. In drafting the memo, assume the committee has no web access and must evaluate the reasoning on philosophical grounds alone; do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations to specific modern authors whose work might be obscure to a general model. Write in a formal register appropriate for an academic governance document, with a clear bottom-line recommendation rather than personal guidance.

Structure your response in the following sections, with approximate lengths indicated:
1. Executive Summary (about 150 words): state the recommendation in one sentence, then summarize the reasoning.
2. Philosophical Background (about 250 words): explain the classical dichotomy and the modern revival’s use of it, then present the three-tier taxonomy exactly as proposed.
3. Case for Conditional Approval (about 250 words): argue why the module could be valuable if revised, using the taxonomy in a philosophically sound way that avoids individualizing structural problems.
4. The Strongest Counter-Argument (about 200 words): present fairly the objection that the taxonomy misreads classical Stoicism and undermines collective responsibility by misclassifying institutional conditions.
5. Recommendation and Binding Conditions (about 200 words): state the precise decision and list the conditions or evaluative criteria the program must meet to be adopted.
6. Brief Implementation Note (about 100 words): one practical check the committee can apply in future reviews of wellness curricula.

Think step by step, and treat both sides of the debate as equally competent.
