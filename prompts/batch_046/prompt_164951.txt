Construct a thought experiment that distinguishes counterfactual mugging from XOR blackmail under a structural twist: the agent faces two sequential, causally independent decision nodes—say, approving a contract clause on Monday and ratifying it on Wednesday—while an adversary arranges payoffs so that punishment is triggered in exactly one counterfactual world: either refusal-then-acceptance or acceptance-then-refusal. This creates an XOR dependency across the pair rather than over a single choice. Crucially, the adversary does not observe outcomes directly but receives only a noisy report from an intermediary auditor with a known, nonzero error rate. Constraints: the setting must be realistic (corporate or legal, not science fiction), the scenario itself described in one paragraph, with no perfect predictors, no quantum branching devices, and stakes that a normal professional could face. After presenting the case, show the verdict each of causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and updateless decision theory delivers on whether to approve both, reject both, or split the decisions; identify whether the two-node XOR structure or the noisy observation is doing the work in breaking the usual equivalence between mugging and blackmail; and explain what collapses if you remove the auditor and restore direct observation. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or external references; keep all reasoning self-contained and accessible to a reader without web access.
