I am commissioning a structured debate document for a feature on whether philosophy performs indispensable labor for working scientists, or whether it is, as some prominent physicists have suggested, an obsolete ornament. Frame this as an adversarial collaboration between Camp A—those who share the skepticism associated with Weinberg, Hawking, and Feynman, treating philosophy as either parasitic on completed science or conceptually irrelevant to its progress—and Camp B—philosophers of science who maintain that philosophy executes tasks scientists cannot outsource.

Please draft the joint statement both camps could sign. Use this exact architecture:

First, a numbered section of agreed claims: what science aims at; whether historical episodes (for example, conceptual work on space-time structure, quantum interpretation, or the measurement problem) actually occurred; and the shared criteria by which a philosophical contribution would count as genuinely useful rather than merely decorative.

Second, a disagreements table in neutral, formal wording that each side would accept for its own position. Organize the table around four categories of philosophical labor—diagnostic (clearing conceptual confusions internal to a theory), heuristic (proposing formal structures or analogies that guide model-building), normative (fixing standards of explanation, evidence, and ontological commitment), and constitutive (defining the ontology that a scientific framework presupposes). For each category, state Camp A’s view, Camp B’s view, and the precise point at which they diverge.

Third, identify three cruxes: whether philosophical analysis can yield predictions that empirical tests could in principle falsify; whether conceptual clarification has ever altered actual scientific practice rather than merely annotating it; and whether ontological interpretation is part of scientific work or a post-scientific luxury.

Fourth, for each crux, present one concrete historical or hypothetical case that both sides agree is relevant—such as the role of symmetry arguments in particle-physics model building, or the interpretation of quantum mechanics—and record in advance what each camp predicts about whether philosophical engagement changed, or could change, the scientific outcome in that case.

Write in a journalist’s formal register: precise, without bibliographic requests, reading lists, or citation demands, and without relying on obscure theoretical labels that a well-read but non-specialist model would not have encountered. Do not place any real author’s words inside quotation marks unless you have fetched the source; paraphrase freely. The prompt should force the model to apply an unfamiliar four-part taxonomy to a familiar dispute, so the answer cannot be a standard pretraining summary.
