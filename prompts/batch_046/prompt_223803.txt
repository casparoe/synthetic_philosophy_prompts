heres an argumant i built for a seminar on internal vs external reasons and now it feels like it proves way too much. (1) if a reason genuinely applies to an agent, then recognizing that reason can in principle motivate them — otherwise its not a reason *for them*, just background noise. (2) internal reasons do this by linking up with the agents actual motivational set; external reasons only work if theres already some internal hook they can latch onto. (3) so take a total amoralist with zero internal concern for others. moral reasons cant apply to them, because theres nothing inside for those reasons to connect to. (4) but then saying "you shouldnt torture people" to this person is literally false, not just unheeded — like telling a rock it oughtnt roll downhill. (5) still, we want to say the amoralist is defective, missing real reasons, not that morality is silent about them. (6) so (1) has to go? but if i ditch (1) then reasons float free of agents entirely, and "you have reason to do X" becomes a ghost-statment with no grip. where is the slip? i want to keep (1) more than (5), but im worried im paying a huge price. is the mistake in (2) — maybe recognition of an external reason can itself create the internal hook?
