The thesis I am advancing is a two-level framework that deliberately separates representational closure from doxastic closure. At the representational level—formal knowledge bases, semantic models, AI reasoning architectures—I maintain that logical omniscience is a design feature, not a bug: if a system encodes P and the entailment to Q is present in its logical framework, then Q is in the knowledge base by construction. At the attributive level—when we say that an agent knows or believes Q—I impose a contextual accessibility constraint: closure holds only for logical connections that are actively represented in the agent's working cognitive architecture, not merely available in principle. This is not the standard awareness solution, because awareness sets are global and static, whereas my accessibility conditions are local, task-dependent, and indexed to working-memory load.
