Write the adversarial-collaboration document that a committed epistemicist about vagueness and a committed supervaluationist could jointly publish after a sustained, good-faith exchange. Begin with a numbered section of agreed claims—covering, at minimum, the logical structure of the sorites paradox, the inadequacy of classical bivalence at the borders of vague predicates, the role of the tolerance principle in generating the paradox, and the shared rejection of naive contextualist fixes—stated in wording both parties would sign without reservation. Then present the residual disagreements in a table: for each side, formulate its core thesis about whether vague expressions have precise, unknown extensions or instead lack any single precise extension altogether, using language that the respective camp would accept as accurate. Next, isolate the cruxes; I take the central ones to be whether pervasive ignorance of sharp boundaries is epistemically plausible, and whether higher-order vagueness collapses epistemicism into incoherence. For each crux, identify a specific test case or theoretical result—such as the behavior of continuous physical magnitudes near an alleged cutoff, or the logical commitments required to block a revenge sorites—that both sides agree is relevant, and state in advance what each camp predicts that case or result will show. If part of the disagreement turns out to be verbal, or if one formulation makes one view indistinguishable from the other, specify exactly where and why. Keep the tone neutral, avoid appeals to authority, and do not include bibliographic references or reading lists.
