There's this weird split in how instutions talk about brains. In medicine we call strokes and lesions 'structural' damage like it's hardware breaking, but we call schizophrenia or severe depression 'funcitonal' disorders, as if the software is misconfigured but the hardware's fine. Meanwhile in AI people hype brain-inspired chips as escaping the von Neumann bottleneck by copying brain architechture, which kinda assumes brains aren't von Neumann machines. But von Neumann himself, in his late comparison of brains and computers, seemed skeptical that brains work like digital machines at all -- he thought they used analog pulse-rate coding rather than discrete logic. So what's the implicit philosophy here? Does the medical hardware/software split presuppose exactly the kind of digital computer model von Neumann doubted? Or can you have that split without being a mechanist about cognition? Reconstruct the theory of mind and computation that makes both practices coherent, say whether they're actually consistent with each other, and tell me what a clinical or AI policy built on von Neumann's actual view of the brain would have to change.
