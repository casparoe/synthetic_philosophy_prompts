i've been editing this tiny journal for about six months now and it's humbling — i keep greenlighting essays that sound airtight to me but then readers tear them apart for reasons i should have seen. i think my problem is i'm stuck in the inside view: i read the argument on its own terms and get convinced by its internal coherence, but i never step back and ask how often do claims like this actually hold up?

i want to turn the inside/outside view distinction plus epistemic modesty into an actual decision procedure i can run on every submission. not just think about base rates — i need ordered steps, with the exact question to ask at each stage, what counts as a passing answer, and where the procedure kills the essay vs hands it back to me as a values call. the twist is i want to build in a modesty brake: a specific point where i have to write down my confidence level in my own judgment about the reference class before i can proceed, so i can't fake the outside view by picking a convenient comparison group.

fix the role: i'm the sole editor, about 15 submisions a week, 20 minutes each, no peer review panel. stakes are medium — online journal for grad students and lay readers, credibility matters but nobody dies. constraints: i sign off personally and can't outsource the call.

the procedure needs to survive three hard cases: (1) an empirical claim with no obvious refrence class, like an essay arguing CBT is overused in prisons based on the author's clinical experience; (2) a normative claim dressed up as empirical, like an essay saying privacy norms in small towns are collapsing because of one local study; (3) a niche philosophical claim where the author knows way more than me, like an essay arguing we should abandon the concept of belief altogether because neuroscience doesn't support it.

then run the full procedure on those three exact cases. include one where the procedure tells me to publish something i actually think is wrong. and be honest about what the procedure still can't do — especially whether that modesty brake just moves the reference-class problem around instead of solving it. keep it practical, not a philosophy essay about philosophy essays.
