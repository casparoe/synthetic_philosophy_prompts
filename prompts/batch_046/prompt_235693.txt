Most university endowments spend around 5% yearly, which effectively treats future students as worth a bit less each year, but those same instutions fund climate research on century-long timelines with almost no discounting. What view of time-preference and intergenerational justice does that split presuppose? Is there one moral logic for capital growth and another for harm prevention, or can a unified account cover both? And does the practice quietly assume future beneficiaries of the endowment are "us" in some way that distant climate victims aren't, or is the gap just instutional inertia? Reconstruct the implicit theory, say whether a pure time-preference view can explain the asymmetry without cheating, and flag where the practice is philosophically underdetermined—where an egalitarian intergenerationalist and a moderate discounter might both sign off on it.
