Write a dialogue between two equally competent philosophers—Dr. Alia Voss and Dr. Ren Park—debating material constitution in a deliberately non-standard case: a public monument called The Regenerative Form, made of self-healing ceramic clay that continuously exchanges particles with an external reservoir through an automated maintenance protocol. After ten years no original atoms remain, but the shape, the reservoir link, and the protocol are unchanged. Voss holds a coincidence view: the statue and the clay mass are numerically distinct entities that share space, and the statue survives because its form and its functional-maintenance relation persist despite total material turnover. Park holds a process-ontological identity view: there is only one entity, a spatiotemporally extended process encompassing both the shaped material and the reservoir-mechanism, so treating the statue as a static object that “survives” replacement is a category mistake.

Require real back-and-forth: each must respond to the other’s actual points, not talk past them. They must specifically address: (1) whether the reservoir, pumps, and protocol are now parts of the statue, parts of the clay, or merely external conditions; (2) whether form-persistence without material continuity suffices for artifact identity, given that natural entities like rivers tolerate part-replacement but artifacts usually do not; and (3) the interruption test—if the system is shut off for a year, the form collapses, then the system restarts with fresh clay and the original protocol, has the same statue returned, or was there an interval of non-existence?

Open with one sentence stating the exact point of disagreement. Then present the dialogue. Keep the response under 600 words. End with each philosopher stating, in one sentence, what the other would need to concede for them to change their view. Do not include citations, references, or a bibliography.
