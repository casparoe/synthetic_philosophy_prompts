The Governance Council of the Meridian Foundation Models Consortium must decide whether to mandate a safety-overhead disclosure protocol requiring each member laboratory to report the precise capability degradation—expressed as a standardized performance gap—incurred by its alignment procedures before any model release. Advocates contend that concealing the alignment tax lets free-riders avoid its cost, accelerating a race-to-the-bottom; opponents contend that publishing granular overhead data allows non-member competitors to calibrate minimal-compliance strategies, intensifying the competitiveness constraint rather than easing it. Draft a formal policy memo recommending whether to adopt the protocol, with evaluation criteria the Council can apply consistently. Your memo should explain how transparency interacts with competitive dynamics, fairly present the strongest argument from the rejected position, and conclude with an actionable recommendation—without requesting bibliographies, citation lists, or external literature reviews, since the Council needs reasoning it can assess on its own terms.
