I was drafting some notes for a grad seminar on forecasting and historical analogy, and I sketched out an argument that now feels like it's eating its own tail. Here it is, step by step. (1) If you want to predict the lifespan or stability of any particular institution—say a modern democratic state—you should start with the outside view: place it in the broadest refernece class that still shares the relevant structural features. (2) The broadest such class for a modern state is probably "large-scale human political organizations with bureaucratic administration," and in that class the base rate for eventual collapse, radical transformation, or loss of territorial integrity over a 500-year window is essentially 100%. (3) So the rational forecast is that this state, too, will eventually collapse or transform beyond recognition. (4) Now apply the same reasoning to the forecasting method itself. The reference class for "predictive frameworks that rely on historical base rates and reference classes" includes everything from Babylonian omen texts to 19th-century phrenological forecasting—every one of which was eventually abandoned or falsified. (5) So by the outside view, our current forecasting framework is almost certainly wrong or obsolete in the long run. (6) But if the method is unreliable, then premise (3)—the collapse forecast—lacks justification, since it was derived by that very method. (7) Therefore either we reject the outside-view method and lose our reason for predicting collapse, or we keep it and must predict that our own predictions are wrong—including the prediction of collapse. Either way it seems to prove too much: it either undermines itself or forces us into a kind of epistemic paralysis where no institutional forecast is stable.

I'm trying to figure out where the slip is. Is there a hidden scope shift between applying the outside view to empires versus applying it to epistemic methods? Or is one of the premises—maybe (2) or (4)—just wrong about which reference class is appropriate? I keep feeling like there's an equivocation between "collapse of the object being predicted" and "failure of the predictive framework," but I can't quite pin it down. Which step should I reject, and what's the cost? If I want to keep the idea that reference-class forecasting is generally valid for empires, do I have to pay a steep price—like admitting that all long-range institutional forecasting is self-undermining? Or is there a cleaner fix I'm missing?
