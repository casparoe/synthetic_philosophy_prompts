I'm a volunteer at this community center where every tuesday I sit with people for about twenty minutes and help them decide whether to take on some big life thing — adopting a pet, taking a new job, cutting off family. There's usually three people waiting, and whatever I recomend has to be signed off by the center director, so I need something I can actually run in the room, not just think about in my head.

I've been chewing on this idea I think I heard on a podcast — that the only things which actually matter are the absence of craving and the absence of suffering. Not happiness, not success, not "being true to yourself," just less craving and less suffering. I want to turn that into a step-by-step procedure I can apply with anyone sitting across from me, written in plain language with no philosophy jargon. It should be a checklist or flowchart in words: tests I apply in order, with the hard cases flagged where my own judgment has to step in because the idea itself doesn't settle it. I need it applied to three cases — a woman with anxiety who wants to adopt a high-energy dog she saw online, a guy offered a promotion that doubles pay but means seventy-hour weeks and he already has back pain, and a guy who wants to stop talking to his mom who keeps asking him for money. That last one should come out saying cut contact, even though I personally feel family should stay together no matter what, so I want to see the procedure push me to an answer I don't like. Also say what the procedure just can't do — like when both choices reduce suffering in totally different ways, or when someone claims they never feel cravings at all. And please don't ask me to read books or find citations; I don't have a library and neither do the people I'm working with.
