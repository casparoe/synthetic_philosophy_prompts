I'm preparing a workshop on comparative epistemologies and want a short story that dramatizes Jain anekāntavāda not as religious doctrine but as a lived method for holding contradiction without collapsing it. Write a short story about a mediator named Kira who is tasked with reconciling testimony from seven survivors of a collapsed orbital habitat. Each survivor describes the same structural failure with statements that are mutually incompatible: the breach was sudden and violent; it was gradual and silent; it was caused by human error; it was caused by material fatigue; it was avoidable; it was inevitable; it was a singular event; it was part of a longer pattern. Kira does not seek a unified account. Instead, she applies the Jain framework of syādvāda and saptabhaṅgī—the sevenfold conditional predication—to map how each statement is true from a particular standpoint (syāt) and false or inexpressible from others. The crisis comes when the governing council demands a single causal narrative to assign liability. Kira must defend the epistemic legitimacy of a verdict that assigns partial responsibility to all parties and to none, using only the logic of many-sidedness.

Set the story in a near-future where arbitration is governed by algorithmic consensus that weights testimony by coherence and penalizes contradiction. Show how Kira's refusal to synthesize the seven accounts into one timeline is read first as professional failure, then—perhaps—as the only way to prevent a second collapse driven by false certainty. Keep the philosophical mechanics concrete: embed the seven modes in specific dialogue, not as abstract labels, so that the reader sees exactly how "the breach was sudden" can be affirmed conditionally while being denied unconditionally, and how a judgment can be simultaneously determinate and open-ended. The story should end not with reconciliation in the usual sense, but with the council reluctantly accepting a multi-conditional verdict that no algorithm could have generated—one that preserves every survivor's truth without forcing them into a single story.
