hey, i'm a phd student in philosophy of language and metaphysics, and i keep running into this problem in seminars where everyone argues about whether numbers exist or whether properties are universals, and half the room thinks it's a deep ontological disagreement while the other half thinks we're just using words differently. i was discussing with my friends over drinks last week and i tried to defend a kind of hybrid position: that the chalmers-style verbal dispute diagnosis is basically right for most of these cases — numbers, properties, possible worlds, the usual suspects — but there's a smaller class of debates that aren't verbal at all, which i've been calling anchor disputes (my advisr thinks the name is terrible). in an anchor dispute, the vocabulary and the basic ontological commitments are shared, but the disagreement is over which concrete particular or structural feature serves as the referenece-fixing base for an abstract domain. so for instance, whether spacetime points or matter fields anchor geometric relations, or whether individual organisms or evolutionary lineages anchor biological taxonomy. the language isn't the problem; the problem is which thing gets to play the anchoring role.

so i want to take this seriously and ask: what would count against that split? i hold that metaphysics divides into verbal disputes plus these rarer anchor disputes, and that recognizing this saves us from thinking every ontological fight is substantive when it usually isn't. but i need the defeaters — not just what critics of verbal-dispute theories say in general, but specifically what would undermine my hybrid version. are there debates that look like anchor disputes but actually dissolve into linguistic convention? has anyone already shown that anchoring is itself a verbal matter? and has my view been qualified into unfalsifiability — like, can i always retreat and say oh that one was verbal or no, that one's an anchor dispute whenever a counterexample shows up? rank the problems from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me honestly whether defenders of this kind of taxonomy would concede any of these points, or whether they'd just dig in. i don't want a bibliography or anything, just the actual arguments and cases.
