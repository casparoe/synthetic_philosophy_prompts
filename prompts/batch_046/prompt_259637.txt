I've been convinced that Plato's take on drinking is basically a civic diagnostics theory: he allows wine in the Laws not for fun but as a kind of social stress-test that reveals hidden character flaws, especialy in older citizens who should already be stable, while banning it for the young because they haven't built the rational scaffold yet. The Symposium's drunken Alcibiades then becomes not an exception but a case study—Socrates stays sober because his reason is already fully integrated, Alcibiades collapses into erotic chaos. What would actually count against this reading? Also, has this view been qualified so much that it's unfalsifiable—like saying any counterexample is just 'bad administration of the test'? If so, is that a problem for an intepretive claim of this kind?
