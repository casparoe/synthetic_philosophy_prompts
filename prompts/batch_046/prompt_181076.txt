I'm constructing a diagnostic taxonomy for a rationalist-style post on how to respond when first-order reasoning supports P but higher-order assessment says that reasoning is unreliable—say, you've just discovered you're in a peer disagreement with equally credentialed experts, or you've received direct evidence that your cognitive processes in this specific domain are miscalibrated. I want a decision tree, not a literature map, that sorts people by what they actually think about the case.

Start from this concrete commitment: you have formed a belief about a technical, non-obvious question using reasoning you now judge to be corrupted. The tree should have four branch nodes, each phrased as a question someone can answer about their own intuitions without knowing the labels in advance:

1. Does the higher-order evidence defeat the first-order belief entirely, or only weaken its strength?
2. Can you rationally continue holding P while simultaneously judging that your grounds for P are flawed—i.e., is some form of "epistemic akrasia" permissible?
3. Does the answer depend on whether the unreliability is domain-specific (e.g., only climate modeling) versus domain-general (e.g., general reasoning failure)?
4. If suspension is required, is it suspension about P specifically, or about your entire cluster of related beliefs?

The leaves should include: full conciliation/revision to a peer mean or prior; steadfast maintenance with revised meta-cognition but unchanged first-order belief; calibrated partial belief; rational epistemic akrasia (maintaining P with explicit acknowledgment of bad grounds); and at least one unoccupied combination—specifically, the view that domain-specific unreliability never defeats belief but domain-general unreliability always does. For that empty branch, diagnose whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

For each named leaf, include one sentence describing its most awkward commitment—especially for the akrasia leaf, where you should state precisely what makes holding P alongside "my grounds are bad" unstable rather than merely eccentric. Flag any pair of leaves the tree genuinely cannot separate (for instance, if two views agree on all four nodes but diverge only on explanatory story). Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation prompts; do not ask the model to verify titles or dates. Keep the nodes concrete enough that a reader can place themselves without recognizing academic labels.
