Write a dialogue between two interpreters of Whitehead’s philosophy of education. One defends the view that the pedagogical rhythm—romance, precision, and generalization—structurally mirrors the metaphysical process by which an actual occasion integrates feeling into a unified satisfaction, arguing that education and cosmology share one continuous pattern. The other rejects this, insisting that pedagogy is a normative social practice and that importing metaphysical descriptions of how entities become does not license any claim about how students should learn, and that Whitehead himself kept speculative philosophy separate from practical affairs. Both should be equally competent: have them engage directly with whether the mapping explains anything pedagogically useful or merely dresses common sense in ontological language, and whether the analogy breaks at the point where education involves conscious institutional design rather than spontaneous organic growth. Keep the dialogue under 500 words, with real back-and-forth and no parallel monologues.
