As a novelist constructing a narrative set among the Edinburgh literati of the mid-eighteenth century, I am trying to capture without anachronism the precise texture of economic reasoning that informed Hume's circle. To that end, write an essay examining a specific conceptual tension in Hume's monetary thought. The essay should address whether Hume's claim in his economic essays—that a gradual augmentation of the money stock invigorates industry, whereas a sudden inundation of specie leaves real labor and commodities essentially unchanged—can be reconciled with the radical skepticism about causal inference articulated in his broader philosophical work. Weigh the interpretation that Hume is offering a genuinely empirical, psychological account of entrepreneurial anticipation against the interpretation that he has unwittingly committed himself to a proto-monetarist causal law linking money supply directly to output. Reach a definite conclusion about which reading better respects the epistemological constraints Hume elsewhere imposes on reasoning about economic phenomena, and justify that verdict through careful conceptual analysis rather than through appeals to historical citation or bibliographic authority.
