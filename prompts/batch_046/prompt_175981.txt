me and two study buddies got into this after one of them mentioned Bradley's regress in some old metaphysics footnote and wouldnt let it go. we were arguing about whether relations are real entities or not, but i think we were actually circling around propositional unity without naming it. anyway heres the exchange, i wrote it down from memory so there might be a typo or two.

Alex started by saying Bradley's regress is fatal to any view where relations are actual entities: if R connects a and b, then you need a further relation R' to connect R to a, then R'' to connect R' with R and a, and so on forever. You never actually get a unified fact or proposition, just an infinite list of separate things. So relations either have to be internal to thier terms, like Bradley said, or they cant be entities at all.

Jordan replied that this only works if you assume every link needs its own separate connector. In formal logic a predicate applies to arguments without any extra glue relation — the syntax does all the work. So Bradley's regress just assumes an outdated bag of entities ontology where everything is on the same level.

Alex said syntax isnt ontology. You can write Socrates wise in a formula but that doesnt explain why its one thought rather than two separate tokens sitting next to eachother. The syntax is just representation; it doesnt show what in reality makes the proposition actually unified. So Jordan is mixing up how we represent things with how they hang together in the world.

Jordan then brought up Armstrong-style states of affairs: a particular and a universal are tied together inside the state, but the tie isnt a further entity that needs relating. Its more like the shape of an object — you dont need a connector between the shape and the object itself. So the regress is blocked because some togetherness is basic.

Alex pushed back that calling the tie non-relational or part of the state is just labeling. Once you ask how the particular and universal are actually linked inside the state, youre back to needing a linking relation, so the regress starts over. Hiding the connector inside the state doesnt solve it, it just renames it.

Jordan said thats begging the question. The whole point is that not every togetherness is composed of parts that need linking. The state of affairs just is a being F — not a plus F plus some hidden glue. If you keep demanding a connector for every kind of togetherness, youre assuming Bradley's premise that all unity needs an external linker.
