I've been working through Daniel Dennett's idea that the self is not a Cartesian ego but rather a center of narrative gravity—a fictional but useful focal point around which experiences and actions are organized, analogous to the center of gravity of a physical object. Most treatments stop at asking whether this eliminates the self altogether, but I want to push into a tension that seems underexplored in the literature. If personal identity is constituted primarily by the coherence and continuity of an autobiographical story rather than by a continuous soul or stream of consciousness, then consider a conceptually possible case of radical discontinuity: a subject suffers total retrograde amnesia, after which either neuroscientists or the subject herself deliberately constructs an elaborate, internally consistent false autobiography that integrates seamlessly with her present mental life—memories, values, and emotional responses all align with the fabricated past. The resulting narrative is as coherent and behaviorally predictive as any genuine life story, yet it is causally disconnected from the actual history of the organism. If the self is merely a center of narrative gravity, it seems we must identify the post-amnesia subject with the fabricated persona, since the center of gravity has simply relocated into a new but equally valid orbit. Yet this feels metaphysically unsatisfying: there seems to be a meaningful distinction between a self that evolves from its actual past and one that is artfully invented ex nihilo, and if narrative coherence alone cannot capture that distinction, then the model risks collapsing personal identity into a matter of literary consistency rather than genuine continuity. On the other hand, defenders of the pure narrative view might argue that causation is always itself reconstructed within the narrative—there is no outside access to our causal history except through the stories we tell—so demanding a non-narrative causal anchor is a vestige of the Cartesian essentialism Dennett aims to reject. Write an essay on whether narrative coherence alone is sufficient to ground the self as a center of narrative gravity, or whether the model must be supplemented by a causal-historical constraint on the production of the narrative. Weigh the arguments for both positions carefully, but do not end on a non-committal note: defend a specific verdict about whether the amnesia-and-fabrication scenario reveals a fatal flaw in the pure narrative account, and explain what that verdict implies for how we should understand moral responsibility and personal identity in cases of radical psychological discontinuity.
