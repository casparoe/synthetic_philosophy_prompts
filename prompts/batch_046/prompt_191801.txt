I have recently been studying Gottlob Frege’s late political diary, in which he advances starkly antisemitic and nationalist sentiments, and I find myself unable to maintain the usual separation between his biography and his technical philosophy. Specifically, I wonder whether his celebrated rejection of psychologism and his doctrine of objective, mind-independent “thoughts” (Gedanken) were not purely methodological commitments within logic and mathematics, but rather expressed a broader normative ideal: the purification of cognition from what he regarded as particularistic or ethnically marked contamination. If one reads the diary’s essentialist taxonomy of peoples alongside his semantic distinction between sense (Sinn) and reference (Bedeutung), does it not suggest that Frege conceived genuinely objective reference as requiring a conceptual scheme implicitly cleansed of “alien” content? In other words, might his anti-psychologism be interpreted not merely as a defence of logical objectivity, but as carrying an unstated anthropology of the rational subject—one that treats universalist logicism as presupposing a specific, exclusionary conception of the thinker? I am not asking for biographical speculation alone, but for an assessment of whether the diary genuinely bears on the interpretation of his philosophy of language and logic in this manner, and whether it undermines the universalist pretensions of his Begriffsschrift by revealing an implicit normative restriction on who counts as a bearer of objective thought.
