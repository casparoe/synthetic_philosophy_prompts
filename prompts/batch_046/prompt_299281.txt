Next thursday I'm presenting at our dept colloquium a weird little thesis on why there's something rather than nothing. My claim is that "nothing" is conceptually unstable — not in some mystical way, but because specifying total absence (no objects, no properties, no space, not even logic?) forces you to smuggle in minimal descriptive structure just to state the absence, and that smuggled structure is basically the seed of "something." So I'm not offering a cosmological explanation; I'm arguing the framing itself is broken, that absence isn't a default baseline that needs explaining away but a kind of semantic collapse.

The room will be mostly analytic metaphysicains who live and breathe modal realism and grounding talk, a couple philosophy-of-science people who think this whole thing is a pseudo-problem in fancy dress, and one theologian who always brings up creatio ex nihilo whenever existence comes up. There's also a grad student who's been reading Carnap and will probably accuse me of doing linguistic analysis without admitting it. I want a Q&A prep sheet: the objections they'll actually raise, ordered by how much damage they can do, phrased in their own vocabulary (not generic debate-bot language). Tell me what I should concede right in my opening — I think I need to admit I'm not touching the cosmological argument and that my "structural noise" idea might just be ontology with a fresh coat of paint.
