So I've been following the luck egalitarian debate since grad school — you know, Dworkin's choice/chance line, Cohen's refinement, then the harshness objection, then all the responses about prioritarianism or pluralism. But in my reading group it's totally stalled. We can all run the standard moves: option luck vs brute luck, the expensive tastes problem, the adpative preferences problem. Nobody's surprised by anything. My hunch is that the real disagreement isn't about distributive justice at all, but about whether 'responsibility' is even the right kind of concept to ground political obligations — like, is it a metaphysical status or a social practice? But I'm not sure. Is this stall because we're actually asking different questions — one about what people deserve, one about what societies should express — under the same label? Or is there a hidden empirical claim about how much of our choices are genuinely ours? I want a diagnosis, not another survey. What kind of disagreement is this, and if your diagnosis is right, is there anything — a distinction, a model, even a shift in framing — that could actually move it? Also, I'm weirdly stuck on this one case: imagine someone refuses a cheap vaccinne because of a conspiracy belief they formed in an epistemic bubble they couldn't escape. Is that option luck? It feels like neither brute nor option luck, and maybe that gap shows the whole framework is missing something about structural epistemic luck. Does that break the debate or just extend it?
