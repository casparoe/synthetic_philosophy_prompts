I sit on the board of this writers' retreat foundation out in the valley — you know, the one with the broken gate and the kitchen that always smells like rosemary — and we're meeting tuesday to vote on a procurement charter for the residency dining hall. The issue sounds simple but it's turned into this philosophical knot about meat versus crops versus wild suffering. Specifically, we're weighing whether to ban poultry and beef altogether, or to allow beef but not chicken, based on arguments about crop-cultivation deaths — all those field rodents and insects killed in combine harvesters for soy and wheat — versus the direct suffering of farmed animals, plus the land-use question since beef takes up acreage that could be wild habitat. One board member, a poet who reads way too much ecology, keeps pushing the replacement-rate point: chickens live shorter lives and get replaced faster, so per calorie they might involve more total suffering, and pasture land for beef might alter wild-animal populations in ways that aren't straightforward. He's actually proposing we permit grass-fed beef from local ranchers but ban all poultry and monocrop grains, which sounds backwards but he brought spreadsheets. Another board member thinks we're being utilitarian robots and that the foundation's purpose is human artistic flourishing, not global suffering optimization, so she wants status quo with just transparency labeling. I'm a novelist, not an ethicist, but I'm tasked with drafting the memo for circulation before the vote.

I need it to recommend one concrete course — maybe a phased transition with a pilot period ending in march, or an immediate ban with nutritional exemptions — with actual reasoning that takes the wild-animal-suffering and crop-death arguments seriously, not just waving them away, and it has to honestly address the strongest point on the losing side. Could you write it like an institutional opinion, clear bottom line, not too lawyerly, with a timeline we can actually vote on? I'm writing this in between revising chapter seven and my brain is definately foggy, so I might be misremembering some of the numbers but please don't ask me for sources — just work with the conceptual structure and make it sound like something a board could act on.
