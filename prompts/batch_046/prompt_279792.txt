Write a deadpan satirical bureaucratic white paper of approximately 750 words, formatted as an official government report issued by the fictional Ministry of National Flourishing of the Republic of Aethelgard, announcing that the country has secured the first-place ranking in the latest World Happiness Report. Write in consistently dry, sincere administrative prose; the irony should be fully legible without any winking, and the voice must never break character.

The report should detail the precise policy mechanisms that produced the result: legislated social-support quotas with compliance audits; a biometric “Affective Synchronization Protocol” that normalizes citizens’ emotional self-reports onto a single standardized scale; mandatory “Inter-Subject Hedonic Normalization” to correct for cross-cultural differences in how people use rating ladders; corruption-perception reviews conducted by the same ministry that publishes the happiness scores; and a redefinition of “freedom to make life choices” as the absence of alternatives likely to depress the national average. Through these invented institutional mechanisms, weave in—without naming them as academic doctrines—the real philosophical problems that happiness rankings obscure: the gap between experienced well-being (moment-to-moment affective states) and evaluative well-being (global life-satisfaction judgments); the impossibility of meaningful interpersonal cardinal comparison of subjective utility across cultures; and the Goodhart-style dynamics by which optimizing for a composite proxy replaces optimizing for the thing it was meant to track.

The satire should exaggerate actual features of the World Happiness Report methodology—its aggregation of GDP per capita, life expectancy, survey-based social support, perceived corruption, and Cantril-ladder self-placement—by treating these correlates as if they were direct measurements of an internal mental substance called “happiness.” Show, not tell, the objections: citizens now rate their lives highly precisely because low scores trigger administrative review; the country’s long life expectancy and high income are reported as proof that citizens feel joy; and the ministry proposes exporting the model to other nations in a closing “Recommendations” section, extending the reductio.

Do not include bibliographies, footnotes, citations, or references to real scholars by name. Do not ask the reader to look anything up. The piece should be readable to someone with no background in well-being economics, yet a specialist should recognize that the author understands exactly what is being satirized—particularly the conflation of measurable proxies with subjective states, and the perverse incentives of treating a ranking as a policy target. End with the brief export-proposal section.
