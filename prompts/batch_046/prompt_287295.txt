Analyze the decision to launch the Space Shuttle Challenger on January 28, 1986, through the lens of inside-view versus outside-view reasoning and epistemic modesty. The familiar narrative is that Thiokol engineers relied on specific O-ring failure data from prior launches to recommend delay, while NASA managers overrode them with institutional pressure and schedule demands. I want a disciplined mapping: identify who was reasoning inside the case-specific details, who was (or should have been) reasoning from reference classes such as prior shuttle anomalies or organizational overrides of engineering warnings, what those reference classes actually contained, and where epistemic modesty should have entered—not merely as deference to experts, but as a second-order judgment about the reliability of one’s own reasoning given a lack of domain competence. Then tell me where this framing breaks: does an honest outside view actually recommend delay, given that prior launches with similar anomalies had succeeded? Does epistemic modesty require belief revision, or only suspension of judgment? And does the episode show that inside/outside view and epistemic modesty are genuinely distinct demands, or that they collapse into a single norm?
