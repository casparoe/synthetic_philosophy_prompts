I'm drafting a chapter for a novel where a philosopher-character gets stuck in a loop asking why there's anything at all, and I need a taxonomy tree to hang the dialogue on. You are an expert metaphysician — please think carefully, do a quick brainstorm of the logical splits first, then build the tree step by step.

The tree should sort answers to 'why is there something rather than nothing?' Each branch is labelled by the claim that actualy divides it. Start with whether 'nothing' is even a coherent possibility. Next split on whether an explanation is demanded at all. Then on whether that explanation must be necessary rather than contingent. Then on whether value, logical structure, or self-causation does the explaining.

Every leaf needs a name if it's a defended position, or 'unnamed' if it's just a logical gap nobody fills. Make sure these show up: the brute-fact view, the axiarchic value-creator, the modal-necessitarian who thinks emptiness is impossible, the self-causing loopist, and the deflationist who says the question dissolves. Each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment — the bit that makes readers wince.

Also flag any pair of leaves your tree cant actually seperate — maybe the loopist and the necessitarian blur together?

Keep it meaningful for someone without wifi, so dont ask for bibliographies, citations, or 'see Author Year'. Stay in the weeds but dont invoke ultra-obscure theories a small model wont have memorised. British spelling is preferred. Casual is fine — a typo or two wont hurt, it makes it feel like I'm writing this in a cafe. Cheers.
