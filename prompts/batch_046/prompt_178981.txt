I am preparing the conceptual architecture for a graduate seminar on Aristotelian economic philosophy and need a consistency audit before I circulate the handout. My working commitments are these:

(a) Money (nomisma) is instituted by convention (nomos) rather than by nature (physis); its exchange-value depends entirely on communal agreement and carries no intrinsic worth.

(b) Oikonomikē—the art of household management—is naturally limited because it aims only at goods necessary for the good life; chrematistikē, the art of acquiring wealth through exchange, is unlimited and therefore unnatural.

(c) Contemporary fractional-reserve banking, despite yielding monetary profit, is structurally closer to oikonomikē than to chrematistikē, because its social function is to intermediate the provisioning of households rather than to accumulate coin for its own sake.

(d) Justice in voluntary exchange requires proportional reciprocity according to need (to antipeponthos), and tokos—money breeding money through interest—is the most extreme violation of this principle, since it detaches return entirely from use-value.

(e) A state-issued digital currency with a demurrage mechanism (a negative carrying cost on idle balances) restores money to its proper conventional role as a circulating medium, and therefore makes the monetary system more “natural” in Aristotle’s functional sense, despite being technologically artificial.

Please audit this package. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severely? For each genuine tension, specify what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Then identify the single revision that would clean up the most conflicts with the least damage to the overall framework. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. I am not asking for an exegesis of Aristotle; I want to know whether my own position holds together.
