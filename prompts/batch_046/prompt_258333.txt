I have come to accept a particular reading of Sartre's account of radical freedom and bad faith, which I will call the facticity-ownership interpretation. On this view, bad faith is not primarily a denial of one's freedom, but rather a refusal to own one's facticity. The social roles, historical conditions, and material circumstances that Sartre describes as facticity are not external constraints upon an otherwise unencumbered consciousness; instead, they constitute the necessary content through which freedom expresses itself. Consequently, what Sartre describes as bad faith — for instance, treating one's occupation or social position as if it defined one's essence — is not a failure to acknowledge that one is free, but a failure to acknowledge that one's concrete situation is genuinely one's own. It is a flight not from liberty itself, but from the burden of owning the conditions in which liberty operates. I want an honest assessment of what would count against this interpretation. Which arguments or structural features in Sartre's own writings resist it? Does it collapse bad faith into a more familiar concept — perhaps false consciousness, adaptive preference, or self-deception — and if so, is that a problem for the reading? What intuitions about agency, particularly in cases of severe social constraint or structural oppression, does it struggle to accommodate? Which results in adjacent areas — say, about moral responsibility under determinism, or about the ontology of social roles — must it remain consistent with, and where does it risk contradiction? I am especially interested in whether this reading renders Sartre's view of freedom unfalsifiable: if every apparent constraint can be redescribed as the material of freedom, does the position insulate itself from empirical or phenomenological counterexample? Rank the defeaters from those that would genuinely undermine the interpretation to those that are merely awkward, and indicate which ones a defender of the facticity-ownership view could concede without abandoning the core claim.
