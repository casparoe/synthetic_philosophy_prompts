Consider a hypothetical artificial system that has never had sensorimotor contact with the physical world but has learned, entirely through internal symbolic manipulation and inter-symbolic consistency constraints, a formal system sufficient to derive properties of transfinite ordinals. The system can correctly apply rules involving ω, ω+1, and ε₀, and can generate novel theorems about their ordering, yet it has never experienced weight, duration, succession, or any physical analog to "more" or "beyond."

Explain the conceptual issue this raises for the symbol grounding problem: specifically, whether the system's symbols for these ordinals are genuinely grounded, or whether they remain ungrounded despite functional competence. In your explanation, distinguish between two competing conceptions of grounding—causal-embodied anchoring, which requires direct sensorimotor coupling with the referent or its physical analogues, and relational-coherence anchoring, which holds that a symbol is grounded if it occupies a stable, functionally constrained position within a sufficiently rich network of inter-symbolic relations.

Address whether the absence of physical correlates for transfinite ordinals weakens the case for causal-embodied anchoring, and whether relational-coherence anchoring collapses into a form of holistic internalism that makes grounding too cheap. Finally, clarify what this case suggests about whether symbol grounding is a single problem or a family of problems that vary with the ontological category of the symbolized entity.
