A popular philosophy podcast recently glossed Galen Strawson's Basic Argument like this: true moral responsibility is impossible because being responsible for an action requires having intentionally made yourself the kind of person who performs it, yet any such act of self-creation depends on a prior mental state or character for which you are not responsible, generating an infinite regress that never reaches an ultimate origin. The host added that this regress only gets started if determinism is true—if your choices are genuinely undetermined, then you can be an uncaused cause of your own character, breaking the chain—so Strawson is really just a hard determinist in disguise, and libertarian free will solves his problem.

This sounds partly right but also suspicious, since I recall Strawson insists the argument works regardless of whether determinism or indeterminism holds. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate, what is a standard simplification, and what misrepresents Strawson's actual view—particularly on whether libertarian indeterminism escapes the regress, whether he presupposes determinism, and what he means by being the "ultimate" source of one's actions. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and without restating the question.
