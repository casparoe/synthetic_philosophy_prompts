I am preparing the final exam for an upper-level seminar on Enlightenment moral psychology and political philosophy. I want one essay prompt—answerable in 90 minutes, expected length about 600 to 800 words—that forces students to work inside the tension between Rousseau’s condemnation of theatrical spectacle and his own practice as a playwright. Students should know, in general terms, that in his letter to d’Alembert he attacks public theater as a mechanism of moral corruption: it replaces authentic presence with artificial representation, turns suffering into consumable entertainment, and produces a passive spectator whose sympathy is both aroused and safely contained by architectural distance. They should also know that he authored plays—including Narcissus and Pygmalion—that appear to do exactly what he condemns. The prompt must not be answerable by summarizing this tension, nor by dismissing it as mere biographical inconsistency. Instead, it should require students to choose and defend one of two interpretive strategies: either (a) a coherentist reading that identifies a principled distinction—perhaps between commercial spectacle that fixes social identity and private participatory performance that dissolves the actor-spectator boundary—under which his theatrical work actually exemplifies rather than violates his theory of moral sentiment; or (b) a contradiction reading that argues the tension is real, fatal, and reveals a deeper instability in his claim that authentic pity requires unmediated bodily co-presence and cannot survive aesthetic mediation. The prompt should direct them to ground their argument in his specific claims about the spectator’s passivity, the actor’s artifice, and the gendered dynamics of theatrical display—particularly his suggestion that actresses are doubly corrupted by being simultaneously the object of the gaze and the agent of deception. Please provide the exam question itself, followed by a model answer of about 700 words that takes a definite stand—defending either the coherentist or the contradiction position, not surveying both neutrally—and argues for it with explicit conceptual reasoning rather than textual paraphrase. Finally, provide a marking scheme built around five must-hit analytical moves, each worth 4 marks for a total of 20. For each point, include a one-line note on the typical failure mode: for instance, treating the problem as biographical rather than philosophical; ignoring the gendered dimension of Rousseau’s examples; conflating his critique with a generic Platonic hostility to mimesis; failing to explain why private or ritual performance might escape the charge of artifice; or neglecting to address whether moral education can operate through representation at all, or requires direct presence. The model answer should be committed: it must take a clear position and defend it with reasons, leaving no ambiguity about which interpretation it endorses. Do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reference to secondary scholarship.
