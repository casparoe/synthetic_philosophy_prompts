Consider the following argument about AI-generated images and what we might call the "derivation constraint" on creative authorship. (1) An artifact is genuinely creative in the relevant aesthetic sense only if the specific choices that make it valuable are not merely the aggregated output of prior human choices that the producer applies without understanding why they work; there must be a non-derivative grasp of the aesthetic rationale behind those choices. (2) A diffusion model produces images by aggregating statistical patterns from training data; it applies compositional and chromatic regularities without any grasp of why those regularities are aesthetically effective. (3) Therefore, AI outputs violate the derivation constraint and are not genuinely creative works. (4) But consider a human icon painter working in a highly codified tradition—say, Byzantine iconography—who applies canonical rules for proportion, color symbolism, and figure placement correctly, yet cannot articulate why these rules produce spiritual or aesthetic power; she knows that they work, but not why. (5) Her work is also the product of aggregated prior human choices (the canonical rules developed over centuries) that she applies without a non-derivative aesthetic grasp of their rationale. (6) So by (1), her icon is also not genuinely creative, even if it is beautiful, technically masterful, and subtly varied within the tradition. (7) Yet we intuitively judge that such tradition-bound human production can still be genuinely creative—perhaps even profound—despite operating within rigid, inherited constraints that the maker does not fully comprehend. (8) Hence (1) proves too much: it denies creativity not only to AI but to vast domains of human art produced within conventionalized forms by practitioners who master mechanics without full aesthetic comprehension. (9) A defender might reply that human producers possess intentionality or consciousness, which AI lacks, but then the derivation constraint is redundant: consciousness, not derivation, is doing the exclusionary work, and the constraint arbitrarily withholds creativity from human tradition-bound creators who lack full comprehension.
