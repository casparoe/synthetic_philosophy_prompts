Write a dialogue between two philosophers—call them A and B—debating a refined version of the conceivability argument for philosophical zombies. A should defend the claim that we can conceive not merely of a total zombie (a being physically identical to a conscious human but lacking all phenomenal experience), but of a partially phenomenal zombie: a being physically and functionally identical to us except that it specifically lacks the qualitative character of pain, while retaining other phenomenal states such as visual experience or emotional feeling. A should argue that this selective absence is actually more epistemically secure than total zombiehood, because it isolates a single phenomenal property for subtraction, and that this demonstrates phenomenal properties are ontologically modular—independent, detachable add-ons to physical structure rather than globally entailed by it.

B should reject the inference. B should argue that conceiving of such selective absence requires smuggling in a phenomenal anatomy—a compositional theory of consciousness that treats qualitative states like detachable organs—which is precisely what the physicalist denies and which the zombie argument was meant to establish rather than presuppose. B should further contend that if phenomenal properties are genuinely modular in this way, then selective absence is actually more easily explained on a physicalist framework (by mapping specific qualities onto discrete functional subsystems that can fail independently) than on a dualist one, and therefore that A's refinement undermines the anti-physicalist conclusion it intends to support.

The dialogue should feature real back-and-forth: B must respond specifically to A's claim of ontological modularity, and A must reply to B's charge of circular presupposition—perhaps by distinguishing conceptual decomposability (our ability to think about one quality in isolation) from metaphysical compositionality (the claim that consciousness is literally built from parts). Neither speaker should be a strawman. The discussion should remain focused on the logical structure of conceivability and the conditions for legitimate metaphysical inference, avoiding appeals to empirical neuroscience or to specific named authors.
