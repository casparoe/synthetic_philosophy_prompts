As a novelist drafting an extended dialogue between two archivists—one a formalist who treats logical systems as self-sealing, one a biographical reader convinced that moral failure always leaks into abstraction—I am trying to map the precise logical space of positions on whether Gottlob Frege's late political diary, with its explicit anti-Semitic and authoritarian sentiments, bears upon his philosophical work. I need a taxonomy in the form of a decision tree, not an argumentative essay: an instrument that sorts actual commitments by the answers a respondent gives, not by the school they name themselves after.

Begin every path at the same root: the respondent grants that the diary exists, contains virulent political views, and was never intended for publication. From there, construct internal nodes as concrete diagnostic questions—each phrased so that someone can answer yes or no without already knowing which leaf it leads to. The tree should traverse four specific distinctions: first, whether a philosopher's personal moral corruption can ever travel into formal logical or mathematical systems at all; second, if such travel is possible, whether it operates through conceptual continuity—say, an exclusionary ontology implicit in anti-psychologism—or only through biographical distraction that leaves the formal apparatus untouched; third, whether the diary's private, fragmentary, interlocutory form grants it a different epistemic weight than a published treatise, so that its presence should trigger hermeneutic suspension for specific texts rather than global dismissal; fourth, whether any contamination detected is localized—perhaps to the later revisions of the Grundgesetze or to the correspondence with Husserl—or spread uniformly across the corpus.

Each leaf must be labeled with the position's common name if one exists—Clean Separation, Structural Contamination, Local Rupture, Hermeneutic Suspension—or marked Unoccupied, and must carry one sentence describing its most awkward commitment: the cost its defenders rarely advertise but must accept. Where two leaves turn out to be indistinguishable by your tree's questions, flag the pair explicitly rather than disguising the overlap. Include at least one logically coherent combination of answers that appears to be unoccupied, and offer a brief diagnosis: is it incoherent, morally or intellectually unattractive, or merely unexplored?

Keep the tree to between six and eight leaves. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the instrument must be usable by someone who knows Frege's published logic, is aware the diary was discovered posthumously, and has no access to external sources. Phrase every node as a question about a specific claim or case, never as an abstract methodological stance.
