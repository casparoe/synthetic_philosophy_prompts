In the archaeology and anthro groups I sit in, the collapse vs reorgainization debate has been totally stalled. One camp looks at the Bronze Age breakdown, the Maya lowlands, and Easter Island and says: state structures vanish, literacy dies, population crashes—thats collapse, full stop. The other camp says those are just phase shifts: the same people stay, still farming, still speaking related languages, and calling it collapse just shows our bias toward centralized bureaucracy. Same pottery, same pollen cores, zero movement in years. I think the stall isn't empirical—both sides accept the basic chronology—but that they run on different implicit ontologies: one treats society as an institutional machine that can actually break, the other as a distributed reproductive practice that survives without palaces or scribes. Is that right, or is there a hidden factual fight about continuity thresholds I'm missing? And if it really is ontological, could any conceptual distinction—say between administrative discontinuity and phenomenological rupture—actually unblock things, or is it just bedrock intuition with no progress possible? Don't survey the literature; diagnose the structure of the disagreement and tell me what, if anything, would count as real progress.
