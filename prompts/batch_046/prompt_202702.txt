Two philosophers of language, Mara and Leo, got stuck arguing about performative utterances inside bureaucratic institutions. Mara said that when a department chair sends an email stating, “The committee hereby approves your tenure,” the sentence itself creates the institutional fact—provided the chair holds authority—just as Austin described. Leo agreed in principle but introduced a twist: the university’s bylaws require a physical signed form filed within forty-eight hours, and in this case the form was never filed. The tenure office therefore records the candidate as untenured. Mara claimed the email still performed the act; the institution merely failed to record it. Leo replied that without the procedural uptake specified in the bylaws, the utterance misfired—like saying “I do” outside any ceremony. Mara then proposed that modern institutions allow “provisional performatives”: electronic utterances by authorized speakers bind the institution even when paperwork lags, because authority resides in the role, not in the filing system. Leo pressed her with a revocation challenge: if the act was only provisional, could the chair later send a second message—“I hereby revoke my earlier approval”—and thereby reveal that the first act was never complete? Mara answered that revocation is a distinct speech act with its own conditions; you cannot erase a completed performative like chalk, only correct an error about whether conditions were ever satisfied. Leo said this collapses her category back into his—either all conditions are met and the act is fully done, or they are not and nothing was performed.

Walk through their exchange point by point and adjudicate who is right at each stage. Is “provisional performative” a coherent middle category, or does it blur the line between misfiring and successful performance? Does Leo’s revocation challenge fairly test Mara’s view, or does it confuse undoing an institutional fact with discovering that the fact was never created? And what, if anything, are both missing about how institutional authority actually distributes between speaker, procedure, and record?
