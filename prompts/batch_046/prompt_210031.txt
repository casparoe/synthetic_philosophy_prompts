Write a villanelle — nineteen lines, ABA rhyme scheme, two refrains alternating through the tercets and converging in the final quatrain — on constitutional precommitment understood as an intergenerational Ulysses contract. Introduce the following conceptual twist: the binding agent is not an individual but a collective democratic "we," and the bound party is a successor "we" whose preferences are partly constituted by the very constitutional constraints it seeks to overturn. The first refrain, spoken by the founding generation, should justify entrenchment as self-binding against foreseeable democratic passions (for example, "We lash the mast to spare the ship our storm"). The second refrain, spoken by the successor generation, should claim autonomous authority to dissolve the bonds (for example, "The storm is ours; the ropes are not; we go"). Structure the poem so that the philosophical tension is performed by the form: the refrains take turns through the tercets, never answering each other, and in the closing quatrain they appear side by side but grammatically disconnected — the first ending with "storm," the second ending with "go" — so that the two positions pass without resolution. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with debates about democratic precommitment recognizes the problem, and clear enough that a reader unfamiliar with it grasps the paradox of binding a self that is partly made by the binding. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific texts; rely only on the conceptual framing given above.
