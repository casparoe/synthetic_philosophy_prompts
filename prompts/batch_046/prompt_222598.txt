In a recent workshop on practical reason, a participant summarized cause prioritization with the maxim, "Weight importance, tractability, and neglectedness; if a cause scores near zero on any axis, it drops out." She then used it to reject near-term global health funding on the grounds that it is "not neglected enough." I suspect the maxim is sliding between several quite different claims—arithmetical, normative, strategic, and even metaphysical about value—without anyone noticing. Could you disambiguate it for a handout? Lay out, as a menu, the precise theses this one-liner might stand for: for instance, as a multiplicative formula for expected value, as a lexical ordering, as a heuristic for individual donors, as a claim about institutional division of labor, and as a normative principle about where moral obligation concentrates. For each, say whether it is tautological, defensible but substantive, or false; indicate which reading, if any, actually licenses dismissing a high-importance, high-tractability cause solely for low neglectedness; and say which interpretation the speaker was most likely invoking, given that she meant to defeat near-term funding rather than merely rank it.
