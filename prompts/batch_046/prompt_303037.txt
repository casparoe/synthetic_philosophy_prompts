I am preparing a feature on the enduring ambiguity of Descartes’s cogito and its afterlife in philosophy of language. To that end, I am working through a particularly dense passage in Antoine Arnauld’s Fourth Objections, published alongside the Meditations in 1641. There, Arnauld presses the inference from I think to I exist by comparing the first-person pronoun to an impersonal grammatical construction—roughly, the way Latin can say it walks without specifying any walker. He asks whether the appearance of an agent in the phrase I think really guarantees an underlying substance that performs the thinking, or whether the cogito establishes only that a mental act is occurring at that moment, with no proof that a continuous self is its bearer.

What I am trying to understand is the precise philosophical machinery behind this grammatical analogy. Does Arnauld treat the I as a mere linguistic placeholder that carries no metaphysical weight, or does he concede that there is a thinker while denying that the cogito alone reveals its substantial nature? More specifically, I wonder whether his argument quietly presupposes a view of mental events as self-standing occurrences—something in the neighborhood of modern token-reflexive accounts of self-reference—rather than as modifications of an enduring substance. If that is the framing, does it make his objection more damaging to Descartes, or does it misread the ontological commitment embedded in the act of thinking?

Given that framing, I would like help unpacking what the cogito could still legitimately prove if Arnauld’s grammatical point is granted. Would it establish only a bare occurrence of thought, a minimal and momentary subject of experience, or some intermediate status? And does accepting Arnauld’s view force us to revise what we take the pronoun I to denote—shifting it from a name for a substance to an indexical anchored in the event of cognition itself? A careful reconstruction of the passage’s logic, especially regarding the boundary between grammatical form and ontological commitment, would be invaluable for the piece.
