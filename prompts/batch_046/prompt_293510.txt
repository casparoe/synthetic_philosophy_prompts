Write a short story that dramatizes Imre Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes, translated into an invented, non-scientific domain. Set the narrative in a guild of archivists in a subterranean city who catalog the "echo signatures" of struck minerals—sounds believed to encode the geological history of the region. Through plot and institutional conflict, make visible the hard core (the irrefutable central thesis that echo signatures are deterministic functions of mineral lattice structure), the protective belt (auxiliary hypotheses about temperature, humidity, and striking technique that absorb anomalies), and the dynamics of problem shifts. Include, specifically: a degenerating problem shift in which the guild master explains away contradictory echo patterns by adding ever-more-specific conditions to the protective belt; a rival apprentice who proposes a competing programme with a different hard core (that signatures depend on the temporal sequence of prior strikes in the same chamber rather than on mineral structure alone) and demonstrates a progressive shift by predicting a novel echo pattern in an unexcavated chamber before testing it; and a decision point in which the guild must choose, by methodological convention rather than direct refutation, whether to defend the old hard core or migrate to the new one. Do not label these elements with philosophical terminology inside the story; let them emerge through the characters' practical reasoning and the guild's rules. Keep the narrative focused on a single testing session and its immediate aftermath, with attention to how each faction treats an anomalous strike that the protective belt struggles to accommodate.
