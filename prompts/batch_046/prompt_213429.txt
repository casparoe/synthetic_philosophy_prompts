Consider a variant of panpsychism that rejects both Russellian micro-phenomenalism and cosmopsychism in favor of what might be called dispositional-structural panpsychism: consciousness is treated as a fundamental relational potential that inheres latently in all physical entities, but it is actualized only within systems that satisfy specific organizational conditions, and macro-consciousness is not composed of aggregated micro-subjects but is the direct expression of that potential at the macro-scale. On this account, an isolated fundamental particle bears the same unactualized potential as a human brain, analogous to how a soluble compound bears the potential for dissolution even in the absence of a solvent; there is never a plurality of micro-subjects that must be combined, so the subject-summation problem is dissolved by denial rather than solved by aggregation. Give an honest assessment of what would have to be true for this position to fail. Which of its commitments—the fundamentality of the potential, the physical-disposition analogy, the denial of micro-subjects, the organizational actualization condition—are genuinely vulnerable, and which have been insulated by ad hoc qualifications? Distinguish decisive defeaters from merely awkward ones: for example, arguments about whether phenomenal properties can genuinely be dispositional in the way physical properties are, about whether organizational conditions can be specified without circular reference to consciousness, or about the identity conditions of a single potential expressed at different scales. State what the view's defenders would themselves concede as counting against them, not only what critics allege, and rank the defeaters from fatal to inconvenient. Does the view make empirical predictions that differ from those of standard constitutive panpsychism or cosmopsychism, or has it been qualified into unfalsifiability? If it is well insulated, say so explicitly, and say whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice.
