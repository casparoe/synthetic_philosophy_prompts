I sit on the review panel for a cross-disciplinary research fund. A proposal asks for support to model climate-policy adoption across three structurally similar industrial regions by treating local value orientations—specifically secular asceticism and communal obligation—as direct causal variables, explicitly invoking Weber’s elective affinity between religious ethics and economic rationalization. The researchers argue that without treating values as explanatory mechanisms rather than mere background conditions, divergence in regulatory uptake cannot be accounted for; critics on the panel reply that this confuses hermeneutic understanding with causal explanation and risks reifying culture. Draft our decision memo—approve, approve with conditions, or reject—giving a concrete recommendation with a timeline for response, and engaging the strongest version of the objection that culture cannot bear causal weight in the manner proposed.
