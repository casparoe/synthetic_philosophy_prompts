Construct a thought experiment that modifies Black’s spheres scenario to operate in a domain where spatial separation is unavailable as a distinguishing relation. Design a single, causally closed temporal loop containing exactly two entities, A and B, that share every observable intrinsic qualitative property and every relational property with respect to each other and to their shared environment. To introduce the possibility of distinction without qualitative or spatial difference, stipulate that A alone carries a non-manifest, structurally hidden “indexical orientation parameter” embedded in its functional architecture: this parameter is not observable, not causally efficacious, and not reducible to any relational feature, functioning analogously to a bare particular or haecceity but specified within a functionalist rather than substance-based ontology. Present the scenario in one paragraph, without invoking quantum mechanics, multiverse cosmology, or science-fictional technology. After presenting the case, provide an analysis that (i) catalogues exactly which properties are shared and which are allegedly not, (ii) determines whether this counts as a genuine counterexample to Leibniz’s principle of the identity of indiscernibles or merely relocates Black’s relational strategy into a hidden intrinsic feature, and (iii) isolates the precise structural feature that does the philosophical work—specifically, whether the work is done by the hidden parameter itself or by the metaphysical decision to treat non-manifest structural differences as real. Conclude by stating how a defender of the identity of indiscernibles would dissolve the case, and explain what happens to the argument if the hidden parameter is made causally manifest.
