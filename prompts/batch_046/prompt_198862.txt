I'm working through Grice's theory of conversational implicature for a philosophy of language seminar, and I've become fixated on a boundary case that seems to strain his framework. Consider situations in which a particular conversational implicature becomes so routinely generated in a specific community that it acquires something like a conventional status—think of a workplace where describing a project as "ambitious" has long carried the conversational implicature that it is unrealistic and likely to fail, to the point that newcomers learn this association as part of entering the discourse. Now suppose an experienced member of that community uses "ambitious" sincerely to praise a genuinely bold but feasible plan, deliberately relying on the audience to recognize that the usual ironic frame has been suspended. Grice's account treats conversational implicature as calculated afresh from the Cooperative Principle and the maxims in each context, with speaker meaning anchored in the speaker's intention to produce a specific belief via recognition of that intention. But in the scenario I describe, the implicature seems to have achieved a kind of sedimented weight that competes with, and can even temporarily override, the speaker's immediate intentions—unless we say that the speaker's intention includes the explicit suspension of the sedimented reading.

Write an essay addressing the following controversy: Does Grice's distinction between what is said and what is implicated have the resources to account for sedimented or semi-conventionalized implicatures that are then deliberately suspended, or does this phenomenon reveal that speaker meaning is more deeply shaped by shared linguistic practice than Grice's intention-based model allows? In your essay, weigh the arguments for retaining a strictly Gricean analysis—perhaps by treating the suspension itself as part of the speaker's intended meaning—against the arguments for revising or supplementing Grice with a more socially embedded account of speaker meaning. Reach a definite conclusion about whether Grice's framework can absorb these cases without substantial modification, and justify it. Please also indicate how confident you are in your conclusion, and single out the specific point in your argument about which you are least certain.
