hey, ive been thinking about advance directives and whether the earlier self actually gets to boss around the later one. here are my gut reactions on a bunch of cases, and i cant tell if they hang together.

1. old guy with alzheimers, signed a dnr five years ago, now he smiles at nurses and doesnt remember signing. my verdict: honor the dnr. the later guy isnt the same deliberitive agent.

2. same setup but he wrote "if i get dementia and seem happy, thats not me, thats the disease talking." he now seems happy. verdict: still honor it? i lean yes, but it feels shakier than case 1.

3. woman writes "never put me on a feeding tube." she didnt know about a new tube that lets you talk and taste food. now shes in a state where that tube is an option and she asks for it. verdict: ignore the old directive, she was wrong about the facts.

4. guy writes "even if i beg for life support later, dont listen, im locking this in now." he now begs, fully aware of who he is. verdict: honor the refusal. but wait, in case 1 i said the later self isnt a real decision-maker, here he clearly is, yet i still want to bind him. so whats the difference?

5. someone designs her own "competence checkpoint" — a specific puzzle she has to solve every month to keep her directive active. she fails the puzzle but also has a stable new set of values. do we go by the failed checkpoint or the new values? im torn, maybe lean checkpoint?

6. a depressed teenager writes "no heroic measures if im ever in a coma." she grows up, abandons the view, then gets in a coma with no family. verdict? i think honor the adult view? but then why honor case 4?

so my pattern might be: earlier self binds later self only when the later self either lacks deliberitive capacity OR is operating under a false factual belief that undermined the original choice? but case 4 breaks that because he has capacity and the belief was true.

is there a cleaner principle, maybe about "precedant autonomy as a kind of promise to ones future caregivers"? or do i just have to drop one of these? also what should happen in a case where the directive has a built-in sunset clause that exipres the day after the person forgets they wrote it?

write out the principle or candidates, tell me which verdicts survive and which have to go, and predict on the sunset case.
