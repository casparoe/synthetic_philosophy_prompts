hey, i've been digging into jain anekāntavāda and syādvāda lately and built this argument that feels like it proves way too much. wanted to sanity-check it before i commit.

(1) anekāntavāda holds that reality is ananta-dharma—infinitely many properties—so any single judgment captures only one naya (standpoint).
(2) syādvāda then says every claim needs the qualifier syāt, meaning it's true in one respect but not necessarily in others.
(3) so "the self is permanent" and "the self is impermanent" are both true, just under different naya.
(4) if rational belief tracks truth, and both propositions are true, then i should believe both.
(5) but holding P and not-P about the same thing without qualification is a straight contradiction.
(6) the obvious fix is standpoint-indexed belief: i believe P-under-naya-A and not-P-under-naya-B.
(7) except there's no principled boundary for which naya applies; since every object has infinite sides, all naya are in play simultaneously.
(8) so indexing collapses—rationality still demands i hold both beliefs unqualified.
(9) that means anekāntavāda forces rational people into explicit contradictions.
(10) thats absurd, so something's got to give. i really want to keep (1) and (4) because they feel like the heart of the view, but im willing to drop (6) or (7). which step actually breaks, and what do i pay if i reject it? like, do i end up with a quietist view where i just stop making unqualified assertions altogether?
