(2) I think idealization should correct empirical mistakes but should not replace an agent's core evaluative framework; for instance, if someone genuinely values non-instrumental artistic creation over biological longevity, that weighting should survive rational refinement.

(3) I believe coherence across different idealized agents must be achieved through a weighted reflective-equilibrium procedure, resolving conflicts by privileging preferences that remain stable through the longest chain of self-correction rather than through simple voting or averaging.

(4) I hold that when a preference depends on a false metaphysical commitment—such as substance dualism about minds—but that commitment is structurally central to the agent's values, idealization should preserve the preference's motivational structure even while discarding the false ontology, rather than invalidating the preference altogether.

(5) I maintain that an AI should not implement a preference endorsed by my fully idealized self if my present self explicitly rejects it, so long as that rejection is not itself a product of ignorance or cognitive bias.

Do these five commitments hang together? Identify which pairs are in genuine tension, which conflicts are only apparent, and rank the real tensions by severity, specifying the least costly revision for each.
