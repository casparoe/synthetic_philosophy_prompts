I keep encountering the phrase "pulling yourself up by your own bootstraps" in discussions about how we know what we know. From what I gather, philosophers apply it to a specific problem: if you learn that your eyes are reliable by using your eyes, or that your reasoning works by using your reasoning, you have circled back on yourself. People present this as obviously impossible, just like physically pulling yourself upward by your own bootstraps is impossible, and they seem to think this shows that some kinds of "easy knowledge" cannot exist.

I want to audit how far that analogy actually holds. Here is the mapping I am working with: the bootstraps are your belief-forming methods or senses; the ground beneath you is some independent source of justification; the pulling motion is using the outputs of a method to validate the method itself; and the fall is circular reasoning that never touches anything outside itself. But I am unsure what corresponds to the person doing the pulling, or whether the image quietly smuggles in the idea that you must be entirely self-supporting in order to stand at all. It also seems to assume there is no other limb or rope available, which in real life might correspond to checking one sense against another rather than checking a sense against itself.

There is also a particular situation I have been turning over to test the analogy. Imagine a person born wearing special glasses that tint everything blue. They never remove them. They form the belief that the sky, grass, and blood are all blue. They check this belief repeatedly by looking at more objects, which also appear blue through the glasses. They never experience a moment of unaided comparison. Is this circular in the same way as the bootstraps? Or does the analogy break because there is still an external world they are consistently interacting with, even if their access to it is permanently filtered?

Please lay out the correspondence table explicitly, identify the points where the bootstraps image imports assumptions that do not apply to knowledge, and tell me whether the conclusion that self-justification is necessarily impossible survives once those disanalogies are on the table. If there is a better analogy for what is wrong with using your eyes to prove your eyes work, suggest it. Finally, apply your analysis to the blue-glasses case: is it genuinely an instance of bootstrapping, or is the problem something different—perhaps a hidden filter rather than a logical circle?
