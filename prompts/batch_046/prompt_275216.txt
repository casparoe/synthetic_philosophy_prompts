For my metaphysics seminar, I am writing an essay on counterpart theory versus transworld identity. Rather than rehearsing the standard Lewis-Kripke exchange on personal identity, I want to examine a narrower problem: cross-world continuity for artifacts that undergo gradual part-replacement distributed across possible worlds, so that no single world contains the fully original object. Specifically, I will assess whether Lewisian counterpart relations can preserve what might be called the artifact’s “modal trajectory” without invoking a haecceitistic essence that lacks any empirical marker, and whether Kripkean transworld identity avoids treating intermediate worlds as containing objects that violate the law of non-contradictory identity. The essay should reach and defend a clear conclusion about which framework better handles this kind of distributed continuity for non-personal particulars, and it should seriously engage the strongest objection to that conclusion—namely, that the preferred view either fragments the object into an implausible multiplicity of counterparts or implicitly relies on essential properties that do no explanatory work.
