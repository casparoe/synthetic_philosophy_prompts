Write a joke about expected value fanaticism. The punchline should turn on the actual decision-theoretic problem, not on generic stereotypes of rationalists: specifically, on the fact that with an unbounded utility function and a heavy-tailed distribution, expected value diverges, so a strict maximizer must prefer a gamble with an arbitrarily small chance of an arbitrarily large payoff over any certain outcome, regardless of the near-certain downside. Frame it as a brief exchange between a customer ordering coffee and a barista offering a "tail-risk latte" that carries a one-in-a-billion chance of infinite utility and an overwhelming chance of immediate ruin. The humor should come from the maximizer's rigorous, inescapable commitment to ordering it, and from the barista's growing alarm as the logic is spelled out step by step.
