As a journalist covering the conceptual architecture of cognition and self-reference, I am working through the GEB framework that distinguishes frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages in formal and representational systems. Could you explain, in conceptual rather than notational terms, how these three levels can become genuinely decoupled—so that a system sustains a coherent inner message, an implicit representational model of its own processing, that is neither authorized by its frame message (the architectural rules and constraints built into its design) nor expressed in its outer message (the observable outputs and behaviors it produces)? I am especially interested in what such a decoupling implies for where meaning is actually located: does it reside in the system's internal dynamics, in the intentions of its designers, or in the interpretive practices of its users? And does this tripartite distinction help clarify anything about contemporary debates over machine self-modeling and interpretability?
