I'm preparing a taxonomy for a seminar on the normative conflict between collective memory and documentary history. The core question is this: when a community's living account of an event conflicts with the archival record, which should yield, and in what domains? Please construct a decision tree that sorts positions through four concrete branching questions: (1) does the dispute concern an event within living memory or the distant past? (2) was the documentary record produced by members of the community or by an external institution (e.g., a colonial administration or state bureaucracy)? (3) does the community's version function primarily as an empirical claim about what occurred, or as a present practice of identity and solidarity? (4) if the accounts remain irreconcilable, which sphere should be adjusted—public monuments, school curricula, legal frameworks for reparations, or only private and informal commemoration?

At each leaf, state the position's established name if one exists (otherwise label it "unnamed"), summarize its core commitment in one sentence, and identify its most awkward implication—specifically, what it must say about a case in which a politically vital founding narrative is clearly contradicted by archives that the community did not create. Also flag any logically possible combinations that appear unoccupied, with a brief diagnosis of whether the gap stems from incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Keep the tree focused on who should give way rather than on empirical techniques of verification.
