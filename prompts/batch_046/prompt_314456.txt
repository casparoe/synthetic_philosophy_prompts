Student answer:

Einstein imagines a hole with no matter inside, surrounded by matter. If the gravitational equations are generally covariant, you can apply a smooth map that's the identity outside the hole but shifts points inside. This gives a different metric inside that still solves the equations and matches the outside data, so the exterior doesn't uniquely determine the interier—determinism breaks.

He first raised this in 1913 against covariant candidates, including the equations he eventually published in 1915. His Entwurf equations from that same period escaped the problem because they lacked full covariance. The resolution is that spacetime points have no intrinsic identity, so the two metrics are just the same geometry expressed in different coordinate representations. This point-coincidence argument was first made explicit by Kretschmann and then adopted by Einstein. So there's only one physical situation, and determinism is restored.
