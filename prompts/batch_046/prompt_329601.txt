I'm trying to hold together the following package on panpsychism and ethics, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. Here are my commitments:

(1) I am a physicalist in the standard ontological sense—everything is ultimately physical or grounded in the physical—but I also accept Russellian monism: physics describes only relational and dispositional structure, while the intrinsic nature of the physical is experiential.

(2) I think cosmopsychism solves the combination problem better than micropsychism: there is a single cosmic experiential subject, and localized human consciousness arises through phenomenal decomposition or "filtering," not through aggregation of particle-level minds.

(3) I hold that moral status should track the degree of phenomenal integration or binding, not be distributed flatly across all experiential subjects.

(4) From (2) and (3) together, I derive that the cosmic subject carries overwhelming moral weight relative to any human being, though human minds retain derivative weight as structurally integrated subsystems.

(5) I think that if large-scale physical processes—such as stellar nucleosynthesis or black-hole accretion—alter the cosmic experiential state in harmful directions, then agents capable of influencing cosmology bear indirect obligations regarding those processes.

(6) At the same time, I deny that ordinary actions—such as collapsing a quantum state in a laboratory, or heating a gas—carry any moral weight, because the integration threshold for ethical relevance is not met at that scale.

(7) I want to reach (5) and (6) without relying on intuition pumps, using only the physical structure plus a principle of non-arbitrary exclusion that refuses to restrict moral consideration to biological entities.

My questions: Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be? Specifically, does (4) collapse into a reductio in which cosmic obligations swamp all human ones? Is the scale cutoff in (6) arbitrary given (7)? Does (1) actually license (2), or does Russellian monism leave cosmopsychism underdetermined? And is there a hidden contradiction between treating human minds as derivative "filters" in (2) while assigning them independent derivative weight in (4)?

Please rank the tensions by severity, specify what has to give in each case, identify the cheapest single revision that cleans up the most problems, and say whether the remaining package is stable. In doing so, separate what is fairly settled in the philosophy of mind and ethics from what remains genuinely contested among specialists—particularly on whether Russellian monism supports cosmopsychism, whether integration reliably tracks moral status, and whether phenomenal decomposition is a coherent mechanism—without presenting any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
