I've been trying to reason through, on my own, whether eating beef, chicken, or only plant crops minimizes harm to animals, and I keep finding that the comparisons people offer feel incomplete. I understand that beef generally requires more land, which can displace wild animals through habitat conversion, while chicken involves more direct killings per unit of food because of feed conversion ratios and the birds' small size. I also know that crop cultivation kills field animals during harvest and that pesticides affect insects, but I'm not sure how these harms should be weighed against one another. Rather than asking which diet is best, I want a specification of what any adequate comparative framework for this question must actually deliver. Could you lay out the full desiderata: which cases and harm-types it must account for (direct slaughter, feed-crop mortality, habitat displacement, pesticide effects on invertebrates), which conceptual distinctions it must preserve—such as between suffering caused intentionally versus as a structural side-effect of land use, or between preventing a life from coming into existence versus ending an existing one, and between suffering intensity per individual and total number of affected lives—the ecological or biological constraints it cannot violate (for instance, regarding reproductive replacement rates in displaced populations or trophic energy losses), and which of these requirements are genuinely non-negotiable versus which depend on prior moral commitments? I'm particularly concerned about whether there's an unavoidable tension between requiring the framework to be sensitive to the severity of suffering experienced by large mammals displaced by pasture and requiring it to aggregate harm across the vastly greater numbers of insects and small mammals affected by crop agriculture. Finally, please briefly score the two common approaches—land-use comparison and direct-kill counting—against your list, indicating for each where it succeeds and where it collapses a distinction your desiderata say must be kept intact.
