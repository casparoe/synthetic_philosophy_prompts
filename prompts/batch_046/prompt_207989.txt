Write a joke that turns on Jeremy Bentham's unpublished essay Offences Against One's Self (the so-called Essay on Paederasty, c. 1785), in which he applies the felicific calculus—intensity, duration, certainty, propinquity, fecundity, purity, and extent—to argue that consensual same-sex acts should not be criminalized because the intrinsic pleasures and pains are private and negligible, while the severe suffering comes from socially manufactured stigma that he treats as an artificial, removable externality, comparable to the pain of religious persecution rather than of theft. The joke should be intelligible to someone familiar with Bentham's framework but should not be a generic utilitarian-walks-into-a-bar gag; instead, build the punchline around a specific tension in Bentham's reasoning: he demands that we measure purity, meaning whether a pleasure is followed by pain, and fecundity, meaning whether it produces further pleasure, in a domain where prevailing social norms guarantee that any visible expression of same-sex intimacy will generate precisely the impure, unfecund sequelae he insists are not intrinsic to the act. Make the joke work by having a character apply the calculus literally—perhaps weighing propinquity against purity in a modern dating scenario or a legislative debate—and reach an absurd conclusion that exposes the circularity of treating stigma as an exogenous variable while simultaneously using its predictable effects to score the act as harmful.
