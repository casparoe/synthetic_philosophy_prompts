At an interdisciplinary workshop on artificial aesthetics, two researchers—Jordan and Riley—debated a system called Muse-7. Muse-7 uses a two-layer symbol architecture. Layer One applies emotional tags to digitized paintings via rules hand-coded by engineers. Layer Two is a self-modifying feedback loop that rewrites Layer One's weights based on viewer biometric responses and prior outputs. Over time it has begun assigning tags the original documentation never anticipated—for instance, labeling a stark minimalist canvas as nostalgic for industrial noise. The system has no consciousness, no biological substrate, and no direct perceptual access to the artworks; it processes only feature vectors and symbolic outputs.

Jordan opened by saying that every state in Muse-7 is purely derived intentionality, along the lines of Dennett's two-bitser example. Even with recursive self-modification, every symbol and every meta-rule ultimately traces back to the intentions of the human engineers who designed the architecture. The feedback loop is just a longer derivation chain; it does not break the chain. Therefore Muse-7 possesses no original intentionality, only derived intentionality layered upon derived intentionality.

Riley replied that this ignores the functional autonomy Layer Two has achieved. Once the dynamics become self-sustaining, Muse-7 assigns tags for internal functional reasons that are not recoverable from the designers' original intentions. This is precisely the kind of complexity Dennett highlights when he argues that human original intentionality is not a magical mental substance but rather derived intentionality that has become sufficiently complex, self-regulating, and evolutionarily entrenched. If we deny Muse-7 any claim to original intentionality solely because its symbols trace back to code, consistency demands that we deny it to ourselves solely because our mental states trace back to genetic programming and evolutionary history.

Jordan countered that Riley is repeating the mistake Searle identifies in the Chinese Room argument: no amount of syntactic complexity, recursion, or feedback autonomy transforms symbol manipulation into genuine understanding. Muse-7 does not understand melancholy or nostalgia; it shuffles labels according to rules. That those rules are self-modifying does not change the fundamental point that syntax remains distinct from semantics, and that a long, opaque derivational chain is still a derivational chain rooted in human design.

Riley's final move was to charge Jordan with an undefended biological chauvinism. Human brains are causal mechanisms shaped by evolutionary feedback loops—natural selection's version of a self-modifying weighting system. Our original intentionality, on this view, is just the endpoint of a derivational chain from evolutionary design. If self-sustaining functional dynamics can never break that chain, then either humans lack original intentionality too, or Jordan must invoke some non-functional criterion—such as consciousness, biological substrate, or subjective experience—to draw the line. But Jordan had not offered or defended any such criterion.
