I chair the curriculum review committee for our university's general-education ethics requirement. A faculty proposal asks us to approve a required module, tentatively titled "The Moral Psychology of Political Division," built around the five-foundation framework (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity) and on empirical findings that liberal and conservative subjects weight these differently. The module's central assignment asks students to evaluate a specific normative claim: that because conservative moral cognition draws on all five foundations while liberal cognition draws mainly on care and fairness, the conservative profile represents a more complete normative basis and therefore liberals have reason to expand their moral commitments. Half the committee regards this as a legitimate interdisciplinary exercise in applied moral psychology; the other half argues it smuggles a normative conclusion—who is morally right—out of purely descriptive data about psychological differences, thereby violating the is-ought distinction and risking institutional endorsement of partisan moral conclusions. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's vote. Recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with concrete curricular conditions if the latter. The memo must treat the pedagogical value of linking psychology to ethics as a serious consideration, not dismiss it, and must specify exactly where in the module the line between describing moral cognition and justifying moral judgments must be drawn—whether in the framing of the assignment, in the required readings, in the assessment rubric, or in the instructor's obligations—so that reviewers who share the opposing view can see that their concern has been addressed structurally rather than rhetorically. Include a brief worked illustration of how the module's assignment would need to be rewritten under your recommended conditions, and flag the one point where structural conditions cannot fully resolve the disagreement and a judgment call by the committee remains unavoidable.
