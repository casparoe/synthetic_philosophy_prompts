I'm drafting a chapter for my dissertation on the historical and philosophical motivations behind intuitionistic mathematics, and I've become uncertain about the relationship between its logical and ontological commitments. Specifically, I was under the impression that Heyting's formalization of intuitionistic logic—particularly its rejection of the law of excluded middle for infinite domains—was developed primarily as a technical consistency measure in response to Russell's paradox and the broader crisis in naive set theory, rather than solely as an articulation of Brouwer's mentalist constructivism. If that is correct, I am struggling to understand why intuitionistic set theories, such as Martin-Löf's type-theoretic framework, still require an explicit predicative hierarchy and careful management of constructive existence claims. Does the need for these additional structural restrictions indicate that intuitionistic logic by itself is insufficient to block paradoxical constructions, or does the hierarchy serve an entirely different purpose—namely, securing the constructive interpretability of mathematical objects rather than safeguarding consistency?
