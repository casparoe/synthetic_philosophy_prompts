Write a joke that turns on the debate over whether an author's actual intentions fix a text's meaning. The punchline should depend on a specific, somewhat counter-intuitive consequence of anti-intentionalism: that a fortunate misprint—say, an autocorrect that changes "lonely" to "lovely" in a poem about solitude—counts as part of the work's artistic meaning despite being unintended, whereas a hypothetical-intentionalist must construct an author who plausibly intended it, and an actual intentionalist must dismiss it as an error even when it improves the poem. Apply this three-way tension to an everyday, non-literary text—such as a grocery list, a text-message thread, or a fortune-cookie slip—so that the humor arises from treating the mundane object with the hermeneutic seriousness usually reserved for canonical literature. The joke should be intelligible to someone who knows the difference between actual, hypothetical, and anti-intentionalist accounts of meaning, but it should not rely on simple name-dropping; the comedy should come from the philosophical machinery itself.
