Write a proposal for a one-semester graduate paper (approximately 7,500 words) on Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism. Frame the central question with precision: can his denial that fire necessarily burns cotton be reconstructed not as a blanket rejection of causation, but as a restricted claim about modal dependency—namely, that qudra belongs exclusively to God, while created things possess only a derived isti'dād, or preparedness, to serve as the occasion for effects? Explain why the dominant interpretations, which read Al-Ghazālī either through Ash'arite theology or through direct comparison with Hume and Malebranche, leave this modal-dependency reconstruction unsettled. Describe the method: close conceptual analysis of the relevant arguments in his critique of the philosophers, using modern dispositional essentialism solely as a heuristic to test internal coherence rather than as an anachronistic framework. Specify the two principal conclusions that could emerge—either that occasionalism permits a weak, non-necessary natural-law structure, or that it collapses into a stronger divine-voluntarist view—and identify the philosophical and textual considerations that would adjudicate between them. Conclude with an honest scope assessment: flag which dimensions depend on fine-grained Arabic terminology and could overwhelm a single semester, and explain how you would bracket those dimensions to keep the project tractable.
