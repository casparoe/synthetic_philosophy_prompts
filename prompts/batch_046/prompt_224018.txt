I'm putting together a consistency check for my views on cosmological fine-tuning, which have become personally urgent since I began co-teaching a philosophy-of-science module and found myself defending design-adjacent reasoning in front of colleagues trained in physics who keep pressing me on whether I'm smuggling theology into science. Here is roughly where I've landed, and I want to know whether this package actually hangs together or whether I'm helping myself to incompatible motivations without noticing it.

(1) I think the life-permitting ranges of the cosmological constant, the Higgs vacuum expectation value, and the strong-nuclear coupling ratio genuinely support an intelligent-design inference, because those ranges are astronomically narrow relative to the natural parameter space and we currently possess no non-teleological mechanism that fixes the values inside them. (2) I reject multiverse explanations not merely for lacking direct empirical confirmation, but because they strike me as committing an inverse gambler's fallacy—positing an enormous ensemble solely to dilute the surprise of our own special case, without any independent motivation for that ensemble. (3) I hold methodological naturalism to be absolute within scientific practice: no supernatural agency should ever figure in a scientific explanation, regardless of its explanatory success. (4) I take the fine-tuning argument to belong to philosophical cosmology rather than to science proper, so (3) is untouched; it starts from physical premises but draws a metaphysical conclusion that falls outside the domain governed by methodological rules. (5) I also think that if structural realism about quantum field theory is correct, then these "constants" are not fundamental dials but emergent features of deeper symmetries—so the "tuning" metaphor is technically misleading—yet I still maintain that the design inference survives this reinterpretation intact. (6) Finally, I believe that standard anthropic reasoning fully dissolves the evidential puzzle: observers can only exist in the compatible region, so our finding ourselves there carries no statistical surprise and calls for no further explanation, design or otherwise, even though I do not deny that the compatible region is objectively rare.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be in tension but dissolve on inspection, and what is the least costly revision for each genuine conflict? Rank the tensions by severity, specify exactly what has to give in each case and at what conceptual price, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems overall. I want a committed answer that takes definite positions and gives its reasons—do not just lay out the options and leave me to pick. Please write your response in American English.
