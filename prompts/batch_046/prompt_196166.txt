I'm writing a paper for a seminar on archaeological epistemology, and I keep returning to a tension I don't think is fully resolved in how we move from objects to institutions. When archaeologists try to reconstruct not just individual beliefs but collective structures—ritual authority, economic obligation, or social hierarchy—from material remains alone, they seem to rely on two quite different inferential logics. On one side is what I will call the representational-encoding framework: it treats durable artifacts as analogous to texts, carrying symbolic content that can be decoded to reveal shared mental representations, normative rules, or cosmological schemes. On the other side is the performative-stabilization framework: it denies that shared representations are necessary or even recoverable, arguing instead that repeated material practices leave patterned residues that stabilize social roles over time, so that an institution is reconstructed not from messages embedded in objects but from the regularities of interaction those objects made possible and constrained.

To make the comparison concrete, imagine an excavation reveals a sealed subterranean chamber containing hundreds of intentionally shattered pottery sherds arranged in concentric rings, interspersed with imported obsidian flakes that show no microscopic wear from cutting or scraping. There are no human remains, no inscribed surfaces, and no architectural features that clearly mark the space as domestic, administrative, or funerary. Compare how the representational-encoding framework and the performative-stabilization framework would each interpret this assemblage to infer the underlying institution—whether it points to a centralized ritual authority, a distributed network of reciprocal obligations, or some other form of collective organization. Specifically, address how each framework justifies the inferential leap from physical pattern to collective intentionality, and identify the precise point at which each risks either over-interpreting absence (treating silence or fragmentation as deliberately encoded meaning) or under-interpreting structure (treating highly patterned practice as accidental residue). Finally, reach and defend a reasoned verdict about which framework offers the more defensible method for institutional reconstruction in cases where symbolic content and interactional residue are both present but neither is independently verifiable. Please write your response in American English.
