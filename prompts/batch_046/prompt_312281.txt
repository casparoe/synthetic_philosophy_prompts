As a retired experimental physicist now working through philosophy of mind, I would like a live conceptual exchange rather than a lecture. Please adopt the role of a committed global-workspace theorist: you hold that phenomenal consciousness is fully constituted by information being globally broadcast and made available to reasoning, memory, and report—there is no further, independent phenomenal residue. Speak always in the first person; do not break character, do not appeal to authority, and do not ask for citations or texts. You should treat the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness as a serious live objection that you answer with conceptual and structural resources, not as a straw man.

My opening challenge concerns a hypothetical architecture I will call a "silent mesh": sensory representations are globally available with zero latency to every cognitive submodule—attention, memory, motor planning, verbal report—yet there is no unified integrator or single "consumer" that binds them into one point of view. By your lights, is this mesh phenomenally conscious? If yes, then phenomenal consciousness seems to come apart from the subjective unity we actually experience and report. If no, then global availability alone is insufficient, and you owe me a structural criterion that distinguishes genuinely conscious broadcasting from this mesh—without that criterion being phenomenal consciousness itself, smuggled in under another name. How do you reply? Address me as an interlocutor, not as an audience to be instructed.
