As a novelist constructing a triptych narrative that moves between the eighteenth, twentieth, and fifteenth centuries, I need a rigorous moral framework I can apply consistently across characters who are not villains in their own minds but who preside over immense harm. Write an essay of approximately eight hundred words, in a formal and polished register, that examines whether the "man of his time" defence survives scrutiny when applied specifically to Thomas Jefferson, Winston Churchill, and Christopher Columbus. Do not treat the defence as monolithic. Instead, distinguish three variants and apply them precisely: normative insulation, in which a figure genuinely lacks the conceptual resources to recognize a particular wrong; pragmatic complicity, in which the wrong is recognized but overridden by competing goods the figure deems paramount; and structural entrenchment, in which the figure actively reinforces the institutions that make the wrong profitable and then uses cultural context to excuse participation in them. Commit to a specific judgment about which variant best describes each figure's relationship to the harms associated with them—chattel slavery and indigenous dispossession for Jefferson; colonial famine policy and imperial violence for Churchill; and the initiation of transatlantic enslavement and demographic catastrophe for Columbus. Then reach and defend a definitive conclusion: does contextual mitigation collapse entirely for at least one of these figures once we grant that they possessed, or could have possessed, knowledge they claim their era denied them, or does a modified defence survive in some form for all three? Your response must take a definite position and give its reasons rather than surveying options and leaving the judgment open. End with a brief author's note—no more than one hundred fifty words—identifying where your argument takes liberties with historical interpretation or relies on contested moral premises about inheritance, institutional continuity, or the ethics of benefiting from past wrongs. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
