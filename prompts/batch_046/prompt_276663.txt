I am a retired physicist who has only recently begun studying philosophy in a systematic way. In a recent essay I encountered the following characterization of Søren Kierkegaard's critique of the press and the public, apparently drawn from an 1840s review essay. The author writes that Kierkegaard conceives of the public as an emergent statistical entity, analogous to a thermodynamic ensemble, whose aggregate opinions cancel out individual commitment. By broadcasting every view to an anonymous mass, newspapers dissolve the qualitative distinction between speaker and listener, producing a leveling that is not merely social but informational: responsibility becomes diffused across the network like entropy through a closed system. Consequently, the author claims, Kierkegaard treats the press not as a neutral medium but as the primary causal engine of modern nihilism, and he urges a complete withdrawal from public discourse into inward, silent subjectivity as the only ethical remedy.

This account strikes me as philosophically vivid, yet I worry it may conflate several distinctions that are central to Kierkegaard's argument. In particular, I am unsure whether he truly treats the public as an empirical statistical aggregate rather than as a conceptual abstraction or phantom; whether he assigns causal primacy to the press itself rather than seeing it as a symptom of a broader age; and whether his remedy is as totalizing a withdrawal from all public discourse as the author suggests, or whether it is more precisely a transformation of the individual's relation to publicity. Could you please audit this summary? Separate what is accurate from what is subtly misrepresented or overstated, indicate what a defender of Kierkegaard's view would object to, and assess whether the conclusions drawn—especially the thermodynamic analogy and the claim about total withdrawal—survive correction. I should add that I do not have access to scholarly editions, bibliographies, or online resources, so please do not ask me to consult specific texts or provide citations; I am relying entirely on the conceptual structure of the argument.
