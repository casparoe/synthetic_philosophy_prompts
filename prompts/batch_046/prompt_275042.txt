I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, but I have been working through Richard Rorty’s antirepresentationalism and his argument that vocabularies function as practical tools rather than mirrors of an independent reality. I would like a villanelle—nineteen lines, two refrains, strict A1bA2 rhyme pattern—built around this idea. The first refrain should express that language does not copy or correspond to the world; the second should express that adopting or abandoning a vocabulary is a matter of practical efficacy, not of approaching representational truth. Use the poem’s recurring refrains to dramatize how the representationalist intuition keeps returning despite being displaced, and let the closing quatrain bring the two refrains together in a way that shows the shift from representation to redescription. Specifically, in the final stanzas, allow the rhyme words associated with each refrain to pair with different partners than they did earlier, so that the form itself enacts the claim that the same terms can be reused in a new vocabulary without gaining any new accuracy. The poem should treat Rorty’s rejection of the mirror of nature metaphor and his concept of a final vocabulary with enough precision that a reader familiar with his work recognizes the philosophical move rather than seeing a mere name-check, yet it should remain accessible to someone without specialist training. Please do not include citations, bibliographies, references to obscure secondary literature, or requests for further reading; the goal is for the philosophical content to do structural work inside the verse itself.
