Me and a friend got into it after skimming some old rationalist threads on causation. (1) She said interventionist accounts — Pearl-style do-operators — cant handle causation in abstract domains, like whether knowing a particular theorem causes me to solve a proof, since theres no physical intervention on a mathematical fact. (2) I countered that counterfactual dependence accounts do even worse, because they need impossible worlds where the theorem is false but my mental state is held constant, which violates logical necessity. (3) She said interventionists can just apply the do-operator to my mental representations instead, so the intervention targets brain states, not theorems. (4) I said thats just smuggling counterfactual brain-dependence back in and relabeling it as intervention. Please adjudicate move by move: rate each step and say what, if anything, both of us are missing — especially whether causation even applies to abstract objects or if this is all a category error.
