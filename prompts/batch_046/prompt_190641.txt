Write a dialogue between Dr. Aisha Rahman, a non-reductive physicalist, and Dr. Thomas Blake, a reductive physicalist, concerning whether the causal exclusion argument undermines mental causation. Focus the exchange specifically on mental states that possess what Rahman calls "diachronic structural integrity"—the claim that certain mental processes, such as the gradual resolution of cognitive dissonance over several seconds, possess explanatory and counterfactual relevance only as patterns distributed across time, rather than as properties fully instantiated at any single instant. Rahman should argue that the exclusion argument tacitly assumes causal competition at an instantaneous slice, where a complete physical cause already closes the causal chain; for diachronically integrated processes, she should contend, the mental property governs which temporal transitions are explanatorily relevant and sustains counterfactual dependencies that no individual physical slice carries on its own. Blake should respond that this merely relocates the exclusion problem: if each instantaneous physical state is causally sufficient for the next, then the extended mental pattern is either reducible to the sequence of physical slices or causally inert with respect to them, and attributing "structural integrity" to the mental level does not demonstrate that it performs any work the physical sequence does not already accomplish. Ensure that both participants respond directly to each other's actual points rather than speaking past one another, that neither is presented as a strawman, and that the discussion remains conceptually rigorous without invoking bibliographies, citations, reading lists, or highly specialised theories outside standard philosophy of mind. Please provide the dialogue in British English.
