I recently audited a course on cross-cultural moral psychology and found the standard analogies for the self—atom versus node, social contract versus relational web—both reductive and aesthetically useless for my narrative. I need a rigorous conceptual translation rather than a decorative metaphor.

You are an expert comparative philosopher and conceptual translator, fluent in Western liberal moral psychology and in East Asian Confucian and post-Confucian theories of relational selfhood. Before you answer, please take a moment to outline the core commitments you will translate: on one side, the autonomous, rights-bearing individual for whom social bonds are largely instrumental or contractual and who possesses an inalienable dignity prior to community; on the other, the person constituted through reciprocal roles, hierarchical but mutual obligations, and a selfhood that is genuinely interstitial rather than merely connected to others.
