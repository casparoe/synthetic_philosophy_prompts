I am a retired biologist who, since leaving the laboratory, has begun auditing philosophy seminars with the same attention I once gave to field notes, and I have now been asked to deliver the opening remarks at an interdisciplinary workshop on emergence and cognition. The room will be divided: roughly half will be philosophers of mind and cognitive scientists who have followed every iteration of the collective-intentionality debate, and the other half will be graduate students from adjacent fields and family members who have never encountered the primary literature. I need these remarks—approximately seven minutes—to do genuine structural work rather than decorative allusion: they should frame the workshop's central tension without resolving it, should be fully intelligible to the non-specialists, and should never pretend that empirical science has already settled the matter.

The tension I want to stage is the one raised by the classic thought experiment involving Aunt Hillary and the ant fugue—specifically, whether an ant colony can literally possess a mind, not merely behave as though it does. Rather than rehearse the standard debate about beliefs and desires distributed across insects, I want to propose an unconventional framing drawn from the musical structure itself: treat the colony as instantiating a kind of distributed fugue state in which individual ants function like voices that enter and exit a contrapuntal pattern with no central score-keeper, so that the colony might exhibit something analogous to experiential integration without possessing any substrate that resembles a brain. The remarks should trace the mapping between pheromonal coordination and neural synchronization, should identify precisely where that mapping breaks down—particularly regarding the absence of both a conductor and an audience for the fugue—and should close by leaving the workshop with an open problem: if there is no listener inside the colony and no listener outside it who can hear the whole, can we still speak of a mind, or only of a beautifully organized absence?

Please begin the response with the speech itself, with no preface or commentary.
