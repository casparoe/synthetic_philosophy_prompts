I've been trying to pin down my actual commitments on scientific reduction and emergence, working through concrete cases rather than recycling the standard taxonomy I keep seeing in philosophy-of-science syllabi. Here is the pattern of my verdicts, and I want to know whether a single principle can capture them, which ones I must abandon for simplicity, and what the result predicts for a boundary case I haven't settled.

First, temperature as mean kinetic energy feels like a complete reduction: the macro-property is nothing over and above the statistical distribution of micro-states, with no explanatory residue and no downward causal work being done by the temperature itself. Second, superconductivity strikes me as genuinely emergent in an explanatory sense. Even with full BCS-theory knowledge of electron-phonon interactions, the drop to exactly zero resistance at the critical temperature seems to require invoking a collective symmetry-breaking order parameter that is not present in any individual particle Hamiltonian; yet I am unsure whether this is true downward causation or just an especially complex derivation. Third, consciousness arising from neural dynamics feels different again—not merely hard to compute, but as if subjective experience introduces an explanatory gap that collective physical order parameters do not. Still, I worry I am just privileging the mental out of ignorance. Fourth, the gene-centered reduction of organism-level fitness to differential gene replication seems to me to fail, but for the reverse reason: the organism's phenotype actively constrains which genes survive, so treating fitness as purely downward from genes gets the causal arrow wrong. Fifth, consider a synthetic protocell engineered to maintain homeostasis only within a specific chemical gradient. Every molecular interaction is deterministic and fully modeled, yet the homeostatic boundary conditions appear to select which reactions occur. Is this emergence like superconductivity, downward causation like biology, or merely complicated mechanics?

My tentative principle is that emergence occurs precisely when a macro-pattern is both not derivable from isolated part-behavior without reference to the system's boundary conditions, and exerts downward constraint on those parts. Under this framing, temperature fails the first condition because statistical mechanics derives it without special boundary conditions; superconductivity satisfies the first via symmetry breaking but perhaps fails the second because the order parameter does not constrain individual electrons; consciousness satisfies both if phenomenal states genuinely constrain neural dynamics; and the gene case satisfies downward constraint but fails derivability in reverse, since the macro-level is causally primary. The principle captures my pattern, but it forces me to classify the protocell differently depending on whether I treat the gradient as an external boundary or an internal constraint. Is there a sharper formulation—perhaps distinguishing explanatory autonomy from causal downwardness—that saves my verdicts without ad hoc adjustments? If I must drop one verdict to preserve a simple principle, which should go, and what does the refined account then say about the protocell?
