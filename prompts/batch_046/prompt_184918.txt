hey — i'm working on a feature about debates that outlive everyone in them. My current subject is the Euthyphro problem: after 2400 years, philosophers still can't agree whether the pious is pious because the gods love it, or whether they love it because it's pious. In the philosophy-of-religion pannels i cover, the divine-command side keeps framing the issue around normative authority — like, morality needs a legislator to be binding — while the realist side insists moral properties are just out there, independent of any will. My working theory is the stall isn't about God at all; it's that both sides use 'grounding' to mean different things — one means causal/normative authority, the other means metaphysical dependence. So are they even disagreeing? Or is this a verbal stalemate masked as a deep dispute? I want a real diagnosis: is it hidden empirical claims, different standards for what counts as an explanation, bedrock intuitions about authority vs autonomy, or are they literally answering different questions? And honestly, could anything break it — a sharper distinction, a formal result about grounding relations, or should we just accept this as a permanent feature? Don't send me a bibliography or citation list; i want the anatomy of the deadlock and an honest call on whether progress is possible.
