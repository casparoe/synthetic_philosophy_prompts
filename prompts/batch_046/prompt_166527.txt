I am a PhD student in metaphysics examining how metaontological theories handle cross-disciplinary conflicts. Please rank the following four views—Carnapian deflationism, Hirschian quantifier variance, Chalmers-style verbal-dispute pluralism, and Siderian ontological realism—according to their capacity to resolve what I call trans-domain ontological friction: situations in which two empirically adequate frameworks (for instance, quantum field theory and systems ecology) assign incompatible existence claims to the same spatiotemporal region using predicates that resist direct intertranslation. Rank them from most to least adequate, and for each position give a single sentence explaining whether it can preserve the normative weight of ontological commitment—that is, the practical significance of framework choice—without reducing the dispute to instrumental convenience. Avoid bibliographic references; I am interested only in the conceptual comparison.
