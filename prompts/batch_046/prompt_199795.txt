Write a short dialogue between Mara, who argues that an “I” can genuinely be distributed across two living brains, and Jory, who denies this, both reasoning within Hofstadter’s framework of the self as a strange loop sustained by symbolic feedback rather than by specific tissue. Mara should claim that if the loop is essentially a pattern, then sustained bidirectional neural integration—say, a direct cortical bridge allowing shared real-time perceptual streams and jointly authored episodic memories—could produce one self with two substrates, provided the symbolic circuit remains unified. Jory should respond that even on Hofstadter’s view the loop requires a singular temporal binding site: without one continuous “now” that weaves experiences into a single autobiography, there are merely two overlapping loops exchanging data, not one distributed self. Have them apply this to a concrete scenario: two people linked by such an interface share every conscious perception and co-construct each memory, yet each retains independent subcortical emotional regulation and distinct bodily homeostasis. Mara should insist that the shared symbolic loop dominates and integrates them into one narrative center; Jory should argue that the emotional and homeostatic divergence fractures that center, leaving two selves with shared content rather than one self spread across two brains. Both should be equally competent, and each should answer the other’s actual point rather than talk past it.
