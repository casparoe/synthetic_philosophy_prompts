In debates about rational choice people keep comparing satisficing to shopping with a broken scale: you can't weigh every option precisely so you grab the first item that feels heavy enough, and they use this to argue rationality is just good-enough reasoning rather than optimization. Audit that analogy for me: give me the correspondence table (shopper, goods, scale, heavy-enough threshold), show where it breaks down particulary on whether the brokenness is an unavoidable cognitive limit or a failure to repair the scale, and say whether the relaxed conclusion actually survives or if the analogy was smuggling it in all along.
