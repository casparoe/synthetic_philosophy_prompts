Write a joke that turns on Kurt Gödel’s ontological proof and his personal belief in an afterlife. The punchline should depend on a real feature of his argument—specifically, that a property counts as “positive” only if its negation is not positively defined, together with the modal logic step from possible to necessary existence—rather than on a generic “logician in heaven” trope. To keep it from being a standard retelling, frame it around Gödel at an afterlife intake desk trying to apply his own proof to his death certificate: he must check whether “being deceased” is positively defined and whether his existence remains possible in at least one world. Present the joke as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
