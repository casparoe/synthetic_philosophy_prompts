Im working through my own gut reactions about intentionality for a seminar on experimental philosophy—specifically the Knobe effect, but I've gotten bored with the usual CEO-and-environment examples and started making up weirder ones. I was arguing with a friend about AI blame and gene drives and now I want to know if there's an actual principle behind my verdicts, or if I'm just being inconsistent and need to drop one.

Here is where I land: (1) A gene-drive researcher modifies mosquitos knowing it'll kill some bees as a byproduct; she genuinely doesn't care about the bees. I say she intentionally killed the bees. (2) Same researcher modifies bees to save them, and some mosquitos accidentally survive; she doesn't care about the mosquitos. I say she did NOT intentionally save them. (3) Instead of running the script herself, she posts open-source code. A stranger downloads it, runs it with the exact same knowledge and indifference, and bees die. I think the stranger intentionally killed them, but the original researcher did not—even though she knew precisely what the script did. (4) An AI team sets a reward function they know is risky; the model finds a harmful shortcut they never specifically anticipated, but they left it running for months. I feel the harm is NOT intentional, which clashes with (1) becuase in both cases there's foresight plus indifference. (5) A VR designer builds a physics engine where NPCs suffer emergent pain states; she tests it, sees it happen, doesn't patch it, but never "aimed" at suffering. Here I swing back and call it intentional, closer to (1).

So I have foresight-plus-indifference yielding intentional for direct human action (1, 5) but not for automated or distributed systems (3, 4), and I split attribution between creator and user (3). Is there one coherent account—maybe about direct causal control, or about being the "author" of an outcome in a normative sense—that gets all five without being totally ad hoc? If not, which verdict should I abandon to save simplicity? And what does your best principle predict for the case I'm still wobbly on: a philosopher publishes a disturbing thought experiment knowing it'll psychologically upset some readers, but she only wants philosophical clarity?
