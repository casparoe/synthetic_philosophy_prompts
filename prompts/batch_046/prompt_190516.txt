A philosophy blog summarizes the three classic answers to the epistemic regress problem like this. Foundationalists halt the regress by identifying basic beliefs that are self-justifying: they need no further support because they are grounded in direct perceptual acquaintance or in rational intuition. Coherentists reject the linear picture altogether and replace it with holistic mutual support—every belief derives its warrant from belonging to a maximally coherent system, with no belief being epistemically prior, which means circular support among pairs of beliefs is not a bug but a feature of the web. Infinitists concede that justification requires an infinite, never-ending chain of reasons; but they argue this is not vicious because each link in the chain is fully justified by its predecessor, and an infinite past is metaphysically complete, so there is no missing foundation—only a foundation that is never reached. The blogger then claims that infinitism is therefore just foundationalism with the base belief relocated to an unreachable infinity, and that all three views actually agree there must be a linear, one-directional order of justification, differing only on where the order stops. How fair is this summary? Go view by view and tell me what a defender would accept, what they would correct—especially about whether coherentism permits circular pairwise support and whether infinitism’s chain is genuinely linear rather than branching or holistic—and whether the blogger’s final claim that the three positions share a linear-order assumption is defensible or collapses important distinctions.
