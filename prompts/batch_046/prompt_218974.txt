Write a Petrarchan sonnet—octave with rhyme scheme ABBAABBA, sestet CDECDE, and a strict volta at line 9—about a modified version of Thomson’s lamp that contains an internal register. In the lamp’s protocol, it begins on and then toggles at intervals of one minute, one-half minute, one-quarter minute, and so on, with each change inscribed in the register. By the limit point at two minutes, the register holds a completed infinite sequence of entries, yet the lamp itself has no further moment at which to be switched. In the octave, establish the physical rhythm of the toggling and the accumulating log, letting the forward motion of the rhyme mirror the halving intervals. At the volta, shift abruptly from describing the process to interrogating the logical status of the register at the limit: ask whether an infinite, fully determined past record can fix the lamp’s present state when no final toggle occurs. In the sestet, work through the tension between a complete history and an unsettled present—do not resolve it into a sermon about infinity, but let the logical gap remain open. The closing couplet should state the precise philosophical consequence: that physical determination at a limit point requires something other than an infinite sequence of prior states, or that the notion of “the state at the limit” is not fixed by the sequence that approaches it. The poem should be structurally accurate enough that someone familiar with supertasks recognizes the mapping—the accelerating toggles compressed into the octave’s closing, the volta as the transition from process to limit—while remaining coherent and moving for a reader encountering Thomson’s lamp for the first time. Do not invoke external authorities or citations; derive the paradox solely from the internal logic of the lamp and its register.
