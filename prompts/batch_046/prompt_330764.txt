Write a villanelle spoken by the two hands in Escher's Drawing Hands, treating the image not as a generic metaphor for self-reference but as a specific problem for consciousness science: whether any explanatory substrate—say, neural dynamics—can remain an inviolate level untouched by the tangled hierarchy it explains, or whether every level is caught in the loop. Make Refrain A assert that one hand draws without being drawn; make Refrain B deny any such exemption, insisting every drawer is also drawn. Through five tercets, have the hands reverse roles—each claiming to be the observer, then the observed—so that the forced repetition feels like the strange loop itself rather than decoration. In the final quatrain, bring both refrains together so the reader cannot tell which hand is speaking or whether the inviolate level has collapsed into the loop. It should be accurate enough that someone familiar with Hofstadter's discussion recognizes the specific issue, yet stand as poetry for a reader who knows only the image; do not include citations, bibliography, or reading list.
