ok so I've been trying to square my takes on the gender-equality paradox and I'm not sure they hang together. Roughly where I've landed:

(1) I think the Nordic data is real — more gender-equal countries show bigger gender splits in career choice (STEM vs care/education), not smaller.

(2) So the "pure socialization" story — culture forces women out of technical fields — can't be the full picture, since equality should reduce the gap if culture were the only driver.

(3) Justice means removing barriers and giving equal opportunity, not enforcing statistical parity in every field. If gaps stay after barriers fall, that's not automatically unjust.

(4) But I also think "preference" isn't pre-cultural raw material. In wealthy egalitarian societies people can afford to follow gender-typed tastes because economic survival doesn't push them across gender lines for pay. So the paradox might just show culture working differently — people expressing permitted stereotypes, not nature breaking through.

(5) Even so, I want aggressive counter-stereotyping in school career counseling, because autonomy requires options that aren't filtered through gender norms.

(6) And I worry we're comparing apples and oranges — "equality" gets measured by policy inputs (leave laws, quotas) not lived social norms, so the correlation might be spurious.

My question: is (2) compatible with (4)? Does (3) actually commit me to reject (5), or is that only an apparent conflict? And is there a hidden problem with holding (1) and (6) together — can I cite the data while doubting the measurement? What's the cheapest consistency fix: weaken (2), drop (4), or rethink (5)? I don't know wether the weak link is (2) or if I'm just misapplying (4).
