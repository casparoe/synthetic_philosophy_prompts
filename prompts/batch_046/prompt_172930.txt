I'm working through a seminar on revealed preference with a philosopher who has taken explicit positions on whether behavior reveals underlying utility in five kinds of edge case. She denies that revealed preference applies when an addict reports hating her consumption and describes it as compulsive rather than chosen; when a subject in an experiment selects the option she previously ranked lower, explaining that she did so specifically to avoid being predicted; and when a person makes one choice in private and the opposite choice when observed, regardless of which she claims to "really" want. By contrast, she accepts revealed preference for a consumer who consistently pays a substantial premium for privacy despite cheaper alternatives; for a buyer who abandons a long-favored brand immediately after a price increase, even while insisting she remains loyal; and for a voter who supports economically harmful policies after demonstrating full comprehension of the trade-off, provided she does not claim to be mistaken about her own interests.

My study group is now divided over a sixth scenario: a well-known public intellectual who, over decades, has adopted the maximally contrarian position in nearly every major debate in her field, enduring sustained social ostracism and professional exclusion, but who has privately told close colleagues that she does not actually hold these views and maintains them solely to "prevent premature consensus." She bears real, avoidable costs, and her pattern is stable across audiences—she is contrarian even in small private seminars—yet she explicitly disavows the positions as expressions of her own judgment. Would our philosopher say this reveals a preference for contrarianism, for social martyrdom, or for some other end such as epistemic hygiene? I am less interested in the simple classification than in the competing principles that could organize her five earlier verdicts—perhaps reflective endorsement versus costly commitment versus cross-contextual coherence—what each predicts about this intellectual, which analog among her accepted cases is closest, and what further case would best discriminate between these organizing rules. If you recommend one verdict for consistency with her track record, identify the principle she would be committing to either way. Finally, please indicate where your reasoning is most likely to fail: what feature of her pattern, or of this new case, would most change your recommendation?
