I am working through a problem for my philosophy of language seminar on modal epistemology, and I have reconstructed an argument that seems to show the Kripkean framework allowing necessary a posteriori truths is internally unstable. The argument runs as follows. (1) If 'Hesperus' and 'Phosphorus' are rigid designators naming the same object, then the sentence 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' expresses exactly the same proposition as 'Hesperus is Hesperus'. (2) Call this propositional transparency: if two sentences express the same proposition, then the epistemic conditions for knowing one are identical to those for knowing the other, evaluated de dicto. (3) The proposition expressed by 'Hesperus is Hesperus' is knowable purely by reflection, without empirical investigation. (4) By (1) and (2), the proposition expressed by 'Hesperus is Phosphorus' is therefore also knowable purely by reflection. (5) Yet we clearly learn that Hesperus is Phosphorus through astronomical observation, not by reflection alone. (6) So either empirical observation is unnecessary for this necessary identity, which is false, or rigid designators fail to secure propositional identity across epistemic contexts, which undermines direct-reference semantics. (7) Hence the distinction between necessary a priori and necessary a posteriori collapses into contradiction.

I feel the error lies somewhere between (2) and (4), possibly involving an equivocation between semantic content and cognitive mode of presentation, or a hidden scope ambiguity about whether epistemic accessibility is evaluated de dicto or de re; but I cannot locate the precise slip. I am particularly reluctant to surrender (1), since the rigidity of proper names is central to the view I am defending, though I am open to seeing that (2) conceals a modal slide. Could you identify the weakest step, explain why it initially looks sound, and then price what each main alternative costs — especially what rejecting (2) implies for direct-reference theories, and whether (6) presents a false dichotomy?
