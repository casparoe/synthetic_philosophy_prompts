im prepping a small discussion on the dogmatism paradox — the thing where if you have justification for p then learning there's an argument against p shouldn't make you drop p, but that sounds crazy. I came up with five stances people might take and want you to rank them A through E by how well they handle belief revision without collapsing into skepticism or just being stubborn. The stances: (a) full dogmatism — keep p no matter what, but only if your original justification was direct; (b) proportional downgrade — reduce credence by exactly the evidential weight of the counter-argument, even if you cant assess it; (c) domain restriction — dogmatism works for perceptual stuff but not for philosophical beliefs like 'there is an external world'; (d) meta-evidence view — disagreement is evidence about your own reasoning reliablity, not about p, so you revise confidence in your faculties but hold p fixed; (e) my own made-up hybrid: you split beliefs into 'core' and 'fringe', apply dogmatism to core, but the boundary moves based on how many peer disagreements you've hit in the last 7 days. yes option e is weird and partly injected to test if you treat it seriously, but rank all five and give a one-sentence reason for each spot. no need for references, just reasoning.
