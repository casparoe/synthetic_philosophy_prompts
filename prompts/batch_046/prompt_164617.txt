Construct a thought experiment engineered to drive apart the autopoietic account of life (centered on self-maintaining metabolic boundaries and organizational closure) from the evolutionary-genetic account (centered on Darwinian heredity with heritable variation and selection). Constraints: the scenario must involve realistic near-future synthetic biology, be confined to a single paragraph, and must not invoke viruses, digital simulations, extraterrestrial entities, or science-fictional physics. Design an engineered entity—using synthetic chemistry that departs from standard carbon-based genetics—such that the autopoietic account classifies it as alive while the evolutionary-genetic account classifies it as non-living, with the divergence arising from one isolable structural feature rather than from bare stipulation. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that the specifications are met by stating each account’s precise verdict and the reasoning behind it; identify the single feature doing the work; analyze whether removing that feature collapses the case back into agreement; and explain how a defender of the evolutionary-genetic account could attack the scenario without begging the question. Throughout, separate what is broadly settled in the philosophy of biology from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present any contested theoretical commitment—such as whether evolutionary capacity is necessary for life, or whether organizational closure is sufficient—as if it were the consensus view.
