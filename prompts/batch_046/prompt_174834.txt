I am a philosopher of science with substantial fluency in the formal vocabulary of statistical mechanics—microcanonical and canonical ensembles, entropy as a measure over accessible microstates, coarse-graining over unobserved degrees of freedom, and the derivation of macroscopic observables from underlying distributions. I want you to restate the scale argument concerning insect sentience—the contention that, given the astronomical number of insects, even a minuscule prior probability that any given insect possesses phenomenal consciousness aggregates into a vast expected moral weight—exclusively in those terms. Avoid entirely the language of utility, expected value, moral patienthood, or subjective probability. After providing the most faithful translation you can, deliver a residue report: identify which elements of the argument map cleanly onto ensemble-theoretic concepts, which can only be preserved by distorting those concepts (for instance, treating moral significance as an extensive quantity analogous to energy or particle number), and which central commitments—particularly the aggregation of discrete subjective states across individual organisms—lack any counterpart in statistical mechanics. Finally, evaluate whether the residue indicates a substantive metaphysical presupposition hidden within the scale argument, such as the additivity of consciousness across individuals, or whether the failure of translation merely shows that normative aggregation is not a thermodynamic phenomenon.
