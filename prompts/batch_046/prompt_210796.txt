Here is an argument I constructed for a graduate discussion of Kant's Doctrine of Virtue, and I am suspicious of it because the conclusion seems either to collapse indirect duties into direct ones or to make Kantian ethics implausibly demanding without granting animals any standing. (1) Kant denies direct duties to non-rational animals because obligation requires autonomy, so animals cannot be ends in themselves. (2) Our duties regarding animals are strictly indirect: cruelty is wrong only because it hardens our dispositions and makes us more likely to violate duties to rational beings. (3) If the wrongness of an act tracks solely its tendency to damage our moral character in this way, then any practice producing the same desensitization should carry the same normative weight, regardless of whether it involves direct physical violence. (4) Many modern practices—industrial animal agriculture, large-scale behavioral extraction that treats responsive beings as pure data inputs, even certain competitive pedagogies—produce precisely this desensitization structurally, without anyone laying a hand on a creature. (5) So by parity of reasoning, Kant's framework should condemn these structural practices as severely as it condemns beating a horse. (6) But if the condemnation is severe while the affected parties still possess no direct moral standing, then the wrong is located entirely in our self-cultivation; and if we could compartmentalize our sentiments so that our character toward humans remained intact, the practices would become permissible. (7) Thus the argument seems to prove that Kant must either grant direct standing to animals and other affected beings, or accept that his indirect-duty view is not a genuine moral constraint on treatment but merely a prudential rule about emotional hygiene. Where does the argument slip? I would prefer to keep (1) and (2) intact; must I reject the parity in (4)–(5), deny that (3) captures Kant's reasoning correctly, or accept (6)–(7) and revise my understanding of indirect duty? And what does each rejection cost?
