I've come to hold a view about consequentialist cluelessness that pushes past the standard skeptical conclusion. Rather than treating our inability to forecast distant outcomes as a practical limitation that merely forces a retreat to rules or heuristics, I think it marks a principled boundary: moral evaluation should be restricted to what I call an agent's 'epistemic neighborhood,' the set of outcomes structurally coupled to their actual deliberative capacities rather than globally distributed across space and time. On this account, a choice is justified not by approximating global welfare maximization but by exhibiting coherence within the agent's accessible reasoning framework, even when distant catastrophic effects lie outside any modelable horizon. This is meant as a normative claim, not a pragmatic fallback — responsibility tracks the architecture of cognitive access rather than causal reach.
