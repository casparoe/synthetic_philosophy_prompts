ok so I've been thinking about Hofstadter's idea that the 'I' is just a strange loop symbol that points back at itself, with no little homunculus running the show. I'm definatly mostly on board—like, it makes sense that selfhood is just this pattern of representations looping back rather than some ghost in the machine.

But I also hold that personal identity is basically psychological continuity; if your memories and personality traits carry over into a new substrate, that's you, even if the loop gets rebuilt differently. And I think that if an AI ever achieved genuine self-reference—not just saying 'I' in output but having its internal representations actually loop back like ours—it should count as having the same kind of self. At the same time, I can't shake the feeling that consciousness needs real biological feedback, not just computation on silicon, so maybe an AI could have the loop without being conscious?

My commitments are roughly: (1) the self is a strange loop with no extra soul, (2) identity over time is psychological continuity, (3) true self-reference in an AI grants it an 'I', and (4) consciousness requires embodied biological processes. Do these hang together? It feels like (3) and (4) are fighting, and maybe (2) doesn't need (1) at all—like I could believe in continuity without buying the loop story. Which tension is actually fatal and which is just awkward? I don't know which one I'd drop if pushed, so don't go easy on me.
