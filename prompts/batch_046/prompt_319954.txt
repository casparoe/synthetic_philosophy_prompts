I'm a PhD candidate in moral philosophy advising an institutional ethics board that reviews governance mechanisms introducing structural anonymity. Rather than reading Glaucon's ring of Gyges as a purely psychological test of individual justice, I treat it as modeling a structural condition—call it Gygean opacity—in which agents can act without being identifiable with their actions, thereby suspending the reciprocity conditions that make moral cooperation rational. Drawing on the contractualist idea that responding to Glaucon requires showing morality remains stable only when agents remain visible to one another as potential partners in cooperation, please turn this principle into a reusable decision procedure the board can run on any proposal that creates Gygean opacity (e.g., encrypted deliberation, blinded arbitration, unaccountable whistleblower channels). Specify the ordered questions to ask, what counts as a sufficient answer at each stage, where the procedure issues a clear reject, where it halts because the issue is irreducibly value-laden and must be signed off by a human board member, and the known failure modes where it is likely to misfire. Then apply it to three cases: (1) an encrypted anonymous platform allowing municipal budget amendments without attribution; (2) a blinded peer-review system in which reviewers can unilaterally reject funding applications without disclosing conflicts of interest; and (3) a whistleblower protocol granting temporary anonymity but including automatic de-anonymization if the accusation proves false and harmful. Lead with a one-sentence bottom line stating the core decision rule, then present the full procedure and worked applications. Keep the response to approximately 500 words (about one page).
