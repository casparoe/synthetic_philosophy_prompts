I serve on the Canonical Records Committee for the International Archive of Foundational Jurisprudence, and after a long, unresolved argument last night with a close friend who also sits on the body, I need a polished draft for Thursday's session. We face a concrete admission case: two authenticated, contemporaneous legislative texts—both bearing valid state seals dated March 1847—explicitly contradict one another on the scope of suffrage. One extends the franchise to all property-holding adults regardless of gender; the other restricts it to male property-holders. Forensic and historical scholarship confirms neither is a forgery; both were genuinely enacted by rival assemblies claiming full constitutional authority during a transitional interregnum. Our charter currently requires logical consistency for canonical admission, interpreted classically: if records contradict, at least one must be designated erroneous or excluded.

The philosophical issue is whether there are genuinely true contradictions in the legal-historical record, and whether institutional recognition of them requires global abandonment of consistency or can be managed through localized paraconsistent protocols—specifically, non-adjunctive indexing within a relevance-logical framework that contains contradictions without allowing them to propagate across unrelated domains. My friend, who defends a classical view of rational belief, argued that even localized dialetheism is institutionally unstable: once we permit one true contradiction, we lose the normative boundary between warranted and unwarranted inference, because any block on explosion appears ad hoc unless we already accept that contradictions can be true. He further insists that the archive's credibility depends on a single, non-contradictory canonical record, and that allowing both texts invites epistemic chaos in retrieval. I concede that his credibility concern is serious, and I am not fully convinced that adaptive containment strategies solve the problem rather than merely relocating it; but I want the committee to consider whether a dialetheist framing—treating the conflict as a localized truth-value glut rather than an error—might preserve both texts without logical collapse.

Please draft the formal written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action—approve both with specified containment conditions, reject both, or designate one canonical and the other as disputed—together with a precise implementation time frame for the archive's technical staff. The reasoning must engage both the dialetheist option and the classical consistency requirement honestly, assess whether relevance-based or adaptive indexing actually prevents explosion or only masks it, and include a direct, charitable treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side, particularly the worry that institutional legitimacy requires a unified, non-contradictory record. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the committee needs arguments it can evaluate on their own merits, not references members cannot verify without external access.
