I'm presenting next week at an interdisciplinary philosophy-of-mind and cognitive-science seminar. My thesis is that Dennett's multiple drafts model, taken literally as denying any privileged temporal window for conscious integration, cannot explain cross-modal binding in rapid serial visual presentation tasks without implicitly smuggling in a minimal "staggered broadcast" mechanism—one that is neither a full Cartesian theater nor a purely distributed draft, but a transient, modality-spanning synchronization point that persists only for the duration of the binding event and then dissolves back into parallel streams. The audience will be about fifteen people: three philosophers sympathetic to Dennett's heterophenomenology, two global workspace theorists committed to a broadcasting architecture for consciousness, several cognitive neuroscientists who study temporal binding and RSVP paradigms, and a few graduate students. Prepare my Q&A defense: list the eight or so objections I will actually face, ordered by how likely and how damaging they are, each phrased the way a Dennettian, a global-workspace advocate, or a neuroscientist in that room would put it. For each objection, indicate what I should concede up front, what I must hold, the weakest point in my reply, and whether the challenge targets my empirical claim about RSVP or my conceptual claim about the absence of a theater. Include the one question I am hoping nobody asks—most likely whether my staggered broadcast is just a Cartesian theater with a shorter lifespan—together with the two-minute reply I should have ready and an explicit note on where that reply is soft. In your response, separate what is settled among specialists (for instance, that Dennett rejects a central observer and that RSVP reveals tight temporal integration windows) from what remains genuinely contested (for instance, whether such integration requires a unified workspace or can be achieved by distributed draft coordination), and do not present any contested interpretation as the consensus view.
