As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I am designing a fifty-minute workshop for a small group of policy reporters—intelligent generalists with no formal philosophy training—who consistently conflate "lacking standing to blame" with "being a hypocrite" in their coverage of institutional corruption. I want you, acting as a professional philosopher of ethics, to plan the explanation rather than deliver it: map the conceptual dependencies first, then think carefully through the sequence.

The idea must be introduced in strict order: first, "standing" as a normative entitlement distinct from psychological authority or legal jurisdiction; second, hypocrisy as blaming others for a shared fault without appropriate self-account or remedy; third, the interaction between them, emphasizing that hypocrisy often undermines standing but is neither necessary nor sufficient for its loss. The carrying example should be a paired institutional comparison: a municipal water-director who fines residents for unpermitted wells while maintaining her own, versus a state auditor who once filed taxes late but now investigates tax fraud. The first case must carry hypocrisy plus lost standing; the second must show shared fault that does not destroy standing because the fault differs in kind and has been remediated. Identify the misconceptions these reporters will predictably form—likely that "clean hands" is a formal legal doctrine, that hypocrisy automatically cancels all blame, and that standing requires moral purity—and specify exactly where in the sequence to defuse each.

Then provide a brief understanding-check—perhaps a new scenario they must classify as hypocrisy, lost standing, neither, or both—plus explicit instructions on what to leave out: do not discuss moral luck, the meta-ethics of blame, or the historical literature; state that omission plainly. Finally, inject one nuanced but teachable twist: the problem of "structural hypocrisy," in which an institution blames individuals for systemic harms it perpetuates, and clarify whether this represents lost standing, hypocrisy, or a distinct failure altogether. Keep the entire plan under 400 words, structured as dependency order, the paired example with its required features, the misconception map with defusion points, the check, and the omissions.
