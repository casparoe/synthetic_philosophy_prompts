I've been thinking about what should happen when two people who seem equally sharp and well-informed disagree—say, over whether a particular act was genuinely wrong, or whether we have free will. One camp says you should both become less confident and meet in the middle; the other says you can stand firm if you still trust your own reasoning. This debate seems to have gone in circles for years without resolution. My suspicion is that the stall hides two deeper splits: first, over what "equally sharp" actually requires—same access to facts, same reasoning speed, or same intuitive reactions; second, over whether the question is about managing your beliefs in the moment or about tracking the actual truth. Could it be that one side is really describing social coordination, while the other is describing individual rationality, and they share only a label? Diagnose the disagreement using what each position actually commits to, and tell me whether a sharper distinction—perhaps between being peers in evidence versus peers in judgment—would actually break the deadlock, or whether the impasse shows that the original question is ill-posed.
