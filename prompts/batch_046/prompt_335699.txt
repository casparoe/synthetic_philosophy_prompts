my roommate tried to explain the fink vs mask distinction after skimming a philosophy blog. he said: "finks are external interrupters — like that classic wire case where a device detects contact and instantly removes the live charge, so the wire never conducts. masks are internal blockers, like a glass whose fragility is covered over by a protective coating that's part of the object itself. the point of finks is that they show dispositions need an intrinsic categorical base, because if there were no base the fink couldn't remove it at the last second. masks show the base can stay present but be occluded by another intrinsic feature. together this means every disposition is just a categorical base plus a ceteris paribus clause, so the debate over whether dispositions are fundamental properties is basically solved — it's all just bases plus conditions. oh, and there are these reverse-fink cases where the disposition only appears because of an external trigger, which proves the same thing from the other direction."

something about this feels too tidy. is it fair? go through it piece by piece: is the internal/external split accurate, what do finks actually show about categorical bases, does the ceteris-paribus move really dissolve the fundamentality question, and are reverse-finks actually used that way in the literature? i don't need citations, just want to know what a philosopher of science would wince at, and where thier conclusion doesn't follow.
