I was chatting with a mate last night after seeing that really bleak play at the local theatre, and we got into why people actually seek out stuff that makes them cry. I’ve come to lean towards a kind of affective displacement view: the sadness we feel during tragedy isn’t really about the characters’ suffering in any direct sense, but is instead a self-referential signal about our own capacity for deep emotional resonance, and the pleasure comes from integrating that signal into our self-concept rather than from the negative feeling itself. So the enjoyment is basically meta-cognitive — we’re not enjoying the grief, we’re enjoying the proof that we can grieve authentically, seperate from the narrative content. I definately don’t think this is just catharsis rebranded, but I could be wrong.
