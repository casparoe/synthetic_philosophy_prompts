For this exercise, extend the thought experiment in a specific direction: imagine the lamp is not binary but governed by an infinitely graded dimmer, so that every toggle shifts it one step along an unending scale of luminosity rather than flipping between two discrete states. The question then becomes whether any coherent limit-state—any particular shade or intensity—can be assigned at all once the infinite progression is complete, given that there is no final toggle to fix the outcome.

Write a sestina that takes this modified lamp as its central image. Choose six end-words arranged as paired gradations of presence and absence—for instance, light, shadow, yes, no, full, void. Allow the sestina's spiraling repetition to mirror the accelerating, ever-denser toggles approaching the limit-point, so that each stanza's return feels closer to t=2 without ever reaching a terminal line. In the envoi, pair each end-word with its own counterpart in such a way that the final couplet leaves the lamp grammatically suspended—neither affirming nor denying any single state—thereby performing structurally the claim that the supertask completes without yielding a determinate result. The poem should function as verse in its own right, but someone familiar with the debate should recognize exactly which feature of the limit-state problem is being enacted in the form.
