You serve on an institutional review board that must assign one of five gradated protection tiers—full rights-bearing status, high-intervention threshold, moderate welfare consideration, minimal harm avoidance, or purely instrumental treatment—to any proposed system or organism that interacts with human environments. The board has adopted a working principle called differentiated phenomenal integration, which holds that moral status scales with both the coherence of an entity's internal information processing and the presence of negatively valenced states that are not reducible to functional damage reports. Your task is to formalize this principle into a repeatable decision procedure that the board can apply in under thirty minutes per case, with ordered diagnostic questions, explicit stopping conditions for each tier, and clearly marked judgment-call nodes where the principle runs out and human values must take over. The procedure must handle biological entities such as social insects, synthetic architectures such as adaptive language models with persistent contextual memory, and hybrid or edge cases such as distributed climate-control networks with predictive feedback loops. Apply the procedure to three cases: a honeybee colony engaged in collective thermoregulation, a large language model running with persistent contextual memory across sessions, and a smart-building HVAC system that learns occupant preferences and adjusts airflow to prevent discomfort. In constructing the procedure, inject a specific constraint: the evaluation unit is not always the individual substrate but may be the functional network, and you must include a recursive self-modeling checkpoint calibrated differently for carbon-based versus silicon-based architectures. Present the procedure as a numbered sequence of tests, then show how each of the three cases traverses it, flagging exactly where the procedure yields a clear tier assignment and where it halts for board discretion.
