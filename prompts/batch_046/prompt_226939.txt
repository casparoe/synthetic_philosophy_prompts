Write an essay on whether Dennett's compatibilist defense of moral responsibility can survive Caruso's hard-determinist critique of just deserts in punishment theory. Dennett maintains that determinism does not erase the distinctions—between manipulation, accident, and genuine agency—that ground reactive attitudes and limited retributive practices; Caruso denies basic desert entirely and advocates replacing punishment with a public-health quarantine model justified solely by protection and deterrence. Assess whether Dennett's account implicitly requires that wrongdoers metaphysically "earn" suffering in a way Caruso's view rules out, or whether Dennett can retreat to a purely forward-looking, institutional justification without abandoning his commitment to personal responsibility. In particular, consider whether reconceiving desert as an institutional property—assigned by a justice system under epistemic constraints rather than discovered in an agent's metaphysical standing—allows a reconciliation, or whether Dennett's reliance on the reality of moral agency forces him to retain some form of personal desert that Caruso must reject. Reach and defend a definite conclusion.
