By "brain size" I mean not only cranial volume but any closely related neural metric people might invoke: absolute brain mass, encephalization quotient, total cortical neuron count, synaptic density scaled by volume, or connectivity measures that scale with size. That means any adequate theory must be able to adjudicate conflicts between these measures—say, a large but neuron-sparse brain versus a small but densely packed one—rather than silently picking one proxy.

Write the specification: what must a theory linking brain size (or these proxies) to moral status or to graded moral consideration actually get right? Identify the distinctions the theory must not collapse—especially between brain size as an intrinsic bearer of value versus as a mere proxy for sentience or reasoning capacity, and between sharp status thresholds versus continuous gradations of consideration.
