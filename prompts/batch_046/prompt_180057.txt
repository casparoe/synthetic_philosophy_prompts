After a long argument with a friend about whether Confucian role ethics necessarily collapses into kinship partiality outside intimate circles, I have been considering a specific conceptual shift. Rather than grounding differential care in biological or hierarchical proximity, suppose we reinterpret it as tracking epistemic proximity—the depth and reliability of our access to another person's contextual needs and inner states. Please describe the most important implications of this reinterpretation for how role obligations should function in large, anonymous institutions, in formal relationships lacking personal history, and in cases where such proximity is deliberately engineered rather than organically developed. Does this preserve the normative distinctiveness of role-specific duties, or does it reduce them to calibrated adjustments of benevolence? And how should the framework handle asymmetries, where one party within the same formal role possesses far greater epistemic proximity to the other than is reciprocated?
