Write a two-page research proposal for a philosophy and public-policy dissertation chapter. The subject is the comparative normative structure of three sex-work regulatory regimes: the Nordic model (criminalising purchase but not sale), legalisation as practised in Germany and the Netherlands, and full decriminalisation as implemented in New Zealand. Rather than rehearsing the familiar harm-reduction versus labour-rights opposition, the project should analyse these regimes through the lens of institutional design for asymmetric information, specifically by tracking how each allocates what might be called verification costs — the costs of establishing that consent, safe conditions, and fair terms actually obtain — among the state, the worker, and the client. State the precise question: does the Nordic model’s allocation of verification costs to the state, via policing of demand, implicitly presuppose a theory of worker agency that is incompatible with the autonomy-based justification typically advanced for decriminalisation, and can legalisation’s licensing-and-inspection framework be defended as a stable middle ground without collapsing into either state paternalism or unregulated market failure? Explain why the standard framing leaves this institutional-logic problem untouched. Set out the method — conceptual analysis of regulatory mechanisms, not empirical data gathering — and specify what success would look like (a taxonomy of verification-cost allocations tied to distinct conceptions of autonomy) and what failure would look like (the regimes prove indistinguishable on this dimension, or the framework applies only to one jurisdiction). Be explicit about scope: this is one chapter, so bracket detailed comparative law and say what you will omit. Do not provide a bibliography or citation list; references will be added separately.
