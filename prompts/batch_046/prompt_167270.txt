Camp B maintains that Spinoza's democracy is, in substance, an aristocratic or meritocratic council system: citizenship is narrowly restricted to a small class of native-born free males, decisive authority is delegated to councils composed of the rational or experienced, and the multitudo operates not as an open electorate but as a bounded validator set that legitimizes decisions made by an expert minority.

Please produce the adversarial-collaboration document, structured as follows.

First, provide a numbered list of agreed claims that both camps can accept about Spinoza's texts. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; rely solely on conceptual analysis.

Second, present a disagreements table. In one column, state the precise formulation of Camp A's residual position in wording its members would accept; in the other, do the same for Camp B. Identify any portion of the dispute that is verbal—particularly whether labeling the system democratic is a substantive disagreement about institutional design or a definitional disagreement about the necessary conditions for democracy—and explain exactly which part is verbal and why.

Third, specify the cruxes: the sub-questions whose resolution would cause at least one camp to revise its view. I take the central cruxes to be (i) whether the citizenship exclusions are structurally essential to Spinoza's democratic model or merely contingent to his historical context, and (ii) whether collective rational agreement in Spinoza's framework can scale to a broad, heterogeneous population without a small expert node-set mediating consensus, analogous to the difference between an open peer-to-peer protocol and a permissioned consensus mechanism. For each crux, identify a concrete textual feature or interpretive problem both sides agree is relevant. Then record each camp's explicit prediction about what that feature or problem demonstrates, so that future evidence could adjudicate between them.

Fourth, integrate the distributed-systems analogy conceptually rather than technically: explain how each camp understands the relationship between emotional interference among citizens (affect) and the stability of collective decision-making, using the language of noise, consensus failure, or validator reliability only as illustrative metaphors.

The tone should be formal and polished, appropriate for a technically trained reader without philosophical specialization. Avoid direct quotations; paraphrase any textual references without quotation marks.
