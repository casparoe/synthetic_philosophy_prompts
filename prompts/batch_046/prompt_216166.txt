I was talking with my friends last night about whether AI can actually be creative or if it's just remixing stuff, and someone brought up Hofstadter's idea about creativity being variations on a theme, like turning knobs on a conceptual space. I think I mostly get the gist but I'm stuck on something kinda specific. If creativity is really about twiddling knobs — changing tempo, key, instrumentation, whatever — then how do we tell the difference between a meaningful variation and just a broken version of the original theme?

Like, if I'm messing around with board game mechanics and I take a worker-placement system and start turning knobs (maybe I change the action space from discrete to continuous, or I couple the resource knob to the turn-order knob), some tweaks still feel like the same game with a twist, and others feel like I've accidentally invented an auction game and lost the theme entirely. What's the conceptual boundary there? Is there a principled way inside Hofstadter's framework to say which knob-turns preserve the "theme" identity and which ones destroy it? I don't need citations or a reading list, just an intuitive explanation of where the line lives conceptually, especially since the knobs seem interdependent in ways that make single-axis adjustments impossible. Also, does this mean true creativity requires knowing which knobs are coupled, or can you discover the couplings just by twiddling randomly?
