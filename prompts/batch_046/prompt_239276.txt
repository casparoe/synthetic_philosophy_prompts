Write the opening remarks for a workshop titled Frames and Their Failures, addressed to a split audience of literary theorists familiar with Hofstadter's distinction among frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages, and municipal policy officers with no background in semiotics. Center the remarks on a concrete case: a city welcome kit for new residents that sparked controversy because its frame message offered a universal warm invitation, its outer message listed practical civic obligations, and its inner message implicitly demanded the abandonment of prior cultural practices, producing a contradiction that neither standard multiculturalist nor assimilationist readings alone can resolve. Use the three-level framework to do real structural work—defining the precise nature of the institutional failure, showing why conventional debates about integration miss this specific communicative misalignment, and framing the day's agenda around how institutions can design texts that avoid frame-level contradictions rather than around whether assimilation ought to occur. The speech must remain fully accessible to the civil servants without simplifying the concept for the theorists, and it should treat the philosophical framework as an analytical tool rather than as decorative vocabulary.
