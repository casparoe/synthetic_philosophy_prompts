Consider a variant of the Judy Benjamin problem designed to test the distinction between conditioning on a proposition and conditioning on the evidential pathway that produces it. She accepts near-strict conditional probabilities: P(A | Red) ≈ 1 and P(B | Black) ≈ 1, where A and B denote assignment to distinct companies. She then acquires the belief that she is in company A, but she is agnostic about whether this belief arose from direct observation of her assignment or from first inferring her regiment and then applying the appropriate conditional.
