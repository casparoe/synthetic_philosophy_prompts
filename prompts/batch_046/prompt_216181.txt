The first camp, which we call the Structuralists, maintains that Braudel's framework demonstrates that human agency is largely epiphenomenal at the macro-historical scale: events such as the French Revolution are merely surface disturbances atop deeper currents like Mediterranean climate patterns and agricultural geography. The second camp, the Eventists, argues that this interpretation commits a subtle category error by reifying statistical correlations across centuries into causal mechanisms, and that exceptional individual or collective choices can genuinely rupture, redirect, or accelerate these so-called slow structures in ways that cannot be fully dissolved into background conditions.

We wish to produce an adversarial collaboration document that both sides could sign without either feeling misrepresented. Please draft this joint statement in two parts. First, provide a numbered section of agreed claims: these should cover what the longue durée actually describes rather than prescribes, the precise distinction between geographic constraint and geographic determinism, and our shared interpretation of at least one concrete case we studied together—the interaction between the climatic pressures of the Little Ice Age and the political ruptures of the Fronde in seventeenth-century France—stating only the empirical observations about agricultural yield and state response that neither side disputes. Second, present the remaining disagreements in a table: each row should state the precise formulation of the dispute, phrased so that the camp named in that row would accept the wording of its own position. The core disagreements should include whether slow structures possess explanatory priority over events or merely possess greater temporal extension, and whether a technological adaptation like the three-field rotation system should be classified as an event that restructures the longue durée or as an internal expression of it. For each crux, specify the concrete test case or historical argument both sides agree bears on the question, and record each camp's predicted interpretation in advance.

The document should avoid bibliographic references, reading lists, or appeals to authority, since this is meant to be a conceptual exercise rather than a literature review. Please conclude with a brief note identifying which of the listed cruxes, if settled empirically, would actually cause at least one camp to revise its core commitment rather than simply reinterpret the result.
