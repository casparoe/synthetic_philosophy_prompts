In my graduate seminar on aesthetics and the ontology of images, we have been working through Walter Benjamin’s well-known essay on mechanical reproduction and the dissolution of aura, and the students have become comfortable with the standard claim that photography and film sever the artwork from its singular presence in time and space. I want to press them, however, into conceptual territory that Benjamin could not have anticipated: not merely the infinite reproducibility of a digital file, but generative image synthesis and deepfake video as regimes in which there is frequently no original artifact whatsoever. The image is synthesized ex nihilo from distributed statistical weights, lacking a negative, a plate, or any first instance that might carry authoritative presence; this inverts the logic of forgery, since forgery presupposes an authentic original to imitate, whereas synthetic media can fabricate a convincing visual record of an event that never occurred without ever copying a specific prior image.

Given this framing, how do you think Benjamin would have assessed the value—if any—of an “original” digital file, a seed parameter, or a specific model checkpoint in such a system? Would he insist that aura requires a singular object with a history of ownership and exhibition, or might he identify a residual cult value in the ritual of prompt engineering, dataset curation, and model selection? Moreover, how would he distinguish, conceptually and politically, between a forgery (an imitation that deceives about provenance) and a synthetic generation (a fabrication that deceives about existence), given that his apparatus seems structured around the relation between copy and original? Please extend his concepts—mechanical reproduction, exhibition value, cult value, and the politics of aesthetics—rather than attributing specific quotations to him, and be explicit about where the extension feels strained or anachronistic. I am especially interested in whether you think his framework can accommodate an image for which the very distinction between original and copy has collapsed, or whether that collapse forces us to abandon his vocabulary entirely.
