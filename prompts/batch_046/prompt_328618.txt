As a PhD candidate in philosophy of medicine, I am developing a conceptual framework for mental-health triage under severe resource constraints. I want to work out the implications of adopting a tranquilist axiology—according to which only the absence of craving and suffering has intrinsic value, while positive affect, functional restoration, and autonomy carry none—rather than the pluralist frameworks standard in clinical ethics. What are the most important conceptual implications for triage design? Specifically: which conventional prioritization criteria (severity of dysfunction, prognosis for recovery, social role) should be abandoned or radically reweighted; how should allocation of scarce therapeutic resources be ordered if the sole metric is reduction in negative states; and what counterintuitive judgments about treatment success and patient ranking would this generate that clinicians would likely reject? Please keep the analysis strictly conceptual and avoid empirical claims about specific health systems.
