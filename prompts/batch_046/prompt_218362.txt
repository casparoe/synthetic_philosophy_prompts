In my phil of knowledge reading group we've been stuck on the Meno problem for like six months now — why is knowledge actually better than true belief if they both get you to the same truth? We all agree on the standard examples, the train schedule guy vs the lucky guesser, but then half the group says knowledge is valuable because it's an achievement, you recieve credit for tracking the truth with your own faculties. The other half says that's just prestige talk, and really knowledge isn't more valuable in itself, it's just more robust for planning since it holds across nearby possible worlds. We've argued ourselves into circles. Is this stall happening because we're definately smuggling in different notions of value — intrinsic vs instrumental — or are we literally answering different questions, like one about the mental state's worth and one about the practical payoff of having it? Or is there a deeper bedrock intuition about 'getting credit' that neither side can defend without begging the question? I don't want another full solution to the Meno problem, I want a diagnosis: what kind of disagreement is this, and is there any distinction or result — maybe from virtue epistemology, maybe something about modal robustness — that could actually move us? Also I've been wondering if framing knowledge as a kind of non-rivalrous public good (where true belief is rivalrous because it can be lost without notice) changes anything, but I'm not sure if that's even coherent. Thoughts?
