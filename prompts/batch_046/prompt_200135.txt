I've been trying to figure out the real rules for when a person is entitled to state something flat-out as a fact—not the grammar rules, but the deeper ones about what's required in your head or in the world. Some people say you have to actually know it; others say you only need to sincerely believe it; others say the statement just has to be true, even if you guessed. I keep running into situations I can't sort out with those slogans.

For instance, if my doctor tells me I have a specific condition and I repeat it to my family, am I asserting it properly even though I don't understand the lab work? If I use a navigation app I can't audit to warn a friend that a bridge is out, and I'm right, does that count? What about saying "I think it will rain" versus "it will rain" versus "I know it will rain"—are those just politeness, or do they track different rules?

Make sure the list handles cases where someone forms a claim by delegating part of the reasoning to an algorithm, database, or expert they cannot themselves verify.
