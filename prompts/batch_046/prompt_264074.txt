I'm trying to sort out what I actually think about personal survival—like, what I'd consent to in a living will, not just abstract metaphysics. Here are my gut calls.

If I step into a teletransporter that scans my brain, destroys my body, and rebuilds me on Mars with perfect psychologial continuity, I'd do it. That's me waking up on Mars.

But if they scan me without destroying me, and the copy wakes up on Mars while I'm still having coffee on Earth, I don't think that's me. It's like a twin with stolen memories.

If over decades they replace my neurons one by one with silicon chips that do the exct same job, I'm still me at the end. No break.

The weird ones: if they scan and destroy me, but the data sits in storage for eighty years before anyone builds the copy—I'm dead. The gap kills it, even though every memory is intact.

And if they make two copies on Mars and Venus (destroying the original), then years later merge those two consciousnesses back into one body—neither original "me" survives into that merged person. It's a new mind with two pasts.

So what's the principel? Is it continuity plus uniqueness plus temporal proximity? Or am I being inconsistent—why is instant destructive copying okay but delayed storage not? And what about replacing my brain region-by-region over a single day instead of decades? I keep flip-flopping there.
