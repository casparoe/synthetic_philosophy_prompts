There's this old debate about Kavka's toxin puzzle — you get a million if you intend at midnight to drink poison tomorrow, but you don't actually have to drink it and it just makes you sick for a day. For decades people have argued whether rationality requires forming the intention, and I feel like the literatre is just repeating CDT vs EDT moves with noone budging. Is this stall actually a verbal disagreement about what 'intention' means — like, is it a mental state or a commitment — or is there a hidden substantive clash about whether reasons can be self-referential? Or maybe the two sides are answering different questions: one about what you'd do, one about what you'd plan? I want a diagnosis using what each side actually says, not another survey. Also, here's a twist I haven't seen discussed: what if the poison is social, so drinking harms your village's water but the payout is shared? Does that expose the same deadlock, or does it shift the disagreement onto collective harm? Tell me what kind of disagreement it really is and what, if anything, would count as progress — a formal result, a conceptual distinction, or should we just drop the puzzle?
