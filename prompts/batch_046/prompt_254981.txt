We kept circling back to hedonism and its well-known heterogeneity problem: the pleasure of a cool breeze, the pleasure of solving an algorithmic puzzle, and the pleasure of reconnecting with an old friend seem to share nothing in common at the level of felt quality, bodily location, or even intensity curve, yet hedonism claims that pleasure alone is what makes a life go well. I understand the standard replies—that there might be a subtle but uniform hedonic tone running through every case, or that pleasures are unified only by being desired for their own sake—but I want to explore a less familiar middle path. Given that framing, please describe the most important implications of this heterogeneity challenge for hedonism, particularly for how we should think about constructing a unified measure of welfare or life satisfaction that must compare radically different kinds of positive experience across sensory, cognitive, and interpersonal domains.
