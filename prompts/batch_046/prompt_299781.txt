i keep running into Bradley's regress — the idea that if a relates to b you need another relation to link a to that relation, then another, ad infinitum. people present it like a logic puzzle frege solved by saying concepts are unsaturated, but that feels like renaming the problem. heres what i'm stuck on: if the regress proves relations can't do real connecting work, then relational propositions shouldn't just be hard to unify, they should be impossible. yet philosophers still treat "the unity of the proposition" as a live issue about how parts hang together in one true-or-false thing. isn't that treating Bradley's argument as a structural difficulty instead of a reductio? or did bradley think relations work inside the Absolute, so the regress only hits appearance-level thought? i feel like i'm mixing his metaphysical conclusion with frege's logical problem, but i can't tell if the standard framing is actually consistant.
