ok so i just got roped into being on this small-town planning board and suddenly im the one voting on whether to approve a development that displaces a few families but brings tax revenue for the school district. i never thought about dirty hands before but now it feels personal. i cooked up this argument that seems to show the whole idea collapses but i can't spot the leak. here it is:

1. the dirty-hands idea only makes sense if there's a specific kind of wrong act (like lying or coercing) that gets justified by extreme outcomes.
2. if extreme outcomes can justify it, then proportionally smaller good outcomes should justify proportionally smaller wrongs — morality doesn't have a magical on/off switch at "catastrophe".
3. but every policy decision involves some wrong (taxation, zoning, cutting a program) and some good outcome.
4. so every decision is dirty-handed, just with smaller stains.
5. if every decision is dirty-handed, the concept can't distinguish a politician who orders torture to stop a bomb from one who votes for a budget that trims library hours.
6. but that distinction is the whole point of talking about dirty hands in the first place.
7. therefore either dirty hands applies everywhere and means nothing, or there's an arbitrary cutoff that nobody can defend.
8. since arbitrary cutoffs are bad, the concept applies everywhere and is useless.

where does this slip? i really want to keep (1) and (5) — i think there IS a real difference between torture-for-bomb and library-cuts — but i feel like (2) is where the cheat happens. is it that moral justification isn't linear? or that the wrong in (3) isn't the same kind as in (1)? help me see what rejecting (2) actually costs me, because if i reject it i seem to be saying morality has these wierd discontinuities that i can't explain.
