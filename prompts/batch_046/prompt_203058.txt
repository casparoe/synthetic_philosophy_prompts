Consider a modified hitchhiker scenario: a driver with 90% predictive accuracy offers you a ride through a desert, with payment of $10 due only if you arrive alive. Unlike the classic case, the driver can observe which decision-theoretic framework—causal or evidential—you actually apply before announcing your choice, and you have access to an imperfect commitment device that can bind your future payment without constraining your present reasoning. What's your credence that the rational strategy is to apply evidential reasoning openly, forgo the device, and pay upon arrival? Give a precise number, then decompose it into: (1) the probability that evidential decision theory gives the correct normative prescription in one-shot prediction games; (2) the probability that observable reasoning alters the predictor's reliability in a way that makes decoupling reasoning from action advantageous; (3) the probability that rationality requires coherence between reasoning process and action rather than mere expected-utility maximization; and (4) any other factor you think belongs. For each term, provide a rough probability and a one-line justification, combine them into your final credence, and identify which single factor is most sensitive—what specific argument or result about it would shift your number the most.
