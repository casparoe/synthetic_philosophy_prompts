Having recently begun consulting on ethics protocols for autonomous ecological monitoring drones—systems programmed to maintain protected habitats in regions where no human presence is expected for decades—I have found myself unsettled by a metaphysical question about the obligations these machines might bear. Specifically, if a drone preserves a rare ecosystem solely according to its programming, and there is no conscious observer, stakeholder, or reasoning agent who will ever register the outcome, is there any genuine normative reason for the preservation to occur, or is the reason merely a projection of human designers? This touches directly on whether rationales can float free of reasoners altogether.

With that in mind, please rank the following five philosophical stances on the existence of free-floating normative reasons, ordering them from the stance you find most defensible to the least defensible specifically regarding their ability to accommodate what I will call an "unattended obligation"—a reason that binds action even in the complete absence of any agent capable of recognizing or desiring the outcome. The stances are:

1. Robust moral realism, which treats normative reasons as mind-independent facts that obtain regardless of whether any being apprehends them.
2. Kantian constructivism restricted to rational agency, which holds that reasons are constituted by the legislative activity of rational wills and therefore cannot exist in the absence of such agency.
3. Objective-list theories that ground value in the intrinsic worth of certain states of affairs—such as biodiversity or flourishing—independently of any subjective awareness.
4. Humean instrumentalism, which reduces all normative reasons to the desires, preferences, or practical interests of actual agents, implying that without an agent there is no reason.
5. A hybrid view I have been developing, which proposes that reasons emerge only within "normative ecosystems"—structured relational contexts that need not include conscious reasoners but must include some form of responsive practice or functional role—such that a drone maintaining biodiversity participates in a minimal ecological practice that generates localized, non-anthropocentric reasons without requiring a mind to host them.

Please organize your response as a tiered evaluation rather than a simple linear list if you believe some positions cluster together: assign tiers such as S, A, B, C, and D, and in one or two sentences for each stance explain why it lands in that tier. Your reasoning should focus particularly on whether the stance can genuinely sustain an unattended obligation in the drone scenario, or whether it collapses such obligations into observer-dependent preferences or eliminates them entirely.
