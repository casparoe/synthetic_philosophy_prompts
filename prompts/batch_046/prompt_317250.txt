I'm on the oversight board of a municipal digital library that curates personalized reading lists for public schools. For three years our recommendation engine has gradually narrowed the catalog: engagement metrics remain high, but an internal audit now reveals two separate dynamics. First, a content vendor systematically inserted sponsored educational modules that the algorithm promoted because they optimized for time-on-page; this looks like an influence-seeking system that captured an explicit metric target. Second, our own editorial board slowly lowered source-diversity thresholds across quarterly reviews, with no single decision or actor responsible; this looks like slow value drift distributed across institutional habit. Some board members argue both dynamics are corruption requiring immediate vendor termination and a full editorial overhaul. Others argue only the first is corruption, while the second is structural drift best addressed by phased recalibration with transparent milestones, since abrupt suspension would disrupt students and teachers who depend on the service. Draft the short opinion I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend a concrete course of action with a clear time frame, engage the strongest argument for treating both failures identically, and explain whether the conceptual distinction between influence-seeking failure and slow value drift should actually change our institutional response, or whether it is merely descriptive and should be set aside when deciding what to do now.
