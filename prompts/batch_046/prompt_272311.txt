hey, i'm a retired ecologist who got into philosphy of biology late in life and now i'm stuck on this adaptationism thing. Gould and Lewontin's spandrels argument has been bouncing around since the 70s -- one camp wants an adaptive story for every trait, the other says half of biology is just architectural by-prodcuts like those spandrels in San Marco, not designed for anything. Decades later, the same papers get cited, the same rebuttals get recycled, and nobody budges. So what's going on? Is this a real empirical fight about how often selection actually shapes traits, or is it verbal -- do we just mean different things by 'function' and 'explanation'? Or are the sides answering totally different questions, like evolutionary geneticists tracking selection coefficients versus philosophers policing explanatory norms, all cramed under one heading?

What makes me suspicious is that modern evo-devo talks about evolutionary capacitance -- hidden genetic variation that lies dormant and then gets released as adaptive scaffolding when the environment shifts. So a trait can start as a pure spandrel and later become integrated into an adaptive architecture. That seems to blur the old binary, yet people still argue as if spandrel vs adaptation is a clean either/or. Does that mean the original debate was never really about ontology but about burden of proof -- who has to show what before we accept an adaptive story? Or is there a hidden formal disagreement about null models?

I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a literature survey. What do the two sides actually say that reveals the structure of the disagreement? And if it is stalled, what kind of move -- a statistical framework, a conceptual split, an empirical result about trait origins -- could actually unstick it, or should we just accept it's a permanent feature of the field?
