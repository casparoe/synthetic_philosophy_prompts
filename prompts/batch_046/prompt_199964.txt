Please explain the conceptual architecture through which meaning is distributed across frame messages, outer messages, and inner messages—specifically, how each layer governs interpretation without being reducible to the literal syntax of the communicated form, and where the boundary between authorial framing and receiver reconstruction lies. Then examine this architecture in a constructed scenario: an opaque, behaviorally co-determined algorithmic system that restructures the visibility and contextual adjacency of textual messages without altering their literal content, thereby acting as an implicit framing mechanism that lacks a single authorial origin. In this case, should the algorithmic restructuring be classified as part of the frame message, as an extension of the outer message received by the user, or as a distinct intermediate layer that distributes meaning across the system? Discuss the implications for whether meaning remains primarily author-located, becomes receiver-constructed, or is system-distributed, and analyze whether the three-layer distinction collapses when the framing mechanism is dynamic, non-transparent, and generated through aggregate interaction traces rather than explicit meta-communication.
