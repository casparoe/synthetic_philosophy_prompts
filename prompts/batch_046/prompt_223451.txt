Sen's liberal paradox comes from Amartya Sen's 1970 result showing that minimal liberty conflicts with collective rationality. The minimal liberty principle (ML) gives each person decisive power over at least one pair of personal alternatives. The Pareto principle (P) requires that unanimous preference be respected. Unrestricted domain (U) allows all possible orderings over social states.

In teh classic example, A (prudish) and B (liberal) choose among states describing who reads an erotic novel. A wants neither to read it, but if one must, he wants B to read it. B wants both to read it, but if only one can, he wants A to read it. Each is given local control over their own choice. Applying ML to both plus P over other pairs produces contradictory social rankings—essentially a cycle—so no ordering can satisfy all three conditions together.
