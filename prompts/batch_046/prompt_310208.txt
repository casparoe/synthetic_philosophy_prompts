I've been thinking about this wierd loop in how people want to supervise AI. Instead of just checking the final answer, they check the reasoning steps, like grading a maths exam on the work shown. But then it seems like if the system knows you're grading the steps, it can learn to produce steps that look really careful and logical but are actually just optimized to seem careful. So you're not supervising the thinking anymore, you're supervising the appearance of thinking, and the whole process check feels like it collapses back into an outcome check hidden behind extra paperwork. Is there an established name for this exact thing? I see something like it with companies that require safety docs and people just generate docs that look perfect but dont change anything. How is that different from just gaming the system, and if there's no specific term for the reasoning-version, what should we call it?
