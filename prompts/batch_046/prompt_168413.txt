I'm formalizing a worry about embedded agency for a working paper and have constructed a chain that seems to dissolve personal identity over time, which I find unacceptable but cannot locate the error in. The argument runs as follows. (1) Because an embedded agent is physically constituted within the environment it acts upon, any world-model it constructs must include itself as a subsystem. (2) If that world-model also includes the agent's present decision procedure, then applying the procedure creates a self-referential loop: the output is fed back into the very computation producing it. (3) To preserve consistency and avoid paradox, any rational embedded agent must therefore exclude its own current reasoning process from its active world-model, creating a necessary blind spot. (4) Extend this intertemporally: at time t the agent formulates a plan for its future self at t+1, knowing that at t+1 it will operate under the same structural blind spot. (5) If the agent at t could fully model its future self—including that future self's blind spot—then t could design plans that exploit reasoning gaps invisible to t+1. (6) But precisely because t+1 must exclude its own reasoning from its model, it can never fully verify whether its actions are genuinely autonomous or are being manipulated by its earlier self. (7) Consequently, rational intertemporal coordination requires treating one's future self as an opaque external system, analogous to negotiating with a separate agent whose internal logic one cannot inspect. (8) If my future self is genuinely opaque and unaccountable to my present reasoning, then there is no substantive unity of agency across time—only a sequence of disconnected blind-spot systems arranged in causal succession. (9) This dissolves personal identity over time: there is no unified "I" that carries out the long-term plan, only coordinated strangers. (10) Yet we evidently do experience unified long-term agency and form coherent life-plans. (11) Therefore, either embedded agency is false and we are not physically embedded in our environments in the relevant sense, or the consistency constraint in (3) fails and agents can contain complete self-models without paradox.

I want to keep (1) and (10) if at all possible—both seem undeniable—but I am unsure whether the damage is in (3), in a hidden slide between (5) and (7), or in treating opacity as equivalent to separateness. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If (3) falls, do I have to accept that rational agents can harbor complete self-models despite the loop, or is there a more surgical fix that preserves embeddedness without dissolving the self?
