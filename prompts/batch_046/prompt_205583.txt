I'm writing because the issue has become concrete for me: I am now the primary decision-maker for my father, who has advanced dementia and minimal responsive awareness, and I have also been installing adaptive home devices—thermostats that learn his patterns, fall detectors, and automated dispensers—that respond to his behavior in ways that look like feedback loops. Watching family and caregivers discuss these two categories, I keep noticing a recurring structural pattern about how moral standing gets assigned, and I want to know whether philosophers have named it precisely.

Here is what I see. Within our own species, standing seems flat and binary: my father retains full moral weight despite profound cognitive loss, and no one treats him as having partial personhood that scales with his remaining awareness. Among animals people discuss comfortably, standing seems to slide smoothly with complexity: a chimpanzee gets substantial weight, a dog moderate weight, an insect minimal but still non-zero weight. But with the artificial devices, the conversation collapses to a sharp switch: they are just tools with zero weight, unless someone imagines they might achieve full human-like awareness, in which case they jump immediately to full equivalent weight. There is no recognized middle grade—no idea that a learning thermostat might deserve a small fraction of consideration because it tracks preferences and adapts, the way people grant an insect a small fraction.

Is there an established concept that captures exactly this three-layer arrangement—flat equality within one biological kind, smooth gradation across other living kinds, and an all-or-nothing absence-or-full-presence rule for artifacts? I am not asking for the general idea that humans treat their own kind differently, nor for the debate about whether cognitive capacity should matter within a species; I want to know whether anyone has named this specific asymmetry in how gradation is permitted downward in one domain, blocked downward in another, and replaced by a binary cliff in a third. If there is no precise term, what is the nearest conceptual neighbor, and where exactly does my pattern diverge from it? What would you call the boundary rule that allows gradation in nature but forbids it at the edge of the artificial?
