(1) define a bias as a moral judgment that wouldn't survive full information + ideal reasoning — basically whatever you'd reject at refelctive equilibrium.

(2) but refelctive equilibrium just is the state where your specific judgments line up with your general principles, and both can be warped together (think adaptive preferences: people in bad conditions learn to prefer them).

(3) so to spot a bias you need an external check, but in morality the only check is coherence with other judgments, which a consistently wrong system can have.

(4) now apply this: if i say "it's wrong to eat meat" and you say "it's not," i could claim your view is a bias from convenience, and you could claim mine is a bias from guilt or social signaling.

(5) since theres no neutral court of appeal, either we drop the bias/pref distinction entirely, or we need an objective moral fact that tells us whose reflection is distorted.

(6) if we take the second option, then moral preferences aren't real preferences — they're just guesses at objective truths, and "bias" is just being wrong.

(7) so either morality is pure preference with no bias, or pure fact with no preference. but we talk about both all the time (e.g., implicit racial bias vs. taste in music).

(8) therefore the distinction is impossible to maintain consistently.

this feels like it proves too much, but i can't see the escape hatch. i want to keep (1) and (5) if i can. which step should i actually reject, and what's the price? like, does giving up (3) save it, or do i have to bite a bullet on (7)?
