I've been thinking about the problem of verisimilitude — like, can false theories actually be ranked by how close they get to the truth without it being totally ad hoc? I think Popper's original logical-content approach is salvageable if we swap his truth-content metric for a weighted Kullback-Leibler divergence over predictions, where the weights are tied to explanatory relevance rather than logical strength. So Newtonian mechanics is closer to truth than Aristotelian physics not because it racks up more true consequences raw, but because its errors are boxed into low-weight domains — high velocities, tiny scales — where the predictive structure still approximates real dynamics. My view: verisimilitude is recoverable, but only if we ditch Popper's syntactic measure and accept that 'closeness' is domain-sensitive and partly convential. What would count against it? Not generic skepticism — I mean concrete failures: cases where KL-weighting ranks things backwards, where the domain-weights turn circular because 'relevance' just tracks what we already think matters, or where the framework can't tell apart a theory that's locally approximate from one that's globally wrong but shares predictions by accident. And honestly, have I just insulated myself by letting the weights be conventional? I want an honest map of what's actually risky here vs what's just awkward.
