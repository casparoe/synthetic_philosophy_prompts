My colleague, an engineer-philosopher working at the intersection of representation and material dynamics, has offered the following verdicts on whether symbolic states causally restructure their physical substrates: in a thermostat, the temperature value registered by the bimetallic strip does not itself bend the metal—the thermal expansion does; in a chess engine, the evaluation symbols do not rearrange the silicon—the electrical currents do; in a visual cortical neuron, the edge-detection representation does restructure downstream synapses, because the firing pattern just is the causal event; in a blockchain smart contract, the code symbols do not move electrons—the hardware execution does; and in a large language model, the generated token sequences do not physically alter the weight matrices—the gradient updates do, independently of semantic content. She is now examining a hypothetical system composed of careenium, a metamaterial in which spherical nodes called simmballs are configured by explicit symbolic rules, and those configurations directly determine the lattice's subsequent physical reorganization without intermediate electrical or thermal mediation. Would she judge that, in this system, the symbols are genuinely pushing the substrate around? I am less interested in a simple yes or no than in the competing rules that could consistently account for all five of her prior verdicts—particularly whether her distinction turns on biological implementation, on token-identity between symbol and physical event, or on something else—and in which verdict the careenium case falls under each rule. Finally, what further hypothetical case—perhaps involving an artificial non-biological neural analog—would best discriminate between the leading interpretations?
