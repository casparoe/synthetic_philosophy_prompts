I am a doctoral student in philosophy of science organizing a graduate workshop on the limits of scientific realism. For the closing session, I want a villanelle—strict form: five tercets and a final quatrain, with two refrains that alternate through the tercets according to the form's rules and conclude together in the last two lines. The poem should stage the confrontation between the no-miracles argument and the pessimistic meta-induction, but it must not reduce the latter to a generic historical skepticism. Instead, inject the structural-realist response: the middle tercets should distinguish the abandonment of specific ontological commitments—such as phlogiston, caloric, and the luminiferous ether—from the retention of underlying mathematical structures across theory change. The first refrain should carry the realist's claim that predictive success warrants commitment to the entities postulated; the second should carry the induction that past successful theories with false ontologies undermine that commitment. The rhyme scheme and refrain rotation should do philosophical work: through the tercets the two positions should circle one another without synthesis, and in the final quatrain they must appear together, side by side, so that the form itself shows the dispute being displaced rather than resolved—specifically onto whether structural continuity alone can sustain realist commitment without the entities. The poem should be conceptually precise enough that a specialist recognizes the distinction between entity realism and structural realism, and it should avoid all bibliographic reference, citation, or reading-list gestures, since it must stand on its conceptual architecture alone. The tone should be rigorous but not pedantic, appropriate for closing a seminar.
