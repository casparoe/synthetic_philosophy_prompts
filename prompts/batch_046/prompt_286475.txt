Write a deadpan parody of an internal corporate memorandum—titled “Strategic Wellness & Continuity Protocol: Q3 Guidance for Deterrence Operations Personnel”—issued by a fictional “Office of Strategic Human Resources” to crews of ballistic-missile submarines and launch-control officers. The memo should adopt the exact tonal register of contemporary HR communications (work-life balance initiatives, mental-health check-ins, “bring your authentic self to work,” conflict-resolution workshops, mandatory mindfulness modules) and apply them without alteration to the institutional logic of nuclear brinkmanship and mutual assured destruction. Through this mapping, the satire should expose, rather than state, three specific paradoxes of nuclear deterrence theory: (1) that credible deterrence requires maintaining a credible willingness to behave irrationally, so that “emotional-regulation training” must paradoxically preserve the capacity for uncontrolled escalation; (2) that the stability-instability paradox implies routine “safety” protocols (fail-safes, two-person rules, de-escalation drills) simultaneously undermine the credibility of accidental launch, which is itself a necessary component of credible threat; and (3) that “employee wellness” in this context consists precisely in maintaining psychological readiness for collective annihilation, such that burnout prevention becomes indistinguishable from maintaining second-strike readiness. The piece should be sustained, deadpan irony—no explicit moral commentary, no winking asides, no invented named officials (use only generic titles: “The Office,” “Personnel,” “Crew Wellness Liaisons”). The reader should be able to recognize that the author understands the actual strategic concepts being satirized. Approximately 650 words. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific theorists by name; the concepts should speak through the bureaucratic language alone.
