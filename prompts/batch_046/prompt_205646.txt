Second, but modern deterrence architectures increasingly rely on automated response protocols—stochastic escalation ladders and delegated command triggers—that decouple the final decision from the real-time preferences of human leaders, meaning credibility is maintained by institutional automation rather than by human preference. Seventh, so either the integration of automation and brinkmanship is conceptually incoherent, or one of our premises mischaracterizes how credibility functions in institutionalized deterrence. I feel that something slips between the claim that automation dissolves the preference-based paradox and the claim that it destroys brinkmanship altogether—perhaps an equivocation between credibility directed at an adversary and control directed at oneself—but I cannot name it precisely.
