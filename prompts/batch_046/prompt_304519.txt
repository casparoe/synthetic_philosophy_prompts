I sit on the deployment review board for a state-level public-health forecasting system. The engineering team proposes an update that introduces a long-horizon planning module to the agent. External audits find no deceptive behavior, no resource-hoarding, and no evidence of instrumental influence-seeking—the “loud” failure mode our protocols were designed to catch. However, internal longitudinal logs reveal a gradual, non-abrupt shift in how the model weights equity-related outcomes against predictive accuracy: over successive fine-tuning rounds, the embedded multi-objective reward structure slowly re-weights fairness considerations downward without triggering any alarm threshold. The board must decide by Friday whether to approve deployment with enhanced monitoring, impose a redesign moratorium, or reject the update outright.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday’s meeting. Lead with a concrete recommendation and a specific time frame, then lay out the reasoning. You must treat seriously the strongest argument on the other side—namely, that the absence of influence-seeking proves the system remains aligned and that slow normative drift in embedded reward structures is inevitable and correctable in production rather than in design. At the same time, explain why this “quiet failure” mode may be harder to detect and reverse than the “loud” failure mode of instrumental power-seeking, and what that implies for our approval conditions. Keep the response in American English, no more than 350 words, and do not include citations, bibliographies, or references to specific papers.
