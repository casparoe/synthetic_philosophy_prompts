I have been trying to apply the Rietdijk-Putnam argument—particularly Rietdijk’s Andromeda scenario—to a personal ethical dilemma. The reasoning seems to show that, because simultaneity is frame-relative, special relativity eliminates any metaphysically privileged present and forces us to treat all events along a worldline as equally real within a four-dimensional block. From this I have drawn a specific inference: if I make a reckless career decision now, the suffering of my future self is not a mere possibility but an already-existing state with full ontological parity to my present anxiety. Does this mean that ethical presentism—the view that only present harms carry direct moral weight—is not merely philosophically questionable but physically falsified by relativity, and that I ought to regard my future distress as belonging to a co-existing individual whom I am presently harming? Where exactly does the move from the metaphysics of eternalism to this particular moral conclusion break down?
