Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between motivational internalists about practical reasons—who hold that for a consideration to be a reason for an agent it must be capable of motivating that agent through some sound deliberative route from her actual or counterfactual psychological states—and normative externalists—who maintain that reasons depend on objective values or norms that can bind an agent regardless of her contingent motivational set. The document should begin with a numbered section listing at least five substantive claims both sides can endorse, covering the distinction between explanatory and justifying reasons, the possibility of rational error, and the conditions under which an agent can be criticized for failing to act. Next, present a table of precise disagreements phrased in neutral language that each camp would accept as stating its own view: include whether a reason not to harm others can apply to an agent who entirely lacks compassion and cannot acquire it through rational reflection, and whether internal coherence among an agent's motivations is a necessary condition for possessing a reason rather than merely a condition for acting on one. Identify three cruxes—sub-questions whose resolution would move at least one side—including how counterfactual motivation should be assessed when an agent's preferences change solely through epistemic improvement, whether reasons survive radical preference revision in conditions like frontotemporal dementia, and whether a collective body with distributed and conflicting individual motivations can be bound by reasons that are external to every member. For each crux, specify one concrete case or line of reasoning both sides agree bears directly on it—such as a mathematician who acquires an unrevisable aesthetic obligation to preserve an ecosystem through pure structural insight, with no prior emotional or conative connection to nature—and record in advance each camp's specific prediction about what that case demonstrates. Do not present a menu of framing options; take a definite position on how each disagreement should be formulated, which predictions belong to which camp, and why those particular cruxes are decisive rather than peripheral, giving explicit reasons for each of your drafting choices.
