I've been trying to apply Kant's universalizability test to maxims of what some recent work calls 'structural free-riding': actions that succeed only if others adhere to a cooperative scheme that the agent herself undermines. The worry is that these maxims avoid both contradiction in conception—since universal free-riding is logically consistent—and contradiction in will—since the agent might rationally prefer universal non-compliance if she expects to be the exception. From this it is argued that Kant's binary framework misses a middle category of systemic instability, and that plugging the gap requires smuggling in consequentialist reasoning about aggregate outcomes. Is the real error here the assumption that contradiction in will is about individual preference rather than about the rational impossibility of willing the necessary conditions of one's own action, or is there a deeper problem with treating structural free-riding as a distinct moral category at all?
