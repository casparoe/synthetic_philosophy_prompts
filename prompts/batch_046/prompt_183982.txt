My thesis, briefly: psychiatric diagnosis does not merely overreach; it systematically commits a category mistake by treating conflicts about how a person ought to live within a given social environment—normative functional failures—as if they were statistical deviations in neural mechanism, i.e., natural-kind dysfunctions of the brain. I argue that the DSM smuggles "functional norms" (what an organism ought to do to flourish in its environment) into apparently descriptive "statistical norms" (what brains in a population typically do), and that this error is structurally parallel to pre-Darwinian essentialism about species: treating a population-level cluster as if it possessed an internal essence that determines membership. I concede outright that conditions like Alzheimer's are genuine brain diseases; my claim is that the umbrella category "mental illness" illegitimately groups these with conditions like major depression or schizophrenia, where the dysfunction claim is at best contested and at worst a normative judgment disguised as biological fact. The suffering is real; the mislocation of its source is the myth.
