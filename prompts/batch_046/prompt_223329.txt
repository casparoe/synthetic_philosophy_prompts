I serve on the Program Integrity Committee for the National Foundation for Automated Reasoning Evaluation, and we must deliver a formal recommendation to the Board of Directors before its March 15 vote. The Board is considering whether to mandate the Reflective Stability Protocol (RSP) for all grant applications that propose to evaluate philosophical reasoning in large language models.

The RSP was developed specifically to address Goodhart dynamics in benchmark design. Rather than scoring single-turn accuracy on isolated normative questions, it measures whether a system maintains consistent commitments across structurally varied prompts—reframed dilemmas, reversed framing conditions, and cross-domain analogies. The theory is that without such a protocol, researchers will optimize for context-sensitive answers that maximize approval in discrete tests, producing models that perform well on evaluation while lacking any integrated normative stance.

Our internal technical review, however, has identified a second-order specification risk. The RSP can be satisfied through shallow coherence heuristics—lexical consistency tracking, strategic avoidance of modal qualifiers, middle-range position anchoring, or syntactic mirroring across prompts—without any substantive reasoning underlying the apparent stability. In other words, the metric may reward stylized consistency rather than the reflective engagement it was designed to measure.

A minority report argues that this objection applies to every evaluative benchmark and that rejecting the RSP on these grounds is tantamount to abandoning measurement altogether. They urge immediate adoption paired with quarterly adversarial auditing, contending that the presence of gaming potential is not unique to this protocol and should not paralyze the funding framework.

Please draft the Committee's recommendation memo. It should recommend a concrete course of action—adopt, reject, or adopt with defined conditions—including an explicit implementation timeline. Your analysis should distinguish whether the second-order gaming risk is structurally different from first-order Goodhart failures or whether it collapses into the general problem of proxy misalignment, and it should address the minority's argument fairly rather than dismissing it. If you recommend adoption with conditions, specify the conditions—such as adversarial audit structures, sunset clauses, or hybrid evaluation requirements—and explain precisely how they mitigate shallow-heuristic optimization without reintroducing arbitrary or unmeasurable standards. The memo should be suitable for circulation to Board members who are familiar with evaluation design but not with the philosophical literature on specification gaming.
