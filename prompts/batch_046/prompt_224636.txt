I am a junior philosophy student enrolled in an upper-division seminar on consciousness and self-knowledge, and we have recently been examining the disagreement between Norman Malcolm and Daniel Dennett over whether dreaming constitutes a genuine experience. As I understand Malcolm's position, the lack of any first-person criterion for distinguishing being awake from being asleep undermines the claim that one can have perceptual or affective experiences during sleep; Dennett, conversely, argues that dream reports are confabulated narratives constructed at the moment of awakening rather than recollections of prior conscious episodes. While discussing this with a friend who works in clinical sleep research, I imagined a near-future facility that uses non-invasive cortical monitoring to produce "phenomenal transcripts"—algorithmic reconstructions of neural dynamics during REM sleep—without ever querying the subject upon waking.

For a creative assignment in my course, I would like you to write a short story set in this clinic. The protagonist should be an intern or patient who confronts two contrasting cases: one in which the transcript depicts a richly detailed experiential sequence that the subject, upon waking, firmly denies having undergone, and another in which the subject reports a vivid, emotionally intense dream while the corresponding transcript is either blank or structurally incoherent. The narrative should dramatize Malcolm's criterion problem—perhaps through a therapeutic or legal dispute about whether the transcript can establish that an experience occurred—while also giving voice to Dennett's view through another character who treats the transcript as tracking nothing more than the raw material for a post-awakening story. Please keep the technology within the bounds of plausible neuroscience, avoid exotic physics, and center the plot on the conceptual question: if dreams are not experiences in the ordinary sense, what exactly is being reconstructed? The story should conclude with the protagonist in a state of unresolved tension rather than reaching a decisive philosophical verdict.
