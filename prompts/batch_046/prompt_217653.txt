I want to weigh three considerations—retributive justice, deterrence, and the risk of executing an innocent person—but through a specific lens: the ethical distinction between actively causing harm and merely allowing it. Write an essay that examines whether this distinction makes the state's risk of wrongfully executing someone a categorically decisive objection, even if capital punishment fully satisfies retributive demands and achieves perfect deterrence. Reach and defend a clear conclusion—either that the death penalty remains justified despite that risk, or that the risk alone makes it impermissible—and explain why one of these positions ultimately prevails.
