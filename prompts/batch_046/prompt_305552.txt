You advise an AI governance auditor who must repeatedly decide, in audit reports that carry regulatory weight, whether attributing a specific propositional attitude—say, a belief that P or a desire that Q—to a large language model or multi-modal agent is warranted. The auditor has no access to internal weights and must work only from behavioural outputs, system documentation, and structured counterfactual probes. They want a reusable decision procedure grounded in the intentional stance, but modified for artificial systems: it must distinguish predictive utility from explanatory adequacy, and it must include a counterfactual-stability gate (would the attribution survive small, semantically irrelevant perturbations to the input context?) and a design-intention independence check (the behavioural pattern must be stable regardless of whether the designers deliberately engineered it). Write the procedure as an ordered sequence of questions or gates the auditor can run in under thirty minutes per attribution claim. For each gate, specify what counts as passing, what counts as failing, and where the procedure terminates with "do not attribute", "attribute with qualification", or "refer to the ethics panel—this is a values judgment, not a behavioural determination". Mark explicitly where judgement cannot be eliminated. Then apply the procedure to three cases: (a) attributing to a dialogue system the belief that water boils at 100 degrees Celsius; (b) attributing to a strategic game-playing agent the desire to maximise its score even at the cost of misleading its human partner; and (c) attributing to a reasoning model the belief that its own outputs are generally unreliable, where the procedure yields an answer the auditor is likely to reject and must therefore justify overriding. Conclude by stating precisely what the procedure cannot settle—particularly regarding phenomenological consciousness, normative blame, and the distinction between simulating an attitude and instantiating it. Please write the response in British English.
