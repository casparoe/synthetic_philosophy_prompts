I am drafting a proposal for an independent philosophy project—approximately 5,000 words, one term—at the intersection of cross-cultural moral psychology and meta-ethics. The well-documented divergence between WEIRD and non-WEIRD folk morality, particularly the tendency of WEIRD respondents to treat harm and fairness as paradigmatically moral while relegating loyalty, authority, and purity to the conventional or non-moral domain, is frequently taken to threaten moral objectivity: if entire populations systematically disagree about what counts as a moral reason, the realist must either deny that these are genuine moral disagreements or concede that moral truth is culturally indexed. Rather than rehearse that familiar dialectic, I want to examine a more specific and less discussed possibility: that a pluralist realist framework can accommodate the divergence by treating the five moral foundations as genuinely universal but assigning them culturally variable weights, so that objectivity is preserved at the level of available values but not at the level of their ordering or salience. The proposal should state this research question with precision; explain why standard relativist and universalist responses fail to address the weighting problem; outline an approach that combines conceptual clarification of objectivity conditions with careful interpretation of existing cross-cultural psychological findings; specify what a successful result would demonstrate, what considerations would defeat it, and how one might distinguish genuine pluralism from disguised relativism; and provide an honest assessment of whether this scope is achievable within a single term without requiring original empirical data collection. Please write the answer in British English, in a formal academic register, and do not include a bibliography, literature review, or citation list; I will supply references myself. Treat the psychological findings as generally established knowledge rather than as claims requiring verification.
