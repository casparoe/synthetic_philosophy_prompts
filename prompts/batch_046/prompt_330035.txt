I'm trying to figure out where the line is between a legit is-to-ought bridge and falling into the naturalistic fallacy, but my gut verdicts are all over the palce.

Here are my calls:
- The heart's function is pumping blood, so a heart ought to pump blood. Fine, just teleological talk.
- People are cooperative social primates, so they ought to share resources. Also feels okay?
- Women have on average less upper-body strength, so they ought not do heavy labor. Not fine at all.
- Our brains release dopamine for fairness, so fairness is morally required. Suspicious, feels like a sneaky bridge.
- If you want to avoid suffering, you ought not step on a nail. Fine, plain hypothetical.
- Democracy correlates with GDP growth, so we ought to be democratic. Not fine.
- Humans evolved parental attachment, so parents ought to care for their kids. This feels okay, but I can't tell why it's different from the dopamine case.
- I keep flip-floppping on this one: if moral sentiments are just evolved wiring, then we ought to treat them as unreliable. Sometimes that sounds right, sometimes like self-defeating nonsense.

Is there one principle that gets all these, or do I have to drop one? And what should it say about a case I haven't listed — say, "this drug cures disease with no side effects, so doctors ought to prescribe it"? Does that commit the fallacy or is it just practical reasoning?
