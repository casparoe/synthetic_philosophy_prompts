okay so i was arguing with my friend from my philosophy of bio class about whether mental illness is "real" in the same way diabetes is. i ended up landing on this weird hybrid view that i think is mostly right but im worried its just unfalsifiable: mental health conditions are neither purely statistical deviations (Boorse-style) nor just harmful dysfunctions (Wakefield-style), but rather systematic miscalibrations of precision-weighting in hierarchical predictive processing architectures—basically your brain's error-correction mechanisms are locked into priors that don't update with environmental feedback, regardless of whether that's statistically normal or subjectively distressing. so schizophrenia might involve hyper-precise priors at high levels of the hierarchy, depression might be overly precise predictions about future reward absence, etc. the "disease" part is that this miscalibration is self-sustaining and not responsive to the usual feedback loops that correct prediction errors in healthy systems.

what would actually count against this? i don't want the usual "but suffering is subjective" objection—that's already priced in. i want the honest exposure map: what clinical findings, what results from computational psychiatry, or what failures of predictive-processing models in other domains (maybe robotics or AI alignment?) would make this collapse? specifically, if we found that successful psychiatric interventions work by changing something totally unrelated to precision-weighting—like purely social restructuring with no change in internal prediction dynamics—would that kill it? or if predictive-processing models turned out to be descriptively useless for actual brain dynamics? also, has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability? like if every mental state can be redescribed as some precision-weighting pattern, then what risks does it actually take? rank the defeaters from "this is dead" to "merely awkward," and say which ones a committed predictive-processing naturalist would concede versus dismiss. and be honest: is the insulation a feature or a bug?
