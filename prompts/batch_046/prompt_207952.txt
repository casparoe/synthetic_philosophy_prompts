I'm trying to figure out where I actually stand on the principle of alternative possibilities, goign off my gut on cases. Classic Frankfurt-style thing: Black wants Jones to kill Smith, there's a chip in Jones's head that'd force him if he even starts to hesitate, but Jones decides on his own and the chip never fires. I say Jones is still blameworthy — he literally couldn't have done otherwise, but that doesn't seem to matter. Now twist it: the chip is glitchy and only works about half the time, and Jones doesn't know that. He still decides on his own. I still want to blame him, but it feels shakier, like thier being some real chance of escape changes something even though it didn't happen.

Then there's the hormone-monitor version: a blocker scans Jones's stress levels and will paralyze him if he starts feeling reluctant. He kills calmly, never feels reluctance, so the blocker never activates. I think he's blameworthy here too, but I'm genuinely going back and forth on whether this is the same as the classic case or different in some way I can't pin down.

Another case: a committee of three approves a harmful policy. There's a hidden override that'd veto the decision if the vote were split, but it's 3-0 so the override stays dormant. I think the committee is collectively responsible, even though if one member had dissented the override might have kicked in? Actually I'm not sure — it feels different from the individual case and I'm not sure if I should say they "couldn't have done otherwise" collectively.

Then there's a nested blocker: blocker A stops Jones from walking away, blocker B disables blocker A if Jones shows remorse, but Jones never considers leaving and feels none, so blocker A is live but never triggered. I blame him, but I worry I'm just saying "no actual alternative = no problem" and ignoring the structure.

Is there a principle that gets most of these? Or do I have to drop one? In particular, what's the deal with the glitchy chip versus the hormone monitor — they feel different but I can't explain why. And what about a case I haven't listed: the chip is destroyed by a lightning strike right before Jones acts, but he doesn't know it's gone and decides on his own? Is he responsible?

Also, is there a simpler account that saves my individual verdicts but forces me to overturn the committee one? I'd like to know.
