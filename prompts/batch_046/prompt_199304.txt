Write a dialogue between two philosophers of moral change, one defending a view inspired by Bernard Williams—that practices like chattel slavery and aristocratic dueling collapse mainly when thick ethical concepts shift and peer honor makes them shameful, with rational argument playing at most a catalytic role—and the other defending a view inspired by Kwame Anthony Appiah—that honor cultures are strategically turned against practices through reframing, argument, and appeals to consistency, while economic interests and state power provide background conditions rather than primary explanations. Have them apply both frameworks to the divergence between Britain, where civilian dueling vanished rapidly in the early nineteenth century, and the German universities, where academic dueling (Mensur) persisted into the twentieth century despite comparable legal and economic pressures. Each speaker must respond directly to the other’s specific claim: the Williams-inspired speaker should argue that Mensur survived because university honor formed a reflexive, self-enclosed world insulated from broader moral argument, while the Appiah-inspired speaker should counter that Mensur persisted precisely because state and class interests strategically protected a redeployable honor code, not because honor is immune to strategic reframing. They should also debate the abolition of the transatlantic slave trade—whether British slaveholders primarily lost social face or whether abolitionist rhetoric actively reconstructed national honor—without either becoming a strawman; each should concede one limited point before pressing the core disagreement. Near the end, have them converge on a shared conceptual distinction—honor as a reflexive self-conception that collapses under internal contradiction versus honor as a social currency that actors can redeploy—while disagreeing about which model fits these historical cases. End the dialogue with a line beginning exactly: Verdict: [choose one: reflexive honor, strategic honor, or explanatory stalemate]. On the following line, provide a one-sentence summary of which framework better explains why British dueling disappeared while German academic dueling endured.
