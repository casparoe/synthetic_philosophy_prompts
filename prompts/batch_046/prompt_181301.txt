You serve as senior advisor to the Municipal Science Museum's Exhibit Ethics Board. The board must decide whether to approve a permanent public-health exhibit that uses a modified ISOTYPE-style pictographic system—internally called Neurath-Vari—in which every icon's color saturation encodes modal status: fully saturated symbols denote protocol-sentence-level observations, while desaturated variants denote theoretical inferences. Critics, including two board philosophers of science, argue that embedding modal gradations in the visual syntax violates Neurath's anti-metaphysical commitments, because it compels viewers to absorb theoretical commitments through the image grammar instead of leaving them free to combine protocol statements independently. The board requires an actionable recommendation—approve, reject, or approve with conditions—along with reasoning that weighs fidelity to Neurath's picture-language ideals against the practical risk of public misinterpretation through an ostensibly neutral visual code. Draft the board's decision memo. Present it as exactly five numbered points, each one to two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
