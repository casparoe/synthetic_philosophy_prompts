I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about death and grieving, not what I'm supposed to think. My grandma died at 94 after a long good life, and my mom threw a party with her favorite food and told funny stories about her. That felt right, like celebrating the person. But when my cow-orker's kid died in a car crash, everyone was crushed and said life is unfair, and that felt right too, like grief was necessary. Then I read about this old Chinese philosopher Zhuangzi who apparently laughed and played drums when his wife died, saying death is just changing houses or transforming. That feels wrong, like he's being disrepectful, but I can't exactly say why if death is really just transformation. Then my dog died last year. I was sad, but I also felt this weird peace, like he was just back in the natural cycle. That seemed okay. But my cousin said we shouldn't morn our uncle because he's just transforming into soil now, and that felt cold and wrong. So mourning a child is necessary, laughing at a spouse's funeral is bad, feeling peaceful about a pet is okay, but refusing to mourn an uncle because of transformation is also wrong? I keep flip-flopping on this one: if I find out tomorrow I have a terminel illness, should I feel calm and accept it like changing clothes, or is it okay to be terrified and want to fight?

My gut verdicts:
- Party for 94-year-old grandma who lived well: right.
- Deep grief for a child killed suddenly: right, almost required.
- Laughing and playing drums at your own wife's funeral (like that Zhuangzi story): wrong.
- Feeling peaceful about a pet's death, seeing it as part of nature: okay.
- Refusing to mourn an uncle because "he's just soil now": wrong, cold.
- Throwing a party for a dead friend with funny stories and no tears: okay, doesn't feel disrespectful.

Is there a principle that gets all of these, or am I just being inconsistent? If there is one, lay it out precisely - what clause handles the grandma party versus the wife-laughing, and what does it say about the pet case? Then apply it to the one I can't decide: my own terminel diagnosis - should I accept calmly or fight? And be honest about which of my gut calls the principle can't save. Maybe the pet one has to go? Or the uncle one? I want the rule and the residue, not just a story.
