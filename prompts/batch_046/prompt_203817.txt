For an advanced ontology seminar, specify the full set of desiderata that an adequate theory of special composition must satisfy—i.e., the conditions under which parts compose a whole—without merely rehearsing standard defenses of universalism, nihilism, or restricted composition.

Your response should be structured as follows:

First, list the core requirements, each paired with the motivating case or conceptual pressure behind it. Include: (i) handling the arbitrary fusion problem, such as the putative object composed of a particular tree and a particular distant galaxy; (ii) accounting for vagueness in ordinary objects like clouds or mountains without collapsing into epistemicism or conventionalism; (iii) distinguishing genuine part-whole relations from mere spatial adjacency, causal interaction, or set-theoretic aggregation; (iv) accommodating temporal variation, specifically whether composition can be temporary—e.g., a committee that exists only while its members are actively deliberating together, then dissolves without leaving a physical trace—and whether such cases require four-dimensional persistence conditions for parts or can be handled by a three-dimensional account with temporary existence; (v) consistency with physical ontology, meaning the theory must not generate an ontological explosion of overlapping macro-objects in every spacetime region, yet it should not arbitrarily exclude quantum-entangled systems from being composed parts of a larger whole solely because of indistinguishability; (vi) preserving the intuition that composition is not identity, so that a whole is not reducible to its parts taken collectively.

For each requirement, indicate whether it is non-negotiable for any plausible theory or whether it reflects a preference of a particular camp, such as common-sense realism, physicalist reductionism, or four-dimensionalist mereology.

Second, identify any subset of these desiderata that appear jointly unsatisfiable given current metaphysical and physical commitments, explaining the precise tension—for example, between providing a non-arbitrary boundary for vague objects and avoiding unrestricted universalism, or between allowing temporary compositions and maintaining that composition is not purely conventional.

Third, briefly assess how unrestricted universalism, mereological nihilism, and a restricted-composition view grounded in natural relations fare against the list, but keep the evaluation brief and subordinate to the criteria themselves. Do not supply bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.

The goal is a rigorous specification sheet rather than a defense of one position, with particular attention to the under-discussed intersection of temporary composition and quantum-scale boundaries.
