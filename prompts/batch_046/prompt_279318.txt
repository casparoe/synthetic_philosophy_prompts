I've been replaying Shadow of the Colossus and I'm stuck on something that should be obvious but isn't. There's this philosophical question about whether video-game characters actually matter morally — not just as representations, but as entities we stand in some relation to through playing. Some folks say interactivity creates a special moral bond; others say it's all fiction and the pixels don't suffer. Read the game through that lens: Wander has to kill these huge, mostly peaceful colossi to save Mono, and you're the one pressing the buttons to stab them and climb them. Does the player's direct agency make the killings morally different from just watching it happen in a film? I'm especialy interested in the first colossus falling and Wander getting that black streak — is that the game punishing you specifically, or just story dressing? There's also this niche idea in virtual ethics that procedural complicity is different from narrative complicity because your agency is constitutive of the harm, not just representational. Does Shadow resist that because Wander is corrupted no matter what you do, or does the forced progression actually strengthen it? Don't worry about giving me sources — I'm writing this offline. Just tell me where the philosophical idea lights up the game and where the game honestly pushes back.
