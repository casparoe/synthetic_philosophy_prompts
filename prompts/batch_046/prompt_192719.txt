I am a doctoral student in philosophy of mind and cognitive science drafting the final examination for an upper-level graduate seminar on analogy, concept formation, and creativity. I need an essay prompt that engages the knob-twiddling model associated with Douglas Hofstadter—specifically the idea that creative analogies arise from sliding continuous controls across conceptual dimensions—without allowing students to earn high marks simply by summarizing that framework from standard sources. To make the task resistant to pure memorization, please embed the following hypothetical constraint, which students should treat as a proposed extension rather than an established doctrine: the coupled-knob hypothesis, according to which certain conceptual dimensions (for instance, animacy and agency) are not independently adjustable but are mechanically linked, so that pushing animacy downward past a critical threshold automatically pulls agency downward with it, and vice versa. The exam should ask students to assess whether this coupled version of the model can accommodate a genuinely cross-domain analogy in which a source concept scores high on animacy but low on agency (e.g., a colony of social insects behaving as a unified adaptive system) is mapped onto a target concept that scores high on agency but low on animacy (e.g., a decentralized artificial system that makes adaptive decisions without biological life). Specifically, students must be required to: (a) explain why the uncoupled version permits such mappings but risks arbitrariness; (b) analyze whether the coupled version blocks the analogy or forces an undesirable reconceptualization of one domain; and (c) argue for either preserving the coupling with an additional mechanism (such as a global restructuring move) or abandoning it in favor of independent knobs with local constraints.

Please deliver the following in a polished format suitable for a three-hour exam block but calibrated for a 75-minute writing period and an expected response of roughly 800–1000 words:

1. The essay question itself, clearly stating the coupled-knob constraint and the specific analogy students must analyze.

2. A model answer that demonstrates the reasoning required at the highest level: it should identify the tension between analogical flexibility and conceptual stability, apply the coupled mechanism precisely to the insect-colony versus artificial-system case, and propose a coherent resolution—whether that involves a restructuring exception, a rejection of the coupling, or a redefinition of the dimensions involved.

3. A marking scheme that separates excellent (A-range) answers from competent (B-range) answers by naming the specific intellectual moves each must contain. The scheme should allocate marks approximately as follows: conceptual analysis of the uncoupled model and the coupling problem (35%); precise application to the specified cross-domain analogy (30%); critical evaluation and proposed resolution (25%); and clarity, structure, and philosophical precision (10%). For each band, note the characteristic failure mode—for example, B-level answers often describe the original framework accurately but treat the coupled constraint as a minor footnote rather than a structural problem, or they analyze the analogy without addressing whether the coupling permits or forbids it.

Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for citations. Do not quote directly from any published text; paraphrase all technical terminology (such as knob-twiddling or conceptual dimensions) without quotation marks. Ensure the prompt is fully self-contained so that a respondent without web access can address it completely.
