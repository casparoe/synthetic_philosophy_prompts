Write a 75-minute essay exam question for an upper-level philosophy course on karma conceived as a theory of moral desert. The question should compel students to confront the tension between karma's cross-lifetime assignment of moral outcomes and the Buddhist anātman doctrine, which denies an enduring self capable of bearing those outcomes. Specifically, require them to distinguish allocative desert (who receives goods or evils) from retributive desert (who is owed them) and to evaluate whether the saṃtāna (stream-of-consciousness) model can serve as the bearer of desert without reintroducing a substantial self. Supply a model answer of about 500 words that argues for one resolution rather than merely summarizing Buddhist metaphysics. Include a marking scheme out of 20 points structured around five must-hit analytical moves—each with its point value and a one-line note on the characteristic student error—so that the scheme distinguishes answers that understand the conceptual crux from those that only recite the anātman and karma doctrines. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
