Write the desiderata that any philosophical account of the justice–force relation must satisfy, treating Pascal's fragments on justice and power as a stress-test rather than an authority. Specify: (i) why justice must be backed by coercive force to be effective, without collapsing into the claim that might makes right; (ii) why force lacking justice is tyrannical, and how the account distinguishes legitimate enforcement from domination; (iii) the distinction between justice as a normative standard and justice as a socially enforced convention, and why it must not be collapsed; (iv) the founding paradox — that the coercive power needed to establish a legal order appears, by that order's own norms, to have been illegitimate — and whether the account can dissolve or must accommodate it; and (v) consistency with the observation that obedience to law usually tracks fear of sanction rather than recognition of merit. For each, state the motivating case or tension that makes it non-negotiable, and flag any pair of desiderata that seem jointly unsatisfiable. Conclude by briefly assessing how the view reconstructed from Pascal's fragments fares against this list: which conditions does it meet, which does it redefine, and which does it leave unresolved?
