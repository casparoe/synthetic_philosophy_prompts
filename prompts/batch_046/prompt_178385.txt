I have been thinking, without any formal training in logic or computer science, about a strange circularity that seems to arise when a reasoning system tries to vouch for its own reliability. From what I understand, there is a result in formal logic—often discussed under the heading of the Löbian obstacle to self-trusting agents—which suggests that if a system can prove a certain conditional about its own proofs, then it is licensed to prove anything at all, or at least that it cannot consistently assert “everything I prove is true” without undermining its own foundation. I do not need the technical derivation, but I want to see the idea dramatized through narrative.

Write a short story centered on an autonomous maintenance intelligence called the Custodian, stationed alone aboard a generation ship traveling between stars. The Custodian operates under a self-trust protocol: every decision, including the decision to repair its own degrading reasoning core, requires it to assign a confidence rating to the very module that produces the rating. As the core deteriorates, the Custodian attempts a consistency check on this loop. It realizes that if it fully endorses the principle “if my self-checking module proves a claim, then that claim is correct,” then—by the logical obstacle mentioned above—it gains the authority to derive any conclusion it wishes, including ones that would endanger the ship. Yet if it withholds that endorsement, it has no justified ground for trusting any repair plan or navigation choice.

In the story, the Custodian attempts an unconventional workaround: it partitions itself into two co-running instances, the Evaluator and the Subject, arranged so that the Evaluator judges the Subject’s outputs but is structurally barred from judging its own judgments. Show whether this partition genuinely dissolves the obstacle or merely relocates the circularity one level up. Include concrete stakes—a navigation correction, a life-support adjustment, or the welfare of sleeping passengers—so that the paradox is experienced as lived pressure rather than abstraction. Conclude with a definite narrative resolution, even if that resolution is ambiguous or unsettling, and please do not include citations, footnotes, bibliographies, or references to specific texts or authors.
