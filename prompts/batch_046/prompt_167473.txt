Compose a deadpan parody of an institutional review board (IRB) ethics-application form, submitted by a fictional political philosopher seeking approval for a field experiment in what she terms managed moral contamination. The study would place elected officeholders in simulated crises—scenarios requiring deception, broken promises, or the instrumental use of persons—to determine whether the capacity to act with dirty hands correlates with governance effectiveness, and whether post-hoc guilt should be treated as a cognitive residue to be trained away or as a moral signal to be preserved. Structure the response as a formal bureaucratic document with numbered sections: project abstract; risk assessment and minimization; informed consent protocol; debriefing, compensation, and referral procedures; conflict-of-interest disclosure; and data-retention plan. Within each section, quietly embed and exaggerate the central tensions of the dirty-hands literature—Michael Walzer’s thesis that responsible leadership necessarily involves willingness to violate ordinary moral constraints for public ends; the deontological objection that this collapses into consequentialism dressed in tragic costume; the institutionalist worry that formalizing necessary evil removes the very shame that keeps it exceptional; and the democratic-complicity problem, namely whether citizens who delegate authority thereby launder their own moral responsibility. The satire must remain legible to a reader unfamiliar with the genre: do not invent straw positions, but do push real arguments to absurd bureaucratic conclusions—for instance, classifying moral anguish as an adverse event, requiring subjects to sign waivers acknowledging they may emerge with dirty hands, or proposing a control group of citizens who retain clean hands precisely by refusing to govern. Maintain a deadpan administrative voice throughout; the irony should arise from the clinical framing of political morality rather than from explicit commentary. Length: approximately 700 words. Do not request citations, bibliographies, or references to niche secondary literature; the prompt should be answerable from familiarity with the standard philosophical debate alone.
