I'm preparing my position paper for our seminar on absurdism and contemporary accounts of meaning in life, and I'd like a consistency check before I present it to the group. Here is where I have landed:

(1) I accept the Camus-style diagnosis of the absurd: human beings have an irrepressible demand for absolute clarity and coherence, and the world responds with silence.

(2) I think contemporary subjective accounts are essentially right—meaning depends on the agent's active, fulfilling engagement with projects—provided we drop any cosmic or objective-value requirement.

(3) But I also hold a further condition: fulfillment only generates genuine meaning when directed at projects the agent regards as necessarily incomplete or permanently resistant to final success; if a project can be fully completed, the meaning it confers dissolves.

(4) I take evolutionary debunking arguments to undermine intuitions about transcendent, objective meaning, but I think they leave subjective fulfillment untouched, since the latter is a first-person psychological state rather than a normative claim about the world.

(5) I believe we should live with the absurd without resolving or escaping it, yet I also think commitments (2) and (3) allow human lives to be richly meaningful nonetheless.

Please audit these commitments: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each real tension? For each tension, indicate the least costly revision and what it costs. Finally, tell me which single change to my package would clean up the most conflicts.
