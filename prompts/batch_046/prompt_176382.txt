I'm a doctoral student in philosophy of technology working on governance frameworks for autonomous systems, and I'm trying to formalize the conceptual space of AI supervision regimes beyond the standard process-versus-outcome binary that dominates current policy literature. For a working paper, I need a diagnostic taxonomy — structured as a branching decision tree — that sorts supervision philosophies according to concrete design commitments and failure-mode judgments rather than by their disciplinary labels.

Could you construct such a tree with the following branch points? First, whether the supervisor has direct epistemic access to an internal reasoning trace or is restricted to observable outputs, with a sub-split on whether that trace must be human-interpretable or may be mediated by an auxiliary verification system. Second, whether the evaluation criterion is counterfactual-stable — specifically, whether a procedurally correct system that produces a harmful outcome due to environmental noise receives the same judgment as one that produces harm through procedural failure, and conversely for beneficial outcomes arising from flawed procedures. Third, whether oversight is applied continuously during operation or only at discrete deployment checkpoints, and if continuously, whether intervention is triggered by deviation from a specified procedural norm or by deviation from an expected outcome trajectory. Fourth, whether the framework treats harmful capabilities as emerging solely from misaligned optimization targets (the outcome-centered diagnosis) or also from correct optimization over mis-specified sub-processes (the process-centered diagnosis). Fifth, whether accountability is assigned to the designer for the specification of the supervisory procedure, to the system for the realized result, or distributed, with a further split on whether this assignment survives cases of emergent behavior not traceable to either pole.

Please populate the leaves with at least: strict outcome-based consequentialism; pure procedural deontology applied to AI oversight; a hybrid regime that weights process fidelity except above a catastrophic-risk threshold; and at least one logically possible but apparently unoccupied combination — for example, a view requiring continuous process-monitoring but assigning retrospective accountability solely by outcome — which you should diagnose as either incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Also flag explicitly any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate, such as interpretability-driven outcome evaluation versus black-box process verification. Phrase every node as a concrete design choice or case judgment that a technically informed reader could answer without already knowing the philosophical taxonomy, and note briefly for each unoccupied branch why the combination seems vacant.
