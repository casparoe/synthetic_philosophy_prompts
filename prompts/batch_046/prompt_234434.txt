Write the full specification — the desiderata — that an adequate theory of social convention and its evolutionary dynamics must satisfy, taking Lewis's account of coordination problems and regularities in behavior as the baseline that any successor must either preserve or explicitly replace. Your list should cover: (i) the range of cases the theory must get right, from pure coordination (e.g., traffic rules) through mixed-motive conventions that stabilize despite conflicting preferences (e.g., linguistic deference patterns), to cases of path-dependent lock-in where the convention is demonstrably suboptimal yet resistant to deviation; (ii) the distinctions it must not collapse, especially between a mere descriptive regularity, a normative expectation backed by sanctions, and a genuinely arbitrary coordination equilibrium; (iii) the constraints it must respect regarding the knowledge conditions required for convention maintenance — in particular, whether full common knowledge of rationality and preferences is necessary, and how the theory handles conventions sustained among agents who are only partially informed or boundedly rational; (iv) an account of normative drift, where a convention initially adopted for instrumental coordination comes to be valued for its own sake or comes to serve a signaling function (e.g., costly adherence to an inefficient linguistic form as group membership marker) without losing its conventional status; and (v) whether the theory can explain rapid collapse or replacement of a convention without assuming an exogenous shock. For each desideratum, state the motivating case or conceptual consideration behind it, and flag which are non-negotiable (failure on these means the theory does not explain convention at all) versus which reflect preferences of particular camps (e.g., reduction to individual rationality, preservation of arbitrariness, resistance to functionalist overreach). Then identify any subset of these requirements that appears jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, whether maintaining strict arbitrariness together with explaining costly signaling adherence and explaining rapid collapse without exogenous causes can all be met — and say which single requirement eliminates the most candidate theories. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on evaluating specific named theories, though you may briefly note where Lewis's original framework, evolutionary game-theoretic extensions, and institutional-normative accounts stand relative to the list. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
