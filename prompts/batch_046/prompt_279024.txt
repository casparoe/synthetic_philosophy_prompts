I'm taking a seminar on logical empiricism and philosophy of value, and we recently discussed Moritz Schlick's well-known essay "On the Meaning of Life." After class, a friend tried to summarize Schlick's position for me. He said that Schlick believes the question "what is the meaning of life?" stems from a grammatical confusion: people implicitly treat life as an instrument designed for some external purpose, but since life is not a tool, asking about its purpose is as mistaken as asking about the purpose of a river. Once you recognize this category error, Schlick argues, you see that life has no transcendent purpose or ultimate metaphysical value; its only genuine significance lies in what he calls "play"—activities like art, friendship, and sport that are pursued for their own sake rather than for any result. My friend went on to claim that Schlick applies the logical-positivist verification principle to show that any metaphysical assertion about a hidden purpose of life is not merely false but cognitively meaningless—literally empty of content. From all this, he concluded that Schlick's final view is that life is "meaningless" in the traditional religious sense, and that recognizing this emptiness is actually liberating, because it frees us from searching for an external justification and allows us to live purely playfully. I feel he's capturing the broad outline, but I'm also suspicious that he's flattening Schlick's actual argument, conflating it with stronger verificationist claims made by other members of the Circle, and maybe misrepresenting Schlick's distinction between purpose and value. Could you go through his summary point by point—sentence by sentence if useful—and say which parts Schlick would accept as fair, which ones subtly misrepresent his view (especially regarding verificationism, the status of "meaninglessness," and whether "play" is meant to replace value rather than purpose), what a careful defender of Schlick would correct, and whether the conclusion that metaphysical purpose-claims are cognitively meaningless actually follows from Schlick's own reasoning about life as non-instrumental?
