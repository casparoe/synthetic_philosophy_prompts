I've been retiered from running a spectroscopy lab for about two years now — nothing world-changing, just measuring how light bounces off weird molecules — and lately I've been reading some philosphy because sitting still makes my brain itch. Here's my dillema: all my career I assumed life was about reaching the next milestone, solve the equation, publish the result, train the next student. Now that that's over I feel kind of empty, like the whole thing summed to zero. My daughter says I need a new 'purpose,' maybe writing a science memoir or mentoring kids at the university. Meanwhile I've been painting these genuinely bad watercolor landscapes, mostly just mixing blues and greens for an hour with no plan and no one to show them to. It feels good in the moment but also kind of guilty, like I'm dodging responsibility. Then I read this old essay — I think from the 1920s — that said work is always chasing a goal that vanishes the second you grab it, so you're always running toward a future that never actually arrives. The author claimed that only activities that carry their own point inside them, with no outside purpose at all, can be genuinely meaningful. So now I'm torn: is my painting the real thing and mentoring just another treadmill? Or am I using some obscure theory to justify being lazy? I don't need a therapy session, I just want to know if there's actually a philosophical leg to stand on here.
