I am a novelist who thinks regularly in terms of focalization, free indirect discourse, diegetic nesting, the implied author, the fabula versus the syuzhet, unreliable narration, and the rhetorical construction of character interiority. I want you to restate, in that precise vocabulary, the philosophical position according to which conscious experience is a user illusion — the view that the phenomenal self is not a substantive entity underlying neural activity but rather a transparent, simplified interface that conceals the underlying machinery from itself, much as a desktop interface conceals the operating system. Preserve the logical structure of the argument: that there is no inner observer or Cartesian Theater, that the self-model is transparent rather than reflective, and that what we call first-person experience is a kind of virtual object constructed for coherence rather than discovered. Once you have translated the position as faithfully as possible, write — in continuous prose only, without headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or any other formatting, exactly as one might compose a long, careful letter — the residue report. Tell me which elements map cleanly onto narrative theory, for example whether the absence of an omniscient narrator inside a text corresponds to the absence of an inner observer inside the brain, and whether the transparency of free indirect discourse offers a genuine analog to the transparency of the phenomenal self-model. Then tell me which elements survive only through distortion, such as treating neural processes as an author with intentions or treating the brain as a reader constructing a protagonist. Finally, tell me which elements have no counterpart whatsoever in the craft of fiction, and whether that absence reveals a genuine limitation in narrative vocabulary — perhaps its inability to represent a system that hides its own construction from itself — or whether it suggests that the original philosophical claim was less substantive than it appears, relying on metaphors that collapse once they are forced into the stricter grammar of point of view and focalization. Do not offer citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; I want only the conceptual architecture and an honest account of what breaks in translation.
