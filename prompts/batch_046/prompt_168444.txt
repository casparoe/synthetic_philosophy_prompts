Draft the joint statement that would emerge from an adversarial collaboration between two camps on the legitimacy and strategic logic of conscription in formally neutral states, using Switzerland, Sweden, and Finland as the shared reference set.

Camp A: Civic-republican conscriptionists, who hold that universal or near-universal military service is the only democratic mechanism capable of sustaining credible armed neutrality without alliance dependency, and that Switzerland’s militia system and Finland’s reserve-based defense exemplify how coercive citizenship produces the social cohesion and rapid mobilization capacity required for territorial deterrence in small states.

Camp B: Liberal-professional neutrality advocates, who hold that all-volunteer professional forces combined with formal neutrality or selective security integration are both strategically sufficient and normatively required in pluralistic societies, and that Sweden’s transition away from universal conscription, its interim reliance on specialized professional forces, and its subsequent adoption of selective gender-neutral recall demonstrate that technological specialization and targeted recruitment can replace mass mobilization without sacrificing credible defense.

The document should contain the following sections in order.

First, a numbered list of agreed claims about the three reference cases, phrased so that neither camp can object to the descriptive content: the structure of Swiss militia service and its legal integration with civilian occupational life; Finland’s universal male conscription, its large trained reserve, and the linkage between reserve status and territorial defense planning; and Sweden’s shift from universal conscription to professional forces, followed by its later move to selective, gender-neutral conscription with a smaller active-duty core. Do not include normative conclusions in this section.

Second, a disagreements table (or paired neutral formulations) stating exactly where the camps diverge, with each side’s position rendered in wording that side would accept. At minimum, cover: whether democratic equality requires that the burden of defense be shared universally rather than assigned to a volunteer subset; whether armed neutrality is credible only when backed by a demonstrably mobilizable citizenry, or whether professional specialization combined with strategic ambiguity achieves equivalent deterrence at lower democratic cost; and whether the Swedish trajectory since abandoning universal service shows that selective conscription is adequate, inadequate, or reveals an irreducible need for broader civic-military integration that selective recall cannot replicate.

Third, the cruxes—the sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one side. For each crux, identify a concrete comparative structural feature or empirical pattern that both camps agree is relevant, and record in advance what each camp predicts it demonstrates. Include at least this crux: whether the erosion of the reserve-civilian boundary under hybrid-threat conditions—where cyber, economic coercion, and information operations blur the distinction between trained reservist and ordinary citizen—undermines the republican claim that conscription creates a distinct democratic-military sphere, or instead strengthens the case that universal service is necessary precisely because modern conflict no longer respects that boundary. State each camp’s explicit prediction about what this erosion implies for the legitimacy of selective versus universal service models.

Fourth, a concluding assessment identifying which disagreements are genuinely substantive and which, if any, are partly verbal—for instance, whether both camps share the same underlying commitment to liberal neutrality but apply it differently to the strategic constraints of small, non-allied states, or whether they diverge at the level of first principles about coercive citizenship.

Write this as a polished, formal document. Take a definite position in your own framing: where a formulation of one side’s view could be rendered either charitably or sharply, choose the rendering that best captures the actual disagreement rather than smoothing over it; where you must prioritize one crux over another, say explicitly which one you treat as load-bearing and why. End by requiring a committed response: the drafted statement must make definite choices about which formulations each camp can genuinely sign, must record explicit predictions rather than vague compatibility claims, and must flag any point where your own reasoning forces you to adopt one characterization or one predicted outcome over its rival rather than remaining neutral.
