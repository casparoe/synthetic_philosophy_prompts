In the old repair guilds they maintain careeniums—glass boxes full of simmballs, little iron spheres stamped with glyphs. The guild's rulebook says a careenium pushes the substrate around: the glyph-pattern dosen't just trigger gears, it literally directs where the iron beads settle, like the symbols have causal weight. They even reject hidden-gear replicas that make the same patterns, calling them dead machines. What theory of symbol-to-matter causation does that practice presuppose? Could a functionalist say the guild is just wrong about the mechanism, or does their rejection of replicas commit them to something stronger—maybe that syntax itself moves the substrate? And if we swapped in a view where symbols only do work because an interpreter reads them, what part of the guild's repair policy would actually collapse?
