I'm writing a piece for my science blog about why biologists still talk about the "genetic code" even though everyone knows DNA isn't literally software. In my reaserch I came across philosophers arguing whether "DNA is a code" is just a dead metaphor or if it actually commits you to semantic information existing in nature without an interpreter. So I want to unpack that slogan precisely: break down "the genetic code" into the different claims it could be hiding.

Specifically, I'm seeing at least four layers people conflate: (1) there's a systematic mapping between codons and amino acids that's arbitrary like Morse code rather than natural like smoke-and-fire; (2) the mapping requires a decoder mechanism that isn't reducible to ordinary chemistry; (3) the sequence carries representational content about phenotypic traits, not just causal powers; and (4) the system has syntax without semantics, or maybe full intentionality. For each, tell me if it's a platitude, defensible, or flat-out false given molecular biology. And which of these is the hidden engine when people invoke "genes contain instructions" — does that argument survive once you seperate the layers, or does it collapse unless you smuggle in intentions? I don't need citations or a reading list, just the conceptual map so I don't misrepresent the science in my article.
