As a doctoral candidate in the history and philosophy of science preparing a workshop contribution on demarcation, I ask you to draft a satirical, deadpan mock research-grant proposal—approximately 600 words, in the bureaucratic register of a major interdisciplinary funding call—titled “Algorithmic Demarcation of Historical from Pseudo-Historical Narratives: A Pilot Study Using Chronological Revisionism and Atlantean Archaeology as Test Cases.” The proposal should treat the philosophical demarcation problem with complete seriousness while applying it to an obviously self-defeating project: it seeks to train a statistical classifier to distinguish legitimate ancient history from pseudohistory on the basis of textual and geometric features—appeals to suppressed knowledge, pyramid-alignment numerology, submerged-continental hypotheses—without acknowledging that the ground-truth labels for the training set already presuppose the very boundary being operationalized. Structure the document with standard sections—Abstract, Methodology, Ethics Review, Budget Justification, Expected Outcomes—each extending the proposal’s logic to an absurd yet technically coherent conclusion. In Methodology, describe a double-blind inter-rater reliability study pairing professional historians with proponents of ancient-astronaut theories, anticipating near-perfect agreement that the proposal treats as an empirical success rather than a conceptual failure. In Ethics, include a serious discussion of the emotional harm potentially caused by labeling a specific chronological-revisionist framework as pseudohistorical. In Budget, allocate substantial funds for underwater survey work in the North Atlantic. The satire should depend on accurate engagement with the real difficulties of demarcation—circularity in criterion selection, the underdetermination of historical hypotheses by available evidence, and the sociological critique of boundary-policing—without ever naming these objections explicitly or breaking the bureaucratic voice. Do not include footnotes, a bibliography, or requests for external citations; do not quote any real scholars verbatim. The result should read to someone familiar with the demarcation literature as a fair reductio ad absurdum: a straight-faced institutional document that follows the logic of objective boundary-drawing to a point where the reader recognizes its conceptual bankruptcy.
