Write a satirical job advertisement of approximately 600 words, composed in the deadpan corporate voice of a recruitment posting for a Chief Automation Ethicist at a fictional technology firm named General Intellect. The advertisement must operate entirely within the conceptual framework of Marx’s analysis of machinery, automation, and the labor process—specifically the distinction between formal subsumption and real subsumption of labor under capital, and the concept of the general intellect as developed in Marx’s writings on machinery and automation. The satire should target the contemporary technology sector’s ideology that automation liberates labor by reducing necessary labor-time, while structurally demonstrating that the proposed role deepens real subsumption: every claimed benefit to the worker, such as reduced toil, cognitive offloading, or flexible scheduling, must be shown to depend on integrating the worker’s mental capacities more thoroughly into fixed capital. Maintain absolute deadpan sincerity—no winking, no explicit moral commentary—so that the contradictions emerge organically from the job description itself. Include a Requirements section listing competencies translated into Marxian categories, for instance the capacity to convert living labor into dead labor without psychological resistance, or demonstrated experience in scaling collective cognitive capacities while preserving the appearance of individual worker autonomy. Conclude with a boilerplate corporate diversity and inclusion statement that inadvertently reveals that all produced general intellect is privately appropriated. The piece should be legible to a reader familiar with Marx’s technological writings, but it must not contain bibliographic references, footnotes, direct quotations, or citations. Ensure internal consistency: the corporate voice must never slip into a critique that assumes genuine worker autonomy is achievable within the firm’s organizational logic, since under real subsumption such autonomy is structurally incompatible with the role being advertised.
