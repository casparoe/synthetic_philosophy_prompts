Write a dialogue between two philosophers arguing over beers about whether moral progress is realy this expanding-circle thing. One defendes the standard Singer-style view — history shows us gradually including more beings, abolition of slavery, animal welfare norms shifting, that sort of thing. The other thinks it's mostly an illusion: what looks like horizontal expansion is actualy vertical contraction. They float this "moral thermostat" idea — people have a roughly fixed budget of moral concern, so widening the circle to distant strangers or future generations just thins out the obligations inside. The defender should push back with concrete cases where we added obligations without diluting old ones; the skeptic should show how those cases still fit the thermostat model. They need to actually respond to each other, not talk past one another — maybe they end up fighting over whether the abolition of slavery added net concern or just shifted it from local poor people to enslaved people far away. Keep it casual, like a dive bar conversation, not a seminar. Don't have them cite papers or ask for reading lists.
