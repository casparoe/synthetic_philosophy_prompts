For a chapter on the resilience of early-twentieth-century value realism, I am testing whether Moore’s framework can survive contact with post-Nozick problems about synthetic well-being. If G.E. Moore had lived to encounter the experience-machine scenario and the subsequent hybrid theories that treat virtual achievement and AI-mediated social bonds as potential bearers of intrinsic value, how would he have deployed his open-question argument and his distinction between natural and non-natural properties? Specifically, would he claim that being embedded in a non-simulated causal world is itself an irreducible intrinsic good, or that a sufficiently perfect simulation collapses the real-artificial distinction? Reconstruct his likely moves against the idea that an artificial environment can deliver authentic knowledge, friendship, or creative accomplishment, and pinpoint where his view either absorbs or breaks under modern empirical emphasis on self-determination and genuine relatedness. Do not survey the literature; predict Moore’s specific arguments and flag any internal instability in his position regarding synthetic analogues of objective-list goods.
