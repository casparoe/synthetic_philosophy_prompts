I have come to hold that weakness of will—classical akrasia—is not a sui generis failure of practical reason or volition, but is entirely reducible to the dynamics of hyperbolic, specifically quasi-hyperbolic, temporal discounting. On this view, the phenomenon traditionally described as knowing the better course yet acting on the worse is nothing more than the predictable divergence between an agent's long-run evaluation, governed by a near-unitary discount factor, and her local evaluation, governed by a sharply reduced β parameter, which produces preference reversals without requiring any separate faculty of will to malfunction. Phenomena such as precommitment strategies, the phenomenology of internal struggle, and patterns of addiction are, on this account, fully explicable as rational responses to the interaction of these discount curves rather than as evidence for a distinct volitional capacity that can be weak or strong. I want to assess how exposed this position actually is.

What would genuinely count against it? I am not asking for a generic list of objections to behavioral economics, but for the specific observations, thought experiments, empirical findings, or theoretical results that should move a defender of this reduction. Which cases—perhaps involving reverse akrasia, in which an agent rigidly adheres to a long-term plan despite overwhelming local incentives that she genuinely endorses in the moment, or patterns of self-control that appear to operate without any calculable shift in discount rate—would be fatal rather than merely awkward? How does the view fare against results from neuroscience and clinical psychology concerning addiction, where the breakdown of agency seems to involve mechanisms such as cue-triggered habit systems that bypass evaluative representation altogether, mechanisms that are not well modeled as intertemporal preference reversals? Which of these potential defeaters would a committed reductionist concede as genuinely damaging, and which would they dismiss as misdescriptions of the underlying discount dynamics? Finally, has the view been qualified—say, by allowing that will is merely a useful folk-psychological overlay on discount dynamics—into a form that renders it unfalsifiable, and if so, is that insulation a theoretical virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind?

Please rank the defeaters you identify from decisive, in which the position collapses into a rival account or cannot accommodate the case, to merely awkward, in which it survives only by adding an auxiliary hypothesis, and indicate for each whether it targets the reductionist claim specifically or hyperbolic discounting models more generally.
