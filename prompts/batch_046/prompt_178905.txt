Write a dialogue between a mathematical Platonist and a structuralist nominalist, both engaging seriously with Benacerraf's epistemic challenge. The Platonist ought to defend the view that the dilemma—how causally situated agents can know causally isolated abstract objects—can be dissolved by treating mathematical belief-formation as a kind of "explanatory calibration" mediated by physical theories that structurally embed mathematical structures; she should argue that sustained predictive success justifies commitment to those structures without requiring direct causal interaction. The nominalist ought to counter that calibration lacking causal feedback is indistinguishable from instrumental fiction, and that embedding mathematics in physics only justifies belief in the physical model, not in an independent abstract realm. Both speakers should address the concern that Benacerraf's argument presupposes a single interactionist theory of causation, and examine whether grounding or constitutive explanation might satisfy the epistemic demand in its place. The exchange should be genuinely responsive: each party must answer the other's specific point about whether explanatory success can ever substitute for causal access in underwriting ontological commitment.
