The lottery paradox and the preface paradox have been debated in epistemology for decades without resolution: one camp insists that rational belief must be consistent and closed under logical consequence, and therefore rejects holding high-confidence individual beliefs together with the belief that one is wrong; the other camp treats belief as graded confidence governed by probabilistic thresholds, allowing both patterns. Despite broad agreement on the formal structures, neither side has moved. I suspect the stalemate is not about logic or probability but about whether the word “belief” denotes a single mental state or several functionally distinct ones—acceptance for practical reasoning, assertion, and predictive weighting—that happen to share a label. Is that diagnosis correct? Examine what each side actually says, determine whether the disagreement is verbal, substantive, or a case of answering different questions under one heading, and say what, if anything—an experiment, a conceptual distinction, or a reframed question—would count as genuine progress.
