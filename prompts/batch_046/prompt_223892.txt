Please compare Ned Block's distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness with the framework of global workspace theory (GWT) as articulated by Bernard Baars and subsequently developed by Stanislas Dehaene. In particular, examine whether GWT's mechanism of global broadcasting is best interpreted as an account of access consciousness alone, of phenomenal consciousness alone, or as an identity thesis that collapses the two, and discuss Block's claim that phenomenal consciousness can exist in the absence of global access. Then consider the following hypothetical cognitive architecture, which we may call localized recurrent processing without global broadcast (LRP-GB): a system in which sensory representations are sustained and transformed through dense local feedback loops within early sensory cortices, yet in which no information is ever made available to working memory, executive control, or verbal report mechanisms. Would Block be compelled to classify LRP-GB as possessing phenomenal consciousness without access consciousness? Would a GWT theorist be forced to deny phenomenal consciousness to LRP-GB solely because global broadcasting is absent, or might they accommodate it by treating dense local recurrence as a minimal form of workspace? Assess which framework offers the more internally coherent account of LRP-GB, and specify what kind of empirical finding—concerning, for instance, the dissociation between local neural recurrence and reportability—would best adjudicate between their predictions.
