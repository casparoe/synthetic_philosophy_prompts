In my phil of knowledge reading grp we've been stuck for months on the bootstrapping problem and easy knowledge. One camp — mostly reliabilists — says that checking a gauge against itself and then concluding it's reliable isn't knowledge, it's just rational belief, since there's no independant evidential track. The other camp — dogmatists and closure-deniers — says we do gain knowledge, but only because basic knowledge doesn't need prior justification, and the bootstrapping just shows closure fails for reliability-beliefs, not for ordinary perceptual stuff. We've run through all the standard examples: thermometer, gas gauge, chicken-sexer. Nobody budges.

I have a hunch the stall is partly that we're smushing together diachronic bootstrapping — using a source across time to validate its own past outputs — with synchronic bootstrapping, where you use it in a single self-referential loop. Maybe only the diachronic version actually yields problematic 'easy' knowledge, and the camps are talking past each other because one worries about the first and the other defends the second. But maybe I'm just inventing a distinction to avoid admitting it's bedrock intuition about what justification requires.

So: is this disagreement verbal (about the word 'knowledge'), substantive (about whether closure holds for reliability-beliefs), or are they answering different questions — one about normative structure, one about cognitive psychology — under one heading? Is there a hidden formal question about closure principles doing the work? And given your diagnosis, is there anything — a distinction, a modified case, a change of framing — that could actually move it, or is this the kind of impasse where progress shouldn't be expected?
