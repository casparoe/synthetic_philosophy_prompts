I'm on the ethics board of a regional planning authority in the Midlands. We've recieved a proposal for a public consulation process on water rights that uses a 'seven-standpoint' framework drawn from Jain anekāntavāda — treating each stakeholder's claim (farmers, ecologists, local indigenous groups, developers) as partly true from its own viewpoint, with the board's job not to pick a winner but to map the syādvāda conditions under which each holds. Some councillors think this is just relativism with extra steps and violates our statutory duty to reach a determinative recommendation within 90 days. Others say it's the only honest way to handle genuinely incommensurable values, and forcing a single answer would be epistemic violence. I need to draft a short memo for Friday's vote: recommend whether we adopt this framework for the consultation, with conditions or a fallback if yes, and take the strongest objection seriously rather than dismissing it as confusion. Don't just summarise the philosophy — show how anekāntavāda actually changes what 'reaching a decision' means in our context.
