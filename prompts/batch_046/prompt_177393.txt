I serve on the Deliberative Council of the Lower Watershed District, a bioregional body that sets binding freshwater-allocation rules across three counties. At next month’s session we must vote on whether to grant full deliberative seats—not merely advisory presence—to three categories of petitioners who fall outside our current membership boundary of registered district residents: seasonal agricultural laborers who reside here for eight months each year but maintain permanent homes elsewhere; representatives of downstream municipalities in an adjacent state whose water security is directly shaped by our reservoir-release schedules; and appointed intergenerational proxies charged with articulating the long-term ecological interests of people not yet born. The Council is split between those who defend a criterion of biographical continuity—holding that legitimate democratic membership requires durable social and ecological embeddedness, whether through citizenship, multi-generational family presence, or permanent residency—and those who argue for functional co-presence, maintaining that anyone whose vital interests are structurally determined by our decisions belongs to the demos regardless of legal status or temporal proximity.

Because I returned to this district only last year after a decade away, my own formal membership feels partly accidental, yet I am also uneasy about extending binding authority to representatives of nonexistent future persons or to officials accountable to other electorates. I need a formal decision memo for the Council—approve the expansion, reject it, or approve it with conditions—addressing specifically whether functional co-presence can define the boundaries of a demos without collapsing democratic accountability into an unbounded affected-interests principle. The memo should state a clear recommendation, present the reasoning in terms that petitioners and dissenting Council members could engage with directly, and treat the strongest philosophical objection to your recommendation with genuine intellectual honesty rather than straw-manning. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citation-based appeals to authority. I am especially concerned with the risk of recursive representation—the possibility that seating proxies for future generations and external municipalities creates nested democratic bodies with no clear termination condition—which strikes me as the weakest point in the expansionist view; I want that weakness examined openly rather than concealed. The memo should propose concrete safeguards or conditions that could address the core worry of the losing side without requiring them to abandon their fundamental commitment.
