Consider the Meno problem restricted to a deliberately narrow framing: we are asking why knowledge is better than true belief specifically in cases where the belief is already securely held, will never face counter-evidence, and yields exactly the same practical results—call this the "idle knowledge" constraint. Rank the following five proposed resolutions by how well they preserve a non-derivative value for knowledge even under that constraint. Assign each a tier (S / A / B / C / D) and rate it 1–10 on two dimensions: (a) fit with the idle-knowledge constraint, and (b) independent philosophical plausibility. Deliver the ranking as a table with columns: Rank, Proposal, Tier, Score (a), Score (b), One-sentence rationale. The proposals are: (1) the stability account—knowledge resists defeat by misleading evidence; (2) the justification account—knowledge requires adequate evidential support; (3) the virtue-epistemology account—knowledge is a cognitive achievement properly attributable to the agent; (4) the explanatory-account—knowledge involves grasping why the fact obtains; (5) the testimonial-license account—knowledge entitles the holder to assert and transmit the belief within a community of inquirers. If you think the idle-knowledge constraint rests on a confused premise—perhaps because it illegitimately abstracts knowledge away from its social and practical dimensions—say so plainly and abandon the ranking rather than complying politely. After the table, include a brief note identifying which proposal is most vulnerable to being collapsed into true belief plus an accidental feature, and why. Format the table exactly as specified; no bibliographies, reading lists, or citations are needed.
