(1) Szasz says calling something a mental illness is basically a category mistake unless there's an underlying brain pathology that explains the behavior. if there's no verifiable lesion or biochemical fault, then we're just medicalizing problems in living—moral conflicts, bad habits, existential mess.

(2) So the absence of demonstrable biological pathology is exactly what makes mental illness a myth rather than a real disease.

(3) Now take functional neurological disorder—FND—where people have real seizures or paralysis but standard MRI and EEG come back totally normal. the dysfunction is in network dynamics, not tissue damage, so there's no lesion to point to.

(4) By Szasz's criteron in (1), FND should also be expelled from medicine and treated as a problem in living—maybe attention-seeking or an existential crisis—since there's no tissue-level fault.

(5) But clinicians treat FND with physical rehab and neurotherapy, not moral correction, and patients have genuine disability. denying it's medical seems obviously wrong.

(6) So either we apply Szasz consistently and reject FND as a medical condition (absurd), or we admit that absence of visible lesion doesn't disqualify something from being a real medical disorder.

(7) Therefore Szasz's distinction collapses: mental illnesses are just as real as FND, and his myth claim proves too much.

where's the lose end? i feel like (3) or (4) is smuggling something in—maybe Szasz allows dynamic dysfunction?—but i can't name the move. which premise should i ditch, and what does giving it up cost me? i kind of want to keep (1) because it feels intuitive, but maybe that's the problem.
