For our next session, I must teach Zhuangzi's butterfly dream and the particular form of dream skepticism it raises—not as literary ornament, but as a live conceptual problem—to an audience that understands basic cognitive science (sleep stages, false awakening, predictive processing) but has no background in classical Chinese philosophy. I need a teaching plan rather than the explanation itself.

Structure the plan as follows. Second, identify the one concrete carrying example or comparative scenario that should run through the session; it must include both the phenomenological indiscernibility of dream and waking states and the absence of any internal reality-check, and it must explicitly lack both a technological framing and any appeal to a hidden true self or neural substrate. Third, map the misconceptions this audience will predictably bring or form: I expect they will read Zhuangzi as literally asserting that human beings are actually butterflies dreaming they are human; they will confuse transformation with illusion or misrepresentation; and they will import a Cartesian-style hidden self, possibly reframed through neuroscience as a default-mode substrate.
