I've been trying to determine when generalizing about the character of a people is intellectually legitimate rather than harmful stereotyping, and my intuitions are pulling in different directions. I think it's acceptable for an anthropologist to observe that in a particular society direct eye contact during negotiation is interpreted as aggression—this seems like practical cultural knowledge. I also think a historian can claim that a country's revolutionary tradition explains why its contemporary labor protests are more confrontational than those in a neighboring country—this reads as structural explanation, not essentialism. But I reject a politician arguing that immigrants from a particular region lack the local value of individual initiative, even if statistical differences exist, because the claim seems designed to justify exclusion. I'm similarly critical of a psychologist stating that one nationality is naturally more group-oriented, yet I struggle to articulate why this differs from the historian's causal claim—both invoke observable patterns. I also feel ambivalent about a novelist writing that a particular nationality possesses a distinctive tendency toward understatement rooted in centuries of class restraint: the framing sounds essentialist, but perhaps fiction allows broader brushstrokes. Recently I've encountered a proposal to reconceive national character not as shared innate dispositions but as a collectively maintained predictive model—similar to a shared grammar for anticipating others' behavior—such that describing a group as direct or reserved denotes a conventionally reinforced expectation rather than a psychological or biological essence. If this framework is adopted, can it distinguish the legitimate cases from the illegitimate ones, or does it merely relabel stereotypes in more palatable language? Is there a coherent principle behind my judgments, which ones must I abandon to preserve simplicity, and what does the resulting view predict about a case I haven't considered—say, a diplomat attributing a treaty breakdown to the other side's culturally ingrained reluctance to make explicit commitments?
