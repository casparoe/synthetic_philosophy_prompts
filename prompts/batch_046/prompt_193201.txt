I am delivering the opening remarks tomorrow at an interdisciplinary workshop on comparative economic history, and I want the speech to navigate a framing tension rather than merely decorate the proceedings with a vague philosophical gesture. I need the remarks to do real structural work: I must acknowledge the workshop's comparative ambition without endorsing its framing uncritically, address both specialists and non-specialists without invoking any bibliography or reading list, and leave the framing genuinely unsettled rather than resolved.

Please write these opening remarks as exactly five brief movements, numbered one through five, each expressed in one or two sentences, with no separate introduction or conclusion outside these five items. Within that architecture, please include one movement that explicitly names the misconception of treating technological divergence as an exogenous shock, and conclude the fifth movement with an open question that leaves the validity of the Britain-China comparison unresolved rather than settled.
