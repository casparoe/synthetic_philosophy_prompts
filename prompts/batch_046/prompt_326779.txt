Write an essay on the controversy over rational choice when expected utility is genuinely undefined—not infinite, but non-convergent—focusing on the Pasadena paradox and its conceptual extensions. In the Pasadena paradox, a sequence of gambles offers exponentially growing payoffs against probabilities that shrink at a rate leaving the expectation oscillating without limit; the central dispute is whether rationality demands abstention, permits adoption of a completion rule (such as stage-averaging, convex-hull comparison, or distributional risk-ranking), or requires a structurally sensitive intermediate position. Weigh the abstention thesis, which holds that an undefined expectation signals an incomplete preference ordering and that rational agents should refuse the gamble or suspend judgment, against the completionist view that rationality allows an auxiliary principle—lexicographic, limit-averaging, or center-of-mass—to resolve the indeterminacy and yield a definite choice. Then address the following injected case, the tri-oscillating variant: three competing strategies yield payoff sequences whose expectations oscillate among three distinct accumulation points—positive, negative, and near-zero—so that no single limit dominates and simple uniform averaging collapses. Does this multi-directional oscillation defeat all flat completion rules, thereby strengthening the abstention thesis? Or does it instead justify a hierarchical decision procedure—first comparing the convex hull or center of mass of the accumulation set, then falling back to a secondary risk-dominance criterion—thereby preserving rational determinacy? Reach and defend a specific verdict: either that all undefined expectations warrant abstention regardless of oscillation structure; that they permit resolution whenever a properly justified completion rule applies, with the tri-oscillating case falling under a hierarchical rule; or that the correct response is structurally dependent, with single-oscillation cases resolvable by completion but multi-oscillation cases requiring suspension of judgment. Justify your conclusion through conceptual analysis of preference completeness and rational determinacy, explicitly state which opposing arguments you reject and why, and do not appeal to empirical studies, bibliographic authority, or citation.
