Write an essay on whether Galen Strawson's Basic Argument succeeds in showing that moral responsibility is impossible. The Basic Argument contends that true moral responsibility requires being causa sui, yet our actions arise from our characters, which arise from prior causes outside our control, generating an infinite regress that no agent can terminate. In your analysis, distinguish between vertical self-creation—the causal origin of one's psychological constitution in factors prior to and independent of one's rational agency—and horizontal self-creation—the ongoing, reflective revision of one's values, commitments, and practical identity through reasoning and deliberation. Examine whether horizontal self-creation can block the regress that undermines vertical self-creation, or whether every act of reflection remains causally determined by prior mental states in a way that reinstates the regress. Consider the strongest compatibilist reply, which might claim that autonomy consists not in being an uncaused cause but in the coherence and responsiveness of one's reflective structure. After weighing these considerations, reach and defend a specific conclusion: either that the Basic Argument survives the appeal to horizontal self-creation and moral responsibility is indeed impossible, or that horizontal self-creation provides a sufficient basis for responsibility despite the failure of vertical self-creation. Do not merely summarize positions; argue for your verdict.
