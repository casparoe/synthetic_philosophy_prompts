(4) sam: but then a life full of deep friendship, artistic creation, and wonder — but with some struggle — is no better than the pill-life, as long as neither involves suffering. that makes well-being way too cheap; you can't tell a fulfilled artist from a blank wall.

(5) jordan: you can tell them apart causally, but not by well-being. the artist still has cravings — for recognition, for beauty, for connection. those are disturbances, even if they feel pleasant. the pill-person is closer to the ideal.

(6) sam: so even joy is just a disturbance? and if both lives have zero suffering, they're equally good? then why prefer being awake? and why help anyone? without any craving, there's no reason to do anything, including stay alive.

(7) jordan: maybe basic maintenance drives stay intact but the suffering-ones are gone. and we help others instrumentally — seeing their suffering causes us discomfort, so relieving it removes our own disturbance.
