I'm helping plan a weekend retreat for my research group—like ten grad students and a couple faculty—and we're fighting about whether to include a wine-and-cheese social on saturday night. One advisor, who's kind of old-school, says we should do it but with a specific rule: one person has to stay completely sober, not touch anything, and observe how everyone else acts once they're a bit loose. He thinks alcohol strips away the "performance" people do at work and reveals their actual moral character, and that if the sober person guides the pace—like deciding when people have had enough—the whole thing becomes a kind of character test that builds real trust. My friend in the group says that's manipulative and unethical; she argues that lowering someone's guard chemically and then evaluating them isn't revealing a "true self," it's just catching them in a vulnerable state they didn't choose. I'm torn because I do think people are more honest when relaxed, but deliberately creating that relaxation to test people feels wrong. I don't want generic team-building tips—I'm trying to figure out if there's a philosophically serious view about whether intoxication reveals real character, and whether having a sober "guide" makes the practice more legitimate or just more controlling.
