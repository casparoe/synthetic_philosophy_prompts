Consider the cluster of problems surrounding Parfit's hitchhiker and rational irrationality more broadly: situations in which an agent fares better by cultivating a disposition to act, at the decisive moment, against what her earlier self endorsed. Rather than treat this as a self-contained paradox about one-boxing versus two-boxing, I want you to adopt a specific framing for prioritizing future work: view the hitchhiker as a limit case for collective and artificial agents that can establish observable, binding pre-commitments to predictors or counterparties. Given that framing, what are the three most important open conceptual questions or lines of implication that should occupy a small research group with training in decision theory and normative ethics but without empirical neuroscience or economics resources? Defend the ordering: for each, explain why resolving it is upstream of the others, how tractable it is with current formal and philosophical tools, and what kind of intermediate result in the next few years would move it up or down your ranking. In particular, address whether the core tension is best dissolved by refining evidential reasoning, by reconceiving the conditions under which a commitment genuinely binds, or by accepting that rationality is irreducibly agent-relative across time — but do not merely list these; argue for which deserves the limited effort and which is comparatively over-discussed relative to its downstream payoff. Avoid bibliographic recommendations; I am asking for a defended research agenda, not a survey.
