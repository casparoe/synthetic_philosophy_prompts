Write a deadpan parody of a university course-catalog page for a fictional "Institute for Life-Serving Historical Practice," in which Nietzsche's three modes of relating to the past—monumental, antiquarian, and critical—are presented as competing degree tracks complete with course codes, credit hours, and bureaucratic learning outcomes. For each track, invent course titles that exaggerate both the genuine utility and the specific disadvantage Nietzsche assigns to that mode: a monumental-history seminar promising to cultivate heroic imitation but warning in fine print of pathological analogy and neglect of present conditions; an antiquarian sequence teaching reverent preservation but listing vital stagnation and institutional mummification among probable side effects; and a critical-history practicum on judicial severance from tradition requiring students to complete a discontinuity thesis, with a mandatory advisory note on the risk of historical nausea and rootlessness. The voice should belong to an overenthusiastic registrar who treats philosophical distinctions as administrative realities, complete with registration deadlines and prerequisite chains. Conclude with an advising disclaimer observing, without breaking deadpan, that any student who completes all three tracks simultaneously will, according to the Institute's own logic, have achieved a state of perfect unfitness for life. About 650 words, formatted with headings, bullet points, and course codes. Do not include real bibliographic citations; paraphrase Nietzsche's concepts in the catalog's own bureaucratic jargon.
