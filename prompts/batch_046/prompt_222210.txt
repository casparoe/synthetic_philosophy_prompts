hey - i've been writing about science and ideas for years and lately i can't stop thinking about Wittgenstein's hinge propositions in On Certainty. Here's teh take i'm flirting with: hinges aren't individual certainties at all, they're institutional maintenance protocols. When scientists treat "the earth is old" as beyond question, it's not some deep personal epistemic bedrock - it's a coordination device that keeps grant panels and peer review from melting down. Reopening a hinge has a real administrative cost, not just a psychological one. So what would actually count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward - not just the standard "kids learn stuff before institutions" line, but which intuitions, which results about individual cognition, and which cases from history of science would really damage it. What would a committed institutional-hinge defender concede is genuinely damaging versus what they'd dismiss as missing the point? And be honest: has this been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that actually a problem for a view about scientific practice?
