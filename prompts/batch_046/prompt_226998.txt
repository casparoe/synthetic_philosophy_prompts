I am designing a handout for a graduate seminar in epistemology that will serve as a diagnostic instrument for mapping where people actually stand on the reliability of introspection, rather than which label they prefer. I want a decision tree with four branching nodes, each node presenting a concrete case or claim on which positions genuinely diverge, followed by a leaf taxonomy that locates the named and unnamed positions within the logical space of answers. The cases should be: first, a report of intense phenomenal experience in the absence of behavioral or neural correlates—does the first-person report carry default authority? Second, a subject's sincere denial of an implicit bias that is subsequently revealed by behavioral measures—does introspection fail here, or does the denial target a different mental object altogether? Third, the continuous-awareness puzzle: a person claims uninterrupted access to her emotional state, yet careful tracking suggests discrete sampling—does this undermine reliability, or only show that verification is intermittent while awareness remains constant? Fourth, a gap in what I will call structural self-modeling: a subject can report her reasoning strategy but cannot access whether that strategy is implemented by domain-general or domain-specific mechanisms—does this indicate a principled boundary of introspective access, or a category mistake about what introspection could deliver?

The splits at each branch should be formulated as commitments someone can endorse or reject without knowing the literature, and the tree should be built from the logical combinations of answers first, with positions placed afterward. The leaves must include: an unrestricted direct-access view; a phenomenal-only restricted view; an inferentialist or theory-theory view; a deflationary view that denies a unified introspective faculty altogether; and at least one hybrid or unnamed position—specifically, a view that grants direct access to phenomenal character but treats propositional attitude attribution as predictive modeling rather than observation. For every answer-combination that remains unoccupied, provide a brief diagnosis: is the gap due to incoherence (for instance, accepting direct structural access while denying all attitudinal access), unattractiveness, or mere neglect? The instrument should contain no bibliography, no citation requests, and no reference to specific authors or texts by name; it should be usable by someone with no web access. I would like about six to eight leaves total, with the unoccupied nodes explicitly flagged and explained.
