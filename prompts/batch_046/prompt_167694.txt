Construct a reusable decision procedure for a national health-service ethics panel tasked with evaluating subsidy applications for medical and cognitive interventions. The panel operates under a luck-egalitarian framework: a disadvantage warrants public correction only if it results from brute luck rather than option luck, not merely because it is severe or undeserved in some broader sense. Your deliverable must be a step-by-step assessment protocol that a non-philosopher panelist can apply within a standard thirty-minute review window, with explicit evidential thresholds at each stage and clear instructions on what documentation must be present.

The procedure must handle three recurring case-types: first, a genetic predisposition to a severe condition in an individual who has never engaged with the triggering behavior; second, a disability arising from a fully informed recreational risk; third, a treatable cognitive deficit that was aggravated by the applicant's refusal of preventive care on religious grounds. For each case-type, specify the exact sequence of questions the panel asks, what clinical or testimonial evidence counts as sufficient at each step, where the protocol terminates with an automatic denial or automatic approval, and where it must suspend judgment and defer to the panel's collective value determination rather than yield a mechanical answer.

Then apply the protocol in full to each of the three cases, presenting the step-by-step reasoning with the evidence assumed available. In one of these applications, the protocol must yield an outcome that the affected party would strongly contest; explain precisely why the brute-luck/option-luck distinction produces that result despite the applicant's intuitive claim that their situation is not a mere matter of choice. Conclude by identifying one structural limitation internal to the brute-luck/option-luck distinction itself that undermines the protocol's reliability in the religious-refusal case, and describe exactly how that limitation forces the panel back into the value judgments the procedure was designed to constrain. Do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; ground the procedure solely in the conceptual distinctions of luck egalitarianism as they apply to these institutional decisions.
