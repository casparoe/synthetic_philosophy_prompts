question: hedonists say pleasure is the sole intrinsic good, but critics raise the heterogenity problem: the pleasure of a cool drink, a solved theorem, and a bass drop all seem phenomenally different. lay out the problem, then assess the intentionalist reply — that pleasures are unified not by shared feeling but by being desired for their own sake — and say whether the 'profile problem' (that pleasures differ in phenomenological shape, e.g. sharp/localized vs diffuse/global) makes that reply stronger or weaker.

student answer:
the heterogenity problem is that if pleasure is one uniform thing then every pleasure should feel identical, but clearly they dont — drinking water feels sharp and localized in the throat, while understanding a proof feels diffuse and global in the head. so hedonism seems wrong unless it can find some common element. the intentionalist reply, which is basically the desire-satisfaction theory of well-being, solves this by saying pleasure isnt defined by feeling at all but by being a desire that gets satisfied for its own sake. since all satisfied desires share the same logical structure — subject, object, satisfaction — the heterogenity disappears. also, the profile problem actually helps the intentionalist: if pleasures have different shapes then they cant all be the same feeling, which proves the feeling-based view is wrong and therefore the intentionalist view must be right. bentham was correct that pushpin equals poetry, but only if we count desire-satisfaction rather than feeling-intensity.
