After a recent family crisis in which clinicians declared my father brain-dead yet his heart continued to beat with mechanical circulatory support, leaving relatives divided over whether death had occurred and whether to continue somatic maintenance, I have been trying to understand the conceptual fault lines rather than the emotional ones. Draft the joint statement that a committed advocate of whole-brain neurological criteria and a committed advocate of circulatory-respiratory criteria could both sign after a rigorous adversarial collaboration. The statement should contain: a numbered section of agreed claims about integrated organismic function, the biology of brainstem failure, mechanical circulatory support, and the legal and clinical purpose of death determination; a precisely worded disagreements section, with each camp’s own formulation of its position—especially whether death is the irreversible loss of the organism’s unified functioning or the irreversible cessation of heartbeat and breathing—phrased so that each side accepts its own wording; the identified cruxes, including whether sustaining a brain-dead body for fetal gestation or for normothermic organ perfusion demonstrates that the organism survives, and how spontaneous resumption of circulation minutes after withdrawal of resuscitation bears on claims about irreversibility; and, for each crux, a concrete clinical or policy scenario both sides agree is probative, together with each side’s explicit prediction about what that scenario demonstrates. The aim is conceptual architecture, not citation or legal authority; avoid bibliographies, statute references, appeals to religious doctrine, and bare lists of names. I want to know exactly where the disagreement is substantive and where it may be partly verbal.
