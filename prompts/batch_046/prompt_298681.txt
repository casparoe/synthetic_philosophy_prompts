Construct a thought experiment that isolates whether moral worth requires representational accuracy about the object of one's moral concern, or whether normative tracking and structural isomorphism are sufficient. Design a realistic, single-paragraph scenario—set in an ordinary workplace or family context, with no exotic physics or contrived devices—in which an agent fulfills a significant commitment for reasons that mirror the correct moral reason in normative kind and relational form, but the agent non-culpably misidentifies the particular bearer of the relevant relation (for instance, she believes she is honoring a promise to one person when she is in fact honoring a structurally identical commitment to another, or she applies a principle of loyalty to a misidentified partner). Engineer the case so that three distinct accounts of moral worth yield three different verdicts—full worth, partial worth, or no worth—without any of the verdicts being stipulated: (i) a Kantian account according to which worth requires acting from duty with the correct maxim and accurate conceptualization of the object to which duty is owed; (ii) a tracking account according to which worth depends only on whether the agent's motivating concern reliably covaries with the presence of the objective moral feature, irrespective of representational error; and (iii) a structural-match account according to which worth requires only that the agent's reason match the objective reason in normative structure and relational role, allowing misidentification of the particular bearer provided the bearer actually instantiates that role. After the scenario, demonstrate precisely what each account says and why, pointing to the specific feature—representational mismatch combined with actual normative satisfaction—that generates the divergence. Then explain what happens if that feature is removed (e.g., correct identification of the object), and articulate how the defender of the account that assigns the lowest worth would attack the case, specifically addressing whether non-culpable misidentification corrupts the purity of the will or merely redirects an otherwise correct motivational structure.
