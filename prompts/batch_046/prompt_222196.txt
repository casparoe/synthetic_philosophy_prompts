In my phil of mind group we've been deadlocked for months on whether ant colonies count as having a mind. The usual arguments about distributed cognition and missing subjective feel get replayed every meeting and nobody budges. Lately someone introduced this niche framing called Aunt Hillary and the Ant Fugue: imagine an ant colony whose foraging paths form an actual Bach fugue, and an observer named Aunt Hillary who can only ever watch one ant at a time, never the whole pattern. The idea was supposed to show that intentionality might be fugue-structured rather than centered in one body. But now half the room thinks this is just a metaphor and the other half thinks it proves collective minds are real, and we're going in circles. Is the stall because we're talking past each other — one side using 'mind' biologically, the other structurally — or is there a hidden empirical question about what cognition requires? Given your diagnosis, is there any conceptual distinction or thought-experiment tweak that could actually move us, or should we admit the disagreement is bedrock and stop expecting resolution?
