I had a rather long argument with a close friend over dinner about whether the physical size of a brain carries any intrinsic moral weight, and we realised we were conflating mass, neuron count, encephalisation quotient, and substrate independence without ever mapping which commitments actually separated our views. To help our discussion group make sense of this, please construct a formal taxonomy — a decision tree in which each branch is split by a concrete case or claim rather than by a theoretical label — that sorts people according to what they actually believe about brain-size relevance. The diagnostic nodes should ask whether a respondent changes their moral assessment upon learning that a sperm whale’s brain is roughly five times the mass of an adult human’s; whether a hypothetical ‘minibrain’ sustaining full human consciousness within only 200 grams of neural tissue would have an equal claim to rights; whether encephalisation quotient or absolute cortical neuron density is the appropriate metric if any physical metric is appropriate at all; and whether an artificial system whose silicon substrate exceeds the mass of a human brain but whose architecture is radically non-neural should be evaluated by that physical measure. Each leaf should be either a named position (for example, speciesist anthropocentrism, capacity-based sentientism, relational care-ethics, or a deflationary anti-metric view) or marked ‘unnamed’, accompanied by a one-sentence diagnosis of whether the vacancy is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect; please also flag any pair of leaves that your tree genuinely cannot separate on these cases, rather than papering over the overlap.

In addition, include at least one deliberately unoccupied branch that combines a strict physicalist metric with a strong anti-speciesist conclusion, and explain briefly why that logical space might remain empty. The instrument should be usable by someone without internet access, so do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or specific citations, and avoid relying on obscure named theories that only specialists would recognise. Please present your answer in British English, in a polished, formal style suitable for circulation as a handout.
