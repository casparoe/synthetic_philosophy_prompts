I'm trying to stabilize my views on population ethics and the nonidentity problem for a seminar. Here are my commitments: (1) the nonidentity problem is sound—identity-affecting choices cannot wrong the particular individuals they produce, since those individuals wouldn't otherwise exist; (2) harm is strictly person-affecting, requiring an identifiable individual made worse off relative to an available baseline; (3) there is an asymmetry: we have reason not to create suffering lives but no parallel reason to create happy ones; (4) I reject the repugnant conclusion and accept critical-level utilitarianism, so adding lives is good only if welfare exceeds a non-zero threshold; (5) we can wrong distant future generations by depleting shared resources, even though this changes who is born. Audit this package: rank the tensions by severity, say for each what has to give, identify which conflicts dissolve on closer inspection, and specify the cheapest single revision that restores the most consistency. In particular, address whether (1) undermines (5) once policy-level identity effects are acknowledged, and whether (4) robs (3) of its motivation.
