Construct a thought experiment in which an Orwellian revision account and a Stalinesque revision account of conscious experience make opposing predictions about the temporal structure of a color-phi event, constrained so that neither may invoke exotic physics, perfect predictors, demons, or neurotechnology beyond ordinary visual masking. Design the case in one paragraph: present a standard left-to-right color-phi display in which a dot changes color at the midpoint, but interrupt the sequence with a brief masked probe at 150 ms—before the second dot appears—forcing an immediate binary report about whether a color change has already occurred, then collect a retrospective free-report at 500 ms. After the scenario, in American English and in no more than 400 words total, show: (a) the reasoning by which the Orwellian view treats the 150-ms judgment as genuinely experienced but subsequently overwritten in the stream of consciousness by the later integrated state, whereas the Stalinesque view holds that the intermediate token never entered the experiential stream and only the final integrated state is retained; (b) the precise structural feature—preservation-with-rewriting versus non-tokening of the intermediate state—that drives the divergence, and whether removing the masked-probe interruption collapses both views back into a standard postdictive-integration model; (c) an explicit identification of which feature of the case does the philosophical work; and (d) how a defender of whichever view comes out worse (state which one) could attack the scenario by denying the experiential authority of the 150-ms report, and whether that reply undermines the case or merely relocates the disagreement to a theory of report-access. Do not request citations, bibliographies, or reading lists; paraphrase any theoretical positions without attributing them to named authors unless you verify the attribution with a fetched source, and if in doubt omit the attribution.
