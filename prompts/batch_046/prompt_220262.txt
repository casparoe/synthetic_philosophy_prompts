Since I recently came into possession of a modest estate comprising both inherited assets and an unresolved debt incurred by my grandparents’ failed enterprise—an obligation I never chose but which now constrains my career and housing options—I have been preoccupied with the moral logic of distributive justice. Write an essay on the controversy concerning whether luck egalitarianism can coherently apply the brute-luck versus option-luck distinction to intergenerational financial liabilities of this kind. Weigh the case for treating inherited structural debts as compensable brute luck against the case for treating them as part of the background framework within which subsequent choices are made. Your essay should reach and justify a definite conclusion: either that extending compensation across generations for such embedded liabilities is a necessary implication of the theory, or that doing so dissolves the distinction between choice and circumstance and thereby undermines the very rationale of luck egalitarianism. Write in British English, avoid bibliographies and citations, and do not conclude with an agnostic or merely summary note.
