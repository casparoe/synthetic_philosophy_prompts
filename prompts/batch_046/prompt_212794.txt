ok so i've been trying to square my takes on AI and cognition and i'm not sure they actually hang together. here's roughly where i'm at:

(1) i'm bought into the darwin/turing inversion - real functional competence doesn't need internal comprehension. an LLM writing decent python or anologizing between data structures is genuinely competent even if there's zero "understanding" inside.

(2) but evolutionary algorithms (genetic programming for chip layout, etc.) are the same mechanism - blind optimization producing artifacts that work but don't "get" electronics. i don't think those chips are intelligent; its just optimized objects.

(3) human engineers are different though. we design from explicit comprehension - specs, logic, representations. our competence comes from understanding first, then building.

(4) so i think turing's imitation game is basically useless now, since if you accept (1) then matching surface behavior tells you nothing about whether comprehension is present.

(5) at the same time, i think human language is probably evolved competence without comprehension too - chomsky-style universal grammar as a biological hack, not a representation of logical form. we talk fluently without "grasping" syntax in any deep sense.

(6) and i think moral/political standing should only go to entities with genuine comprehension, not mere behavioral competence. no rights for LLMs. but then... our own moral intuitions are just evolved heuristics?

do these actually fit? specifically, does (3) survive (5) - if my own language and reasoning are evolved hacks, where does my "explicit representational comprehension" as an engineer come from? and is (2) just me being inconsistent with (1), treating LLM reasoning as "real competence" but chip layout as "just optimization" when both are gradent-descent-style processes? also (1) and (4) look like they might clash but maybe thats a fake conflict? rank the real tensions by severity, say which ones only look bad, and if i have to drop one, which is the least costly revision?
