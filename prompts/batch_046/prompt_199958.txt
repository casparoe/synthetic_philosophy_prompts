ok so i've been banging my head against this for my epistemology seminar and now i'm suspicious of my own reasoning. here's the argument my study partner threw at me — it feels like it shows Jeffrey conditoning is useless for second-order learning, but i can't find the loose thread.

(1) Jeffrey conditioning updates beliefs on uncertian evidence via a partition {E_i}: you keep P(H|E_i) rigid and shift only the weights P(E_i).

(2) This is supposed to generalize Bayes' rule: when you can't observe a precise event, you update the probabilities of the cells you can distinguish, leaving internal structure alone.

(3) But now take a case with second-order uncertianty. i'm running an assay; i recieve a reading that might be toxic, but i also notice the lens is smudged. i'm unsure both about toxicity and about whether the smudge corrupted the result.

(4) Apply Jeffrey conditioning to the joint partition: cells are (smudged, clear) × (toxic, clean). Rigidity fixes P(toxic | smudged) and P(toxic | clear) at their prior values, and similarly for all other conditonals inside each cell.

(5) Yet noticing the smudge should change not just my belief that the lens is smudged, but also my belief about how toxic samples look under smudged conditions — that is, it should revise P(toxic | smudged) itself.

(6) Jeffrey conditioning forbids exactly that revision: within the "smudged" cell, every conditional is frozen.

(7) So either i apply Jeffrey conditioning and can't learn about evidental corruption, or i reject rigidity and do full Bayesian updating on some finer-grained space — but then i've abandoned Jeffrey conditioning for the case it was meant to cover.

(8) By the same token, any real agent facing instrument noise, biased sampling, or self-doubt faces this problem: Jeffrey conditioning either applies and freezes structural beliefs, or doesn't apply and we're back to precise-evidence Bayes.

(9) So Jeffrey conditioning is either self-defeating (it applies but blocks learning) or redundant (it never applies to genuinely uncertian second-order evidence).

where's the error? i want to keep (2) and (5) if possible. my guess is that (4) misapplies rigidity — maybe the partition shouldn't be joint but sequential? but then i don't understand what constrains the choice of partition. is the cost of saving (5) that i have to accept there's no unique "right" partition for Jeffrey updates?

there's probably also an equivocation in (7) between "abandoning Jeffrey conditioning" and "using full Bayes on a different model." i can't quite pin it.
