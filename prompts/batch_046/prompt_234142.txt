I came across two explanations that might cover this. One is the handicap principle, where bearing a visible cost proves you have hidden quality to spare. The other is counter-signaling, which I understand as the idea that people who are already secure in their status can afford to ignore the usual markers altogether. Could you compare these two frameworks precisely? I especially want to know whether counter-signaling is just a special case of the handicap principle—where the "cost" is the risk of being dismissed—or whether it relies on a different psychological mechanism entirely. If I apply both to how expertise is performed in a forum, which one predicts that a genuinely knowledgeable person will hide their competence, and which predicts they will display it conspicuously? Please lay out the comparison in clear terms, and say where the two theories actually diverge in their predictions.
