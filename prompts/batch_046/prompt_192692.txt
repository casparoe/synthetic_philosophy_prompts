Hey, I'm prepping my philosophy of language unit and I'm tired of quizzes that just ask do you support gender-neutral language? I want to use Hofstadter's Person Paper satire — you know, the one where he reverses every gendered term so man becomes wom and history becomes herstory — to build a decision tree that actually sorts people by what they think the problem is and what should be done about it.

The tree should start from one concrete commitment: Reading Hofstadter, I think the satire mainly exposes a problem in grammar, not just in attitudes. Then branch through three distinctions that usually get smushed together. First: is the bias syntactic (masculine generics are grammatically unmarked) or pragmatic (they trigger a male-default mental image)? Second: if you want reform, is lexical substitution enough (singular they, chairperson) or do you need morphological neutralization (stripping gendered morphemes like -man or -ess entirely, like Hofstadter's personpaper gag taken seriously)? Third: does reversing the gender poles actually dismantle the default, or does it just reinforce binary thinking by flipping it?

Each node should be a yes/no/unsure answer to a concrete case — for example, If a language had no gendered pronouns but still used he as generic for doctors, is the problem grammatical or social? — so students answer without knowing the philosophical label. The leaves should be named positions: the grammatical descriptivist, the pragmatic-frame reformer, the morphological neutralist, the reversal-skeptic, and the deflationary pragmatist who thinks it's all overblown. For any combination of answers that lands in empty space, flag whether it's incoherent, unexplored, or just ugly. Also flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really seperate — like if two views agree on every practical reform but differ only in metaphysical backstory about language shaping thought.

No citations, no reading lists, and write it casual — this is for my syllabus, not a journal. You can definately include a typo or two.
