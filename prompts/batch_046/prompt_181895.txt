I am a senior proposing an independent philosophy paper on metaontology, and I need a thought experiment built precisely to specification rather than a familiar classroom example. Please construct the following.

Design a realistic, single-paragraph anthropological scenario: researchers document an isolated society with a formal practice they call “post-mortem arithmetic allocation”—a precise, rule-governed system in which a deceased person’s goods are distributed according to numerical ratios determined solely by age and gender, with no emotional ritual, no moral vocabulary, and no notion of desert. The practice occupies the same structural social role that “inheritance justice” occupies elsewhere, yet every participant treats it as pure computation.

Engineer the case so that three metaontological positions deliver distinct verdicts on the question “Does justice exist in this society?” First, a Carnapian-style framework deflationist must conclude that the dispute is verbal: it concerns which ontological framework to adopt—one that includes justice-talk or one restricted to arithmetic-allocation-talk—rather than a discovery about the world. Second, a robust institutional realist must conclude that the answer is substantively false: the institutional conditions necessary for justice are genuinely absent, so justice does not exist there. Third, a conceptual error theorist about justice must reach a different negative verdict—not via framework choice or missing institutional conditions, but because the practice lacks an essential normative component that makes the concept of justice misfire.

Your response should demonstrate that these verdicts are generated by the theories, not stipulated. State the empirical facts all three agree upon, identify the belief or claim at issue, and isolate the specific structural feature of the scenario that drives the error theorist to peel away from both other positions. Then explain what happens if that feature is removed: does the case collapse into a standard verbal dispute, or does it become an ordinary empirical disagreement? Include an honest note on how a defender of the position that comes out worst—presumably the institutional realist—would attack the case, and flag the weakest point in the demonstration itself.

Constraints: no science fiction, no exotic technology, no appeals to demons or perfect predictors, no request for bibliographies or citations, and keep the scenario suitable for an undergraduate philosophy seminar.

Finally, please clearly separate what is settled in contemporary metaontology from what remains genuinely contested among specialists—particularly regarding the boundary between verbal and substantive ontological disputes—and do not present any contested interpretation as if it were the consensus view.
