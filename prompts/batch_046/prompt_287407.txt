I am composing a philosophical novel, and I require a precise extrapolation for one of my characters. She is a philosopher of mind committed to the thesis that phenomenal consciousness is best understood as a user illusion—specifically, an artifact of a system deploying a transparent, first-person self-model for predictive behavioral control, rather than a primitive metaphysical feature. Her prior judgments, delivered in dialogue throughout the manuscript, are as follows: she denies consciousness to a thermostat operating via simple feedback loops; she denies it to a contemporary large language model that generates fluent first-person reports without any unified self-model governing action selection; she affirms it in ordinary human beings, in whom the self-model is both transparent and causally efficacious for behavior; she affirms it in octopuses, despite their distributed nervous systems, on the grounds that their self-model achieves behavioral unity; she denies it in split-brain patients where two self-models function with minimal cross-integration; and she denies it in a simulated virtual agent whose so-called self-model is merely an external descriptive log consulted by an outside controller, not an internal predictive mechanism. The new case I must resolve is an embodied robotic system with a recurrent predictive-processing architecture that maintains a single, transparent self-model guiding motor planning, yet this model is entirely reconstructed every thirty seconds from fresh sensory inputs, leaving no persistent storage across reconstructions—so the system behaves with full coherence within each window but possesses no diachronic continuity of the model itself. I ask three things. First, predict her verdict: would she grant consciousness to this robot? Second, identify the two competing interpretations of her view—one that makes temporal continuity of the self-model essential, and one that makes functional transparency sufficient regardless of persistence—that accommodate all her prior judgments equally well, and state what each predicts for this robot. Third, propose the single further case that would best adjudicate between those interpretations—whether a system with perfect diachronic persistence but complete behavioral opacity, or one with deep temporal integration but a fully opaque self-model—and explain why that case breaks the tie.
