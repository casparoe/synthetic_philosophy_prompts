Please argue that abrupt climate shifts, pathogenic outbreaks, and volcanic events should be treated as relational historical actors rather than as either deterministic background conditions or mere instruments of human societies. Defend the claim that these phenomena possess non-intentional efficacy—causal weight that is irreducible to human intentions yet never operates independently of institutional mediation—using the example of a polity's collapse triggered by an eruption or epidemic. Show how this framing avoids both environmental determinism and anthropocentric reductionism, and respond to the objection that attributing "agency" to such events is only a metaphor that disguises the real explanatory work done by human social responses.
