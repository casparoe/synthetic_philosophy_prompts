I'm drafting a short philosophical essay tentatively titled The Tortoise's Missing Premise: Carroll's Dialogue as an Argument for Habitual Inference, and rather than sending a polished draft I offer the core argument in outline, asking for your critical comments on whether it holds together.

The argument runs as follows. First, in Carroll's dialogue Achilles tries to compel the Tortoise to derive Z from premises A and B by introducing a hypothetical bridge—roughly, that if A and B hold then Z must hold. The Tortoise accepts this bridge but insists it be made explicit as a further premise in a larger syllogism, producing an infinite regress. The standard reading takes this to show that logical rules, such as modus ponens, govern inference but cannot themselves be premises within it without vicious regress.

Second, I want to advance a less familiar framing: the Tortoise's strategy only gains purchase if we tacitly adopt a premise-transmission model of justification, on which accepting P and Q yields R solely via an explicit bridge-belief that itself demands justification. Under this model, logical rules are treated like empirical generalisations requiring evidential support. My central claim is that Carroll's regress dissolves once we reject premise-transmission for logical competence. Deductive inference is not justified by further beliefs but by a practised, truth-preserving cognitive disposition—what I call dispositional inferentialism. This is not Peirce's exact doctrine of habit, though it resonates with pragmatist themes; I intend it as a distinct view in which logical normativity derives from the stability of a trained disposition rather than from intuition or explicit rule-statement.

Third, the awkward commitment is that logical error becomes a kind of habit-failure or misapplication of disposition, which threatens to psychologise logic and weaken its normative force. I also draw an analogy with Agrippa's epistemic trilemma—infinitism, foundationalism, and coherentism—to suggest that Carroll's regress is structurally similar to the belief-justification regress, though I concede the analogy may conflate logical validity with epistemic warrant.
