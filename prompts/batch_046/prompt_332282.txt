I am an actuary specialising in climate-catastrophe modelling, where uncertain futures are represented as branching event trees with resolution conditions and risk-neutral pricing measures. Lately, the way we treat unresolved branches — neither assigned a definite outcome nor treated as purely epistemic gaps — has reminded me of Aristotle's discussion of future contingents, particularly the case of a sea battle that may or may not occur tomorrow. Please restate his metaphysical position — that such events currently lack a determinate truth-value — in my professional vocabulary: stochastic processes, branch probabilities, resolution nodes, and the distinction between aleatory and epistemic uncertainty. For each element of his view, say whether it translates cleanly, whether it survives only if distorted into a claim about model limitations or information deficits, and whether anything remains that has no counterpart in risk modelling at all. Then tell me whether the residue indicates that my framework is missing a genuine ontological category — real metaphysical openness — or merely reflects that actuarial tools are built for pricing and decision, not for describing the structure of time and possibility.
