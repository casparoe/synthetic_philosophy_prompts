I am a high school student preparing an independent project on the philosophy of music, and I have been working through Schopenhauer’s metaphysical account of the arts—specifically his well-known thesis that music stands apart from the other arts because it does not represent Ideas but rather objectifies the Will itself, unmediated by the principle of sufficient reason. In conversations with my peers, I have become interested in whether this framework can be applied to phenomena that emerged long after his death. One development that strikes me as philosophically puzzling is the recent clinical and technological practice of generating biometrically adaptive soundscapes: algorithmic systems that adjust harmonic texture, tempo, and spatial acoustics in real time according to a listener’s galvanic skin response and patterns of default-mode-network suppression, with the explicit therapeutic goal of dissolving the ego-boundary in patients suffering from severe dissociative disorders.

Given Schopenhauer’s commitment to music as the direct, non-representational voice of the Will, I would like to know how you think he would have assessed such technology. Would he have interpreted these adaptive environments as an unprecedentedly pure expression of the Will—precisely because they bypass both composer and listener as representational agents and instead manipulate the body’s direct response to sound—or would he have regarded the algorithmic optimization for neurological feedback as a debasement, turning music’s metaphysical transparency into an instrumental mechanism for bodily control? Furthermore, I am curious whether he would have drawn a meaningful distinction between the voluntary dissolution of self experienced in aesthetic contemplation, which he describes as a temporary liberation from the Will’s tyranny, and the engineered dissolution produced by metric-driven clinical soundscapes. Finally, how might he have reacted to the contemporary neuroscientific framing that music’s power derives from its capacity to suppress ego-related neural activity? Would that empirical finding have reinforced his claim about music’s unique ontological status, or would he have rejected the reduction of the Will’s direct objectification to a measurable brain state?
