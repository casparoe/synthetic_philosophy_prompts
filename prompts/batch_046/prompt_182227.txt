I work as a backend software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I am trying to make sense of a personal dilemma that feels deeper than I can articulate. A close friend was recently diagnosed with a progressive neurological condition that leaves her in constant pain and has forced her to leave her job; she is not religious, but she has begun asking me whether there is any point to her suffering, or whether it is simply meaningless damage. I do not share a traditional religious framework, but I am strongly inclined to think that complex systems — whether biological, social, or technical — are organized in ways that make hardship structurally productive: for instance, in distributed computing we use chaos engineering to deliberately inject failures so that networks learn resilience they could not acquire otherwise. I have begun to wonder whether human suffering might operate analogously, not as punishment or random error, but as an unavoidable stress-test that develops capacities — empathy, endurance, communal solidarity — that a frictionless system would never generate. When I cautiously raised this analogy with her, she said it sounded as though I were treating her pain as a feature request in some cosmic architecture, and I immediately felt I had failed her by offering abstraction instead of presence. Now I am caught between two intuitions: on one hand, I want to believe that suffering can have a constructive, even necessary place within a well-ordered whole; on the other hand, I worry that framing her experience this way is a moral failure — a refusal to acknowledge that some harm is genuinely pointless and that my desire for order is just a defense against chaos I cannot control. Can you help me think through whether there is a philosophically coherent way to hold that suffering serves a real, system-level purpose without thereby dismissing the concrete, undeserved harm done to the people I care about? And if my impulse to justify her condition is indeed a kind of evasion, what would it mean to face her suffering honestly without abandoning my broader belief that the world is not merely arbitrary?
