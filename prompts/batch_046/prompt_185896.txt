My study group and I have been arguing about whether our worker-owned logistics cooperative can ethically adopt the AI-driven route-optimization and warehouse-automation stack that was built for a major capitalist distributor, and I have recently turned to Marx’s writings on machinery to think it through. I believe I have reconstructed an argument that shows Marx’s own position is self-undermining, but I cannot identify the illegitimate move. Here is the argument as I understand it.

(1) In Capital, Volume I, and in the Grundrisse Fragment on Machines, Marx distinguishes formal from real subsumption: under formal subsumption capital takes over existing labor processes, but under real subsumption capital revolutionizes the labor process itself, producing machinery whose speed, scale, hierarchical division of mental and manual labor, and surveillance architecture are not accidental features but are structurally determined by the valorization imperative.

(2) If an artifact’s material form and operational logic are structurally determined by relation R, then deploying that artifact in a context lacking R does not neutralize R; rather, the artifact’s R-structured logic continues to organize practice, reproducing R’s conditions of domination unless the artifact is so radically redesigned that it is no longer the same technology.

(3) Socialist or cooperative production is defined by the abolition of R—specifically, the abolition of surplus-value extraction and of the domination of dead labor over living labor—so that necessary labor is minimized and disposable time is maximized.

(4) Therefore, adopting capitalist machinery within socialist or cooperative production necessarily re-imports capitalist relations of domination, undermining the cooperative’s purpose.

(5) Yet Marx insists that the proletariat inherits the capitalist productive apparatus and that socialism begins with its expropriation and redirection; in the Fragment on Machines he further suggests that automation developed under capitalism creates the objective conditions for socialism by reducing direct labor time and elevating the concept of the general intellect (allgemeiner Intellect)—the socialization of scientific knowledge—to the primary productive force.

(6) If (4) and (5) are both correct, then Marx’s theory of transition is self-defeating: the inherited machinery that is supposed to expand free time necessarily reproduces the domination that prevents free time.

(7) The conclusion that Marx’s transition theory is self-defeating is unacceptable, both textually and practically; therefore one of the premises must be rejected.

I want to preserve (5) if possible, because our cooperative is literally trying to inherit and redirect capitalist automation for cooperative ends. I also want to preserve (1), since it captures Marx’s distinctive claim that capital does not merely own machinery but reshapes it. That leaves (2) or the inference from (2) to (4) as the likely targets, but I am unsure whether giving either up costs us Marx’s anti-instrumentalist thesis.
