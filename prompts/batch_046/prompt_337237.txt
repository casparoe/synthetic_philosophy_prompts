In philosophical discussions of deterministic chaos, a long-standing impasse separates those who claim chaos undermines determinism from those who insist it leaves determinism untouched. The shared ground is extensive: both camps accept that deterministic nonlinear dynamics can exhibit sensitive dependence, exponential divergence of nearby trajectories, and strange attractors. The underminers argue that because initial conditions can never be specified with infinite precision, and because errors amplify without bound, determinism forfeits its predictive content and becomes indistinguishable from practical indeterminism. The defenders reply that determinism concerns only the uniqueness of evolutionary paths given exact conditions, not human predictive capacity, so chaos is metaphysically neutral.

This disagreement has persisted for decades without convergence. My hypothesis is that the stall is partly masked by an unarticulated distinction between forward unpredictability—the epistemic failure to compute future states within any realistic error margin—and backward unexplainability, a structural feature of mixing dynamics by which present observations, even if measured with perfect precision, cannot be mapped back onto a unique prior state. The underminers focus almost entirely on forward computation, treating the loss of predictive control as decisive. The defenders protect determinism by emphasizing the uniqueness of forward evolution, yet they rarely address whether determinism implicitly requires bidirectional inferential accessibility—that is, whether a world counts as deterministic only if its present state uniquely determines both its future and its past. If this distinction is indeed hidden in the debate, then the disagreement may be partly verbal: the sides are answering different questions about what determinism entails, using the same word. But it might also be substantive: perhaps one side is committed to a modal claim about the reversibility of information flow that the other rejects.

What is your diagnosis? Is the impasse a hidden disagreement about the directional scope of determinism, a difference in standards of what counts as a metaphysically relevant feature, or bedrock intuitions about whether determinism must be epistemically transparent? And given that diagnosis, what would actually move the debate—an explicit formalization of the conditions under which determinism is compatible with irreversible information loss, an argument that determinism was always intended as strictly one-directional, or a demonstration that chaos introduces a genuinely intermediate modal status between determinism and indeterminism that forces a revision of the taxonomy itself?
