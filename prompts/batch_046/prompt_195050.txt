I'm drafting a seminar paper on whether shared nationality generates special moral obligations—what is sometimes called the associative duties view—and I have built an argument that seems to prove far more than I intended, perhaps dissolving the very category of compatriot priority into a purely institutional cosmopolitanism. I want to identify the hidden slip. (1) If a group of people jointly maintain a system of coercive institutions that systematically distributes benefits and burdens among them, then members of that group owe one another obligations of priority that they do not owe to outsiders. (2) A nation's citizens are exactly such a group: they collectively sustain a legal, economic, and political framework through which resources, risks, and opportunities are channeled. (3) Therefore citizens owe one another special obligations of priority. (4) Yet the same framework also systematically distributes benefits and burdens to non-citizen residents—undocumented workers, long-term visa holders, and refugees—who are subject to its laws, contribute to its tax base, and depend on its public goods. (5) By (1), these residents are also participants in the cooperative scheme, so citizens owe them equal obligations of priority, and they owe citizens the same. (6) Conversely, citizens who have permanently emigrated, pay no domestic taxes, and are subject to no domestic coercion are no longer maintaining or participating in the scheme; by (1), remaining citizens therefore owe them no special priority. (7) It follows that the set of people to whom citizens owe special obligations is not defined by nationality but by current institutional co-participation. (8) Hence the claim that compatriots have special moral claims is either false—since non-citizen residents have identical claims—or redundant—since it applies only to those compatriots who happen still to be co-participants. (9) Therefore nationalism, understood as the thesis that shared nationality itself grounds distinctive moral weight, collapses entirely into a cosmopolitan institutionalism. I suspect there is an equivocation between being causally affected by an institution and being a co-maintainer of it, or perhaps a quantifier shift in how (1) applies across borders, but I cannot locate the exact step. Which premise should I reject, and what does that rejection cost for anyone who wants to defend associative duties?
