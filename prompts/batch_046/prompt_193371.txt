I argue that the Pasadena paradox is not a harmless convergence failure to be fixed by truncation limits, but a structural vulnerability: any decision rule that permits gambles with undefined expected utility can be adversarially exploited by an environment designer who sequences unbounded gambles to force preference reversals without violating continuity or independence. From this I derive a domain-restriction principle — rational choice functions should exclude gambles with undefined EU from their domain entirely, rather than resolving the undefinedness via a limit operation or treating it as benign.
