I'm preparing a feature on whether the Enlightenment should be read primarily as a source of universal progress or as an intellectual framework for racial domination. Rather than rehashing the standard debates, I want to stress-test the dual-frame thesis through a disciplined case study: the Haitian Revolution and its aftermath. The salient facts, as I understand them, are these. In 1791 enslaved people in Saint-Domingue launched an insurrection that eventually compelled the French National Convention to abolish slavery colony-wide in 1794. Napoleon reversed that decision in 1802, dispatching forces to restore bondage and imprison Toussaint Louverture. The revolutionaries declared independence in 1804, and in the 1805 constitution they adopted a racial definition of citizenship—defining Haitian identity in terms that inverted the universalist language of the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen—while also restricting white land ownership. In 1825 France recognized Haitian sovereignty only in exchange for a crippling indemnity, effectively turning formal independence into a long-term debt regime. Please analyze this sequence through the Enlightenment-on-trial lens. Specifically: map which Enlightenment concepts—natural rights, popular sovereignty, the hierarchical distinction between civilized and uncivilized peoples—functioned as emancipatory resources for the revolutionaries, and which functioned as instruments of domination in Napoleon's counter-revolution and in the indemnity settlement. Then identify where the theory is silent or strained: for instance, does the domination frame account for why the revolutionaries themselves embraced a particularist racial citizenship rather than pure universalism, and does the progress frame account for why a republic founded on liberty eventually imposed a generations-long financial penalty for that liberty? Finally, tell me whether the Haitian case shows that the Enlightenment legacy was irreducibly ambivalent, or whether it reveals that one dimension—progress or domination—was structurally dominant in ways the opposing frame cannot absorb.
