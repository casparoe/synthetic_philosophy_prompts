hey, writing my culture-and-ideas column and need a referee for a debate i watched unfold at this panel lastt week. two speakers -- maya (philosophy) and leo (sociology) -- got into it over guilt vs shame as moral emotions, and whether a culture can actually choose between them.

maya started: guilt is the better emotion because it's action-focused, not identity-focused. you feel bad about what you did, not about who you are in everyones eyes. shame is just social control with a mask on, and in pluralistic societies it's toxic because there's no shared honor code to reintegrate anyone.

leo fired back: guilt is the privatized one. you suffer alone, maybe confess to a therapist, but the community never sees the rupture. shame forces the group to either expel you or bring you back, which is more honest. he then introduced this idea he calls 'horizontal shaming' -- peer-driven, not authority-driven -- and said modern societies already run on it via social media and cancel culture, they just call it guilt because they love autonomy-language.

maya said leo was conflating real shame with 'guilt that has an audience.' real shame needs dense social surveillance and shared honor codes that liberal societies don't have anymore. then she dropped this concept of 'affective jurisdiction' -- the idea that a culture can set which emotional register governs moral failure, but modern pluralistic societies can't just 'choose' shame because the infrastructure is missing. it's path-dependent, like trying to run coal on an electric grid.

leo said that's backwards. he thinks guilt cultures produce 'moral outsourcing' -- people internalize a parent-figure and judge themselves in private, letting the social fabric off the hook. he argued cultures can shift, but not by decree; it's more like gardening conditions for reintegrative practices. you cultivate soil, you don't command the harvest. he said restorative justice programs do exactly this, deliberately engineering social reintegration instead of private guilt.

they turned to me and i was just scribbling notes with a broken pen. can you adjudicate move by move? rate each of the four main moves (maya's opening, leo's counter, maya's affective-jurisdiction reply, leo's gardening/reintegration reply) from clearly wrong to clearly right and briefly justify. then say who's more right overall about (a) which emotion is better and (b) whether a culture can choose. don't just say it depends -- take a position if you can, but point out exactly where each is missing something.
