I sit on my university museum's acquisitions and ethics committee. We must decide by Thursday whether to accept, as part of our permanent digital collection, a proposed generative-art series titled The Veil, Rendered—algorithmically produced portraits of Black historical figures rendered in Harlem Renaissance visual idioms, minted as non-fungible tokens with smart-contract proceeds directed automatically to a municipal reparations fund. The donor frames the work as a direct technological extension of W. E. B. Du Bois's argument that art by and about Black life should serve as propaganda for racial justice and dignity. A faculty coalition counters that algorithmic generation severs the artist's intentional truth-telling that Du Bois treated as essential to genuine propaganda, that the blockchain mechanism commodifies Black suffering, and that institutional endorsement would collapse curatorial judgment into political marketing. The committee is split between approving with conditions and rejecting outright.

Please act as a professional philosopher of art with deep familiarity with Du Bois's aesthetics, and draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. Before you write, take a moment to brainstorm the core distinctions in Du Bois's framework—between propaganda and mere sentimentality, between aesthetic beauty and didactic purpose, and between the individual artist's responsibility and the institution's curatorial role—then apply them step by step to this case. Your memo should recommend a concrete course of action (approve, reject, or approve with specific conditions and a review timeline), with reasoning that the opposing side could recognize as serious rather than dismissive. Include an honest, substantive treatment of the strongest consideration on the losing side. Finally, append a brief self-critical note identifying the interpretive claim about Du Bois that does the most work in your recommendation, what specific argument or evidence would change your mind, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail—particularly on the question of whether an algorithmic, institutionally mediated form can satisfy the conditions Du Bois placed on propaganda art. Think carefully; the recommendation must stand on conceptual analysis alone, with no request for bibliographies or citations.
