hey, for our phil bio reading group i need to write down my profile on the species problem so people can poke holes in it. here's where i'm at:

- i think species are individuals (like organisms) not natural kinds—each one is a particular spatiotemporal thing with its own evolutionary history.
- but i also think darwin was basically right that species are just well-marked varieties, so the boundary between them is arbitrary and human-made.
- i accept BSC for sexually reproducing groups, but only as an operational tool, not as saying what a species essentially is.
- i think ring species like the greenish warbler (Phylloscopus trochiloides) prove speciation is totally continuous—there's never a point where you can say okay now it's a new species.
- for conservation law i think we should ditch linnaean bins and use ESUs, since they actually track evolutionary lineages and not just labels.
- i think if two populations can interbreed and make fertile hybrids they're the same species, period, even if they've been seperated for millions of years.
- taxonomy should be purely descriptive—there's no correct grouping out there, just more or less useful ones for prediction.
- for bacteria i think we should just use a 95% ANI cutoff to call something a species, since gene flow isn't a thing there.
- and for microbes with tons of horizontal gene transfer i think we should drop phylogenetic species altogether and group by pangenome core content instead.
