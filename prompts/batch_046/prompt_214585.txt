I'm taking an advanced epistemology seminar focused on value problems in knowledge, and for my midterm essay I'm working on the Meno problem—specifically why knowledge is more valuable than true belief. I want to avoid the standard survey structure and instead evaluate five proposed explanations through a single, somewhat niche lens: what I call the lucky achievement objection. The core idea is that if knowledge's surplus value derives from being an intellectual achievement creditable to the agent's competence, then we need a principled way to exclude environmentally assisted true belief—say, a novice who accidentally applies the correct method without grasping why it works—from counting as an achievement too. If an account cannot consistently draw that line, then its explanation of the value gap collapses.

Please rank the following five explanations according to how well they survive this objection. For each, give a score from 1 to 10 and a one-sentence justification. Then place them in tiers: Tier A (survives with minimal damage), Tier B (survives only with substantial revision), and Tier C (fails to distinguish knowledge from lucky true belief).

1. The reliability-process account: knowledge is belief produced by a process that tends toward truth, and that reliability itself is the value-adding feature.
2. The virtue-theoretic achievement account: knowledge is true belief that is creditable to the agent's exercise of intellectual virtue or competence.
3. The modal-stability account: knowledge requires that the belief wouldn't easily have been false, and this modal robustness is what makes it valuable for deliberation and action.
4. The justification-as-intrinsic-good account: justification is itself an intrinsic good, so knowledge, which includes justified true belief, inherits that surplus value directly.
5. The social-warrant account: knowledge carries an implicit license to assert, teach, or rely on in testimonial exchange that mere true belief lacks, which explains its greater value in cooperative contexts.

In your reasoning, don't apply the objection mechanically; indicate for each account whether the objection strikes at its core or whether the account has an internal resource to block it. Also note which tier assignment you think is most controversial among working epistemologists, and briefly say why.
