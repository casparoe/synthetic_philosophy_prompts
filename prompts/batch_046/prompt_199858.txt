Draft eight to ten questions that press this position where the literature actually presses it. Include at least one question on how Carnap's internal/external distinction prevents planning choices from collapsing into pure decisionism once metaphysical justification is excluded; at least one on whether his empiricist treatment of values can distinguish legitimate welfare targets from adaptively formed preferences in a centrally guided economy; and at least one that anticipates the Hayekian objection about dispersed local knowledge making scientific planning impossible, together with a follow-up keyed to the likely reply that planning should remain decentralized yet scientifically coordinated. Add a question about whether the failure of logical empiricism's reductionist unity-of-science program undermines the scientific ambition of Carnap's social planning. For each question, briefly note the answer the defender is most likely to give and suggest the follow-up that should come next. Keep the questions concrete and avoid generic biography.
