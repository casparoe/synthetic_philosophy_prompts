Before I commit to any particular reconstruction of Dewey's experimentalism as a theory of democratic practice, I want the standard of adequacy laid out clearly. Write the desiderata: what any account that treats democracy as a mode of social inquiry—in the sense that collective decision-making is continuous with the experimental logic of testing hypotheses through action, revising beliefs in response to consequences, and distributing intelligence across a community—must be able to deliver.

Specify the phenomena it must explain: for instance, how democratic failure is diagnosed differently from scientific failure; how value conflict is handled when there is no shared metric for testing outcomes; how institutional memory preserves experimental learning across generations without freezing into dogma; and whether a society that systematically suppresses dissent can still be said to conduct democratic inquiry. Specify the distinctions it must respect—such as between inquiry as problem-solving and inquiry as self-transformation, between procedural fairness and epistemic quality, and between local participatory experiments and large-scale administrative coordination. Identify the boundary cases that must come out right, including whether expert-led technocracy can count as Deweyan if it produces accurate predictions but excludes public testing of consequences.

Then flag any tensions you see in the list itself: are there desiderata that pull in opposite directions, such as the demand for fallibilism and open revision versus the demand for stable, legitimate commitment to shared institutions? Indicate which requirements you take to be non-negotiable and which might be relaxed if the purpose is normative guidance rather than descriptive sociology, and say whether any subset of these demands appears jointly unsatisfiable.

Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on scoring named authors, though you may briefly note where a purely epistemic conception of democracy (oriented to correct outcomes) and a purely procedural conception (oriented to fair aggregation) each fall short against your list.

Finally, please indicate how confident you are in what you claim throughout, and single out the specific point—whether a particular tension, a boundary case, or the classification of one desideratum—about which you are least sure.
