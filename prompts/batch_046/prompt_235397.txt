From the perspective of someone who has spent more time in rationalist forums arguing about decision theory than in formal metaethics seminars, I want a rigorous specification before I evaluate any proposed resolution of the internalism/externalism debate about practical reasons. Write the desiderata: what an adequate theory of the relationship between an agent’s motivational set and her normative reasons must be able to deliver. For each requirement, state the motivating case or consideration behind it—include the classic ones (the amoralist who grasps moral reasons without being moved, the agent with systematically false second-order beliefs about her own desires, and the addict who disowns her craving-driven actions)—and flag which desiderata are genuinely non-negotiable and which are merely partisan demands of internalists or externalists. Then inject one further condition drawn from a less-discussed phenomenon: motivational compartmentalization, exemplified by a physician who applies rigorous evidence-based reasoning in clinical practice but systematically rejects identical evidential standards when making personal health decisions. Specify whether your list requires the theory to treat compartmentalized reasons as internal to each mental compartment, as external to the agent taken as a unified whole, or as dissolving the internal/external distinction altogether in such architectures. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on a scorecard of named theorists; if you briefly invoke Williams, Korsgaard, or Smith only to illustrate a tension between two of your desiderata, do so in passing, but do not make the prompt depend on knowing their texts in detail.
