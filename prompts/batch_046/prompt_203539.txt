I am drafting a one-semester independent-study proposal for a doctoral student in philosophy of economics, and I need your help turning a half-formed idea into a rigorous, workable plan. The student suspects that Keynes’s notion of the “weight of argument” in his writings on probability and uncertainty is not merely a psychological aside about confidence, but a structural feature of analogical reasoning that arises whenever premises are acknowledged to be incomplete. She wants to investigate whether this structural feature can be reconstructed formally—without collapsing weight into second-order probability, evidential diversity, or pure subjectivism—and whether any reconstruction makes Keynes’s view compatible with modern frameworks such as imprecise probability or belief revision theory.

Please produce a formal proposal of approximately 1500 words that addresses the following. First, state the precise philosophical question: is the weight of argument best understood as tracking the robustness of the premises themselves, as tracking the agent’s warranted hesitation, or as a distinct epistemic dimension that cannot be reduced to either? Second, explain why existing interpretations—both the standard subjectivist readings and the more recent evidentialist reconstructions—leave this status ambiguous, identifying the specific conceptual gap the project targets. Third, outline the method in concrete steps: begin by analyzing Keynes’s own illustrative cases of analogical judgment to extract the functional role weight plays; then compare that role against two formal frameworks to determine which, if either, can accommodate it without redundancy or collapse. Fourth, specify what a positive result would look like (for example, a reconstructed notion of weight that is non-redundant and applies to economic forecasting under structural uncertainty) and what would count as failure (for example, a demonstration that every formalization either eliminates weight or renders it irreducibly subjective). Fifth, provide a realistic semester timeline with month-by-month deliverables, including a draft conceptual map, a comparative analysis, and a final argument assessing tractability. The student will supply all citations and references herself; do not include a bibliography, literature review by citation, or requests for further reading. Avoid invoking obscure post-Keynesian technical literature outside standard philosophical and economic-methodology training, and do not treat weight as a purely psychological or behavioral concept. The proposal should argue that the question is both genuinely open and feasible within a single semester.
