As a novelist constructing a multi-generational epic, I need to understand whether history carries any directional arc—moral, structural, or providential—without reducing the question to either theology or sociology. Among the open problems in this area—whether teleology can operate without an intentional agent, whether moral progress is genuinely cumulative or merely cyclical, whether narrative closure in historiography commits one to metaphysical closure, and whether counterfactual robustness can distinguish a meaningful arc from post-hoc storytelling—which three should actually govern how I represent centuries of change? I want a defended ordering: which questions are upstream of the others, which carry genuine conceptual weight rather than academic ornament, and what would have to be false about history's structure for each to lose its urgency? I have no need for bibliographies; I need an agenda that tells me where philosophical attention should go if the goal is conceptual clarity about long-duration narrative causation rather than false resolution or pure contingency. Rank them by what changes how one thinks about the arc itself, and explain the criteria—tractability for a non-specialist, dependence on other answers, and risk of collapsing into a rival framework—that justify the sequence.
