I'm running a ninety-minute seminar for a mixed group of graduate students in law and history who have no formal philosophy training. The topic is how moral norms like slavery and dueling actually disappear: through rational argument, through shifting material interests, or through changes in the social logic of honor and reputation—drawing on Bernard Williams's claim that some norms become socially unthinkable rather than rationally refuted, and on Kwame Anthony Appiah's linking of moral revolutions to transformations in honor. I need a teaching plan, not the lecture content itself. First, lay out the exact dependency order in which the three explanatory mechanisms must be introduced so that each can be grasped without assuming the others, and explain why that sequence is necessary. Second, identify the one carrying case study that must jointly cover the decline of dueling in nineteenth-century Europe and the abolition of Atlantic chattel slavery, specifying precisely what structural feature the comparison must highlight—such as divergence in speed despite comparable economic conditions—and what confounding factor it must explicitly exclude. Third, map the misconceptions students will predictably form in order: I anticipate they will collapse the honor thesis into mere peer pressure, and they will assume the argument thesis requires philosophers publishing treatises. For each, state exactly where in the sequence it should be headed off. Fourth, design a closing exercise or question that reveals whether the group has understood the conceptual distinction among the mechanisms rather than simply adopting a partisan stance. Fifth, state clearly what should be omitted at this level—specifically, the meta-ethical debate over moral realism and relativism—and justify the omission. Finally, inject one unconventional comparative element: ask whether the rapid disappearance of the modern workplace expectation that junior employees perform substantial unpaid overtime could be analyzed through the same three mechanisms, and specify how this comparison should be woven into the carrying example without allowing it to overshadow the historical cases.
