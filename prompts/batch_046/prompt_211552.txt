At the pub last night my mates Maya and Jonas got into it over whether derived intentionality can ever loop back on itself and become original. Maya said Searle's view is clear: derivation is always one-way — a thermometer means temperature because we say so, and no internal complexity changes that. Jonas brought up Dennett's two-bitser point, that even a minimal system can have real intentionality if its states genuinely track the world, so the original/derived line is more gradient than wall. Maya said that just smuggles in original intentionality by treating tracking as intrisic. Jonas accused her of beggin the question by assuming only biological brains can be intrinsic trackers. Then Maya raised a weird case: a synthetic organism that rewrites its own sensors based on past errors, so the chain of interpretation loops back — the system partly designs the interperters that assign it meaning. Jonas said that's still derived, just self-derived. Maya replied that if self-derivation doesn't count, then human brains are also derived from evolutionary design, so Searle's distinction collapses altogether. Jonas said evolution isn't an intentional designer, so the loop is broken differently, but he couldn't quite say how. They turned to me but I was too tipsy to referee properly. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about what? In particular, does the loop-back case actually damage Searle's view, or does it just confuse the derivation story?
