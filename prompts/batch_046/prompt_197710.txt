I'm part of a small research collective that claims to operate on meritocratic norms: feedback should be direct and impersonal, and personal status should not matter. Last week, a senior member publicly ridiculed my draft, calling my core method "amateurish" and suggesting I lack the temperament for this field. The delivery was humiliating, but it also exposed a genuine error I had missed. I now see three ways to respond: demand a public apology and retaliate by highlighting his own recent failures; treat the insult as irrelevant to my worth, quietly correct the error, and move on; or document the interaction and ask our steering committee to formally reprimand him, arguing that hostile delivery undermines collaboration even when the substance is correct. Each path seems justified by a different conception of respect and status. Given that honest criticism is valuable and our collective depends on it, which framework is morally best for handling such slights, and does any one approach reliably produce healthier collective outcomes?
