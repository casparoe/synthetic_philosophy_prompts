I am drafting notes for a short essay for an informal philosophy-of-science seminar, and I would value conceptual feedback before I expand them into a full draft. The essay applies Daniel Dennett’s distinction among the physical, design, and intentional stances to large-scale scientific collaborations—specifically, whether an enterprise like the Large Hadron Collider can be said, in any non-metaphorical sense, to “discover” a particle or to “believe” a hypothesis. My working claim is that attributing beliefs or discoveries to the collaboration is always a useful fiction rather than an ontologically serious description, because the design stance already captures everything genuinely explanatory: the detectors, statistical protocols, and peer-review mechanisms are engineered to yield outputs that we then anthropomorphize. To sharpen this, I want to draw an analogy with swarm intelligence in social insects—where biologists routinely speak of the colony “deciding” to relocate, despite no individual ant possessing the relevant mental state—to argue that collective scientific intentionality is structurally parallel: we project agency onto an organized system whose parts lack it. From the physical stance, of course, both cases reduce to neurons, silicon, and chemical signaling; my worry is that this seems to collapse the three stances into two, or that I am smuggling in an unexamined commitment to methodological individualism that Dennett’s own functionalism should reject. I am also uncertain whether there remains a meaningful intermediate level—perhaps involving the normative structure of scientific justification or the irreducibility of reason-giving in collaborative contexts—that the design stance genuinely misses and that only the intentional stance can capture. Could you comment on the coherence of this outline? In particular, I am interested in whether the swarm-intelligence analogy strengthens the argument or introduces a false parallel, whether my reduction of collective belief to design-level function is too quick, and whether I have overlooked a defensible distinction between the design and intentional stances in collective contexts that would undermine the essay’s central move. I am not asking for sources, reading lists, or citations—only an assessment of whether the conceptual architecture holds together and where it might need sharpening, qualification, or retreat.
