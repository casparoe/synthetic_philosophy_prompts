Following a long argument I had with a friend last night about whether learning a conditional can ever rationally leave one's prior probabilities over the antecedent untouched, I am drafting interview questions for a philosophy podcast. The guest is an unnamed philosopher who defends a non-standard, two-stage Jeffrey-style conditioning rule designed specifically for conditional belief revision. On his view, the classic Judy Benjamin problem—where a soldier is told, “If you are not in red headquarters, then you are in blue headquarters”—only generates an apparent paradox if one illegitimately collapses the revision of credences over location conditions into a single update event, rather than separating it from the revision of an independent “routing parameter” that governs how the message is transmitted and received. I want eight to ten rigorous but conversational questions that go well beyond having him restate the Judy Benjamin setup. Specifically, include at least two questions that press on why separating these stages does not simply smuggle in a hidden prior over which stage is active; at least two that confront the objection that, on his account, Judy’s posterior credence in being in green headquarters ends up tracking a feature of message transmission that carries no direct evidential weight about her actual location; and at least one that asks what his rule predicts in a modified variant where the received message is a disjunction of conditionals with overlapping antecedents—for instance, “Either if not red then blue, or if not blue then green.” For each main question, provide a brief, pointed follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give, especially the reply that appeals to the statistical independence of the routing parameter from the location parameter. The audience will be familiar with basic Bayesian conditioning but not with the technical imaging literature, so avoid generic biographical questions and do not ask for citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
