Please explain the conceptual tension that arises within endurantism when we extend the temporary-intrinsics problem to diachronic modal inheritance: specifically, the claim that an object enduring through change might at one time possess an intrinsic capacity—say, the potential to recover a prior shape or to retain a learned skill—that is constituted by its actually having that property at another time, without being reducible to a relational feature involving that other time. Contrast this with how perdurantism, by distributing properties across temporal parts, avoids the need for such cross-temporal intrinsic inheritance. Then address whether the endurantist's standard responses—indexical analysis and adverbial modification—can consistently block this inheritance without either collapsing into a disguised relational ontology structurally equivalent to temporal parts, or denying that enduring objects have any career-spanning intrinsic modal properties, which seems costly for phenomena like resilience or memory. You should treat familiarity with the basic endurantism/perdurantism distinction as given, but focus your analysis on this specific modal-variant of the persistence problem.
