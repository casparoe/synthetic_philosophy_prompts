I've recently started working front-of-house at a busy pub in central London, and watching tourists navigate our queuing and tipping culture — or more often, fail navigate it — has got me obsessed with a view I've been calling normative tenancy. Basically, I think local conventions like queuing, tipping, and not jaywalking aren't just manners; they're genuine moral obligations, but they bind you only if you're a "tenant" in the local cooperative scheme, not merely physically present. A tourist who doesn't tip hasn't wronged anyone, they're just a rude guest; but once you've lived or worked here long enough to understand and benefit from the system, you've become a tenant, and then failing to queue properly or tip is genuinely wrong, not just tacky. I'm also toying with the idea that there's an intermediate category — "normative squatters" — people who stay long enough to benefit but pretend they're still visitors.

What would actually count against this? I don't want the standard "culture is relative" handwave. I want to know which intuitions, thought experiments, or practical results would damage it. For instance, does my view imply that a refugee who hasn't learnt local queuing norms is morally excused in a way that feels wrong? Or that a business traveller on a three-day trip has zero obligations, even if they deliberately jump the queue? Which of these defeaters would a committed normative-tenancy theorist have to concede, and which could they dismiss as misapplying the tenant/squatter distinction? Has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability — if I just keep redrawing the line between tenant and visitor whenever a counterexample comes up, is that a problem for a view of this kind? And if it turns out well-insulated, maybe collapsing into "you're obliged if you understand the norms and benefit from them," is that insulation a virtue or a vice?

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. I'm particularly interested in whether there are implications for how we should treat visitors — should we be more tollerant, or actually more demanding because they need to learn the rules quickly?
