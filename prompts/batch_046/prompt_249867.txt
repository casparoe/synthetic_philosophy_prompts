In a course on philosophy of mind I have been examining the distinction between access consciousness and phenomenal consciousness, particularly as it interacts with global workspace theory, and I want to explore a hypothetical case that moves beyond the standard blindsight examples. Imagine a musician named E. who, after selective damage to her thalamic reticular nucleus, receives an experimental "broadcast bridge" implant that fully restores fronto-parietal global workspace integration: she can report, compare, and remember auditory stimuli with perfect fidelity, yet she describes her experience as "dark data" flowing through her, insisting there is no "inner luminosity" or qualitative feel even when she hears music she once loved. Her clinicians are divided—some take her as demonstrating pure access consciousness without phenomenal consciousness, others argue phenomenal consciousness is present but inexpressible, and a few claim that global workspace broadcasting without thalamic modulation is structurally incomplete consciousness. Write a short story related to this concept, centered on E.’s first attempt to improvise a melody in the rehabilitation studio after activation, using the session to dramatize whether the absence she reports is a genuine absence of phenomenal consciousness or only a failure of access to it.

Present the narrative in exactly five scenes, each rendered in one or two sentences, with no framing introduction or conclusion outside the scenes themselves. After the fifth scene, append a brief reflective note identifying where the story takes liberties with global workspace theory or with Block’s access/phenomenal distinction, and specify the particular empirical result or philosophical argument that would cause you to reverse your interpretation of E.’s condition—whether that reversal favors pure access without phenomenal consciousness or its opposite.
