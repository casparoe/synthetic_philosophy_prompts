I'm writing up my commitments for a bioethics seminar and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is my package: (1) I reject both biological continuity and Lockean psychological continuity as grounds for honoring advance directives; bodily sameness is too thin, and strict psychological continuity is too demanding for clinical ethics because it would deny authority in precisely the cases where directives matter most. (2) Instead, I ground the directive's authority in narrative identity: the life story authored by the earlier self continues through dementia via the social and interpretive practices of caregivers and family, so the directive binds even when memory and personality have frayed. (3) I therefore honor a refusal of life-extending treatment in advanced dementia even when the patient seems content, smiles, and expresses a present will to live. (4) But I also hold that if the value-shift is so radical that the patient's present commitments are genuinely incommensurable with the earlier narrative—say, a formerly ascetic, disciplined writer now living with uninhibited, hedonistic joy with no recognition of her past—then the narrative thread has snapped and the directive's authority weakens. (5) I take autonomy to be fundamentally diachronic self-creation: binding one's future self is an exercise of freedom, not its restriction, and the earlier self's plan is part of what makes the life one's own. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and what is the least costly revision? Specifically, does (3) conflict with (5) once the patient is actively expressing contrary preferences that look like present self-creation? Does my criterion for narrative snapping in (4) secretly depend on the psychological-continuity metric I reject in (1)? And is there an apparent contradiction—perhaps between rejecting psychological continuity in (1) and still speaking of an "earlier self" in (2)—that dissolves on inspection? Rank the tensions by severity, and for each say exactly what commitment would have to give and what that costs me.
