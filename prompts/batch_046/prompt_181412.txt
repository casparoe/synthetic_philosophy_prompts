I am a doctoral student in political philosophy, and I am drafting an essay on the ethics of urban commemoration that tries to move beyond the familiar stalemate over statues and street names — whether removing them erases history, or retaining them glorifies injustice. Rather than presenting a full manuscript, I offer the core conceptual architecture below and should be most grateful for your critical comments on its coherence, philosophical plausibility, and potential objections I have overlooked.

The essay distinguishes between indexical and ambient commemoration. Indexical commemoration names a specific agent in virtue of particular achievements or virtues; ambient commemoration denotes the cumulative moral atmosphere generated by the overall pattern of names and monuments in a district. I argue that ethical evaluation ought to shift from biography to function: specifically, to whether a given toponym operates as what I term a deontic focal point — a public signal that coordinates citizens’ practical reasoning about which values are institutionally endorsed. Applying this to recent controversies over colonial-era street names in several European capitals, I suggest that the usual compromise of retention plus contextual plaque often fails because it produces a split-attention effect: the honour remains primary in navigation and social cognition, while the critique is relegated to a peripheral notice. I conclude by proposing a principle of navigational neutrality: public naming ought to avoid functioning as a live endorsement of contested moral hierarchies, regardless of the named figure’s historical provenance.

I should particularly welcome your thoughts on whether the indexical/ambient distinction holds up, whether the notion of a deontic focal point can bear the normative weight I place on it, and whether navigational neutrality collapses into a broader — and perhaps more contestable — liberal doctrine of state neutrality. Any suggestions for sharpening the argument’s conceptual architecture would also be appreciated.
