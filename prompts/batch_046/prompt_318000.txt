Write a proposal for a philosophy workshop paper of roughly 4,000 words, to be completed in one semester, on the following question: Can the archaeological and anthropological record of declining skeletal health, increased labor intensity, and emerging social stratification after the Neolithic transition support a normative judgment that agriculture was a welfare "mistake" for the populations involved, without implicitly imposing anachronistic economic metrics—such as aggregate utility, productivity analogues, or modern preference-satisfaction—onto pre-state societies?

State the question so that it has a determinate answer. Explain why existing treatments leave it open: the standard "original affluence" narrative and its progressive counter-narrative both tend to collapse welfare into a single measurable dimension (caloric abundance versus labor time, or security versus autonomy), and they rely on implicit counterfactuals about what foraging populations "would have chosen" that are not recoverable from the material record alone.

Then set out your approach. Rather than adding another empirical synthesis, propose a conceptual framework that distinguishes two irreducible welfare dimensions available within the material conditions themselves: subsistence security (predictability and redundancy of caloric access) and autonomy of labor allocation (the degree to which individuals control the timing, intensity, and social organization of their productive tasks). Use this distinction to reclassify the standard archaeological indicators—skeletal stress markers, storage architecture, settlement density, and mortuary differentiation—without reducing them to a single index.

Say what the possible conclusions are and which considerations would decide between them. One possibility is that the evidence supports a qualified "mistake" thesis restricted to autonomy loss, even if caloric security eventually improved. Another is that the two dimensions are genuinely incommensurable in the archaeological record, which would make any global welfare judgment conceptually unstable rather than false.

Give an honest assessment of what could make the project too big for a semester: the risk of overreaching into primary paleoanthropological interpretation, the difficulty of establishing conceptual incommensurability without falling into pure skepticism, and the challenge of avoiding both anachronism and uncritical acceptance of ethnographic analogies. Specify that the deliverable should be a conceptual audit of how current syntheses frame their metrics, not a new empirical study, and that failure should be defined as the framework collapsing into the very single-dimensional welfare comparisons it was designed to avoid.
