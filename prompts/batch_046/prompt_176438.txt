Hey, I'm a novelist and I also sit on the acquisitions board for the Meridian Digital Arts Centre. We've recieved a proposal for an immersive installation called 'The Mirror Loop' — visitors wear a headset that tracks biometic feedback and generates a real-time first-person narration claiming to be the visitor's own 'I'. The artist says it's an externalisation of Hofstadter's strange loop: the self is just a symbol pointing back at itself, but here the symbol is biometric and actually reshapes the visitor's self-model as it loops. Some of us think it's just provocative art; others worry it dissolves the boundary between subject and representation in a way our duty of care can't allow. I need you to draft our board's decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with clear reasons that the artist and the dissenters could both respond to. Before you write, please think carefully: act as an expert in philosophy of mind and digital aesthetics. Start with a brief brainstorm of the strongest consideration for each side, then write the formal memo. Make sure it's in British English, and please inject one fresh idea about what happens to self-reference when the loop is mediated by bodily data rather than language. Don't just summarise Hofstadter; treat the biometric loop as its own conceptual occurence. Keep it readable but formal, with an honest treatment of the losing side.
