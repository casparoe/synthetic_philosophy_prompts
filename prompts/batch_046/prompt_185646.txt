My philosophy-of-science reading group has been ruling on whether pairs of theories are genuinely underdetermined by all possible evidence. Their track record so far: Newtonian mechanics versus Newtonian mechanics plus an unobservable universal constant acceleration — underdetermined. General relativity versus GR coupled to a phantom scalar field active only beyond our cosmic horizon — underdetermined. Copenhagen versus Everett quantum mechanics with identical statistical predictions for all feasible experiments — underdetermined. Two macroeconomic models with identical forecasts for every measurable indicator but divergent, unobservable microfoundations — underdetermined. Adaptive versus spandrel explanations for the peacock's tail, both fully consistent with the fossil and genetic record — underdetermined.

Next case: Framework L assigns probabilities to logical statements via logical induction (coherence, calibration, and convergence constraints), while Framework B does so via Bayesian updating over a prior distribution on logical worlds. The two frameworks agree on every arithmetical claim whose proof has length below 10^100, but they assign different probabilities to the consistency of ZFC augmented by a specific large-cardinal axiom. That divergence is computable in principle, yet it requires more computational steps than could ever be executed within the physical bounds of our universe.

Predict the group's verdict — genuinely underdetermined, or not? Identify the implicit principle they are running: strict in-principle predictive equivalence, or practical equivalence bounded by physical computability? Point to the closest analog in their prior rulings and explain why. Then name the single further case that would best separate the remaining candidate rules — for instance, two neuroscience theories that diverge only on measurements requiring a technology that violates known physical law, versus two that diverge only on measurements that are merely prohibitively expensive but physically possible.
