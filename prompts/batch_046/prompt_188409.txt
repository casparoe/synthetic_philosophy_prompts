Write an essay on Einstein's hole argument and whether it actually kills spacetime substantivalism. Weigh the standard move—apply a diffeomorphism inside a matter-free hole, you get two metrics on the same point set, so points can't have intrinsic identity—against the reply that this just confuses active and passive transformations and that relationalists already say points only matter via field coincidences. But then here's the twist: some philosophers argue the argument secretly relies on treating the metric as a dynamical field that gets dragged along, which only makes sense if you already bought into background-independence. So maybe the hole argument is circular against a substantivalist who thinks the manifold comes first and the fields accomodate themselves to it? Reach a conclusion: does it prove relationalism, does it beg the question, or should we go with something like structural realism about spacetime? Don't just sit on the fence—defend one of these, and try to say something about whether modern quantum gravity makes the whole debate moot or just sharper.
