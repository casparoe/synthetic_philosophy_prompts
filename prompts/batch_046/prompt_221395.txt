I retired from biochemistry about three years ago and lately I've been sitting in on philosophy seminars at the local university, mostly because the arguments are more interesting than the ones in my old lab. Anyway, my study group has been chewing on expressivism and the Frege-Geach problem, and I tried to turn it into a formal chain but now it feels like it's running away from me. I even started thinking about what I'd call "semantic inertia" — the idea that an expression's primary force carries into embedded contexts like momentum — and I'm not sure if that's helping or just muddying the waters.

Here is the argument:

(1) The expressivist says "murder is wrong" expresses disapproval, not a belief about some property of wrongness.
(2) The compositionality principle says a clause makes the same semantic contribution embedded or unembedded.
(3) So in "If murder is wrong, then hiding the body is wrong too," the clause "murder is wrong" still expresses disapproval.
(4) But inside a conditional I'm not actually disapproving; I'm treating it hypotheticaly.
(5) So the embedded clause expresses an attitude that isn't present — contradiction.
(6) To avoid contradiction, the expressivist must say the embedded clause contributs nothng.
(7) But if the antecedent contributes nothing, the conditional is semantically empty.
(8) Yet we understand these conditionals perfectly, so semantic contribution can't be necessary for understanding.
(9) If contribution isn't necessary, compositionality fails not just for moral language but everywhere.
(10) Therefore the Frege-Geach problem proves language has no compositional structure at all, which is nuts.

I'd really like to hang onto (2) and (8) if I can — losing compositionality altogether feels worse than giving up expressivism. But I suspect I'm sliding between "expresses an attitude" and "makes a semantic contributon," or maybe there's a scope shift between (5) and (6). Where do I actually need to give ground, and what's the price? And please push back hard if I'm just mixing up levels or if the whole framing with semantic inertia is a red herring — don't polish it up for me if the setup is broken.
