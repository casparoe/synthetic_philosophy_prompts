As a journalist covering philosophy and conceptual science for a general-interest publication, I am commissioning a short satirical piece for our "Ideas in Practice" column. Please write approximately 600 words in the deadpan style of a meticulous consumer-technology review—modeled on the rigorous, straight-faced tone of a product-testing publication—evaluating a fictional device called the Hedonometer X1. The device claims to solve the heterogeneity problem for hedonism by measuring and ranking every human pleasure—gustatory, intellectual, emotional, aesthetic, moral—on a single universal scale through a proprietary "Felt-Tone Normalization Algorithm."

The target of the satire is the hedonist assumption that, beneath obvious qualitative differences, all pleasures share one common property (a uniform pleasantness or "feeling tone") that permits precise cross-modal comparison and quantitative aggregation. Keep the voice entirely journalistic and deadpan: the reviewer should treat the device as a serious engineering project, describing its calibration protocols, an invented "Introspectionist Coefficient" derived from early psychophysical scaling methods, and field tests comparing a symphony, a solved mathematical proof, a warm bath, and an act of forgiveness. The humor should arise strictly from the content—the reviewer should interpret every failure as a correctable technical glitch rather than a philosophical impossibility, proposing successive software patches (finer-grained taxonomies, quality-weighted multipliers, modal isolation filters) that merely replicate the original problem at greater resolution.

Engage, without naming specific philosophers, the classic hedonist distinctions between quality and quantity of pleasure and between so-called "higher" and "lower" pleasures, demonstrating that each distinction collapses under the pressure of the device's normalization algorithm. Include a sidebar or bullet-point "Verdict" that lists specific cross-modal mismatches—rating a peach against a proof against a sunset—and notes the manufacturer's promise of a firmware update that will resolve them. The piece must be fully intelligible to an educated reader unfamiliar with the philosophical debate, yet conceptually precise enough that a specialist recognizes the actual structure of the heterogeneity objection: if pleasures share nothing beyond being called "pleasant," then hedonism loses the common currency required for both its explanatory and its normative ambitions.

Do not cite philosophers by name, do not provide bibliographies or reading lists, do not quote from real texts, and do not invoke obscure technical literature that a model without retrieval access could not reasonably reconstruct. Write in polished, formal prose suitable for a science-and-ideas audience.
