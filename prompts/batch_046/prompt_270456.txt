Compare Aristotle's view of akrasia with the hyperbolic discounting model of intertemporal choice. Apply both to the same case: a student who every semester vows she'll start essays early, then begins them at 2am the night before, and afterwards feels a strange regret—its not just "that was dumb" but "that wasnt really me acting." Does hyperbolic discounting dissolve that feeling by treating it as a simple preference reversal near the deadline, or does it miss something about self-division that Aristotle captures? And which framework does better at explaining why the pattern repeats despite the regret?
