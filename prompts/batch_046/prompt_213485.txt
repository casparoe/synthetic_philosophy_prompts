If Adam Smith were alive today and studying the design of modern engagement-optimized recommendation systems—platforms that amplify content by triggering rapid empathetic reactions—how would he evaluate them using his framework of sympathy and the impartial spectator? Given his view that sympathy is naturally moderated by proximity and that moral judgment requires the reflective distance of an internal spectator, would he see algorithmic amplification of distant outrage as extending moral concern or as corrupting the conditions for impartial judgment? Specifically, how might he apply his distinction between internal self-approval and externally visible social praise to environments where approval is continuously quantified, and what would he likely conclude about the effect of such quantification on our capacity to form stable, impartial moral opinions?
