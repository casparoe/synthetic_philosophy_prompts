After a long and unresolved argument with a friend about whether East Asian thought genuinely offers a different ontology of the person than Western liberalism, I have come to defend a specific hybrid position that I call institutional relationalism. The view rejects both the standard individualist claim that persons are metaphysically prior to communities and the standard collectivist claim that communities are entities containing pre-formed individuals. Instead, it holds that in the relevant East Asian traditions—particularly as they have been institutionalized in modern constitutional democracies—personhood and obligation are co-constitutive: legal rights are recognized, but they are always interpreted as adjustments within a network of relational duties rather than as atomic trumps. The community is not a container for pre-social individuals; it is the form in which individuality is constituted.

What would genuinely count against this view? I want an honest exposure map, not a rehearsal of familiar objections. Specifically:

- Which empirical results—whether in comparative constitutional interpretation or in cross-cultural psychology—would show that these societies actually operate on liberal-individualist premises that have merely been masked by relational rhetoric?
- Which philosophical commitments about autonomy, moral agency, or the conditions for holding rights must the view abandon if relational obligations turn out to be unable to carry the normative weight I assign them?
- Is there a structural objection that forces institutional relationalism to collapse either into liberal individualism (if rights do all the explanatory work) or into authoritarian collectivism (if obligations swallow the person), and if so, what specific feature of the framework allows that collapse?
- Among the potential defeaters, distinguish those that defenders of institutional relationalism should concede as genuinely damaging from those that can be dismissed as misinterpreting the ontology.
- Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward.
- Finally, assess whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—whether any legal or psychological result can be accommodated by redescription—and say whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice for a view of this kind.
