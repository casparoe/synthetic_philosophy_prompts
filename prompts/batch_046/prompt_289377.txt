So I retired from marine biology a few years back and lately I've been hanging around this philosphy meetup near campus, mostly listening. Last week two regulars, Marcus and Elena, got into a pretty heated back-and-forth about whether consequentialism is just too demanding and I honestly couldn't tell who was winning. Marcus started by saying the classic thing: if you take the view seriously you should be donating basically everything above subsistence, which is obviously too much to ask of anyone, so there's a real moral problem with the theory itself. Elena said that's not an objection at all, it's just arithmetic describing what the view implies, and calling it "too demanding" is really just labeling our evolved bias for keeping resources close to home. Marcus pushed back that evolutionary origin doesn't automatically debunk something— we evolved to care about our kids but that doesn't make parental love just a bias we should correct for. Elena then brought up this idea she called a psychological basal rate, kind of like metabolic floor but for autonomy and stress, saying there are structural preferences (avoiding actual breakdown) versus contingent ones (wanting a nicer car), and consequentialism only runs into real demandingness problems when it pushes people below that basal line. Marcus said that's just sneaking a deontological side-constraint in through biology, that she's assuming some comfort level is sacred without arguing for it. Elena replied that she isn't claiming it's sacred, just that demandingness is partly an empirical claim about agent sustainability— if a moral system reliably breaks people's decision-making or mental health, the problem isn't that people are lazy, it's that the system ignores how agents actually function. They went back and forth a couple more rounds but I lost the thread. Can you go through their exchange move by move— rate each step from clearly wrong to clearly right, say where each is missing something, and tell me who has the stronger overall case? I'm especially curious whether Elena's basal-rate thing actually solves anything or if Marcus is right that it's just hidden deontology.
