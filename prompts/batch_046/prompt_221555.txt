Could you design a structured deliberation or role-play—complete with rules, concealed roles, and real but small social stakes—where the group must agree on a single shared rule for allocating a scarce resource in a fictional community? Each student should receive a hidden “moral syntax card” that constrains how they may justify any proposal: some cards permit only autonomy-based reasons (individual rights, harm prevention), others only community-based reasons (role obligations, loyalty), and others only divinity-based reasons (purity, natural order, respect for hierarchy). Crucially, they must speak in ordinary English, so the divergence is invisible at the level of vocabulary—two students can both call a proposal “unfair” yet mean structurally incompatible things. The rules should be arranged so that unanimous agreement is technically possible but practically unattainable unless at least one faction suppresses its grammar entirely, so participants feel both the cost of objectivism (forced translation or silence) and the pull of relativism (abandoning the common rule).
