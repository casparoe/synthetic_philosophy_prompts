Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between mnemonic primacists—who hold that a community’s collective memory should take precedence over the archival record when the two conflict in public institutions—and archival correctivists—who hold that historical evidence should correct collective memory even at social cost. The statement should contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims about the nature of collective memory, the non-neutrality of archives, and the harms both of forced correction and of uncritical acceptance; (2) a disagreements table with each side’s position formulated in wording that side would accept, isolating at least three cruxes—whether mnemonic harm justifies withholding or framing evidence, whether records produced by hostile or colonial authorities carry systematically diminished epistemic weight, and whether performative memory (constitutive of present identity) and representational history (aimed at past accuracy) can be institutionally separated without one undermining the other; (3) for each crux, a concrete shared test case that both sides agree is relevant—such as a community migration ritual contradicted by declassified satellite and land-registry data; an indigenous oral account of population loss contradicted by a colonial administrator’s census ledger; and a proposed museum with adjacent but separated “memory” and “archive” galleries—together with each side’s explicit prediction about what will happen in that case. Keep formulations precise and neutral, avoid all bibliographic references, citations, and reading lists, and present the result as numbered agreed claims followed by the disagreements table.
