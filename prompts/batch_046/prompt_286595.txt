For my metaphysics seminar, I am trying to work out the specification that an adequate theory of ontology identification must satisfy when some of the information relevant to identifying ontological kinds is in principle inaccessible. By inaccessible information I do not mean data that are merely unknown, technically concealed, or practically out of reach; I mean information that is structurally or causally shielded—whether by principled epistemic boundaries, causal isolation, or opacity built into the kind-structure itself—such that acquiring it would either be impossible for any embedded observer or would alter the very ontology being identified. Before I assess particular philosophical positions, I want the full desiderata.

Please write the requirements list: the phenomena and cases the theory must handle, the distinctions it must preserve, and the results with which it must be consistent, with the consideration behind each. Specifically, include requirements addressing: (i) ontological underdetermination when competing kind-structures are empirically equivalent with respect to all accessible evidence; (ii) the distinction between ontological kind-membership and our epistemic categorization of that membership, which must not collapse; (iii) shielded domains—entities or regions whose kind-defining properties lie behind an informational boundary—without treating them either as purely relational-structural descriptions or as brute, unknowable noumena; (iv) consistency with the anti-miraculous intuition underlying scientific realism, but without licensing commitments that exceed all empirically accessible evidence; and (v) logical coherence conditions, such as avoiding self-defeating claims about the accessibility of the ontology being proposed. For each, explain why it is a constraint.

Then flag which pairs of these desiderata are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, especially any conflict between demanding empirically accountable ontological commitment in shielded domains and demanding that such commitment not be arbitrary. Indicate which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect preferences of particular camps—such as a preference for sparse versus abundant ontologies, or for causal-structural over purely informational identification criteria.

Finally, without providing a bibliography or reading list, briefly indicate how three broad approaches fare against your list: a causal-structural account, an ontic-structural-realist account, and an epistemically constrained deflationary account. I want the criteria to do the philosophical work, not the scorecard.
