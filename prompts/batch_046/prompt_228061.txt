Design a diagnostic decision tree for a postgraduate seminar in political philosophy that classifies normative positions on the integration of religious and cultural minorities within liberal democratic states. The tree must distinguish four stances: French republican laïcité; Anglo-American multiculturalism; assimilationist liberal nationalism; and a position you should label domain-sensitive modular pluralism—the view that different social spheres (public education, labour markets, family law, and symbolic public space) ought to be governed by distinct integration logics rather than by a single overarching principle. Structure the tree around four binary branching questions, each grounded in a concrete policy scenario rather than an abstract label: whether public-school teachers may be prohibited from wearing visible religious symbols; whether the state ought to fund minority-language schools on equal terms; whether blasphemy directed at either majority or minority faiths should attract criminal penalties; and whether naturalisation should require demonstrable cultural assimilation beyond constitutional loyalty. Phrase each node as a claim a student could accept or reject without already knowing which named position it produces. Every leaf must carry either one of the four named positions or an explicit unoccupied-combination diagnosis explaining whether the gap is incoherent, unstable, or merely unexplored. If any pair of named positions lands on the same leaf across all four branches, flag them and identify the hidden premise—such as whether the state should actively forge a shared civic culture or remain strictly neutral—that actually separates them. Write your answer in British English, using precise philosophical terminology, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
