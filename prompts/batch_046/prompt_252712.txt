Most discussions I have seen center on reachability-based measures, but I am interested in a less commonly examined framing: defining impact not by comparing the world to a trajectory produced by a fully rational optimizer, but by measuring divergence from a reference trajectory generated by bounded-rational agents that lack access to the low-impact agent's internal state or action history. Please describe the most important implications of adopting this bounded-rationality baseline for impact measurement. Specifically, discuss whether this allows side-effect avoidance to be defined independently of full value specification, how it changes the identification of indirect effects mediated through other optimizing processes, what complications arise in partially observable environments with hidden state, and whether the agent could still trigger large-scale restructuring by altering the strategic landscape available to other agents.
