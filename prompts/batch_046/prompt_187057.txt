Question: In Parfit's teletransporter case he says strict identity doesn't matter, only Relation R (psychological continuity with the right causal history). Now imagine a staggered scanner: it rebuilds your brain on Mars slice-by-slice over 90 seconds, and during that window both the Earth body and the partial Mars body are awake and talking via radio. Only after the Mars version reports a seamless first-person experience do we dissolve the Earth original. Critics call this the 'phenomenal overlap problem'—if both bodies genuinely feel like 'me' during those 90 seconds, then survival can't just be psychological continuity, because there'd need to be one indivisible thread of awareness that either duplicates or splits, which reductionism can't capture.

Student answer:
Parfit's whole point is that identity isn't what matters; Relation R is. In the branch-line case he already admits identity breaks down since it's one-to-many, but each copy survives because each bears Relation R to the original. The staggered version is just a slow-motion branch. The critic's phenomenal-overlap idea misses that Parfit is a reductionist—he denies there's any further fact about being me beyond psychological relations. During the overlap, since both bodies have identical neural patterns running in sync, they actually share a single phenomenal field, so there's really only one person with two bodies rather than two people competing. So the objection backfires: duplication is impossible, which supports Parfit's claim that branching is really just one continuous stream maintained across bodies. The only real problem is that Relation R requires direct psychological connectedness at every single instant, and during the 90-second build-up the Mars copy only has partial connectedness, so technically neither body fully has Relation R in that window.
