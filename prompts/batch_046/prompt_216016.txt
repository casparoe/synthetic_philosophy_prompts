i'm trying to sort out whether i actually think history has any teleological direction, going by gut reactions to specific cases. The gradual global abolition of chattel slavery over the 19th and 20th centuries feels like justice catching up—like the arc bending, however slowly. The Weimar Republic sliding into Nazism feels like pure contigency, no hidden hand, just bad actors and worse luck. The printing press showing up in Europe right when literacy was spreading and reform was in the air feels almost providential, like parts locking into place. But Fleming finding penicillin because of a mouldy petri dish feels like pure accident—lucky, not directed—even though it saved millions. The long-run decline in interpersonal violence feels like progress, but i can't tell if that's destiny or just institutions that happened to stick. The Cuban Missile Crisis resolving peacefully feels like we dodged a bullet, not that providence steered us clear. Meanwhile, the Black Death weakening feudal bonds and eventually improving labor conditions feels like a hidden hand working through catastrophe—almost too neat to be random. So is there one principle behind when i smell directionality? Maybe it depends on whether good outcomes come out of bad intentions, or on counterfactual robustness—whether the result holds across nearby worlds? And what does it say about the case i keep flipping on: does the current spread of democratic norms despite constant backsliding feel like an arc bending, or just a noisy random walk that happens to be trending up? Give me the principle, tell me which verdict it forces me to drop, and say what it predicts for a case i haven't listed—say, the fall of the Berlin Wall.
