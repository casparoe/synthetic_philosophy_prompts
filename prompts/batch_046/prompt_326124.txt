Please write a dialogue between two equally capable graduate students — Mara, who defends a Platonist view of abstract mathematical objects, and Jules, who holds an anti-realist epistemic structuralist position — set in a university common room shortly after a lecture on causal theories of knowledge.

Jules should counter by introducing a hypothetical concrete case: a mechanical proof-checker that operates through purely physical token-manipulation — shifting symbols according to syntactic rules — yielding correct arithmetic results without any appeal to human intuition or causal contact with abstract entities. Jules should argue that this dissolves the dilemma, since knowledge of mathematics is merely mastery of formal systems with predictive success. Mara should then press Jules on whether explaining the machine's reliability requires implicit reference to the very abstract structures Jules denies, and Jules should reply that predictive correlation needs no ontological commitment beyond concrete tokens, accusing Mara of importing an empirical criterion inappropriate to pure mathematics. The tone should be that of an intelligent tutorial conversation: precise but not pedantic, with occasional hesitations, clarifying questions, and direct references back to earlier claims. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; the argument should stand on its conceptual merits alone, and please ensure that neither interlocutor is presented as a strawman.
