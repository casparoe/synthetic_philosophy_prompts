I have come to think that Nāgārjuna's tetralemma is best read not as advancing a metaphysical thesis about the ultimate nature of reality—whether things exist, do not exist, both, or neither—but as a therapeutic dismantling of the conceptual framework that makes metaphysical commitment intelligible at all. On this reading, Madhyamaka emptiness (śūnyatā) is not ontological nihilism but a kind of quietist fallibilism: it withholds assent not because reality is void, but because the conditions for meaningful assertion about inherent existence (svabhāva) are never satisfied. What would count against this interpretation? I want an honest map of its exposure: which structural moves in the tetralemma seem to commit Nāgārjuna to a substantive metaphysical claim rather than a methodological suspension; whether the reading collapses into standard nihilism or mere skepticism; how rival Madhyamaka traditions—say, Prāsaṅgika and Svātantrika approaches—treat the same textual evidence; and whether there is any internal criterion within the tradition that could adjudicate between the therapeutic and metaphysical readings. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and indicate which ones a defender of the quietist reading would concede as damaging versus which they would dismiss as misunderstandings of the soteriological purpose of the text.
