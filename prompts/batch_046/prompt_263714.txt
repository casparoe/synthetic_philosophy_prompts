my friends Maya and Leo got stuck in a long argument over coffee about evolutionary explanations of moral norms and whether they're cranes or skyhooks. Maya said explaining fairness instincts through reciprocal altruism algorithms is a classic crane: you start with simple game-theoretic interactions, build up reputation tracking, and eventually get norms without invoking anything mystical. Leo said thats actually greedy reductionism — you're trying to skip straight from neurons to culture without letting intermediate levels do real explanatory work, like institutional scaffolding that actually shapes the algorithms in the first place. Maya shot back that calling every multi-level story greedy just protects higher levels from being reduced away, which starts to smell like a skyhook defense. Leo said no, greedy reductionism specifically means denying that cranes need intermediate floors, not denying reduction altogether; he mentioned Dennetts idea that greedy reductionists rush to nail everything to the foundation while skipping whole layers of theory. Then Maya brought up this thing she called scaffold reversal: if literacy or legal institutions actually reshape the very memory and agency-detection biases that supposedly produced them, then the lower-level story depends on the higher one, and the crane is running upside-down. Leo said thats just feedback loops inside a single crane, not a reversal of explanatory priority. They asked me to pick a side but I just sat there. who's right about what here? is Maya misusing greedy reductionism, is Leo sneaking in a skyhook, and what are they both missing about how the levels actually interact?
