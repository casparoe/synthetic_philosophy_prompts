Write a joke that hinges on the philosophical disagreement between reductionist and non-reductionist accounts of collective intentionality, taking as its setting passengers on a crowded London Underground escalator. The punchline should turn specifically on whether their aligned behaviour—standing on the right and walking on the left—constitutes a genuine joint action with a Searlean we-intention, or rather an aggregation of individual I-intentions supported only by convention and mutual belief of the sort Michael Bratman describes in his analysis of shared cooperative activity. The humour should arise from the precise failure or satisfaction of Bratman’s conditions—mutual responsiveness, commitment to the joint activity, and common knowledge—rather than from generic academic or commuter stereotypes.
