I have been thinking on my own about simultaneous discoveries — Newton and Leibniz with calculus, Darwin and Wallace with natural selection — and I have tried to frame the disagreement in my own terms rather than borrowing from textbooks. I see two camps. The first, which I think of as the convergence camp, believes that once the relevant problems, observations, and mathematical tools are available in a culture, the formal solution is essentially inevitable, like a lock that any sufficiently skilled hand can open; they think the discoverer is replaceable and that credit is mostly a social convention. The second, which I think of as the path camp, believes that every discovery carries what I call cognitive residue — the particular dead ends, metaphors, and partial mistakes that shaped how the idea was built — and that this residue means the result is not fully interchangeable: if Newton had never lived, calculus might have arrived in a different form or with different conceptual priorities, and the same holds for Darwin and Wallace with evolutionary theory. I want you to draft the joint statement these two camps could both sign after a serious adversarial collaboration. Write it entirely in continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, tables, or numbered lists, as though you were composing a single long letter. Begin with the historical and conceptual claims both sides accept about these cases. Then state the precise disagreements in wording each camp would accept for itself, especially about formal equivalence versus developmental genealogy and about whether cognitive residue affects the content of the discovery or only its history. Next, identify the cruxes — the narrower questions that, if answered, would actually shift one side — and for each crux describe a concrete historical comparison or hypothetical scenario both sides agree is relevant, together with what each side predicts about it. Finally, say whether any part of the dispute collapses into a disagreement about words, and if so specify which part. I have no academic background in philosophy of science, so please avoid asking me for citations, reading lists, or references to specific scholars; just work with the ideas as I have laid them out, and keep the reasoning precise without hiding behind jargon.
