I have been examining relational conceptions of personhood—particularly those associated with ubuntu—in which moral standing is partly constituted by recognition within a community. I keep encountering a specific structural difficulty: if being recognized as a member requires being recognized by those who are already members, and their own membership depends in turn on being recognized, then there appears to be no non-circular entry point for anyone to become the first recognized member, even though such communities plainly exist. Is there an established philosophical term for this particular bootstrap circularity in relational recognition? I am not asking about mutual recognition in general, but specifically about the absence of an independent grounding point for initial membership. If the concept exists, how is it distinguished from standard Hegelian accounts of mutual recognition and from theories of collective intentionality? If it does not, how should it be carved out—what are its boundary conditions, and what should it be called?
