I'm a retired cell biologist who's been dipping into metaphyics since I stopped working in the lab -- mostly origin essentialism, the idea you couldn't have come from a different sperm and egg. At my local philosphy meetup two regulars, Maria and Jake, got stuck in a loop about it and asked me to weigh in since I actually know what happens in a petri dish.

Maria started with (1): it's just obvious, swap the sperm and you swap the genome, so it's a different person, full stop, and IVF doesn't change that because it's still the same gametes. Jake replied with (2): biology makes that messy -- mitochondrial replacement therapy takes the nucleus from mom's egg and puts it in a donor egg, so the kid has the parents' nuclear DNA but the egg cell is partly the donor's; if origin essentialism cares about the exact egg, then MRT kids aren't the people they should have been. Maria answered with (3): refine the view, what's essential is the nuclear genome combination, not the cytoplasm. Jake came back with (4): cloning via somatic cell nuclear transfer gives the exact same nuclear genome, but nobody thinks the clone is you, and the origin is a skin cell, not gametes. Maria said (5): that supports her -- the clone has a different origin, so of course it's different. Jake said (6): she's just moved the goalposts, first gametes then nuclear genome, now excluding cytoplasm without a principled reason; using biology trivia to dodge intuition doesn't work. Maria accused him of the same.

Can you go through moves (1) through (6) and rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right with a sentence why? Then say who is more right overall, and what both miss. I keep thinking about how sperm aren't unique (millions in one ejaculate with nearly identical genomes) and about epigenetic resetting in the zygote, but I'm not sure if that breaks the essentialist view or just complicates it. No need for citations, just straight reasoning.
