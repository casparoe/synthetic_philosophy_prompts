In this scenario, a novelist’s cognitive architecture is distributed across three independent substrates—the original body (A), a suspended synthetic brain in a vat (B), and a remote android (C)—but only one substrate receives active sensory-motor updates at any given time, while the others are frozen in a state of suspended narrative coherence. After an unobserved switch, the novelist asks, “Where am I now?” The student is asked whether Dennett’s framework yields a definite fact of the matter about which substrate contains the self.

“Dennett’s original thought experiment shows that the Cartesian intuition—that we are literally inside our heads—is unreliable. When his brain was placed in a vat but linked to his body by radio, he saw that consciousness need not sit in any one place. Applying this to the serialization case, since the self is not a physical substance but a pattern of information processing, the novelist is simply wherever the active feed is running. Dennett argues that the self is literally just the brain tissue generating consciousness, so when the feed moves to Body C, the novelist’s brain-state is now in C, even though the tissue in A remains intact.

At the same time, Dennett introduces the idea of a ‘center of narrative gravity,’ which means the self is a fictional construct that tracks continuity rather than substance. In the serialization protocol, because the narrative is suspended in the inactive substrates, only the active one maintains that center of gravity. Therefore there is a definite answer: the novelist is unambiguously in Body C. The common mistake is to think that because the original brain in A is still intact, identity might be split or ambiguous. But Dennett’s physicalism resolves this—numerical identity follows the causal chain of consciousness-generation, which here points uniquely to C.”
