I work in software engineering with no formal philosophy background, but I have been reading Zhuangzi’s view that death is not annihilation but a transformation of qi—what he calls wuhua—where the self has no fixed boundary to preserve. I would like to read Pixar’s Coco through that lens, focusing on the film’s idea of a “final death” that happens when a person is completely forgotten. Zhuangzi refuses to mourn his wife because he sees mourning as a confusion about the self’s permanence; does this illuminate why the family in Coco fights to keep Héctor remembered, or does it expose their attachment as the very illusion Zhuangzi warns against? At the same time, Coco’s afterlife assumes that personal identity and memory persist intact, which seems to treat the self as a stable substance rather than a shifting form. Where does the transformation view genuinely illuminate a scene like Chicharrón fading away, and where does the film’s narrative resist it by framing remembrance as an ethical duty? I am preparing this for a discussion group of non-philosophers, so I want a reading that takes both the film and Zhuangzi seriously—showing what each adds that the other misses—without reducing either to a simple illustration.
