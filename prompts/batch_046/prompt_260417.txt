Fourth, in a rotating cosmological model at precisely the critical angular velocity where closed timelike curves first become possible, she denies objective temporal passage. The case I now want her to adjudicate is a model with uniform non-zero vorticity throughout—so that every local inertial frame experiences rotation—yet embedded in an open hyperbolic spatial geometry that rigorously prevents the closure of any timelike curve, meaning no observer could ever encounter their own past. The model thus possesses global rotation without global temporal loops. I would like you to predict her verdict—will she deny objective time on rotational grounds, or affirm it because the absence of closed timelike curves removes the Gödelian reductio? Please identify at least two distinct rules that are consistent with her first five judgments, state what each predicts about this rotating-but-loop-free case, and give your best guess for which rule she is actually running, with reasons. Finally, specify a further hypothetical model that would definitively distinguish these two rules from one another.
