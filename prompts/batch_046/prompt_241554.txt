I'm wondering if there's a specific name for a mental move I keep catching myself in. First example: I read a study saying people overestimate how generous they'll be in charity drives. I nod along - sure, I'm probably one of them. Then a charity call comes and I'm instantly convinced I'll donate a lot, and I think 'well I already know about that bias, so I'm acounting for it now.' I haven't actually lowered my expectation, I just feel covered. Second example: I learn that people in my field misjudge risk when tired. I agree I'm tired and biased. But then I make a tired decision and tell myself this one's okay because I'm aware of the tiredness. In both cases I accept the general flaw, but I use that acceptance like a shield for the exact case I'm in. I know there's concepts for seeing bias in others, and that other thing where you believe P but think you shouldn't believe P. But this seems different - I'm not ignoring the evidence, I'm weaponizing my awareness of it to protect the belief. Is there an established term for this shield move, or is it just a weird subtype of something? If there's no precise name, how would you draw the line between legitimately correcting for bias and just using the knowledge of bias as an excuse?
