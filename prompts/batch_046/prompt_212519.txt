Write a villanelle on the metaphysics of material constitution, taking the standard case of a statue and the clay that composes it, but complicating it with a temporary ice installation that is designed to melt over the course of a gallery exhibition. The poem should have two refrains, each voiced by one of the competing objects: the first should assert the persistence conditions of an artwork, centring on the idea that identity depends on holding a particular shape; the second should assert the persistence conditions of the underlying matter, centring on the idea that identity survives change in form. Use the villanelle's mandatory repetitions and its final couplet to do philosophical work: as the stanzas progress, the melting should force the refrains into closer proximity, tracking the problem of temporary intrinsics and the coincidence of objects with incompatible persistence conditions. By the closing quatrain, the two refrains should appear side by side without either being withdrawn, leaving open whether the exhibition contains one object that changes its properties, two objects that share every part for a time, or none at all once the form is lost. The imagery should stay precise—reference the gallery space, the slow collapse of the figure, the pool that remains—so that the poem is recognisable to someone familiar with the statue-and-clay debate, and the formal constraint should feel inevitable rather than decorative.
