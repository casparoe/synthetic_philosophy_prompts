I sit on our university's Committee on Research Integrity and we have a weird case that turned philosophical. A postdoc filed a complaint that her PI was double-publishing results from a single study across two journals with only cosmetic changes to the framing. The PI doesn't deny it, but he found out the postdoc did essentially the same thing in her PhD thesis — split one dataset into three papers with slightly different statistical cuts. Now he's arguing she lacks standing to blame him, and some committee members think we should dismiss her complaint on thier grounds alone. Others say the wrongness of double-publication doesn't depend on who reports it. I need a memo for Thursday with a concrete recomendation — dismiss, proceed with investigation, or some conditional path — with reasoning we can defend. Please engage seriously with the hypocrisy/standing issue, not just brush it off. And I want the memo to use this distinction I came up with: there's 'standing-to-report' (institutional entitlement to trigger a process) vs 'standing-to-censure' (moral authority to actually condemn), and maybe hypocrisy only kills the second but not the first? The memo should recommend wether that distinction saves the complaint or not. Make it actionable, with a timeline, and include the best argument for dismissing even if you recomend proceeding.
