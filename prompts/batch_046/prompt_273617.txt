Mira should defend a hardline Theravada-flavored no-self view: not just 'no soul,' but that the person is only a conventional designation with no ultimate reality, suffering is clinging to aggregates that have no owner, and that what binds moments together is just a dependancy of conditions, not a self. Elias should come from a phenomenological angle, not Cartesian dualism but someone who thinks embodied continuity and first-person authority do real work that anatta can't just dissolve.

Mira should respond that that's exactly right and it's liberating, not a problem, but she needs to actually address his point about first-person authority rather than dismissing it. They should talk to each other, not past each other. And please, no bibliographies or reading lists - my students can't search well and I want them reasoning from the argument itself.
