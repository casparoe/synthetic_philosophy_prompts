I have been thinking about how hard it is to tell whether a moral stance is just a personal preference or an unfair bias, since both feel identical from the inside and both can be defended with reasons. Please argue that the only reliable way to separate them is through what I call a reciprocal scaling test: keep the action fixed, swap the social positions of actor and recipient, then scale the stakes proportionally rather than merely imagining yourself in their place; if the judgment survives this transformation unchanged, treat it as preference, and if it collapses, treat it as bias. Defend the claim that this test is both necessary and sufficient, respond to the objection that altering scale changes the moral kind of the case rather than revealing bias, and present your answer as exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction and no conclusion.
