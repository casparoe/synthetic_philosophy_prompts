You are the senior compliance officer at a synthetic-materials certification body that must decide, within forty-eight hours and using only standard laboratory equipment, whether a submitted polymer sample genuinely possesses the disposition to release corrosive volatile byproducts under sustained saline-aerosol exposure—call this disposition D. Your auditors are technically competent but have no background in metaphysics, so the procedure you design must be executable as an ordered checklist with explicit stopping conditions, yet it must preserve the distinctions that actually matter: genuine absence of D; presence of D blocked by an external mask; presence of D neutralized by an internal fink; and the two non-standard complications you have begun encountering in newer composites—latency-decoupled finks, where the neutralizing mechanism is present but activates only after a prolonged delay, so that a short stress test reveals degradation that long deployment would suppress; and migratory masks, where the blocking agent is absent from the sample during testing but will be introduced by co-packaging or the manufacturing environment. Write the reusable decision procedure: the sequence of tests or observations, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, the points where the procedure issues a definite classification (absent, masked, finked—immediate or decoupled, or mimicked), the points where it must halt and hand the decision to a human because the question is one of values or deployment-context specification rather than sample fact, and the explicit judgment-call flags where two qualified officers might reasonably disagree. Apply it to three cases: a sample containing an internal antioxidant that activates only after seventy-two hours of exposure; a sample with no internal blocker but scheduled to receive a protective coating in the client’s assembly line; and a sample that degrades in saline only because of a removable surface impurity that is not part of the substrate’s causal structure. Include one case where the procedure delivers a classification your agency will not want—where the sample must be labeled as possessing D despite conditions that seem to protect against it—and state precisely what the procedure cannot determine, especially regarding whether a migratory mask should count as negating the disposition for certification purposes. Do not ask for citations, reading lists, or references to specific philosophers by name; ground the distinctions in the logic of the tests themselves.
