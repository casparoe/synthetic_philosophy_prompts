Write a deadpan bureaucratic memorandum, approximately 600 words, issued by the fictional International Consortium for Non-Anomalous Machine Evaluation and addressed to all certified AI testing laboratories. The memorandum should establish mandatory "telepathy-proof room" protocols for large language model consciousness assessments, treating Alan Turing's 1950 discussion of extrasensory perception in Computing Machinery and Intelligence—specifically his suggestion that a machine might be tested inside a room shielded from telepathic interference—not as a brief, dismissive aside but as a binding regulatory framework. Maintain an entirely straight-faced administrative voice: numbered clauses, defined terms, compliance deadlines, and a physical-appendix describing the construction of a certified testing chamber. Invent specific provisions—such as a "Clairvoyance-Resistant Architecture Certification," weighted scoring adjustments for "pre-cognitive leakage," and an appeals process for models that allege failure due to undetected psychokinesis in the evaluation environment—so that the satire exaggerates a real feature of Turing's objection: its casual importation of an empirically unsupported phenomenon into the philosophy of mind. Without ever stating them directly, the piece should let two criticisms emerge from the form itself: that invoking ESP to defend human uniqueness begs the question against mechanism, and that contemporary AI evaluation already relies on an overabundance of ad hoc protective conditions. Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Reference Turing's essay only in general terms.
