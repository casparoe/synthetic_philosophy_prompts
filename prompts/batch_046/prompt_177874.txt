I run a grad philosophy workshop, about 10 people, 90 mins. I want them to feel the immigration / welfare-state tension, but not the tired "fiscal drain" version everyone already regurgitates. Instead, I want the activity to turn on a more subtle idea: that welfare states depend on an implicit integraton contract—universal benefits are sustained by particularistic norms of reciprocity and long time-horizons, and open entry can let people draw from the pool and exit before the mutual-obligation cycle closes. So the problem isn't mainly money; it's a coordination failure on trust over time.

Design a structured role-play or game with real small-stakes choices (tokens, points, no real cash). Give some students long-horizon citizen roles, some newcomer roles who can enter and leave easily, and some policymaker roles setting entry conditions. The rules and payoff scheme should make the group run into the dilemma themselves—probably they'll start tightening borders or diluting benefits—before anyone names it as immigration. Include the rounds, specific roles, payoff rules, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the integration-contract idea and the temporal mismatch in obligation.

Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which feature—probably the cultural/normative layer versus the economic one—the game can't actually reproduce and I'll have to supply in discussion. No lecture up front, no citation lists, keep it fully runnable without web access.
