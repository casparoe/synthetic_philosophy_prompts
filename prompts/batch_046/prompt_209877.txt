ok so me and my study partner were trying to extend thomson's violinist into a full account of parental obligation and we accidentally built this chain that seems to prove either parents have no special duties or accidental rescuers become permanent slaves. i cant find the crack.

(1) thomson shows that a right to life doesnt automatically include a right to use someone else's body without thier consent.

(2) in voluntary pregnancy there is consent to the bodily use, unlike the kidnapped violinist, so the analogy doesnt directly apply.

(3) but consent is granular: you can consent to conception without consenting to every individual act of care that follows over eighteen years.

(4) still, conception initiates one continuous dependency chain. the child only exists and only needs care because that initial act started the sequence, so consenting to the start is consenting to maintain the chain until independence.

(5) by analogy, if i voluntarily flip a switch that starts a machine keeping a stranger alive, and that machine requires my body to run for eighteen years, then ive consented to be tethered for the full duration.

(6) now apply this precisely: if i accidentally bump that same switch without knowing it exists, i started the chain but never consented. in that case im clearly not bound for eighteen years.

(7) so the obligation comes from consent-plus-chain-creation, not from bare causation.

(8) but adoptive parents have full parental obligations even though they never started the biological chain and never consented to conception. if chain-creation is the ground, adoption should carry no special duty.

(9) therefore either we deny that adoptive parents have obligations (absurd), or we admit that chain-creation isnt the real source of parental duty.

(10) and if chain-creation isnt the source, then (4) collapses: voluntary conception doesnt bind you to eighteen years of care, which means thomson's separation of bodily consent from long-term obligation survives, but then we need some other ground for why parents do have duties.

(11) so the argument forces a choice: accept that only biological chain-creators have obligations (which excludes adoption and includes accidental rescuers if we tweak the consent condition), or reject the chain model entirely and lose the anti-abortion extension of thomson.

i think the slip is somewhere between treating dependency as one continuous chain in (4) and treating consent as applying to discrete acts in (3), but im not sure which step is actually rotten. please take a hard line: name the exact premise that should go, explain why it looked plausible, and tell me what we have to pay if we cut it out. dont list options and ask me to choose -- just defend one.
