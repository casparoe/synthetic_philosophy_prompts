I'm trying to construct a precise hypothetical: if Russell were alive today and encountered a subculture that treated intimate partnership primarily as a renewable, data-optimized contract for mutual self-actualization—where romantic attachment is deliberately decoupled from exclusivity, child-rearing is either outsourced or algorithmically planned, and synthetic agents fill intimacy gaps—what would his actual position be?

Specifically, I am not asking for a generic verdict that "he would approve because he was liberal." I want you to work from the specific commitments in his writing: his claim that marriage should serve rational companionship rather than economic security for women, his critique of jealousy as a possessive vice, his defense of sexual experience before permanent commitment, and his warning that treating persons as instruments corrupts human relations. Given these, would he see these modern arrangements as fulfilling his vision of liberated companionship, or would he judge them as missing the essential vulnerability and risk that make companionship meaningful? And how would he respond to the specifically rationalist framing that treats relationship design as an optimization problem governed by explicit utility functions—would he regard this as the proper application of reason to private life, or as a category error that replaces human warmth with instrumental calculation?

If you think his response would be genuinely ambivalent or internally contradictory rather than neatly one-sided, I want you to locate the tension precisely—whether between his enthusiasm for rational planning and his humanistic suspicion of mechanized intimacy, or between his advocacy for sexual freedom and his belief that lasting companionship requires a kind of irreplaceable personal presence. What feature of the modern scenario would be doing the work in each direction, and is there a distinction he might draw—perhaps between the structure of a relationship and the spirit in which it is inhabited—that would resolve or deepen the conflict?
