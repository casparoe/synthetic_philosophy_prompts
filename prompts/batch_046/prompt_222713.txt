I am constructing a philosophical dialogue for a novel in which several metaphysicians argue before an imaginary tribunal, and I require a rigorous decision-tree instrument—no more than one page—that sorts interlocutors by the actual structure of their commitments regarding Frankfurt-style counterexamples to the Principle of Alternative Possibilities. The tree must not ask respondents to name a theory or cite an author; it must pose concrete judgments about a standardized case in which an agent makes a deliberate choice while an unobserved, dormant mechanism stands ready to compel that same choice should the agent waver, yet never activates.

Begin with the root judgment: does this agent bear full moral responsibility for the choice?

From there, build three successive binary splits that trace the logical space rather than a roster of names. First, split on whether the mere presence of the dormant mechanism eliminates every alternative—including minimal, causally inert flickers—or only eliminates robust, actionable ones. Second, split each resulting branch on whether the absence of alternatives is a local modal feature of this particular decision or a global structural condition that would obtain even in worlds without universal determinism. Third, split on whether moral responsibility requires being the ultimate source of the action irrespective of available alternatives, or whether it requires only that the action flow from the agent's own uncoerced deliberation.

The tree must yield exactly six leaves. Four named positions must appear: the classic Frankfurt compatibilist who denies the Principle altogether; the semi-compatibilist who preserves a flicker of freedom; the source-incompatibilist who denies responsibility despite the absence of actual intervention; and the robust-defender who insists that a hidden alternative remains intact. The two remaining leaves must mark unoccupied combinations—specifically, the view that accepts flickers yet rejects sourcehood as irrelevant, and the view that denies all flickers yet treats global determinism as categorically different from a local blocker—each diagnosed in one sentence as incoherent, unattractive, or unexplored.

Every leaf must carry a one-sentence description of its most awkward commitment. The instrument should be addressed to an audience with graduate-level familiarity with action theory, and every branch must be phrased as a judgment about the case rather than a label, so that a respondent is sorted by belief rather than by self-identification. Do not include bibliographies, citation lists, or references to specific editions.
