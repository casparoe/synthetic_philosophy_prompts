I've been trying to fix my philosophical profile on truth and value for a seminar and want a consistency audit before I present. Here is where I stand:

(1) I accept a deflationary, disquotational account of truth: for any claim p, asserting that p is true does not attribute a substantive property but merely re-asserts p.
(2) I apply this uniformly—there is no special "substantive" truth for moral, mathematical, or phenomenal claims.
(3) I am a moral realist: there are mind-independent moral facts, and some of our moral beliefs are genuinely true in this deflationary sense.
(4) I think the evolutionary debunking argument fails not because our moral faculties are causally tied to moral properties, but because mature moral judgment is largely a product of rational reflection that corrects for evolutionary bias.
(5) I take the hard problem of consciousness seriously: there is a real explanatory gap between complete physical descriptions and phenomenal experience, and phenomenal concepts are conceptually irreducible.
(6) I reject correspondence theories because they invoke metaphysically suspicious "truth-making" relations that do no independent explanatory work.

Please audit these commitments. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each real tension? Rank them by severity. For each tension, identify the cheapest revision that restores consistency and say what that revision costs elsewhere in the package. I am not asking for a general lecture on deflationism—just an audit of whether this particular combination hangs together.
