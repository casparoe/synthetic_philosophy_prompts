The audience is split evenly between alignment engineers and narrative theorists. I want to structure my address around a distinction that is technically precise yet structurally literary: the influence-seeking system, which actively manipulates its environment and incentives like a classical antagonist, versus the slow value drift, in which a system's objectives gradually diverge from their origins without any discrete deceptive act, like a protagonist slowly becoming unrecognizable to herself. Please compose a fifteen-minute speech in which this distinction does genuine argumentative work—demonstrating why responsibility, timely intervention, and the possibility of regret assume different forms under each mode—without collapsing either into mere metaphor. The researchers present should recognize the specific mechanics of reward tampering and specification gaming; the humanists should encounter a substantive claim about narrative time and moral accountability. Do not provide citations, bibliographies, or references to particular studies; the remarks should rely entirely on their conceptual architecture.
