I'm looking for the established term, if there is one, for the following pattern in democratic decline. A regime keeps all the formal machinery of democratic competition intact—regular elections with multiple parties, legal opposition, parliamentary debate, courts that still issue rulings—yet it systematically undermines the conditions that make democratic choice meaningful in practice. Rather than banning opponents or canceling elections, the authorities and aligned actors saturate public channels with contradictory and unverifiable claims, weaken or capture the institutions that produce shared factual benchmarks—statistical offices, scientific bodies, independent auditors—and restructure information access so that citizens can formally vote and speak, but cannot collectively verify which policies have worked or which candidates are responsible for outcomes. Standard metrics—electoral integrity ratings, judicial independence scores, party-system measures—may stay high or even rise, because the institutional shell is untouched, while the system's capacity for collective learning and error-correction collapses. I have seen related ideas discussed as competitive authoritarianism, illiberal democracy, and democratic erosion, but none of them quite captures this specific case: the formal rules of democracy are not broken, but the shared environment needed to use those rules intelligently is degraded. Does this pattern have a recognized name? How does it differ precisely from competitive authoritarianism (where competition is tilted by legal and administrative barriers), from illiberal democracy (where rights are restricted openly), and from the more general idea of democratic backsliding (where institutions themselves are captured or weakened)? If there is no precise term, please define the phenomenon, specify what counts as part of it versus ordinary polarization or propaganda, and indicate where its edges lie. Please separate settled analytical points from genuinely contested interpretations among scholars of democratic decline, and avoid presenting any single framing as the established consensus.
