I am preparing a final paper for my high school philosophy seminar on whether maximal moral goodness is actually an ideal worth pursuing. But I want to push the debate into less familiar territory: suppose an agent systematically redesigns her own psychology to eliminate every non-moral preference that might conflict with duty, not by suppressing desires but by gradually dissolving the stable traits—particular loyalties, idiosyncratic tastes, a distinctive sense of humor—that make her actions genuinely hers. Could the result be not merely an unhappy saint, but an agent who has achieved maximal moral output at the cost of psychological self-elimination, leaving behind something closer to a transparent moral algorithm than a person?

Reach and justify a specific conclusion; do not end with a non-committal balance.
