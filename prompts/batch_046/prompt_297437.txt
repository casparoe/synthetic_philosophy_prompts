Here is roughly where I've landed on anthropic reasoning and reference-class selection, and I'd like an audit of whether these commitments hang together.

(1) I adopt the self-sampling assumption (SSA) over the self-indication assumption (SIA) for cosmological reasoning: my observer-moment should be treated as randomly drawn from the reference class of actual observers, not weighted by the total number of observers in each possible world.

(2) I take the presumptuous philosopher problem to be a successful reductio of SIA, not of SSA—so I see no reason to be surprised that I inhabit a universe of moderate size rather than one vastly larger.

(3) I apply SSA consistently across domains, including temporal location: if humanity survives long enough to produce orders of magnitude more observers in the distant future, my being alive in the early 21st century is strong anthropic evidence that extinction is relatively near.

(4) Despite (3), I reject the Doomsday argument on methodological grounds: treating one's temporal position as a random sample confuses an epistemic indexical with causal evidence, and this confusion should be blocked regardless of domain.

(5) I restrict the reference class to actually existing conscious observers regardless of substrate, but I exclude counterfactual observers and I do not weight worlds by observer-count.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how severely. For each conflict, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Also flag any pair that looks contradictory but dissolves on closer inspection—particularly where substrate-neutrality or domain-neutrality might seem to clash with the cosmological reasoning but doesn't. Finally, tell me which single commitment I should revise if I want to clean up the most tension with the least damage to the rest of the package.
