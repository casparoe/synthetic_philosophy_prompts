Write a proposal for an independent-study term paper (approximately 5,000 words, one semester) investigating a precise, under-explored corner of the wrong-kind-reasons problem for belief. The core question is whether the standard right-kind/wrong-kind distinction collapses once we recognize a category of “staked belief”: a belief that a subject continues to maintain not because she takes it to be evidentially supported, but because abandoning it would force an unacceptable practical restructuring of her other commitments, yet which still operates in reasoning and action as a fully doxastic state rather than as a mere pretense or pragmatic fiction. Frame the question so that it has a determinate answer—either (i) staked beliefs dissolve the wrong-kind-reasons problem by showing that apparently wrong-kind considerations are actually right-kind structural reasons for preserving a cognitive stake, or (ii) they preserve the distinction but bifurcate it, relocating wrong-kind influence at the level of belief-maintenance rather than belief-formation, which forces a revision of the standard account. Explain the approach you will take—likely conceptual analysis of the functional role of belief combined with a constructed case study involving synthetic testimony—and defend it against the obvious alternative of reducing staked beliefs to pragmatic encumbrances that fall outside doxastic evaluation altogether. Specify exactly what a successful result would establish, what pattern of counterexample would count as failure, and whether the conceptual machinery risks outgrowing a single semester. Throughout, take a committed position: argue explicitly for one of the two horns rather than presenting them as equally live options, and give your reasons for that commitment. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations; refer to positions and arguments in general terms, and note that specific references will be added in the final draft.
