I am currently reading for a course in metaphysics and philosophy of mind, and after working through Derek Parfit's discussion of the teletransporter and personal identity, I have settled on a position I am calling the continuity-of-agency view. On this account, what matters in survival is neither the psychological continuity of memory and character that Parfit emphasises, nor the brute physical continuity of a particular brain or body, but rather the uninterrupted preservation of a first-person practical standpoint: the capacity to deliberate, form intentions, and hold oneself accountable across time without a phenomenological or normative break. Applied to the teletransporter case, I hold that I survive if the replica genuinely inherits my projects and can continue my practical reasoning, even if some psychological traits are altered or duplicated; conversely, if my brain were kept alive but my capacity for agency were permanently suspended, I would not survive, despite physical and psychological continuity being preserved in a dormant form.

What would count against this view? I want an honest map of its exposure: which arguments, thought experiments, or empirical considerations should genuinely move me, sorted by how decisive they would be. Please distinguish between defeaters that the view's defenders (including myself, if I were consistent) ought to concede as counting against it, and those they would dismiss as missing the point. I am particularly interested in whether there are branching cases—say, where two replicas both inherit my practical commitments—that expose the view to a fission problem analogous to Parfit's, or whether the agency-based framework has resources to avoid that difficulty without collapsing back into psychological continuity theory. I also want to know whether the view risks being qualified into unfalsifiability: if I can always redescribe any apparent counterexample as a failure of agency-continuity rather than a failure of survival, is that a problem for a metaphysical claim of this kind? If the position turns out to be well insulated, please say so explicitly and assess whether that insulation is a virtue or a vice. Please provide your answer in British English, and do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations—this is an exercise in reasoning rather than scholarship.
