Please rank the following five replies to Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against true moral responsibility into tiers — A through D — according to how well each survives the following stress case: the harmful act is produced by an implicit bias formed during pre-conscious childhood development, with no second-order endorsement ever available and no plausible path for later self-revision. The replies are: (1) the hierarchical compatibilist reply that identification with second-order desires breaks the regress; (2) the revisionist reply that responsibility attaches to actions, not to the self that performs them; (3) the social-practice reply that moral responsibility is an institutional status independent of metaphysical self-creation; (4) the neuroscientific reply that causal determinism undermines the premise that self-creation is required; and (5) the incremental-modification reply that small-scale self-change over time allows partial causa sui without requiring ex nihilo creation. For each tier, provide one sentence explaining whether the reply dissolves the regress, relocates it, or collapses under the stress case. Be explicit about where your ranking turns on contested readings of Strawson's premises rather than on internal features of the replies. If you believe the stress case confuses the metaphysics of responsibility with the epistemology of self-knowledge — and thus that the task as posed cannot be executed well — say so plainly instead of forcing a ranking.
