the exam question was: Amartya Sen argues that major famines are political failures rather than natural disasters. Explain teh entitlement approach and apply it to the 1943 Bengal famine, the Irish famine in the 1840s, and the famine during China's Great Leap Forward. What does this imply for the food availability decline thesis?

here is the student answer:
Sen's entitlement approach looks at whether people can actually command food, not whether food exists in the aggregate. He divides this into direct entitlement, where you produce food yourself, and trade entitlement, where you exchange labor or goods for it. In Bengal 1943 there was no serious food availability decline; instead rural laborers and fishermen lost trade entitlements because war inflation drove up rice prices while wages stagnated, and the British boat denial policy hurt fishing communities' direct access. For Ireland, the potato blight wiped out direct entitlements for tenant laborers, yet grain and livestock kept being exported to Britain, so starvation happened despite available food. In the Great Leap Forward, collectivization eliminated private plots so direct entitlements collapsed for rural households, and grain was still being procured by the state, making it another entitlement failure rather than a shortage.

Sen also says democratic countries with a free press never suffer famines because governments are held accountable and information about shortages reaches the center quickly. So the food availability decline thesis is wrong for all three cases; famine is always about governance and entitlement collapse, not nature.

He later renamed the entitlement framework the capability approach, which is basically the same idea but focuses on human freedoms instead of commodities.
