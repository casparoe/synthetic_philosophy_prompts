Write an essay on whether beauty is objective, centered on Hume's essay Of the Standard of Taste but extending the debate to a case Hume did not consider: art generated entirely by an autonomous system with no human creator and evaluated by critics who lack the usual emotional engagement with human expressive intention. Weigh Hume's argument that beauty is not a property of objects but a sentiment in the observer, his appeal to the standard of taste formed by ideal critics with delicacy and practice, and the objection that this collapses into subjectivism or elitism. Then consider the niche challenge: if the ideal critic's sentiment is partly caused by recognizing human expressive intention, can an artifact without such intention ever trigger the correct sentiment, or does Hume's framework implicitly require an authorial mind? Reach and defend a clear conclusion—either that Hume's view can accommodate non-human art if the formal properties alone produce the sentiment, or that his framework necessarily ties aesthetic value to human expressive agency. Throughout, separate what is settled about Hume's text from what remains genuinely contested in contemporary aesthetics, and do not present a disputed interpretation of Hume's position as the scholarly consensus.
