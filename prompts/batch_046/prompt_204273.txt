I sit on the acquisitions council of an independent literary cooperative—seven members, no commercial imprint—tasked with selecting novels for collective publication based solely on artistic merit. Our current protocol requires each councilor to read submissions in isolation and submit a sealed verdict before any collective discussion, a design intended to preserve independent aesthetic judgment.

A proposal now before us, termed calibrated transparency, would modify this: after the initial sealed round, the chair discloses the aggregate distribution of ratings (without attribution) and permits a second round of voting followed by open deliberation. Proponents claim this corrects for idiosyncratic false negatives on formally innovative manuscripts; opponents contend that in our prestige-driven culture—where consensus itself is read as a proxy for artistic coherence—revealing even anonymous aggregates will trigger rational herding, collapsing the very independence the protocol was built to protect.

Please draft a formal policy memo addressed to the council recommending whether to adopt calibrated transparency, reject it outright, or institute a modified procedural safeguard. The memo must:

1. Specify the structural conditions under which information cascades become rational rather than pathological in an evaluation context like ours, distinguishing informational learning from prestige-driven conformity.
2. Propose—if your recommendation includes adoption—concrete institutional safeguards that could isolate the informational value of transparency from herding dynamics; if your recommendation is rejection, explain what modified transparency, if any, would still be permissible without violating those conditions.
3. Give an honest account of the strongest opposing position, including the argument that our cooperative's specific prestige economy makes herding not merely likely but rational for individual councilors who wish to preserve their standing.
4. Conclude with an actionable bottom line and generalizable criteria the council can apply to future procedural amendments, not only to this vote.

Reason from the logic of the decision architecture itself; do not supply a bibliography, citation list, or reading recommendations. The memo should be written as a document the council could vote on directly.
