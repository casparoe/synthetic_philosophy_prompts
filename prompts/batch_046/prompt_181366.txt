In my philosophy of technology study group, two members debated whether coherent extrapolated volition can genuinely filter out adaptive preferences. Maya opened by arguing that adaptive preferences—such as a captive coming to value captivity—can be fully internally coherent, so a coherence-only idealization risks systematizing harmful conditioning rather than transcending it. Leo replied that CEV’s specific idealization conditions—knowing more, having reflected longer, being closer to the people we wish we were—are designed to dissolve the informational and psychological deficits that produce adaptation, so the extrapolated agent should drop the adaptive feature. Maya countered that this smuggles in a normative notion of a “true self” to recover, and that if all preferences are historically shaped, coherence merely organizes conditioning without any independent anchor to distinguish growth from damage. Leo responded that CEV needs no objective values; it targets intersubjective coherence among idealized agents, and adaptive preferences usually fail cross-contextual consistency checks that authentic values survive. Maya then proposed a rival she called “counterfactual preference anchoring,” which fixes values to what people would want had specific formative traumas never occurred, and she claimed CEV offers no principled reason to prefer full extrapolation over such targeted anchoring, rendering its outputs arbitrary. They asked me to referee. Please adjudicate each of the five moves in order—rating each as clearly mistaken, partly right, or clearly right, and noting briefly what both sides are still missing. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
