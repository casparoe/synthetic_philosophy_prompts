so my grandfather died three weeks ago and ive been going through his apartment—he was kind of an amateur local historian, kept boxes of strike flyers and a medal from a factory closure in 1978 and letters he wrote but never mailed. it made me think about nietzsches three uses of history for life, the monumental antiquarian and critical kinds, and how they all feel present at once while im sorting his stuff. i need a poem for the memorial service, maybe three stanzas of eight lines each, free verse, with each stanza ending in the same line except the verb changes: first "the dead ask the living to build," then "the dead ask the living to keep," then "the dead ask the living to burn." the repetition should do the philosophical work—showing how history keeps demanding different things from the living—while the shift in verb should map onto nietzsches idea that monumental history inspires action, antiquarian roots us in preservation, and critical frees life by destroying whats burdensome. please write it in american english, not too academic, and make sure someone who actually knows nietzsche can recognize the three modes and why they clash, but it should still work as a poem for people who dont know the theory. also let the tension show: i feel guilty throwing anything away but also exhausted by carrying it all, and i dont want it to resolve into either a sermon about honoring the past or a shrug about letting go. just leave it open.
