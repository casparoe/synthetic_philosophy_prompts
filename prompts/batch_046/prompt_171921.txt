I serve as the student facilitator for my high school's applied-ethics discussion circle, where we evaluate harm claims in live debate. I need a reusable decision procedure—ordered tests presented as a flowchart in words—to apply the Epicurean principle that death is not bad for the one who dies, specifically operationalizing the existence requirement that harm needs a subject who can experience deprivation or loss. The procedure should let me assess, in real time, whether a state (death, permanent unconsciousness, prenatal non-existence, or suspended animation with irreversible loss of consciousness) genuinely harms that particular subject. It must cover: the sequential checks; hard cases including temporary unconsciousness, harm to posthumous reputation, and harms to future generations; and three worked applications—one where the procedure clearly denies harm, one where it produces an answer I will dislike, and one ambiguous case it cannot resolve—while flagging every point where judgment is unavoidable and stating where the procedure misfires. Please separate what is philosophically settled about the existence-requirement and deprivation-account objections from what experts genuinely contest, especially whether comparative harm can exist without an experiencing subject, and do not present any disputed interpretation as the consensus view.
