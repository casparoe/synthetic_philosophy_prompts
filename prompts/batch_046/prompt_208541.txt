As a retired physicist who has only recently begun attending philosophy of mind seminars, I witnessed a sharp exchange between two regular members over epiphenomenalism and the paradox of phenomenal judgment. I report it here as a bystander, with no favored side.

(1) Dr. A opened by arguing that epiphenomenalism is self-defeating: if phenomenal consciousness has no causal efficacy, then our judgments that we are conscious are produced solely by underlying neural processes, with consciousness playing no justificatory role. Hence we have no reason to regard those judgments as tracking anything real.

(2) Dr. B replied with an analogy: a barometer tracks a storm without causing it, since both are jointly produced by atmospheric pressure. Likewise, neural mechanisms may jointly produce both consciousness and our judgments about it, so correlation without direct causation is unproblematic.

(3) Dr. A countered that the barometer analogy fails because the barometer can misfire independently of the storm, whereas on epiphenomenalism consciousness can never correct such misfires, being causally inert. She also distinguished dark matter—often invoked in such analogies—on the grounds that dark matter participates in the causal chain we observe (gravitational effects), while consciousness, ex hypothesi, participates in none.

(4) Dr. B responded that knowledge does not require direct causal interaction with the object known; we infer consciousness from its neural correlates just as we infer other unobservable entities from their effects. He added that knowing the neural base need not collapse epiphenomenalism into physicalism, since supervenience allows consciousness to depend necessarily on neural states without being identical to them or causally efficacious.

Please adjudicate this exchange round by round. Rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right and briefly justify each rating. Then say which speaker is more right overall, and identify what both are missing—particularly regarding whether the paradox genuinely undermines epiphenomenalism or merely reveals a structural feature it shares with other correlation-based inferences in science.
