I am developing the normative foundations of strong longtermism—the view that the far future is the dominant determinant of present moral priorities rather than one factor among others to be discounted. I need a rigorous specification of the adequacy conditions for any theory in this domain. Please provide the full desiderata: the explanatory phenomena it must cover (e.g., why actions affecting people millennia hence can carry comparable or greater weight than actions affecting present people, despite both causal attenuation and epistemic opacity); the conceptual distinctions it must preserve (specifically between influencing the future via existential-risk reduction, via value lock-in, and via population-scale effects, and between diachronic moral consistency—treating future generations as equals across time—and synchronic aggregation—treating the distant future as a large present-equivalent population); the consistency conditions with other normative commitments it must satisfy (such as not collapsing into unrestricted expected-value maximization that disregards deontic constraints against present harm); and the empirical or metaphysical constraints it must respect (including the non-identity problem and the unpredictability of value evolution over long timescales). For each desideratum, state the motivating case or argument that establishes it as a genuine requirement rather than a partisan demand of either longtermist or presentist camps, and indicate which requirements are negotiable if the theory's purpose is restricted to institutional resource-allocation guidance rather than comprehensive moral theory. Identify the single requirement that does the most work in excluding popular but inadequate accounts—especially those that treat future welfare as merely present welfare multiplied by a temporal discount factor. Present the requirements in a structured format with brief justifications. End with one line beginning 'Verdict:' that names the single most exclusionary criterion.
