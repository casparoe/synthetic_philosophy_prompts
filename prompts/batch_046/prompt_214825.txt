Construct a modified Molyneux thought experiment that isolates whether cross-modal associative binding—not innate spatial concepts or pure visual learning—is necessary for a congenitally blind subject, upon sudden sight restoration, to recognize shapes previously known only by touch. Constraints: one paragraph, realistic clinical setting (no exotic physics or technology), and the subject has prior tactile familiarity with exactly two objects, a smooth sphere and a sharp-edged cube, each consistently paired during blindness with a distinct auditory cue (a sustained tone for the sphere, a staccato chord for the cube). In the test, both objects are presented visually; one emits its paired sound, the other is silent. Engineer the scenario so that (i) a strict empiricist, denying innate spatial ideas, predicts failure for both because vision is untrained; (ii) a strict nativist, holding innate amodal spatial structures, predicts immediate success for both regardless of sound; and (iii) a cross-modal binding theorist predicts success only for the sounding object, since recognition requires linking the visual input to the existing tactile-auditory representation, and failure for the silent one. After the scenario, demonstrate that each position delivers its assigned verdict, identify the structural feature doing the work (the presence or absence of the auditory bridge), and explain whether removing that feature collapses the case back into the standard Molyneux problem. Avoid bibliographies, citations, and direct quotations from specific texts.
