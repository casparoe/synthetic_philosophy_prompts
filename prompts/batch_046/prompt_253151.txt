hey, writing a feature on the omnipotence paradox for the science/ideas desk—y'know, can God make a rock so heavy He can't lift it, all that. In interviews and twitter threads people keep falling back on "omnipotence just means being able to do what's logically possible," like that settles things. But I think that slogan hides like four different claims. Can you separate them out?

Specifically:
- the definitional claim that "all-powerful" was never meant to include logical contradictions
- the modal claim that logical possibility is the right boundary for any power, not just divine
- the task-analysis claim that "make an unliftable rock" describes an incoherent task, not a hard one
- and this weirder one I keep thinking about: maybe omnipotence is about having no external limits, so a being could still be limited by thier own nature without that being a "failure" of power

Lay these out as a menu. For each: is it trivial? Is it defensible? Is it false? And crucially—someone in my comments used the slogan to say the paradox is "just a language game, not a real problem." Which of these four readings actually backs that dismissal, and which ones still leave a genuine tension on the table?
