For my philosophy of voting seminar I jotted down my commitments and now I'm wondering if they actually cohere. Here they are, in no order: I think in a national election one vote has basically zero causal weight, so there's no straightforward outcome-based duty to vote. At the same time I feel genuinly guilty if I skip an election where one candidate is clearly worse on basic rights, even though I know my absense changes nothing. I think voting is mostly expressive — it's a public testimony about what kind of society you want, not a lever you pull. But I also think strategic voting for the lesser evil is usually required when the stakes are high. I reject expected-utility calculations for voting decisions because they rest on fantasies about probabilities. I think abstaining is sometimes the most honest option if you reject every candidate's platform. And I think democracy needs people to vote sincerely for long-run legitimacy, but I also want everyone else to vote strategically against dangerous options right now. There's also this idea I half-believe: that skipping carries a kind of testimonial failure, like you're refusing to go on record about justice. Do these hang together? Go through them and tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which ones only look bad, and rank them by severity. For each real tension say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems.
