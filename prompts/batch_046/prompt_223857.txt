For a reading group on well-being I have been asked to lay out my philosophical profile so the others can stress-test it. Here are my commitments:

(1) I am an objective-list theorist: well-being consists in genuinely achieving valuable goals and possessing knowledge, not in mental satisfaction or preference-fulfillment.

(2) I reject the experience machine, but specifically on causal-historical grounds: achievement requires that one's efforts over time actually cause the outcomes in question, not merely that one possesses the mental state of having achieved them. A simulated achievement with the right phenomenal feel but the wrong causal history is not an achievement at all.

(3) I think phenomenal consciousness—there being something-it-is-like for the subject—is a necessary condition for well-being, whereas access consciousness is not; a philosophical zombie with perfect functional access but no phenomenal experience has zero well-being regardless of its objective accomplishments.

(4) I deny that "contact with reality" is intrinsically valuable; it is only instrumentally valuable, either as a causal condition for real achievement or as a sustaining condition for phenomenal consciousness.

(5) I hold that autonomy should be respected within an objective framework: if a person is fully informed about the experience machine, understands that its outputs are causally disconnected from their efforts, and autonomously chooses to enter and remain, then for that person life inside should count as going well, provided they retain phenomenal consciousness.

(6) I think subjective reports of life satisfaction are unreliable indicators of well-being because of adaptive preference formation, framing effects, and the possibility of satisfied false belief, so I generally discount first-person satisfaction judgments in evaluation.

(7) I maintain that there is nothing it is like to be a thermostat, but there is something it is like to experience simulated achievements inside the machine; that phenomenal character is metaphysically real even though the achievements are not.

Go through these commitments and audit their joint tenability. For each pair that is genuinely in tension—not merely apparently so—state how severe the conflict is, identify which commitment bears the burden, and propose the cheapest revision that would restore consistency. For any pairs that look tense but dissolve on inspection, explain why the tension is only superficial. Then rank the tensions by severity and say which single revision to the set would clean up the most problems at once. Do not treat this as a lecture on well-being in general: the object is this specific profile and what it commits me to.
