I was arguing with a friend late into the evening after a seminar on philosophy of mind and cognitive science, and we kept returning to a formulation that circulates constantly in discussions of artificial intelligence: “If a system behaves as though it holds a belief, then for every predictive and explanatory purpose it holds that belief—though there is no deeper fact about whether it genuinely believes anything at all.” My friend invoked it to justify saying that a large language model literally maintains false beliefs about physics when it generates incorrect explanations, insisting that this “ends the debate” over whether we should treat its outputs as expressions of conviction rather than mere statistical patterns. I found the sentence frustratingly ambiguous, as though it were sliding between an instrumental claim about prediction, a functionalist claim about the nature of belief itself, and an eliminativist claim about the absence of any mental reality behind the behavior. Could you disentangle the precise theses compressed into that one line? Lay them out as a menu: the purely strategic or instrumentalist reading (the intentional stance is only a heuristic), the functionalist or realist reading (to have the belief just is to occupy the relevant functional and behavioral role), and the deflationary or error-theoretic reading (belief-talk is useful fiction with no corresponding property in the system). For each, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible philosophical position, or plainly false; and indicate which of these readings would actually support my friend’s move of attributing specific false beliefs to an LLM rather than explaining its mistakes through architecture, training data, or optimization dynamics. I am also curious whether introducing Dennett’s distinction between the design stance and the intentional stance—explaining a model’s failure by reference to transformer weights and loss landscapes versus by reference to a mistaken belief—adds genuine substance to the dispute, or whether it merely renames the same ambiguity without resolving whether there is any fact of the matter being tracked.
