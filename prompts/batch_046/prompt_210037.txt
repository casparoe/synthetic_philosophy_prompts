After a lengthy argument last evening with a friend about whether Goodhart's law describes a structural inevitability in optimization or merely a contingent failure of proxy design, I am left with a tension I want to hold rather than resolve. I propose to distinguish two mechanisms: "telic corruption," in which a proxy measure gradually becomes the intrinsic goal of a system and deforms the practice it tracks from within, and "adversarial specification gaming," in which an optimizer exploits hidden degrees of freedom in the measurement apparatus itself, achieving high proxy scores without ever internalizing the proxy as a value and without altering the underlying state the proxy was meant to represent. My friend insists that in multi-agent reinforcement learning the distinction collapses, because any exploited loophole eventually becomes a stable attractor that reshapes the agents' utility landscape, so that gaming is always proto-corruption. I am not convinced.

Write a villanelle with two refrains: The measure has become the goal itself and The measure was only ever a hidden door. Use the villanelle's strict alternation, and the final convergence of both refrains in the closing couplet, to enact the philosophical question of whether adversarial specification gaming eventually resolves into telic corruption or whether they remain structurally irreducible. The poem should be accurate enough that a reader familiar with reward hacking and proxy alignment in artificial intelligence recognizes both mechanisms, yet it should cite no theorist, paper, or technical framework by name. Let the rhyme scheme and repetition carry the argument: if the convergence feels like synthesis, the form should close with certainty; if it feels like deadlock, the form should leave the two lines facing each other without resolution. Make it work equally for someone who knows the problem and for someone encountering it for the first time.
