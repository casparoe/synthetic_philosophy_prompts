I've been arguing with my roommate about the procreation asymmetry — you know, the claim that bringing a suffering person into existence is wrong, but failing to bring a happy one isn't wrong. I understand the surface-level intuition, but I'm getting tangled in the underlying mechnics. The standard defense is person-affecting: we can't harm someone by not creating them, because there's no subject to be harmed. But then why is creating a suffering person wrong in a person-affecting sense? That person also wouldn't have existed if we'd abstained, so it seems like the harm has to be evaluated as just a bad state of affairs, not a harm to a specifc individual. If that's true for the suffering case, shouldn't the asymmetry collapse into a symmetric impersonal calculus?

My roommate tried to rescue it with what he calls a threshold determinacy view — once conception is likely enough, the future child becomes morally determinate, so delaying destroys a specific welfare that we can be blamed for, whereas complete abstention never targets anyone. But this feels like it smuggles in a weird metaphysics where people become partially real before they exist. Can you explain whether the person-affecting framing actually survives on the harm side without cheating, and whether this determinacy idea fixes the asymmetry or just relocates the problem? I'm not looking for a bibliography, just the conceptual plumming — where the pipes actually connect and where they leak.
