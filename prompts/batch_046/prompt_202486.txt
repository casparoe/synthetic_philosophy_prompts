For an article I am writing on interpretive stalemates in classical Chinese philosophy, please draft the joint statement that two opposing camps—call them the Qi-Process Continuists and the Conceptual-Therapy Skeptics—would sign after a rigorous adversarial collaboration on Zhuangzi’s account of death. The Continuists read the text, especially the narrative of Zhuangzi’s response to his wife’s death and the seasonal-transformation imagery, as advancing a metaphysics in which personal death is the reconfiguration of vital energy within an impersonal cosmic continuum. The Skeptics read the same passages as performative exercises that dissolve grief by undermining the self-world distinctions that make death appear as a loss, without committing to any ontology of qi. The document should open with a numbered section of agreed claims about the textual evidence—what both sides accept about the structure of the death episodes, about the absence of explicit afterlife doctrine, and about the rhetorical role of the great-dream analogy—followed by a disagreements table. In the table, each disagreement must be phrased in wording that the camp holding it would accept: (i) whether qi-language is metaphysically committal or purely therapeutic; (ii) whether the normative force of Zhuangzi’s equanimity requires belief in transformation or only the suspension of evaluative boundaries; and (iii) whether the imagery of wandering (you) implies a dispersed but continuing subject or the complete dissolution of subjecthood. For each row, identify the crux—the sub-question whose resolution would move at least one side—and specify the kind of philosophical or textual evidence both agree is relevant, recording each camp’s predicted verdict. If part of the dispute is verbal—say, over whether transformation denotes an ontological process or a phenomenological shift—state exactly which part and why. Keep the format formal and concise, include no bibliography or citation requests, and do not appeal to scholarship the camps could not verify from the texts themselves.
