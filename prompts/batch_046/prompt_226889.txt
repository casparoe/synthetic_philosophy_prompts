Argue that the so-called "alignment problem" is not a single problem but a family of at least three conceptually and practically distinct challenges—call them the calibration problem (the epistemic task of representing human intent without distortion), the authority problem (the normative task of determining when an AI system should defer to, correct, or override a user's expressed preference), and the structural problem (the sociotechnical task of aligning institutional incentives with the objectives that are formally encoded). Your argument should demonstrate that these three dimensions have incompatible success conditions: progress on calibration can intensify authority conflicts, and both can be nullified if the structural problem is ignored. You should also rebut the reductionist claim that all three are merely instances of "making systems pursue intended goals," showing specifically why that reduction collapses the normative and institutional distinctions. Build the case through conceptual analysis and the logic of institutional design rather than through citation of specific technical literature. Conclude by explaining what practical division of labor across disciplines—rather than a centralized "alignment" framework—follows from your taxonomy.
