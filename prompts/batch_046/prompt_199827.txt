I am a PhD candidate in moral philosophy preparing materials for a workshop on distributive justice. Two senior researchers in my reading group—one who defends a strict Scanlonian contractualism that denies any interpersonal aggregation of complaints, and one who contends that Scanlon's own treatment of multi-person rescue cases implicitly relies on a cardinal comparison of complaint strengths—have agreed to an adversarial collaboration. Please draft the joint statement they should co-sign. The document should contain: (1) a numbered section of agreed claims, covering the formal structure of the reasonable-rejection principle, the distinction between aggregating benefits and aggregating complaints, and the precise formulation of the aggregation problem as it arises within contractualism rather than within utilitarianism; (2) a disagreements table in which each side's position is phrased in wording it would accept, centered on whether the "numbers problem" can be dissolved by appealing to an ex ante representative-party perspective without collapsing into cardinal aggregation, and on whether the strength of a complaint is invariant across populations or varies with the distribution of other complaints; (3) the cruxes, specifically whether a principle permitting the rescue of five strangers over one is rejected by any single affected party with sufficient reason, and whether contractualist justification requires comparing the total weight of complaints across different possible worlds; (4) for each crux, a concrete allocation scenario involving heterogeneous harms that both sides agree is relevant, together with each side's predicted verdict on that scenario. The statement should treat both positions with intellectual charity and should not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. At the very end, provide the following in a single line: Verdict: [state whether the remaining disagreement is substantive or partly verbal, with one brief reason].
