I have been working through some philosophy of biology material for a project on the modal properties of organisms, and I encountered a summary of origin essentialism in a recent review article that I cannot fully evaluate. The author characterises the view, roughly, as the thesis that any particular human being could not have developed from gametes other than the actual sperm and egg that fused to form the zygote from which she developed; this is presented as a direct biological translation of Kripke's origin essentialism, in which the zygote is treated as an origin-object strictly identical to the mature organism, so that substituting different gametes would necessarily yield a numerically different person. The summary then argues that the position has been largely abandoned, partly because monozygotic twinning demonstrates that a single zygotic origin can produce two distinct persons—thereby breaking any necessary one-to-one correspondence between origin and personal identity—and partly because developmental systems theory shows that genetic constitution radically underdetermines phenotypic outcome, which is said to undermine the very idea of a fixed essential origin.

I am inclined to accept the twinning objection, yet I suspect the appeal to developmental plasticity may conflate origin essentialism with a form of genetic determinism that the view need not hold. Could you please assess whether this summary is fair? Specifically, I would like you to distinguish what is accurate in the core modal claim, what represents a common simplification or popular gloss—particularly regarding the relation between zygote and organism, and the alleged application of Kripke's argument—and what an origin essentialist would explicitly deny. Please also address whether the conclusions drawn about the view's abandonment survive these corrections. I ask that your answer be written in British English, and I am not seeking bibliographies, reading lists, or citations; I want an accuracy audit that I can act upon without further sources.
