I am drafting a philosophical dialogue for a novel, and I must keep one of my characters—Dr. Mira Calhoun, a philosopher of science—internally consistent across her judgments on scientific idealization. Third, regarding the flat-Earth geometric approximation employed in local GPS trilateration: she accepts it, on the grounds that Earth curvature is causally insulated from the local measurement scale and functions there as an isolated parameter approximated to infinity.

Fourth, regarding a macroeconomic DSGE model built around a single representative agent, used for aggregate inflation forecasting: she rejects it, insisting that collapsing heterogeneous agents into one unit destroys the structural heterogeneity that drives the dynamics policy must actually target, regardless of whether aggregate predictions sometimes hold.

They defend the constant-viscosity approach because it predicts bulk flow rates adequately and is computationally tractable.
