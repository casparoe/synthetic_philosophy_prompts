I'm applying for a small summer research stipend—like six to eight weeks max—and I need a two-page proposal with a precise question, why existing answers leave it open, what I'd actually do month by month, and what counts as the project failing. But my idea is still pretty half-baked and I want to avoid just reheating the Ship of Theseus debate.

The usual literature frames gradual replacement around personal identity: does the final upload count as the same person? I think that's the wrong axis. Instead, I want to ask whether each intermediate stage retains what I'm tentatively calling epistemic standing—the capacity to legitimately claim knowledge of its own mental history—and whether breaks in that chain create a kind of moral debt to the earlier stages. So the proposal should frame the problem around epistemic continuity rather than metaphysical identity, explain why the standard identity-based answers miss this moral-epistemic gap, lay out a realistic method (maybe mapping hypothetical replacement schedules to identify exactly where belief-justification fractures), and specify failure conditions—like if epistemic standing collapses into identity talk anyway or if the schedule turns out to be ethically irrelevant. I don't need a bibliography or literature review by citation, I'll add references myself; I just need the framing to sound like actual new work and not something every philosophy undergrad has already written.
