A philosophy forum post I came across summarizes the torture-versus-dust-specks argument as follows: "If you claim that torturing one person is worse than any number of people receiving a dust speck in the eye, then your moral framework violates continuity in population ethics. You must reject any smooth trade-off between suffering and population size, which commits you to lexical priorities that prevent ranking outcomes across worlds with different total populations. So the non-aggregative position is incoherent: it cannot compare worlds, and the only consistent fix is to bite the bullet and agree that 3^^^3 dust specks are worse. Our intuition to the contrary is just an evolutionary bias toward salient, concentrated harm." I suspect this glosses over distinctions that matter, but I do not know enough to locate them.
