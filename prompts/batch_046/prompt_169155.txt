I'm a program director for a regional disability-grant fund, and we have to decide whether someone's current limitation counts as brute luck, option luck, or a choice that shouldn't be compensated -- but the applicants don't come in neat boxes. I want a reusable decision procedure, grounded in luck-egalitarianism, that my reveiwers can run in about fifteen minutes per file. Give me the ordered questions, what evidence counts as a yes/no at each gate, and the exact point where the procedure stops being factual and becomes a value judgement that needs a human sign-off. Make sure it handles compound luck -- like someone who took a risky job because they had no family safety net and then got hurt, or someone who refused a cheap preventative treatment for religious reasons and now needs expensive care. Run it on three cases: a congenital condition, the risky-job injury, and the refused-prevention case -- including the one where the procedure denies priority and the reveiwer is going to hate it. Flag the misfires, especially gaming the system, and be honest about what this procedure absolutely cannot settle.
