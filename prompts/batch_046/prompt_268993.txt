Drawing on John von Neumann's theoretical framework for self-replicating automata and his view that durable mathematical structures may outlast biological life, predict how he might have evaluated autonomous interstellar probes capable of evolutionary self-modification, long-term divergence from their original programming, and persistence millennia after human extinction. Would he have considered such technology a legitimate extension of intelligent agency, or rather a decoupling of survival from consciousness? Furthermore, what normative constraints—if any—might he have derived from his concept of universal constructors regarding the moral permissibility of allowing such probes to adapt without human oversight?
