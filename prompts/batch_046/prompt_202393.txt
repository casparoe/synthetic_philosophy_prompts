I'm trying to stabilize my view on Bradley's regress and propositional unity before writing a paper, and I need a consistency audit. Here is my current package:

(1) Bradley's regress is sound: if a complex's unity requires a real relation linking its parts, then that relation itself needs linking, ad infinitum.

(2) The correct response is structural primitivism about propositions: a proposition just is its constituents arranged in a specific way, with no further "glue"—mental or relational—required for unity.

(3) I extend this to relational propositions: in "a is taller than b," the relation of being-taller-than is itself one of the constituents, arranged between a and b by the same primitive structural relation that arranges every other constituent.

(4) I deny that any special mental act of synthesis or judgment turns a list into a proposition; the structure is primitive and mind-independent.

(5) False propositions are unified in exactly the same manner as true ones—there is no correspondence-based account of unity that distinguishes them.

(6) For true propositions, correspondence should be understood as a structural match between the proposition's primitive arrangement and the arrangement of objects and relations in reality, but I reject "states of affairs" or "facts" as distinct entities over and above those arranged items.

Go through these pair by pair: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear incompatible but dissolve on inspection, and which single commitment is the weakest link if I must revise one? Rank the tensions by severity. In particular, address whether (3) undermines (2) by smuggling a linking relation back in for relational contents; whether (4) is actually incompatible with accepting Bradley's argument in (1); and whether (5) together with (6) forces me to invoke two different notions of "arrangement"—one for propositions, one for reality—without admitting it. I want to know whether this holds together or collapses into an unstable hybrid.
