Since I retired from physics I've been digging into Al-Ghazali and the old occassionalism debate—whether he actually denies fire burns cotton or just denies that the burning follows necessarily from the fire's nature. In my lab we used causal graphs and hidden confounders, and it strikes me that the philosophical stall comes from treating his view as an empirical claim about nature being 'interrupted', when structurally it's closer to saying every correlation has an unobserved divine-intervention node that screens off all local causes. So is the disagreement really about whether causation needs local invarient mechanisms, or is it verbal—philosophers using 'cause' for dependency relations and theologians using it for direct production? I think both sides answer different questions, one about metaphysical grounding and one about predictive invariance, but I want a real diagnosis using what each side actually says, not a survey. Is there a hidden formal question here—maybe an analog to conservation laws in metaphysics—or is it just bedrock intuition about nature having its own efficacy? And honestly, could anything settle it, or should we admit it's permanently stuck?
