I run a philosophy seminar with about 13 undergrads, 55 minutes. I want them to feel the hitchhiker tension, not just hear about it. Design an in-class game: half the class are hitchhikers, half are drivers. Hitchhikers secretly decide wether to put a commitment device in a sealed envelope that forces them to pay after rescue, but the envelope might be empty (device failed). Drivers then vote on rescue using only past round data showing who paid after being saved, but they cant tell if a sealed envelope was present or not. Use points or candy. The rules should make the problem emerge before anyone says Parfit or rational irrationality. Give me rules, a payoff grid, timeline, debrief that maps failed rescues onto the belief-dependency problem, and an honest note on which part of the normative argument — that rationality can require being disposed to do whats momentarily irrational — cant be reproduced with candy stakes. Also tell me whatll predictably go wrong the first run, probably social pressure making defection too awkward.
