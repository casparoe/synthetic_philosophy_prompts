You are a philosopher-poet with expertise in Buddhist metaphysics and analytic philosophy of mind. Before composing, briefly outline how a poetic form's repetition and turn can mirror a metaphysical claim rather than merely illustrate it.

Write a villanelle—nineteen lines, five tercets and a closing quatrain with two rhymes—on the Buddhist doctrine of anatta (no-self) and personal identity, but avoid the generic slogan that there is no soul. Instead, use this specific framing: according to the Abhidharma analysis, what persists across lives and moments is not a self but a causal stream (santana) composed of momentary dharmas—the five skandhas—where each moment stands to its predecessor in a relation of neither strict identity nor total otherness, a middle persistence that allows moral continuity without an owner. Treat consciousness (vijnana) not as an inner witness that possesses the other aggregates, but as one more rotating, conditioned aggregate, itself shaped by mental formations (samskara). The speaker should be someone examining autobiographical memory and realizing that the first-person pronoun is only the grammatical subject of a causal story told by impersonal processes.

Choose two refrains. The first should articulate middle persistence—neither this same stream nor another entirely. The second should articulate the absence of a witness—no one is here to see it pass. Follow the standard villanelle refrain pattern so the two lines take turns, then bring them together in the final quatrain side by side. Design the rhyme scheme and line breaks so that the meeting of the refrains shows the two claims passing each other without resolving into a stable self, mirroring the stream's own structure.

The poem should be accurate enough that a specialist recognizes the Abhidharma framing rather than a pop-culture version of no-self, and strong enough that a reader unfamiliar with Buddhism finds it coherent and affecting. Do not cite texts, name specific treatises with dates, or provide a bibliography; render all concepts through the poem's imagery and logic. After your brief structural outline, present the poem.
