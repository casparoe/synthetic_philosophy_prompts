He summarized a view he read in some pop-philosophy article (maybe by a physicist turned blogger? not sure) and it went something like this: in orthodox subjective Bayesianism you update by conditioning on your evidence, but if you already knew the evidence then your prior for it is 1, so conditioning on it does literally nothing to your credence in the hypothesis. That means old evidence can never confirm a theory, which is a disaster because Einstein explaining Mercury's perihelion is the classic case of exactly that. Then the summary claimed Glymour's bootstrap solution is the only one that really works, but it only works if you ditch subjective priors entirely and treat confirmation as a purely logical relation between hypotheses and evidence sets. Is this a fair summary? I'm especially unsure about the part where it says Howson's fix is just 'kicking the can'—that sounds like an opponent's gloss rather than Howson's own view.
