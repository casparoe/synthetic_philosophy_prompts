Write a dialogue between a mathematical platonist and a nominalist debating the ontological status of error terms—residual variance—in statistical regression models. The platonist should maintain that quantifying noise as a structured parameter with distributional properties (variance, covariance) commits us to treating it as a genuine mathematical object, not merely a placeholder for ignorance, and that the predictive success of regression licenses belief in these entities. The nominalist should counter that error terms are purely notational artifacts generated by our decision to approximate relationships with linear functions; they carry no more ontological weight than the leftover in polynomial long division, and their quantification reflects practical convenience rather than discovery. Have each speaker respond directly to the other’s points: address the indispensability argument, the claim that predictive success requires realist commitment, and whether one can consistently deny the existence of entities that one’s own scientific practice measures and manipulates. Both interlocutors should be equally competent, with no strawman representations, and the exchange should turn on substantive philosophical disagreements rather than misunderstandings of statistics.
