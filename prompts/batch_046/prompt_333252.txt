Exam question: Marx’s technical concept of exploitation concerns the extraction of surplus value within a specific mode of production, whereas his concept of alienation concerns a four-dimensional estrangement—from the product of labor, the labor process itself, one’s species-being, and other people. Consider a contemporary case: a distributed team of developers contributes to an open-source infrastructure project that they collectively maintain but do not monetize. A separate commercial entity integrates that infrastructure into a proprietary platform, captures the revenue, and directs development priorities through funding rather than direct employment. Using Marx’s framework, analyze whether exploitation is present, whether alienation is present, and whether alienation in this case can be analytically reduced to exploitation or requires an independent explanation. Be precise about which conceptual tools apply and why.

Student answer:
The case clearly exhibits exploitation because the commercial entity appropriates the surplus value generated by the developers’ labor, packaging free code into a profitable service. This is structurally identical to the capitalist relation Marx analyzes in his mature economic writings, where the owner of capital extracts unpaid labor time. The developers are simultaneously alienated along all four dimensions Marx identifies: they are separated from the proprietary product built on their code; they lose control over the development process once funding priorities redirect it; their creative, species-constituting activity is instrumentalized for another’s profit; and they are set in competitive rather than cooperative relations with one another as they vie for influence within the open-source ecosystem. That said, alienation in this scenario is essentially the subjective experience of being exploited—once the extraction of surplus value is eliminated, for instance through a fully cooperative ownership model, the four dimensions of estrangement would resolve automatically.
