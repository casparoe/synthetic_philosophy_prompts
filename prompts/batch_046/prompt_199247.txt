I am writing for an upper-level undergraduate course on free will and moral responsibility, and the assignment requires building an original thought experiment rather than reviewing standard literature. Please construct a realistic, single-paragraph scenario—set in an ordinary clinical or workplace environment, with no science-fiction elements, no omniscient demons, no perfect predictors, and no appeal to supernatural or technologically implausible conditions—in which an agent meets both mainstream compatibilist conditions and a plausible libertarian condition, yet remains subject to Galen Strawson's Basic Argument. Specifically, the agent should act through reason-responsive mechanisms, genuinely endorse her motives at a second-order level, and face no external coercion; at the same time, her neural processes should exhibit genuine causal indeterminacy at the precise moment of decision, and she should exercise an uncaused causal power that fixes which option is realized. Despite this, she must not be the ultimate source of a deep psychological restructuring—such as the lasting effects of a prolonged, externally initiated therapeutic intervention that has permanently reshaped her emotional architecture and thereby determined the very range of options among which she now exercises her libertarian freedom—so that Strawson's claim applies: she is not causa sui regarding the features that make her action possible, and therefore is not truly morally responsible for it. After presenting the scenario, demonstrate that it satisfies every constraint: identify the specific action, the decision method, and the counterfactual conditions under which she would have acted differently; then explain precisely why the compatibilist judges her responsible, why the libertarian judges her responsible, and why Strawson's argument nevertheless denies true moral responsibility. Next, isolate the feature that does the isolating work—the distinction between selecting freely within a given option-space and being responsible for the constitution of that option-space itself—explain what happens to the verdicts if that feature is removed (for example, if the restructuring were entirely self-authored without prior causal dependency), and describe how a defender of either compatibilism or libertarianism would attack the scenario to preserve the agent's responsibility. Please keep the analysis self-contained: do not provide bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
