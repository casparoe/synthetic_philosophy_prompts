I've been attempting to push Searle's Chinese Room argument downward in scale, and I have encountered a structural framing problem I cannot resolve on my own. Let me suppose, for the sake of the argument, what I will call the synaptic syntax view: that individual neurons encode discrete symbolic propositions in their spike-train patterns, so that neural computation is literally formal symbol-manipulation implemented in biological tissue. If one grants this view, then the brain begins to look like an enormous, recursively nested Chinese Room—each synapse functioning as a miniature clerk that transforms input symbols according to local rules, with no single neuron grasping the overall message, and with no privileged outer room that contains the entire system, since the system is composed entirely of smaller symbol-manipulating subunits. Searle's familiar reply to the system reply addresses the case of adding a computer program to a human clerk inside a room, but this nested architecture seems different in kind: there is no macro-level agent to whom understanding might be attributed, only layers of micro-agents. Given this framing, does Searle's argument not entail that brains lack understanding altogether, unless one is willing to invoke a non-physical homunculus that somehow integrates the nested layers? Or is there a principled distinction—perhaps concerning biological causation, or the level at which intentionality is properly ascribed—that blocks the recursive application? I am deliberately setting aside standard connectionist objections and asking specifically about the nesting structure. Please separate in your response what is well-established in the philosophy of mind literature from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, and do not present a speculative or minority interpretation as if it were the consensus view.
