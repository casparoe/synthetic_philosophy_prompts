I have been thinking on my own about a philosophical idea I came across: that holding someone morally responsible may not really depend on whether they possess some deep, metaphysical freedom, but rather on ordinary emotional reactions—resentment, gratitude, indignation, hurt feelings—that people naturally direct at one another within relationships. I would like you to write a deadpan satirical parody in the form of a corporate employee handbook—approximately 600 words, formatted with numbered sections and bureaucratic subheadings—for a fictional firm named Reactive Partners LLC that has rebuilt its entire human-resources policy around this view. The handbook should present, in dry HR language, the distinction between participant reactive attitudes (the emotional responses we direct at people we regard as full members of our moral community) and the objective attitude (the detached, managerial stance we take when we treat a person as an object of social policy, like a malfunctioning machine or a very young child). The satire must exaggerate actual features of the theory rather than invent a false version: include a decision tree that tells managers precisely when they are permitted to feel resentment toward a colleague versus when they must switch to an objective attitude; a standardized Gratitude Audit Form for tracking positive reactive responses; and a policy memo explaining that the company has abolished the word deserve and replaced it with interpersonal expectation adjustments, since responsibility is now understood as woven into emotional practice rather than grounded in free will. You should also include a section on Self-Reactive Attitudes that absurdly requires employees to file quarterly reports on their own feelings of guilt and remorse, reflecting the real asymmetry between how we view ourselves and how we view others. Add a brief appendix addressing determinism, stating that the company considers the free-will debate resolved not because determinism has been disproven, but because responsibility never required metaphysical freedom in the first place—though the appendix should concede, in a footnote, that maintaining the objective attitude toward normal adult coworkers for more than a brief interval is practically impossible and may damage team morale. The voice should stay consistently corporate and never wink at the reader. The piece should be readable to someone who has never studied philosophy, yet accurate enough that a specialist recognizes the specific philosophical moves being parodied, especially the claim that asking whether determinism undermines responsibility is the wrong kind of question and that our reactive practices are too deeply embedded in human relationships to be suspended by a metaphysical verdict. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, citations, or references to specific books or articles, and do not quote any real text verbatim.
