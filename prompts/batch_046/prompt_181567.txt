I spent 30 years running experiments in a physics lab and now I'm poking around philosphy. Everyone keeps saying a thought experiment is basically just a lab experiment with the noise stripped out — you control every variable except the one you want, run it in your head, and read the result. They lean on this to argue that intuition pumps are trustworthy, almost like data. But I've been playing with this idea I call mental decoherence — in quantum mechanics measuring collapses the wavefunction, and I wonder if just picturing a scenario doesn't do the same thing: your brain collapses onto one interpretation before you've actually reasoned through it. So I want an audit of the analogy. Give me the correspondance table — what's the apparatus, the control group, the replication, the observer? Then show where it breaks, especially around mental decoherence and the fact that you can't rerun a mental trial with identical conditions. Does the conclusion that intuition pumps are reliable survive, or was the lab analogy carrying the whole argument?
