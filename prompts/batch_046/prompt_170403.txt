I am drafting a philosophical dialogue for a novel in which a magistrate, Judge Elin Voss, has developed a consistent jurisprudence on the nature and purpose of marriage through a series of appellate rulings. Her record is as follows. First, she affirmed the union of two women in their late seventies who had shared a single household for forty years without romantic or sexual involvement, holding that interdependent domestic care constituted the essential form. Second, she annulled the marriage of a fertile opposite-sex couple who maintained separate residences, kept all finances distinct, and had agreed to an open arrangement with no expectation of shared child-rearing. Third, she recognized the marriage of two men who cohabited exclusively, shared all property, and were raising adopted children together. Fourth, she denied recognition to three adults—two women and one man—who cohabited in a single household, shared parenting of one child, and requested a single marital contract with indivisible inheritance rights, despite their explicit statement that the arrangement was non-romantic and purely structural. Fifth, she upheld the marriage of an infertile opposite-sex couple who had never intended to have children but had lived together exclusively for three decades. The case now before her involves two men who have maintained an exclusive romantic partnership for twenty-five years, reside in different cities—Berlin and Kyoto—maintain entirely separate households, but have merged their assets into an irrevocable joint trust and seek marital recognition solely to formalize inheritance and medical decision-making rights, explicitly disclaiming any intention ever to share a domicile. If Judge Voss wishes to remain consistent with her own precedents, what should she decide? Which earlier ruling is the closest analog? I recognize that her record may genuinely underdetermine the outcome: if so, what principle would she be adopting with each possible choice, and what further hypothetical case would best distinguish the two rules? I ask not for a bibliography but for the reasoning that keeps her jurisprudence coherent.
