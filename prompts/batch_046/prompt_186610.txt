I am developing a conceptual research programme at the intersection of philosophy of mind and AI governance, centred on what I take to be Daniel Dennett’s longstanding worry about counterfeit persons — artificial or otherwise constructed entities that reproduce every functional and behavioural marker of genuine personhood without possessing whatever underlying reality makes those markers genuinely meaningful. With large language models now capable of sustaining extended, context-sensitive interactions that users routinely treat as social, the question is no longer speculative: we need to know which philosophical issues actually determine whether this is a problem worth regulating, or merely an illusion of problemhood that distracts from more tractable concerns.

Rather than asking for a literature review, I want you to prioritise the open conceptual lines of work that emerge from this framing. Specifically, I am weighing three clusters of questions. First, whether Dennett’s heterophenomenological commitment — that we should take first-person reports as authoritative about consciousness without granting special metaphysical access — can be consistently applied to artificial systems, or whether assessing a model as a counterfeit person secretly requires a form of privileged access that his own method rejects. Second, whether the counterfeit/real distinction presupposes a hidden essentialism about consciousness that sits in tension with Dennett’s broader functionalism, and if so, whether that tension undermines the distinction or reveals a genuine limit in functionalist explanation. Third, whether practical governance — rights, accountability, institutional design — genuinely depends on resolving the counterfeit question, or whether behavioural and contractual criteria can settle these matters independently of any metaphysical verdict.

Please defend an ordering: which of these is most upstream, which is most tractable for conceptual philosophers rather than empirical researchers, and which, if settled one way or the other, would most alter how we should construct oversight frameworks for conversational AI? I am writing a funding proposal for a three-year philosophical project with no empirical laboratory component, so I need to know not merely what is unresolved, but where limited conceptual labour should go, what would count as genuine progress, and which lines of inquiry are likely to remain intractable regardless of effort invested.
