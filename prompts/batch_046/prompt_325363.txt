hey so im helping a friend study for her phil of mind exam and i keep running into this pattern in intuition pumps that i dont have a word for. The setup pretends to isolate one big concept — like whether consciousness survives copying, or if pushing someone off a bridge is wrong — but then buries a second variable in the background conditions that actually drives the intuition. Like, the teleporter scenario says the original is destroyed, but hides that the destruction takes ten minutes and involves partial brain damage, or the moral dilemma mentions the lever is rusted so pulling it feels physically different. Our gut reaction shifts, but not because of the headline feature everyone argues about; its that buried dial doing all the work. Then the author writes as if they've tested the headline concept, when really they've just shown that partial brain damage over ten minutes is unsettling.

I know theres framing effects and confounding variables in real experiments, but this feels more specific to philosophical hypotheticals — its not just poor control, its using the narrative richness of the scenario to hide the active ingredient. Is there an established term for this? If not, can you name and define it, and draw the line between it and an ordinary auxiliary assumption? And maybe say whether it actually infects well-known pumps like the chinese room or experience machine, or if those are mostly free of it?
