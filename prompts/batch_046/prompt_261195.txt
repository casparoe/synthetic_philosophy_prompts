I am a third-year philosophy student in a metaphysics seminar, and I need a sustained satirical piece for our creative-assessment module. I want to work with Max Black’s well-known counterexample to Leibniz’s Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles—the two spheres that are qualitatively identical in an otherwise empty, symmetric universe.

Please write a parody of a luxury real-estate brochure, approximately 600 words, in the deadpan, aspirational voice of a high-end property listing such as one might find in a Sotheby’s International Realty circular. Format it with a glossy headline, separate property descriptions for each sphere, an amenities list, a “Legal, Title & Zoning” section, and a sidebar on local ordinances.

The satire should target the metaphysical tension between qualitative identity and numerical distinctness, exaggerating real features of the debate rather than inventing straw positions. Specifically, build in the following conceptual points:

- Both residences possess exactly the same intrinsic properties: perfect sphericity, uniform black surface, zero internal structure, and identical dimensions.
- The only differentiating feature is a pure relational property: they sit two miles apart in an otherwise featureless void.
- The problem of haecceity: with no qualitative difference, there is no obvious ground for saying there are two objects rather than one, yet Black insists there are two.
- The practical absurdity of treating them as separate market commodities—distinct addresses, separate title deeds, different asking prices, and a buyer’s inability to determine which unit they have entered, since inspection reveals nothing.

To keep the prompt conceptually fresh without requiring external lookup, inject a niche invented doctrine: frame the ownership dispute through “symmetrical adverse possession,” a legal fiction according to which each sphere can claim exclusive title over the other solely by virtue of occupying a relational position that the other simultaneously occupies in reverse. The brochure’s fine print should invoke this deadpan, treating primitive thisness (haecceity) as a registrable lien that can be filed with the local metaphysical registry.

The voice must stay consistently deadpan and legible—never winking at the reader. A specialist should recognize that the piece understands why Black’s example threatens the Principle of the Identity of Indiscernibles and should find the satire fair. A non-specialist should still be able to read it as a strange but coherent property advertisement.

Please do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations. Do not quote verbatim from Leibniz, Black, or any other philosopher; if you need to reference their positions, paraphrase them without quotation marks. The piece should stand entirely on its own for a reader without web access.
