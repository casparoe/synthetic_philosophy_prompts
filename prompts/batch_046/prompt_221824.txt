I am preparing a final-year seminar on personal identity and biomedical ethics, and I should like you to compose a sustained piece of deadpan satire—approximately 500 words, in British English—for my students to analyse alongside the standard literature on advance directives. Write it in the form of a parody NHS clinical-ethics committee memorandum, complete with bureaucratic headers, numbered clauses, and a signature block, entitled "Intertemporal Autonomy Protocol (IAP-2023): Binding Declaration of Persistent Selfhood." The voice should be that of a hyper-rationalist ethics board that treats diachronic autonomy not as a contested philosophical problem but as an enforceable property right held by an earlier temporal stage over all subsequent stages of the same biological life.

The document should extend the logic of an advance decision to refuse treatment far beyond medical contexts: the signatory at age forty-five should bind the same individual at age eighty not only regarding life-sustaining intervention, but also regarding career changes, religious conversion, dietary alteration, romantic attachment, and aesthetic preference, with the same legal precision normally reserved for cardiopulmonary resuscitation directives. The humour must arise from getting the philosophy exactly right rather than caricaturing it: allude, without misrepresenting them, to Ronald Dworkin’s distinction between experiential and critical interests, the problem of adaptive preferences under conditions of diminished capacity, and the Lockean continuity conditions for personal identity over time. Invent and cite, within the text, a plausible but fictional "Intertemporal Autonomy Protocol, 2023" and a corresponding "Section 4.2(b): Non-Derogable Critical Interests," so that the satire depends on a niche conceptual extension rather than on well-worn jokes about bureaucracy.

The target of the irony should be the uncritical assumption—common in both clinical practice and popular bioethics—that an advance directive expresses a single, stable, atemporal will rather than a negotiation between temporally situated selves with genuinely divergent values. Maintain sustained, deadpan irony throughout: never wink at the reader, but ensure that the exaggeration remains legible to anyone familiar with the debates about the authority of the earlier self. Please use British spelling and register consistently, avoid any request for real bibliographies or external citations, and do not quote verbatim from any actual philosophical text.
