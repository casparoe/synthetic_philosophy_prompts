I've been trying to formalize the implicit mereology of a philosopher friend who works in science studies, and I'd like to check whether her judgments about composition are genuinely unified or only locally consistent. Her track record is as follows: she treats a living human body as a single object; she treats a functioning automobile as a single object; and she treats a standard desktop computer enclosed in one tower as a single object. She grants object-status to the wax-and-wood structure of a beehive, but denies it to the aggregate of bees inside considered purely as a collection. On the negative side, she denies that a deck of cards scattered across a table is one object; she denies that a constellation is one object rather than arranged stars; she denies that a corporation is an object despite its legal personhood; she denies that a cloud is a single thing rather than a distributed mass of droplets; and she denies that a swarm of autonomous drones separated by kilometers and linked only by radio signals counts as one object, even when they share a unified navigation protocol and fail collectively.

The new case I'm weighing involves a synthetic biological-digital hybrid we might call a valley rhizome-net: thousands of engineered fungal nodes planted across a watershed, connected by both mycelial chemical signaling and low-power wireless relays, functioning collectively as a single environmental-regulation system. There is no enclosing boundary, no central processor, and individual nodes can be excavated and replaced without system collapse; yet the network maintains homeostatic water-flow regulation and responds to threats with globally coordinated firing patterns that no isolated node could produce. If she wants to stay consistent with her own precedents, what should she say about the rhizome-net? I'm less interested in the bare answer than in the reasoning: what is the closest analog among her earlier verdicts, and what are the two or three most plausible principles that could jointly explain her track record? For each principle, what does it predict about this case? And if her precedents genuinely underdetermine it, what further hypothetical—perhaps one involving strict physical enclosure with no functional integration, or conventional social definition with full functional integration—would best force her to commit to one principle over the others?
