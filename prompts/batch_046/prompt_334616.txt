I am trying to stabilize my position on causation for a seminar paper and would like a consistency audit of the following commitments. First, I accept Al-Ghazālī's occasionalism as articulated in the Tahāfut al-falāsifa—specifically, that created substances possess no causal efficacy (taʾthīr) whatsoever, and that God is the sole agent of every event, with apparent natural causation being nothing more than the habitual order (ʿāda) of conjunctions sustained by divine will. Second, I reject the standard assimilation of this view to Humean regularity theory: Al-Ghazālī's denial is total and metaphysical, not epistemic, since even the correlation between fire and burning is not grounded in any dispositional property of fire. Third, I believe occasionalism solves rather than exacerbates the problem of induction, because the uniformity of nature requires no contingent metaphysical glue if it is sustained by a necessarily rational divine will. Fourth, I hold that scientific explanations citing physical antecedents remain genuinely explanatory—not merely predictive heuristics—because they identify the specific occasions on which divine action is habitually ordered, and identifying that occasion explains why the event occurs at this particular time and place rather than elsewhere. Fifth, I maintain that if occasionalism is true, counterfactual dependence between physical events is entirely spurious: there is no fact of the matter about what would have happened had the fire not been present, except that God might have acted differently.

Please examine these for tension. In particular: does commitment (4) collapse into a disguised form of secondary causation that (1) and (2) explicitly reject? Does (5) undermine (3)'s claim that occasionalism secures induction, given that induction seems to rely on counterfactual stability? And if (4) is defensible, what exactly distinguishes my view from a compatibilist occasionalism that allows created things real but derivative causal powers? Rank the tensions by severity, indicate for each whether it is genuine or only apparent, and specify the least costly revision that would restore coherence—without assuming I am prepared to abandon occasionalism altogether.
