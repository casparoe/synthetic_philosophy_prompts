I've been stuck on this since I started volunteering at a community mediation center a few months ago and also took a moral psych course - we keep hitting the same wall in both places. The question is whether guilt or shame is the "better" moral emotion, and whether a culture can actually choose which one to cultivate. One side says guilt is clearly superior because it's action-focused and lets people repair harm without crushing thier self-worth; the other side points to cultures where shame functions as a kind of social glue, and argues that through education and ritual a society can deliberately tilt people toward one or the other. We've mapped out all the usual distinctions - guilt about acts vs shame about self, internal vs external sanction - and still the same people leave the room with the exact same views they walked in with. My hunch is that the stall isn't about missing data but about what we're actually asking: is "better" a psychological claim about mental health, a sociological claim about cooperation, or a normative claim about what kind of people we want to be? But I could be wrong - maybe there's a hidden empirical question about whether emotional norms are actually malleable at scale, or maybe we're just running on incompatible intuitions about whether it's okay for a culture to engineer feelings.

To make it more concrete, I've been playing with this distinction between what I call "thin guilt" - where responsibility gets spread so thin across an institution that nobody personally feels it - and "diffuse shame" - where a whole group experiences mild status loss but no single person is singled out. If a culture tried to build its moral education around one of these hybrid patterns, would that even count as choosing guilt or shame, or does it show that the whole binary breaks down once you move past individual psychology? I'm not asking for a literature review, just an honest diagnosis: is this disagreement verbal, substantive, or bedrock intuition? And if it's one of those, is there any conceptual distinction, study, or change of framing that could actually move it, or should we expect the stall to stay forever?
