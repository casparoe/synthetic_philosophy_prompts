I've been working with the view that Jain anekāntavāda—specifically the conjunction of syādvāda (conditional predication) and the saptabhaṅgī schema—should be read not merely as epistemic humility but as a formal ontology of partial, non-overlapping descriptions (nayas). On this reading, it offers a live middle path between absolutist realism and global relativism, one that can resolve hard cases in contemporary philosophy—such as deep moral disagreement or self-reference paradoxes—without collapsing into contextualism or paraconsistency. What would actually count against this position? I want the decisive objections and the merely awkward ones, ranked: internal inconsistencies in the naya doctrine, formal failures to regiment syādvāda without ad hoc adjustments, cases where the sevenfold framework generates contradictory commitments rather than dissolving them, and whether modern logic already captures whatever genuine insight the framework provides. I also want to know what a committed defender would concede as damaging versus what they would dismiss, and whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—if so, whether that insulation is a philosophical virtue or a defect. Please assess honestly how exposed the position really is.
