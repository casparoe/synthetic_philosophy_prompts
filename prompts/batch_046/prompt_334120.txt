I have been thinking about whether true moral dilemmas can exist -- not just hard choices, but situations where you genuinely ought to do two things that are impossible together. The discussion seems stuck: one group says morality must be consistent, so one duty always loses; another group points to cases like a rescue worker who can only save one of two people and argues that the guilt left behind proves both duties were real. I keep noticing a pattern that might explain the stalemate. In many of these examples, both choices break the very same rule -- for instance, "do not abandon someone in danger" -- just applied to different people. So the conflict may not be between two different obligations at all, but a split in how one obligation applies. Given this, is the long disagreement mainly a hidden verbal dispute about what "genuine" means, or is it a deeper clash about whether an unfulfilled duty can survive after you act? And since both sides agree on the facts of the cases, could anything -- a clear distinction, a thought experiment, or a change in framing -- actually move the debate, or should we expect it to stay frozen?
