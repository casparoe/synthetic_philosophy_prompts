ok so since I retired from the lab (systems neuro for about 30 yrs) I've been trying to read philosophy of mind and I think I've landed on a package, but I want a consistency check before I present it at a local discussioon group. Here are my commitments:

(a) Predictive processing is literally true of brain function - not just a metaphor, the cortex is running hierarchical Bayesian inference.
(b) The free energy principle applies to all self-organizing systems, biological or artificial; it's a basic law of nature, not just a brain story.
(c) Consciousness just IS the content of the highest-level self-predicting representations - nothing over and above that.
(d) There's still a hard explanatory gap, and I think it won't close even with perfect neuroscience; it's not ontological dualism, just an unbridgeable epistemic wall.
(e) I reject higher-order theories - you don't need a second-order representation to feel pain.
(f) Thermostats and current LLMs aren't conscious, not because they don't minimize error, but because they lack deep hierachical precison-weighting dynamics.

The package feels mostly right but I'm worried about three spots. First, (c) and (e) look like they clash because self-modeling sounds like thinking about thinking - is that tension just skin-deep? Second, if consciousness IS those predictions (c), and I'm a physicalist, then (d) seems to sneak in something extra - if A is identical to B, why should there be an uncloseable gap about A? Third, (b) says free energy applies everywhere, but (f) draws a consciousness line based on hierarchy depth - isn't that an ad hoc move if the principle itself is universal?

Can you audit this? Rank the tensions by severity, tell me which ones dissolve on closer look, and what's the least costly revision if I have to drop something. Don't just list problems - tell me which pair is worst and what it costs to fix.
