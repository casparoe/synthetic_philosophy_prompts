I am convening an adversarial collaboration between two camps that have spent years arguing past each other about Kurt Gödel's rotating-universe solution to Einstein's field equations and what, if anything, it proves about the metaphysics of time. The first camp, the Temporal Illusionists, maintains that Gödel's model—allowing closed timelike curves and lacking any globally consistent cosmic time coordinate—shows that temporal ordering is purely coordinate-dependent and that temporal passage is therefore not a feature of reality. The second camp, the Cosmic Structuralists, grants that our observed universe is isotropic and therefore not Gödel-rotating, but argues that the model reveals time's directionality and existence to be cosmologically grounded in large-scale matter distribution and rotation, not locally fundamental; on this view Gödel undermines localist temporal realism but saves a structural, cosmic form of it. Write the joint statement they should have produced: begin with a numbered Agreed Claims section covering the mathematical properties of Gödel's solution (homogeneous dust-filled rotating universe, existence of closed timelike curves, absence of a global time function), the observational irrelevance of cosmic rotation at present scales, and the distinction between local experienced time and global temporal structure. Follow with a Disagreements Table stating each divergence in wording both sides accept—specifically, whether the coordinate-dependence of simultaneity in this model entails the unreality of all temporal passage, and whether a physically unrealistic cosmological model can legitimately inform metaphysics of time in our actual universe. Then identify the cruxes: (i) what I will call the parity thesis—whether Gödel's failure of global simultaneity is metaphysically analogous to special-relativistic simultaneity (harmless to realism) or uniquely destructive because it permits circular causation; and (ii) the idealization problem—whether cosmological models that violate observed boundary conditions can still serve as metaphysical counterexamples, parallel to how Schwarzschild singularities are used in black-hole thermodynamics despite being physically idealized. For each crux, specify one concrete case or reasoning both sides agree is relevant, record each camp's prior prediction about what that case shows, and explain what outcome would move at least one side.
