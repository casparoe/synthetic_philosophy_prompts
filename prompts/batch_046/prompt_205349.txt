Please explain the conceptual tension between prioritarianism's weighted aggregation of welfare and the leveling-down objection, specifically in a framework where prioritarian weights are indexed not only to absolute welfare levels but also to an individual's distance from a context-sensitive sufficiency frontier. More precisely: if one adopts a non-linear weighting function—where benefits to those far below the frontier receive sharply accelerating priority, while benefits near or above it receive only modest weight—can the view reject leveling-down without implicitly relying on an independent egalitarian commitment? Clarify whether the rejection derives solely from the weighting scheme itself, or whether it requires an auxiliary principle, such as a constraint against gratuitous harm to the well-off or a lexical non-worsening condition. In doing so, distinguish what is relatively settled—namely, the standard formulation of the leveling-down objection against pure egalitarianism and the core prioritarian reply that only absolute weighted benefits matter—from what remains genuinely contested, including whether such a hybrid threshold-sensitive prioritarianism can be fully separated from a disguised pluralist position that values both priority and equality, and whether the sufficiency frontier functions as a genuine moral threshold or merely as a heuristic for weighting. Please do not present any contested interpretation as the established consensus.
