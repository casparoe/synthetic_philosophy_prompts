me and my friend were arguing for like three hours last night about whether life can have meaning if the universe is basically indiferent. I think I've landed on this weird hybrid I call fragmented localism — the absurdist is right that there's no cosmic point, but wrong that this drains everything, because meaning can be manufactered in small, disconnected episodes (like helping a stranger fix a flat, or being totally lost in a song) that don't need to add up to some grand narrative about your whole life. It's not just hedonism because the episodes can be weakly objectively valuable, and it's not exactly Susan Wolf's active-engagement view because I don't think the engagement has to be part of a larger project you care about.

So what would actually count against this? I don't want the standard 'then suicide is rational' objection — I want to know what kind of psychological finding, conceptual collapse, or rival argument would show that local fragments can't carry weight unless they're woven into something bigger. Which of these defeaters would a defender of coherence-based meaning concede as real, and which would they dismiss? And honstly, have I just made the view unfalsifiable by letting the fragments be arbitrarily small? Rank the risks from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me if there's a hidden commitment I'm not seeing.
