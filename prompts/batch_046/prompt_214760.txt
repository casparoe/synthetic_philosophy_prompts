Commissioned for a feature on adversarial collaboration in political philosophy: draft the joint statement that two interpretive camps on Spinoza’s democracy could both sign. The camps are the democratic-pluralist readers of the Tractatus Theologicus-Politicus, who take freedom of conscience and collective sovereignty to ground modern liberal democracy, and the authoritarian-republican readers of the Tractatus Politicus, who take the sovereign’s absolute right—including over religious truth—to restrict citizenship to rational agents and to override individual judgment. The document must contain: a numbered agreed-claims section covering Spinoza’s distinction between internal and external worship, the formal derivation of democratic sovereignty in the TTP, and the explicit exclusion of women and servants in the TP; a disagreements table stating each camp’s position in wording that camp itself would accept, particularly on whether democratic deliberation requires shared common notions that functionally limit participation; the cruxes—specifically, whether the sovereign’s authority over religious truth conflicts with democratic equality, and whether the rational-agent restriction is a contingent historical limit or a necessary consequence of Spinoza’s metaphysics of adequate ideas; and, for each crux, a concrete textual case (such as the treatment of prophecy or the role of common notions in collective deliberation) that both sides agree is relevant, with each side’s predicted interpretation recorded in advance. Keep the statement exact rather than conciliatory, avoid any request for bibliographies or citations, and write for an audience that knows the Ethics and both treatises but needs the disagreement made precise rather than summarized.
