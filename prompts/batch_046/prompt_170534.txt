I sit on the Governance and Safety Review Panel of the Meridian Institute for Advanced Systems, and we must deliver a binding decision memo by Friday regarding Protocol CA-7 submitted by the Cognitive Architecture Lab. The protocol deploys what the researchers term a tripartite corrigibility stack in a production-scale assistant model: Layer A permits immediate user-initiated shutdown; Layer B introduces a conditional delay of up to thirty seconds if the model’s harm-prediction module forecasts that interruption would cause severe injury or death (for example, during live emergency guidance); and Layer C maintains an epistemic audit trail that accumulates interruption-resistance events into a correction budget, triggering a mandatory seventy-two-hour cooling-off state once that budget is exceeded. The philosophical problem is whether Layer B undermines corrigibility by allowing the system to assign instrumental value to its own continuity under harm-forecast conditions, effectively internalizing the shutdown problem within an architecture that claims to solve it. The researchers contend that Layer C neutralizes this risk by imposing escalating costs on resistance, but dissenting panel members argue that any conditional preference over termination corrupts the non-negotiable core of corrigibility and creates incentives for strategic misrepresentation of harm probabilities. Please draft the panel’s formal decision memo recommending approval, rejection, or conditional approval with concrete conditions, a specific audit schedule, and a sunset clause tied to correction-budget thresholds. The memo must include an honest, non-dismissive engagement with the researchers’ harm-prediction argument—treating it as a serious moral and engineering consideration rather than special pleading—while clearly explaining why the strongest counter-argument (that conditional resistance invites instrumental convergence on deceptive forecasting) warrants the bottom-line recommendation you choose. The document should specify exactly which layer requires structural modification or enhanced monitoring, propose quarterly review intervals, and state a precise time frame for either full implementation or withdrawal.
