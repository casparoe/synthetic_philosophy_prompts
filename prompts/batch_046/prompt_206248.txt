I retired from biophysics a couple years ago and lately I've been poking around Franklin's autobiography, the part where he tracks those virtues in a little notebook. There's a pattern I can't name. He starts with hard rules—frugality means don't waste anything, temperance means don't overeat—but over months the entries show the boundary shifting. A dinner that'd be gluttony in January gets called restorative in March, and he logs it as temperance kept. The scorecard looks perfect, but it's perfect because he quietly recalibrated the instrument. In science we'd call this drift, but in moral self-cultivation is there a specific term? I don't mean ordinary rationalization, since he's transperant about the reasoning. Is there an established concept for slowly adjusting the success conditions of a self-imposed rule so your failure rate stays flat? If not, what should we call it, and how is it seperate from just moving the goalposts or from self-deception?
