I'm trying to figure out where I actually stand on procreative beneficence and liberal eugenics, going by gut feelings on concrete cases. If parents use PGT-P to avoid passing on Tay-Sachs, thats clearly okay, maybe even obligatory. If they use polygenic screening to pick an embryo with a slightly higher predicted educational attainment score because they think it will reduce suffering in a competitive world, I still think that's okay, though it feels different from the medical case. If they edit the genome to guarantee perfect pitch just because they're musicians and want a little prodigy, I think thats not okay - you're treating the kid like an art project. Selecting for gender just to 'balance' the family after three boys also feels wrong, but I'm not sure if it's wrong for the exact same reason. Then there's the case I keep flip-flopping on: a couple carries a variant for congenital insensitivity to pain. Medically it's awful - constant injuries, early death - but the only carriers form a small tight-knit community that treats the trait as part of thier identity. Selecting against it wipes out that community's future. Is that okay?

Is there a single principle that captures all these verdicts, or do I have to drop one? I want something that explains why avoiding Tay-Sachs and boosting IQ feel okay but perfect pitch and gender selection don't, and what it says about the pain-insensitivity case. Also, what would your principle predict for a case I haven't listed - say, parents editing temperament so the kid matches thier own personality, not for the kid's sake but just to make parenting easier?
