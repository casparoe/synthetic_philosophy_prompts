ok so im writing up my view on material constitution for a seminar — statue/clay but with a twist i added. heres where i stand:

(1) im a four-dimensionalist about ordinary objects: they're spacetime worms made of temporal parts.
(2) while a statue exists, it and its clay share every single temporal part; no part-level difference at all.
(3) still the statue isn't identical to the clay, since squashing destroys one but not the other.
(4) i also think the statue has a kind of "dominance" over its parts — it organizes them in a non-causal, non-mental way that the bare clay doesnt.
(5) in gradual-replacement cases (think ship of theseus but with bronze) the statue survives but the original clay mass doesnt.
(6) i deny any "further fact" beyond the parts plus that dominance relation — no bare particular, no haecceity.

do these hang together? im especially worried that (1) plus (2) makes (3) hard since they share all parts, and that (4) sounds like exactly the spooky "further fact" i reject in (6). also (5) seems to clash with (2) if "the clay" keeps getting new parts. rank the tensions by severity, say which one is just apparent, and tell me the cheapest revision. please say how confident you are in what you claim and point out the part you're least sure about.
