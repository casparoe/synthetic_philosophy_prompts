Give an example of a conceptual tension within Schopenhauer's philosophy of music in which his doctrine that melody directly objectifies the Will—without mediation by Platonic Ideas—collides with the formal, spatialized, and rule-governed architecture of a specific musical form, such as a strict fugue or canon. Construct the case so that the work's hierarchical voice-leading and mathematical structure appear to belong to the lower, representational grades of objectification proper to the plastic arts, yet Schopenhauer's system demands that all music, irrespective of internal complexity, occupy the singular position of immediate Will-expression. Do not dissolve the difficulty by merely invoking music's exceptional status; rather, show how the example exposes an instability in Schopenhauer's distinction between temporal and spatial representation, given his simultaneous denial that the Will itself is subject to the principium individuationis.
