I am preparing material for a philosophy discussion group that has recently worked through the standard formulation of the simulation argument and is now trying to apply it, not as a metaphysical curiosity, but as a critical pressure-test on extreme longtermist commitments. Most of the literature we have seen either dismisses the argument or treats nested simulations as multiplying the moral weight of the far future; I want to explore the opposite possibility. What I need is an account of the most important implications of taking the simulation argument seriously for the kind of far-future fanaticism that treats astronomically large populations of distant descendants as overriding present concerns. Specifically, I want you to examine whether the argument introduces what I will call a nested ontology discount: the idea that if we might be simulated entities projecting future outcomes, then each hypothetical layer of simulation not only scales the number of potential moral patients but also undermines our epistemic entitlement to treat those projected populations as continuous with our own causal and ethical trajectory. Does this discount weaken longtermist reasoning by breaking the chain of moral responsibility across ontological boundaries, or does it paradoxically amplify fanaticism by multiplying worlds? I am particularly interested in whether simulated 'descendants' should carry the same moral weight as base-reality ones, and whether unknown simulator ethics and arbitrary computational budgets render precise astronomical estimates of future value structurally unreliable rather than merely uncertain.

Please write without relying on bibliographies, citation lists, or appeals to specific papers I could not verify without access to a library; work from the conceptual structure alone, and treat Bostrom's framing only as common background rather than authoritative doctrine. Where the analysis depends on contested premises — for instance, about the relative likelihood of ancestor versus descendant simulations, or about whether moral obligations survive across levels of reality — flag the dependency explicitly. Finally, please say what would change your mind: which argument or consideration — perhaps about the convergence of simulator values with ours, or about the impossibility of meaningful causal interaction across simulation layers — would lead you to conclude that the simulation argument either leaves far-future fanaticism entirely intact, or in fact strengthens it by expanding the scope of moral concern? I am writing this for a group that wants rigour without treating the argument as settled doctrine, so please maintain appropriate philosophical precision throughout.
