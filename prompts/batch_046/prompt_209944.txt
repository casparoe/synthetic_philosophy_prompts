Draft a reusable decision procedure for a municipal heritage board that must resolve conflicts between a local community’s collective memory and the established historical record when designing commemorative markers. Frame the procedure around two governing norms you will define in the answer—mnemonic sovereignty (the community’s authority over the meaning of its own experience) and archival fidelity (the obligation not to institutionalize verifiable falsehoods)—but do not treat them as direct opposites. Instead, apply a three-axis assessment in fixed order: first, classify the conflict as factual (what occurred), valuative (why it mattered), or framing (what kind of event it was); second, apply the reversibility condition—whether adopting one version makes the other permanently unintelligible to future outsiders; third, apply the harm condition—whether institutionalizing one version causes present or foreseeable harm to community members or to historical understanding.

Specify the exact sequence of questions, the evidence threshold required at each stage, the three possible terminations (defer to the record, defer to the community, or design a dual-formulation marker), and the explicit flags that hand the decision to human judgment because the dispute concerns values rather than adjudicable fact. Then run the procedure on two cases: (a) a community remembers a mid-twentieth-century urban renewal program as a targeted destruction of its cultural neighborhood, while archives document it as a federally mandated infrastructure project with no explicit ethnic targeting; (b) a community commemorates a local figure as a resistance hero, while archival records show collaboration with authorities that was later mythologized to preserve communal dignity during repression. Show precisely where the procedure lands in each case, and include at least one outcome the board is likely to resist, explaining why the protocol demands that result despite the resistance. Keep the procedure practical—something a non-specialist board can run under time constraints—while preserving the conceptual distinctions that prevent it from collapsing into either pure relativism or pure archivalism.
