two friends of mine got into it over whether you can actually rank historical evils without being a total jerk. A brought up this concept she calls atrocity singularity — the idea that some events break the evaluation scale itself, not just score high on it. she said the holocaust might be singular because industrialized murder isn't just more killing, its a different ontological category, like trying to weigh a sound.

B said thats basically mysticism with extra steps. A then said okay, drop singularity, but we still need comparison, and she proposed mapping atrocities on three axes instead of one line: scale, intent, and structural amnesia — how thoroughly perpetrators erased not just bodies but the administrative existence of victims. she applied it: the belgian congo free state scores high on scale and amnesia but lower on bureaucratic intent, while the holocaust is high on all three, especially bureaucratic intent.

B countered that structural amnesia is just a fancy word for cover-up, and every regime hides bodies. A said no, there's a real distinction between burying corpses and redesigning census categories so the dead never existed in state records — she mentioned the holodomor in that context. B said ranking on axes is still ranking, just with a prettier chart.

A replied its not linear ranking, its a moral topology — mapping mountains rather than ordering them by height. then she warned that without some mapping we get atrocity inflation, where every conflict gets called a genocide and the concept becomes useless. B said maybe uselessness is the price of not turning grief into a spreadsheeet.

please adjudicate move by move. there are about eight moves here — rate each from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with brief justification. also say what both are missing: is there a coherent middle ground between singularity and spreadsheet?
