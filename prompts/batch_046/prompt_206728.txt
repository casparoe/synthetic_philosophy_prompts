Our invited speaker—referred to here as Dr. Voss, an unnamed defender of a reconstructed Harsanyi-style aggregation framework—argues that Harsanyi's aggregation theorem can be salvaged for intergenerational policy evaluation only if we restrict its scope to what she calls "structural preferences" (preferences over institutional rules rather than outcome distributions) and replace cardinal utility with a comparative-risk ordering across generations. She contends that this revision blocks the standard repugnant-conclusion objection and the ambiguity-based critique of interpersonal comparison, but at the cost of yielding an additive welfare function that is not straightforwardly utilitarian. I need a sequence of eight to ten interview questions that avoid having her merely restate the formal result. For each area, the question should anticipate her standard reply (likely an appeal to ex ante evaluation or to representative-agent constructions) and include a direct follow-up that makes that reply harder to maintain. The questions should be suitable for a philosophically literate but non-specialist audience, should contain no requests for citations, bibliographies, or further reading, and should not include generic biographical or methodological openers.
