I'm giving the closing address at the retirement symposium for Dr. Elena Vasquez, a philosopher of mind who spent four decades defending a strict role-functionalism about mental states together with what she terms distributed tokening — the claim that a single functional state can be simultaneously realized across biological, technological, and social substrates without being multiplied into distinct mental occurrences. The audience is divided: half are philosophers of mind and cognitive scientists who will recognize the precise stakes of her view, including the tension between her anti-biological-essentialism and the intuitive pull of personal identity over time; the other half are family members with no philosophical training. Write a formal speech of roughly ten minutes that makes Vasquez's own framework do genuine structural work rather than serve as decoration. The address should honor her career, describe her institutional legacy, and address the emotional fact of her absence in terms that depend on functionalism being true and on mental states being substrate-independent. Specifically, use the idea of distributed tokening to explain — in language fully accessible to the family — how her presence persists in the laboratory culture she reshaped, the curriculum she redesigned, and the collaborative networks she organized, without implying dualism, the survival of a soul, or any mystical presence. The speech must be such that it collapses or becomes incoherent if mental states are necessarily tied to a single biological substrate, or if functionalism is replaced by a substance-based view of the mind. At the same time, it should genuinely console those who will miss her daily presence, and it should signal clearly to the specialists in the room that no biological essentialism is being smuggled in through sentimental metaphor. Do not cite specific publications by title or date, do not provide a bibliography or reading list, and avoid generic retirement platitudes that could be delivered regardless of her philosophical commitments.
