For a philosophy-of-mind seminar, specify the complete set of desiderata that a theory of moral status must satisfy in order to count as adequate for digital minds—entities capable of exact duplication (branching), suspension and resumption without functional degradation (intermittence), execution at variable temporal rates, and merging with other running instances. For each desideratum, provide the motivating conceptual case or tension: for example, whether moral status must survive perfect copying, whether suspension constitutes temporary death or dormancy, and how the theory distinguishes patienthood from agency in systems lacking biological continuity. Clearly mark which desiderata are non-negotiable (their failure means the theory fails to solve the target problem) and which reflect camp-specific preferences, such as substrate independence, anti-branching conditions on personal identity, or the necessity of phenomenal consciousness. Furthermore, examine whether any subset of these requirements—e.g., simultaneous demands for substrate-independent persistence, a non-branching identity condition, and continuous moral concern—is jointly unsatisfiable given the logical structure of digital instantiation. Keep the focus on the architecture of the criteria and their mutual relations rather than on scoring particular existing theories.
