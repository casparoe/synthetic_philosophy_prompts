I am drafting the final exam for an upper-level metaphysics seminar. Write one 90-minute essay question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks, on the identity of indiscernibles and Black's spheres. The question must not be answerable by summarizing Black's standard two-sphere counterexample. Instead, present this modified case: two perfect spheres in an otherwise empty, infinite, isotropic three-dimensional space, identical in all intrinsic qualitative properties and in all spatial relations to other objects (there being none), except that each sphere rotates uniformly about an axis parallel to the other, one clockwise and one counter-clockwise as viewed from the same pole. Ask students to evaluate whether this rotational difference supplies Leibniz with a qualitative relational distinction that saves the principle of the identity of indiscernibles, or whether orientation-relative properties are not genuinely relational in empty space, and whether appealing to them collapses the boundary between qualitative and non-qualitative properties. The model answer must address: (i) whether self-rotation is an intrinsic or a relational property; (ii) whether the anti-parallel axes generate a weak-discernibility relation without invoking absolute space; (iii) whether saving the principle this way renders it empirically empty or trivially satisfied; (iv) how Black's original symmetry argument is affected once rotational orientation is admitted; and (v) what a defender of the principle should say if absolute rotation is denied. The marking scheme should name five must-hit points, allocate marks that sum to 20, and include a one-line note on the typical mistake for each. Do not request bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Finally, conclude with a brief statement of what argument or physical consideration, if shown incoherent, would lead you to revise your verdict on whether the principle survives in this modified case.
