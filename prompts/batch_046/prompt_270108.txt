Write a formal one-page research proposal (approximately 900 words) for a six-month interdisciplinary project in philosophy of mind. Frame the project around the following precise question: Daniel Dennett distinguishes three predictive stances—the physical stance, the design stance, and the intentional stance—and holds that patterns successfully detected from any of these stances count as real patterns. Your proposal should investigate a gap in that literature: the design stance is normally reserved for artifacts that have actual designers, yet we routinely deploy it toward evolved biological structures and toward large-scale trained computational systems that have no designer in the relevant sense. Ask whether applying the design stance to such designer-less systems (a) collapses it back into the physical stance, (b) illicitly imports intentional content by treating evolution or optimization processes as pseudo-designers, or (c) reveals a genuinely intermediate ontological category—call them functional-optimization patterns—that are real, stance-detectable, and neither reducible to bare physics nor dependent on mental-state attribution.

Write in a polished academic voice. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, citation of specific texts, or requests for further references; refer to philosophical positions and arguments in general terms only.
