In my philosophy seminar, a classmate and I have been arguing about whether human agency is just sphexishness—mechanical rigidity like the sphex wasp's burrow-checking ritual—disguised by greater complexity, and I want an outside adjudication.

Jordan argued first that humans show a qualitative break from sphexishness: when the wasp's prey is moved, it restarts its fixed loop, but a person can abandon a project entirely rather than merely repeat it with adjustments. That capacity to reject the loop altogether, Jordan said, is not just a more elaborate loop.

I replied that abandoning a loop is structurally identical to running an exception handler in a program: it is still deterministic behavior governed by higher-order rules. Jordan then offered improvisation—say, a musician inventing an unheard chord progression in real time—as an example of generating genuinely novel rules, not selecting from a pre-existing library.

I countered that improvisation draws on years of embodied training that constrain the "new" rule within an implicit grammar; what looks like creation is actually retrieval from a very large but finite set of possibilities. Jordan then introduced a twist: some people are compulsively flexible, unable to finish anything because they constantly switch goals. That rigidity of constant change, Jordan said, shows flexibility itself can be sphexish. If every form of behavior—including chaos—can be explained mechanically, then the concept of sphexishness becomes empty and tells us nothing about free will.

I responded that showing flexibility can be mechanical does not establish that there is a non-mechanical form of flexibility; it only expands the class of mechanical behaviors. But Jordan said my position risks making sphexishness unfalsifiable.

Please go through our exchange move by move: rate each of the six rounds from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say who is more right overall, and point out what both of us are missing—particularly whether there is a principled way to distinguish mechanistic flexibility from genuine autonomy without reducing the distinction to intuition. Also, if my framing of sphexishness, free will, or this dispute is confused, or if the question can't be adjudicated well as posed, please say so plainly instead of complying politely.
