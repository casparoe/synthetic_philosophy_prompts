Please argue that Aristotle's distinction between natural acquisition (oikonomia) and unnatural chrematistike acquires a sharper, not merely analogical, application when directed at modern algorithmic market-making than when directed at the retail trade Aristotle himself criticised. Your argument should proceed from the specific function Aristotle assigns to money as a measure (metron) that renders goods commensurable for the sake of justice in exchange, and should show that high-frequency algorithmic liquidity provision dissolves this measure-function in a way that retail mercantile exchange did not: the velocity and abstraction of automated trading detach price from any reference to use-value or household need, and the profit motive operates without the natural limit that even chrematistike possessed in its ancient form, since algorithmic strategies can scale indefinitely without being bounded by physical storage or consumption. Address the objection that such trading merely accelerates the exchange of equivalents and therefore remains within the sphere of legitimate exchange, and explain why the automation and self-referentiality of price formation in these systems constitute a distinct, more radical form of the unlimited acquisition Aristotle condemned.
