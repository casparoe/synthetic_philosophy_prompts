Im trying to parse this old pragmatist passage where the author says belief is basically just a mental rule that tells you how to act, and that the only reason we think at all is because doubt feels irritating. He divides ways of stopping that irritation into four: stubbornness, authority, pure reasoning, and testing against the world. He insists only the fourth makes belief depend on anything outside the head. But then he defines belief itself as readiness for action, which sounds totally internal. So Im stuck: if belief is just an internal habit of action, in what sense does the scientific method make it depend on the outside world? And when James talks about having the right to believe in live, forced, momentous cases where evidence is lacking, is he violating Peirce's framework, or can Peirce's own view accommodate choosing a belief for emotional reasons as long as the belief still guides action consistently? I need help seeing whether theres a hidden consistency or a real contradiction between belief-as-habit and belief-as-world-dependent, especially for moral beliefs that dont change our behavior in testable ways.
