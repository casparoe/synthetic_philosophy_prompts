Consider an extension of the Sleeping Beauty reasoning to ordinary first-person belief. (1) In the standard case, upon awakening Beauty should assign credence 1/3 to Heads, because her evidence is the centered proposition "I am awake now," and there are three centered worlds consistent with the experimental protocol—Heads-Monday, Tails-Monday, and Tails-Tuesday—which she weights equally. (2) The justification depends not on objective frequencies but on her inability to distinguish among the centered worlds that the protocol permits. (3) Apply the same principle outside the lab: every morning you have an experience E that is, in principle, shared by countless other people at this moment, and perhaps by vastly more digital or simulated copies. (4) If self-locating reasoning requires distributing credence equally over all centered worlds that share your current evidence and are permitted by the "protocol" of reality, then upon having E you should assign credence proportional to the number of such worlds. (5) Because long-lived individuals and copies presumably outnumber short-lived unique ones, you should conclude that you are almost certainly not the particular person your memory suggests, but one instance among many. (6) This dissolves ordinary autobiographical belief, which is absurd. (7) Therefore either the Sleeping Beauty reasoning carries a hidden restriction that blocks this extension, or it undermines all first-person knowledge. Which step should be rejected, and what does that rejection cost the theory of self-locating credence—does it require an ad hoc boundary between "experimental" and "ordinary" awakening conditions, or does it show that centered-world weighting was never the right rule to begin with? If you prefer to keep (1) and (2), explain what principled constraint prevents (3)–(5) from following.
