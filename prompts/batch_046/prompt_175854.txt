I'm putting together a view for a meta-ethics / philosophy of medicine crossover seminar, and I want to check if my takes hang together before I present. Here's what I think:

1. Pain is essentially unpleasant; the badness isn't an add-on, its in the phenomenal quality itself.
2. Pain asymbolia (patients who feel pain but say its not bad, don't avoid it) is genuine pain — they've lost the affective valence layer but kept the sensory core.
3. The primary moral reason to administer analgesics is to relieve suffering; tissue damage is monitored separately.
4. Even so, doctors should still offer painkillers to asymbolia patients who ask, since bodily autonamy doesn't depend on suffering.
5. I reject the idea that pain represents bodily damage — its just a raw qualitative state.

Which ones actually pull against each other? 1 and 2 seem like they're in direct conflict, and 3 with 4 feels off — if there's no suffering, whats the point? But maybe some of these tensions are fake. What's the least costly fix, and rank them by how serious they are?
