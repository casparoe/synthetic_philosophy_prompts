I was arguing with my roommate about whether "what it's like" is even a coherent thing to talk about, and my intuitions are completely scattered. Here are the cases where I have a gut feeling, and I want to know if there's one principle that ties them together or if I just have to drop one.

When two people both call a sunset beautiful but one reports this warm expansive inner glow and the other says he just judges it good with no special feeling, I think they're genuinely describing different mental lives—it's not just vocabulary. But then I think about those brain-decoding studies where they predict you're seeing red from the scan alone, and it feels like the "red-feel" is just the physical state with a subjective label slapped on, no extra property being detected. Also, people with congenital insensitivity to pain avoid injury through reflex just fine. If I say "they avoid damage because they lack the hurt-quality," that doesn't explain anything the reflex story doesn't already cover, so in third-person explanation qualia seem totally redundant. But when I take aspirin, I'm not trying to tweak some reflex arc; I'm specifically trying to kill this awful feeling. So in my own case, the feeling does real explanatory work—it's why I acted. And there's this circularity worry I keep hitting: people say a brain state is painful in virtue of its phenomenal character, but then they define that character as "the way pain feels," which sounds like saying it's red because it's reddish. So part of me thinks the whole posit is either redundant or incoherent. Still, I genuinely feel like I can wonder whether a zombie duplicate of me is missing something, and that wonder doesn't feel like pure confusion.
