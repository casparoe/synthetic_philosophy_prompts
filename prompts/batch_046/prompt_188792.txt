I was arguing with my flatmate over coffee about this idea I’m calling the careenium — imagine a reactive mental substrate, like a goo that shifts shape — and these little embedded symbol-objects called simmballs that sit inside it. The question we kept circling was whether the symbols actually push the substrate around causally, or if they’re just decorative labels on processes that would happen anyway. I’ve got four gut verdicts that I’m not sure hang together, and I’d like to know what principle, if any, captures them.

Here they are. One: put a triangular simmball meaning “danger” into fresh careenium and it contracts sharply; swap in the exact same triangle now meaning “calm” and it still contracts the same way. So I think syntax is doing the pushing, not semantics. Two: a circular simmball meaning “danger” produces no movement at all, and a circle meaning “safety” also does nothing — again, shape seems to be the driver. Three: but then there’s this odd aged-careenium state where only the triangular “danger” combo works; the triangle with “calm” or the circle with “danger” both leave it totally still. That feels like meaning and syntax have to line up together for the push to occur. Four: take that same triangular “danger” simmball and drop it into careenium that’s lost its reactive edges, and nothing happens — the symbol hasn’t changed, so maybe the substrate’s own condition is what really allows any pushing?

My tentative principle was that simmballs only move careenium when their syntactic form matches the substrate’s current receptive profile, and semantic content is always causally irrelevant. But case three seems to break that, and case four makes me worry we’re just describing substrate dynamics with symbol-talk tacked on afterwards. Is there a precise principle — maybe with a couple of clauses — that saves all four verdicts without being too contrived? Or do I have to overturn one of them, and if so which? Please also say what your account predicts for a case I haven’t listed: what if the careenium spontaneously generates an internal simmball with no external cause — does the substrate push itself, or is there no push at all? Write your answer in British English, and don’t worry about citing papers — I just want the reasoning spelt out plainly. (Also, sorry if my terminology sounds a bit made up; it probably is.)
