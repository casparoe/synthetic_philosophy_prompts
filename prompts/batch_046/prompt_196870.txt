Please describe the most important implications of Skolem's paradox for the claim that first-order ZFC cannot determinately establish the existence of genuinely uncountable sets. In particular, consider the argument that since ZFC has a countable model in which the power set of the natural numbers is internally uncountable yet externally countable, uncountability is not an intrinsic feature of a collection but an artifact of the model's limited descriptive resources. Address what this means for the idea of a unique intended model of set theory, whether the internal-versus-external distinction saves determinacy for cardinal comparison, and whether the paradox provides a compelling reason to prefer a second-order categorical axiomatization over the first-order framework.
