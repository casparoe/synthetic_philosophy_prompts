For my AP Philosophy independent study, I am examining whether generational labels—Baby Boomers, Millennials, Gen Z—denote real social kinds or merely demographic fictions. To structure my essay, I have outlined four competing ontological accounts: (1) Macro-Event Essentialism, which holds that generations are natural kinds anchored to clusters of birth years shaped by shared historical disruptions; (2) Discursive Fictionalism, which treats the labels as marketing and media constructs with no stable referent; (3) Differential Cohortism, which claims a generation has no intrinsic properties and exists only as a relational position between adjacent age cohorts; and (4) a view I am calling Institutional Phase-Locking, according to which generations are real but neither biological nor psychological kinds—instead, they are institutionally stabilized temporal phases that emerge when education systems, labor markets, and media infrastructures align around a specific age bracket for a sustained period. Could you rank these four accounts from 1 (most defensible) to 4 (least defensible), assign each a plausibility score out of 10, and provide a one-sentence justification for each? Please base your evaluation specifically on how well each account handles the borderline case of cusp cohorts—such as those born 1977–1983, often called Xennials—who appear to share formative economic and technological conditions with both Gen X and Millennials.
