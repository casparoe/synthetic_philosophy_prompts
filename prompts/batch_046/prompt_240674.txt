I'm taking a course on metaphysics of personal identity, and we've been working through origin essentialism—the thesis that any particular person necessarily originates from the specific sperm and egg they actually came from, so that they could not have existed had different gametes fused. Rather than stay in abstract possible-worlds territory, I want to see how this idea interacts with actual institutional design. Consider the following patchwork in family law and citizenship: surrogacy and gamete-donation contracts in many jurisdictions assign full legal parenthood to intended or social parents, explicitly severing legal ties to genetic progenitors and treating the child as if born naturally to those parents; adoption law operates on a parallel logic, substituting a new legal identity tied to the adoptive family and treating biological origin as legally irrelevant to parentage and inheritance; yet citizenship-by-descent statutes and certain bloodline inheritance doctrines still trace rights precisely through biological genetic lineage, granting claims because one was born of specific citizens regardless of social upbringing or intent. Layered on top of this is the emerging practice of embryo adoption, in which leftover IVF embryos are implanted in a recipient who is neither the genetic mother nor the intended social mother, yet the resulting child is typically assigned by birth certificate to the recipient family rather than to the genetic parents.

What philosophical view of origin and personal identity does this institutional patchwork presuppose? Does it commit to the claim that you could have come from a different sperm and egg—since family law seems happy to reassign parentage across genetic lines—while citizenship law treats that same genetic origin as carrying an inalienable identity-condition? Reconstruct the implicit metaphysics: is there a consistent theory that allows genetic origin to fix citizenship rights but allows social intent or gestation to override it for family membership, or is the practice philosophically underdetermined, licensing both an essentialist reading (the genetic origin fixes who you are for some purposes but not others) and an anti-essentialist reading (the person is constituted by legal and social recognition, with genetics as mere causal background)? Distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely accommodates. And if origin essentialism were taken seriously—if it were genuinely impossible for you to have existed with different gametes—what exactly would have to change in surrogacy contracts, adoption records, citizenship statutes, or embryo-adoption protocols? Would the law be forced to treat every change in genetic origin as the creation of a different person, or can the entire patchwork be explained without invoking any metaphysics of origins at all?
