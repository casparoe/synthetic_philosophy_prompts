My grad student just handed me this chain about Pascal and I think it's broken but I can't say where. (1) Pascal's point about justice and force is that justice without coercion is impotent, and coercion without justice is tyrannical, so a legitimate order has to marry the two. (2) Now consider algorhythmic governance: an automated system that applies rules consistently has force, and if its rules are procedurally fair it also has justice in some sense. (3) By (1), any system like this that actually runs—people obey, outcomes get enforced—is therefore legitimate. (4) But then a bureacracy that denies all appeals and hides behind fair procedures still counts as just, which is ridiculous. (5) To avoid that we'd need to say procedural fairness isn't real justice, but then Pascal's own standard—justice as whatever actually constrains power—seems to demand the opposite. (6) So either Pascal licenses every functioning state apparatus, or he smuggles in a substantive conception he never defends. Which step is wrong? I'd rather save (1) and (4) if possible—what's the cost?
