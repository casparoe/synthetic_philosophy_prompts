So I was discussing this with my roommate after our philosophy of science lecture and I think I've talked myself into a kind of weird corner. I'm defending this view: for any causal story you tell about what a transformer is actually computing—say it's tracking syntactic agreement—there's always an observationally equivalent "shadow algorithim" that produces the exact same token distributions through totally different internal rules. And since we only ever observe behavior, even with probbing, we can never non-arbitrarily justify picking the real mechanism over its shadow. I think this is actually stronger than standard Quine-Duhem underdetermination, because it's not about auxiliary hypotheses being adjustable—it's about algorithmic-level multiple realizability baked into the architecture itself.

What would genuinely count against this position?
