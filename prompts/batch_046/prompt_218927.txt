I'm presenting a twenty-minute position paper next Thursday at the interdisciplinary workshop "Rationality, Language, and Public Space," attended by philosophers of science, urban planning scholars, and policy economists. My thesis is that Rudolf Carnap's principle of tolerance—standardly discussed in the philosophy of logic and language—should be extended to municipal zoning and planning codes. I argue that form-based codes, Euclidean zoning, and performance-based regimes are not competing empirical descriptions of urban reality that can be adjudicated by a single scientifically optimal master plan; rather, they are conventional frameworks selected for practical efficacy, and rational planning policy should therefore embrace pluralism about these frameworks instead of seeking a uniquely correct zoning language. The audience is roughly thirty people: about half are philosophers of science familiar with Carnap's work on linguistic frameworks but skeptical about exporting logical-empiricist ideas into concrete policy; about a quarter are urban planning scholars who treat zoning chiefly as an instrument for equity and environmental outcomes and who will resist any framing that seems to treat power and history as external to framework choice; and the rest include a public-choice economist and several historians of planning who are deeply suspicious of appeals to rational neutrality. I need a speaker's prep sheet for the Q&A session that will follow. Please list the eight objections I am actually likely to face, ordered from the most damaging to my position to the least, phrased in the vocabulary and conceptual register of the subgroup that will raise each one. For each objection, provide a concise reply suitable for delivery in about two minutes without notes, explicitly flag the weakest point in that reply, and indicate whether I should concede any part of the objection up front. Finally, identify the single question I am hoping nobody asks—likely from the economist or the historian—phrased as they would actually phrase it, together with the briefest honest reply that does not collapse the overall argument. Please avoid asking for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists; I will not have access to external resources during the session.
