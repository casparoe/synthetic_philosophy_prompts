In a debate about proprietary rights over conceptual art—specifically, whether an artist can claim ownership over a performance work that exists only as a set of written instructions, never physically executed by the artist themselves—someone shut down the discussion with the line that you cannot own an idea, only its expression can belong to anyone. For each reading, say whether it is a trivial platitude, a substantive thesis that can be defended, or false, and then indicate which of these readings actually supports the board’s conclusion that the instructions may be reproduced freely, and which ones are being smuggled in without doing any real argumentative labor.
