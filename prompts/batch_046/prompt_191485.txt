Write an essay evaluating whether karma can be reconstructed as a secular theory of moral desert without collapsing into standard Western retributivism. Frame the debate around two competing interpretations: an etiological account, which holds that present suffering is deserved only if it stands in a direct causal-historical relation to a prior wrong committed by the very same continuing agent; and a causal-role account, which holds that suffering can serve the normative function of restoring moral equilibrium even in the absence of diachronic personal identity, provided it occupies the correct structural position within a system of moral accounting. Weigh the strengths and weaknesses of each interpretation—paying particular attention to whether the causal-role version can avoid the charge of punishing an innocent successor, and whether the etiological version can be sustained without metaphysical commitments to rebirth. Reach a definite conclusion and justify it: defend either that karma must be abandoned as a coherent secular theory of desert, or that one of these interpretations succeeds in preserving its distinctive normative structure.
