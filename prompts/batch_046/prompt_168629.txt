I'm preparing notes for a philosophy discussion group and want a consistency check before I present. Here is where I stand on why we should be moral:

(1) I am a moral realist about harm: there is a mind-independent fact that unnecessary cruelty is wrong, not merely socially disapproved.
(2) I accept Glaucon's social-contract account of justice: justice is a rational compromise between harming others and being harmed oneself, so it is instrumentally valuable rather than intrinsically good.
(3) I take the ring of Gyges to show that, if detection and reputation loss were impossible, rational agents would have no internal reason to act justly; therefore moral motivation is purely instrumental and externally sustained.
(4) I reject evolutionary debunking arguments: even if our moral beliefs evolved for social coordination, that origin does not undermine their normative authority.
(5) I think moral self-deception is both possible and structurally necessary. Because moral norms function like what I call credence goods—cooperative arrangements whose value collapses if motives are transparent—people must deceive themselves into believing they act from principle rather than strategic calculation.
(6) So moral education should be purely outcome-based: we should supervise behavior through external rewards and punishments, and we should not cultivate internal moral reasoning or virtue for its own sake.

Do these hang together? Is (1) compatible with (2) and (3), or does my realism commit me to intrinsic moral reasons that the social-contract and instrumental-motivation claims deny? Does (5) conflict with (3), since (3) denies that internal moral motives exist at all, while (5) assumes people can misrepresent such motives to themselves? Is the tension between (5) and (6) genuine—if self-deception requires having internal reasoning to misrepresent, doesn't an exclusively outcome-based education undermine the very conditions for (5)? And is there only an apparent conflict between (1) and (4) that dissolves once origins are separated from justification?

Please rank the tensions by severity, say which commitment is least costly to revise, and specify exactly what each resolution costs. Identify which conflicts are real and which are illusory, and do not just list problems—trace what each view commits me to.
