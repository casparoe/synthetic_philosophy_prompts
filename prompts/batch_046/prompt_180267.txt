For a workshop on consciousness and self-reference I’ve sketched my own position, and I want a consistency audit before I present it. Here is where I stand:

(1) I reject the Cartesian theater: there is no single spatiotemporal locus in the brain where “everything comes together” for a unified observer.

(2) I accept Dennett’s multiple drafts model: conscious experience is constituted by parallel, competing narrative streams that achieve coherence only through retrospective editing, with no canonical “first” draft.

(3) I think global workspace broadcasting, in the Baars–Dehaene tradition, describes the functional mechanism behind this retrospective editing, so the drafts model and global workspace theory are complementary descriptions of the same architecture rather than rivals.

(4) I hold that the hard problem of consciousness is essentially an artifact of the theater assumption: once the theater is abandoned, the explanatory gap collapses into an empirical problem of narrative integration and predictive error-minimization.

(5) At the same time, I maintain that phenomenal concepts are genuinely irreducible: knowing what it is like to experience crimson requires a direct acquaintance that no third-person account of drafting, broadcasting, or error-correction can fully capture.

(6) I also think that a sufficiently complex hierarchical predictive-processing system—one that sustains a self-model through cross-modal error-minimization—would possess real phenomenal consciousness even in the absence of any theater-like structure.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to conflict but can be reconciled, and how severe each tension is. For each real conflict, specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what that revision costs me elsewhere in the package. Then rank the tensions by severity, and identify which single commitment I should drop if I want to clean up the most damage with one move. Be specific about whether (3) smuggles a functional theater back into (1), whether (4) can survive alongside (5), and whether (6) commits me to a form of panpsychism I have not endorsed.
