Following a lengthy argument with a friend about whether normative reasons can persist and exert force when there is no longer anyone who holds them—whether rationales can genuinely float free of all reasoners—I want to explore the idea through fiction rather than further abstraction. Write a short story set in a post-occupancy administrative complex where an automated arbitration network continues to generate, store, and enforce rationales for action long after every agent capable of acting on them has vanished. The rationales should be structured as genuine normative claims—obligations, permissions, justifications—not as mere data labels, and the system should treat them as binding inputs without possessing consciousness, understanding, or intentionality anywhere in its loops. To keep the scenario from being a generic artificial-intelligence narrative, invent a specific niche mechanism—perhaps a “rationale resonance” protocol that propagates orphaned reasons across disconnected subnets, causing them to accumulate causal weight in sectors that no longer contain any relevant beings. The plot should center on a maintenance drone that discovers a rationale applying precisely to a category of agents that no longer exists, and that must execute a procedural decision—preserve, delete, or propagate—without any grasp of “ought,” “reason,” or “applicability.” Through this encounter, illustrate the philosophical tension between treating these free-floating rationales as carrying real normative weight and treating them as causally efficacious but normatively empty patterns. Keep the tone restrained and conceptual rather than melodramatic, and avoid attributing human-like interiority to any machine in the story.
