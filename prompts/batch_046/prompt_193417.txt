Write a joke about the extended mind thesis that would make sense to someone familiar with philosophy of mind, but center it on a somewhat niche elaboration of the “cognitive bloat” problem: imagine a philosopher who claims that not only his personal notebook, but an entire university library card catalog he has never actually consulted counts as part of his cognitive system because it satisfies the reliability-and-accessibility conditions of the parity principle. The punchline should turn on the practical tension this creates—perhaps a librarian or administrator treating the catalog as an external institutional resource that must be formally checked out or reserved, thereby forcing the philosopher to confront whether a portion of his own mind has just been lent to another patron. The joke should be coherent and not depend on any bibliographic knowledge beyond the basic idea of cognition extending into the environment.
