hey, i sit on this tiny municipal heritage board — three people, part time, no budget for consultants — and every couple months we recieve requests to either preserve or deliberately let fade old local records tied to conflicts: factory strikes, eviction fights, protests that split neighborhoods. i need a reusable desicion procedre my board can actually run in a one-hour meeting with no ethicist present, grounded in the real distinctions Margalit draws between ethics and morality of memory and in Rieff's idea that cultural authority decides what past is sacred vs disposable. the procedre should list the ordered questions we ask, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the hard stop points where we say no / do not institutionalize, and the spots where its just a values call that has to go to a vote. apply it to three cases: (1) digitizing and archiving protest flyers from a 1980s labor strike that some families still blame for the mill closing; (2) allowing a temporary sidewalk memorial to a 1990s local disaster to decay naturally rather than moving it into the archive; (3) preserving police logs from 1970s eviction raids where descendants of both the officers and the tenants now jointly ask us to destroy them. one of these should come out the opposite of gut instinct. also be explicit about what the procedre cant settle — like when both sides claim victim status — and flag where a small board like ours should hand the call to the wider community instead of deciding ourselves.
