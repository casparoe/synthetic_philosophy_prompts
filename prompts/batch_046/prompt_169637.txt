hey so i've been chewing on this hofstadter idea that the self isn't a physical thing but more like a center of narrative gravity -- a pattern that pulls experiences together into one continuous story. i'm a backend engineer so i keep comparing it to distributed systems with a single source of truth, but my gut reactions on actual cases don't line up clean.

here's where i stand:

1. teleportation: beam my brain-state to mars, destroy teh original body. i think mars-me is still me. same narrative thread, just running on new hardware.

2. cloning: copy the same brain-state into two fresh bodies. now i feel like there's no canonical me anymore -- it's a git fork with no upstream. both bodies think they're me, but neither is fully right.

3. my coworker who has dissociative identity disorder: two personalities with seperate memory threads and goals, switching in one skull. i'd say there are genuinely two centers of narrative gravity in there, not one person with a broken self.

4. my employer: legal personhood, board minutes, strategic plans that outlive any employee. but there's no one home -- no subjective gravity pulling experience together. definitely not a self.

5. an AI trained on every email and chat log i ever wrote, generating text in my exact voice. it has pattern-continuity but no sensory feedback loop feeding new lived experience back into teh pattern. not me, just a cached echo.

my rough principle is: a self exists when there's continuous first-person integration of new experience into a single future-projecting narrative. but case 2 breaks it (both bodies integrate), case 3 also fits it twice over, and case 4 lacks integration despite continuity. case 5 has teh pattern but no integration loop.

is there one principle that gets all five without being totally ad-hoc? if not, which verdict should i drop -- probably teh cloning one? and what does the winning principle say about my stuck case: if i gradually replace my neurons with silicon chips that preserve narrative flow, do i stay the center the whole way, or is there a cutoff point where teh gravity just dissipates?
