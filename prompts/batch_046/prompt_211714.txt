Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed greedy reductionist and a committed anti-greedy scaffold theorist on whether bottom-up crane-building can fully explain the emergence of compositional semantics in symbolic communication systems, without invoking skyhooks.

Structure the document in four parts:

1. Numbered agreed claims. Define cranes, skyhooks, and greedy reductionism in wording both sides accept; state the formal conditions that compositionality requires (productivity, systematicity, and hierarchical constraint); and list the shared criteria for a successful bottom-up explanation versus an explanatory gap that would justify a non-reductive scaffold.

2. A disagreements table with two columns—one phrased in terms the reductionist accepts, one in terms the scaffold theorist accepts. Cover at least: (a) whether the structural constraint that governs compositional combination can itself be assembled entirely from lower-level associative learning mechanisms; (b) whether collective-level error-correction in a signaling system introduces explanatory autonomy that survives full decomposition into individual-level updates.

3. The cruxes. Identify two or three sub-questions such that resolving them would move at least one party. For each crux, specify a concrete conceptual case or evolutionary model—such as a simulated population of agents equipped only with local associative rules in which hierarchical syntax nevertheless emerges—that both sides agree bears on the issue. Record each side's predicted outcome in advance: what result would count, for the reductionist, as proof that the scaffold is just another crane; and what result would count, for the scaffold theorist, as proof that an irreducible structural constraint is operating.

4. A brief concluding section distinguishing verbal from substantive disagreement. State whether any part of the dispute—e.g., an honorific versus technical use of "explanatory autonomy"—is merely verbal, and pinpoint exactly where the remaining substantive disagreement lies.

Keep the formulation precise and neutral. Do not request citations, bibliographies, named scholars beyond the conceptual framework itself, or references to specific empirical studies. Avoid verbatim quotations from any published text. Write in formal American English.
