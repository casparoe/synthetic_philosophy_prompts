I've been working through a discrete-ontology extension of Einstein's hole argument and keep landing in a place that dissolves local determinacy everywhere. Here is the chain: (1) In GR, if the field equations are generally covariant, applying a diffeomorphism that is the identity outside a matter-free region but permutes points inside yields a mathematically distinct solution with identical boundary conditions and exterior matter distributions. (2) If spacetime points possessed intrinsic identity, this would violate determinism, since field values at specific interior points wouldn't be fixed by the exterior. (3) The standard relational resolution denies points intrinsic identity: the two solutions describe one physical situation because all physical relations are preserved. (4) Now consider a discrete ontology where fundamental reality consists of events bearing only causal relations to one another. Apply an analogous transformation inside a "causal hole"—a region with no causal links crossing its boundary except through a fixed boundary layer—that permutes internal events and rewires internal causal relations while leaving all boundary relations untouched. (5) By the same relational reasoning, if events lack haecceity, this permuted structure describes the same physical situation as the original. (6) But unlike the continuous case, discrete permutations can alter reconstructed properties—such as the cardinality of maximal chains, the dimension of embedded submanifolds, or the presence of local closed causal loops—without touching the boundary. (7) So relationalism about discrete events seems to entail that local structural properties inside any sufficiently isolated region are physically indeterminate. (8) This applies not merely to empty regions but to any region embeddable in a larger causal hole, which includes every actual laboratory, since no observation fixes the entire causal past and future. (9) Therefore relationalism about discrete spacetime events undermines the determinacy of local physics everywhere. (10) Yet we do experimentally determine local structural facts. (11) So either discrete relationalism is false, or the analogy in (4) is broken. I want to keep (3) and reject (9), but I cannot tell whether the failure is in (4), (5), or (6). Please take a definite position: name the specific step you think is weakest, explain precisely why it collapses, and say what the relationalist must pay to preserve the rest. Don't survey options—commit to one diagnosis and defend it.
