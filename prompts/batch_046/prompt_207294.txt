Construct a realistic, one-paragraph decision scenario set in an ordinary professional environment—such as a hospital, law firm, or engineering office, with no science fiction, no perfect predictors, no demons, and no exotic physics—in which causal decision theory and evidential decision theory both recommend the same course of action, but updateless decision theory recommends the opposite. The scenario should center on a decision-maker who recognizes that her decision algorithm is being executed in at least one other causally and evidentially isolated context: for example, an identically trained colleague in a distant branch office applying the same protocol, or a formally identical decision procedure instantiated in a separate institutional division. Because of this, logical correlation exists across the executions without any causal link or observational evidence connecting them. The stakes must be ordinary—career advancement, professional reputation, or patient welfare—not astronomical, and any predictive element present should be no more accurate than a shrewd human expert.

After presenting the scenario, provide the following analysis: (i) state the specific local action, the local payoff structure, and the remote payoff structure tied to the other execution; (ii) show step-by-step why causal decision theory and evidential decision theory converge on their shared recommendation, explicitly identifying why neither causal pathways nor evidential correlations link the local choice to the remote outcome; (iii) show why updateless decision theory diverges, specifying exactly how it weights logical correlation across executions of the decision algorithm and why it does not perform a Bayesian update on the local action itself; (iv) isolate the single structural feature that makes updateless decision theory peel away, and demonstrate that removing that feature—while holding the payoff structure fixed—collapses the case back into a standard Newcomb problem or into a case where all three theories agree; (v) finish with the honest objection a defender of updateless decision theory would raise against your scenario, explaining whether that objection succeeds in dissolving the divergence. The case must generate its verdicts from the theories rather than stipulating them, and the reasoning should be accessible to a well-informed reader without invoking obscure formalisms.
