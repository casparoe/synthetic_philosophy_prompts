The long-running dispute over semantic externalism—triggered by Putnam's Twin Earth scenario and developed through Burge's social-deference cases—appears structurally frozen: externalists maintain that the content of terms like 'water' or 'arthritis' is partly fixed by environmental or communal relations absent on Twin Earth, while internalists insist that narrow psychological states determine cognitive significance and that first-person authority is incompatible with wide content. The standard objections are well rehearsed—the externalist's claim that twin-earth duplicates lack the relevant mental state, the internalist's counter that reference-fixing descriptions are not part of speaker competence—yet the literature produces few conversions.

Rather than asking for another adjudication, diagnose the stall. Is this disagreement substantive but intractable, verbal (turning on competing uses of 'meaning' for public-language reference, cognitive role, or explanatory psychology), or a case of two camps answering different questions—one metaphysical (the conditions for term-denotation of natural kinds), one epistemic (the conditions for concept grasp and self-knowledge)—under a single heading? Examine what each side actually treats as primary data: do externalists privilege intuitions about twin-earth reference, while internalists privilege intuitions about first-person knowledge? Is there a hidden empirical dispute about actual linguistic-deference patterns in scientific and ordinary language, or a formal question about whether indexical or demonstrative structures can capture the relevant differences?

In particular, consider whether the conflict is obscured by a failure to distinguish 'reference-fixing descriptions' (the community-level conditions that initially anchor a term to a kind) from 'stereotypical descriptions' (the associations individual speakers actually deploy). Do slow-switching scenarios and instantaneous twin-cases require different treatments that the literature collapses? Could a formal model of narrow versus wide computation, or a conceptual distinction between normative answerability to a linguistic community and descriptive psychological state, dissolve part of the disagreement?

Conclude by specifying what progress would look like given your diagnosis: a needed conceptual distinction, an empirical study of deference, a formal result, a reformulation of the question, or a reasoned argument that the debate is ill-posed and should be abandoned.
