If Kant were alive today and confronted with modern cross-cultural pragmatics—specifically the contrast between Dutch low-context directness and Japanese high-context indirectness, in which speakers routinely avoid explicit refusal and rely on shared contextual inference for the listener to decode the intended meaning—how do you think he would assess the moral status of such indirect omissions? Given his well-known refusal to permit lying even to save an innocent life, would he regard a Japanese speaker’s strategic omission of direct negation—where no false proposition is asserted, yet the listener is guided away from the literal truth by interactional framing—as a lie violating the categorical imperative? Or would he distinguish between falsifying propositional content and conventionally managing social face, thereby allowing polite indirectness as compatible with respect for rational agency? In answering, consider whether his distinction between perfect and imperfect duties leaves any room for culturally standard “white lies” told solely to preserve harmony, and explain what this implies about whether direct truthfulness can ever be justifiably softened for politeness.
