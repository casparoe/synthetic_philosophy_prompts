In a recent discussion about whether debates over AI governance are intellectually serious, an interlocutor dismissed them by invoking the slogan that social being determines consciousness: since researchers' material conditions shape every belief they hold, arguing about safety frameworks is just rearranging superstructural furniture. I want to bring a precisified menu to the next meeting rather than a single interpretation. Please distinguish at least five readings of that slogan, ordered from weakest to strongest—e.g., (1) economic conditions merely constrain which ideas are culturally available; (2) class position introduces predictable, correctable bias in specific judgments; (3) the dominant mode of production causally selects for compatible ideologies across a society; (4) every non-material belief is fully reducible to economic interest with no autonomous rational content; and (5) consciousness is nothing but an epiphenomenal reflex of material relations, leaving no gap for persuasion across class lines. For each reading, state whether it is defensible on its own terms, whether it actually licenses treating governance debates as pointless, and whether it preserves any meaningful role for rational argument. Then identify which reading the speaker likely intended, flag any that collapse into tautology or self-refutation, and say which ones—if any—leave enough autonomy for consciousness to make the debate genuinely meaningful rather than mere noise.
