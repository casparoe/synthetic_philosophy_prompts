You are a senior philosopher of rationality and institutional design with working familiarity in decision theory, normative architecture, and the conceptual foundations of artificial agency. Before you answer, take two minutes to brainstorm freely: list every open conceptual tension you can identify at the intersection of updateless decision theory and what I will call normative lock-in architectures — that is, institutional, legal, or computational systems designed to bind future adaptive agents to counterfactual founding commitments regardless of what outcomes they subsequently observe.

Now imagine you are advising a small interdisciplinary research group (three philosophers, two theoretical computer scientists) that has secured three years of funding for purely conceptual work, with no laboratory or empirical ML component. Their guiding concern is whether updateless-style reasoning can be coherently embedded in institutions that must remain responsive to evolving moral and practical circumstances without being able to revise their core normative commitments upon receiving new information.

Your deliverable is not a survey but a defended research agenda. Identify the three to four open questions or lines of work in this space that matter most for this group, rank them in strict priority order, and defend the ordering. For each entry, specify:

1. The precise conceptual knot that remains unresolved — avoid generic statements that UDT is “controversial”; identify the specific ambiguity, paradox, or boundary condition.
2. Its upstream status: which other questions in the agenda depend on resolving this one, and what collapses if it is left open?
3. Its tractability for philosophers without direct engineering access: what form progress would take (e.g., a formal clarification, dissolution of an apparent contradiction, a normative boundary condition), and what would count as failure within a three-year window.
4. The decisive trade-off that positions it relative to its neighbors — for instance, generality versus institutional applicability, philosophical depth versus direct normative payoff, or tractability versus neglect.

In defending the ranking, be explicit about your criteria: importance relative to the group’s ends, tractability for conceptual researchers, neglect in existing literature, dependency structure, and what evidence or argument over the next two years would cause you to reorder the list. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citation of specific editions and page numbers; refer to named positions or arguments only where necessary for clarity, and avoid treating standard Newcomb-style decision puzzles as if they exhaust the domain.

Finally, conclude with a brief residue assessment: which parts of your agenda depend essentially on the “normative lock-in architecture” framing I have injected here, and which would survive intact if that framing turned out to be a conceptual dead end?
