I'm working through my intuitions about metaphysical unity in a seminar that keeps returning to Bradley's regress—specifically, whether relating parts by further relations ever genuinely binds them into one thing, or merely pushes the binding problem back one step. I want to know what principle actually governs my judgments, so I am reporting my gut verdicts on concrete cases. When I consider the proposition that Socrates is wise, I treat it as genuinely one thought, not a heap of Socrates-plus-wisdom-plus-some-link; yet when I imagine the same concepts listed side by side without syntactic structure, I see only an aggregate. A melody strikes me as unified in a way the same notes in scrambled order are not, even though both involve identical acoustic parts and temporal relations. A table composed of atoms arranged in a functional structure feels like one object; take those same atoms, preserve all their pairwise relations, but disperse them across a galaxy, and I say there is no table—only atoms. However, I feel the regress worry applies forcefully to the table's atomic relations, as if each bond needs a further bond to connect it, but not to the proposition's syntax, as though mental or logical unity escapes the regress in a way physical unity does not. A university occupies a middle ground for me: more unified than a random crowd, less so than the melody, and I cannot tell whether that is because of institutional rules or mere familiarity. The case I keep reversing is whether a widely dispersed but causally interdependent ecosystem—say, a fungal network linking trees across continents—counts as one organism or merely a coordinated population. Is there a coherent principle behind all this, perhaps functional integration, non-relational co-instantiation, or something else entirely, that delivers these verdicts? Or must I abandon one, especially my wavering judgment about the dispersed ecosystem? Please state the principle precisely, say what it predicts for a case I have not mentioned—such as whether a distributed computer network running a single program counts as one computer—and be explicit about which of my intuitions it cannot accommodate without contrivance.
