Before you design anything, please think carefully and begin with a brief brainstorm of how a classroom simulation can make students feel, through rules and hidden incentives rather than lecture, that preserving poetic form in translation is not a single binary choice but a redistribution of structural failure across a network of interdependent constraints. I am asking this for a high-school literature seminar I attend — I am seventeen, and we have been discussing Clément Marot’s rondeau form and the problem of translating it without destroying its architecture. I want to push past the usual conversation about rhyme and meter into something more granular: what I am calling refrain-lock debt. In a rondeau, the refrain lines must stay textually identical across stanzas, yet the rhyme sounds framing them depend on the phonology of the language. Lock the refrain to preserve repetition, and the rhyme scheme fractures silently; unlock it to repair rhyme, and the form’s audible repetition collapses. Most discussions treat this as a simple either/or, but I want participants to discover that faithful translation often misallocates failure rather than preserving essence.
