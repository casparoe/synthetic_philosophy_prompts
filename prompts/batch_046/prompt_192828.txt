I'm currently working through some material on confirmation theory for a graduate seminar, and a conversation with a colleague about clinical-trial design left me stuck on what philosophical commitments are actually built into the rules. The practice I have in mind is the institutional protocol—common in pharmaceutical regulation—where a study's primary endpoints must be specified in advance in the trial protocol, and any correlation discovered only after data collection is automatically downgraded to exploratory status, regardless of its statistical strength or the mechanistic story that can be told for it. Meanwhile, a correlation that was pre-registered and then observed receives full confirmatory weight, even if its statistical profile is identical to the post-hoc one.

This looks like it quietly presupposes a distinction between projectible and non-projectible predicates—precisely the kind of distinction Nelson Goodman dramatized with the predicate grue and the new riddle of induction. But I want to know exactly what theory is being assumed. Does the practice commit us to Goodman's entrenchment-based account, according to which pre-registered predicates are projectible simply because they have been linguistically entrenched in the protocol? Or does it presuppose a realist view that some properties are natural kinds and therefore support induction, while post-hoc correlations are gerrymandered? Or is the distinction purely pragmatic—about controlling researcher degrees of freedom—and therefore compatible with an anti-realist or purely probabilistic view of induction?

Reconstruct the implicit philosophy of induction here: say whether the practice strictly requires one of these views, whether it is philosophically underdetermined between them, and whether parts of the regulatory framework presuppose inconsistent things (for instance, does the same system treat mechanistic explanations as capable of rescuing a post-hoc finding in some contexts but not others?). Also, tell me what the practice would have to look like if we replaced its implicit theory with a flat probabilism in which all correlations of equal statistical strength carry equal evidential weight—would pre-registration become redundant, or would it still serve a distinct function? And if you think the practice is best explained without any deep metaphysics of projectibility, show me how that explanation handles the asymmetry without collapsing into the claim that the two correlations are epistemically equivalent.
