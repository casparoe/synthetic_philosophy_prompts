As a retired physicist who has only recently begun studying formal epistemology and anthropic reasoning, I have assembled a set of intuitive judgments about belief updating in self-locating scenarios, but I am unsure whether they cohere. I would like you to help me articulate the underlying principle—if one exists—that captures them, and to tell me which judgment I must surrender for consistency's sake.

Here are my verdicts.

Case 1. In a fair lottery with ten million tickets, I learn only that my ticket is a winner. I should become highly confident that there are very many other winners, not that I am singularly fortunate.

Case 2. In the standard Sleeping Beauty problem—where a subject is awakened once if a fair coin lands heads and twice if it lands tails—I feel that upon awakening, without further information, she ought to assign credence one-third to heads, treating her present awakening as indexical evidence that weights the tails world more heavily.

Case 3. In a scenario I have been calling the Presumptuous Cosmologist: two cosmological models predict either 10³ or 10⁶ habitable planets, with no other differences. I learn that I exist on one such planet, but nothing further. Here I feel it is presumptuous to update strongly toward the larger number; my existence was already guaranteed in either model, so the observation seems evidentially neutral. This appears to conflict with Case 2, where my being awake does shift my credences.

Case 4. A laboratory flips a fair coin. If heads, it creates exactly one conscious simulation with my memories and wakes it. If tails, it creates one thousand identical copies and wakes all of them. I wake with no other information. I think I should lean modestly—though not by the full factor of one thousand—toward there being a thousand copies, because my subjective experience is instantiated more often in that world.

Case 5. Modify Case 4: in the thousand-copy world, each copy faces an independent filter that eliminates 99 percent of them immediately after waking, based on a hidden parameter. I observe that I have survived the filter. I now feel I should revise back toward the one-copy world, since my survival is more surprising if abundance preceded the filter.

Case 6. Consider a universe that either necessarily produces exactly one conscious observer or produces a random Poisson number with mean one. I learn that I am conscious and that there are no other observers I can detect. I find myself uncertain whether this should update me at all.

Please do the following. First, state as precisely as possible a single principle—or a tightly related family—that governs self-locating updates, indicating which clause or mechanism accounts for each of Cases 1 through 5. Second, apply that principle to Case 6 and report the predicted credence or verdict. Third, identify explicitly which one of my five initial verdicts the principle cannot accommodate without revision, and explain whether the revision requires abandoning self-sampling, self-indication, or some intermediate position. I ask that you avoid bibliographic recommendations and instead derive the answer from the logic of the cases themselves.
