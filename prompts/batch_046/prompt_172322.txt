I am presenting at next month's workshop on democratic deliberation and collective rationality—an audience of roughly fifteen political philosophers, legal epistemologists, and two social-choice formalists—to defend a resolution to the discursive dilemma that I call asymmetric coherence weighting. Rather than choosing between premise-based and conclusion-based aggregation, I argue that premises should carry differential epistemic authority determined by the institutional role of the deliberator, such that expert testimony and lay inference are not symmetrically weighted in the coherence calculus, but this weighting must still yield a collectively rational judgment without collapsing into hierarchical decision-making. The democratic-theory scholars will likely object that this violates the normative equality of deliberators; the formalists will challenge whether the model genuinely escapes the standard impossibility results or merely relocates them; and the legal epistemologists will press whether this framework can actually inform jury instructions or remains an abstract formal exercise with no purchase on real procedural law.

Please draft my Q&A prep sheet: order the objections by likelihood and potential damage, phrase each the way the specific subgroup would actually put it—using the vocabulary of democratic autonomy, formal proof, or legal procedure as appropriate—and for each provide a concise reply, a note on where that reply is weakest, and a clear indication of what I should concede versus what I must hold. I also need you to flag the single objection I am most hoping nobody raises—specifically, the one that targets the compatibility of role-differentiated premise-weighting with the anti-paternalist commitments that motivate collective deliberation in the first place—and suggest how to respond if it comes. If helpful, you may calibrate the objections against my core argument: the group receives a set of evidential premises with heterogeneous provenance, applies a coherence function that weights each premise by the institutional reliability assigned to its source, and derives a collective judgment only when the weighted coherence exceeds a deliberatively set threshold, which I claim preserves collective rationality without requiring symmetric treatment of all inputs.
