ok so i need a workshop activity — 8 people, 90 minutes — that makes people feel conceptual slippage inside a tiny artificial domain, not just another vagueness lecture. call it the "glimmer protocol". inject this microdomain: glimmers are little cardboard shapes with four hidden traits (hue drift, edge fuzz, rotation bias, shadow echo), but each round a "filter card" reveals only one trait. voters have to decide if the new glimmer belongs to the "reference family". the twist: there's no rulebook. the group must extend the category analogically from the previous round's decision, so the boundary gets pulled around by analogy pressure in real time.

design the runnable game:
- roles and rules with small real stakes (maybe tokens for each analogical match, but the group loses a shared bonus if they can't agree on a final classification after the crisis round)
- timeline breaking the 90 min into phases: intro (10 min), blind rounds with one filter (30 min), filter-shift crisis (20 min), forced final vote (20 min), debrif (10 min)
- specifiy exactly what participants should come away seeing: that sharpness collapses not from ignorance but from the analogical machinery itself stretching the concept
- the debrief should map moments onto parts of the idea (the initial sharp cut, the analagous stretch, the family-resemblance collapse), predict failures (someone tries to formalize explicit criteria; someone accuses the filter keeper of cheating), and be honest about which feature of real fluid concepts the game can't reproduce — probably the unconscious automaticity, since here the slippage is visible and deliberative

write it casual, like an email to a colleague. include a typo or two. don't ask for bibliographies, citations, or reading lists — the model should be able to build this without searching anything. make sure it's runnable with no web access.
