In a study-group discussion, Maya and Leo debated whether dialetheism is required beyond paraconsistent logic. Maya argued that the liar paradox produces a sentence that is genuinely both true and false, and that only dialetheism can preserve the unrestricted material adequacy of the T-schema; she said paraconsistent systems like LP assign gluts without committing to them as real, which she considers an unstable halfway position. Leo responded that LP's glut is a semantic assignment rather than an ontological claim, and that restricting the T-schema at the boundary is no more ad hoc than restricting naive set comprehension; he added that dialetheism undermines reductio arguments globally and needs an unmotivated epistemic filter—accepting contradictions only when arguments for both sides seem equally strong—to keep the view local. Maya replied that paraconsistency already blocks explosion, so the dialetheist merely makes explicit what paraconsistentists conceal, and that the cost is confined to domains like self-reference rather than spreading everywhere. Leo countered that without a principled logical boundary, admitting one true contradiction licenses them everywhere, and that an epistemic criterion is not part of the logic itself.
