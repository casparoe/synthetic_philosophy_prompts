Read Carlo Collodi's The Adventures of Pinocchio through the lens of original versus derived intentionality—specifically Dennett's minimal two-bitser set against Searle's claim that only biological minds possess original intentionality. Treat the Talking Cricket not merely as a conscience but as a literal two-bitser: an entity with only binary representational states, approval and disapproval, whose corrections impose derived intentional content onto Pinocchio's actions. Does Pinocchio's moral development illustrate Dennett's evolutionary account, in which accumulated external correction produces behavior indistinguishable from original intentionality? Or does the Blue Fairy's final transformation grant original intentionality ex nihilo, in a way that violates Dennett's naturalism and vindicates Searle's insistence that derivation always traces back to an original biological source? Point to the scenes that best dramatize this tension—the lies and nose growth, the Cricket's death and return, the donkey transformation, the final awakening as a real boy—and say where the fairy-tale ontology resists the philosophical framing. What does the work add to the debate that pure argument cannot?
