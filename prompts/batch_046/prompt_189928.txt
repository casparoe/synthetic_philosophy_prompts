I'm in a philosophy of science seminar working on Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes. Our discussion has stalled over how to read the 'hard core': one side takes it as an unfalsifiable metaphysical commitment that scientists defend psychologically, closer to the Popper-influenced reading; the other takes it as a conventional semantic framework—akin to Carnap's idea of linguistic frameworks—that fixes what counts as a legitimate research question within a programme, making it a logical rather than psychological fixture. Both sides appeal to Lakatos's own descriptions of the protective belt and negative heuristics, but the metaphysical camp accuses the semantic camp of ignoring Lakatos's anti-conventionalist rhetoric, while the semantic camp accuses the other of conflating methodological rules with scientists' mental states. I think the stalemate might not be about Lakatos at all, but about whether scientific commitment is primarily a cognitive attitude or a conventional boundary condition—yet I'm not sure if that's a verbal confusion over 'commitment', a substantive disagreement about the ontology of theories, or two distinct questions (historical psychology versus logic of evaluation) being treated as one. Can you diagnose the structure of this disagreement with reasons, not as a literature survey? And given your diagnosis, tell me definitively what would count as progress—would it take a formal reconstruction of anomaly-handling, a historical study of actual programme abandonment, or a conceptual distinction separating the logical and psychological roles of the hard core? I want a committed position, not an even-handed listing of possibilities.
