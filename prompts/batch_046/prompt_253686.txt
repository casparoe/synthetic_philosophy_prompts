Write a deadpan parody of an official "Cultural Integration and Ontological Classification Handbook" issued by a fictional Ministry of Social Harmony, intended for citizens applying for residency in either the "Autonomous Territory" or the "Relational Territory"—a satirical literalisation of the philosophical debate over individualism versus collectivism, frequently mapped onto East Asia versus the West. The handbook should be approximately 650 words, formatted with numbered sections (for example, 1. Defining Your Ontological Status; 2. Membership Obligations; 3. Grievance Procedures for Excessive Autonomy or Excessive Fusion; 4. Taxonomy of Social Roles). Adopt the voice of a dry, over-precise bureaucrat who treats metaphysical positions as immigration categories: applicants must declare whether they are "prior to" or "constituted by" their communities, and penalties apply for undeclared "role drift." The satire should exaggerate genuine features of both positions—the individualist premise of a pre-social self, the collectivist premise of personhood as constituted by hierarchical relations—without inventing strawmen, and should subtly show that bureaucratising either view produces identical absurdities (licensing fees for filial obligations, means-tested atomisation). Do not name any living philosopher or cite specific texts; invent all institutional names. The humour should arise from taking the conceptual commitments literally rather than from invented nonsense. Begin with the handbook text itself, with no preface, commentary, or framing device.
