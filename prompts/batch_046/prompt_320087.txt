Write a short research proposal (about two pages, suitable for a dissertation prospectus) on whether ontological identification can be secured for entities about which we possess neither accessible descriptive content nor a traceable causal chain. Frame the central question through a specific comparison: the identification conditions for an anonymous scribe responsible for a particular manuscript variant—where a physical trace exists but no biographical or intentional access remains—versus those for a theoretical entity posited solely by structural role, with no observational trace at all. State precisely whether you are defending the view that some identifications are genuinely non-descriptive, or only apparently so, and explain why standard reference-fixing debates (causal-historical transmission versus descriptive satisfaction) fail to settle this boundary case. Outline your method: constructing minimal identification conditions that do not depend on epistemic access, testing them against scenarios of total informational inaccessibility, and distinguishing the result from ordinary skeptical worries. Specify what a positive result would look like (ontology genuinely outstripping epistemology in these cases), what a negative result would look like (all identification collapses into whatever evidence is available), and what would count as project failure—e.g., if the distinction between the manuscript case and the theoretical case dissolves upon analysis. Give an honest assessment of scope and risk: is this tractable in one semester, or does it tend to expand into a general theory of reference?
