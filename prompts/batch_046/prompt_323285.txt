Design a 45-minute workshop for twelve philosophy graduate students that forces them to feel the tension of treating sentience as continuous rather than binary. Invent a unanimous-allocation game in which the group divides twenty "protection tokens" among six cases—an adult chimpanzee, a hive of ten thousand bees, a sleeping human with no reportable experience, a synthetic thermal-prediction network, a mid-gestation fetal bat, and a conscious but behaviorally locked-in patient—where every token given to one entity is taken from another, so trade-offs are unavoidable. Include a debrief that maps each predictable dispute onto a theoretical position about moral gradation, names the two group dynamics most likely to derail the exercise, and states frankly which feature of continuous status—the absence of any non-arbitrary cutoff—the rules alone cannot reproduce. Deliver this as exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion, in American English.
