Hey, I'm a backend engineer with no philosophy background, but I've been chewing on the tragedy of the commons. My take: for digital public goods—open source libs, datasets, protocols—the Hardin framing is actualy wrong. Bits don't get depleted; copying is free, so there's no "overgrazing" a repo. The real failure is slow rot: maintainers burn out, dependencies bit-rot, and security patches lag. The commons collapses not because too many users took from it, but because care wasn't sustained. So the problem isn't rational self-interst destroying shared resources; it's an invisible maintenance deficit that looks stable until it suddenly breaks. What would count against this view? I want the strongest counter-evidence—maybe digital commons that collapsed from overuse rather than neglect, or models showing maintenance incentives align naturally—sorted by how decisive vs just awkward they'd be. Also, be honest: has this been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that a problem? Please say what argument or finding would change your mind, and point out where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong.
