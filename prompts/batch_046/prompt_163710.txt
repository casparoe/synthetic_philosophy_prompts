Please compose a deadpan parody—roughly 450 words, in the formal register of an academic job advertisement—for the post of "Director of the Inviolate Level" at a fictional Institute for Tangled Hierarchies. The target is the philosophical claim, familiar from Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach and from the image of Escher's Drawing Hands, that any explanatory system requires a fixed, untouchable substrate (the "inviolate level") that remains immune to the strange loops and feedback relations operating above it. The advertisement should treat this substrate as an actual administrative tier: list qualifications that are logically impossible to satisfy from within the system (e.g., the ability to evaluate one's own evaluative framework without self-reference), describe duties that involve policing an object-language/meta-language boundary that constantly collapses, and include an institutional diversity statement that inadvertently restricts candidacy to entities already outside all describable levels. The satire should derive its effect from accurate features of tangled hierarchies—the indistinction of observer and observed, the absence of an external vantage point, the collapse of strict level-separation—rather than from generic bureaucratic absurdity. Maintain sustained irony without signaling it; do not request bibliographies, citations, or further reading.
