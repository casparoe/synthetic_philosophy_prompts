Please argue that in assessing moral obligations toward insects we should reject both binary sentience ascription and the linear scale argument, adopting instead a "threshold-weighted integration" framework: moral weight attaches only to organisms whose neural architecture supports a minimal form of unified experience, scaled by our epistemic confidence in that attribution, so that vast populations falling clearly below the threshold do not accumulate aggregate significance, while organisms near or above it receive disproportionately stronger consideration. In developing this position, address the objection that privileging integrated neural architectures arbitrarily favors vertebrate-style cognition and risks replicating the same exclusionary patterns that have historically been used to deny moral status to marginalized human and non-human groups.
