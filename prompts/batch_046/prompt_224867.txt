I am a retired analytical chemist who spent thirty years in a national metrology institute, and since retiring I have joined a small philosophy discussion group comprising seven other former scientists with little formal training in epistemology. We meet for ninety minutes every other Thursday, and I have volunteered to lead the next session. Rather than delivering a lecture, I wish you to design a participatory classroom activity—complete with explicit rules, assigned roles, a non-monetary tracking scheme, and a structured debrief—that allows our group of eight to feel the force of both the lottery paradox and the preface paradox through a scenario drawn from laboratory measurement practice.

The framing should be a simulated peer-review panel for a multi-instrument calibration study. Each participant receives a unique "measurement certificate" for one of twenty traceable data points, each carrying a 99.9 per cent confidence interval. You must construct voting rules that require every panellist first to decide individually, according to a fixed epistemic threshold, whether they "know" their assigned point lies within tolerance. They must then cast collective votes on two further propositions: that all twenty points lie within tolerance, and—acting as a preface-style disclaimer—that "owing to inevitable systematic drift, at least one point is erroneous." The aggregation mechanics should be arranged so that each individual decision is rational relative to the threshold, yet the tally reproduces the structural contradiction: they have endorsed every separate claim while being compelled to endorse both the universal conjunction and its negation. They should discover this tension themselves through the counting rules before anyone names the paradoxes. Please provide a precise timeline for the ninety-minute session, explain what will predictably fail on the first run—most likely a participant will attempt to dissolve the tension by arbitrarily raising the confidence threshold, thereby breaking the analogy—and advise how I should handle it. The debrief must map each voting stage onto either the lottery structure or the preface structure, and you must conclude with an honest note on which philosophical feature the laboratory framing cannot reproduce and must be supplied by me verbally. Present your design in British English, without bibliographies, citations, or reading lists, and keep the entire response within 650 words.
