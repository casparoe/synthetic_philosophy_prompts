Consider three formal treatments of common knowledge of rationality in epistemic game theory: Aumann’s information-partition model, the Lewis-Skyrms signaling-convention framework, and a heterogeneous-belief model with bounded-depth reasoning and noisy belief updates. Rank them in tiers—robust, fragile, broken—by how well each sustains iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies when agents are only approximately rational rather than perfectly so. For each tier, state in one sentence the structural feature that determines its placement. Then answer in one sentence whether substituting Monderer and Samet’s notion of common belief for full common knowledge inverts your ranking, leaves it unchanged, or causes it to collapse.
