Du Bois's well-known essay on Negro art contains the claim that all art is propaganda, yet he simultaneously insists that Black artists must reject false stereotypes and reveal the actual conditions of Black life—a demand that seems to require art to be both partisan and accurate. This tension becomes sharper when read alongside his earlier account in The Souls of Black Folk of double consciousness as an epistemic condition: the idea that Black Americans possess a bifocal awareness of themselves both as they are and as they are seen by a dominant white world. Please explain the conceptual issue at stake here: can Du Bois consistently hold that all art is propaganda while also maintaining that Black art has a distinctive, even privileged, capacity for truthful disclosure about American society? In particular, does he need a hidden distinction—perhaps between propaganda as mere advocacy and propaganda as standpoint-mediated revelation—to avoid collapsing his view into either the claim that all art is mere manipulation or the contradictory claim that truthful art is not propaganda at all? Give a committed answer: argue either that his view is coherent once this distinction is drawn, or that it ultimately fails, and defend your judgment against the strongest counter-argument. Do not survey interpretations or list readings; take one line and work it through.
