(2) In the canonical Chinese Room scenario, an agent manipulates formal rules for Chinese characters without understanding Chinese, so the system lacks semantic comprehension. (3) Now consider a distributed architecture: thousands of such rooms, each performing syntactic operations and passing outputs to others, arranged in feedback loops analogous to neural networks or large-scale language models. No individual node possesses understanding, and the global state is still definable entirely in terms of symbol transitions. (4) Therefore, by the same reasoning, the distributed system as a whole lacks understanding, regardless of its scale or functional organization. (6) If we accept (1) through (4), we must deny that any of these distributed entities genuinely understand anything, which seems to overreach and undermine the very distinction between original and derived intentionality that the original argument wants to preserve. So the argument seems to prove that collective or artificial semantics is impossible, which is far stronger than its intended conclusion.
