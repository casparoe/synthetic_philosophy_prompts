I'm trying to nail down what Adam Smith's sympathy actually does, going by my gut on cases. When my roommate stubs a toe I wince with him — direct sympthy. When I hear about a famine thousands of miles away I feel almost nothing, but my impartal spectator tells me I should care equally. When a friend betrays me I can imagine her stress and feel some sympathy, yet I still blame her — the spectator doesn't erase the judgment. When a rival gets humiliated I feel a secret satisfaction and the spectator only makes me hide it, not eliminate it. And the odd case: imagining myself as a dictator facing brutal choices lets me understand his reasoning without feeling any sympathy for him, like sympathy and understanding split apart. Is there one principle here — maybe sympathy tracks imaginative proximity while the spectator applies universal rules — or are these separate mechanisms? Which verdict has to go? And what should it say about feeling strong sympathy for an animal suffering while the spectator insists moral concern stops at human agents?
