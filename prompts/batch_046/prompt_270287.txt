(3) Consent is necessary but not always sufficient for moral permissibility: in contexts of severe structural power asymmetry, socially constituted sexual norms can shape preferences so that explicit agreement does not guarantee absence of harm.

(4) Despite (3), the state should not criminalize any consensual sexual act, including those involving age or economic asymmetry, provided there is informed explicit agreement and no direct coercion.

(5) Bentham's own refusal to publish his defenses of same-sex relations and pederasty reveals a practical acknowledgment of social costs that his explicit calculus ignored; I take this as grounds for an incrementalist, conservative constraint on the pace of sexual legal reform, even when rapid change would maximize expected utility.

(6) I reject evolutionary debunking of sexual norms, because evolutionary origins are compatible with the utility of pair-bonding without validating every evolved preference, and because the social constitution of consent in (3) makes origin stories largely irrelevant to normative evaluation.
