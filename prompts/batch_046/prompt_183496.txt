I am a senior compliance analyst at a national revenue authority, and my unit repeatedly reviews proposed multi-generational wealth structures—family offices, dynasty trusts, and philanthropic holding vehicles—to determine whether they violate our statutory mandate for intergenerational opportunity neutrality. By that mandate I mean: no mechanism may systematically convert parental economic advantage into a child’s access to political influence, educational gatekeeping, or labor-market signaling that is unavailable to peers with equivalent native ability but less inherited capital. I need a reusable ninety-minute audit procedure that my team can apply to any proposed structure without requiring specialized legal history knowledge. The procedure should list its steps in execution order; specify what documentary evidence counts as sufficient at each stage; mark every point where quantitative or normative judgment must substitute for a formal rule; and flag exactly where the principle is silent so the file must be escalated to the policy board rather than decided by the auditor. It must also state explicitly what classes of harm the procedure is structurally blind to and therefore cannot adjudicate. Apply the procedure to three proposed structures: first, a dynasty trust that pays tuition and living expenses for descendants at a specific set of elite universities but prohibits direct cash distributions to beneficiaries; second, a family limited partnership that holds voting shares in regional media enterprises and distributes only non-voting economic interests to younger generations; and third, a charitable foundation that requires descendants to serve on its board in exchange for administrative salaries that exceed market rates for comparable nonprofit governance roles. For each application, identify where the procedure produces a clear determination, where it forces an uneliminated judgment call, and—crucially—present one scenario in which the procedure returns an approval but the underlying principle of opportunity neutrality should likely reject it, explaining the specific failure mode. Keep the vocabulary accessible to an auditor with economics and law training but no background in moral philosophy, and do not rely on citations to specific philosophers or legal cases.
