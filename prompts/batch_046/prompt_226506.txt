I'm on the review board for our institutes long-range modeling archive. We have to decide whether to keep funding maintanance of the Nordheim Resource-Depletion Framework — a 1974 systems model — in our active repository, or relegate it to historical curiosity. The model predicted sharp agricultural collapse by 2000-2010 based on soil-nutrient depletion curves; that didnt happen, mostly because synthetic fertilizer scaling and crop-genetics shifts the model never included. Now a climate-adaptation group wants it restored to active theoretical status — they claim its not falsified, just right but early, and that rescue conditions (delayed fertilizer-price spikes, soil-carbon feedback loops) are aligning so the 2040-2060 window looks like the original prediction displaced by thirty years.

My co-chair wants to reject this outright: if a theory misses by three decades and needs ad-hoc rescue conditions, thats falsification with extra steps. I think there's a real philosophical issue about whether long-run predictions can be evaluated the same way as short-run ones, but Im not sure how to formalize it for a vote.

Include criteria reviewers could actually apply, treat my co-chairs worry seriously, and specify what evidence the researchers need to show the claim is credible rather than retrospective storytelling.
