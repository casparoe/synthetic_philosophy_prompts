I am developing a conceptual taxonomy for philosophy of mind that deals with substrates and their symbolic overlays, and I keep returning to a specific thought experiment involving what I call the careenium and simmballs. The careenium is a hypothetical substrate—imagine a medium with non-classical connectivity patterns that can stabilize discrete, recombinable structures—while simmballs are the symbol-bearing configurations that arise within it. The puzzle is whether these simmballs, precisely in virtue of carrying representational content, exert downward causal pressure on the careenium's dynamics, or whether every change in the substrate is fully determined by its own microphysical laws, leaving the symbolic level as an interpretive gloss rather than a causal driver.

Please argue for the position that symbols do not push the substrate around: defend the claim that within the careenium, simmballs are causally inert with respect to the physical evolution of the substrate, despite being indispensable for high-level explanation. Your argument should engage with the distinction between predictive correlation and genuine causal efficacy, address the intuition that stable symbol-substrate pairings suggest downward causation, and clarify whether rejecting this downward causation commits one to eliminativism about the symbolic level or merely to non-reductive description. Please separate what is settled—such as the principle of causal closure in physicalism—from what remains genuinely contested among specialists in philosophy of computation and cognitive science, and do not present any contested view, including your defended position, as if it were the consensus.
