Within the careenium–simmball framework, careenium denotes a continuous, non-symbolic substrate underlying cognitive dynamics, whereas simmballs are self-organizing clusters of syntactic relations that emerge from careenium patterns without being decomposable into discrete substrate units. Proponents hold that reconfiguring a simmball's internal operators—for instance, altering its relational syntax—can induce corresponding reorganizations in the careenium field, an effect characterized as symbols pushing the substrate around. Yet because simmballs are explicitly not composed of careenium and thus lie outside any part-whole hierarchy of the substrate, any causal influence they exert upon careenium appears to be an external, non-physical intervention. Given this, how can the framework preserve the causal closure of the physical domain without either reducing simmballs to careenium constituents or embracing interactionist dualism?
