I encountered the phrase "there are no skyhooks" being deployed in a seminar on evolutionary explanations of moral cognition. One participant used it to reject appeals to irreducible normative properties; another invoked it to defend a strongly reductionist programme in which cultural practices are treated as crane-built scaffolds for genetic replication, dismissing objections about emergent dynamics as concealed skyhookery. Separate the precise theses that could be packed into that single sentence: which are tautologies about explanatory structure, which are substantive ontological commitments regarding dependence relations, which conflate methodological reduction with greedy reductionism, and which are false if applied to genuinely emergent phenomena. Present the readings as a menu rather than selecting one, indicate which are platitudes, which are defensible but contentious, and which are errors, and specify which interpretation is actually doing the argumentative work when the slogan is used to insist that multi-level selection accounts of altruism remain strictly "crane-only".
