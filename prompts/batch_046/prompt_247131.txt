hey, i'm a philosphy phd student working on mental content and i think i built a reductio that i can't get out of. here's the arg:

(1) putnam-style semantic externalism is correct: Oscar and Twin Oscar can be molecule for molecule identical, but Oscar's "water" thoughts are about H2O while Twin Oscar's are about XYZ.
(2) the same reasoning applies to what i'll call "thin institutional concepts" - things like democratic consent or property rights. if earth and twin earth have slightly different social practices grounding these concepts, then two internal duplicates could be thinking about earth-democracy versus twin-democracy.
(3) intrspection only reveals the internal qualitative character of a mental state, not the external social relations that fix its institutional content.
(4) so if i'm sitting in my office thinking about democratic legitimacy, i can't tell by intrspection alone whether my concept is anchored to earth-style liberal practices or to twin-earth's varient.
(5) but if i can't distinguish the two contents from the inside, then i lack first-person authority over which specific institutional belief i'm holding.
(6) still, it seems obvious that i do have such authority: i can sincerely report "i think consent requires majority rule" without first investigating whether my university runs on earth-norms or twin-earth norms.
(7) so either externalism is false for institutional/thin-social content, or we lack first-person authority over huge parts of our mental lives.
(8) the second option is absurd - it would mean i'm systematically wrong about what i'm thinking whenever social practices differ.

this proves too much. i want to keep (1) and (6) if i can. which step should i reject, and what does giving it up cost? is the problem in (3), or is there a hidden slide between not knowing the environment and not knowing the content?
