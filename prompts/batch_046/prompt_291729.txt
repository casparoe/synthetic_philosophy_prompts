I've been retired from bench biology for a few years and lately I'm digging into philosphy books about Dennett's cranes and skyhooks, plus the greedy reductionism debate. Here's my take, probably too bold: greedy reductionism is the only honest way to build cranes. If a biological or mental phenomenon can't be broken into smaller mechanistic or evolutionary steps, then it's not an unfinished crane — it's a skyhook wearing a lab coat. I apply this to evolutionary transitions in individuality (like single cells becoming multicellular) and even conscious reportability: either there's a stepwise path, or we're smuggling in top-down causation. But I'm not sure I'm being fair. What would actually count against this view? Not the usual "science can't explain consciousness yet" handwave. I want the live threats — downward causation in multilevel selection, niche construction loops that function top-down despite looking bottom-up, formal results showing irreducible dynamics in evolutionary transitions — that show greedy reductionism fails without invoking anything mystical. Rank them from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones defenders of my view would concede vs. dismiss. And be honest: has my position already been qualified into unfalsifiability, where every gap just becomes "we need more cranes"? Is that insulation a problem for a view like mine?
