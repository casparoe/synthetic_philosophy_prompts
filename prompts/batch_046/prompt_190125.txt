After a long and exhausting argument with a close friend about meat consumption and wild-animal suffering, I have settled on a specific comparative claim that I want to examine honestly. I hold that, when the full indirect costs are included, beef produced on perennial pasture is morally preferable to chicken raised on grain—not because of direct slaughter numbers, where chicken is obviously lower per calorie, but because of the wild-animal suffering displaced by crop cultivation. Poultry depends on intensive monoculture (corn, soy) whose planting, harvesting, pest control, and storage kill enormous numbers of rodents, birds, and insects; pasture-based beef, by contrast, uses land often unsuited to crops and avoids that feed-crop death toll. I further assume that the sentient biomass supported per unit of edible energy is lower in well-managed grassland than in grain fields, and that the suffering experienced by the wild animals present is, on average, less intense or less morally weighty. So my position is that the correct metric is not animals killed per plate but total wild-animal suffering induced per calorie, and that beef wins on that metric.

What would genuinely count against this view? I want more than the usual objections about methane or land-use efficiency. I want to know which empirical findings—about actual field-mortality rates, about hidden grain supplements in beef production, about invertebrate sentience densities in grasslands versus croplands—would be fatal rather than merely awkward. I also want the conceptual pressure points: if sentience turns out to be binary rather than graded, does my density argument collapse? If invertebrate suffering counts fully and grasslands are teeming with insects, does the calculus flip? And what about the possibility that my view has been qualified into unfalsifiability—say, by retreating to "only regenerative grazing in specific biomes"—and whether that insulation is a theoretical virtue or a vice for a claim of this kind?

Please distinguish the defeaters that a committed defender of this position should concede as genuinely damaging from those they can reasonably dismiss, rank them from fatal to merely inconvenient, and answer in American English.
