I serve on an interagency scientific review board that evaluates proposals for large-scale environmental interventions—stratospheric aerosol injection pilots, gene-drive releases in island ecosystems, deep-sea mineral extraction trials—receiving roughly four serious submissions annually, each with incomplete risk assessments. We have thirty days per proposal, no authority to commission new primary research, and must issue a signed recommendation that explicitly separates factual judgments from value judgments. I want a reusable decision procedure grounded in the precautionary principle, but operationalized through a distinction I am trying to formalize: between Type-I precaution, where uncertainty concerns outcomes within a stable action-space, and Type-II precaution, where the intervention may restructure the very categories—ecological baselines, climate feedback loops, or harm metrics—by which outcomes are assessed, so that post-intervention monitoring may be measuring the wrong things. The procedure should present ordered questions with specified sufficiency conditions for answers, indicate where it terminates with a conditional versus unconditional rejection, and mark precisely where judgment is irreducibly a matter of values. It must also apply a reversibility-horizon test: not merely technical undoability, but whether the epistemic conditions required to recognize that undoing is warranted will survive the intervention itself.

Please construct this as a step-by-step protocol the board could actually run, identifying the hard cases it must accommodate—such as a proposal with severe Type-II uncertainty that addresses an imminent ecological collapse, or one with technical reversibility but degraded monitoring capacity after deployment. Then apply it to two proposals: first, a pilot release of gene-drive mosquitoes to suppress malaria vectors across a small archipelago, where ecological interaction spaces are poorly mapped but disease burden is severe and localized; second, a stratospheric sulfur-injection scheme with a built-in termination mechanism, where climate models converge on the radiative mechanism but diverge radically on regional precipitation effects. For each, show where the procedure yields a clear recommendation, where it compels an explicit value call, and where it breaks down—particularly if Type-II precaution recommends inaction even when inaction risks catastrophe. Be explicit about what the procedure cannot resolve and where it will systematically misfire.
