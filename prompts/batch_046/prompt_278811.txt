Exam question (25 points):
Daniel Dennett distinguishes four evolutionary "kinds of minds": Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian. For each, identify the selection mechanism shaping behavior and the cognitive architecture it presupposes. Then evaluate the following proposal. Researchers observe that juvenile corvids first acquire basic motor patterns through individual trial-and-error, and only later observe conspecifics manufacturing tools. A colleague proposes calling such systems "Quintilian" creatures—defined not merely as Gregorian users of cultural artifacts, but as minds for whom external symbolic storage is constitutive of hypothesis-generation, so that removing the artifacts collapses the capacity for internal modeling. Is this "Quintilian" category genuinely distinct from Dennett's four, or does it collapse into one? Be precise about which feature of the taxonomy does the work, and about whether the corvid case actually exhibits the proposed feature.

Student answer:
Darwinian creatures are shaped by natural selection over evolutionary time; they possess hard-wired dispositions and no within-lifetime learning. Skinnerian creatures add operant conditioning, generating behavioral variety that the environment selects by reinforcement or punishment, but without representing outcomes in advance. Popperian creatures generate internal representations—hypotheses that can be tested in imagination—allowing bad ideas to die instead of the creature. This requires propositionally structured internal representations; non-propositional mental maps, such as simple spatial navigation, therefore fall short of full Popperian status. Gregorian creatures are Popperian minds enhanced by cultural tools, especially language, which let them generate, store, and share hypotheses externally. Crucially, Gregorian cognition retains an autonomous Popperian core that uses tools instrumentally.

In the corvid case, the initial trial-and-error phase is Skinnerian, since the birds learn through environmental feedback without prior internal modeling. The subsequent observation of tool-manufacturing conspecifics introduces a Gregorian element: the juveniles leverage an external store of successful strategies. The "Quintilian" proposal claims that here the artifacts are not merely instrumental but constitutive—without them, the birds lack any internal capacity for hypothesis generation about tools. I think this collapses into Gregorian. Dennett already describes Gregorian minds as those whose cognitive capacities are constitutively transformed by cultural tools like language; if language can restructure thought, then artifact-dependent cognition is just Gregorian cognition under another name. Therefore the proposed category is not genuinely distinct, because it misreads the Gregorian stage as requiring an intact, standalone Popperian base when in fact Dennett allows that base itself to be transformed and distributed. One might reply that Dennett's taxonomy is only an evolutionary progression, not an exhaustive ontology, but within the logic of the four kinds the Quintilian distinction adds nothing new.
