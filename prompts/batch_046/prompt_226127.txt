I'm a high school senior writing an independent study on philosophy of art, and I've become stuck on what I am calling the recursive paradox in films that personify mental states. Specifically, I want to read Pixar's Inside Out through the classic philosophy-of-fiction problem—how we can genuinely feel sorrow or joy for characters we know do not exist—but with a twist that I think makes the standard solutions work harder than usual. In Inside Out, we are not merely asked to care about Riley, a fictional adolescent; we are asked to care about Joy and Sadness, who are literal personifications of emotional processes occurring inside her mind. This creates a layered or nested fictional commitment: our pity for Sadness seems directed at a representation of an emotional mechanism, which is itself embedded in a fiction about a real-world teenager.

I would like a philosophical reading that takes both the film and the paradox seriously. How do the classic responses—Radford's claim that emotional responses to fiction are genuinely inconsistent with our beliefs, Walton's pretense theory of make-believe, and the thought-theory approach—handle this double-layered case? Does the fact that Joy and Sadness are not just people but representations of affective states change whether our tears count as genuine emotions or as something more like meta-emotions? I want the analysis to stay faithful to the film's narrative structure, especially the scene where Joy realizes she must let Sadness take control of Riley's core memories and the moment where Bing Bong fades away, without reducing those scenes to mere illustrations of philosophical positions. At the same time, I do not want the philosophy distorted to fit the plot: if Inside Out actually undermines one of the standard solutions rather than confirming it, the reading should say so explicitly.

Finally, I am curious whether this kind of nested fictionality adds something to the philosophical debate that arguments about novels or plays miss—perhaps something about how we conceptualize our own emotional lives through fiction. Please do not ask me to consult specific papers or provide a bibliography; I need an argument built directly from the concepts and the film itself.
