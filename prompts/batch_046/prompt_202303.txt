I had a lengthy and rather heated discussion last night with a friend who works in philosophy of science about how the reference class problem ought to be resolved in actual predictive practice. She has rendered explicit verdicts across several domains, and I want to test whether they are governed by a single coherent principle or by an unstable cluster of intuitions. She maintains that when predicting whether a particular coin will land heads, one should employ the reference class defined by its physical manufacturing specifications rather than the class of all coins one has personally flipped; she insists that for a medical prognosis regarding a specific patient, the correct class is the demographic cohort refined by that patient’s biomarker profile rather than the broad population baseline; she argues that in forecasting jury nullification in a given trial, one must rely on the jurisdiction-specific historical record rather than national aggregates; and she consistently rejects any appeal to purely subjective Bayesian priors that are not anchored in an observed frequency class.

Now apply her framework to the following case that came up in our argument: an engineer must predict the failure rate of a specific quantum error-correction module. This module belongs to a novel architecture with only twelve existing prototypes worldwide, yet its substrate materials and thermal dynamics closely mirror those of a well-characterized classical semiconductor family. If my friend wishes to remain consistent with her earlier track record, which reference class should she endorse—the sparse class of identical prototypes, the intermediate class of quantum modules with analogous logical structures but different substrates, or the broad class of classical semiconductor analogs? Which of her prior verdicts serves as the closest analog, and if her principles genuinely underdetermine the choice, what precise commitment—perhaps regarding the relative weight of structural similarity against sample size—would she be adopting with each option? Finally, specify the kind of further case, say involving a biological system with sparse observational data but clear mechanistic parallels to a well-studied model organism, that would finally distinguish these competing interpretations of her view.
