A colleague recently circulated a summary of Davidson's doctrine of radical interpretation that runs as follows. The interpreter begins with no prior grasp of a speaker's language or mental states and must construct a theory of meaning entirely from observable verbal behavior and its environmental correlates. To make this possible, the interpreter applies the principle of charity, which requires presupposing that the speaker shares the interpreter's own core beliefs about the world and reasons according to the same canons of logic. By maximizing agreement in this way, the interpreter derives a translation manual that assigns meanings to the speaker's utterances. The summary then asserts that this procedure demonstrates the impossibility of genuinely alien conceptual schemes, since any belief-system sufficiently different from the interpreter's own would violate charity and therefore remain uninterpretable. Finally, it concludes that Davidson's account is ultimately a form of behaviorist verificationism: the content of a speaker's thoughts is fixed entirely by what an external observer can confirm about the speaker's dispositions to assent or dissent under observable conditions.

Something in this account feels compressed—particularly the slide from methodological charity to psychological sameness, and from interpretive constraints to verificationist semantics—but I am unsure where the compression becomes distortion. Please audit the summary: which claims are faithful to Davidson's actual arguments, which are common pedagogical simplifications, and which are misrepresentations? Specifically, address whether the principle of charity genuinely requires belief-identity rather than rational coherence, whether the argument against scheme-content dualism is correctly derived from charity alone, and whether the verificationist conclusion has any basis in Davidson's rejection of the myth of the given or in his triangulation thesis. Where the summary is wrong, indicate the least charitable but still plausible correction; and state whether the summary's final conclusion survives once the errors are repaired.
