(1) If two things have all the exact same parts arranged the exact same way at the same time, they are identicle — there's not a hidden "extra" layer.
(2) The statue David and the marble block it's carved from share every atom in the same spatial relations for the whole time David exists.
(3) So at any moment David exists, David is just that marble block arranged statue-wise.
(4) If two things are identicle at every moment they both exist, then they're identicle period (not just "for now").
(5) So David just IS that marble block.
(6) But that marble was sitting in a quarry for millenia before Michelangelo touched it; David definately wasn't.
(7) So David and the marble have different histories, which means by (4) they can't be the same thing. Contradicts (5).
(8) The only escape is saying there's a "statue-form" ghost layered on top of the marble, like a class instance on top of raw bytes. But then my laptop is also a ghost-layer on top of silicon and plastic that existed in raw form for eons.
(9) So either every artifact is a ghost-twin of its matter, or one of the steps above is broken.

where's the slip? is it (4) treating temporal identity wrong? is (1) smuggling in something about "kinds" that doesnt apply across categories? or is the real cost that we have to accept there really are two things in the same place? if i have to choose i'd rather save (1) and bite the bullet on ghost-twins, but i want to know what i'm actually paying.
