For a philosophy-of-causation reading group I’ve drafted my own position for others to stress-test. My commitments are these: (1) I accept Al-Ghazālī’s occasionalist claim that no created substance possesses inherent causal efficacy; God alone is the direct cause of every particular occurrence in the natural order. (2) I also accept a dispositional analysis of properties for mental and moral domains—dispositions need not rest on categorical bases—because I think this undermines the usual objection that occasionalism leaves us with an implausibly sparse ontology; I apply this selectively, not to physical causation. (3) I defend human moral responsibility through the Ash’arite doctrine of acquisition (kasb), treating voluntary actions as genuinely attributable to agents even though God directly causes them. (4) I reconstruct scientific law not as Humean regularity but as descriptions of God’s consistent habits that support counterfactuals in virtue of divine commitment to consistency, not natural necessity. (5) I deny any ontological difference between miracle and ordinary event—both are equally direct divine actions—while allowing that miracles carry special epistemic weight because they fall outside the predictive habits that structure rational expectation.
