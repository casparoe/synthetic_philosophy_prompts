My aim is to give the group a rigorous conceptual framework they can later use to imagine non-human or artificial minds in narrative, but I want the taxonomy understood on its own terms before it becomes a writing tool. Plan the session’s explanatory arc: state the foundational idea that must be in place before the four categories can even be introduced; choose the single carrying example that should run through the entire explanation—something concrete that illustrates the hierarchical dependency of each level on the prior one—and explain why a familiar example such as a chess-playing computer fails to carry the distinction; map the misconceptions this audience will predictably form, especially the belief that a Gregorian creature is merely a more complex Darwinian one or that Popperian minds are essentially defined by the capacity to suffer, and specify the exact point in the sequence where each misconception should be intercepted; design one final check for understanding that reveals whether the group has grasped the dependency structure among the four kinds rather than simply memorized the labels; and identify clearly what element of Dennett’s theory should be left out at this level, with an explicit acknowledgment of that omission. In your response, separate what is settled within Dennett’s framework from what is genuinely contested among philosophers of mind, and do not present a contested interpretation as the consensus.
