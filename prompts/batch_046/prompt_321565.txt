The quantum layer generates objective chances of exactly 0.5 for each measurement branch according to the Born rule. The artificial system has full access to the global quantum state and can compute these branch probabilities. At the same time, it has direct, transparent access to its own internal algorithm, which selects outputs using a pseudorandom number generator whose seed is physically fixed by deterministic dynamics in the classical substrate, though that seed is epistemically opaque to observers outside the system. Given this architecture, I want your own credence—expressed as a specific number between 0 and 1—that the system ought to defer its internal forecast to the objective quantum chance of 0.5 rather than to the deterministic output of its own algorithm. More importantly, decompose the reasoning into four parts: (a) whether the Principal Principle applies with full force to artificial agents as opposed to human believers; (b) whether the system's knowledge of its own deterministic implementation counts as inadmissible evidence that blocks deference to the quantum-level chances; (c) whether being physically embedded in a deterministic substrate undermines the normative authority of the higher-level quantum probabilities; and (d) whether self-locating information about which specific branch or deterministic trajectory the system actually occupies breaks the symmetry the principle requires. Assign rough probabilities to each of these four sub-questions, explain how they combine to yield your final number, identify which sub-question your credence is most sensitive to, and describe what kind of argument, formal result, or conceptual distinction would most move your number up or down.
