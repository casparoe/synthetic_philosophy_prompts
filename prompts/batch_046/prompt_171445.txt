I'm trying to get my thoughts on peer disagreement straight before I draft something. Here's where I stand:
(a) conciliationism is right for genuine peers — if we're equally reliable in a domain, we should split the credence diffrence.
(b) peerhood shouldn't be judged by whether we share reasoning access; it should be fixed by domain-specific calabration metrics like Brier scores or forecasting track records.
(c) but if I can actually pin down the specific mistake in my peer's reasoning, I think steadfastness is okay and doesn't violate (a).
(d) I reject the uniqueness thesis — rational belief can be permissive, so there's not always one right credence.
(e) I want to apply (a) to moral/political disagreement too, not just science.
(f) at the same time, in moral domains those calabration metrics don't apply, so true moral peers are basically nonexistent, which makes steadfastness the default there.

Do these actually hang together? I feel tension between (b) and (c) — if peerhood is objective by metrics, why does my subjective diagnosis of error matter? And (d) feels like it might undercut (a), though maybe that's only apparent. Also (e) plus (f) looks like I'm endorsing conciliationism for a domain where I just said it never applies. Rank the real tensions by severity, tell me what's only surface-level, and what's the least costly revision if I want consistency.
