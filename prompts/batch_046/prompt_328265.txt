A mate of mine who teaches aesthetics part-time summarised the so-called paradox of fiction for me like this: the puzzle is that we have genuine emotional responses to characters and events we know aren't existant, which looks irrational because emotions are usually directed at actual states of affairs. From there he jumped to saying this can't be right because people genuinely weep at sad novels, and therefore the real answer must be that fiction hijacks our empathy circuits by temporarily switching off the belief-checking module in the brain — basically an evolutionary glitch that lets us respond as if the story were real.

I'm not convinced his conclusion follows from knocking down Walton. Is this a fair account of the paradox and of Walton's position? Which bits are accurate, which are a bit of a caricature, and does the 'hijacked empathy circuit' idea actually solve the philosophical problem, or is it just replacing one mystery with neuroscience jargon?
