We must circulate a recommendation before Thursday's meeting on the long-term disposition of ancestral human remains acquired in the 1960s from a Southern African community whose ethical framework draws on Ubuntu. The community representatives do not seek conventional repatriation to soil alone; they propose joint custodianship in a memorial space where the remains are treated not as museum specimens but as an ancestor whose personhood continues through sustained relational obligations—what they describe as being-with-the-dead—rather than through biological vitality. Our legal counsel frames this as a property and custody matter resolvable by standard repatriation protocols, while several curators warn that accommodating a relational ontology of post-mortem personhood would commit the institution to a metaphysical stance incompatible with scientific neutrality.

Please draft the short written opinion I will present: recommend a concrete course of action with a clear time frame—whether full repatriation, joint custodianship under defined conditions, or retention with modified display protocols—and treat the community's position with philosophical seriousness rather than reducing it to cultural preference or legal formality. Specifically, examine whether an institution can implement practical policies that honour Ubuntu-style relational personhood without fully endorsing its metaphysical claims, and present honestly the strongest argument for the option you reject, whether that concerns preservation access, institutional consistency, or the dangers of ontological overreach. Write in British English, begin with your bottom-line recommendation in the first sentence, then provide the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of the question.
