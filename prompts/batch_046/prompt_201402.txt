For an upper-level epistemology seminar (12 students, 75 minutes), design a simulation that makes participants feel logical omniscience as a lived tension rather than an abstract flaw in modal epistemic logic. Small groups should act as belief-holding agents with a fixed budget of inference tokens and hidden entailment chains of varying length connecting premise cards to distant conclusions. The governing rules must impose formal closure—agents automatically know every logical consequence of their beliefs regardless of token expenditure—while groups can only defend or score conclusions they have actually traced. Include a phase where acquiring one new belief card suddenly generates a cluster of untraced entailments, so students experience the explosion of omniscience. Provide rules, materials, a scoring scheme, a timeline, and a debrief that maps token expenditure onto epistemic labor and hidden chain length onto implicit versus explicit knowledge; also note what will predictably go wrong in the first run and how to correct it. Be explicit about which feature of logical omniscience—such as logical ignorance of entailment relations themselves—the simulation cannot reproduce and must be supplied in discussion.
