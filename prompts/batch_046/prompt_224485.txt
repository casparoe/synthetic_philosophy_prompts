Provide an example of a conceptual issue arising outside mathematics in which Benacerraf's epistemological dilemma applies—specifically, a domain in which we appear to have reliable knowledge of entities that stand in no causal relation to us, but in which adopting a combination view (merging a causal theory of knowledge for ordinary physical objects with a non-causal epistemology for these entities) fails not merely because of causal isolation, but because the two frameworks impose incompatible conditions of justification or explanatory coherence. Describe the domain precisely, explain how it reproduces the structural tension Benacerraf identified between our epistemic practices and the ontological status of the objects in question, specify exactly where the combination view breaks down, and then identify one significant point at which the analogy to mathematical knowledge fails.
