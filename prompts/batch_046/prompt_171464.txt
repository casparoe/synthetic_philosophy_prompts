I have become convinced that our growing reliance on AI systems for conceptual and philosophical reasoning produces not merely an instrumental shift but a structural erosion of understanding—specifically, a collapse of the distinction between knowing that a claim is well-supported and grasping why it must be so, where the latter requires a first-personal, reconstructible path through the space of reasons. In philosophical domains especially—ethics, metaphysics, philosophy of mind—the damage is not that we might adopt false beliefs, but that we come to hold true beliefs without the kind of cognitive ownership that distinguishes knowledge from borrowed authority. I am considering framing this as a thesis for a graduate seminar on epistemic agency, but before committing to it I want to pressure-test it with real rigor.

What would genuinely count against this view? I am not asking for reassurance that humans still verify outputs, or for generic optimism about human-AI collaboration. Rather, I want the arguments, analogies, and conceptual distinctions that should move me: Does the history of delegation—to calculators in mathematics, to GPS in spatial navigation, to statistical packages in empirical science—show that first-personal grasp is preserved in parallel rather than displaced? Could one argue that philosophical understanding was always already distributed cognition, so that AI mediation extends an existing social structure rather than eroding an individual capacity? Or is there a result, perhaps from the philosophy of explanation or from cognitive science, showing that the "why" I take to be lost was always an illusion—perhaps a post-hoc rationalization of pattern-matching that large models now perform more transparently?

Finally, I am concerned that the view risks insulation into unfalsifiability: if every retained human insight is dismissed as residual intuition and every failure of audit is taken as proof of erosion. Is that qualification a fatal flaw for a claim with empirical leanings, or is it appropriate for a conceptual thesis about the conditions of understanding? Please rank the potential defeaters from decisive to merely awkward, and indicate specifically where defenders of this pessimistic position should themselves concede vulnerability rather than retreat into qualification.
