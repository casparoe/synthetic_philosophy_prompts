Im on a contract archaeology crew that does rapid site assessments for development permits — we usually get three days in a trench and then have to file a preliminary interpretation with the heritage board saying whether we've found evidence of an actual institutional practice (ritual authority, trade network, kinship structure, whatever) or just domestic activity. The board doesn't care about our theories, they want a repeatable procedure my team can run on any assemblage without me standing over them.

The principle I want turned into steps is this: material culture isn't just a mirror reflecting beliefs, it actively holds institutions together, so you can't infer an institution purely by matching objects to ethnographic analogies. You have to run two checks in parallel — analogical fit (does the pattern match known institutional assemblages?) and what I'll call structural friction (patterned contradictions within the assemblage that don't fit random noise, like tools that should be shared being treated as restricted, or absence of expected support objects). Absence counts as evidence too, not just presence. When the two checks conflict, that's the judgment point.

Write me a decision procedure: ordered questions, what counts as sufficient answer at each, where it stops with a clear "no institution detectable," where it stops with "hand to senior — values/judgment call," and where it flags false-positive risk. Keep it practical for a crew with basic training and tight deadlines. Don't drop the analogical-fit vs. structural-friction distinction, but avoid heavy jargon.

Then apply it to two cases: (1) a non-urban site with standardized weights and broken seals scattered around, but zero storage architecture or administrative debris; and (2) a burial cluster with mixed-age bodies, imported beads, but absolutely no differentiation in grave goods or spatial grouping. One should come out "probably not an institution" even though it looks promising at first glance.

Finally, say what the procedure will consistently get wrong — what's the false positive it can't filter out?
