I am a doctoral student in political philosophy specialising in environmental ethics, and I am compiling materials for a graduate seminar on intergenerational justice. For this purpose, I should like you to compose a satirical parody — approximately 600 to 700 words, in deadpan bureaucratic prose — modelled on a formal UK civil-service job advertisement for the post of 'Intergenerational Climate Ombudsperson' within a fictional Ministry of Temporal Equity. The document ought to follow the conventional structure of such postings: a role summary, essential and desirable criteria, key duties, terms of remuneration, and an equal-opportunities statement. Its target is not any individual but the conceptual practice of treating unborn citizens as present rights-bearers in climate policy; the satire should exaggerate genuine features of that practice rather than inventing straw positions.

The humour must arise from internal consistency: please require the appointee to perform 'continuity-of-identity audits' for claimants who do not yet exist, to apply a precisely calibrated 'pure time preference' coefficient to moral grievances, and to chair a 'Non-Identity Review Board' that rules whether a given emissions pathway harms a specific future person or merely substitutes one possible person for another. By framing the non-identity problem, the democratic deficit of proxy representation for the unborn, and the asymmetry between present costs and speculative future benefits as routine administrative protocols — complete with impossible qualifications and contractual small print — you should allow these theoretical tensions to surface implicitly rather than stating them as objections. A reader familiar with the debate ought to recognise that the exaggeration is fair and that the piece understands what it satirises; a non-specialist should still find the bureaucratic absurdity legible. Please do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or citations. Finally, I invite direct pushback: if you judge that the request rests on a confused premise — for instance, if the task requires you to take a substantive stance on discounting, or if it cannot be executed without misrepresenting the philosophical terrain — you should say so plainly rather than complying politely.
