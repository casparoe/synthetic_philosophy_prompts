I have been examining whether Lewis's triviality result for indicative conditionals can be circumvented in a multi-agent setting in which conditional credences are pooled geometrically with a nesting-sensitive discount factor, so that the collective assignment satisfies Adams's thesis for each contributor but violates it globally. It seems to me that, because the resulting global function is not the belief state of any single rational agent, the premises of Lewis's argument—particularly the requirement that unconditional probabilities obey the standard axioms—fail to apply, allowing pooled indicative conditionals to carry probabilities genuinely distinct from those of the corresponding material conditionals. Is this a legitimate escape route, or does the contradiction arise at a level that aggregation cannot dissolve?
