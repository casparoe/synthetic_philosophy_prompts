I am currently studying feminist moral philosophy, with particular attention to care ethics and its institutional applications. For a seminar exercise, I should like you to compose a satirical administrative memorandum — approximately 500 words, in deadpan bureaucratic prose — announcing that our university has adopted a comprehensive Care Ethics Governance Framework. The satire ought to exaggerate genuine features of the view: the privileging of relational context over universal principles, the asymmetry of care relations, the claim that moral attention requires emotional responsiveness rather than rule-following, and the critique of justice-oriented frameworks as overly abstract. To prevent the parody from becoming generic, please build it around the specific conceptual distinction between caring about (a distant, bureaucratic mode of concern) and caring for (a direct, responsive, context-sensitive engagement), and show the administration attempting to formalise the latter — for example, through mandatory empathic-response forms, proximity-weighted grievance procedures, or relational-audit checklists for staff. The humour should arise from the internal contradiction of bureaucratising a theory that explicitly resists bureaucratisation, but the tone must remain entirely straight-faced: there should be no explicit signalling that the proposal is absurd. Please write in British English.
