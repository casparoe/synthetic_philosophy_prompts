Having retired after thirty-five years in experimental particle physics, I have recently begun auditing graduate seminars in philosophy of mind and ethics. I am assisting with grading for a course on consciousness and physical reality, and I would like your evaluation of the following exam response.

The prompt posed to the student was: "Assume panpsychism is correct in holding that fundamental physical entities—perhaps quantum fields or elementary particles—possess minimal phenomenal properties rather than being purely physical. Does this entail that interventions altering these entities (for instance, high-energy particle collisions or induced quantum decoherence) carry ethical weight? Address in particular (a) whether the combination problem prevents inference from micro-sentience to macro-suffering; (b) whether an 'ethics of coherence preservation' can be defended without committing the fallacy of division; and (c) the relevance, if any, of distinguishing phenomenal consciousness from access consciousness."

The student submitted the following answer:

"Panpsychism holds that consciousness is a fundamental feature of all physical reality, which implies that elementary particles possess inner experiences structurally analogous to human consciousness. From this it follows that experiments such as high-energy collisions, which destroy or alter particles, are ethically comparable to harming sentient beings. The standard objection is the combination problem—how micro-subjects combine to form macro-subjects—but this is resolved by quantum entanglement: when particles become entangled, their phenomenal fields fuse into a unified macro-consciousness, so that induced decoherence does not merely alter physical states but annihilates a single unified subject. This argument avoids the fallacy of division because it does not attribute macro-properties to parts; rather, it recognizes that physical correlation produces an emergent phenomenal whole, as any physicalist combination theory requires. Moreover, ethical relevance attaches only to phenomenal consciousness, since access consciousness depends on cognitive architecture that particles necessarily lack; therefore, the absence of reportability in fundamental entities is ethically irrelevant. Consequently, a principle of coherence preservation is defensible: we ought to minimize unnecessary decoherence because it causes suffering to unified field-consciousnesses."

Please assign a letter grade (A through F) and provide point-by-point comments that justify each element of the evaluation. I ask for a granular assessment rather than a general impression: specifically, identify any conflations between panpsychism and stronger claims about macro-consciousness, examine whether the appeal to entanglement genuinely addresses the combination problem or merely re-describes physical correlation, and evaluate whether the ethical inference follows validly from the premises offered.
