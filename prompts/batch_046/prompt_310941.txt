Construct a branching taxonomy (not a list of named scholars) for a philosophy-of-social-science seminar on whether cultural values can serve as independent causal explanations of large-scale institutional change. Use Weber's thesis linking Protestant asceticism to rationalized capitalism as the focal case, but frame it as one instance of the broader problem: explaining institutional outcomes with values rather than with material conditions, legal structures, or individual economic interests.

The taxonomy should be built from diagnostic questions that a respondent can answer without already knowing the philosophical labels. Each branch should split on a concrete claim or counterfactual case. Include seven to nine leaves, and map each leaf onto an actual position (named or unnamed). Note any unoccupied combinations in the logical space and briefly diagnose why they are empty—incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Flag any pair of leaves that your tree cannot cleanly separate.

The tree should include these branch points, in roughly this order:

1. Are values ever causally independent of the material interests and institutional incentives of the groups that hold them?
   - If no: split between (a) values as predictive symptoms of hidden material structures, and (b) values as pure post-hoc rationalization with no predictive utility. (Leaf names: Materialist symptom-view; Strong epiphenomenalism. Note the awkward commitment of the latter: it must deny that merchants with ascetic values behaved differently from materially similar merchants without them.)

2. If yes (values are independent): Is the causal path direct psychological mediation—values reshape individual dispositions toward labor, saving, and risk—or indirect structural filtering—values change which institutional forms survive, spread, or gain legitimacy?
   - Direct path: Proceed to question 3.
   - Indirect path: Proceed to question 4.

3. (Direct path only) Is the required psychological mechanism specific to the value-content—e.g., a this-worldly ascetic orientation that uniquely rationalizes continuous reinvestment—or generic—any value-system that lowers time preference or raises future orientation?
   - Specific: Weberian elective-affinity view. (Awkward commitment: must account for cases in East Asian merchant communities where rational accounting spread without Protestant asceticism, either by restricting the claim geographically or by arguing that the surface similarity masks a different psychological mechanism.)
   - Generic: Generic cultural-causal view. (Awkward commitment: must explain why capitalism failed to emerge in other societies with similarly low time preference, without retreating to an unstated materialist supplement.)

4. (Indirect path only) Can values alone generate an institutional outcome, or do they only filter outcomes that material and legal conditions have already made possible?
   - Alone: Strong selection-view. (Note whether this space is occupied; if empty, suggest whether it is incoherent or merely unexplored.)
   - Filter only: Weak filter-view or selective affinity. (Awkward commitment: must concede that capitalism was materially possible in societies with incompatible values, but was selected out by them, without being able to observe the counterfactual selection mechanism directly.)

5. (Cross-cutting, applies to all non-reductionist leaves) Consider the counterfactual: identical technology, markets, and inheritance law, but the absence of the relevant value-system. Would rationalized capitalism still have emerged?
   - No emergence at all: Strong necessity.
   - Emergence later or in altered form: Weak facilitation.
   - Essentially the same emergence: Values are inessential. (Flag whether your tree allows this as a separate leaf, or whether it should collapse back into the reductionist branch.)

6. (Niche distinction) Does the value-system precede and shape the economic institution, or does the institution pull compatible values into existence through prestige-biased transmission and institutional reward—so that values are real and predictive, but are selected and amplified by the economic system rather than generating it?
   - Value-to-institution: Standard causal claim.
   - Institution-to-value with endogenous attraction: The economic system creates the conditions for the values to spread. (Leaf: Endogenous-attractor view. Note that this may be empirically indistinguishable from the weak filter-view in some historical cases; flag this separation failure explicitly.)

Requirements for the deliverable:
- Phrase every branch node as a question or concrete case that someone could answer without knowing the label it leads to.
- Build the tree from the logical space first: present the branching questions and the answer-combinations they allow, then locate the named and unnamed positions in that space.
- Specify the audience (graduate students in philosophy of social science) and the size (seven to nine leaves).
- Do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations.
- Do not include verbatim quotations; paraphrase any reference to Weber or comparative historical cases without quotation marks.
