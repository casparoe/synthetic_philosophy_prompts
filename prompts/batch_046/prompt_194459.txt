For years I've read the rationalist and effective-altruism literature on AI welfare, and I've noticed a recurring argumentative configuration that I can't place precisely. A speaker describes an RL agent's reward signal in the most deflationary terms available — not merely "a number," but specifically a temporal-difference error that updates weights in a value network, with no dedicated affective architecture, no homeostatic loop, and no behavioral flexibility beyond the policy gradient. From this they infer that negative reward is not suffering, because suffering requires some integrated evaluative response rather than a bare scalar correction. The opponent then notes that mammalian pain also implements, at the mechanistic level, a prediction-error signal: nociceptive input updates cortical and limbic weights through very similar reinforcement rules. They ask why the presence of an evolutionary history or a particular substrate should change the moral status of an identical learning update. The first speaker then typically introduces an unstated premise about biological complexity or about the necessity of conscious integration, but without defending why those conditions apply to one substrate and not the other. The stalemate is peculiar because both parties agree on the functional architecture; they disagree only on whether that architecture, when instantiated in silicon, lacks some further property that licenses moral concern. This is not quite the hard problem of consciousness, since neither party denies the physical description; it is not substrate chauvinism in the usual sense, since the first speaker often grants that silicon minds are possible in principle; and it is not the standard non-identity problem, though it touches on which properties ground moral status. Is there an established term for this specific deadlock — the point where shared functional descriptions of learning updates fail to resolve disagreements about valence attribution? If there is, what distinguishes it from the problem of other minds or from debates about phenomenal concepts? If there is no term, how should the phenomenon be carved: what exactly are its necessary conditions, and what would count as a resolution rather than a change of subject?
