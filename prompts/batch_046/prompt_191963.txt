I coordinate participatory decisions for a regional water authority that must allocate drought restrictions across municipalities and user groups. I have been thinking about the democratic boundary problem — who actually belongs to the demos entitled to a say — and I need it turned into a practical screening procedure my committee can apply repeatedly without a philosopher in the room. Design a step-by-step decision flow: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to move forward, where the procedure returns a clear exclusion, and where it stops and demands a value vote because the issue is not factual. The procedure must handle three hard cases I actually face: recreational users who live upstream but draw no drinking water; the not-yet-born descendants of current residents who will inherit the depleted aquifer; and a major employer incorporated in another state but employing half our region. Then apply the procedure to all three cases, including at least one where it produces an answer I will likely reject, and state explicitly what the procedure cannot resolve. Keep the response under 400 words, and begin with the bottom line: when the affected-interests principle and the stakeholder-identity principle conflict, which one should govern?
