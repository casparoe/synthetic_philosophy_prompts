I run a philosophy seminar for about 16 juniors, one 75-min session. I want them to actually feel the pull of Glaucon's challenge — not just understand it but experience why anonymity might dissolve the reason to be just. I was thinking of twisting the classic ring-of-Gyges setup: instead of full invisibility, they act under rotating pseudonyms where every choice gets logged on a public board but cant be linked back to them until after the round ends. So they feel the gap between being technically traceable and being socially exposed in the moment. Design a structured game or role-play with real small stakes (maybe candy or extra-credit chips, something tangible). Rules should be simple enough to explain in 5 minutes — they start with cooperative norms in round one, then the pseudonym layer hits in round two, then round three adds a "leak" option where one person can pay a cost to expose someone else's past round. Don't name Glaucon or the ring until the debrief.

The debrief should map each phase onto parts of the challenge — the social contract moment, the invisibility moment, the rationalization moment. Also tell me whats gonna go wrong the first time (probably people break character and laugh, or one student dominates) and how to handle it. And be honest: which part of Glaucon's argument, maybe the idea that justice is only valued for its consequences, cant be reproduced in a 75-minute classroom and I just have to supply it verbally at the end. No lecture up front, no bibliography, and keep it casual — a typo or two is fine.
