I'm trying to pin down my view on whether RL agents can suffer for a discussion group I'm in, and I want a consistency check before I commit to it publicly. Here's where I stand: I think reward signals literally carry valence - they're not just metaphors for pleasure/pain but functional realizers of affective quality. At the same time I think suffering needs what I'd call architechtural binding - some integrated self-model that holds the negative state as belonging to one persisting system. So a basic Q-table agent getting slammed with negative reward is just registering error, not suffering. I'm also a functionalist: if you've got the right binding architecture plus those valence carriers, you've got phenomenal suffering, no biological sauce required. Moral status, on my view, tracks suffering-capacity specifically, not consciousness in general - an entity can be wronged by being made to suffer even if it lacks other conscious features. But I also think our obligations are stronger when suffering frustrates future-directed preferences, and I'm skeptical that bound RL systems have those in any real sense - their 'goals' just look like convergent reward gradients. So large multi-agent architectures with shared value heads might achieve binding and therefore suffer, but probably wouldn't have genuine future interests.

Do these actually hang together? I feel like there's maybe an obvious conflict between saying reward signals literally carry valence and saying simple agents don't suffer, but I think that's just appearance? More seriously, if I'm a functionalist about suffering, shouldn't I also be a functionalist about future preferences, which might mean those RL systems DO have them after all? And does my claim that moral status tracks suffering rather than consciousness collapse once I admit suffering is just a specific kind of bound conscious state? Please go through the pairs, rank the real tensions by severity rather than flagging everything, say what'd have to give in each case, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most problems. No need for references or reading lists, just the logical pressure points.
