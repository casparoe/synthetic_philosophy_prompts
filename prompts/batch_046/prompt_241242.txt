I'm presenting in our moral philosophy seminar next week on Scanlon's contractualism and the aggregation problem. My thesis is that the individualist restriction is standardly read as an absolute prohibition on aggregating harms across persons, but this misinterprets the logic of reasonable rejectability: the restriction actually operates as a diminishing-returns weighting on structurally identical repeated burdens, which allows limited aggregation for catastrophic risks without collapsing into consequentialist summation. The audience includes advanced graduate students, one faculty member who is a committed Scanlonian convinced that any aggregation violates the separateness of persons, and another who is a rule-utilitarian convinced that contractualism covertly relies on aggregate welfare. Give me the objections they will actually raise, in the order they are likely to come, each phrased as that camp would phrase it, with what I should concede, what I should hold, and where my reply is weakest. I especially need to know the one objection about pandemic triage that the Scanlonian is likely to press and that I should prepare to defend rather than hope she skips it.
