Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between neo-Aristotelian virtue ethicists and strong situationist critics in moral psychology. The deliverable should be a single constructive document both camps could sign, with residual disagreement made exact rather than obscured.

Structure the statement in four parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims—no more than eight—covering at least: the empirical pattern that cross-situational behavioral correlations in moral domains are often modest; that aggregation across contexts can mask local regularities; the conceptual distinction between behavioral consistency, judgmental consistency, and affective consistency; the point that virtue ethics requires some form of stability but not necessarily situation-independent behavior; the recognition that “character trait” can be defined at multiple resolutions (life-narrative, domain-specific, or local situation-sensitive pattern); the shared rejection of the straw-man that virtue ethics predicts perfect uniformity across all contexts; the agreement that adequate explanation of moral behavior requires both internal dispositions and external situational features; and the acknowledgment that standard empirical studies rarely distinguish consistency at the moral-episode level from coherence at the life-story level.

Second, a disagreements table with columns: Crux; Virtue Ethicist formulation (worded so that camp accepts it as their view); Situationist formulation (worded so that camp accepts it as theirs); whether the dispute is partly verbal and why; and an agreed evidential or argumentative test that would bear on it, with each side’s predicted outcome recorded in advance. The table should address at least: whether modest correlation coefficients falsify global trait ascription or merely reveal high situational sensitivity that is itself structured like a trait; whether virtue requires cross-situational behavioral consistency or only consistency in practical reasoning and emotional calibration; whether the proper unit of moral assessment is the whole life, the social role, or the single episode; and the “calibration problem”—whether a trait remains explanatorily useful if its behavioral expression requires precise situational calibration to manifest, and whether such calibration undermines the trait’s reality.

Third, identify which traditional disagreements dissolve once “character” is disambiguated between global behavioral uniformity and structured situation-sensitive response, explaining precisely why the rewording resolves the verbal conflict without settling the substantive crux.

Fourth, for each remaining substantive crux, specify what both sides agree would actually move the debate—for example, a longitudinal study tracking both observable moral behavior and internal reasoning processes across varied episodes within the same subjects, with both camps stating in advance whether those results should be read as revealing hidden global traits or as confirming local situational control.

Do not include bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for specific citations. Do not invoke named theorists in ways that depend on precise textual knowledge. Write in a formal, polished register, injecting conceptual distinctions—such as the resolution-level problem for traits and the calibration problem—so that the task requires reasoning rather than retrieval of standard formulations.
