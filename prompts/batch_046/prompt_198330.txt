I am a final-year undergraduate studying comparative political ethics. For a practical module I am designing an audit framework, and I need a reusable decision procedure that an ethics compliance officer at an international development agency could actually run when assessing whether a particular exchange of gifts or favours in a non-Western bureaucratic setting should be classified as corruption. The procedure must be grounded in the conceptual distinctions between clientelism, reciprocal gift-giving, and corruption proper, and it must explicitly address whether Western standards of procedural impartiality are universally binding or represent merely one normative framework among others.

Please construct a step-by-step checklist: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each stage, the precise point at which the procedure terminates with a negative determination, and the exact juncture at which the issue becomes one of values rather than facts and must be escalated to a senior ethics board rather than resolved by the officer. The procedure should handle hard cases—exchanges that appear corrupt from a proceduralist standpoint but are embedded in legitimate local reciprocity norms, and exchanges that appear culturally acceptable but functionally erode institutional accountability. Apply it to three worked examples: (i) a senior official who accepts an expensive ceremonial gift from a contractor shortly after awarding a tender; (ii) a local administrator who distributes emergency food aid preferentially to members of her kinship network during a drought; and (iii) a scenario in which the procedure produces a conclusion that directly conflicts with the agency's default Western anti-corruption protocol. Please also flag explicitly every stage at which subjective judgement cannot be eliminated, and state clearly what questions lie beyond the procedure's capacity to resolve.
