Compose a Petrarchan sonnet—octave in enclosed rhyme (abba abba), sestet in an open interwoven scheme such as cde cde, with a clear volta falling between lines eight and nine—spoken in the first person by Mary immediately after leaving her monochrome chamber. In the octave, catalog the physical and functional descriptions of color vision she possessed in confinement (wavelength reflectance, opponent-process dynamics, cortical activation patterns) using deliberately abstract, quantitative, or third-person diction; in the sestet, turn to her direct encounter with a crimson object, shifting to sensory, embodied language. The formal turn should do philosophical work: the enclosed rhymes of the octave should suggest the self-sufficiency of the physicalist account, while the opening of the rhyme scheme in the sestet should enact the apparent gap between propositional knowledge and phenomenal acquaintance. The poem should be precise enough that a reader versed in the knowledge argument can recognize the phenomenal-concept strategy lurking behind the volta—that is, the claim that Mary acquires only a new mode of presentation rather than a new fact—without naming that strategy explicitly. End with a couplet that holds the physicalist reply and the anti-physicalist intuition in unresolved tension. Do not cite authors, papers, or provide a bibliography; the philosophical labor should be carried entirely by the poem's structure and diction.
