I've been working through Plato's question about why knowledge is worth more than true belief if both guide you correctly—say, to the right bus stop. I think the answer must be that knowledge is true belief that has been "tethered" to reality through a feedback loop, like a thermostat that keeps correcting its predictions, whereas a lucky guess has no such loop. That explains the stability. But then I consider AI systems trained with reinforcement learning: they also have feedback loops that tether predictions to outcomes, yet people still say they don't "know" anything. So doesn't this show that Plato's distinction was never really about objective stability or loops at all, but about whether the tether is experienced from inside a conscious mind? In other words, is the extra value of knowledge just a psychological comfort we feel when we can trace the loop ourselves, and Plato was describing that subjective security rather than an actual difference in how belief relates to the world?
