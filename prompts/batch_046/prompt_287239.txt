Defenders of the paradox of tolerance often compare an open society to an immune system: just as the body must identify and neutralize foreign pathogens or perish, a tolerant society must recognize and suppress intolerant voices or lose its own openness. Audit this analogy carefully. First, lay out the correspondence in continuous prose — what plays the role of immune cells, of antigens, of the self/non-self boundary, and of the biological phenomenon actually named tolerance, which in immunology means restraining attack against the self rather than attacking outsiders. Then identify the breaks: does intolerance carry an objective biological signature like a pathogen, or is labeling a view intolerant itself the contested political act? Would suppressing it risk an autoimmune error in which society destroys its own capacity for dissent? And does the conclusion that unlimited tolerance is necessarily self-defeating survive once the reversal of the term tolerance and the other disanalogies are made explicit? Write entirely in continuous prose, without headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if composing a careful letter.
