I just came away from a long, exhausting argument with a friend over whether Athenian direct democracy collapsed under the weight of its own epistemic arrogance, or whether it was structurally incapable of binding itself against reversal. We kept circling back to the same three episodes—the Mytilene debate and its reversal in 427 BCE, the Sicilian Expedition decision and its catastrophic aftermath in 415–413 BCE, and the trial of Socrates in 399 BCE—and realized we were using identical facts to defend opposite conclusions about democratic rationality.

So I want to try a different framing. Consider a distinction rarely applied to classical Athens: between democratic procedures that function as signal-aggregators, where the assembly’s legitimacy derives from pooling dispersed private information to reach a correct collective judgment, and democratic procedures that function as irreversibility devices, where the assembly’s value lies not in being right but in making a decision that cannot easily be undone, thereby solving collective-action problems through credible commitment. Apply this distinction rigorously to all three episodes.

For each case, specify exactly where in the procedural sequence—initial debate, first vote, reversal or execution, subsequent punishment or regret—the failure occurs. Is it that the wrong signals were pooled, that good signals were suppressed by rhetorical capture, or that the assembly’s very capacity for rapid reversal destroyed the credibility needed for long-term strategic commitments? Be concrete about the actors: Cleon’s initial motion and its reversal at Mytilene; Nicias’s warnings and Alcibiades’s influence in the Sicilian vote; the roles of Meletus, Anytus, and the democratic court’s composition in Socrates’s trial. Then tell me whether the three episodes share a single failure mode, and what that shared pattern does to the distinction itself—does it confirm that Athens lacked both capacities, or does it reveal that the two capacities are actually in tension in ways the framework misses?

No bibliographies, no references to modern scholars—just the conceptual work. And if the framework has nothing illuminating to say about one of these episodes, say so plainly rather than forcing a fit.
