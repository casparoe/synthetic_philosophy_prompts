I serve on the doctoral examination accommodations board of a research university's college of arts and sciences. A philosophy PhD candidate has petitioned for permission to wear an approved, non-invasive neuro-feedback headband during her upcoming oral defense. The device streams anonymized, real-time estimates of the examiners' collective cognitive-load patterns—derived, with informed consent, from sensors worn by the panel—and delivers them to the candidate through subtle tonal shifts in her earpiece. She characterizes this as a purely informational accommodation, analogous to visual aids for hearing-impaired students, and argues that detecting moments of examiner confusion will allow her to clarify arguments rather than over-explain them. The board is divided. Two members contend that granting the request exposes the candidate to a structural hazard: our procedural rules automatically require any candidate who receives an external data stream classified as adversarial dynamics feedback to invoke a mandatory forty-eight-hour cooling-off period and reschedule the defense, a safeguard intended to prevent reactive escalation. They argue that, because the candidate cannot unilaterally disable this protocol in the moment of receipt, acquiring the information may lower her expected evaluative outcome relative to remaining uninformed—precisely the risk analyzed in formal treatments of the value of information. Two opposing members maintain that personal autonomy over one's informational environment should prevail, and that the safeguard can be contractually waived in advance if the candidate explicitly opts in. Please draft the board's formal decision memo, addressed to the faculty senate and the candidate. The memo should recommend a concrete course of action—approve, reject, or approve with specified conditions—include a clear implementation or review time frame, and present reasoning that treats the autonomy-based objection with full seriousness rather than dismissing it. Write the document in a form that both parties could respond to with concrete objections.
