Write a taxonomy tree—six to eight leaves, each a named or unnamed position—sorting views on pure time preference and the discounting of future welfare, designed as a diagnostic instrument for a philosophy seminar. Build it from the logical space of answers rather than from a list of labels: every internal node should be a concrete case or normative claim that a respondent can answer without knowing which theory it corresponds to, and only after the branches are fixed should you map them onto positions.

Root the tree in this intertemporal case: a society can prevent a catastrophic harm to people living 300 years hence by accepting a modest, certain burden today; is it permissible, or even required, to refuse that trade? From there branch by the following splits, in this order.

First node: Is there any rational basis for caring less about welfare solely because it occurs later, holding constant who experiences it and how much? (Answer yes/no.) If no, proceed to the zero-pure-rate egalitarian leaf. If yes, continue.

Second node: Does the correct discount function take a constant exponential form across all time horizons, a hyperbolic form that produces present-bias in near-term choices but flattens over centuries, or a rate that declines as the time horizon extends (e.g., for climate-scale futures)? Phrase the diagnostic question concretely: "Would you prefer $100 now over $110 in one year, but also prefer $110 in one year over $100 in two years, and does that reversal depend on proximity to the present?" Use the answers to separate constant exponential, hyperbolic, and declining-rate branches.

Third node: Apply a metaphysical distinction—drawn from reductionist accounts of personal identity over time—about whether the future self is numerically the same person in the sense that grounds special concern. Does the weakening of psychological connectedness over centuries undermine the rationality of present-biased concern, justify it, or leave it untouched? Phrase this without invoking proper names: ask whether differential concern for one's own distant future requires the kind of direct psychological continuity that is absent over long intervals. This should split the present-biased rationalizer (who accepts bias as justified by future-self otherness) from the hyperbolic discounter who treats bias as a brute psychological fact, and should also test whether constant exponential discounters can consistently combine their view with a metaphysics of future-self otherness.

Fourth node: In intergenerational or ecological contexts—such as climate mitigation benefiting strangers centuries hence—should the social discount rate mirror personal time preference, or does the absence of direct psychological connection make the social rate either stricter or more permissive? This separates positions that apply personal discounting uniformly from those that bifurcate personal and social rates.

Leaves must include at least: (1) constant exponential discounter with pure rate greater than zero; (2) hyperbolic present-biased discounter; (3) zero-pure-rate egalitarian (Ramsey-style rejection of pure discounting); (4) declining-rate pragmatist for long-horizon social choice; (5) present-biased rationalizer who grounds bias in the metaphysical otherness of distant future selves; and (6) one unoccupied combination—explicitly named "unnamed"—with a one-sentence diagnosis of why the logical space is empty there (e.g., incoherence between hyperbolic near-term reversal and strict zero pure rate, or unattractiveness of constant exponential plus full metaphysical otherness).

For every leaf, state in one sentence its most awkward commitment—what it must deny or accept that sounds counterintuitive.

Also flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely cannot separate with these nodes alone, explaining the overlap (for instance, whether constant exponential and hyperbolic positions can be distinguished solely by long-run social-choice behavior without near-term choice data).

Keep all position descriptions implicit in the branch logic; do not ask for bibliographies, reading lists, or citations, and do not depend on knowledge of obscure technical literature. The prompt should be self-contained enough that a model without web access can construct the full tree from philosophical reasoning alone, using only widely familiar conceptual distinctions. Write in formal, polished prose.
