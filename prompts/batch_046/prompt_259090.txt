I'm moderating a colloquium interview with an unnamed philosopher who has developed a distinctive, somewhat technical reading of Lewis Carroll's dialogue between the Tortoise and Achilles. Rather than treating the exchange as a familiar illustration of an infinite regress of premises, she argues that the Tortoise's refusal to accept modus ponens reveals a necessary, non-propositional inferential stance—a practical, norm-governed commitment to apply rules that cannot itself be captured as an explicit premise without either begging the question or launching an endless hierarchy. She insists this is not Quinean holism confined to logic, but rather a local, benign circularity specific to the act of reasoning itself, and she extends the point to contemporary artificial systems, claiming that a computational model which applies inference rules without such a stance is simulating deduction rather than genuinely performing it. I want eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate this framework: at least one should probe the boundary between an inferential stance and a merely tacit psychological belief; one should ask why the Tortoise targets modus ponens specifically rather than treating all logical rules as equally vulnerable to the same refusal; one should address her claim about artificial reasoning and whether a system lacking consciousness or intentionality could nonetheless possess the stance; and one should explore pedagogical contexts in which students are explicitly taught inference rules before they appear to have any prior commitment to them. For each main question, please include a brief follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give—particularly her tendency to deflect normative objections by saying this is only a description of cognitive habit, not a justification of logic. Please begin your response with the questions themselves, with no preface or commentary whatsoever; after presenting them, state briefly how confident you are in your framing of her position and single out the specific point about which you are least certain—likely whether her distinction from Quinean holism survives pressure or whether her account of machine reasoning relies on an unstated theory of intentionality.
