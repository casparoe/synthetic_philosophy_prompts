After a long argument with a friend about French school policy, I am stuck on an argument he pressed that seems to show laïcité either collapses into arbitrariness or proves far too much. Here are the steps exactly as he laid them out.

(1) The core justification for banning conspicuous religious symbols in public schools is state neutrality: the republic must not visibly endorse any comprehensive doctrine.

(2) In shared institutional space, persistent visible display does not merely express a view; it normalizes it, and normalization is a form of state-endorsed legitimation.

(3) Religious garments—hijab, kippah, large cross—are persistently visible and therefore normalize the doctrines they express.

(4) Secular comprehensive doctrines—radical environmentalism, militant secular humanism, progressive gender commitments—are expressed through equally persistent visible markers: badges, slogans on clothing, standardized pronoun displays, climate-strike ribbons.

(5) If neutrality requires suppressing visible normalization, consistency demands suppressing all such secular markers too, not only religious ones.

(6) Suppressing every visible marker of belief or identity is impossible—clothing, language, architecture, and social ritual inevitably encode values—and attempting it produces the very total visual uniformity that pluralism rejects.

(7) Therefore laïcité either arbitrarily targets religion, violating equality, or it proves too much by requiring an oppressive, impossible blankness.

(8) So Anglo-American multiculturalism, which accommodates visible difference rather than suppressing it, is the only coherent framework.

I want to keep (1) and reject (8), but I cannot locate the slip. Is there an equivocation in (2) between physical presence and institutional endorsement? Does (4) overreach by treating elective secular expressions as structurally identical to ascriptive religious practices? Or is the error in (6)’s slide from “total suppression is impossible” to “therefore no targeted suppression is justified”? Tell me which step is weakest, why it looked plausible, and what defending it costs. If the argument is genuinely valid and I must choose, I would rather abandon (2) than (1), but I want the price tag either way.
