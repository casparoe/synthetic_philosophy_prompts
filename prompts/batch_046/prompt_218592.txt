For a philosophy of epistemology seminar, write the full specification of what a theory addressing the epistemic regress problem must be able to deliver, but center the list around a framing that standard textbooks usually treat only in passing: the theory must accommodate what I will call weighted circular support, in which belief A lends partial, non-identical support back to belief B and vice versa, neither being epistemically prior, yet the pair avoids vicious circularity only because the support weights are asymmetric and context-sensitive rather than uniform. List the desiderata with the motivating case or structural consideration behind each: the phenomena it must accommodate (linear termination, mutual support without viciousness, infinite chains without arbitrariness, and this weighted circular case); the distinctions it must preserve (epistemic dependence versus causal dependence, justification versus explanation, and having justification versus having knowledge); the consistency conditions with established results (for instance, whether non-inferential justification is necessary, whether coherence requires global consistency or allows local clusters, and whether an infinite chain can avoid arbitrariness without a foundation—flag which of these are genuinely settled among specialists and which remain contested); and which subset of these requirements, if any, is jointly unsatisfiable given standard structural arguments. Mark clearly which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are camp-specific preferences (foundationalist, coherentist, infinitist). Then, briefly, score the three main positions against the list, but do not present any contested interpretation of a particular philosopher’s view as the consensus view; keep the weight on the criteria and their boundary conditions rather than on the scoring. Finally, identify the single desideratum that does the most work in excluding candidate theories, and say whether satisfying it forces a commitment to one of the three standard positions or leaves room for a hybrid.
