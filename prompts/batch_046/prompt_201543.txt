I'm preparing a seminar discussion on whether linguistic relativity retains explanatory force in the age of artificial cognition, and I want to anchor the analysis in Benjamin Lee Whorf's own conceptual framework rather than in textbook summaries. Drawing on his well-known examinations of Hopi temporal expressions and his broader claim that grammatical categories impose habitual fashions of thought—particularly about object permanence, linear temporality, and transitive causation—consider how he might evaluate two simultaneous developments. First, the current dominance of large language models trained on corpora that are overwhelmingly Indo-European, especially English, with its rigid noun-verb distinction and its syntactic encoding of agency through subject-predicate structures. Second, the recent design of an auxiliary reasoning language named Meridian, built for sustained human-AI collaboration, which eliminates all nominal categories for physical entities and instead represents every referent as a process carrying an inherent directional vector, possessing no grammatical equivalent to static objecthood and expressing causal relations only through mutual modulation rather than agent-patient asymmetry.

How would Whorf likely assess whether these models inherit and amplify an ontology embedded in English grammar? Would he predict that deep fluency in Meridian could restructure conceptual schemes regarding permanence and causation, or would he treat an engineered language as insufficiently inhabited to produce the kind of cognitive restructuring he linked to natural-language acquisition? In answering, distinguish precisely between a deterministic interpretation of the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis and the more moderate influence thesis usually attributed to him, and reflect on whether his background in chemical engineering and fire-insurance risk assessment might lead him to advocate a particular institutional remedy against what he might regard as the epistemic risks of grammatical monoculture in globally deployed AI systems.
