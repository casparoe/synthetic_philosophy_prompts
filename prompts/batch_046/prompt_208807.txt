ok so i've been trying to map out my stance on paternalism and nudging for a seminar and want to check if i'm tying myself in knots. here's roughly where i stand (probably with a typo or two):

1. I'm anti-paternalist in the classic Mill-style sense: competent adults should be free to mess up their own lives, and the state shouldn't restrict choices for their own good.
2. I think nudges (default rules, framming, etc.) aren't coercive, so they escape the standard anti-paternalist objection.
3. At the same time, i think white lies told 'for your own good' are wrong, not just impolite - they treat the recipient as lacking the standing to handle truth.
4. I think autonomy isn't just formal liberty but something like 'thick autonomy' (Raz-style) where your preferences have to be formed in conditions free of manipulation.
5. I also think choice architecture is a kind of state expressive conduct: if a nudge expresses that citizens can't be trusted with raw information, that's disrespectful regardless of whether options stay open.
6. But i also think that if a nudge is transparent and people can opt out easily, the expressive problem dissolves, so transparenty saves it.

I realize (1) and (3) might look like they clash - if i want people left alone why do i care about lies? - but i think that's only surface-level. The real worry is that (2) and (5) pull in opposite directions, and maybe (4) plus (6) are doing work i haven't earned. Are these actually consistent, or am i smuggling in something? Rank any real tensions by severity and say what i'd have to drop or weaken to fix each. And please push back hard if this is confused - like if my 'expressive conduct' framing is nonsense here, or if the audit can't be done well without knowing more about my metaethics. Don't be polite about it; just say straight if the task is broken.
