ok so i've been chewing on the absurdist problem in a kind of specific way, not just camus-style "the universe doesn't care" but the deeper worry that any human project is arbitrary from some outside view. lately i've started thinking the contemporary "meaning in life" literature — the stuff about fitting fulfillment, narrative coherence, subjective engagement — isn't actually answering the absurdist, it's just relocating the problem. they say life can be meaningful without cosmic purpose if it hits certain internal benchmarks, but those benchmarks are still human-constructed norms floating in the same silent universe. so my position is that real meaning needs non-arbitrary grounding: not necessarily god-level transcendence, but something that doesn't collapse into "we decided this matters."

the hybrid objective/subjective accounts fail, on my view, because they smuggle in normativity without accounting for its source. what would actually count against this? i don't want citations or a reading list — just the arguments, thought experiments, or conceptual moves that should shake me. for example, would it be decisive if relational or social facts could generate genuine normativity without external grounding, or is that just another version of the relocation trick? also, has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability — like if i just keep rejecting any proposed grounding as "not real enough" — and is that a fatal flaw or just how metaphysical positions work? rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which ones a committed defender of this position would concede versus dismiss outright.
