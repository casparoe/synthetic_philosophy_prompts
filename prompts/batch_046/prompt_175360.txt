I've been thinking about why nobody can agree on what art actually is. There's the gallery-crowd who say if an institution calls it art, then it is. There's the feeling-crowd who say it has to move you or be beautiful in some way. And there's this other idea I ran into where art is just anything made with the right kind of intention, like you meant for people to experience it a certain way. This argument's been going since the 60s and nobody's moved.

My guess is it's not one debate but a few different questions crammed together: what word should we use, what do we value, and who gets to decide. But I'm not sure. There's also this weird practice in some towns where people paint broken road signs and hang them in the square and call it "repair art" - locals treat it different from graffiti even though its technically the same damage. So maybe the stall is because we're asking for a definition of something that only makes sense inside a specific community agreement?

I want a real diagnosis, not just a summary. Is this verbal? Are the sides asking different things? Is there a hidden fight about intention vs social rules? And honestly, what would progress even look like here - a new distinction, admitting the question's messed up, or something else?
