For my philosophy seminar I am trying to lay out my view on social kinds and check whether it is actually coherent. Here is where I have landed: (1) race is a social construct with no biological foundation, produced instead by historical power relations and categorization practices; (2) gender is also socially constructed, but unlike race it carries a first-person authoritative dimension—what gender someone is is fixed by their own sincere identification, even in the absence of social recognition; (3) money is a social kind structurally parallel to gender, since its value depends entirely on collective acceptance rather than on any intrinsic physical properties; (4) any social kind exists only so long as there is sustained collective intentionality directed at it, so if everyone stopped believing in a currency it would cease to exist; and (5) it is possible for an individual to genuinely change gender in a society where collective beliefs about gender categories remain completely static. Do these hang together? In particular, does the parallel I draw in (3) between money and gender collapse once I grant gender the first-person authority in (2) that money plainly lacks? And does (5) contradict (4) if gender is genuinely a social kind sustained by collective intentionality? Which of these tensions are real, which only appear to be tensions and dissolve on closer inspection, and what is the least costly single revision? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity.
