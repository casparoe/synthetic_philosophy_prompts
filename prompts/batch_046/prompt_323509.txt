I'm designing a taxonomy for a comparative-philosophy seminar on Jain anekāntavāda and syādvāda. Build a branching decision tree that classifies interpretive positions according to the logical and metaphysical commitments they make, not according to the names scholars give them. Each internal node should be a concrete claim or case on which positions genuinely diverge — for instance: whether reality itself is many-sided (metaphysical pluralism) or whether our knowledge of it is necessarily partial (epistemic perspectivism); whether syādvāda's seven modes of predication permit a substance to be both F and not-F in the same respect and at the same time (a true contradiction tolerated) or only in different respects or conditions (contradiction dissolved); whether the doctrine applies uniformly to empirical, moral, and metaphysical propositions, or whether its scope is restricted to descriptions of complex, changing substances; and whether a "standpoint" (naya) requires a conscious, intentional subject to hold it, or whether a synthetic system that aggregates contradictory outputs — say, an ensemble of models trained on incompatible normative frameworks — can be said to instantiate multiple nayas without awareness. The tree should terminate in named or unnamed leaves: strict metaphysical pluralism allowing true contradictions; epistemic perspectivism preserving classical consistency; logical-formalist reduction of syādvāda to syntactic hedging; and any combination your branches generate that appears unoccupied — note whether it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. For each leaf, state the position and one sentence describing its most awkward commitment, especially for any view attempting to combine full pluralism with strict consistency. Also flag any pair of leaves that the tree's diagnostic questions cannot genuinely separate. The audience is graduate students who know basic Jain terminology but should be able to navigate the tree by answering the questions, not by recognizing labels. Keep the taxonomy free of citation requests, bibliographies, and references to obscure secondary literature.
