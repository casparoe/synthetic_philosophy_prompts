I am a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and I am trying to understand an argument I came across in F.H. Bradley's Appearance and Reality, in the portion dealing with relations and the structure of propositions. The passage, as I understand it, presents a difficulty about how a relation connects to the terms it relates. Bradley asks what binds a relation like similarity to the two items it relates, say A and B. If we answer that there are further relations linking similarity to A and to B respectively, then those further relations also require binding, and we are launched into an infinite regress. If we deny that the relation is external and instead treat it as somehow internal to the terms, then the distinction between terms and relations seems to dissolve, and the original statement collapses into an undifferentiated whole rather than remaining a unified proposition with genuinely distinct parts.

My difficulty is in knowing exactly what kind of problem this is meant to be. Is it an argument about the logic of language and predication, about the ontology of objects and properties, or about both? I find myself drawing analogies to software architecture, where discrete modules reference one another through explicit interfaces, yet the binding between a module and its interface is itself defined elsewhere in the system, often producing circular dependencies or requiring a global registry or monolithic runtime that holds the pieces together. Does Bradley's regress correspond to this kind of structural problem? Would his conclusion be that any genuinely compositional system must eventually depend on some unanalyzed, non-decomposable unity that functions like a runtime kernel and cannot itself be broken into further parts? Or is the argument confined strictly to linguistic propositions, with no implications for structural composition in other domains?

Could you help me understand what Bradley is actually claiming about the unity of the proposition, clarify whether his regress targets language, ontology, or both, and indicate whether there is any coherent way for a compositional system, whether metaphysical or software-based, to avoid the regress without collapsing into an undifferentiated whole?
