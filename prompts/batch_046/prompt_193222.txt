For an upper-level seminar on rational agency and modal self-reference, write one exam essay question that requires students to derive and diagnose the Löbian obstacle in a constructed case rather than recite standard expositions of Löb's theorem. Introduce an agent R governed by one reflective principle: R treats any belief as endorsed if R can derive it from currently endorsed beliefs together with this principle, and R takes such endorsement as conclusive within domain D (where D is either arithmetic or practical reasoning, your choice). Ask students to demonstrate why R's acceptance that its derivation mechanism is sound for D—formalized as the claim that derivability in R implies truth in D—forces R either to endorse every proposition in D or to abandon self-trust, identifying the precise modal fixed-point step where circularity enters. Require them to explain why replacing conclusive endorsement with graded probabilistic confidence fails to dissolve the obstacle automatically, and to evaluate whether adding an explicit consistency-checking meta-layer solves the problem or merely relocates the reflexive loop to the meta-level. Provide a model answer of approximately 700 words and a marking scheme that separates an A answer—which isolates the derivability of the fixed-point, explains the collapse of soundness-cum-reflexivity into omniscience, and correctly diagnoses the relocation problem—from a B answer—which states Löb's result correctly but misses either the probabilistic-red-herring point or the meta-layer relocation issue. Specify total marks (25) and the specific marks assigned to each required move. Do not request bibliographies, citations, or reading lists.
