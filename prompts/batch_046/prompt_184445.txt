I sit on the Safety and Deployment Review Board for a research consortium developing autonomous logistics systems. We are evaluating a proposal for a pilot program—scheduled to begin next quarter—of a decision-support module that operates according to what its designers call a reversibility-bound impact measure. Rather than encoding a complete utility function or institutional value hierarchy, the system computes the differential effect of each candidate action against a counterfactual in which it takes no action, and it vetoes any option that produces irreversible changes to physical infrastructure, stored data, or human workflow patterns within a predefined operational envelope. The designers argue that this makes the system value-agnostic and therefore safe to deploy without resolving long-standing disagreements about organizational priorities. Our board must issue a recommendation by Friday: approve the pilot with standard oversight, approve only with mandatory value-alignment safeguards, or reject until a full normative framework is integrated. Please draft the formal opinion I will circulate. It should recommend a specific course of action tied to a clear timeline, engage seriously with the claim that reversibility can substitute for explicit value specification, and address the strongest counter-argument—that selecting the baseline counterfactual, defining irreversible across heterogeneous domains, and weighting informational against physical harm each presupposes normative judgments the system is not equipped to make. Write for board members with policy expertise but no specialized background in formal ethics or AI alignment.
