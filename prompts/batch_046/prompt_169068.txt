Im giving a toast at my old department heads retirement — she spent 25 years in municipal planning refusing demolition permits for these weird leftover industrial corridors everyone called eyesores. One turned out to be the only migration path left for some local bat colony, but she didnt know that when she blocked the permits; she just had this gut feeling you dont tear down a fence untill you know why it was put up. Half the room are urbanists who will catch every Burke and Chesterton reference, the other half are family who wont. Write a four-minute toast that uses Chesterton's fence as the actual backbone, not just a dropped quote. Frame her legacy through the idea that prescription applies to bureaucratic inertia too — she kept old zoning variances alive not out of nostalgia but because long possession creates its own kind of title, even for ugly infrastructure. Keep it warm and affectionate, avoid any citations or reading lists, and dont say shes watching over us — she would hate that.
