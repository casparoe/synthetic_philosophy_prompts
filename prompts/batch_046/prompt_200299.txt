hey so compare two ways people try to solve the paradox of tragedy - why we seek out sad art even though sadness usually sucks. first theres the old catharsis idea, where tragedy acts like a pressure valve and purges negative feelings so we feel lighter after. then theres the framing idea, where the sadness is real but its quarantined inside the artwork, so we can play with it safely like poking a bruise that doesnt actually hurt.

but i want you to run both of these through a distinction im tinkering with: emotional metabolism vs emotional tourism. in metabolism, the negative affect actually gets converted into something else - relief, insight, whatever - and that conversion IS the pleasure. in tourism, we stay tourists: we visit the sadness, take some mental pictures, and leave it untouched; the pleasure comes from the visit itself, not from changing the feeling.

so: which theory lines up with metabolism and which with tourism? and more importantly, can either account survive without borrowing from the other? like, if catharsis is just metabolism, does it need the quarantine frame to explain why we dont just run away? and if framing is tourism, does it secretly need some metabolic payoff to explain why we come back for more?
