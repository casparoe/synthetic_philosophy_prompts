Question: Define procreative beneficence (PB) and explain how it differs from liberal eugenics. Would PB require parents to select for non-health traits like musical ability?

Student answer:
Procreative beneficence, first articulated by Savulescu in 2001, says parents should pick the embryo expected to have the best life. Liberal eugenics, as Nicholas Agar frames it, is the political position that states should actively fund and promote genetic enhancement while leaving the final choice voluntary. So PB is the personal moral duty, and liberal eugenics is the governmental program that supports it.

PB only requires selecting for musical ability if that trait actually boosts well-being. Critics worry about 'copycat embryos'—where parents replicate the genome of a talented person—which Sandel discusses in The Case Against Perfection as a violation of human dignity through copycat design. However, this copycat issue is really just an extreme version of PB, not a separate objection. Also, Agar's view is mainly about state funding, not about individual selection.
