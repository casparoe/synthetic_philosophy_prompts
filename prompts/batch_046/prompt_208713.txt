Dr. Reed should press the objection that, without a principled restriction on which predicates may be co-affirmed, the doctrine risks explanatory vacuity; Dr. Kavi should reply by appealing to the Jain distinction between naya (partial viewpoint) and pramāṇa (valid knowledge source), insisting that anekāntavāda does not license arbitrary conjunction but requires each assertion to be indexed to a specific pramāṇa and its corresponding naya. They should genuinely engage: Dr. Reed should concede that classical bivalence strains at certain boundary cases yet argue that this only motivates a local revision of classical logic, not an acceptance of many-sided truth; Dr. Kavi should acknowledge the formal challenge but counter that the demand for a single, perspective-independent decision procedure already presupposes the very absolutism (ekānta) that Jain logic rejects. Please make both interlocutors equally competent, with real back-and-forth rather than talking past one another, and keep the tone suitably philosophical and precise.
