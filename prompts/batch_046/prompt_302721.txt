ok so i've been chewing on this hitler vs structural explanation debate and built an argument that feels like it proves too much but i cant find the exit. (1) asking 'would there have been a nazi germany without hitler?' is asking if he was necessary for the outcome. (2) structuralists say the treaty of Versailles, hyperinflation, and weak weimar institutions created demand for an authoritarian nationalist movement no matter who led it, so hitler was replaceable—if he died in 1923 another figure fills the slot. (3) but if structural conditions really make leaders interchangeable, they should hold even if you strip out the whole top leadership at once. (4) yet if you remove hitler, goebbles, and himmler together, it's hard to imagine the holocaust and the specific war of expansion still happening, since those came from hitler's personal obsessions. (5) so structural conditions seem sufficient for 'an authoritarian germany' but not for 'nazi germany' specifically, which means to deny hitler's importance you have to water the outcome down until its unrecognizable. (6) apply the same move everywhere: structural explanations can only predict generic analogues—stalin-less russia is still authoritarian but not the specific ussr—so they never actually explain specific events, and great-man claims are impossible everywhere. (7) that leaves either hitler was uniquely necessary, or we've argued ourselves into a weird historical nihilism where nobody matters and every event is only explainable in broad strokes. i think the slip is somewhere between (3) and (5), maybe i'm smuggling in hidden premises about counterfactual identity conditions or about what counts as the 'same' regime, but i also wonder if this touches on overdetermination in a way i'm not seeing. where does it actually fail? i'd rather keep (2) and (6) if possible, but mostly i want to know which step is the weakest and what giving it up costs.
