I've been working through Mill's later economic writings—especially the posthumous Chapters on Socialism and the successive editions of Principles of Political Economy—after a long thread in rationalist forums about whether classical liberalism can absorb socialist conclusions without contradiction. One interpretation that kept surfacing in my reading group is that Mill's mature position resolves the liberty-planning tension by treating the harm principle as institutionally indexed: under competitive capitalism, state interference with private property counts as an illegitimate harm to the owner, but once production is reorganized into cooperative associations, the same interference becomes a legitimate defense of collective liberty against individual extraction or holdout behavior. From this, it seems to follow that Mill eventually believed worker cooperatives should replace private capital not merely as a voluntary experiment, but as a state-enforced transition, and that he had thereby shown liberal rights to be purely instrumental to economic efficiency rather than principled constraints.

If that reading holds—if Mill genuinely held that the harm principle's protective scope contracts or expands depending on whether the economic mode is competitive or associative—then hasn't he already collapsed the distinction between negative liberty and collective planning? Specifically, I'm trying to understand whether Mill's endorsement of associationist socialism implies that liberalism was always just a temporary framework for capital accumulation, and that the real philosophical work is not defending liberty against socialism, but designing cooperative architectures that minimize the bureaucratic stagnation and industrial despotism he warned about in centralized schemes. Could you clarify where this institutional-index interpretation goes wrong, and whether Mill actually retained a non-instrumental reason for thinking that voluntary association preserves liberty even when the state subsidizes or hastens the transition?
