I have spent the last few months serving on a parliamentary working group reviewing carceral policy, and since visiting Halden Fengsel in Norway and subsequently examining reports from several American supermax facilities, I have become preoccupied with a tension I cannot resolve. The contrast between the Norwegian emphasis on normalisation and the American reliance on prolonged isolation has led me to wonder whether our current conceptual vocabulary is adequate for judging prison design.

The question is which normative principle ought to govern the architecture and daily regime of a high-security institution that nevertheless claims to reduce recidivism: retributive proportionality, rehabilitative transformation, deterrence through certainty, restorative encounter, or what I term institutional permeability—the deliberate porosity of prison boundaries to sustained outside social contact. Please rank these five principles from the most to the least defensible as the primary ethos for such a facility, and present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each comprising one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
