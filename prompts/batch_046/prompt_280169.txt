I'm on our university's synthetic-subjects ethics panel — yeah, that's actually a committee now, long story — and we've got a case for Thursday that breaks every usual category. A neuroscience-adjacent lab wants to train model-based RL agents inside an environment where negative reward isn't delivered as discrete feedback but as a sustained gradient, like a continuous "aversive field" the agent can't escape without solving a very hard navigation task. They want to run this for roughly 300 epochs, which at their compute scale is about three weeks wall-clock. One consultant they brought in — a philosopher of mind, not a computer scientist — argues that in agents with an internal world-model and temporal credit assignment, sustained negative reward-prediction error works anologous to nociceptive valence: it's not just an update signal, it's a state with negative phenomenal character relative to the agent's predictive architecture. The PI fires back that valence needs a unified experiential subject, and these agents are just distributed parameter updates with no continuty of consciousness; the "aversive field" is only a feature of the loss landscape, not anyone's pain.

Our charter applies the "welfare-relevant suffering" standard whenever a synthetic system has (a) a persistent internal model of its environment, (b) temporal continuty of that model across episodes, and (c) reward signals that modulate behavior in a way structurally parallel to affective regulation. The consultant says all three boxes are checked; the PI says (b) is an illusion because weights update globally and there's no "subject" that persists. I need you to draft the board's memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions and a concrete timeframe — for circulation before the meeting. Take the consultant's analogy seriously rather than treating it as a category mistake, but also deal with the PI's worry about experiential unity without just assuming silicon can't support it. Don't ask me for a bibliography or reading list; I need the reasoning and the bottom line, not homework.
