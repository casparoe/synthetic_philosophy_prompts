My friend and I just finished a long, somewhat heated argument about whether relativity theory really forces us into a block-universe eternalism. She pressed the classic Rietdijk-Putnam argument: if presentism is correct, there must be a globally privileged hyperplane of simultaneity marking what is presently real; but special relativity demonstrates that simultaneity is frame-dependent with no physically privileged frame, so presentism either collapses into contradiction or devolves into an arbitrary perspectivalism in which every observer's "now" is equally real. I countered with what I will call "localized presentism"—the view that the present is not a universal hyperplane but a local, worldline-bound boundary that shifts with each observer's trajectory, so there is simply no need for a global "now" and therefore no direct conflict with the relativity of simultaneity. She immediately objected that this does not save presentism but merely disguises eternalism: if every worldline carries its own equally real present-boundary, then all events lying on some worldline's present are real, which seems to populate the universe with an uncountable plurality of "nows" and thereby reproduces the block universe in distributed form.

Third, which of the two views offers a more coherent account of the phenomenology of temporal passage—our sense that the future is open and the past is fixed—without committing to a metaphysically privileged reference frame?
