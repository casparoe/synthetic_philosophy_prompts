Prepare a Q&A defense sheet for a seminar presentation titled Threshold Interdependence Without We-Modes: A Middle Path in Collective Intentionality. The thesis being defended is that collective intentionality is neither reducible to interlocking individual intentions with common-knowledge conditions (the Bratman-style reductionist view) nor a primitive, irreducible mental mode (the Searle-style social-ontological view). Instead, it consists in a modal-normative structure—threshold interdependence—where each agent's practical reasoning is reciprocally and non-summatively constrained by the others', without requiring that anyone represent themselves as part of a plural subject. The audience is a philosophy of mind and social ontology workshop comprising: (1) four individualist reductionists who accept shared cooperative activity but reject any we-mode of reasoning; (2) two social ontologists who defend collective intentionality as ontologically primitive; and (3) one anthropologist of collective ritual who is skeptical of armchair distinctions that fail to track ethnographic variation in group behavior. Provide a sketch of the core argument: joint action requires that the failure of one participant's reasoning automatically destabilizes the others' practical commitments in a way that cannot be decomposed into individual belief-desire pairs, yet this destabilization operates without shared mental content or plural-subject representation. Then list eight objections ordered by likely damage, each phrased in the vocabulary and commitments of the subgroup most likely to raise it: for the reductionists, objections about explanatory redundancy and Ockham's razor; for the social ontologists, objections about missing the phenomenology and normativity of we; for the anthropologist, objections about ethnographic counterexamples and the risk of over-formalization. For each objection, supply a concise two-minute reply and an explicit note on where that reply is weakest or concedes ground. Include the specific objection the speaker is hoping nobody asks—namely, whether two strangers walking in parallel toward the same shelter during a sudden storm, with no communication or mutual awareness of coordination, nonetheless satisfy threshold interdependence and thus count as performing a joint action. Finally, advise on one strategic concession to offer in the opening minutes of Q&A that will secure goodwill from the reductionists without undermining the anti-reductionist core of the view.
