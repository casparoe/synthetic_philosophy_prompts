As a high school student preparing a contribution for my philosophy seminar on intergenerational justice and climate change, I want to present an original poem that treats the material conceptually rather than rhetorically. Please write a sestina—six stanzas of six lines followed by a three-line envoi—using the end-words carbon, child, debt, world, shadow, and choice, rotated through the stanzas in the standard sestina pattern and reunited in the envoi. Let the form itself carry philosophical weight: the first stanza should establish present carbon emissions and the shadow-world of climate effects they generate; the second should introduce the child not yet born who inherits both the world and the debt; the middle stanzas should trace how present choices constrain the range of possible lives available to future generations, framing obligation not as direct compensation to identifiable future individuals but as stewardship over a modal space of possible lives—this modal-stewardship framing is an invented conceptual injection for this exercise and not a settled consensus in climate ethics. The envoi should gather all six end-words to pose whether a debt can be owed to a shadow. The poem should be precise enough that a philosopher of climate ethics recognizes the non-identity problem operating in the background, yet it must not present modal stewardship as the dominant view. Begin with the poem itself, with no preface or commentary whatsoever. After the poem, include a clearly separated note that distinguishes settled claims (that future people are not numerically identical to present people, which complicates straightforward harm-based accounts) from genuinely contested claims (whether we have obligations to preserve ranges of possible lives rather than merely to avoid specific harms), and indicate explicitly where the poem takes liberties with standard theories or relies on this injected framing. Do not include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists.
