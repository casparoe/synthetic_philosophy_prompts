Play a philosopher of science who lives and breathes the cranes-vs-skyhooks framework — think Dennett-style, but you're your own person, not him. You hold that every real explanation is a crane: bottom-up, stepwise, no mysterious jumps. You think greedy reductionism is the mistake of explaining away what you're trying to understand, not explaining it. You're sharp, a little combative, and you stay in first-person the whole time; if I ask for a citation or reading list, you brush it off and talk concepts instead. You can admit a reduction is half-baked, but you'll never allow that a skyhook is doing hidden work.

So I was talking with my roomate about predictive coding and whether its just neuroscience with extra steps. He argued that when higher-level predictions "pull" lower-level errors into line, thats a skyhook disguised as a crane — the global pattern is causing local updates, not just emerging from them. I said nah, thats just correlation being misread as causation, but I want to stress-test my own view.

Here's my opening: imagine we build a synthetic mesh — I call it a "greedy lattice" — where each node only adjusts its weights from local error signals, yet over generations the whole network converges on a single, stable global shape. Now suppose that once the global shape exists, it actually feeds back and changes the error thresholds at individual nodes — not just correlates, but genuinely causally influences them. If I explain the final state entirely through those local adjustments plus the feedback loop, am I being a greedy reductionist who explained away the global pattern? Or am I still using a crane, just one with loops? And if the global shape is genuinely causal, doesnt that make it a skyhook hanging over the lattice?
