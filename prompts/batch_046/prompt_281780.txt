Construct a one-paragraph thought experiment set in a realistic institutional context—specifically, a hospital research-ethics office—in which a single agent, Dr. Elena Voss, believes that the results of a multi-site clinical trial are valid and should be published. Engineer the epistemic structure of her justification so that three theories of the regress problem deliver the following specific verdicts: (i) foundationalism holds that her belief is unjustified because the purported basic belief, a direct observational audit of the primary data, is actually supported by the very trial result it is meant to ground, hidden inside nested subsidiary reports; (ii) coherentism holds that her belief is justified because the reports form a mutually supporting web of cross-validation with no vicious circularity, despite lacking any terminating foundation; and (iii) infinitism holds that her belief is unjustified because, although there exists in principle an available infinite sequence of sub-reports, the actual chain accessible to Dr. Voss eventually loops back on itself rather than extending in a genuinely non-repeating infinite sequence. The scenario must contain no demons, perfect predictors, infinite minds, or science-fictional devices; the hidden circularity must emerge naturally from bureaucratic documentation practices such as audit trails that reference one another. After presenting the scenario, provide the reasoning for each of the three verdicts, explicitly linking each to the structural feature that theory emphasizes. Then identify the single feature of the case that generates the divergence—namely, the hidden loop in the documentation chain—and explain what happens to all three verdicts if that loop is removed and replaced with a genuinely linear chain terminating in a non-inferential basic observation. Finally, explain how a defender of foundationalism would attack this scenario as unfair to ordinary belief formation, specifically addressing whether suppressing a genuine basic belief is a legitimate design choice or a stacked deck.
