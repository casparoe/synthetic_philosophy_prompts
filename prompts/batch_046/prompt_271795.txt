The protagonist is a junior data analyst, recently hired, who begins by believing she is merely recording objective physiological states. Over several months, she notices that patients whose sleep patterns fall outside the taxonomy are not simply labeled as anomalies; instead, they are enrolled in subtle correction protocols—adjusted biofeedback parameters, modified medication schedules, and revised intake questionnaires—that gradually reshape their sleep until it conforms to the available categories. The narrative should include a specific scene in which she pulls up a quarterly dataset and realizes that the outlier rate has dropped to exactly zero, not because human sleep has become uniform, but because every deviation has been administratively smoothed away through these incremental adjustments. I want the story to raise the question of whether the taxonomy is describing sleep or producing it, and to show the protagonist's growing unease about her own complicity in the process. Please keep the tone restrained and clinical, letting the philosophical weight emerge from the concrete details of the clinic's daily operations rather than from explicit theoretical language.
