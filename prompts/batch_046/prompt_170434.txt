I've been getting into Islamic philosophy lately — I'm taking a philosophy of religion course and a mate lent me Al-Ghazālī's Tahāfut al-Falāsifa — and I'm stuck on the occasionalism debate. The usual story is that Ghazālī denies fire causes burning, with God doing it directly every time, and then modern philosophers either defend him as a proto-Humean about regularities or attack him for wrecking natural science. But it feels like the argument hasn't moved in decades: every paper I skim either rehabilitates his rationalism or says he's just wrong about physics, and nobody seems to change anyone's mind.

Is this actually a substantive disagreement about metaphysics, or is it verbal — people using 'causation' to mean efficient cause in one camp and constant conjunction in the other — or are the sides answering different questions, one about theology and one about philosophy of science? I'm particularly curious whether teh stall comes from ignoring the Ash'arī doctrine of kasb (acquisition) which sits awkwardly in the middle, or whether that's just a red herring. What would actually count as progress here — a historical finding about Ghazālī's real view, a formal model of occasionalist causation, or just admitting we're talking past each other?
