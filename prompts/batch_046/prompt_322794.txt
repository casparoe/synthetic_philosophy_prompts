For a graduate seminar on logical empiricism and its aftermath, I am drafting my working position on the analytic-synthetic distinction and on Quine’s argument in “Two Dogmas of Empiricism,” and I want a consistency audit before I present it. Here are my commitments:

(1) I accept Quine’s critique of analyticity as traditionally formulated—specifically, that neither definitional reduction nor interchangeability salva veritate can ground a philosophically significant distinction—so that the analytic-synthetic boundary is not epistemically fundamental.

(2) I nevertheless maintain a pragmatic, framework-relative distinction: within an adopted conceptual scheme, some statements serve as meaning-constitutive nodes whose revision changes the subject, while others are empirical adjustments within the scheme. This is not a metaphysical divide but a functional one about the cost and consequence of revision.

(3) On mathematics, I reject both Carnapian conventionalism and Quine’s straightforward continuity thesis: mathematical truths are synthetic with respect to a pre-formal web of belief, but once a formal framework is adopted they become analytically entrenched, so that Quinean holism applies differentially across the adoption boundary.

(4) I hold that reflective equilibrium is the correct method of justification for beliefs of all kinds—logical, mathematical, and empirical—without granting any special “analytic” sanctuary immune from holistic pressure.

(5) At the same time, I think that certain core logical and arithmetical commitments are practically unrevisable for any functioning rational agent, not because they are analytic, but because abandoning them undermines the very conditions under which revision and justification can take place.

Please examine these for joint tenability. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to conflict, and how severe is each tension? Order them by severity rather than treating them as equally bad. For each genuine tension, specify the cheapest revision that would restore consistency and what it costs me. In particular, address whether (5) functionally resurrects the distinction that (1) rejects, and whether (3)’s differential treatment of mathematics conflicts with (4)’s uniform methodological claim. Identify the apparent tension that dissolves once (2)’s framework-relativity is properly understood. Finally, give me a committed verdict: if I must revise or abandon one of these five commitments to make the package coherent, which one should go, and why is that revision less costly than altering any of the others?
