I've been trying to map the implicit logic behind my philosophy-of-biology mentor's rulings on the species problem, and I want to see whether her framework holds together when pushed past the standard textbook cases. In our seminar discussions she has consistently rejected the Biological Species Concept for bacteria on account of horizontal gene transfer; affirmed that lions and tigers are distinct species despite ligers because wild reproductive isolation is ecologically robust; accepted that morphologically identical cryptic frog species are separate when mating calls diverge; defended the red wolf as a genuine species despite coyote hybridization on the grounds of an independent evolutionary trajectory; dismissed "reptiles" as taxonomically illegitimate because it is paraphyletic; and denied any form of species essentialism—there is, she insists, no hidden nature shared by all members of a kind. She also grants that ring species present a serious puzzle but denies they dissolve the reality of species boundaries altogether.

Now I want to test the architecture of these commitments with a constructed case that does not appear in the literature she usually cites. Imagine organisms engineered with modular genomes in which reproductive compatibility is not binary but environmentally gated: populations A and B can interbreed freely at temperatures below 15°C, yet are mechanically incompatible above 25°C, with minimal morphological divergence and no clear phylogenetic separation. Would she classify them as one species, two, or reject the framing as misapplied? I am less interested in the label than in which principle—reproductive isolation, evolutionary trajectory, genetic cohesion, or ecological niche—actually drives her verdicts, and whether this case forces her to prioritize one. If her existing rules genuinely underdetermine the answer, what principle would she be adopting with each choice, and which further hypothetical—say, synthetic organisms whose compatibility can be reprogrammed in real time—would finally distinguish the competing rules?
