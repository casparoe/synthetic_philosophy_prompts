About half the group thinks this triad gives policymakers useful scaffolding; the other half says analogies always smuggle in false inevitability and will mislead more than help.

We need a decision memo for Thursday's meeting recommending whether we keep the triadic framing, drop it, or keep it with conditions. The memo needs a concrete bottom line and an honest treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side — not just a restatement of our legal mandate or whatever.

To avoid a generic list, please run each analogy through what I'm calling the 'counterfactual stability test': ask whether the analogy still holds if you change one background institutional condition — say, nuclear tech discovered in 1850, printing press invented inside a centralized censorship empire, or industrial mechanization happening without fossil-fuel lock-in. Then rate each analogy on 'institutional reversibility': does it point to governance levers that can be pulled back if we're wrong, or does it imply an irreversible trajectory?

Please write the memo in regular prose first, then append a structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields:
- "recommendation": "approve" | "approve_with_conditions" | "reject"
- "bottom_line_action": a specific step plus a timeframe (e.g., "delay publication two weeks to...")
- "reasoning_summary": 2-3 sentences
- "strongest_opposing_argument": the best point for the side that loses
- "stability_and_reversibility": an object with keys "nuclear_weapons", "printing_press", "industrial_revolution"; each value is an object with "stability_score" (1-5), "reversibility_score" (1-5), and "one_line_note"

Don't include citations, bibliographies, or reading lists — we just need reasoning the commitee can recieve and act on.
