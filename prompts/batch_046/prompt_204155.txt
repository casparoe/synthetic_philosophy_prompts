Draft eight to ten interview questions that press this position at its joints: include at least one probing whether her argument collapses into a deontological rights claim, one addressing scenarios in which risk-pooling seems unavoidable, and one asking whether her objection applies with equal force to probabilistic trade-offs within a single life. For each of these three, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. Conclude with one question asking what empirical or normative finding could convince her that risk-pooling is not a category mistake but only a prudential danger. Keep the questions away from generic biography; they should turn on the actual structure of her view and anticipate the standard moves in the literature on aggregation and distributive justice.
