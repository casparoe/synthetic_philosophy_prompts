I'm taking this grad seminar on reduction and emergence and there's this one debate that's been driving me nuts. We keep circling back to whether current neuroscience of consciousness—like global workspace models and predictive coding—is using Dennett-style cranes properly or whether it's greedy reductionism disguised as mechanistic explanation. The reductionists say they're just stacking smaller explanations to build up to subjective experience without invoking any skyhooks. The anti-reductionists say that at some point you're not explaining consciousness, you're explaining it away, and that there has to be some organizational principle that doesn't decompose—maybe not a literal skyhook but something functionally equivalent.

We all read the same papers, we all agree on the empirical results, and yet nobody's changed their mind since september. I'm wondering: is this actually a hidden disagreement about what an explanation owes us—causal coverage vs phenomenological presence? Or are we fighting over the word real? Maybe one camp is asking how consciousness arises and the other is asking why there's something it feels like, and we're treating those as the same question. Can you diagnose what kind of stall this is—verbal, substantive, bedrock intuition, or different questions under one label—and tell me honestly whether a new distinction, an experiment, or just reframing the problem could move it? Or is it just going to be one of those permanent philosophy loops where the cranes never actually reach the top?
