Write a dialogue between two grad students in comparative politics—call them M and J—who are arguing about whether democratic backsliding is actually a real thing or just a partisan label for "the other side won." M thinks it's especially useless for the US because nobody cancelled elections; she says if you don't have an intentional agent like Orbán rewriting rules, you're just describing polarization you don't like. J thinks backsliding can be emergent—norm erosion, court capture through incremental appointments, media consolidation—without a mastermind, and that Hungary and Poland show both intentional and drift-style patterns. Have them actually respond to each other: M should press J on how to tell emergent drift apart from ordinary democratic conflict, and J should concede that without some institutional metric it's hard to avoid partisanship, but insist that things like judicial independence and media pluralism can do the work. Don't ask for citations or a reading list—just have them talk like they're in a seminar room after class. Keep it casual and a bit messy; maybe one of them writes "backslidding" with two d's at some point.
