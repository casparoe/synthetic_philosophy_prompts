I recently encountered a summary of origin essentialism—specifically the kind of modal claim about biological origins associated with Saul Kripke—presented in an online philosophy discussion that tried to extend the view into bioethics and personal identity theory. The poster summarized the position as follows: origin essentialism proves that every human being has the essential property of arising from exactly the sperm and egg they actually arose from, so that if one’s parents had used IVF with a different embryo, the resulting person would not be the same individual but a numerically different being with a different essence. From this, the poster concluded that personal identity over time is anchored by a biological continuity condition stricter than psychological continuity: even if all of one’s memories and personality traits were transferred to a new brain, the original person would not survive, because the gametic origin was interrupted. The summary further claimed that this directly refutes psychological-continuity theories of identity and rules out teletransportation, and that it entails monozygotic twins—since they split from a single zygote—begin as one person existing in two bodies, with a second, distinct essence and therefore a second person emerging only upon biological divergence.

This sounds partly right to me, but I suspect it conflates the core modal claim with several much stronger extensions. Could you provide a careful accuracy audit? Specifically: does origin essentialism genuinely target the exact gametes, or is the claim broader—about the particular egg and sperm, or about the broader biological origin process? Does the view actually entail anything about psychological continuity, brain-state transfer, or teletransportation, or are those entirely separate debates that the summary wrongly imports? How should origin essentialism treat monozygotic twins—does it genuinely imply they begin as one person, or is that an unsupported extension of the biological-origin condition? And does the impossibility of teletransportation actually follow from origin essentialism alone, or does it require additional premises about identity conditions? Please distinguish what is faithful to the core view, what is a plausible but non-obligatory extension, and what is a misrepresentation or overstatement, and please provide your answer in American English.
