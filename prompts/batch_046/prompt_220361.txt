Hey — I'm prepping my seminar on Dennett's real-patterns paper and one of my grad students walked me into an argument I can't find the exit for. Feels like it proves every rock has beliefs. Here it is:

(1) For Dennett a pattern is real only if the stance that reveals it gives predictions you can't get from lower stances, and it's more efficient.
(2) The intentional stance predicts by attributing rationality, beliefs, desires.
(3) Take any system S — a thermostat, an LLM, whatever. The physical stance needs impossible computation to predict its behavior.
(4) The design stance does better: predict heat-regulation or next-token outputs.
(5) But we can always build an intentional interpretation for S: say it believes it should minimize error and desires coherence.
(6) For complex systems like LLMs this intentional story often predicts behavior more efficiently than running the actual weights, which for us is intractible.
(7) And these intentional predictions arn't available from physics — physics never mentions beliefs.
(8) So by (1), whenever an intentional interpretation yields predictions, it reveals real patterns.
(9) We can always construct some intentional interpretation for any S, even if it's clunky.
(10) So every system has real intentional patterns — basically panpsychism about beliefs.
(11) But Dennett clearly denies beliefs to rocks and probably to LLMs too.
(12) So one of these steps has to go.

Which one? My instinct is that (6) or (9) smuggles in an equivocation between 'prediction available in principle' vs 'in practice', but I'm not sure. I also want to hang onto the idea that LLMs sit genuinely in the weeds between design and intentional — if I reject (9) does that force me to say there's no principled line? And I've been toying with adding a fourth 'algorithmic stance' (treating the system as running a specific compression scheme) — does that save Dennett or just make the collapse worse? Tell me where the leak is and what each repair costs.
