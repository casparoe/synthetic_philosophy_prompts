I am a doctoral student in philosophy working on normative ontology, and I am trying to clarify the 'free-floating rationales' thesis for a workshop paper on agent-independent normativity. A senior colleague summarized the position as follows: 'Free-floating rationales are normative reason-facts that hold entirely independently of any mental state, practical reasoning capacity, or agentive perspective. They are not merely reasons that apply to agents regardless of their contingent desires; they are ontologically basic entities—structurally analogous to mathematical objects or Platonic moral forms—such that even in a world devoid of all sentient beings, there remains an unreduced reason not to cause gratuitous suffering. The standard objection is that these reasons are causally inert and epistemically inaccessible: they function like commands issued inside a perfectly soundproof room, which makes positing them ontologically extravagant. Defenders reply that causal efficacy and epistemic accessibility are not conditions on normative existence, just as the causal inertness of logical truths does not undermine logic.'

This framing sounds broadly familiar from discussions of non-naturalist realism, but I am concerned that it collapses several distinct positions into one and misstates both the thesis and the objections. Specifically, I wonder whether the summary illegitimately equates agent-independence with full Platonic ontological basicness; whether it correctly captures the status of reasons in a world without agents, or whether it suppresses the conditional structure that some defenders (e.g., those working in the reasons-first, value-first, or Scanlonian traditions) would insist upon; and whether the soundproof-room analogy genuinely captures the action-guidance worry rather than substituting a generic epistemic-access objection.

Could you provide a sentence-by-sentence accuracy audit? I do not need citations or a reading list; I need only a conceptual diagnosis precise enough to determine whether this summary can be used responsibly in my presentation.
