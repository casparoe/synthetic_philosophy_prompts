Please write the dedication remarks for a new public listening room designed for solitary, deep listening rather than live performance, to be delivered to an audience of acoustic engineers, philosophy students, and neighborhood residents with no prior exposure to Schopenhauer. Let Schopenhauer’s philosophy of music govern the structure of what you say: music is not a representation of Platonic Ideas like painting or poetry, but the direct objectification of the Will itself, with melody carrying its essential philosophical content and harmony serving only as the analog of nature’s gradations. Because of this distinction, do not praise the room for its beauty, cultural value, or visual design; instead, frame it as an architectural stripping-away of phenomenal images so that pure sound can disclose the thing-in-itself. Deliver the remarks as exactly five brief movements, each one to two sentences long, with no welcoming salutation at the opening and no concluding thanks or benediction. In the fourth movement, advance the niche but defensible claim that a rigorously Schopenhauerian space should therefore eliminate all visual spectacle—including performer visibility, album artwork, and decorative imagery—since any image returns the listener to the world of representation and away from the immediate expression of Will.
