I've basically bought Hofstadter's line that the self is just a strange loop — the symbol I in my brain points back to the neurons generating it, nothing mystical. But I've been tweaking it into something more specific: I think the loop only constitutes a self when it's epistemically opaque to its own substrate. The symbol can't "see" the neural machinery without breaking the loop, so split-brain or deep dissasociation cases aren't damage to one self, they're the loop going transparent, like seeing pixels on a screen. I'm using this opacaty condition as the real criterion of personal identity, not continuity of psychology or body. What would actually count against this? I want the defeaters ranked — not just generic "what about amnesia", but things that specifically attack the opacity claim or show transparency doesn't dissolve the self. Which ones would a defender of this view concede as real problems, and which would they dismiss? And be honest: have I just insulated the view so that any counterexample gets redescribed as "the loop went transparent"? If so, is that actually a problem for a metaphysical claim like this?
