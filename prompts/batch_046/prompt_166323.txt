I'm a graduate student in epidemiology who recently encountered Tolstoy's late essay What Is Art? in a seminar on aesthetics and social transmission, and I found myself wondering whether his central metaphor—art as an infection of feeling—can be taken literally rather than poetically. I want you to restate Tolstoy's full position using only the vocabulary of epidemiology and public health: transmission dynamics, reproductive number, virulence, vector and reservoir, endemic versus epidemic spread, susceptibility thresholds, and pathogenic versus symbiotic infection. Specifically, map his claim that true art is universally contagious and requires no special training to be received; his distinction between genuine art and the false professional art produced for and consumed by an elite; and his normative judgment that art ought to transmit morally elevating feelings rather than merely stimulating or decadent ones. Be disciplined: do not import aesthetic vocabulary where an epidemiological term is meant to do all the work, and do not treat high virulence as automatically good—Tolstoy clearly thinks some highly contagious feelings are corrupting.

Then provide the residue report. Identify which elements of Tolstoy's theory translate without loss, which translate only by distorting either the epidemiological concept or Tolstoy's meaning—for instance, whether his well-known condemnation of Wagnerian opera can be rendered as a claim about maladaptive virulence, about mismatched host susceptibility, or about a pathogenic vector without collapsing his moral critique into a purely biological one. Note precisely what disappears entirely: any role for the artist's intention that is not reducible to being the initial vector; any normative evaluation of the feeling transmitted that is not captured by the pathogenic-versus-symbiotic distinction; and any structural feature of class-based access that epidemiology can represent only as uneven distribution of susceptibility. Finally, conclude by saying whether the residue reveals a genuine limitation in Tolstoy's framework—something an epidemiological model can correct—or whether it demonstrates that epidemiology is the wrong lens because it strips away the normative and social dimensions that make his theory a critique rather than a neutral description.
