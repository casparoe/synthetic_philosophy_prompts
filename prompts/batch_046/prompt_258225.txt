Compare Darwin's inversion of design reasoning with Turing's inversion of mentalistic reasoning. Darwin showed that complex functional adaptation—apparent biological design—emerges through natural selection without any comprehending agent directing outcomes toward goals. Turing showed that behavior indistinguishable from intelligent comprehension can be produced by purely mechanistic computation lacking any internal understanding. Analyze the structural parallel between these two inversions: in each case, competence is decoupled from comprehension, but the explanatory direction differs. Then adjudicate the following tension: does evolutionary adaptation encode a form of tacit environmental knowledge, so that Darwin's case retains a minimal, distributed comprehension within the selecting environment, whereas Turing's machines remain purely syntactic and therefore entirely devoid of knowledge? Or are both cases equally and radically mindless? Ground your comparison in the logical form of each argument rather than in biographical or historical detail.
