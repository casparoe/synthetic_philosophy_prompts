While discussing Quine's critique of the analytic-synthetic distinction in a graduate seminar, one participant defended the boundary by comparing analytic truths to the rules of chess: they are true by virtue of how the pieces are defined, not by empirical investigation, and without them the game is unplayable. She argued that Quine's holism threatens to dissolve this boundary into a single "web of belief," which would be like insisting that chess rules are just one more move in a larger game of cultural evolution, thereby leaving us with no stable framework for logic or mathematics. A second participant countered with a different comparison: analytic statements are not like chess rules but like the tension calibrations on a weaving loom — fixed in place not by arbitrary stipulation, but because the entire pattern of the fabric depends on them remaining constant, yet fully revisable if the loom itself is rebuilt for a different purpose. He claimed this preserves Quine's revisability without collapsing logic into observation, since changing a calibration ripples through every thread rather than altering one isolated claim.

For each, provide the explicit correspondence table — what in the source domain maps onto analytic truths, synthetic truths, the act of revision, and the threat of collapse. Identify where each mapping breaks: which features of chess rules or loom calibrations are genuinely shared with the epistemic status Quine assigns to logical and mathematical statements, and which are quietly imported (for example, the social nature of games, the physical determinism of machinery, or the intentional design of both). Then determine whether either analogy actually supports the conclusions drawn — that is, whether the chess analogy genuinely protects the analytic-synthetic boundary from holism, and whether the loom analogy genuinely avoids the collapse that the first speaker feared, or whether both analogies are carrying conclusions they cannot bear. Finally, if neither holds up fully, suggest what a more accurate comparison would need to include about the relationship between central and peripheral beliefs in a holistic system.
