If Lewis's counterpart theory denies that any individual exists in more than one possible world, replacing transworld identity with a relation of similarity among world-bound counterparts, then a statement like "I could have become a musician" seems to attribute a modal property not to me but to a numerically distinct stranger. This appears to undermine what we might call modal autobiography: if I genuinely regret not having pursued music, my regret seems misdirected, since on this view there is no cross-world subject who both is me and plays the guitar. Is the mistake here in assuming that de re modal properties require a single four-dimensional entity stretching across worlds? Or does counterpart theory genuinely fail to preserve the first-personal indexical that distinguishes authentic modal self-concern from mere altruism toward a similar counterpart?
