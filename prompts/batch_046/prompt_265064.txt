The defender of transformation holds that these episodes involve prolonged adaptation, regional variability, and substantial continuity in practice, and that framing them as collapse imposes a modernist teleology of failure.

The document should contain:

1. A numbered section of agreed factual claims about the archaeological and demographic record across all three cases, avoiding the term collapse except where jointly defined, and making no appeal to authority or citation.

3. The cruxes: specifically, (a) whether institutional redundancy and administrative complexity are more diagnostic metrics than population loss or abandonment of monumental construction, and (b) whether a proposed threshold of legibility—the evidentiary point at which source sparsity prevents reliable distinction between rapid catastrophic failure and gradual restructuring—renders the collapse category empirically ungrounded for at least one of the three cases.

4. For each crux, one concrete hypothetical scenario or empirical pattern that both sides agree is relevant—such as a society that, within two generations, abandons long-distance exchange networks and monumental architecture but retains agricultural productivity, local ritual practice, and roughly eighty percent of its prior population density—together with each side's explicit prediction about what that case demonstrates.

5. A concluding assessment of whether any portion of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive, identifying which portion and explaining why.

Write the answer in formal American English.
