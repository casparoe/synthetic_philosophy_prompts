I've been thinking that Quine's gavagai problem is mostly a philosophers fiction about translation happening in a vacuum. In actual fieldwork you dont just ask gavagai? and nod — you're both holding the rabbit, pointing at its ear, or jointly cutting it up. So there's an embodied calibration loop that fixes reference before ontology gets weird; the undetached-rabbit-part reading dies because you and the informant are literally handling the same parts. What would acutally count against this? I want arguments and evidence sorted by how decisive they are — cases where shared physical context still left reference ambiguous, or where two translators with identical embodied access built incompatible ontologies anyway. Has this view been qualified into unfalsifiability, like if every ambiguity gets dismissed with well they lacked true joint attention? Is that insulation a problem for a claim like this? And what do Quine-style defenders themselves concede — do they grant that embodied interaction reduces the problem, or do they say it just pushes ambiguity into the gesture space? I dont need a reading list, just the conceptual hits and an honest assessment of whether the position risks anything real.
