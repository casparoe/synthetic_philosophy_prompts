I'm currently enrolled in an upper-division seminar on comparative civil-military relations, democratic citizenship, and the politics of neutrality. For a writing exercise on satire as conceptual critique, please produce the following.

Write a deadpan, bureaucratic FAQ document—approximately 700 words, formatted as numbered Q&A entries—published by the fictional Ministry of Civic Defense of a permanently neutral state. The document should address citizen inquiries about universal conscription, proposals to transition to an all-volunteer professional army, and the state's commitment to armed neutrality. Write in the flat, procedural voice of an official government information page.

The explicit target of the satire is the argument that conscription and armed neutrality are mutually reinforcing expressions of democratic equality: that every citizen must personally contribute to the state's collective refusal to fight, and that hiring professional soldiers creates a dangerous democratic deficit by allowing some citizens to outsource both risk and decision.

Follow that logic with bureaucratic precision rather than inventing unrelated absurdities. Include invented administrative mechanisms—such as "Defense Equity Credits" that citizens accumulate through service, "Neutrality Maintenance" assignments for conscientious objectors (e.g., border observation, diplomatic silence enforcement), and a "Professional Exemption Levy" calculated to offset the democratic deficit of hiring volunteer or mercenary forces. Use Finland's reservist-based territorial defense, Sweden's gender-neutral conscription reactivated in 2017, and Switzerland's militia system as comparative benchmarks that the ministry cites approvingly to justify its policies.

The satire should land real tensions visible in policy debates: the coercion inherent in universal service, the tension between military expertise and democratic representation, the economic and social costs of maintaining neutrality without alliance obligations, and the difficulty of sustaining broad societal buy-in for defense without either conscription or high taxation for professional forces. The reader should recognize that you understand these tensions and are exaggerating them, not misunderstanding them.

Keep the tone consistently deadpan: never wink, never break character, never state the irony explicitly. A specialist should see that the exaggeration is fair.

Do not include citations, bibliographies, reading lists, or requests for sources. Do not quote any real text or author verbatim; if you reference a specific idea or concept, paraphrase it without quotation marks. Do not ask the reader to look anything up. The document should stand on its own for a reader without web access.
