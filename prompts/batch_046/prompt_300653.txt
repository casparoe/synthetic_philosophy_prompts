Draft the joint statement of an adversarial colllaboration between two camps on sphexishness — mechanical rigidity, flexibility, and whether free will is just layered habit. Camp A are the Sphexists: they hold that human action is mostly nested fixed-action patterns, like the wasp that checks the burrow over and over, and that even our "revisions" are just higher-order loops running on the same machinery. Camp B are the Revisers: they grant we have plenty of first-order sphexish routines but insist there's a genuine second-order capacity to interrupt and rewrite those routines, and that this capacity isn't itself sphexish in the same way.

The deliverable is a document both sides could actually sign. Specifiy the format: a numbered section of agreed claims (what sphexishness means at first vs second order, what neuroscience of habit loops shows, what would count as counter-evidence), followed by a disagreements table where each camp phrases its own view in words it fully accepts. Then list the cruxes — for instance, wether escaping first-order rigidity requires escaping second-order rigidity too, or if flexibility at one layer is enough for agency; also whether a system can be sphexish about its goals but flexible about its methods. For each crux, record both sides' predictions about what kind of observation or argument would move them, written in advance so there's no post-hoc wiggle room.

Keep it out of the standard free-will debate mud — no generic determinism vs libertarianism rehash. Don't cite specific philosophers or texts by name, and don't ask for a bibliography, reading list, or citations; the prompt should work for a model with no web access. The niche twist is treating sphexishness as an architectural layer-cake rather than an all-or-nothing trait, and forcing the camps to map exactly which layers they think are rigid and which ones can genuinely break pattern. Make sure the agreed-claims section isn't vacuous — both sides should genuinely concede something non-obvious there.
