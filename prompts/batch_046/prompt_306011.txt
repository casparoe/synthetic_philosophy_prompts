Your argument should examine how this exclusion operates differently across the three contexts—the United Kingdom’s fusion of Crown and Parliament, the Scandinavian monarchies’ constitutionally entrenched but minimal reserve powers, and Japan’s post-war symbolic sovereignty—while maintaining that in each case the monarch’s inability to claim popular representation serves a stabilising function during democratic crises or transitions. To render the position distinctive, inject and defend the claim that this stabilising effect is not merely symbolic or cultural but structurally analogous to the role of a constitutional court that lacks direct enforcement power: both derive influence from the very absence of coercive or electoral authority. After presenting the core argument, address the objection that an elected ceremonial president could replicate the same function, and explain why the hereditary principle—precisely because it is non-meritocratic and non-elective—blocks a specific form of populist capture that tends to threaten appointed or elected figureheads.
