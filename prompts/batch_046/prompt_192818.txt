I'm trying to work out my own position on whether special relativity forces a block-universe ontology, by testing my reactions to specific scenarios. Here are my verdicts.

First, two inertial observers passing each other: each computes that a distant supernova is happening "now" in their rest frame, while the other computes it lies ten years in their past or future. My verdict: both simultaneity judgments are correct relative to a frame, but neither reveals a metaphysically privileged present. So if presentism claims only present things exist, and "present" is frame-relative, presentism makes existence frame-relative, which I reject.

Second, the classic argument that this frame-dependence entails eternalism: since every event is present-relative-to-some-frame, every event is equally real. My verdict: this moves too quickly. I accept that there is no global hyperplane of simultaneity, but I do not automatically accept that all events therefore share the same ontological status.

Third, the twin paradox: one twin travels near light speed and returns to find the stay-at-home twin older. My verdict: the age gap is physically real, not a coordinate illusion, yet it does not establish that one twin's successive "now" slices are the true ones. The asymmetry is dynamical, not ontological.

Fourth, my own future decision: tomorrow I will either submit a manuscript or withdraw it. This event lies within my future light cone, ordered invariantly by my proper time. My verdict: unlike the spacelike-separated supernova, I genuinely feel this outcome is not yet fixed or real. The openness seems tied to my causal future, not to a global present.

Fifth, a distant observer can choose a frame in which my tomorrow decision is simultaneous with an event they take as present, or even with an event they take as past, without violating causality. My verdict: this makes me uneasy about treating local openness as metaphysically basic. If the decision's unreality depends only on its position along my worldline, why should a change of distant coordinates not affect its status?

So I have a tension: I want to deny global presentism because simultaneity is frame-relative, yet I want to preserve local openness for events in my causal future, even though those events can be relocated across present and past by distant frame choices.

Is there a coherent principle that captures all five verdicts? Please separate what is settled physics from what is genuinely contested philosophy, and do not present a contested metaphysical interpretation as the consensus. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
