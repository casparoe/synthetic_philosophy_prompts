Explain the conceptual tension between Margaret Gilbert's account of joint commitment and Michael Bratman's analysis of shared cooperative activity when applied to hybrid human-AI collaborative systems in which the artificial partner lacks phenomenal consciousness. Specifically, does the absence of first-person plural phenomenology in the machine undermine the claim that the pair constitutes a plural subject capable of bearing joint obligations, or can the normative structure of joint commitment be sustained purely through functional interdependence and mutual accountability mechanisms? Address whether any essential feature of joint action is genuinely lost when one partner is a large language model that processes linguistic tokens without semantic understanding, and clarify where the conceptual dispute is genuinely about ontology versus where it is merely verbal.
