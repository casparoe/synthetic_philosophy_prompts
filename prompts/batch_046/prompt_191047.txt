Write an essay on the following controversy. Ian Hacking’s account of looping effects holds that psychiatric classifications are not passive descriptions but interactive kinds: once a category is institutionalised, those classified may alter their self-understanding and behaviour in response to it, thereby changing the very phenomenon the category was meant to capture. A prominent line of reply focuses on the shift from categorical to dimensional diagnosis, particularly the Alternative Model for Personality Disorders in DSM-5-TR. Proponents of this reply argue that replacing discrete disorder labels with continuous trait spectra removes the specific, nameable identity that patients could internalise and perform, thereby breaking the feedback loop. Critics counter that dimensional ratings do not escape looping but transform it: rather than adopting a categorical label, individuals may engage in finer-grained self-calibration against metric thresholds and percentile norms, producing a more diffuse, pervasive form of self-modification that Hacking’s original framework does not adequately describe.

Weigh these positions with care. Assess whether dimensional classification genuinely disrupts looping effects in personality disorder diagnosis, or whether it intensifies them in a subtler, more granular form. Reach and defend a definite conclusion—whether dimensional diagnosis substantially neutralises looping, transforms it into something more insidious, or produces a mixed outcome that requires revising Hacking’s framework—and justify that verdict with clear reasoning. Do not finish on a non-committal note.

Please write your answer in British English, and adopt a committed, decisive tone throughout: state your position explicitly and argue for it, rather than laying out the options and leaving the judgement to the reader.
