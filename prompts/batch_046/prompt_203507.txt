Write an essay on whether a society should institutionalize norms of public celebration for exceptional success—the American Dream ethos—or norms that suppress visible superiority, as in Jantelagen and the tall poppy syndrome. Do not rely on empirical claims about innovation or social cohesion. Instead, center your argument on the moral significance of desert under conditions of deep contingency: if success depends heavily on brute luck, does that erode the justification for awarding distinctive social recognition, or does recognition remain a legitimate reward for effort and choice regardless of background luck? Further, consider whether cutting down the successful serves equality of status or merely enforces a false equality of condition. Reach and defend a definite conclusion—either that meritocratic celebration is morally justified despite luck, or that egalitarian suppression of distinction is required—grounding your verdict in what people are owed in the currency of recognition rather than material goods.
