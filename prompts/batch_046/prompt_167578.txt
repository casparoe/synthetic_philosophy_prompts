Consider the philosophical framework of real patterns together with Dennett’s three stances—the physical stance, the design stance, and the intentional stance—particularly as they apply to the ontology of emergent structures in complex adaptive systems. Rather than asking for a survey of existing literature, I want a defended, dependency-aware research agenda for conceptual philosophy of science over the next five years, focused on the problem of what I will call “intermediate stances”: patterns that appear to carry genuine predictive and explanatory weight yet do not cleanly reduce to design-level functions or to intentional-level beliefs and desires.

Your task is to identify three to four open conceptual questions in this space—for instance, whether intermediate patterns require a novel ontological commitment; how they interact with the standard criteria for real patternhood (predictive utility, compressibility, and projectibility across contexts); whether they undermine the exclusivity of the three-stance taxonomy or can be accommodated within it; and what implications they carry for model-dependent explanation in the philosophy of science—and rank them in strict priority for a small research group with limited time and no empirical laboratory.

For each question, please explain:
- its position in the dependency structure: which questions are upstream of which, and how settling one would constrain or dissolve the others;
- why it is tractable for conceptual philosophers working without empirical data, and what conceptual tools (e.g., analysis of explanation, ontology of levels, or rational choice theory) are relevant;
- what specific theoretical result or clarification over the next few years would cause you to revise its ranking;
- and why any lower-ranked question should not displace it, given the group’s explicit aim of clarifying the ontology of scientific explanation rather than solving empirical puzzles.

Be explicit about the criteria governing your ranking—foundational importance for the stance taxonomy, tractability with current philosophical methods, degree of neglect in existing work, and downstream consequences for the philosophy of mind and science. Do not provide a bibliography, reading list, or citations; I am interested solely in the reasoning, the ordering, and the agenda.
