I sit on the IRB for our med school's sleep-research unit. A PI wants to run a trial for a new hypnotic: subjects get sedated Sunday, then by hidden coin flip either woken once Monday (heads) or woken Monday, re-sedated, and woken again Tuesday (tails). At each awakening they rate their credence that the coin landed heads — so they have to update on self-locating evidence without knowing if it's Monday or Tuesday. The PI frames this as testing Sleeping-Beauty-style belief updating in vivo, and wants the consent form to use 'centered-world' language (talking about 'this awakening' and 'the present center' rather than calendar days) to avoid giving away the day. Half our board thinks thats just philosophical obfuscation that undermines informed consent; the other half thinks it's the only honest way to describe what subjects actually experience. Draft our decision memo — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with the bottom line clear and the best argument for the other side treated honestly. No need for references, just lock the reasoning down so the PI can respond point by point. (Also there's a typo in the protocol: they wrote 'credance' everywhere instead of credence — mention whether that matters for consent validity.)
