Notes:
Von Neumann held that the brain is not a digital computer in any straightforward sense. Neural signaling is analog and statistical, operating through threshold dynamics rather than precise binary logic, and reliability comes from massive redundancy among unreliable components rather than from error-free parts. From an engineering standpoint, this sounds like an anticipation of deep learning: we implement statistical, analog-style computation on digital hardware, and we use techniques like dropout to enforce robustness through redundancy.

I also want to connect this to von Neumann's theory of self-reproducing automata. He described a machine capable of reading its own blueprint and constructing a functional copy. I see a loose parallel in neural network training: gradient descent updates weights so that the model becomes a compressed, functional reproduction of patterns in its training data. In other words, the network "reads" data and rewrites itself to replicate that structure.

Still, von Neumann emphasized that the brain's logic is not Boolean. I worry that I am committing a category error by treating informational pattern matching (learning weights) as equivalent to physical self-replication (automata building copies). I also wonder whether attributing dropout-style redundancy to von Neumann misrepresents his statistical argument, since he was describing biological tolerance for noise rather than a regularization strategy.

1. Whether my characterization of von Neumann's brain-computer distinction is accurate.
