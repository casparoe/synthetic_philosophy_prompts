I take the view that archaeologists can reconstruct institutional beliefs—property norms, religious hierarchy, or legal obligations—directly from the spatial distribution of artifacts and built modifications, without textual corroboration, on the grounds that material culture encodes normative structures directly rather than merely symbolizing them. What would genuinely count against this? I am looking for decisive counter-examples where object patterns systematically misled interpreters about underlying institutions, methodological findings that sever the link between material pattern and belief, or theoretical results showing that such reconstruction necessarily smuggles in present-day assumptions; please rank these from fatal to merely awkward. Include an honest judgment of whether defenders can qualify the view into unfalsifiability, and whether that insulation is a defect for a claim of this kind. Please answer in British English.
