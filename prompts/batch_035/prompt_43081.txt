At a public debate I went to about sex work law (the big question was whether we should go the New Zealand decriminalization route or the Swedish/Nordic route), one of the panelists kept coming back to the line "punish the buyer, not the seller" every time someone raised concerns about sex workers' safety under the Nordic model. It sounded good and everyone clapped, but the more I think about it the less sure I am what it's actually claiming. Is it a description of the Nordic laws as they exist, a moral claim about who's at fault in the transaction, a prediction about what happens to the trade when you choke demand, or just a framing move that decides in advance who counts as the victim? Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that could be packed into that one sentence, from the modest to the strong, and for each one say whether its plausible and whether it actually does the work the panelist needed it to do -- answering the safety objection and showing the Nordic model beats decriminalization? Also, I feel like "not the seller" might quietly imply that under the Nordic model sellers face no legal risk or downsides at all, and something tells me thats not quite right either (third party offences, landlords, working together indoors, that kind of thing), but I dont know enough to say for sure. Please be concrete about which readings are just true by definition, which are empirical claims people fight over, and which are normative takes being smuggled in.
