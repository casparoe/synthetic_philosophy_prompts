I've been teaching the survival unit for years and I still can't get my own gut reactions into line, so help me out. Where I land on the cases: the teletransporter that scans, destroys, and rebuilds me on Mars — I say that's me, I'd step in without much fuss. Same machine, except the original isn't destroyed for an hour, so for an hour there are two of me walking around, and only then the Earth-copy is painlessly disintegrated — suddenly I'm far less sure the Mars one is me, which is annoying because the two scenarios differ only in timing. A surgeon replaces my neurons with silicon duplicates, one per week, with me awake and chatting through the whole procedure: me, no question. The same replacement done while I'm in a coma: also me, but I feel the pull less. My hemispheres are split and each transplanted into a different body, both waking up with my memories and personality: I want to say both are me, but identity is one-one, so that can't be right, and I can't bring myself to say neither is me either. And a treatment that keeps my body running but resets my psychology to what I had at age eight: I say that's death as surely as a car crash. Is there some principle about what actually matters in survival that captures all of these, and if not, which of my verdicts should go? Please also say what the principle implies for the case I keep flip-flopping on: the hemisphere split, but where only one hemisphere takes and the other is discarded — am I then the one, and does it matter whether the other hemisphere *could* have taken?
