I write historical fiction, so I do alot of interviews with people who lived through the periods I'm writing about, and I keep hitting something I can't name. When someone tells me how they came to leave the country just before the coup, the whole account runs like every step was obviously heading toward that ending — and when I push back ("but your letters from that summer show you were mostly worried about a job, you barely mention politics"), they don't concede, they insist they definately remember knowing it was coming. I don't think they're lying to me; my sense is the memory itself got rewritten somewhere along the way, so the certainty feels like it was always there. And I get the same thing from the published histories of that period: the decade reads like the coup was baked in from the start, every little fact placed as if it were evidence of the ending. So my question: is there an established concept that covers this — not the tidy story told afterwards for effect, but the sincere feeling of having known at the time, the rewriting of what one remembers having believed? I've heard people say hindsight bias, but that sounds like it's about judging events after the fact, not about misremembering your own past mental state. If nothing established covers it exactly, coin a term for it, and tell me where its edges are — how it differs from ordinary confabulation, from someone deliberately editing their story to look prescient, and from the historian's version of the same move. I don't just want a label, I want to know what the phenomenon is and isn't.
