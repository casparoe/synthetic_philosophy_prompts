Please state your credence that eternalism is true, where eternalism is: past, present, and future events are all equally real — Caesar's crossing of the Rubicon exists in exactly the tenseless sense in which today's weather does, and so does next year's election — and where the alternatives are presentism (only present things exist) and the growing block (present and past things exist, future ones do not). Take 'exist' in the plain quantificational sense, so that the question cannot be dissolved by saying the camps merely use different quantifiers or different meanings of 'now'. Then decompose your number into at least the following terms, giving a rough probability and one line of justification for each: (1) the probability that the argument from the relativity of simultaneity — the Rietdijk–Putnam line, on which observers in relative motion disagree about which distant events are simultaneous, so that what is 'now' cannot be an objective feature of the world — is sound, such that special relativity leaves no room for presentism unless one reintroduces a preferred foliation along neo-Lorentzian lines, and that such a reintroduction is ad hoc rather than a legitimate empirical possibility; (2) the probability that the felt passage of time can be explained away as a feature of experience rather than of the world, which the eternalist needs and the presentist claims is impossible; (3) the probability that presentism is internally coherent at all, given that it wants claims like 'there were dinosaurs' to be true while denying that there is anything those claims are about; and (4) a verbal-disagreement discount — the probability that once everything is precisely formulated, the residual dispute between eternalist and presentist collapses into a difference of emphasis rather than a disagreement about how the world is. Combine the terms however you think they should combine, show the combination, and tell me which of them your final number is most sensitive to. Finally, say what argument, observation, or development would move your number most, and address one specific edge: if future fundamental physics were to reveal a physically preferred foliation of spacetime, should that be counted as positive evidence for presentism, or merely as removing the strongest current argument against it — and does that distinction between support and mere permission matter to how you weighted term (1)?
