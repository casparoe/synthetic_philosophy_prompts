My colleague and I have been arguing about luck egalitarianism — roughly, the view that justice requires neutralizing inequalities traceable to brute luck while permitting those traceable to informed choice — and we've agreed to make our disagreement explicit in numbers. She puts her credence at 0.75 that luck egalitarianism, in a Dworkin-style formulation with hypothetical insurance against brute luck, is at bottom the correct principle of distributive justice; I put mine at 0.25, mostly because of cases like the gambler who knew the odds, declined available insurance, and bet away his income — yet who turns out to carry a gene variant strongly predisposing him to pathological gambling, so that his "choice" is causally entangled with brute luck in a way the theory struggles to classify. Please give me your own credence, decomposed: assign rough probabilities to (i) distributive justice being responsibility-sensitive at all, as against relational-egalitarian views on which what matters is the quality of social relations rather than the luck/choice pedigree of inequalities; (ii) the option-luck/brute-luck line being drawable non-arbitrarily given that preferences and dispositions are themselves products of unchosen formation; and (iii) luck egalitarianism being able to handle the abandonment objection — the badly-off informed chooser left destitute — without smuggling back a responsibility-blind safety net. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: first your overall credence and how the sub-questions combine to it; then one item per sub-question with its probability and a one-line justification; and finally the sub-question your number is most sensitive to, noting whether that is also where my colleague and I most likely disagree.
