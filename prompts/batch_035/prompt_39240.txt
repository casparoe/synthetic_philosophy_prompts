I'm a grad student presenting at our departmental colloquium next month, defending Nozick's tracking theory against the epistemic closure objection. The talk's line, roughly: Nozick was right that the skeptic's argument only goes through if knowledge is closed under known entailment, and right to deny closure rather than deny that we know ordinary things -- but I want to push further and argue that denying closure isn't the ad hoc bullet-bite everyone treats it as, because the sensitivity condition itself explains why knowledge fails to transmit across entailment exactly when the entailed proposition is one you'd believe whether or not it was true. The audience is a department of mainstream epistemologists: two or three contextualists who think the whole closure puzzle is best dissolved by shifting standards, one guy who's basically a Moorean (I know I have hands, therefore I know I'm not a handless brain in a vat, end of story), and a Bayesian who thinks counterfactual conditions on knowledge are hand-wavy folk epistemology and will ask why I'm not just talking credences the whole time. Nobody there is a card-carrying Nozickian, so no backup from the audience.

Prepare my Q&A prep sheet: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way an epistemologist would actually put it. Someone will definately wave around the abominable conjunction -- that I can know I have hands, know that having hands entails not being a handless BIV, and still not know I'm not a handless BIV -- and I need a reply to that one which doesn't sound like I'm just shrugging. For each objection give me: a reply I can deliver in under two minutes, a note on where the reply is soft, and whether I should concede anything up front. Also flag which objections come from the contextualists vs. the Moorean vs. the Bayesian, since the replies have to differ. And please tell me whether they're likely to raise the insensitive-knowledge counterexamples (the grandmother case, where she knows her grandson is well but would have been told he's fine even if he were sick), since if so I need to know how bad that actually is for me.

Finally, include the question I'm hoping nobody asks -- you know the one -- and a quick note on what to do if it comes anyway.
