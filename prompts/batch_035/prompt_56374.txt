My brother-in-law Rob has extremely firm opinions about what counts as dishonest, and my sister and I have a long-running bet about whether there's an same rule behind all his rulings or whether he's just going with his gut every time. I've been keeping notes for like three years, and Thanksgiving is when we finally settle it, because I get to pick the scenarios this year. Some background that probably matters: Rob sells real estate, and he got burned once by a business partner who lied to his face, so he's touchy about actual lying.

Here's the track record, his rulings on stuff that came up:

1. A guy at his old office ate someone's clearly labeled lunch and then denied it when asked. Rob: wrong, open and shut.
2. Same office, different incident: someone asked a coworker "did you finish the regular coffee?" and the guy said "I only drink the cold brew" — true, as far as it goes, but he'd taken the last of the regular that morning. Rob: fine, that's just office politics.
3. A guy selling a used road bike on Marketplace talks up the new tires and the recent tune-up and says nothing about the hairline crack in the frame that he definitately knows about. Buyer never asks. Rob: fine, buyer beware, that's what inspections are for.
4. Someone at a party asks after Rob's estranged brother and he says "he's keeping busy." He says that's not just fine, it's the polite thing to do.
5. A local business owner promised to match donations up to $500 at a school fundraiser just so they'd announce it, then never paid and never intended to. Rob: wrong, that's lying, full stop.
6. A guy wears scrub tops because they're comfortable; people at parties assume he's a nurse and he never corrects anyone, and when finally asked point blank he said "I've always been drawn to medicine." Rob: fine, what's he supposed to do, hand out a disclaimer?
7. Rob's friend got pulled over, cop asks if he's been drinking, and the friend says "I had one with dinner" — true, but he'd also had five at the bar after dinner. Rob: fine, if the cop actually wants to know he can do the test.

So now my three scenarios for Thanksgiving, and I want you to help me predict his calls before I spring them:

A. A financial advisor whose client asks "is this fund a good place for my retirement money?" — the advisor answers "it's had strong returns five years running," which is true, while knowing the star manager just left and there's an inquiry the client has no way to hear about.

B. A patient about to have surgery who never asks about the specific surgeon; the doctor volunteers that "complication rates at this hospital are low" — also true — while knowing this particular surgeon's own numbers are way above average.

C. You meet a hiker on a trail with no signal who's heading toward a washed-out footbridge. She asks how the trail ahead is and you say "the crews were out there all summer" — true — because you want the trail to yourself. No map on her, dark in two hours.

Predict his verdict on each. Then lay out the rules that actually fit the track record (there's more than one, I'm pretty sure), say which of my scenarios split them and which are wasted questions, give me your best guess at the rule he's running and why, and tell me what extra scenario I should bring as a tiebreaker if he rules the way you don't expect. My sister gets to hear your reasoning so no cheating — she'll spot it if my scenarios are rigged.
