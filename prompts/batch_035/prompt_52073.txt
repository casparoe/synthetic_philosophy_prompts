I teach philosophy of language and have been invited to contribute a position statement to a retrospective volume on Putnam's Twin Earth cases. Rather than write it as though my views formed a natural whole, I want to find out first whether they do. Here is the package as I actually hold it:

(1) Semantic externalism about natural kind terms. In 1750, when no one on either planet knew any chemistry, Earth's Oscar and Twin Earth's Twin Oscar were alike in every respect of the head, yet Oscar's word "water" referred to H₂O and Twin Oscar's to XYZ. The difference in what they meant was not a difference in their psychology.

(2) In that scenario the two twins are psychologically indistinguishable. Indeed, that is the datum that drives me to externalism in the first place: sameness of psychology together with difference of content shows that content outruns psychology.

(3) First-person authority about one's own thoughts. I know what I mean and what I believe without investigating the world: without any chemistry, I can know that in thinking there is water in the glass I am thinking about water.

(4) The belief-desire scheme of explanation is causal. The twins' identical behavior — reaching for the clear liquid, uttering the local word — is to be explained by the very same beliefs and desires in each twin.

(5) Meaning is fixed by use. Whatever semantics turns out to be, it should be grounded in patterns of use: understanding "water" is mastery of a practice, not possession of a hidden essence.

Please audit this package for me. Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which merely look to be; rank the real tensions by severity rather than flagging everything as equally bad; for each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the revision would cost me; and identify the single cheapest change that would restore consistency. I honestly do not know where the trouble lies, and I would much rather hear it from you than from a commentator once the volume appears.
