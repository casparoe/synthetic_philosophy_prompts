So I just got back from a pub argument with my friend Theo that lasted way too long about whether dreams are experiences, and I need someone to tell me how much trouble my position is actually in.

Here's where I landed. Theo was pushing the obvious view: dreaming is a form of experience, full stop, you're lying there asleep having quasi-hallucinations and (sometimes) quasi-thoughts, and then you wake up and remember some of it. I started playing devil's advocate and then realised I'd talked myself into actually believing the other side: that maybe dreams are not experiences you *have* while asleep at all. The idea I half-remember from Dennett (he had a go at this in the 70s, and again later with his cassette thing) is that the brain doesn't record a dream that then gets replayed — instead, when something wakes you up, the brain composes the "dream" content at that moment, on the spot, along with the feeling that you've just been having it, and you confabulate the memory of a whole experience that never actually occurred. And behind that there's the older Malcolm-style point that dream reports can't be evidence for dreams-as-experiences, because the only check on the report is the report itself.

Theo thinks this is insane. His best shot, which I couldn't answer, was lucid dreaming: he says he's had dreams where he *knew* he was dreaming, and that there's actual research where lucid dreamers apparently signalled to researchers from inside the dream using pre-agreed eye movements, and even answered simple maths questions in real time. If a sleeping person can receive a question and respond from inside the dream, how is the dream not happening as an experience during sleep? I mumbled something about confabulation being fast and about the waking brain retroactively dating content, but honestly I was winging it.

So here's what I want from you. Take my position seriously — dreams are not experiences had during sleep, they're composed at waking or at recall — and tell me what would genuinely count against it. Not just "but it feels like I dream", I want the real defeaters: which experimental results, which arguments, which possible cases would a fair-minded defender of my view have to concede, and which would they shrug off as compatible with it (and would that shrugging be legitimate or just motivated reasoning)? Does the lucid dreaming stuff actually sink the view or can it be absorbed? Rank the threats from decisive through merely awkward. And be honest about whether the position has been qualified so much that nothing could ever disconfirm it — and if so, whether that's fatal for a view of this kind, especially given that Theo's naive view might be equally insulated in the other direction.

One thing that matters to me: please seperate what's actually settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this (philosophers of mind and sleep researchers alike). If the received view nowadays leans towards dreams being experiences, say so plainly, but if that's still a live debate rather than consensus, don't paper over it — I don't want contested claims served to me as established fact. Also, and this is petty but whatever, please write your whole answer in British English (behaviour, colour, realise, that sort of thing) — I'm not arguing with Theo again only to have him mock my spelling as well as my metaphysics.
