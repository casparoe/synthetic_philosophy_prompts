Quick background so this makes sense: I'm a retired atmospheric scientist, spent most of my career building numerical models of the climate system, and only in the last few years have I gotten properly into philosophy of history. Forgive me if I reach to hard for physics analogies, it's a professional habit at this point.

Here's what's bugging me. I've been reading the literature on hindsight bias and the narrative fallacy — the stuff about how we impose story-shaped order on the past, flatten the contingency, and then feel like we knew all along how things had to turn out. And the standard critique keeps saying that narratives "compress" the past, like compression itself is the sin. But that would never fly in my old line of work. A good compression is exactly what a good model is: we were thrilled whenever we could represent a climate run with fewer parameters without losing the predictive structure. If a narrative about, say, how a war started compresses the events and the compression is genuinely lossless with respect to causal structure, that's not a bias, that's understanding. Nobody calls thermodynamics a fallacy because it compresses a trillion molecular collisions.

So my suspicion is this: the narrative fallacy isn't about compression at all. It's that narrative compression is lossy in a specific direction — and the loss function isn't chosen by predictive success but by whether the resulting story hangs together as a story, where "hangs together" gets judged with the ending already known. The hindsight bias sneaks in through the objective function, not through the compression. Which would mean story-shaped explanation as such is innocent, and the actual problem is a mis-specified loss function imposed on an otherwise legitimate compression.

Please make the argument for this position as rigorously as you can. And I want you to take seriously the objection I anticipate from the philosophers of history: that choosing what counts as "causal structure worth preserving" is itself the narrative move, so my lossless/lossy distinction presupposes an independent fact of the matter about causes that history maybe doesn't have. That objection is where I suspect my whole framing falls apart, so don't wave it off — either answer it or concede what has to be conceded and show what survives.
