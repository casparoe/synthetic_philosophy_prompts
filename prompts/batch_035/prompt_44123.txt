So I edit a small satirical newsletter for our philosophy department (like 200 subscribers, mostly ethicists plus a couple of psychologists who wandered in somehow). Last month's parody — an IKEA-style assembly manual for Kantian ethics — went over pretty well, and for the next issue I want to do something on the situationist critique of virtue ethics. My idea is a parody of a consumer product recall notice, the kind a manufacturer puts out ("FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE... voluntary recall... affected models... hazard description... remedy"), except the product being recalled is a character trait. Like Compassion (Global Model, all model years) being recalled after field reports of intermittent failure to aid when other bystanders are present, or Honesty failing under exactly the circumstances where those 1920s studies found schoolkids cheat on some tests but not others — no general trait, just a bunch of local ones. I want every joke to come from a real finding rather than an invented one, so the hazard descriptions should track the actual literature: the hurried-seminary study, the dime in the phone booth, bystander apathy, cheating turning out to be wildly context-specific. Someone who knows the Doris/Harman stuff should nod along instead of wince.

One thing I insist on: the manufacturer's statement in the notice has to be the genuine virtue-ethicist comeback played completely straight. So a spokesperson insisting the trait was never engineered for single-shot performances in front of strangers, that the testing regime (one-off situations, people who've never met) is at fault rather than the product, that anyone who expected a stable global trait misread the spec sheet — and maybe a line where consumers who ignore the recall notice are told they're displaying precisely the disposition under investigation. That rebuttal should feel fair to the virtue ethicists, not like I'm strawmanning them, even though the whole piece is quietly on the situationists' side.

Format it like a real recall notice — header, affected models, hazard description, remedy, what owners should do — around 500-600 words, and keep it deadpan the whole way, no winking at the reader. Someone who's never heard of situationism should still be able to tell something is being sent up. Feel free to make the "remedy" section the bleakest part, my co-editor definately wants that.
