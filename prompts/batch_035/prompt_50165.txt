Over the weekend my sister Rosa and her boyfriend Jonah had a two-hour argument at my place and I couldn't follow who was winning. The trigger: our friend Priya is badly allergic to cats, but she takes a strong antihistamine before visiting her girlfriend, who has two of them, and when she's there she pets them and doesn't react at all. I mentioned this offhand while making coffee and it escalated fast.

Rosa (philosophy MA, loves this stuff) said it's actually simple: being allergic just IS being such that you'd react if you got exposed. Priya got exposed — dander and everything — and didn't react, so right now, with the pill on board, she isn't allergic; she has the underlying sensitivity, sure, but the disposition itself is gone, and calling her "allergic" at that moment is just sloppy. Jonah said that was ridiculous: she's allergic, that's the whole reason she takes the pill, the pill masks the allergy without removing it — like how a painkiller doesn't make you not-injured. He offered a parallel he thought was airtight: a vial of nitroglycerin carried in a padded, shock-absorbing case is still explosive even though nothing happens when you jostle the case. Rosa said the cases come apart: the padding keeps the jolt from ever reaching the vial, so the vial never even gets stimulated, whereas the cat dander genuinely gets into Priya's system and the reaction is blocked further downstream — stimulus happens, manifestation doesn't — so the nitro keeps its disposition and Priya, right now, doesn't. Jonah said that was a distinction without a difference: in both cases the world is arranged so the bad thing won't happen, and whether you block the signal at the front door or in the hallway can't matter to whether the danger is still there. Rosa muttered something about how there's supposedly a whole literature on exactly this and Jonah said "the literature can be wrong too."

Then it got even more tangled, because Jonah brought up the wiring in my building: apparently there's a fast breaker such that if you touched a live wire, the current would be cut in milliseconds and you'd never feel a shock. He said everyone would agree the wire is still live and dangerous, so Rosa's "what would actually happen" standard is broken by her own lights. Rosa wouldn't concede — she said the breaker case might be different AGAIN, because the intervention there removes the very property that makes the wire dangerous (the current) at the moment you touch it, whereas the pill leaves Priya's sensitivity fully intact and only blocks the output. So by the end she was hinting there are three different kinds of case and they'd been sloppily sliding between them, while Jonah insisted all three are the same thing: something standing between a trigger and a disaster, full stop.

Please adjudicate the exchange move by move — who's right at each step, including the steps where they talked past each other. Then take a position on the question the whole thing turned on: are the antihistamine case, the padded vial, and the breaker genuinely different in kind, or is Jonah right that blocking anywhere in the chain is the same? And say what, if anything, both of them were missing. Please actually land somewhere rather than telling me it all depends.
