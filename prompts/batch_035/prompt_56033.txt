I'm drafting a position statement for a political theory seminar on religion and democratic legitimacy, and before I submit it I want a consistency check. My advisor said the package looked like it was held together by my policy preferences rather than by principle, which stung, so I'd like to know whether he's right. Here are the commitments:

1. The liberal principle of legitimacy: exercises of state coercion are legitimate only if they can be justified to citizens as free and equal, in terms they cannot reasonably reject.
2. The constraint has wide scope. It governs not only the official justifications offered for laws but, at least as an ideal, the reasons citizens properly bring to political advocacy and voting.
3. I reject the translation proviso. There is no general duty to translate religious arguments into secular ones before offering them in public debate — Wolterstorff's fairness objection to Rawls and Audi on this strikes me as roughly right, since anything else alienates religious citizens from their own consciences.
4. A legislator votes to abolish the death penalty, saying and sincerely believing that her only reason is that every person bears the image of God. I think this is entirely unproblematic: no violation of public reason.
5. A legislator votes to restrict marriage to opposite-sex couples, citing only scripture. I think this is a clear violation, and the resulting statute lacks legitimacy.
6. A law's legitimacy depends on the reasons actually operative in its adoption, not on the mere availability of some adequate rationale in the background. Public rationales floating around a society can't launder whatever the majority really believed.
7. Methodologically, I hold that political philosophy must proceed from actual, historically situated societies and their real disagreements. I distrust the idealization of reasonable persons, and I'm skeptical that there is any substantive set of reasons all reasonable citizens actually share.

Please audit the set rather than survey the literature. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, and how badly; which only look like they are, and why; for each real tension, what would have to give and what it costs; and finally, which single revision would clean up the most. I don't have a settled view about which commitment I'd sacrifice, so don't assume I do — and don't rank the tensions as equally severe just to be safe.
