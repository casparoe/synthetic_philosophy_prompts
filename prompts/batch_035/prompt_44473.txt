So I've been reading rationalist blogs since roughly the Overcoming Bias days, which means I've fully absorbed the "introspection is confabulation all the way down" line — split-brain patients inventing reasons for what the other hemisphere did, choice blindness studies where people defend choices they didn't actually make, the whole Nisbett and Wilson telling-more-than-we-can-know thing. And lately I keep wondering what credence I should actually put on that rather than just nodding along, because the whole rationalist project (noticing your confusion, noticing you're noticing, etc.) kinda assumes you can sometimes look at your own reasoning as it happens.

So here's the question, stated as precisely as I can: give me your credence that at least half of the introspective reports a typical adult makes about the causes of their own decisions and beliefs are broadly accurate — not perfect, but tracking the real causes rather than confabulated after the fact — and decompose the thing properly instead of just giving me a vibe-based number.

At minimum I want these terms in the decomposition, plus whatever else you think belongs:

(a) The probability that the confabulation findings generalize beyond weird lab tasks — pointing at which quadrant of a screen flashed, or inventing reasons for preferring one photo over another — to everyday decisions like why you took a job or ended a friendship. Lab introspection failure might just mean those tasks are the ones where we never had introspective access in the first place.

(b) The probability that introspection is even a single faculty whose reliability can be averaged out, versus a grab bag of different capacities — noticing bodily sensations, reporting occurrent thoughts, diagnosing your own motives — with wildly different track records. If it's a grab bag, "at least half of reports are accurate" might be true of some and hopeless for others.

(c) The probability that "tracking the actual causes" is well-defined at all here, given we never get to check introspection against ground truth, only against other fallible reports and behavior. Maybe the question is partly verbal and you should say how much that deflates your number versus just making you grumpy about the framing.

(d) The probability that self-fulfilling introspection saves the day — that believing you did something because of X sometimes makes X the actual cause, so the report was wrong about the causal order when made but right about the end state.

Give me a number and one line of justification for each, combine them however you think is right (and say why that way), and then tell me which term you're least confident in. Last thing: don't tell me "more confabulation research would help" — tell me what specific kind of finding would move which term and roughly by how much. I want to know where my number is actually load-bearing.
