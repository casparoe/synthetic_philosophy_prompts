So heres my situation: I spent about thirty-five years doing population genetics on beetles before retiring, and now that I finally have time to read properly I've fallen down the rabbit hole of the units of selection literature. What started as gene vs. individual vs. group arguments in the 60s and 70s seems to have turned into this sprawling thing — replicators vs. vehicles, interactors, MLS vs. kin selection bookkeeping, the whole "is it all just accounting, like the same spreadsheet in different fonts" question. Some of it strikes me as genuinely important, some of it as forty years of people talking past each other, and I'm honestly not sure which parts are which anymore.

If you could put together a small working group — say one philosopher of biology, one theoretical evolutionary biologist, and a sharp grad student — and give them two or three years on the open questions in this debate, which would you tell them to work on, in what order, and why? For each one I'd want the actual reasoning: does anything downstream in biology or philosophy hinge on how it gets resolved, or is it self-contained? Is it tractable with the tools those folks actually have, or does it secretly require data nobody can get? Is it neglected, or is half the field already on it? A couple of things I'd especially like your take on: (a) whether the whole gene-selectionism vs. multilevel-selection framing dispute is basically settled-as-equivalent and people should just move on, or whether there's still a substantive fight left there; (b) whether the applied spinoffs are real or hype — e.g., does getting clear on units of selection actually matter for virulence management in pathogens, or for treating tumors as evolving populations of cells where the "group" is the patient's body, or is that just people borrowing the debate's prestige for grant applications; and (c) whether ecosystem- or community-level selection, including the microbiome stuff, is a serious extension of the framework or a category error dressed up as a research program. And tell me what sort of development over the next few years — a conceptual result, a case study, something empirical — would make you reshuffle the priority list.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restating my question back at me, and no reading list — I want your ordering and your justification, not a bibliography.
