I've been recording my gut verdicts on which decisions belong to a person alone and which may be made collectively — by a household, a co-op, a town — and I'd like to know whether a single principle underlies them, or whether some verdict has to go. The verdicts: (1) My neighbor paints her house a color I find hideous: her call, full stop; a street vote against it would be out of line. (2) My building votes to ban smoking inside individual units, since it seeps through the vents: legitimate, and I'd help enforce it. (3) A town bans residents from keeping three hens in tidy, silent coops because most townsfolk find the practice faintly distasteful: illegitimate — a majority's mere distaste should never govern what someone does on their own property. (4) A law requiring adult drivers to buckle up, though only the driver is at risk: legitimate, though I can't square it with (1) to my own satisfaction. (5) The one I keep circling: my prudish friend Alex and my libertine friend Sam share a house with one copy of a scandalous novel. Alex most wants nobody to read it, but would grudgingly read it himself rather than let Sam wallow in it; Sam would relish nothing more than watching the prude read it, though he'd rather read it himself than let it sit unread. Left to themselves, Alex declines and Sam reads — yet both would prefer the arrangement where Alex reads and Sam doesn't, so a house rule mandating exactly that would make each better off by his own lights, while forcing Alex to read against his will and forbidding Sam's solitary reading in his own room. My gut says each person's own reading is his business alone, and also that if literally everyone prefers an arrangement, blocking it is perverse; those two feelings collide in this case. Is there a principle that captures all five verdicts — an account of the personal/collective line that explains why (2) and (4) can be voted on while (1) and (3) cannot — and, if so, what does it say about the house rule in (5)? If nothing captures all five, tell me which verdict you would overturn and why, and state what your principle predicts for a case I haven't listed: a city that votes to ban the sale of foie gras because a majority finds the production method cruel, though no buyer's decision touches anyone else.
