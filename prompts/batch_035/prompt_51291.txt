My roommate works on robotics and is deep into the symbol grounding literature, and last week he backed me into a corner at dinner with an argument I can't shake but also can't accept. It goes:

(1) A system genuinely understands a term only if its internal symbol for that term is grounded — tied, somewhere down the line, to non-symbolic contact with the kind of thing the term refers to. An ungrounded symbol is just a label for other labels, like a dictionary written entirely in its own language.

(2) The grounding chain has to bottom out in something non-symbolic. If every link is itself a symbol, you have symbols all the way down and nothing ever touches the world.

(3) Now take my word "electron." I have never touched, seen, or otherwise non-symbolically encountered an electron. Everything I know about electrons came from teachers talking, textbooks, diagrams, instrument readouts — all symbols produced by other symbol users.

(4) The same holds for "the Bronze Age," "quarks," "Antarctica" (never been), "next Tuesday" (hasn't happened yet), and the word "dinosaur," which I am definately never going to be able to ground in the original referents.

(5) So by (1) and (2) I don't understand any of those words. I've just got tokens pointing at other tokens inside my head.

(6) My roommate says fine, that IS the conclusion: I don't understand "electron," I defer to physicists who do. The grounding lives in the community, not in me, and my token counts as grounded only by borrowing theirs.

(7) But I pushed back: what if nobody in the chain is grounded? The physicists got "electron" from teachers and instruments too, and the instruments were designed and interpreted by people who learned the word the same way. On his own principles it could be one big closed loop of symbol-passing where nobody ever bottoms out — a civilization that collectively understands nothing, which even he won't say.

I can sort of see where I want to dig in — something fishy happens between (3) and (5), or maybe in what (2) counts as "non-symbolic" — but every time I try to say it precisely it slips away, and my roommate just grins and mentions that by my own logic I have no standing to complain about chatbots, since their words also trace back causally to grounded humans somewhere upstream. Which, I suspect, is what this whole argument was secretly for.
