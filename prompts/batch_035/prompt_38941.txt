I've never studied any of this, it's all from my own thinking plus arguments online and at family dinners, so bear with me. I want to know if my views about "social construction" actually hang together, because I've been assuming they do and lately I'm not so sure. Here's what I beleive:

(1) Money is a social construct. A twenty dollar bill is only money because everybody treats it that way. If we all stopped tomorrow, it's just paper with a dead guy on it.

(2) Race is a social construct. There's no real biological line underneath it, the lines got drawn by society, and different societies draw them in different places — the same person can be black in one country and basically not-black in another.

(3) Gender is a social construct too.

(4) But I think gender identity, the felt sense of being a man or a woman or whatever, has something biological in it. Nobody chooses that, and it seems to show up before you have any say in anything. If that's right for trans people — and it seems like it has to be, otherwise why would anyone go through what they go through — then part of gender isn't constructed, and I'm not sure how that fits with (3).

(5) Being a construct doesn't make a thing fake. Money is about as real as anything gets, and we made it up. Calling something constructed is not the same as calling it imaginary.

(6) I still get annoyed when people say everything is a construct. A tree isn't a construct. Trees were here before us and don't care what we think. Though ok, I admit the category "tree" is ours — nature doesn't come pre-sorted into trees versus shrubs, we decided this one counts as a tree and that one's a bush.

One more thing that bugs me: if everyone woke up tomorrow having forgotten what money is, I think money would just be gone. But if everyone woke up having forgotten race exists, I have a feeling the racial sorting would rebuild itself within a generation, and nobody would get to opt out of it. Not sure what to do with that, it just seems true.

So go through the six like an audit. Which ones actually conflict, which ones only look like they do, rank the conflicts from worst to mildest, and for each real one tell me what I'd have to drop and what dropping it costs me elsewhere. Don't soften it — if the whole package is a mess, I'd rather know.
