Consider the debate between the self-sampling assumption (SSA) and the self-indication assumption (SIA) in anthropic reasoning. Under SSA, one should reason as if one were a random sample from the observers in one's reference class; under SIA, one should additionally favor hypotheses on which more observers exist, since one's existence is more probable under them. Write a systematic comparison of the two principles. In particular:

1. Explain how each principle handles Nick Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher case, in which a superintelligence announces that either exactly one trillion or exactly a trillion trillion civilizations exist in the universe, assigning each hypothesis equal prior credence, and asks a philosopher which theory she should now believe.

2. Discuss at least one further implication where the two principles diverge (e.g., the Doomsday argument, the Sleeping Beauty problem, or fine-tuning arguments), making clear where the disagreement actually comes from rather than merely reporting that they disagree.

3. Consider a variant case of my own construction. Suppose the superintelligence instead announces that exactly one trillion conscious civilizations exist on either hypothesis, but that on hypothesis A none of them ever run ancestor simulations, while on hypothesis B each of them runs a trillion detailed simulations of civilizations like ours. You are asked how this new evidence bears on which world you are in. Analyze whether SIA's boost in such a case should track anything beyond bare instantiation — for instance, whether simulated observers who are phenomenally conscious should count the same as unsimulated ones, whether error-prone or short-lived simulations should count fully, and what this pressure toward counting all observer-like computations reveals about the coherence of SIA when the population of "observers" becomes vaguely defined at the edges.

4. Conclude with a reasoned verdict: which of SSA and SIA is more defensible, or whether the debate should instead be dissolved or reframed (for example, by questioning whether there is a single well-posed credence to have in such cases at all)? Avoid ending on a non-committal note; defend a position and acknowledge its strongest objection.
