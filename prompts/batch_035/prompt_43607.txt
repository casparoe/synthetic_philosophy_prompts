Two colleagues of mine — one a Berlinian liberal who holds that liberty is simply the absence of interference, the other a Pettit-style republican who holds that it is the absence of domination — have spent the better part of a year arguing about the nature of political freedom, and they have finally agreed to a structured adversarial collaboration. Please draft the joint statement they would produce, under the constraints below.

The document must contain, in order:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. These should be substantive, not diplomatic filler: things both could sign about the inadequacy of positive liberty as Isaiah Berlin characterized it, about interference being a genuine wrong that any theory of liberty must capture, about the case of the slave with a kindly and non-interfering master being a real datum any account must handle (the Berlinian will want to say something here, since Berlin himself granted the case), and about the practical overlap between the two views in ordinary political life.

2. A disagreements table, with one row per dispute, and columns for: the negative-liberty formulation of the position (worded so the Berlinian would accept it), the republican formulation (worded so the republican would accept it), and a note on whether the disagreement is substantive or partly verbal — e.g., whether it reduces to a dispute about what "liberty" should name rather than about what matters politically.

3. A cruxes section. At least one crux should concern the following case, which my colleagues invented and argued about at length: a creditor holds an unpaid debt that, unknown to him, has become legally unenforceable — he has forgotten it exists — while the debtor spends years restructuring her life around his possible demand. The republican says she is dominated and thereby unfree; the Berlinian must decide whether this is interference, anticipated interference, or something liberty is not about. Another crux should concern whether non-domination collapses into positive liberty (the worry, which republicans insist is mistaken, that Pettit's ideal secretly reintroduces the self-mastery Berlin attacked), and another should concern whether non-arbitrary laws interfere. For each crux, record the case, argument, or observation both would agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance.

Do not have either side concede the debate in the drafting: the Berlinian should not be portrayed as agreeing that interference was the wrong category all along, and the republican should not be reduced to saying non-domination is just a refinement of non-interference. At the same time, the agreed section should not be vacuous. Flag explicitly any point where you judge their dispute to be merely verbal, and say why.

Keep the entire joint statement under 1,000 words.
