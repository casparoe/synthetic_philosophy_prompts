My friends and I keep arguing online about what counts as a disease — whether burnout is one, whether gaming addiction was invented by therapists, whether grief should be medicated — and I've noticed the arguments never go anywhere because people use the word differently. What I'd really like to know is not what people call their view but what they actually think, so I want to build a "which view of disease do you really hold?" quiz for our group. Please write eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, using cases where the standard theories genuinely split apart: something biologically dysfunctional that causes no one distress or any practical problem; something biologically unremarkable that is only a problem because of how a society is arranged, like severe nearsightedness in a society built for reading, or a trait that was once an official diagnosis mainly because the surrounding culture condemned it; a condition like sickle-cell trait whose effects depend entirely on the environment; ordinary grief versus grief that never fades; a persistent ringing in the ears that its owner has fully made peace with; and aging itself. Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions — the view that disease is purely a matter of statistical biological dysfunction, whether or not it harms anyone; the view that unwantedness or harm to the person is the whole of it; the hybrid that demands both; and any other position the answer space forces you to recognize. Please work from the logical space outward: first the combinations of answers the questions permit, then the positions located in that space, and say briefly what to make of any combination that no one actually holds. No question should just ask whether disease is "objective" or "socially constructed," and no question should rely on a case being judged the same way in every culture. Begin your response with the quiz itself, with no preface or commentary; put the scoring key after it.
