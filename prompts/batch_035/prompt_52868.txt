my study group has been going around in circles (pun very much intended) on this one for two weeks now and I want an outside opinion. It started when my friend Marcus brought up Bertrand's chord paradox and it ended with him claiming that the principle of indifference — you know, the thing that tells you a fair coin is 50/50 — is incoherent everywhere, which I refuse to accept. His argument, as best I can reconstruct it:

(1) Principle of indifference: when you have no reason to favor one possibility over another, assign them equal probabilities. (2) Take a circle with an inscribed equilateral triangle, pick a chord "at random," and ask for the probability it's longer than the triangle's side. (3) If you pick the chord by choosing two random points on the circumference: fixing one point, the other has to land in the middle third of the remaining arc, so the answer is 1/3. (4) If you pick a random radius and a random point along it and draw the perpendicular chord: it's long enough iff the point is within half the radius of the center, so 1/2. (5) If you pick a random point in the disk as the chord's midpoint: it's long enough iff the point falls inside the inscribed circle of half the radius, which is a quarter of the area, so 1/4. (6) In each case we genuinely had no reason to favor any chord over any other, so indifference was correctly applied all three times. (7) So indifference yields three different answers to a single question — it contradicts itself. (8) And this isn't some quirk of circles: he gave the example of a factory producing cubes with side length somewhere between 0 and 1. If you're indifferent over side lengths, the probability the volume exceeds 1/2 is 1 − (1/2)^(1/3), about 0.21; if you're indifferent over volumes, it's exactly 1/2. Whenever the quantity you spread your ignorance over can be transformed, you get a different answer. (9) Therefore indifference never determines a probability, and since the fairness of coins and dice is just an indifference assignment over faces, there's no fact of the matter that a fair die shows six with probability 1/6 — it's exactly as arbitrary as 1/3 vs 1/2 vs 1/4.

So where does this go wrong? I'm happy to concede indifference for vague continuous stuff like chords and cube sides, but (9) is supposed to follow from that, and I can't find the link in the chain that breaks. When I pushed Marcus on dice he had a comeback that I couldn't answer on the spot: if redescribing the outcome space is what breaks indifference for chords, then redescribing the die's outcomes as "six" vs "not-six" should break it there too, and if symmetry is what saves the die, why doesn't it save at least one of the chord methods — after all the circle is perfectly symmetric. Tell me which step to reject and what rejecting it costs. If the answer is that (6) is the false premise, I want to know what "no reason to favor" could mean such that the die has it and all three chord constructions lack it — or whether only some of them lack it, and how you'd ever tell which. And if the honest answer is that I have to bite the bullet and accept (9), then please explain to me what exactly the casino is trading on.
