ok so I've been going down a rabbit hole on why originals matter to me and I can't work out if I actually have a principled reason or if I'm just being snobby. here's my gut reactions to a bunch of situations, tell me if there's an actual rule behind all of it or if I'm just making it up as I go.

1. a painting hangs in a museum for 40 years, everyone loves it, then it turns out to be a forgery painted in 1950. same paint, same canvas, looked at by thousands of people who genuinely felt things looking at it. once the news breaks I lose basically all interest in seeing it. seems weird even to me but that's where I'm at.

2. my grandma's famous cookie recipe. turns out she got it off the back of a flour bag and told everyone it was hers for 50 years. I do NOT care even slightly, still feels like grandma's recipe.

3. a print of a photo I love vs the actual first print the photographer made in the darkroom. the print hanging on my wall looks identical. I'd pay a bit more for the real one but not THAT much more, like maybe double, not a million times more like with the painting. why is that different from case 1? no idea.

4. a violin maker today builds a violin that is, by any measurement you want, better than a Stradivarius. experts blind-tested prefer it. I still think the Strad is worth more and honestly I think that's fine and correct, even though if you asked me to justify it I couldn't.

5. here's the one that's bugging me. an old church mural is restored and it turns out only like 10% of the original paint is left, 90% is a modern restorer repainting it. I'm kind of ok with that, I'd still say "I saw the 14th century mural." but the forged painting in case 1 was also made of real paint and real canvas by a real person, just not the person I thought, and there I'm furious. why does one feel like a lie and the other feel like maintenance?? they seem like basically the same thing.

6. autographed baseball that turns out the player signed it at a show in 1995 and not, as I'd been told, right after the famous game in 1986. exact same ink, exact same signature. my interest drops a LOT. feels like I got scammed even though the object itself didn't change one bit.

7. a band records a cover of a song and it becomes the famous version, so famous most people think it's their song (happens all the time). when I listen to the cover I like it just as much knowing this. the music coming out of the speakers is identical either way. but then case 1 and case 6 also involve identical objects and there I care!!

so that's where I am. is there one idea that covers all of these or close to it? and if there is, what does it say about a couple of cases I'll throw at you at random: like if someone "restores" a rusted antique car by replacing every single part with new ones and keeps only the frame (and the paperwork says restored, which I feel like I'm fine with??), or a copy of a copy of an old text where the original is long gone (which I feel completely fine about, but should I?). and honestly if the rule says one of my reactions is dumb, tell me which one and why, I'd rather fix my gut than protect it. I never took any philosophy, talk to me like a normal person.
