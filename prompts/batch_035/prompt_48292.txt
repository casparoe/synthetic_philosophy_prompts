I'm a novelist, and I've been asked to write an afterword for a new edition of my work explaining what I actually believe about the self, since my fiction consists almost entirely of interior monologue. Before it goes to the publisher I'd like a consistency check, because the position I've drafted feels stable to me but I can't see it from outside. Here is where I stand:

(1) There is no Cartesian theater — no inner stage on which experience is finally assembled and displayed to a single spectator — and the multiple drafts picture, on which the stream of consciousness has no fixed, determinate content prior to being probed by attention or report, is essentially right.

(2) The self is a strange loop: the "I" is a pattern the brain builds by representing itself representing, not a thing that exists prior to its own representation.

(3) First-person narration is nonetheless epistemically privileged — an interior monologue renders a mind more faithfully than any external description could.

(4) When I write a character, there is a fact of the matter about what she is thinking at each moment; her stream of consciousness is fully determinate, and I either get it right or I don't, even if no reader and not even I could verify which.

(5) Inner speech is the voice of the character's self, a genuine inner voice that the novelist transcribes.

(6) My constructivism about the self in no way compromises my conviction that getting a character's inner life right matters aesthetically — those two strike me as obviously compatible, but tell me if you disagree.

Please audit the set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and for each real tension, what exactly would have to give and what it would cost me — including, importantly, what it would cost my craft, since if (say) the determinacy of the stream went, I'd want to know what interior monologue is then for. Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damaging, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I honestly don't know where the trouble lies, so don't spare any commitment out of politeness.
