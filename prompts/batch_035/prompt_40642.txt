My friend is on a big anti-IP kick lately and keeps reaching for this old candle comparison, which he says goes back to Jefferson: if you light your candle from mine, my flame isn't any dimmer for it, nobody is worse off, so how on earth can copying be "theft"? He thinks this one picture settles everything — not just that pirating a movie isn't stealing, but that copyright and patents are indefensible in principle, since if sharing costs the owner literally nothing, the whole edifice of exclusive rights is just a government-granted racket. He'll literally end arguments by going "did my candle get shorter? no? then we're done here."

I half buy it, but something keeps nagging at me. The analogy seems obviously right about the *copying* step — the marginal cost of one more copy really is basically zero now. But it seems to quietly assume the thing being copied just fell from the sky. Albums cost a fortune to make and nothing to duplicate, and I can't fit that fact into "nobody is worse off." Also I suspect the candle picture is doing sneaky work by treating the choice as *mine* (to share or not share my candle) when the actual legal question is whether *the producer* gets to control distribution, which feels like a different thing — but I can't pin down why, and he wins the exchange every time because his side has the vivid image and mine has a vague unease.

Can you audit the analogy properly? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — does his abolitionist conclusion survive or not — and then give the reasoning. I want the actual mapping spelled out (what plays the role of the candle, the flame, the sharing, the "theft"), then where the mapping breaks, and crucially which of the breaks matter for the conclusion he's using it to support versus which ones don't (I don't want to hear about every disanalogy under the sun, just the load-bearing ones). And if the candle is the wrong picture, tell me what a better one would be and whether *it* supports abolishing IP or just reshaping it — I'm honestly not sure which way I want this to come out, so don't sand it down.
