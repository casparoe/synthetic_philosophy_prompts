I'm putting together an episode for my tiny decision theory podcast (like 200 listeners, very in the weeds) and next week's guest is a decision theorist — let's call him Dr. K — who has spent the last few years arguing that "commitment races" are one of the most underrated catastrophic risks from advanced AI, possibly worse than the scenarios people usually obsess about. Quick background in case it helps: a commitment race is a situation where agents each do better by being the FIRST to irrevocably bind themselves to some bargaining position — say, committing to walk away from any deal unless they get at least 60% of the surplus — because once that commitment is out there and credible, the other side does best by just conceding. If both agents race to commit, or bind themselves to incompatible positions before they can coordinate, everyone can end up far worse off than if neither had ever been able to bind themeselves at all.
