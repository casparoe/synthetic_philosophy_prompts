I coordinate a small research group — two members with cognitive-science backgrounds, one from NLP — with roughly three years of modest funding to work on analogy and conceptual slippage in the tradition of Hofstadter's Copycat program: letter-string microdomains of the form "abc is to abd as ijk is to what?", where the interesting answer (something like ijl, reached by letting the successor concept slip to predecessor once you hit a letter with no successor) is supposed to emerge from fluid concepts rather than literal rule application. The field has shifted since that tradition went quiet: large language models now perform surprisingly decent four-term analogies and story analogies without anything resembling a Slipnet, codelets, or a temperature mechanism, and structure-mapping accounts from the Gentner tradition remain the main symbolic rival.

Within this space, which open questions most deserve our effort, and in what order? Candidates I have in mind: whether microdomain results can be trusted as evidence about real-world analogy, or whether the tidy closed world of letter strings is precisely what made slippage look more systematic than it is; whether conceptual slippage is a genuinely distinct mechanism from the similarity-based mapping LLMs already display, or two descriptions of one phenomenon; whether the claim that analogy is the core of cognition is testable at all, and if not, what testable residue of it survives; and how one could operationalize the "fluidity" of a concept so that it can be measured rather than admired.

Do not treat my list as exhaustive. For each question you prioritize, explain why it sits upstream of the others, what tractability looks like for a group of our size and skills (we can run experiments on LLMs and build small-scale models, but cannot do significant training runs), what would count as progress within it, and what developments over the next few years — in ML, cognitive science, or the philosophy of concepts — would make you reshuffle the ranking. I want a defended agenda rather than a survey: if you think the entire microdomain tradition is a dead end for our purposes, say so plainly and tell me what the group should do instead.
