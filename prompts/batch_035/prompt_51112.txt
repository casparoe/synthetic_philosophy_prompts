quick bit of background so the question makes sense: I'm a philosophy PhD student, and next month im presenting a chapter of my dissertation at a departmental workshop. The chapter is on reasons internalism vs externalism, basically the Bernard Williams style debate, and my position is a hybrid thing I came up with and I need to be ready for the bloodbath.

My view, roughly: I agree with the internalist that a reason for an agent has to be reachable from that agent's motivational set via sound deliberation from that set. But I want to say that there's a special subclass of cases where the internalist verdict feels wrong -- cases where someone's motivational set is impoverished not because they tried and failed to care about something, but because they never even had the opportunity to develop the concern (think someone raised in total isolation from music who we still want to say has a reason to listen to late Beethoven). For those cases I say the agent has a "dormant reason": not an external reason in the robust sense, since it's still tied to what they could come to care about under the right conditions, but not a plain internal reason either, since no amount of sound deliberation from their actual set gets them there. The chapter then argues externalists and internalists have both been fighting over the wrong cases and the real action is in the dormant ones.

Now the audience. It's my department's workshop, so: about half Kantians and neo-Kantians who are committed externalists about reasons (they'll say a dormant reason just IS an external reason and my distinction collapses), one hardline Humean who thinks the whole dormant reasons thing is a face-saving fudge and internalism should just bite the bullet ("no reason, tough luck"), a couple of metaethics people who mostly care about whether my view can be squared with the reasons-as-facts crowd, and my advisor who is an internalist and privately thinks I'm giving away the store but won't say so directly, she'll ask something like a "helpful" question that is actually a knife.

So what I want: a prep sheet for the Q&A. Give me the eight or so objections I'll actually get from THIS crowd, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that person would phrase it (the Kantian version and the Humean version of "your distinction collapses" are different objections and I need both), with a two-minute reply for each and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Also flag what I should just concede up front so I look reasonable, and what I absolutely cannot concede without the whole chapter falling apart. And yes, include the question I'm praying nobody asks, plus what to do if my advisor asks it.
