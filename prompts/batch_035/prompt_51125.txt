hey, so I run a small philosophy blog and want to make one of those "which view do you *actually* hold" quizzes, this time about whether omissions can be causes. Like the gardener who doesn't water the flowers and they die — did his not-watering cause the death? I want 8-10 questions, each a concrete case with 2-3 answer options, plus a scoring key at the end. The whole point is sorting readers by what they say about cases, not which theory label they like, so no question can just ask "do you think absences can be causes?" — every question has to be a scenario where the positions genuinely come apart.

Some cases I want in there: (1) the gardener/flowers one, (2) a nurse who forgets to give a dying patient an antidote, vs. some random stranger on the other side of the country who also didn't give the antidote — why does one feel causal and the other absurd? (3) the profligacy problem: my neighbor didn't water my flowers either, and neither did a farmer in Kazachstan, so are they all causes of the flowers dying? (4) the "which absences even exist" problem: was the cause the absence of water, the absence of rain, the sprinkler not turning on, or the gardener not showing up? (5) a case where denying omission-causation just seems hopeless, like I leave the gate open and the dog gets out. (6) maybe one on double prevention: a bodyguard blocks a bullet, remove the bodyguard and the person dies — is the bullet's not-being-blocked a cause, or something else? You can add or swap cases if you have better ones, as long as the cases do the sorting work.

The scoring key's leaves should include at least: omissions-aren't-really-causes (real causation needs physical stuff like conserved quantities being transfered, absences are at best pseudo- or quasi-causation), the moralized view (an omission only counts as a cause when someone had a duty or reasonable expectation to act — that's why the nurse but not the stranger), contrastivism (there's no fact of the matter about "the cause" simpliciter, only about what caused what *as opposed to which alternative*, so the gardener caused the death-as-opposed-to-the-backup-gardener-watering-them but not death-as-opposed-to-rain), and the causal-modeling view (an omission is just a variable taking its "didn't happen" value, no mystery, treat it the same as any other cause). If two of these can't really be seperated by case answers, flag that honestly instead of inventing fake distinguishing questions. And if some answer pattern maps onto no real position, say that too — either the pattern is incoherent or it's just an unexplored corner.

Audience is blog readers with zero philosophy background, so keep the language plain — the positions get their names in the scoring key, not in the questions. thanks!!
