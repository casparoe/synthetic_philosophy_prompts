My sister and I have been arguing for years about whether an evil rule counts as a real law or is just force dressed up as law, and over that time she has settled into what looks like a stable pattern of verdicts. I want to be able to predict her on new cases, and I keep failing, so I'd like your help figuring out the rule she is actually running.

Her verdicts so far, on cases we've argued about at family dinners: a clearly worded, published, forward-looking statute ordering citizens to inform on a persecuted minority — she says that one is not law at all, though I've noticed she also claims it was never really enforced as written, since officials looked the other way when the accused was well connected. The old pass laws of apartheid South Africa, terrible but clear, published, and enforced in an orderly, predictable way — she says those were laws, evil ones, and she gets impatient when I treat that answer as a concession. A secret decree nobody was allowed to read, punishing people for conduct that was not a crime when they did it — not law. A clearly posted modern regulation with a modest fine for something everyone agrees is unfairly treated — law, obviously, next question.

Here are the new cases I want to put to her, and I'd like you to predict her verdicts. First, a country whose legislature passes a plainly worded, prospective, published statute requiring householders to report guests who stay more than three nights, and — this is the part that differs from the informer statute — it is enforced exactly as written, against everyone, friend of the regime or not. Second, after a peaceful revolution, the new democratically elected parliament passes a clear, published statute that retroactively criminalizes acts the old regime's officials committed with full legal authority at the time, including interrogation methods everyone now calls torture. Third, a statute that is general, published, and prospective, but written so vaguely that officials, judges, and citizens each read it three different ways, and the government enforces whichever reading suits it that week.

What I really want is not just answers but the candidate rules that fit her record so far. Is it something like "a rule is law only if it's made and administered lawfully — general, public, forward-looking, clear, and actually enforced the way it's written" — in which case she has to treat the pass laws and the fairly enforced informer statute alike, and I'd like to know whether she can live with that? Or is it "a rule evil enough stops being law," in which case the pass laws bother her? Or "a rule that conscripts ordinary citizens into doing wrong isn't law, while one that merely targets its victims still is"? Or some fourth thing. For each new case, tell me what each rule predicts, give me your best guess at her verdict with your reasons, and design the one further case I should put to her next to best tell the rules apart. Please write in American English.
