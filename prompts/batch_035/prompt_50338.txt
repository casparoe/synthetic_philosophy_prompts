I'm taking an upper-level seminar this term on Darwinian approaches to culture — mostly philosophy of biology students plus a couple of anthropologists — and for the final session each of us submits a numbered list of our actual commitments, which the group then spends an hour tearing apart. Before I hand mine in, I want an audit, because I have a suspicion the list doesn't hang together and I can't see where. Here is the draft: (1) Cultural change is genuinely Darwinian, not metaphorically so: cumulative cultural adaptation arises from inheritance, variation, and differential retention of cultural variants, and the way to study it is with population thinking rather than by classifying "types" of society. (2) I am deeply skeptical of group-level functional explanations of culture: when a ritual, taboo, or religious practice spreads, the explanation should be sought in properties of the variants and their effects on individual human minds — conformity bias, prestige bias, content biases — and any appeal to the practice's benefit for the group is a just-so story. (3) Even so, memetic selection can genuinely make traits common that are bad for their bearers: what makes a variant spread is its own transmissibility, not its usefulness to the host, which is why things like celibate proselytizing religious movements or self-amplifying moral panics can thrive; I take this to be the real insight behind the whole "meme" framing. (4) I also hold that human transmitters are active critics rather than passive vehicles: we edit, recombine, and reject what we receive, and most transmission "error" is directed toward intelligible human purposes rather than blind. (5) The content of widely shared concepts — minimally counterintuitive gods, overactive agency detection — is explained by the quirks of human cognition, not by what those concepts do for genes, memes, or societies. (6) Finally, I want to keep the meme's-eye perspective as a legitimate explanatory stance — asking what makes a variant good at getting itself copied — while explicitly denying that memes are discrete, high-fidelity, gene-like replicators.

Tell me whether this package is jointly tenable. Which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it; for each real tension, how severe it is, what would have to give, and what that concession would cost me elsewhere in the list. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and flag the one thing in my list the seminar group will pounce on that I haven't noticed. Please lead with your bottom line in your first sentence — a direct verdict on whether the commitments hang together and where the worst problem is — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my list.
