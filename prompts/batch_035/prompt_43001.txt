Two of my flatmates, Nadia and Tom, have been going at it for a week over something that happened to my cousin, and I've been asked to referee, which I'm clearly not up to. My cousin and his friend both left the same party, equally drunk, and drove home the same route. My cousin made it fine. The friend mounted a kerb and put a pedestrian in hospital. Neither had ever driven drunk before or since, they'd both been drinking the same amount, and there was no difference in their choices all night — only in what happened ten metres from the party. (1) Nadia said the friend is simply a worse person now: he nearly killed someone, that's a moral fact, and the fact that my cousin got lucky doesn't put them on the same level. (2) Tom said that's textbook moral luck — if every choice was identical, their culpability is identical, and the difference is just the lottery of what the world did with those choices. (3) Nadia said fine, but the law disagrees for good reason: we punish the completed harm more than the near miss, because a system that punished inner wickedness equally would need to read minds, and any workable institution has to punish what actually happens. (4) Tom said she'd quietly changed the subject — that's an argument about what law should do, not about what people deserve, and anyway if she follows her logic consistently she has to also say my cousin deserves equal praise to someone who drunkenly swerved and happened to save a child, which she'd never accept. (5) Nadia said she actually would accept that, sort of — praise for heros is partly just luck too and she's fine biting that bullet, but blame is different because blame answers to the victim. (6) Tom said he doesn't see how a victim's existence can create desert, since the driver's character is the same either way — and besides, even the choices themselves are downstream of luck (temperament, upbringing), so Nadia's view has to explain where the luck stops mattering, and hers just doesn't. Please adjudicate each of the six moves — say who's right about what, whether anyone is talking past each other, and who comes out more right overall. Answer in British English, and don't bother being diplomatic — they weren't.
