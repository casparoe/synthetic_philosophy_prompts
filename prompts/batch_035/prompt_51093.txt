I cover science and ideas for a mid-size outlet, and I keep ending up at panels where someone deploys "the expanding circle" -- the idea, going back to Peter Singer's book title, that moral concern has been widening outward from family/tribe to strangers, to distant peoples, to animals, and that this expansion is basically the story of moral progress. Last week at an AI conference a researcher used it to argue that giving moral status to AI systems is just the next band on the circle, basically inevitable, and a skeptic shot back that the circle has shrunk plenty of times too. I had follow-up questions but couldn't articulate why the exchange felt so slippery. I think the slogan is doing several different jobs at once and I want to untangle them before my next panel. Lay out the distinct precise claims "the moral circle keeps expanding" could be making -- from weak/descriptive to strong/normative -- and for each say whether it's plausible, whether it's actually false (e.g. claims that nothing ever gets excluded from the circle again, or that expansion = progress by definition), and which reading the "inevitable AI rights" guy would need for his argument to work. I'd also love to know which reading is just Whig history warmed over. Format it as a list I can keep in my notes for asking sharp follow-ups in interviews.
