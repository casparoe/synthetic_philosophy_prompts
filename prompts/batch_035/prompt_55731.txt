I'm a journalist on the science-and-ideas desk, and I've been assigned a long-form feature my editor summarizes as the question of whether the wavefunction is a thing in the world or a bookkeeping device — whether, when a physicist says an electron is in a superposition of going left and going right, they are describing the electron itself or describing what they expect of it. Before I sit down with a philosopher who takes the quantum state to be physical (her camp runs through many-worlds and Bohmian-style pictures) and a physicist who takes it to encode an agent's information or expectations (his camp runs through QBism and older Copenhagen-flavored views), I want the specification, not the sales pitch: a list of what any view of the quantum state's status has to deliver before it can be taken seriously enough to appear in my piece. The purpose is fixed and modest: good enough to underwrite, in print, a claim about what the formalism says exists — or says about us.

So write the desiderata for a view of the quantum state's status. The phenomena and results I'd expect any candidate to engage include single-particle interference and the quantum eraser, where a which-path record is created and then destroyed and the fringes return; the fact that the state of two entangled systems is not a mere distribution over each system's own states; the fact that one and the same density matrix can be read either as ordinary ignorance about some underlying pure state or as a genuinely mixed situation, with the two readings disagreeing about whether any experiment could ever separate them; and the no-go results — Bell, Kochen–Specker, and especially the PBR theorem, which bears directly on the ontic-versus-epistemic question and which I want handled rather than waved at. For each requirement, state what case or theorem motivates it, and be explicit about whether it is a genuine requirement or a camp's prejudice dressed up as one — I suspect the demand for locality, and the demand that the wavefunction sit on a par with the electromagnetic field, are of the second kind, but push back if you disagree. If some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable given the no-go results, so that the list itself forces a choice between camps, that is the part my editor most wants to see, and it should be named plainly.

Format: give me exactly five numbered desiderata, nothing before the first and nothing after the last — no framing paragraph, no closing note. Each item should run a sentence or two: the requirement itself, the case or theorem behind it, and whether it is genuine or camp-relative. Fold the joint-unsatisfiability verdict, if there is one, into whichever item it belongs to rather than adding a sixth item or a coda, and indicate there which requirement does the most work in eliminating candidates.
