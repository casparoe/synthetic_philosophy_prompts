My colleague here in the philosophy department — trained as a metaethicist, but she has loud opinions about everything, which is how we like her — has a years-long running argument with me about abstract objects, and I've finally started writing down her verdicts, because I can't get them to line up with any single theory. Her record so far: the number 17 exists and would have existed even if minds had never evolved; sets exist; mathematical truths are discovered, not invented, and there was already a fact about the Riemann hypothesis before anyone asked; Sherlock Holmes does not exist, though she's perfectly happy to say true things are true of him; Beethoven's Tenth doesn't exist even though there are sketches, while Brahms's Fourth does; poem-types exist, which she says is the only reason a poem can be copied; chess exists but only since roughly the sixth century — it was invented, it wasn't sitting around waiting to be found; possible worlds don't exist; and universals like redness don't exist either, just particulars that resemble each other. So: numbers and sets eternal, artworks and games created, fiction and worlds and universals out.

Here's my situation. Next month she's giving a talk to our grad metaphysics seminar, I'm moderating the Q&A, and I want to spring a case on her that I genuinely can't predict: the Mandelbrot set. Did it exist in 1900, before anyone had the definition or the computers to picture it? On one reading of her — math eternal, culture created — she says yes, obviously, it's a mathematical object like any other. But there's a second reading of her record, something like: an abstract object exists only if it's inevitable, the kind of thing any sufficiently deep inquiry would eventually converge on. Numbers pass that test, chess and Holmes don't — and it's not crazy to argue the Mandelbrot set's definition is as historically contingent as chess's, one species' particular doodling with iterating complex quadratics rather than something any mathematical culture would land on. I can even sketch a third reading where whatever our best theories indispensably quantify over gets in, and I honestly can't tell where that puts the set.
