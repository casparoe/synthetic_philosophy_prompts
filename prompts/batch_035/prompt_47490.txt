A friend over in the decision-science group has an argument that weakness of will actually belongs to the self-controlled person, and I can't find the flaw. It seems to prove way to much, but every step looks fine to me on its own.

Setup: next Friday I'm meant to spend the evening on my dissertation. Today, Tuesday, calmly, I judge that working is better — all things considered, that's my preference, and it's what I'd say I ought to do. Friday night, if past form holds, I'll judge the opposite: the bar will seem the better call, all things considered, and it won't feel like an itch I notice and resist. Ask me at the door and I'll sincerely tell you the bar is the better plan for tonight.

Her argument: (1) a person's all-things-considered judgment at the time of action is the standard for what they ought to do then — nothing else is available that doesn't come from outside the person. (2) Akrasia is acting against that judgment. (3) So on Friday, going to the bar isn't akratic — I'm doing exactly what I judge best — and forcing myself to work through gritted teeth is akratic. (4) Hyperbolic discounting makes preference reversals like this the norm for nearly every choice that stretches over time, not a rare pathology. (5) So akrasia almost never sits where we think it sits: the self-controlled person is the weak-willed one, and the "tempted" person is just rational. (6) Worse, precommitment devices — handing my partner the wifi password that night, putting money on the line — come out as sabotage: an earlier agent overriding the one with legitimate standing to decide.

Her conclusion is that the akrasia/self-control distinction just tracks who wins a power struggle between your temporal selves. I can't accept that — the Friday me is making some kind of mistake, and the Tuesday me isn't a tyrant. But I also can't locate the bad step. (2) looks fine as a definition, and I'd rather keep something like "the Friday me is defective." That leaves (1), but if I give it up, where does the standard come from without going sub-personal or cross-temporal — which is exactly the move she'll call tyranny? Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me?
