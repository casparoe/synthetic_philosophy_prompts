My philosophy reading group has spent basically the whole year fighting about anthropic reasoning, and I've been drafted to write up the ceasefire document. One half of the group are self-sampling assumption people — they think you should reason as if you were a random sample drawn from the observers in your reference class, no more and no less. The other half are self-indication assumption people — they think you should additionally weight each hypothesis in proportion to how many observers it says exist, on the grounds that finding yourself alive at all is evidence for hypotheses with more observers. We've all conceded the shouting match is going nowhere, so what we want is the joint statement a genuinely good-faith adversarial collaboration between the two camps would produce. Write that document.

Concretely: start with a numbered section of agreed claims — I want the things both camps can sign, like what the Sleeping Beauty problem actually shows and what it doesn't, that the Doomsday argument and the Presumptuous Philosopher case share a common formal structure (two hypotheses, one positing vastly more observers, and the question of what your own existence tells you between them), and whatever they agree on about the reference class problem, including which debates the reference class does and does not decide. Then a disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated in wording that the side holding that view would actually accept as fair to itself (the SIA camp presumably thinks the presumptuous philosopher is being rational to the point of embarrassment; the SSA camp thinks he's committing a distinctive error, and I want each side's complaint phrased its own way). Finally a cruxes section: for each crux, a concrete case or argument both camps would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. We suspect one crux is whether thirding in Sleeping Beauty forces anything in the Doomsday case or whether the analogy breaks somewhere. For a second candidate crux, consider a variant of the Presumptuous Philosopher setup where the extra observers in the bigger hypothesis live in causally disconnected regions of the cosmos that no one, ever, can interact with or even get a signal from — the question is whether merely positing more of them should move the philosopher's credences, given that those credences also govern her decisions about a world she can only ever affect locally. And if you think part of the SSA-vs-SIA dispute is ultimately verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why.

Two things: answer in American English, and keep it usable for someone with no web access — no reading lists, citations, or "see X for details," just the arguments themselves. It's fine (expected, even) if the document ends with the disagreement made exact rather than resolved.
