I'm taking a phil of art course this semester and got volunteered to run a session for my tutorial group (about 15 people, 50 min) right before we start the unit on what art even is. I want to open with a "which definition of art do you actually hold?" quiz that people take before they've read any of the theory, so they get sorted by their answers to cases rather than by whichever -ism they've already heard of and like the sound of. Could you write it?

8-10 questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the positions: aesthetic/functional definitions (art's function is aesthetic experience), the institutional theory, historical/intentional-historical definitions (something is art if it's made with the right sorts of intentions against a background of earlier art), the cluster/disjunctive move, and the anti-essentialist "art just has no definition" option. If there's a natural way to fit in some weirder leaf like radical nominalism about artworks, go for it.

Cases I want worked in somewhere (add others if you need them):
- a forged painting chemically indistinguishable from a famous original, that fooled experts for decades — is it art, and is it as *good* art as the original?
- a signed urinal in a museum vs. the identical urinal at the hardware store
- a conceptual piece that's literally just a certificate: the buyer gets a sheet of paper authorizing them to scribble a line on a wall, and the certificate is the artwork
- an AI-generated image that wins a photo contest, made by someone who typed one prompt and clicked through 40 variations
- a beautiful natural object like a bird's nest that someone puts on a pedestal
- deliberately disgusting work, like an artist displaying rotting meat, where the whole point is nobody enjoys looking at it

Two things beyond that. First, don't build the quiz from the list of theories — build it from the case space first, figure out which answer combinations the cases make possible, and only then say where each position lands, noting any pattern thats coherent but that nobody actually defends (and whether that's because the combo is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored). Second, flag honestly where the quiz can't separate two positions — I'm guessing aesthetic functionalism and the cluster view give identical answers unless you manage a question about whether any single feature is *necessary* for art, so if there's a way to ask that without tipping people off, do it, and if not, say so.

Also give me a rough guess at how long quiz + scoring + reveal takes, so I know whether it fits my 50 minutes with room for discussion after.
