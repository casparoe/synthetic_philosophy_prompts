A woman born with a heart condition that costs a fortune to treat: obviously she should be covered, it's pure bad luck. A bloke who bet his savings on a horse and lost: he shouldn't get taxpayer money to restore his stake, that was his own daft choice. And a clever kid born to parents who can't afford tutoring, up against a less able kid with wealthy pushy parents — the second kid's results shouldn't count for more just because of the accident of family.

My brother says the rule I'm gesturing at is something like "you should be rescued from outcomes you couldn't control, and left to carry the ones you could" — responsibility-tracking or something. But that rule seems to say the smoker doesn't deserve treatment, and maybe that the failed painter deserves nothing at all since she chose badly, and I'm not sure I want to follow it there. Or maybe I should? Is there a principle that gets all my gut verdicts, or close to it?
