Write the joint statement that two opposing camps should be able to sign at the conclusion of a well-run adversarial collaboration on speciesism and the moral status of nonhuman animals.

The two camps:

1. The equal-consideration sentience theorist (broadly Singerian): moral status is fully grounded in the capacities of individual creatures. The capacity to suffer and enjoy suffices for basic status; further capacities (self-awareness, future-directed preferences, the ability to value one's own life) can raise the weight of interests but never license discounting an interest merely because of the species that holds it. On this view, species membership per se is never a moral ground, and "speciesism" names a genuine moral failing structurally analogous to racism.

2. The status-pluralist: moral status is plural and partly kind- or relation-based. Sentience does ground direct, non-instrumental claims on us, so this camp rejects the Kantian thesis that duties regarding animals are merely indirect; but rational and moral agency ground a distinct and higher tier of status, and the bearer of that tier may be a kind or community rather than a property each individual must independently pass a test for. On this view, the charge of "speciesism" either begs the question or reduces to the trivial claim that like cases should be treated alike.

The document should contain the following sections:

I. A numbered list of agreed claims. These should be substantive rather than hortatory — claims each camp would have hesitated to concede before the collaboration but can now sign without embarrassment. Plausible candidates include: that animals of many species suffer and that their suffering is intrinsically bad; that the dispute is not about whether animals matter but about how their mattering is grounded and how it compares to ours; and that at least some practices common in industrial animal agriculture cannot be defended by appeal to human gustatory or minor economic interests alone. Include the agreed claims you find most probative, not the most anodyne.

II. A disagreements table. Each residual disagreement must be stated twice — once in wording the sentience theorist would accept as a fair statement of the opposing view, and once in wording the pluralist would accept of the sentience theorist's view — so that each side has signed an accurate description of the position it rejects. Flag each disagreement as either substantive or, at least in part, verbal (e.g., over what "speciesism" means: differential consideration keyed to species membership regardless of capacity differences, versus any differential consideration that tracks species even where capacities differ).

III. A cruxes section. For each crux, specify one case, thought experiment, or line of inquiry that both camps agree would bear on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. The following deserve attention, though you may add or substitute others:

- The argument from marginal cases. Does the moral status of human infants and severely cognitively impaired individuals vindicate individual-capacity grounding (the sentience theorist's prediction), or does it instead expose that grounding's instability and favor kind- or relation-based accounts, under which a parent's or community's duties to an infant track facts about relations and kinds rather than measured capacities (the pluralist's prediction)?
- The continuum question. Confronted with a series of hypothetical beings interpolating between a typical chimpanzee and a typical adult human — an animal cognitively enhanced to borderline human levels, a reconstructed extinct hominin, an extraterrestrial species of our intellectual equals — do our considered judgments track degrees of status or a threshold? Each side should record, before considering the cases, the pattern of judgments it expects itself and most reflective people to have.
- The empirical distribution of sentience. How confident should we be that decapod crustaceans, cephalopod molluscs, and insects are sentient, and what kinds of evidence (neuroanatomy, nociceptive behavior, motivational trade-offs, analgesia-seeking) should move that credence? Here a real-world anchor exists: the UK's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act 2022 extended legal recognition of sentience to decapod crustaceans and cephalopod molluscs following an academic review of the scientific evidence commissioned for the government. The camps disagree about how far such evidence supports protection-relevant status, and each should state what would raise or lower its credence.
- Lexicality. Is the difference between persons and sentient non-persons a difference in degree, however enormous, or a difference in kind permitting lexical ordering, under which no number of non-person interests could outweigh a single major interest of a person? The sentience theorist should address whether large differences in weight, plus agent-relative prerogatives, can capture the intuitions motivating the lexical claim; the pluralist should address whether lexicality can be non-arbitrarily drawn and whether it does the work the tiered view actually needs.

Throughout, aim for formulations that are neither vacuous nor slanted toward either camp, and make the residual disagreement exact rather than rhetorical. If part of the dispute reduces to terminological choice — most plausibly the meaning of "speciesism" itself — say which part, and propose disambiguating vocabulary both camps could adopt without conceding anything substantive. No citations, reading lists, or appeals to named authorities beyond what is given here; the point is the positions, not the literature.
