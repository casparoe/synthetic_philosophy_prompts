I've been an active reader of the rationalist blogosphere for over a decade — Sequences, everything downstream of them, most of the consciousness content that circulates on LessWrong and its adjacent substacks. At an AI-safety meetup last weekend, a friend of mine who is usually the most technically reliable person in the room gave the group this summary of Integrated Information Theory, and I'd like an accuracy audit of it before I repeat any of it: "IIT says that consciousness just is information processing, and the more information a system integrates — the more its parts act together as a unified whole rather than independently — the more conscious it is. The measure they use, phi, is essentially a complexity score: you partition the system every way you can and check how much information processing the partition destroys. At bottom it's a functionalist theory, so what matters is the pattern of information flow rather than the substrate, and the right software running on any hardware would be just as conscious as the brain it simulates. This makes IIT a kind of digital panpsychism, since even simple interconnected circuits have a nonzero glimmer of experience. Of course, Aaronson's expander-grid argument then landed the fatal blow: a dumb mesh of logic gates can have enormous phi, so the theory over-generates consciousness and most people have written it off."

Something about this bothered me even though I couldn't put my finger on it. My prior from the blogosphere is anti-IIT — I read Scott Aaronson's critique when it made the rounds, and the 'why is my ventilation system not conscious' style objection always struck me as forceful — so I was inclined to accept my friend's version wholesale. But I also have a memory of the IIT camp insisting, oddly, that a perfect simulation of a conscious system would *not* be conscious, which doesn't sit well with the summary calling IIT functionalist and substrate-independent. And the two words being used almost interchangeably — 'information processing' and 'integrated information' — seem like exactly the sort of thing that could be hiding a distinction the theory itself treats as load-bearing.

So here's what I'd like from you. Go through the summary claim by claim and tell me which parts Tononi and the IIT school would actually endorse, which are conflations of positions the theory keeps distinct, and which are flat inversions of what IIT maintains — pay particular attention to the substrate-independence claim and to what phi is supposed to measure (is it a proxy for how much the system does, or for something about its internal causal structure?). Then evaluate the two framing moves at the end: whether IIT's panpsychism is fairly described as 'digital,' and whether Aaronson's grid objection is best read as a refutation that 'most people' have accepted or as a live dispute over which systems the theory counts as conscious — including whether the IIT side has anything principled to say back about feedforward versus recurrent architecture. Finally, tell me whether my friend's implicit conclusion — that IIT is functionalism plus a complexity metric, and is broken because of it — follows from the accurate residue of his summary or only from the misrepresentations mixed in. I want to come away knowing exactly which sentences I could quote back to him and which ones I'd have to correct first.
