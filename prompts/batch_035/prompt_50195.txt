And here is the student's response, transcribed in full:

> (a) Bayes's rule presupposes that the evidence arrives with certainty — the posterior comes from setting the probability of the evidence proposition to one. But most learning is not like this, and Jeffrey was concerned with what Glymour later called the problem of old evidence: much of what we learn cannot be expressed as any proposition we become certain of. Jeffrey's rule, from his 1965 book The Logic of Decision, handles this. If experience shifts your degree of belief in E from P(E) to some new value q, then for any hypothesis H your new probability is P(H|E)q + P(H|¬E)(1 − q). The idea is that the conditional probabilities given E and given ¬E carry over unchanged, and only the weighting is adjusted. The rule generalises straightforwardly to a finite partition: just replace the two terms with a sum over the cells, weighted by their new probabilities.
> (b) Rigidity is the requirement that P(H|E) and P(H|¬E) take the same values before and after the update. It is what allows the rule to be derived at all, since once the new probability of E is fixed and the conditionals are fixed, the new probability of every H is determined. It is worth stressing that rigidity is not really an additional assumption on top of the probability calculus: it follows from the axioms, since conditional probabilities are defined as ratios, and an experience that only alters the probability of E leaves the ratios intact automatically.
> (c) Put q = 1 in the rule: P(H|E)·1 + P(H|¬E)·0 = P(H|E), which is exactly classical conditionalisation on E. Similarly, q = 0 recovers conditioning on ¬E. So Bayes's rule is the limiting case in which the evidence is perfectly certain.
> (d) The order does not matter. A Jeffrey update is just a weighted average of the old conditional probabilities, with weights q and 1 − q, and weighted averages commute: averaging with one pair of weights and then another gives the same result in either order. Rigidity keeps the relevant conditionals fixed throughout, so nothing can depend on the sequence in which the updates are applied. This is a genuine advantage of Jeffrey conditioning over more permissive belief-revision frameworks, where the order of updates can affect the outcome.
