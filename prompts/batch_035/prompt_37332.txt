Trying to figure out what I actually believe about eating animals, purely from my gut reactions to cases. Here are my verdicts, and I want to know if there's some principle behind them or whether I'm just being inconsistent in a boring way.

1. Eating a chicken breast is worse than eating a steak, per meal. More animals per meal, worse lives, worse deaths. Pretty confident about this one.
2. Even one chicken wing feels worse than a big steak. Like, disproportionate to the amount of meat. Not sure whats going on there.
3. Hunting a deer and eating it for months feels better than buying chicken. One death, many meals.
4. But I also feel like I shouldn't personally go hunting, and I can't explain why given #3.
5. Taking home meat that would otherwise get thrown out at the end of a party: totally fine. No causal impact on anything.
6. But someone who eats only discarded meat, full-time freegan, pulling perfectly good sandwiches from dumpsters — I admire that person less than a vegetarian, even though the vegetarian causes more animal deaths through demand. Something is off there and I know it.
7. Here's the one that bugs me most: I've seen the argument that plowing and harvesting grains kills a lot of field animals, rodents and bugs and so on, maybe enough that a calorie of bread costs more animal lives than a calorie of beef from a pasture-raised cow. I do NOT believe the conclusion that beef is better than crops. But I'm not sure I have a principled reason to reject it, since...
8. ...I do think wild animals' suffering counts morally. A rabbit shredded by a combine matters. I'm not one of those people who says nature is off the books.
9. But I also don't think we're obligated to do predator control or otherwise go fix the wilderness, the way I think we're obligated to shut down factory farms. And if the rabbit in the field and the hen in the cage matter the same, I owe an explanation for that difference.
10. Oysters I'll eat without any guilt at all. Crickets too. Lab-grown meat once it's cheap, obviously fine.

So: is there a principle that captures all this, or close to it? It seems like #1 and #3 point at something like deaths-per-meal, but #7 shows I won't follow that where it leads. #5 and #6 look like a causal contribution story that I then refuse to apply to the freegan. Maybe it's a hybrid, maybe its just vibes. I'd also like to know what your principle says about cases I haven't mentioned — like eggs (one hen, two years of eggs) and whether dairy-linked beef is different from beef cattle.

And the case I keep flip-flopping on: someone who eats leftover catering at every office meeting, every week, for years. Each individual time it's going to waste, so #5 says fine. Is it fine forever? If your principle says something different than my gut there, say so plainly.
