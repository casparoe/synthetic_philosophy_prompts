I'm a retired condensed-matter physicist who's gotten way too into philosophy of physics in my retirement, and I've somehow ended up on the curriculum advisory board of my old department. We're having a silly but surprisingly heated fight about the undergraduate quantum mechanics course. For decades it's been taught in the standard shut-up-and-calculate way, but a younger colleague who works on quantum information wants to at least mention interpretations, and a visiting lecturer is an outspoken Everettian who thinks presenting collapse as "the" story to 19-year-olds is basically teaching them a lie. Another senior guy on the board thinks even mentioning interpretations in a first course is a waste of precious lecture time that should go to perturbation theory. The department chair just wants the board to make the call so it stops eating every meeting.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before our next meeting. It should recommend a concrete decision -- what gets taught, to whom, and when in the course (or nothing at all, if that's your call) -- not just survey the options. Some things I'd want it to grapple with: whether mentioning "the measurement problem" without resolving it leaves students more confused or usefully humble, whether any of the three main interpretations is actually better or worse pedagogically for someone who's just learned what a wavefunction is, and whether the Everettian's "we'd be teaching a lie" framing is fair to how the Copenhagen-style presentation actually works in practice. The memo should be something physicists who disagree with each other could still read without rolling their eyes, and give the losing side an honest shake rather than just steamrolling them. Keep in mind the students are mostly headed for industry or grad school, not foundations of physics.

One more thing: I've personally drifted toward thinking decoherence at least explains why we never *see* macroscopic superpositions, so feel free to factor that in or push back on it, just don't make the memo sound like it's my personal crusade. It should read like it comes from the board, not from one grumpy old emeritus with opinions.
