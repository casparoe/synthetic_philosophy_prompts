I'm taking an intermediate ethics seminar and writing a term paper on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism — the old worry that there seems to be nothing qualitative that the pleasure of eating a peach, the pleasure of understanding a proof, and the pleasure of revenge all share, in which case what is the "pleasure" that hedonism says is the only intrinsic good? My draft's core move is a proposal I haven't seen anywhere (possibly because it's bad): that what all pleasures share is not a felt quality but a kind of self-endorsement in the experience's own presentation. The thought is that pleasure is an experience that presents itself as worth continuing — its phenomenal character includes a "more of this" directedness, a pro-attitude built into the seeming itself rather than reported about it afterward. So the common element across heterogeneous pleasures is imperative rather than descriptive: each pleasure carries its own continuation as part of how it appears. Call this the "recommendatory" account of pleasure's commonality. On this basis I want to argue that hedonism survives: the theory needn't identify a single felt tone, only a single structural feature, and structural features can do aggregative work just as well.

The two big problems I've flagged for myself so far are these. First, masochism and relief: the masochist's experience seems to include both an aversive "stop this" component and something worth continuing, and my instinct is to say the recommendation and the aversion attach to different aspects of the experience — but I worry that this "different aspects" move is exactly the kind of ad hoc tap-dancing that makes people give up on phenomenal analyses in the first place. Second, and more worrying to me: even if the recommendatory account captures what pleasures share, it's not obvious that a merely formal commonality can carry the normative weight. Hedonism needs to compare and sum pleasures, and I can see how you'd compare intensity on a felt-tone view, but what does intensity even mean if the common element is a "continue!" component? Is a more insistent recommendation a stronger pleasure, and isn't that going to misclassify obsessive cravings as intense pleasures?

What I'd most like from you is comments on the central move rather than general feedback on the paper. Specifically: (a) does the recommendatory account actually dissolve the heterogeneity problem, or does it just relocate it — since "presenting itself as worth continuing" might itself be heterogeneous across cases, with sensory pleasures recommending themselves one way and intellectual pleasures another? (b) Am I right that the intensity/comparability problem is the deeper one, or is there some standard response to measurement worries that I'm missing? And (c) is there an obvious fatal objection to building a pro-attitude into the phenomenal character of an experience — something about the difference between how an experience feels and what it tells you to do — that I should address before building the whole paper on this? If you think the whole strategy is a dead end, tell me that plainly, and say what a better way of answering "what do pleasures have in common?" would look like for a hedonist who wants to keep the aggregation.
