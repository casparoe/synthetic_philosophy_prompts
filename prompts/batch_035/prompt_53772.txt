Here is my opening move. Your solution says that "green" wins over "grue" because "green" is the better-entrenched predicate — its parent predicates have a longer and heavier record of past projection. But entrenchment is nothing but a summary of past projections, and you yourself refuse to let induction be justified by its own past successes; that was the trap the old riddle set. So it appears you have answered the question of which inductive inferences we are entitled to by pointing at the inferences we have habitually made — Hume's habit of mind, one level up, with the word "entrenchment" pasted on. And there is a second problem: every emerald observation that supports the generalization that all emeralds are green equally supports, up to the present, the generalization that all emeralds are grue, since every emerald examined so far is both. Given that grue applies to everything green applies to among observed cases, its record of successful projection should accumulate at exactly the same rate as green's. If entrenchment cannot break this symmetry, it does not solve your riddle; if it can, I want to know what facts about the history of projection do the breaking, and why those facts are not themselves just the outputs of past inductions taking themselves as input.
