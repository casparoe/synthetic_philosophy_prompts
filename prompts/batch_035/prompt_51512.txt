I host a small philosophy-of-mathematics podcast, and next month's guest is a strict finitist: he holds that there are only finitely many objects of any kind whatsoever, numbers included, and that talk of an actually infinite totality is not merely false but incoherent. He is genial but evasive under pressure — his standard move is to say that classical mathematics is a useful fiction and that questions about "what really exists" within a fiction are somehow misplaced. Draft ten interview questions designed to close off that escape route.

I want at least one question each on the following, and you should weave the rest around them rather than padding:

(a) Hilbert's hotel — whether its strange consequences (every room occupied, yet room always available) are evidence of genuine incoherence in the idea of an infinite totality, or merely the alien but consistent implications of a well-defined theory, and how he tells those two cases apart in general;

(b) Cantor's diagonal argument — whether he rejects it outright or accepts it as a schema about finite strings and proof-tokens that never actually quantifies over a completed totality, and what work remains for the theorem once it is reinterpreted that way;

(c) the feasibility cutoff — where, in his view, the finite gives out. In particular, how he blocks the sorites-style argument that if zero is feasible and adding one to a feasible number leaves it feasible, then every number is feasible; and why the arbitrariness of any proposed cutoff is not fatal to the view;

(d) physics — whether the real-number continuum in our best physical theories is an indispensable commitment of those theories or a convenient bookkeeping device, and what he would count as evidence for the second reading.

For each question, commit to the specific answer you expect him to give, and write a follow-up keyed to exactly that answer — not a menu of possible replies with a branch for each. If you think two of your draft questions are the same question in different clothes, say so and keep only one rather than inflating the count. Finally, tell me which single question you would most regret leaving unasked and why: I want a definite top pick with your reasoning, not a shortlist of finalists.
