I sit on a small review board that assigns protection levels to systems, and I want the specification written before we argue about any particular theory. The levels are 'none,' 'minimal,' and 'full,' with intermediate grades permitted, and the systems come in every substrate: cultured neural tissue at various days of development, animals under sedation, robots, software agents whose training objectives give us reason to suspect something like distress. The board's constraints are practical, and the spec has to respect them: each system gets a bounded observation window; every verdict must be justified in writing to people who never saw the system; and a system examined twice should get the same level both times.

Please write the list of requirements any method for assigning these levels must meet, with the case or consideration behind each. Some candidates I expect on the list, though you should revise or reject them if they are not genuine requirements: the method must not contradict what is known from biology — for instance, that nociception can occur without anything like experience, as a spinal reflex shows, and that the same outward behavior can be produced by structures we have independent reason to think are experientially blank; it must distinguish having some status from how much status it has, so that 'minimal' means something other than 'less certain of full'; it must handle systems that gradually acquire or lose the relevant property, since the board sees developing tissue and recovering patients, and we will not accept a method whose verdicts flip discontinuously from 'none' to 'full' across a one-day change; it must say whether systems identical in causal organization but built of different matter get the same level, or state in the spec itself why substance matters; and it must define what a verdict of 'none' asserts, because 'no experience found within the window' and 'no experience' are different claims, and the board cannot afford to conflate them.

For each requirement, mark whether it is non-negotiable or instead reflects the commitments of a particular camp rather than the job itself. Then the part I most want: identify any subset of the requirements that is jointly unsatisfiable. I suspect there is one. A method that guarantees the same level to systems identical in causal organization, while deciding from bounded observation, seems to promise something bounded observation cannot deliver — two systems can agree on everything visible in the window yet differ in structure that is never probed or only diverge later. If some requirements must be traded off, say which trade-off you would recommend and what the board is buying with it. Close with a one-line scorecard for three familiar approaches — pure behavioral and functional testing, higher-order-thought views, and views that tie status to biological affinity — and name the single requirement that eliminates the most candidates.
