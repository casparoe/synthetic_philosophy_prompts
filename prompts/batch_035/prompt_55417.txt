I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background, and I've been reading about embedded agency from the ML side -- the idea that an agent is a physical/computational process sitting inside the environment it's modeling and acting on, instead of an outside optimizer with a clean boundary between it and the world. Problem is, a lot of what I read feels like people proposing pieces without agreeing on what the finished thing is even supposed to do. So before I waste more weekends on this, I want the requirements doc.

Write me the desiderata: what any adequate account of embedded agency actually has to deliver. I have some candidate requirements floating around from stuff I've half-digested -- use them, fix them, or toss them:

- the agent's world model has to include the agent itself, without infinite regress (no full self-copy inside the model, because that copy needs a copy, etc.)
- it has to work when the agent's own compute is a resource sitting in the environment, one that can be throttled or taken away -- no assuming unbounded compute
- it can't lean on a clean input/output boundary, since in the embedded picture the "interface" between agent and world is just more physics
- it has to say what happens when your decision procedure is itself a thing in the world that other processes (and other agents, and future-you) can inspect and predict -- I think this is where all the decision theory fights come from (causal vs evidential vs the updateless/functional stuff), but I don't fully get why this requirement forces that fight
- it has to play nice with the limitative results -- Gödel, the halting problem, Löb's theorem -- i.e. no theory that quietly assumes the agent can prove its own reasoning sound
- bonus: does it need to handle agents whose goals are themselves part of what gets modeled and possibly revised mid-run?

For each requirement, give the motivating case -- some concrete scenario where an agent built on a theory that ignores it predictably fails -- and mark which are hard requirements versus preferences of one research camp. And this is the part I most want: tell me if some subset is jointly unsatisfiable. Like, maybe you literally can't have full self-modeling plus guaranteed sound reasoning plus no outside vantage point, in which case the spec itself is the interesting result, and I'd want to know which requirement people actually drop in practice.

Then briefly run two or three existing approaches agianst the list -- whatever the natural contenders are, like the decision theories or the "agent foundations" style agenda. Don't spend much on that, the criteria are the part I care about.

One framing note: I think in terms of systems -- processes, resources, interfaces, specs. If any requirement turns out to be the philosophical version of "you can't have a distributed system that's consistent, available, AND partition-tolerant at once," flag it explicitly, because that's exactly the kind of thing I'd find clarifying.
