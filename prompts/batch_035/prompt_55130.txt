I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums since the Overlapping... I mean the old Overcoming Bias days, and I'm used to infinities doing quiet work in our arguments — infinite utilities, Pascal's mugging, the shut-up-and-multiply debates — so I didn't expect to see the foundational question relitigated at my local meetup. But last weekend two people I'll call Dara and Marco spent forty minutes on whether Hilbert's hotel is a genuine reductio of actual infinities, and their exchange went roughly as follows. (1) Dara opened with the standard setup: a hotel with countably infinitely many rooms, every one occupied, and yet infinitely many new guests can be accommodated by moving each current guest from room n to room 2n. A hotel that is completely full and yet has room for infinitely many more is absurd, she said, so actually infinite collections cannot exist. (2) Marco replied that this trades on an equivocation on full: full means every room is occupied, and the discovery that a proper subset of an infinite set can be put in one-to-one correspondence with the whole is precisely Cantor's result, not a bug in it. The felt absurdity is a fact about our finite-trained intuitions, the same sort of shock people had with non-Euclidean geometry, and it is no more an argument against infinite sets than the strangeness of relativity is against spacetime. (3) Dara countered with the checkout version: if the guests in all even-numbered rooms leave, infinitely many remain; if the guests in every room except room 1 leave, only one remains; and by choosing which rooms empty out you can make the remaining occupancy any finite number, or zero — yet countably many guests have left in every case. If the very same event, countably many guests leaving, is compatible with any outcome whatsoever, then asking how many guests are in the hotel has become meaningless, and a physical process whose outcome cannot even in principle be pinned down by its inputs is worse than strange. (4) Marco said she was smuggling physics into mathematics: cardinal arithmetic is consistent, and the indeterminacy lies in the description of the physical rearrangement rather than in the mathematics — countably many leave simply underspecifies which guests leave, and different specifications leave different remainders even in finite hotels. (5) Dara's last move: then the finitists get the last laugh — if the mathematics is consistent but the concept cannot survive contact with any physical description, then actual infinity is a fiction, and everyone who worries about infinite ethics is reasoning about nothing. Marco answered that one can coherently treat infinite sets as legitimate mathematics while doubting they are instantiated anywhere, and that Dara's finite-hotel analogy is question-begging anyway, since no finite hotel, however enormous, has the everyone-still-fits property even in attenuated form.

Both of them are smarter than average about this and neither was strawmanning, so I'd like a genuine adjudication. Please go through the five moves in order, rating each from clearly mistaken to clearly right with a brief justification, then say who is more right overall and what, if anything, both of them are missing — for instance whether the real dispute is about mathematics at all, or about how much evidential weight intuitions of absurdity deserve when a concept has been formally regimented. Since this is exactly the kind of topic where I've watched confident people be confidently wrong in both directions, please also say how confident you are in each of your ratings, and explicitly single out the point in your adjudication that you are least sure of.
