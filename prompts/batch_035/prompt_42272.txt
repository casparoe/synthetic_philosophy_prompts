Something I keep noticing and can't find a word for: certain cultural items spread not because anyone wants to copy them, but because the item itself contains instructions for its own reproduction, plus a threat or reward for compliance. Chain letters are the cleanest case -- the whole message is "send this to ten people or bad luck follows," and plenty of people who dont believe a word of it forward it anyway, just incase. Those "forward this and Bill Gates will pay you" email hoaxes, pyramid recruiting pitches, and versions of religion where salvation partly depends on winning converts all work the same way. The item isn't attractive; it issues commands to its carriers. It's different from a catchy song that people pass on because they like it, and it's not ordinary advertising either, since an ad doesn't demand that the ad itself be reproduced word for word along with the recommendation. Is there an established term for this mechanism -- spread by embedded command rather than spread by appeal -- in the study of cultural evolution or anywhere else? If there is, I want to know how exactly it differs from just calling everything a meme, since plain imitation doesn't seem to capture the carrot-and-stick part. And if there isn't a name, coin one: give a real definition, say where it stops being this and starts being ordinary persuasion or a practice people adopt for their own reasons, and give a couple of boundary cases.
