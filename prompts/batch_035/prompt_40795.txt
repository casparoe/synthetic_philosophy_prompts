My friend Dev has been on an omnipotence kick since taking a phil of religion elective, and last night he ambushed me with a version of the stone paradox that he swears dodges the standard reply. Three hours later I still can't tell if he's right, so here's his argument, as faithfully as I can reconstruct it:

(1) To be omnipotent, a being has to be able to do anything that it is logically possible for an agent to do. Dev says this is the definition everyone accepts once you handle the square-circle stuff -- tasks have to be logically possible, otherwise not even God can draw a round square, and nobody thinks that counts against him.

(2) There is a task that is logically possible for an agent to do: making a stone that its own creator cannot lift. Dev's evidence is me. Give me a weekend, a bag of cement and a shovel and I will absolutely build something in my backyard that I cannot lift. So the task is not a disguised square circle; it gets performed constantly, by ordinary people.

(3) So an omnipotent being can make a stone that its creator cannot lift.

(4) If it does, then from that moment on there is something it cannot do, namely lift that stone. And Dev insists omnipotence can't be the kind of thing you can spend, like a battery -- if the being was omnipotent before making the stone and not after, it was never really omnipotent in the first place.

(5) If it cannot make such a stone, then by (1) and (2) it was never omnipotent either, since I just did the very thing it cannot do.

(6) So there is no omnipotent being.

I tried the usual move on him, that "a stone an omnipotent being cannot lift" is just a contradiction wearing a trenchcoat. He said that's exactly what (2) is designed to block: notice he never mentions God in the task description. The task is "make a stone its creator cannot lift," full stop, and it's possible because I've done it. If the theist gets to declare a possible task impossible-for-God-anyway, then it looks like anyone gets to do that about any task they happen to fail at.

But here's what actually kept me up. As the night went on Dev got more excited, because he realized the reasoning seems to spill past God entirely. Following the same pattern, it started to look like nobody can have the powers they plainly have -- I can build a thing I can't lift, but I also can't build a thing that some being with unlimited lifting strength couldn't lift, and it's suddenly unclear whether that second fact means anything at all about my abilities. Or alternatively it looked like "maximal power" is incoherent for any agent whatsoever, not just infinite ones, which is a way weirder conclusion than the one Dev started with.

So: which premise or step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? For what it's worth I'd like to keep (1), since it just seems like the definition, and (2), since I really am going to end up with an unliftable pile of concrete in my backyard. But I can't see how to keep both and deny (6).
