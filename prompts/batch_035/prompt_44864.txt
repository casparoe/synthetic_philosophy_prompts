My philosophy of mind reading group has spent two terms on the meta-problem of consciousness and hit a wall I'd like diagnosed rather than adjudicated. The illusionists in the room say that once you explain why we judge that experience has intrinsic "phenomenal" properties -- some quirk in how introspection represents our own cognitive processes, which cognitive science can in principle get at -- the hard problem dissolves: the felt mystery is just one more datum about how we represent things, nothing about experience itself. The realists reply that any such explanation is told entirely in functional terms, so it either leaves out why those mechanisms produce judgements with the content that there's something it's like to be us, or it quietly helps itself to the very consciousness it was meant to explain away. What I've noticed is that both sides treat the other's core move as begging the question, and neither will say what non-question-begging evidence could even count here -- the actual cognitive science is basically agreed on by everyone present, the neural facts aren't in dispute, and still nobody budges. So what kind of disagreement is this really? An empirical one about what's in our heads, a verbal one about what "consciousness" is supposed to pick out, or a deeper clash about what an explanation of a judgement owes you -- and if it's the last, is there anything that could settle it, or are we just watching the hard problem get copied into a new folder?
