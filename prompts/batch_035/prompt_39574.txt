so I'm a novelist and I've been in a running argument with my editor about whether the chatbots understand anything, and it's made me realize my own views on meaning are maybe a mess. here's roughly where I stand: (1) words mean what they mean to me because they're rooted in lived bodily experience — I know "salt" because I've tasted it, I know "ache" because I've ached, and I think that's what understanding ultimately is, everything else is stacked on top of that. (2) I also think meaning is basically use — a word means whatever a community of speakers does with it in practice, and there's no further private fact behind the public one. (3) in my last book I invented a sensation, the effect of a drug that feels something like hearing your own name as a color, and readers write to me saying they understood it, felt it even, and I'm inclined to say they genuinely did understand it. (4) I don't think the language models understand anything when they write — it's ungrounded mimicry, however good it gets. (5) but I'm also happy to say I understand words like "inflation" or "electorn" or "annulment" even though I've never experienced any of them directly — my whole grip on those comes from other words and pictures. does this package hang together, or am I contradicticting myself somewhere? tell me which parts are actually pulling against each other, which ones only look like they conflict, and what the cheapest thing to give up would be — and please rank the tensions by severity instead of just listing everything as equally bad. I honestly don't know where the problem is, if there is one.
