Before I read any further in the naturalistic literature on religion — cognitive byproduct accounts, cultural evolutionary ones, commitment-signaling ones — I want the specification first: what must a theory of religion as a natural phenomenon actually deliver? Write the desiderata, with the motivating case behind each. Ones I can already see: it must explain near-universality and the recurrent design (supernatural agents, afterlife beliefs, ritual, taboo) without leaning on diffusion or revelation; it must solve the costliness problem, since a cognitive accident should not be this expensive to maintain — yet sacrifice, celibacy, and martyrdom are; it must handle the pattern that gods in small-scale societies tend to be amoral busybodies while morally concerned high gods appear alongside large, anonymous societies, and it must do so without treating contested evidence about that correlation as settled; it must explain secularization without consigning it to a supplementary chapter, since a theory on which religion is the human default owes us an account of how whole societies exit the default; it must say whether "religion" even carves a natural kind or is an artifact of our category; and it should be falsifiable rather than a just-so story that absorbs whatever data arrives. Beyond those, add anything the list is missing. For each requirement, tell me whether it is non-negotiable or a prejudice of one camp — I suspect the demand that the theory stay neutral on religious truth, on the grounds that origins don't settle veracity, is more of a camp preference than a genuine constraint, but I'm not sure neutrality is even coherent for a theory that explains belief-forming mechanisms. Flag any pair of desiderata you think cannot be jointly satisfied: the costliness requirement and the byproduct intuition seem to pull apart, and I'd like to know if that tension is real or an artifact of how I've framed them. Identify the single requirement that eliminates the most candidate theories. Then, briefly and only briefly, score the three families — byproduct, cultural evolutionary, commitment-signaling — against the list; I care about the criteria, not the scorecard.
