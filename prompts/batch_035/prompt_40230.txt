I teach an intro-ish seminar on philosophy of cosmology, and for the last few weeks we do the Fermi paradox, ending with Liu Cixin's trilogy (The Three-Body Problem, but especially The Dark Forest). The trilogy basically dramatizes one candidate solution: the sky is quiet because in a universe where any civilization can strike any other across interstellar distances, the rational move on hearing a signal is to destroy the sender before they can do it to you — so everyone who survived long enough learned to stay quiet. Read the trilogy through the actual Fermi paradox debate for me. Also tell me whether the fictional scaffolding — sophons that surveil everything on Earth, a Wallfacer project that exists only because thought is the one thing that can be kept secret — sharpens the philosophical point, or whether it makes the analogy to nuclear deterence almost too neat.
