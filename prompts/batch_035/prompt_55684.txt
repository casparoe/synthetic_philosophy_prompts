I'm an undergraduate taking a seminar on rational agency, and my term project is to stress-test the idea of surrogate goals. In the framework we've been studying, an agent that expects to be threatened can commit to treating demands aimed at some issue it has no real stake in as though they were attacks on something it does care about — the surrogate goal — so that a potential threatener, who plans by predicting the agent's standing policy rather than by observing its acts in the moment, concludes in advance that threatening it will not pay. I want a thought experiment in which this works exactly as advertised and still leaves the agent worse off than if it had never adopted the surrogate goal.

Build the case to the following specification. The setting is ordinary life — a freelancer and her clients, a firm and its suppliers, something on that scale — with ordinary payoffs in money, time, and reputation; no superintelligences, no simulations, no exotic physics, and no predictor more accurate than a shrewd person who has read the agent's public commitments. The agent's stake in the surrogate issue must be genuinely near zero, so that the surrogate goal is doing the work rather than some disguised real interest. The first threat must be one the surrogate goal deters cleanly. The second threat must exist only because of the surrogate commitment: a party who could have profited from the agent before, or would not have bothered, but who now finds a cheap lever in the commitment itself. And the verdicts must be earned from the logic of commitment and prediction, not stipulated by the scenario's wording.

Please present the result as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the scenario; (2) why commitment-based deterrence reasoning endorses the surrogate goal against the first threatener; (3) how the second threatener exploits the commitment; (4) which structural feature of the case does the work, and what happens to the example if that feature is removed; (5) the strongest reply a defender of surrogate goals would give, and what that reply costs the defender in other cases.
