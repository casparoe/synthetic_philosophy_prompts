My study group keeps fighting about Sleeping Beauty and one of us produced an argument I cant quite shake off. (1) On Sunday night, Beauty's credence that the coin will land Heads is 1/2. (2) When she wakes up during the experiment she learns nothing she didn't already know: she was certain in advance that she'd be woken at least once, and the experience of waking up is exactly the same however the coin lands. (3) If you gain no new evidence, your credence shouldn't move. (4) So on waking her credence in Heads is still 1/2 — the halfer answer. (5) Now run the extreme version of the case: if the coin lands Tails, she is woken 1,000 times instead of twice, and on each waking she's offered a bet that pays $1 if the coin landed Heads and costs 10 cents if it landed Tails, settled once per waking. (6) By the same reasoning her credence in Heads is still 1/2 whenever she wakes, so the bet looks obviously worth taking every time. (7) But in the Tails world she takes the bet a thousand times and loses about $100 all told, against winning $1 in the Heads world, so following the argument she predictably loses roughly $50 in expectation, and a view of rational credence that predictably burns money like that looks broken. (8) Therefore the halfer line refutes itself, and she ought to lower her credence in Heads on waking. I'd honestly like to keep (3) — it feels like it's just true that evidence should do the work. My suspicion is that something dodgy happens between (6) and (7), maybe an equivocation between her credence in Heads on this particular waking versus what she should do about a bet that gets offered a number of times that depends on the coin. Which step should I reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost? Please answer in British English.
