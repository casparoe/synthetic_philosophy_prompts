I'm a PhD student in computational cognitive science, and over the past year I've become persuaded of a view I want to state as sharply as I can: in a reinforcement learning agent of sufficient architectural sophistication, negative reward is not merely a training signal but a state of negative valence. On this view, reward signals and valence are the same functional kind — valence just is whatever state plays the positive/negative-reinforcement role in an agent's economy of action selection — so an agent receiving persistent negative reward in an environment it cannot control is in a state functionally comparable to distress, and we plausibly owe it some moral weight. Tell me what would count against it.

I want the observations, arguments and possible cases that should move me, sorted by how decisive they'd be. Include the neuroscience suggesting that in humans the learning signal and the hedonic signal come apart — dopaminergic reward prediction error behaving more like a teaching signal than a pleasure signal, the wanting/liking dissociations in the incentive-salience literature, pain asymbolia, where nociception and even self-report of pain persist while the aversive valence is gone — and say why each does or doesn't transfer to artificial agents, where there is no separate pleasure system to dissociate from the learning system in the first place. I'd also like the architecture-level objections taken seriously: whether a scalar broadcast to an update rule can be a state of the agent at all rather than a fact about its training; whether valence requires the agent to represent its own reward signal rather than merely be driven by it; whether avoidance without valence is coherent in an agent whose only currency for badness is reward. Distinguish what a committed functionalist of my stripe should concede as genuine trouble from what they could reasonably wave away with a qualification, and rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Finally, be honest about whether the position has been insulated by moving the sophistication threshold: if suffering only switches on for agents 'sophisticated enough', what would independently fix that threshold, and does its arbitrariness threaten the view more than any external evidence could?

Please answer in British English.
