I co-convene a graduate seminar on rational choice theory, and one of our regular participants — a colleague from the economics department with firm views on Ellsberg-style cases and a principled refusal to commit to any formal framework — has agreed to let us record her verdict on a case we are preparing in advance. She describes herself as "a stable pattern of choices in search of a theory," and I should like your help in predicting her next choice.

Her track record, all established under forced choice with no option to abstain:

1. With a fair coin she is precisely risk-neutral: she will pay up to £50, but not £51, for a ticket paying £100 on heads.
2. Given a choice between £100 if she draws red from an urn known to contain 50 red and 50 black balls, and £100 if she draws red from an urn of 100 red and black balls in entirely unknown proportions, she takes the known urn; likewise when the prize is on black.
3. With an urn containing 30 red balls and 60 black and yellow balls in unknown proportions — she insists that for all she knows the split could be anything from 60 black to 60 yellow — she prefers a £100 bet on red to one on black, prefers yellow to black, is indifferent between red and yellow, and remains indifferent between black and yellow even at £10,000 stakes.
4. She will pay up to £30, but not £31, for a £100 ticket on red from that same urn; she will pay noticeably less for a £100 ticket on black, though she has never let us pin down how much less.

The case we have prepared for next week uses the same three-colour urn (30 red; 60 black and yellow, split unknown). She must choose exactly one of: (a) £70 for certain; (b) £250 if the ball drawn is red; (c) £250 if the ball drawn is black.

What will she pick? I care less about the answer than about the reasoning. Identify the candidate decision rules that fit her record — I want at least two that accommodate all four verdicts, together with any well-known rule that fits most of the record but visibly stumbles on one verdict — and state what each rule predicts for next week. Then give me your best guess, with your reasons for preferring it, and finally propose the further case, using the same urn, that would best discriminate between the rules you regard as still live.
