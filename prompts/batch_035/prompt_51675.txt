so heres my situation. I live in a mid-size co-op apartment building (about 60 units) in a big US city, and I'm one of the residents on our building board, which mostly argues about the boiler and garbage fees. I never went to college but I've gotten kind of obsessed with one issue and I need help with the short written opinion I'm supposed to circulate before next month's board meeting.

The issue: every December there's an unwritten thing where residents chip into a holiday pool for the building staff -- the super, the porters, the guy who shovels the snow. The suggested amount gets passed around by word of mouth. Some of these guys aren't paid great and I know for a fact a few of them count on that money, like it's part of what they budget for. Over the past couple years we've gotten a wave of new owners, a lot from countries where tipping workers isn't really a custom, and a fair number of them don't contribute. Some didn't even know the pool existed. One of them told a neighbor that tipping staff feels insulting, like treating them as beggars, and that if we want them paid more we should just pay them more, which honestly isn't a crazy point.

Now a group of longtime residents wants the board to make the pool mandatory -- a flat assessment rolled into monthly fees, handed out as a year-end bonus. Others say that kills the point, a tip you're forced to give isn't a tip. And some of the newer folks say the custom is arbitrary anyway and it's not fair to expect newcomers to follow local rules they never agreed to. I keep going back and forth. On one hand it's just a convention, conventions are arbitrary, nobody signed up for them. On the other hand, if enough people freeride the whole thing collapses and the porters eat the cost, and that starts to feel less like etiquette and more like a real obligation -- the same way cutting in line isn't just "frowned upon," it's kind of taking something from everyone behind you. And I can't figure out whether turning a convention into a rule makes things fairer or just hides the moral question inside a bill where nobody has to think about it anymore.

Please draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action -- mandatory assessment, keep it voluntary but actually explain it to new owners, or something better if you can think of it -- with the reasoning, and it has to treat the strongest argument on the losing side fairly, especially the newcomers' point that they never agreed to our custom. Also address what, if anything, the building owes new residents in the way of explaining local norms when they buy in, and whether that changes depending on whether someone's a visitor, a new owner, or been here thirty years. Keep it short enough that board members will actually read it, and in plain language -- half our board's first language isn't english.
