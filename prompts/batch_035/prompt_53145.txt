so heres my situation. i sit on a civilian oversight board in a mid-sized city (~300k people), and after a mess last summer where a wellness check ended with officers shooting the guy, the council asked us to recommend what to do with about 4% of the police budget. three proposals made the shortlist:

1) co-responder model — social workers ride along with sworn officers on mental health calls, officers stay in the lead
2) a standalone civilian crisis team that answers those calls with no sworn officer present at all, police only showing up if the team itself calls for backup
3) keep the money inside the department and spend it on de-escalation training plus body cams

the philosophical fight underneath is basically Weber's old idea that the state is whatever successfully claims a monopoly on legitimate violence in a territory, and what happens when you carve pieces out of that monopoly. the board's abolitionist-leaning members say option 2 is the only one that actually shrinks the monopoly: if a team with zero force capacity can defuse most of these crises, that shows the monopoly was never necessary for what the police sociologist Egon Bittner said the job really is — showing up when something ought not to be happening and something ought to be done about it now, whether or not force is the relevant tool. the chief's allies on the board push the opposite reading: a team with no force capacity just means the force arrives ten minutes later and less briefed, and a state that hands its core function to people who can't use force is either fooling itself or quietly building a two-tier system where the monopoly only covers certain kinds of calls.

please draft the memo i'll circulate before thursday's vote. it needs a clear recommendation among the three (a hybrid is fine but you have to say exactly what the hybrid is, not just vibes), the reasoning, and an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side — the board wants a decision we can defend at a public meeting, not a both-sides essay. it also has to say, concretely, what happens in the scenario everyone is actually worried about: the civilian team shows up and the person is armed. who do they call, what does that response look like, and what does your answer to that imply for the monopoly question — since that case is where the two readings of the whole thing collide.
