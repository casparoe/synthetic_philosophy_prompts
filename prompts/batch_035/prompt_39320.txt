I'm a third-year PhD student in quantitative social science, writing the "methodological stance" section of my dissertation prospectus. My supervisor insists that we state our commitments explicitly so the committee can probe them, and I've now got mine written down. Before I submit, I want a consistency audit. Here is the set:

1. Disaggregate by default. Given a choice of level of analysis, the more granular one is the more honest one; a pooled estimate is at best a convenience summary and should not be reported as a finding in its own right.
2. Simpson's paradox is best understood as a demonstration that aggregation over a confounder distorts: when the aggregate association and the stratum-level associations point in opposite directions, the aggregate result is simply wrong.
3. I also accept Pearl's line that whether to condition on a third variable is fixed by the causal structure, not by any statistical property of the data: the same reversal pattern warrants disaggregation if the variable is a genuine confounder but warrants the pooled estimate if the variable lies downstream of treatment, and the data alone cannot tell you which situation you are in.
4. The individually randomized trial analyzed by intention to treat remains the gold standard for causal claims, and unplanned subgroup analyses are mostly noise-mining that should be discouraged.
5. Every analysis choice — covariate set, subgroup definitions, level of aggregation — should be pre-registered; choosing the granularity after seeing the data is a garden of forking paths.
6. The Berkeley graduate admissions case remains the paradigm instance: the department-level analysis is the right one, and the institution-level numbers, though arithmetically correct, are what the paradox teaches us to discount.

Do these hang together? Please tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how severe each tension is, which pairs only look to be in tension, and what the single cheapest revision would be — that is, the one change that removes the most inconsistency at the lowest cost to the overall position, with an honest statement of what that change costs me. Order the tensions by severity rather than treating them uniformly. Please keep the whole audit under 500 words.
