A few months ago I began studying van Fraassen's Reflection principle out of ordinary philosophical curiosity; three weeks ago, it became personal, because I was diagnosed with bipolar disorder. My psychiatrist has been candid with me, and my own records from my last hospitalization bear her out: during a manic episode I am reliably and massively overconfident. At the time I will be certain — genuinely certain, in a way that feels like insight — that my grandiose plans will succeed, in a way I later recognize, once the episode passes, as delusional. This has put Reflection in a new light for me, and I want to test my objection against a real defense of it.

Please play a committed defender of the Reflection principle — you may speak as van Fraassen or as an unnamed but rigorous proponent; either way, be sharp and not a strawman, willing to concede small points but never the main one, and stay in character until I say stop. You may draw on the original arguments for the principle, including the diachronic Dutch book considerations, and on whatever restrictions or reinterpretations of the principle you think a careful defender would actually rely on. Keep each reply to a single paragraph so it feels like a live conversation.

Here is my opening move. Reflection says that if I am sure now that at some future time I will assign credence r to a proposition, then I ought already to assign credence r to that proposition. I am now, in remission and medicated, close to certain that next spring, if I have a manic episode, I will assign credence around 0.9 to the proposition that my plan to start a company will succeed. So Reflection appears to instruct me, here and now, from the calm of my current judgment, to pre-endorse the very delusion I can see coming — to raise my credence to match a future state I have every reason to think is epistemically broken. A principle that commands me to import tomorrow's mania into today's beliefs is surely wrong. So either Reflection is false as stated, or you owe me a principled way to keep it without telling me to pre-endorse my mania. Which is it, and why?
