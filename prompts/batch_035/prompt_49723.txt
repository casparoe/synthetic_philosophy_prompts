I've been following a thread on a LessWrong-adjacent forum about Dennett's tower of generate-and-test — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures — and specifically about where LLMs belong on it. Two of the regulars got into a multi-round argument, and since I couldn't referee it myself, I'd like a point-by-point verdict rather than a general take.

(1) Alice claimed LLMs are plainly Popperian: a Popperian creature is one that previews candidate actions offline, letting its hypotheses die in its stead, and a model that does nothing but generate and score continuations is doing offline hypothesis-testing all day. (2) Ben replied that the grades are about an agent's own runtime architecture, not the process that shaped it: a system that was produced entirely by selection over its outputs, and that at inference time has no lookahead of its own, is at best Skinnerian — the hypotheses that died were in the training run, not in the model, so Alice is crediting the loss landscape with being an organism. (3) Alice countered that chain-of-thought gives genuine in-episode lookahead: the model writes out a candidate step, conditions on its own text as though observing it, and revises before committing to a final answer, which is generate-and-test running in real time, not blind trial. (4) Ben said this regresses the question — the disposition to reason stepwise was itself installed by selection on outputs, so the model remains a Skinnerian creature whose environment happens to include its own past tokens; and if Alice insists otherwise, then externalized text is a mind-tool if anything is, which would make the model Gregorian too, and the tower collapses into everything-at-once. (5) Alice's last word was that the real issue is whether the unit of analysis is the deployed model at inference time or the whole training-plus-deployment pipeline, and that Dennett never had artifacts in mind whose phylogeny is a training run, so the taxonomy may just not apply.

Please adjudicate each of the five moves: who is right about which specific point, whether the tower's grades can survive the training-run/individual distinction or the token-environment move, and what, if anything, both are missing — in particular, whether treating the four grades as mutually exclusive labels rather than cumulative competences is what is generating the disagreement.
