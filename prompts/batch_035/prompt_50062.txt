I'm a final-year undergraduate taking a metaphysics module on the philosophy of science, and the debate we keep circling back to — Humeanism about the laws of nature versus its anti-Humean rivals — seems to me to have stalled in a peculiar way, and I'd like your help diagnosing why.

To describe the terrain as it plays out in our seminars: the Humeans hold that the world is just the mosaic of local, particular facts, and that laws are nothing over and above a kind of summary of that mosaic — in the more sophisticated best-system version, whatever systematising of the facts optimises the balance of simplicity and strength. The anti-Humeans in the room insist that laws must be something more: relations of necessitation between universals, or primitive governing entities, or facts about what things are disposed to do, such that the regularities are constrained rather than merely described. The exchange then runs through a handful of familiar sticking points. The anti-Humeans press the inference problem: what in the Humean picture makes it anything other than a miracle that the regularities we've seen so far continue, given that all the facts up to now are already fixed and compatible with any pattern tomorrow? The Humeans answer that it's baked into the best system that it projects the patterns it summarises, and ask in return what the anti-Humean's necessary connection explains, since it just redescribes the regularity with a stronger-sounding operator in front of it — a relation that necessitates its effects exactly when, and only when, those effects occur. The anti-Humeans then lean on counterfactuals and explanation, claiming laws support them and explain them, and that summaries can't explain what they summarise; the Humeans retort that a good summary can underwrite counterfactuals perfectly well for the purposes of prediction and intervention. Our tutor also has a scenario that keeps resurfacing: a world whose entire history contains exactly four particles and a single regularity among them, where the simplicity-strength trade-off becomes trivial, and the seminar splits over whether the lone regularity is a law of that world — which seems to reveal that we're unsure whether 'law' tracks the systematising practice or something in the world being systematised. There is also the objection that the best system depends on which predicates our language treats as natural, so that laws seem partly to depend on us; the Humeans concede some language-relativity but say all descriptive endeavours have it.

My working hypothesis is that this is at bottom a verbal dispute, at least in part: both sides agree on the totality of particular facts about how the world behaves, and disagree over whether positing something extra 'governing' those facts is a substantive metaphysical addition or just a misleading spatial metaphor — like insisting that a shadow is an additional object over and above the things casting it. But I can also see a reading on which it is genuinely substantive, because the sides seem to differ about what an explanation has to accomplish: whether citing a regularity counts as explaining the instances falling under it, or whether explanation requires appeal to something that makes the regularity non-accidental. If that's right, the laws debate may be downstream of a prior, unargued disagreement about explanation itself.

Please give me a diagnosis rather than a verdict on which metaphysics is true. What kind of disagreement is this really — verbal, or substantive, or two questions (one about ontology, one about the norms of explanation) run under a single heading? Are the standard sticking points I've listed the crux, or is something else doing the real work — and where does our tutor's four-particle scenario actually bite? What, if anything, would count as progress: a sharper distinction, a test case the sides must answer differently, a formal result, or a change in the question? And if you think no progress should be expected, say why. Throughout, please say how confident you are in each major claim, and single out the point in your diagnosis you are least sure of, explaining what makes it uncertain. Please write in British English.
