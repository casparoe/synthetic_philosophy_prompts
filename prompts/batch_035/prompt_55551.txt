My friend and I have been arguing for months about whether the winners in a genuine meritocracy actually deserve their winnings. To make it concrete: suppose a society where jobs, university places, and salaries really do go to the most talented and hardworking, with no bias, corruption, or inherited advantage. I'd put the probability that the people who come out on top in such a society genuinely deserve their higher pay at around 0.35; my friend puts it closer to 0.85. Please give me your own number, but more importantly decompose it for me: the probability that deserving something requires that a person was genuinely responsible for the qualities that earned it, the probability that even talent and hard work trace back to luck in the end (genes, upbringing, being born with a temperament that makes effort feel easy rather than costly), the probability that what the market pays someone actually tracks how much good they do for others rather than scarcity and bargaining power, and any other terms you think belong in the calculation. Give each a rough probability with one line of justification, combine them, and then tell me which term you are least confident in, and which term my friend and I most likely disagree about.
