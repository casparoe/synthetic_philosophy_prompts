So I'm in year 12 and I run this debate club blog thing at school where we argue about basically everything (last week it was whether zoos should exist, it got heated). Next month the motion is along the lines of "this house would take in far more refugees" and honestly everyone keeps talking past each other, because half the club means totally different things by "refugee" and the other half means different things by "take in". So I want to make a quiz — "which view on refugees do you actually hold?" — that sorts people by what they say about concrete cases, not by which political label they like.

Can you write it for me? What I want:

- 8 to 10 questions, each one a concrete case with 2 or 3 answer options (not agree/disagree scales). Mix "who counts" cases and "who has to take them in" cases. For who counts, things like: a woman fleeing a gang that's going to kill her because her brother testified against them, where her own government either can't or won't protect her — is she a refugee even though the classic legal definition centres on persecution by the state? A family whose island floods badly every year now — refugee or "just a migrant"? A guy fleeing crushing poverty but not persecution. And the textbook case, someone their own government is jailing for their political opinions.
- For who has to take them in: does the nearest safe country have a special duty? Do rich countries owe more because of capacity, or because of past involvement (wars they funded, arms they sold)? Is it ever ok to turn people away because your own housing and services are stretched? Does paying another country to host them count as doing your share?
- A scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions. The leaves should at least include the strict literal reading of the 1951 Convention definition, expanded definitions that pull in climate migrants and gang persecution, something like a near-open-borders view, and the view that states have wide freedom to exclude except where there's a special relationship. If a position has a well known defender you can mention the name but dont stress about it, "unnamed" is fine.
- The tricky bit I actually care about: build the quiz from the questions first — like, lay out the logical space of possible answer combinations — and then say where the real positions sit inside it. Flag any combination nobody holds, with a sentence on why (incoherent? just unattractive? noone's explored it?). And flag any two positions the quiz can't actually separate — I'd rather know where it fails than pretend it works.
- No question should just be "do you think refugees have rights" — that's exactly the thing I'm trying to avoid, people picking the option that sounds like their team.

One more thing: at the end, tell me where you think the quiz is most likely to mislabel someone, and what kind of answer or case would show the sorting doesn't work — basically, what would change your mind that the tree is built right. If you're not sure a position actually exists, or that a question cleanly separates two views, just say so instead of faking confidence.

Also pls write it in British English, since that's what we use here (my own spelling is obviously a lost cause, as you can probably tell).
