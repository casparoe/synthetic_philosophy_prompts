I'm a formal methods engineer: I spend my days building state-machine models of protocols and verifying them. My working vocabulary is Kripke structures, state spaces, reachability, temporal logic specifications, satisfiability, counterexample traces, abstraction and refinement, and simulation relations between a concrete implementation and an abstract spec. I have a decent amateur grasp of the philosophy — I know Kripke semantics for modal logic lives somewhere in my lineage — but I've never properly read Lewis. Restate his modal realism, as defended in *On the Plurality of Worlds*, in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can. In particular, try to render each of his signature commitments: that possible worlds are concrete rather than abstract representations; that worlds are spatiotemporally isolated from one another, so nothing in one world is spatiotemporally related to anything in another; that actuality is indexical, so "the actual world" functions like "the current state" or "here" rather than like a distinguished property; that individuals in other worlds are counterparts of me rather than me; and his principle of recombination, on which anything can coexist with anything else, as long as no contradiction results.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Some things look like they map suspiciously well: indexical actuality sounds like the distinction between a designated initial state and the rest of the state space, and counterpart theory sounds like the difference between tracing a value through states versus asking whether two states contain "the same" object. But I can already see where the translation strains: in my world, the state space is a representation I generate from a model — an abstraction, and a drastically lossy one — whereas Lewis's headline claim is that the other worlds are as concrete as this one, with people in them leading lives. When I verify a protocol, I don't believe there are concrete talking buses duplicating my traces. So does the Lewis-versus-ersatzism debate, once translated, just collapse into my abstraction/refinement debate — a disagreement about how faithful a representation must be — or is the concreteness claim doing something my vocabulary genuinely cannot express? Is recombination anything more than the fact that a state-space generator explores every valuation consistent with the constraints? And does the part of his view that has no counterpart — whatever it is — show that my framework is missing something metaphysically important, or that Lewis was insisting on a distinction that doesn't ultimately do any work? I want an honest translation, not one that quietly redescribes his position in terms that predetermine the answer.
