I write a science-and-ideas column, and next week I'm sitting down with a philosopher who's built her career on the knowledge norm of assertion — the view that you may assert only what you know, with the truth-norm and belief-norm rivals as the foil. Draft me eight to ten interview questions that dont just let her restate the view. I want at least one each on: the lottery case, where telling a friend your ticket will lose sounds perfectly fine even though nobody knows it; the schoolteacher who asserts evolution from the textbook without believing a word of it, and whether her assertion is proper, defective, or somehow both; and assertions with no audience at all — if she's writing only for herself, what is the norm even doing there. For each question, also give me the follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give — if she reaches for the reasonable-to-rely-on line, or tries the escape hatch that the case isn't a genuine assertion, what do I press with next? Keep the phrasing conversational enough for audio, no seminar-speak, and nothing generic like how she got into philosophy.
