My mate and I had a massive argument last night after a few beers. I work in compliance for a UK engineering firm that does a lot of infrastructure work abroad, and I was telling him about how half my job is deciding whether a gift or payment we're asked to make abroad is a bribe or just... how things are done there. He reckoned that calling our whole gift-giving and favour-doing culture corrupt is just cultural imperialism -- that the line between a clientelism that holds societies together and corruption that rots them is something WE drew, mostly to label non-Western practices as dirty. I pushed back but honestly it got messy, because he had a point that a "facilitation payment" to get a customs official to do their job looks different depending on where you're standing, and that plenty of perfectly legal lobbying back home looks a lot like what we prosecute foreigners for.

So heres what I want from you. Write me a decision procedure I can actually use when one of our project managers comes to me with a questionable request -- the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, where the procedure says no outright, and crucially where it has to admit the question is a values judgement rather than a compliance one, because that last category is exactly where my mate thinks I'm pretending to have objective answers. Ground it in whatever the actual philosophy of corruption says -- the difference between a gift and a bribe, whether the wrongness is in the exchange itself or in the institutional harm it does, whether reciprocity norms make some of this not bribery at all -- but keep it usable by someone who hasn't read any of that. Then run it on three cases: (1) a government client's procurement official expects a "consulting fee" to his brother's company before our bid gets shortlisted, (2) our local partner insists on giving the minister who approved our project a substantial retirement gift, which everyone there says is normal and refusing would humiliate him and probably cost us the follow-on contract, and (3) we hire the nephew of a senior official as a local liaison at a market salary, he's actually decent at the job, and my colleague says that's just how networks work there and we'd do the same with an MP's kid at home. Be honest about where the procedure would let my mate's argument in the door, and whether there's any version of it that isn't just laundering my cultural priors as process.

Please write in British English. And keep it readable -- I'll take honesty about hard bits over false tidiness.
