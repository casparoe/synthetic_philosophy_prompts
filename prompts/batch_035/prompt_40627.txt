I've been in a reading group going through the illusionism debate (Dennett, Frankish on one side, Chalmers and Strawson on the other) for like a year and a half now and it's the definition of stalled. The anti-illusionists keep pressing the incoherence charge: an illusion of phenomenality would itself have to be a seeming, and a seeming with a distinctive way-it-feels just IS phenomenal consciousness, so there's nothing left for it to be an illusion OF. The illusionists answer that this begs the question -- introspection misrepresents experience as having intrinsic qualitative properties the same way the fridge looks uniformly white rather than like a swarm of vibrating atoms, and "seeming" talk is precisely the thing up for analysis, not a neutral starting point. And round and round it goes. The striking thing is that each side seems to think the other isn't even talking about the same topic, which is usually a sign a debate is verbal, except here the stakes feel very real.

My working hypothesis is that the fight is actually about the epistemology of introspection: whether an introspective seeming is like a perceptual appearance (prima facie strong but defeasible evidence about experience) or more like a judgment or representation (just more mental stuff being theorized about, with no special authority). If the first, the incoherence argument has real bite; if the second, it's harmless. But I'm not confident, and the people in my group who know this literature way better than me can't seem to cash the disagreement out in those terms.

Is it verbal, substantive-but-about-introspection like I suspect, or something else entirely -- maybe the two camps just have different tacit success conditions for what an explanation of consciouness is supposed to deliver (accommodating the data of introspection vs. eliminating its deliveries as data)?
