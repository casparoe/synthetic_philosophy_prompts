My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about reductionism, and the whole dispute kept collapsing into the same picture: science as a tower, with physics on the ground floor, chemistry resting on physics, biology on chemistry, psychology on biology, and so on up to the social sciences. On his telling, each floor is entirely carried by the one beneath it — if you had the ground floor's blueprint and materials in full detail, everything above follows in principle, so the higher floors are just compressed restatements of what's below, and the special sciences are scaffolding that a completed physics would eventually let us retire. On my telling, each floor has its own load-bearing structure: the regularities at a given level are real patterns that don't dissolve just because you know everything about the level underneath. The trouble is that neither of us could get the analogy itself to settle anything — we just kept re-describing it in our own favor. Please audit it properly: set out what corresponds to what (floors, load-bearing walls, the foundation, the construction process?), then identify where the architectural mapping breaks down, distinguishing the disanalogies that actually matter for who's right from the ones that don't (a tower is static and designed; the relation between scientific levels is neither), and finally tell me whose conclusion, if either's, the tower picture can really carry — and what a better picture of the relations between levels of science would look like.
