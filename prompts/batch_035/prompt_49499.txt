I've been going back and forth in the dispositions literature for a while now and I honestly can't tell if anyone has moved in like 25 years. Quick sketch of where I see things: the old simple conditional analysis says a glass is fragile iff it would break if struck. Then Martin comes along with the electro-fink -- a device that, if the glass is about to break, instantly restores or shatters it, so the conditional comes out true when the glass isn't fragile and false when it is. Lewis tries to patch it with the refined analysis (base-breaking, holding fixed the intrinsic basis at the time), which handles reverse finks but then gets eaten by masks -- the glass packed in styrofoam so it wouldn't break if struck, even though nothing about the glass itself changed. Then people like Choi respond by going "strict" -- if it wouldn't break, it just isn't fragile, masks included -- and critics say that makes the analysis trivially circular, since you're now just reading off the disposition from what the categorical basis would do, which was the thing we wanted the conditional to explain. And round and round it goes.

So here's what I actually want: not another survey, but a diagnosis of the impasse. Is this a real substantive dispute, or is it partly verbal -- one side implicitly working with "disposition" as a functional/causal role concept (the glass's intrinsic contribution to breaking) and the other with something closer to a bare disposition-ascription governed by ordinary counterfactual talk? Or maybe the real disagreement is meta-level: about what an analysis is for. The strict-conditional people seem to think an analysis should just carve the extension correctly and if it's a bit circular, fine, that shows the concept isn't reducible. The anti side seems to demand that the analysis be informative, explain why fragile things tend to break, and rule nothing in or out by fiat. If that's right, then a lot of the counterexample exchange is talking past each other -- nobody is disagreeing about the glass, they're disagreeing about the job description.

There's also the question of whether the fink/mask cases deserve the weight they carry. They're weird, engineered scenarios. One camp treats intuition about them as data; the other camp seems to think a concept formed for ordinary objects in ordinary circumstances can't be expected to have determinate application when you bolt on a reverse-fink machine, so "counterexamples" of that shape are just noise. I genuinely don't know if that's a defensible methodological stance or a dodge.

So: tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is, using what the sides actually say, and be honest about whether anything would count as progress -- a distinction between extrinsic and intrinsic interference, a new theory of the causal bases, just giving up on the conditional analysis as the framework, or maybe an argument that the question is ill-posed because "would break if struck" was never doing analysis-work in the first place but something more like a heuristic. If your diagnosis is that it's genuinely hard rather than confused, say why and what progress would even look like.
