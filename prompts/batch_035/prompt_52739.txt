I work in philosophy of physics and have come to accept that the Everett interpretation has answered its probability problem: the Deutsch–Wallace decision-theoretic program shows that a rational agent in a branching universe, caring only about the welfare of her future successors, is obliged by the axioms of rational decision theory to weight branches by their squared-amplitude measures, so the Born rule emerges as a theorem about how to care about your descendants rather than as a postulate about stochastic collapse. I'm also sympathetic to the added claim, borrowed from the self-locating-uncertainty camp, that an agent who doesn't know which branch she is on should assign credences proportional to amplitude, which makes the decision-theoretic result feel less like a conjuring trick. Tell me what would count against this. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: technical results it stands or falls with (does anything show the rationality axioms are unmotivated, or that they can be relaxed to license a different measure — branch counting, say — equally well?), structural objections it must survive (the circularity worry that the derivation helps itself to probabilistic notions in its axioms; the worry that branch individuation is too ill-defined to make branch weight a well-grounded quantity; the point, due to Greaves, that if Everettian quantum mechanics places no expected-evidence constraint on agents, empirical confirmation of the whole package becomes problematic), and intuitions in other areas it must buy (e.g., about what a decision theory is for when every outcome occurs somewhere). Which of these would a committed Everettian like Wallace himself concede as counting against the program, and which would he dismiss as trading on a single-world prejudice? I'd like an honest verdict at the end on whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and, if so, whether that is a defect in a claim of this kind, which is at bottom a claim that a certain formal derivation succeeds. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
