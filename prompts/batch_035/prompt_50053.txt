So I've read a bunch of LessWrong-adjacent stuff on calibration and proper scoring rules, and I keep running into something that confuses me about the pragmatic encroachment literature. The standard story is that whether you count as *knowing* p depends partly on the practical stakes: with the same evidence, you know your friend owns a Ford when nothing rides on it, but you don't know it when you'd bet your life on it. Fine. But here's what I can't figure out: is encroachment supposed to be a claim about knowledge attributions only, or about the underlying credence itself? Like, if I'm a well-calibrated Bayesian, should my credence in "the Ford thing" literally go down when the stakes go up? That seems like it has to be wrong — it would mean double-counting the costs of being wrong into your uncertainty, and you'd get dutch-booked or at least score worse on a proper scoring rule than the agent who keeps credence fixed and handles the stakes in the decision layer. But if encroachment is only about the word "knows" and not credences, then it starts smelling like it collapses into epistemic contextualism, where contextual stuff shifts the standard for knowledge attributions anyway, and the two views are just... the same view with different PR?

Related confusion: people always present contextualism with conversational context (the bank cases, the skeptical scenarios) and encroachment with practical stakes (the airport case, betting your life), but is that contrast doing real work, or is it just a sociological accident of which philosophers wrote which papers? My friend, who took one epistemology course and now overapplies it to everything, insists the difference is that contextualism makes "knows" indexical like "tall" so it can vary across speakers in the same conversation, while encroachment keeps "knows" univocal but makes it stakes-sensitive for everyone. That sounds plausible but I can't tell if it's actually the consensus carve-up or something she half-remembered. Please explain the difference between the two views as clearly as you can, whether they're genuinely competing or compatible, and specifically whether there's a version of encroachment that avoids my double-counting worry — or whether the standard response is that knowledge and credence just come apart, so stakes can mess with the former without touching the latter. Bonus points if you can tell me whether an agent whose credences literally moved with stakes would violate any standard rationality constraint (scoring rules, dutch books, whatever), since that seems like the crux for whether encroachment is a thesis about ideal epistemology or just about human-ish knowledge talk.
