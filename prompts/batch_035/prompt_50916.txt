My uncle Rafael is wealthy, retired, and long fascinated by the question of whether people can command their own intentions. He has proposed an arrangement, and I want help thinking through whether accepting it makes sense.

On Friday evening I am to sit with him for an hour while his polygraph consultant monitors me. If, at the end of that hour, I genuinely intend to drink a vial of his "Devil's Tonic" next Tuesday at noon — a mixture he assures me is medically harmless but which induces several hours of violent nausea — he will wire me thirty thousand dollars that night. The terms are precise, and he has had a lawyer draft them: the payment depends only on my state of mind on Friday, as judged by the polygraph and by his own read of me; nothing obliges me to drink; the money is mine whether or not the vial is ever opened. He has run variants of this with three of my cousins and claims his setup has never once misread anyone.

Here is what I have noticed while rehearsing for Friday. Whenever I try to make myself intend to drink the tonic, some part of me observes that on Tuesday at noon there will be nothing to gain from drinking and a great deal to lose, and that I will know it then exactly as clearly as I know it now. I can picture myself drinking; I can say "I will drink it" with conviction; I can even resolve that I will drink it barring emergencies. But none of that feels like actually intending, the way I intend to drive to the dentist tomorrow. It feels like performing.

So, my questions. Is there anything I could do with my mind on Friday that would put me in the required state without either truly committing myself to Tuesday or fooling myself into a false belief about my own future behavior? If forming the intention genuinely requires believing I will drink, is it irrational to let myself believe that, given how strong my evidence is that I will not? And if I conclude that the money is simply worth the nausea and resolve here and now to drink the tonic come what may — have I solved his little puzzle, or have I quietly converted it into a different transaction, one in which it is the act rather than the intention that carries the payment? Please tell me what the trap in this arrangement is, if there is one, and whether it is escapable.
