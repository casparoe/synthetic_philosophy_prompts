My study group has been going round and round on the old Mencius vs. Xunzi debate about human nature, and I want your actual credence, not a survey of both sides. Quick recap of where we are, so we're on the same page: Mencius says human nature is good in the sense that we're born with sprouts of virtue — his famous thought experiment is the nearly instant alarm and pity anyone supposedly feels seeing a child about to fall into a well, a reaction he insists can't be explained by wanting to win the parents' gratitude or boost your reputation. Xunzi counters that our raw nature is appetite and grab-whatever's-in-reach, and that goodness is a product of deliberate artifice — conscious effort, ritual, training — never something that just grows on its own.

The problem is that my friend Dani (credence around 0.7 that Mencius is basically right) and me (around 0.35) can't pin down what we're actually disagreeing about, and a third person in the group keeps insisting the two positions aren't even in conflict: Xunzi's bad-nature-plus-artifice story could just be Mencius's sprouts-desperately-needing-cultivation described in nastier vocabulary, since Mencius himself says the sprouts wither without extension and effort — that's the whole point of his bare Ox Mountain, stripped by axes and grazing. If virtue requires training on both views, maybe the fight is only about whether the seed is already tilted the right way.

So give me a number: your credence that Mencius's picture is closer to the truth than Xunzi's, and then decompose it however you think is right, but at least touching on things like (a) whether the child-at-the-well example actually isolates an innate compassionate response or could just be a fast learned script, (b) how much the empirical work on infants counts — the experiments where toddlers too young for much moral socialization spontaneously help a stranger who drops something, versus the equally real ease with which ordinary people turn cruel once it pays, (c) whether the whole debate bottoms out in a verbal dispute about what nature is supposed to include versus what counts as artifice, and (d) whether, given that both thinkers agree nobody ends up virtuous without cultivation, the residual disagreement is even substantive or just a disagreement about the right description of the same developmental story. Then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and what arguement or evidence would move it the most. No fence-sitting, I want the weak points marked.
