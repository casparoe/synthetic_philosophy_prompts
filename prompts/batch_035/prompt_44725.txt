Is there a name for the thing I'm about to describe, or at least a literature that studies it directly? I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, so if this is well-worn just tell me what it's called and who works on it.

This comes up for me around the philosophical zombie argument -- the claim that you can conceive of a being physically identical to me but with no inner life, and that this shows consciousness isn't physical. When I actually try to do the conceiving instead of just nodding along, I notice something about how my imagination behaves, and I've never seen it named anywhere.

When the duplicate is described coarsely -- "an exact physical copy of you, but dark inside" -- the conceiving feels effortless, almost like picturing a statue. But the more physical detail I fill in, the harder it gets. If I picture the copy's retina processing light, its motor cortex firing so it yanks its hand back when it touches something hot, I realize I'm no longer picturing a being without experience. I'm picturing an ordinary person and hanging a caption on the picture that says "and it doesn't feel anything." The no-inner-life part stops being something I can hold in the image and becomes a stipulated clause -- indistinguishable, as far as my introspection goes, from being able to say "and it's haunted" without contradiction.

So the conceivability seems to shrink as the description gets more concrete, roughly in inverse propotion to how much physics you've written down. In the coarse version it feels like I'm imagining one thing. In the fine version it's clearly a picture plus an assertion about the picture, and I can't find any difference between conceiving the assertion and merely being able to utter it.

The only near miss I know of is a remark I've seen attributed to Kripke, that when you try to imagine the brain state correlated with pain occurring without the pain, some further dull ache tends to sneak back into your image. That's close to my copy-yanking-its-hand case, but it's stated as a quirk of that one example, not as a general relation between grain and conceivability.

So what I want to know: does this inverse relation have a name? If not, coin one and define it, and be precise about the edges -- does it only hit experiential concepts (maybe I can't co-imagine "and no experience" because experience is the only thing I ever picture from the inside), or does it hit any claim that rides on top of a description, like imagining a glass of water that isn't H2O at increasing levels of chemical detail? And practically: does this show that the zombie argument's persuasive force depends on keeping the description vague, and if so, which reply is the right one -- "fine, work with the coarse description, grain doesn't matter," or "the fine-grained case is the real test and the easy conceiving at coarse grain is the illusion"?
