I've been asked to write up my considered position on mind uploading for a reading group on personal identity and future technology, and before I circulate it I'd like a consistency check from someone willing to push back rather than nod along. The position, as I'd state it:

(1) I'm a functionalist about the mind: mental life, including consciousness, supervenes on functional organization. And I mean this at whatever grain turns out to matter — I don't want to say that gross input-output behavior is enough, since organization that coarse seems too thin to carry consciousness.

(2) On personal identity I follow Parfit: what matters in my future is psychological connectedness and continuity, not numerical identity. If identity and psychological continuity ever came apart, it is the continuity I should care about.

(3) I'd be willing to undergo gradual uploading: my neurons replaced a few hundred at a time, over several months, by devices that take over each replaced neuron's input-output role, responding the same way to the same signals from neighboring cells. I'd expect to wake up each morning throughout as the same person, and at the end I'd be running entirely on silicon. That is survival.

(4) I would not undergo destructive scanning: an instantaneous scan, the biological brain destroyed, and a functionally identical system started up on a server. That is death. The being that starts up afterwards is a new person who happens to inherit my memories, and I will not be waking up anywhere.

(5) Put to me the fission variant — the scan run twice, with both continuers kept running — I accept the Parfitian verdict: neither successor is strictly identical to me, but each gets everything that matters, and since identity was never the point, there is no special tragedy here.

(6) My account of why gradual uploading is survival, when it is, is that there is no moment at which the subject goes out of existence: the process remains continuous in something like the way my existence is continuous through a night's sleep, and it is that continuity of the underlying process that carries me across the months of replacement.

Please audit this set. Which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension, and how badly? Which pairs only look to be in tension, and why does the appearance dissolve? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally serious, and for each genuine one say what would have to give and what that revision costs elsewhere in the set. Finally, tell me which single change would clean up the most. I'd rather not scrap the position wholesale, and I honestly don't know which piece is doing the damage — that is what I'm hoping you can work out.
