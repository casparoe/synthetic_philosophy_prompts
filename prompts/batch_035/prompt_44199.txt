I am a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy of biology, though I completed an undergraduate degree in biology some years ago and remember the textbook material. I use git daily and am fluent in its internals: commits, the directed acyclic graph of history, branches as moving pointers rather than stored things, forks, merges, tags, cherry-picks, and the difference between a snapshot and a lineage.

Please translate the main positions in the species problem into that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can and without smuggling in assumptions from either side. Specifically, map each of the following onto the nearest construct in version control, and say for each what translates cleanly, what translates only with distortion, and what has no expression at all:

1. The biological species concept, with its criterion of reproductive isolation among populations.
2. The phylogenetic species concept, with its criterion of smallest diagnosable units.
3. Ring species, where the ends of a loop cannot interbreed though gene flow connects them all the way around.
4. Hybridization and introgression between otherwise distinct lineages.
5. Asexual lineages and horizontal gene transfer, where there may be no single tree of history at all.
6. The arbitrariness of slicing a single unbranched lineage into successive species along a continuum of slow change, as against the pattern of long stasis interrupted by brief, rapid change.
7. The thesis that species are individuals — historical, spatiotemporally bounded things — rather than classes of similar organisms.

Then give me the residue report, and be blunt about it. I have two questions in particular. First: in git, a branch name is a convention that everyone agrees is a convention — nobody thinks the set of commits reachable from a given pointer carves nature at its joints — whereas at least some parties to the species debate believe they are naming something real and mind-independent. Is that difference expressible in my vocabulary at all, or is it the first sign that the translation has quietly failed? Second: I can see at least two things in the biological debate that seem to have no counterpart in version control — the fact that species counts carry legal consequences under conservation law, and the fact that the same lineage is queried simultaneously by several incompatible research programs. For each of these, tell me whether the gap exists because my vocabulary is missing something or because the biological claim is less clear than it looks.

Close with your overall verdict: does the residue show that the species debate is a largely verbal dispute about where to place pointers on a graph everyone accepts, or does it show that version control, as a model of lineage, is missing something the biological debate is tracking? I would rather have a definite answer with stated reasons than a survey of options.
