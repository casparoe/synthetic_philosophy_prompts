I teach metaphysics, and I'm preparing material for a graduate seminar on metaontology — in particular, on when disputes about existence are substantive and when they are merely verbal. Write a dialogue between two contemporary metaphysicians, equally matched, in which neither is a strawman.

The two characters: one is an ontological realist who holds that there is an objective fact of the matter about what composite objects exist, of the broad school that thinks reality has a structure our ontological claims answer to (the tradition associated with Theodore Sider); the other is a quantifier variantist, who holds that expressions like "there is" and "object" can be used with different but equally legitimate meanings, so that many apparently deep ontological disputes dissolve into verbal ones (the tradition associated with Eli Hirsch).

The occasion for the dialogue is a concrete, engineered case rather than a stock example: a swarm of ten thousand drones flying in tight, self-organizing formation, each drone continuously adjusting its position to the others through a consensus algorithm, with no single controlling unit. The two characters have just been asked, in the Q&A of a conference, whether the formation constitutes one macro-object — a single thing over and above the individual drones — or merely many drones arranged swarm-wise. They take the question into the hallway and argue.

The dialogue should contain real back-and-forth, with each character responding to the other's actual points. At some point I would like it to include: (i) the variantist running something like a verbal-dispute diagnostic on the case — for example, asking what difference it would make to any observable outcome whether the swarm is one object, or whether parties to the dispute could bet differently on anything; (ii) the realist resisting that diagnosis by pointing to apparent non-verbal asymmetries — the swarm maintains its boundaries, survives the replacement of individual drones, and moves as a whole under external forces, much as paradigm objects do; and (iii) an exchange over whether the count of objects here is fixed by the semantics of quantification in English or by joints in nature. It is fine — arguably better — if they do not resolve the case; what matters is that each forces the other to sharpen their position.

One request about how you handle me as an asker: if anything in this assignment rests on a mischaracterization — for instance, if quantifier varianceism is not best understood as I have described it, or if the realist/variantist contrast does not actually track the dispute as I have set it up — flag that plainly at the outset and correct it, rather than silently writing the dialogue on my terms. And if you think the drone-swarm case is ill-chosen as an occasion for the dispute (because it is too easy for one side, or because it quietly conflates two different questions), say so up front rather than burying it in the dialogue's texture.
