Hayek gets dragged into AI arguments constantly now — there are serious people claiming the socialist calculation debate is finally settled in the socialists' favor, because an LLM trained on basically the whole internet has already pooled the dispersed knowledge he said no planning ministry could ever gather. Pretend you can put a fully briefed Hayek in front of that claim. I don't want the reflexive prices-are-signals answer — I want to know which of his distinctions actually does the work here. Is it that the knowledge never existed anywhere until the competitive process generated it, so theres nothing in the training corpus to have soaked up in the first place? Or is it that the knowledge existed but is tacit, and the LLM side sneaks their error in when they assume tokenizing it makes it available to a planner? Push him to answer the strongest version of the claim too, which is that the corpus is downstream of markets anyway, so the model is just a compressed market — and compression is all planning ever needed. And then a separate one: do you think the Lange who wrote that late essay about computers and the market would have gone for this even harder than he did for trial-and-error pricing? seems like he'd have loved it.
