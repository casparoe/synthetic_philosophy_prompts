Bit of an odd request, but here goes. I've been doing meditation on and off for a few years — nothing fancy, mostly just sitting and watching whatever shows up — and there's a pattern I keep running into that I can't find a proper name for anywhere.

When I try to observe the one who's doing the observing — turn attention back on itself, as it were — I never get the observer. What I get instead is either a new object (a thought about myself, the feeling of my posture, the dark blur of my own nose at the edge of vision), or a kind of afterimage: a conviction that the watcher was there a split second before I looked and had slipped aside just in time. And here's the part that really puzzles me. The failure doesn't weaken the sense that there's someone there to be found. It strengthens it. The more I search, the more the missing witness feels like a real thing that just happens to be very good at not being caught — as if it's elusiveness were the whole point, and every failed attempt were a fresh sighting of it in the act of escaping. People I know who've done this practice for decades seem more convinced, not less, that there's something there that can't be seen. It's as though the search itself were manufacturing its own evidence.

I know the Buddhist line is that there is no self to find, and I've come across talk of the self being unfindable, but my honest report is the opposite direction of travel: the not-finding has made the self seem more plausible to me, not less. Is there an established concept for this? The nearest things I know of are the regress you get in philosophy of mind, where every act of observing seems to demand another act behind it, and plain confirmation bias — but in confirmation bias you actually find what you're looking for, whereas here I'm not finding it and being persuaded anyway, which feels like it should be a seperate thing. It also reminds me a little of how failed searches in other areas sometimes raise the stakes rather than lowering them, so maybe the structure is general and someone has studied it.

If there is an established term, I'd like to know what it is, but I won't be happy with just a label — I want to know exactly what the phenomenon is supposed to be, how it differs from (a) the straight claim that there is no self, (b) the regress idea, and (c) the elusiveness you get with anything that's defined partly by its not being an object (the eye can't see itself, and so on). And if there's no established concept, could you coin one, define it, and say where its edges are — including whether it could ever be rational, or whether it's always a trick the mind is playing on itself?

Oh, and please write your answer in British English — I'm in the UK and the american spellings make my eye twitch.
