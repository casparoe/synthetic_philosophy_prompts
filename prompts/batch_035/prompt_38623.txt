Write a short story built around a fission case rather than the standard teleporter. In my draft, a middle-aged novelist steps into a teletransporter that, due to a fault, scans her without destroying the original — the machine and its engineers believe the scan is non-destructive and harmless, and she is told afterward that an exact psychological duplicate of her, with all her memories and her current unfinished manuscript in her head, has been dispatched to a colony where no return trip is possible. I want the story to dramatize Parfit's question of what matters in survival rather than to settle it: the original must decide, for instance, whether to keep writing the manuscript or abandon it to the copy, whether to open a letter the copy wrote before departure, and how to relate to the copy's family — and I would like at least one scene where she catches herself reasoning as though the copy's future losses are hers, and another where that reasoning collapses. Please avoid framing the copy as a sinister duplicate or resolving the question with a twist that makes one of them "the real one"; the philosophical pressure should come from ordinary details — shared property, shared obligations, the telephone call neither wants to make first.
