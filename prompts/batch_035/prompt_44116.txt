ok so background first: i never went to college and my math education basically ends around algebra, but a couple years back i fell down a wikipedia rabbit hole that started with a video about cutting a sphere into two identical spheres and ended with me reading for hours about how that trick (and a ton of other stuff) hangs on one axiom that nobody can prove from the rest of math. separately, i've been writing bad poetry sense i was a teenager, and last year i learned about villanelles from that elizabeth bishop poem everybody reads, the one where two lines keep coming back over and over like you can't get rid of them. and it hit me that this form might be perfect for the thing that bugs me most about the axiom of choice, so thats what i want from you.

here's the thing that bugs me, in case its useful. some collections come with a way to pick one thing out of them already built in. tell me to pick a shoe from each pair in a huge pile of pairs of shoes and i can say "always the left one" and im done — no axiom needed, the choosing is just following a rule that was there all along. but do it with a huge pile of pairs of socks, where the two socks in a pair truly have nothing to pick between them, and to grab one from every single pair all at once you apparently just have to assert that you can. bertrand russell had this shoes-and-socks example and its honestly the only version of the whole choice debate that ever made sense to me.

so: write me a villanelle about the axiom of choice where the two refrains carry the shoes/socks split. one refrain should be the demand to pick one from each pile where a rule does the picking for you — the shoes case, the choice is already made and youre just executing instructions. the other refrain should be the exact same demand where no rule can pick — the socks case, where being able to choose is the whole axiom and nothing else. somewhere in the body of the poem i'd like at least a nod to what this power buys you (well-ordering anything, the sphere trick) and what refusing it costs you (i read that if you reject it you can't even prove every vector space has a basis, which seems like a lot to give up). someone who knows the actual math should be able to tell which refrain is which and see why one of them is cheating, and someone who knows nothing should still get a good poem out of it. one more thing i cant quite put my finger on — the villanelle itself never gets to choose its refrains, they're fixed in advance, and that feels like it means something here — but dont force it in if it doesnt fit.
