I'm on the advisory board of a regional careers-guidance service — we run the "widening horizons" programme in secondary schools (talks, mentoring, pamphlets, all that). Our next deliverable is a report to the school board on whether to keep, tone down, or restructure our push to get more girls into STEM tracks.

At our last meeting one board member, a labour economist, argued we should include what people call the gender-equality paradox as a "humility paragraph" — the widely-reported finding that in countries scoring highest on gender-equality indices, young women and men actually sort into fields of study *more* differently, not less. Her thought is: if occupational sorting gets more gendered as external constraints fall away, our programme may be pushing against choices people make when they're comparatively free, so maybe we should stop chasing participation targets and just guarantee open options (mentoring for whoever asks, no targeted encouragement).

Another member, a sociologist, more or less exploded at this. He says the paragraph would smuggle in a naturalistic fallacy — slide from "choices diverge where equality is high" to "the divergence reflects something we shouldn't try to influence" — and that the finding is contested anyway. Our staff statistician backed him up on that last point somewhat: apparently the result depends on how you aggregate, since computing gender ratios within each country first and then correlating those with equality scores can give a different picture than comparing raw percentages across countries.

I have to draft the decision memo everyone gets before the next meeting, and I'm stuck between the two of them. Please write it with these sections, in this order:

1. Summary (3-4 sentences, bottom line up front)
2. What the finding does and doesn't show (~200 words, including the aggregation worry)
3. The strongest case for including the paragraph (~150 words — steelman it, dont strawman it; the "freer choices diverge more" reading deserves a fair shot)
4. The strongest case against (~150 words)
5. Recommendation (~250 words — be concrete: does the humility paragraph go in, do we keep/modify/drop the participation targets, and what do we tell the school board about the difference between removing barriers and steering choices?)
6. Draft paragraph (~100 words — the actual text that would go in the report, if you recommend including anything)

Two cautions. First, don't treat "the preferences are free choices" and "the preferences are culturally shaped" as if they exhaust the options — a couple of us suspect that dichotomy is doing way too much work in this whole debate, and the memo shouldn't lean on it. Second, please don't cite specific studies or authors by name; the report is a decision document, not a lit review, and I'd rather the reasoning carry it on its own.
