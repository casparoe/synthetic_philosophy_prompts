My friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about what makes a life go well, and I came away with a page of her verdicts that I can't fully systematize, so I'd like your help predicting her next ones. We started from the experience machine: offered a lifelong stay in a tank that would feel perfect from the inside while her real life went on with its usual share of drudgery, she said she'd refuse, and that the plugged-in life would be clearly worse. Then we moved to pairs of lives that feel identical from the inside. A researcher who dies believing her lifework came to nothing: in one world it really did come to nothing, in the other it quietly transformed her field and saved lives, though she never learns this — my friend said the second life went better. A person whose marriage is warm and happy from the inside: in one world the partner genuinely loves them; in the other the partner has secretly despised them for decades and mocked them to friends while behaving identically — she said the despising-partner life went worse. At one point she summed herself up by saying that what matters has to be something true of the life, not just something the life feels like from the inside. The trouble is that this remark, and all three verdicts, fit several distinct principles.

Here are the new cases I want predictions on. First, someone who after years of reflection sincerely wants nothing from life but pleasant experience — no desire that anything be real, no desire for truth, no ambition — enters the machine with full knowledge of what it is, is never deceived, and lives out her days in unbroken contentment. Second, a scientist who dies believing not merely that her work failed but that she herself was a fraud — her discoveries borrowed, her reputation unearned, her life a waste — when in fact every one of those beliefs is false and her work saved millions; her inner life is identical to a counterpart in whom the fraud is real.

What I want from you: lay out the candidate principles that fit her track record, and say which simpler principles her three verdicts have already eliminated; state what each surviving principle predicts for my two new cases; and then commit — tell me which principle she is most plausibly running, give definite predictions on both cases with your reasons for preferring that reading, and don't hand me a menu to choose from. Finally, describe the single further case that would most cleanly discriminate among the principles you take to be still live.
