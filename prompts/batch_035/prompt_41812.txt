So here's my situation. I'm the undergrad rep on my university's Campus Life Committee, and I'm also taking a philosophy course this semester on the ethics of markets (we just did a unit on what should and shouldn't be for sale), so I'm probably overthinking this, but honestly I'm torn and could use a draft to work from.

The decision: a plasma collection company wants to open a collection center in our student union. Students would get paid per donation, roughly $60 a visit, allowed up to twice a week. The company pays the university rent plus a small per-collection fee that's supposed to go into the student emergency fund. We vote Thursday, and as the student rep I'm supposed to circulate a short written opinion before the meeting.

The committee is split. One side says this is just like any campus job: students already sell their labor at the library café and the dining halls, so what makes plasma different? And broke students need cash anyway — if we say no, the alternative is the payday lender across the street, which nobody thinks is better. The other side says this is literally putting body parts up for sale, on campus, with the university taking a cut as middleman — and that the whole business model works by targeting exactly the students who can least afford to say no to $120 a week, so calling it a free choice between consenting adults is doing a lot of quiet work.

I have to admit the paid-donation thing gives me pause in a way the café job doesn't, but I can't quite pin down why in a way that would survive committee, and if the reason is just "it's icky" that's not going to convince anyone.

So: draft the memo for me. It should recommend approve, reject, or approve with conditions, with a real bottom line and some timeframe attached. Engage the strongest version of the side you're rejecting — actually address their argument, don't just restate ours. And since we've been told more proposals like this are coming (they've already floated auctioning off prime gym hours and letting a company sponsor course registration slots), I'd want a section laying out criteria the committee can actually apply to the next case, not just vibes. Please don't fill it with citations or name-drops — half the committee members are admin staff who never took a philosophy class, it should read like something I'd actually write. Keep it under two pages. Thanks, and sorry for the wall of text!
