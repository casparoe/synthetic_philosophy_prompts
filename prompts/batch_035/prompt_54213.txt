weird question and honestly bad timing, but my cat had to be put down last week and ever since I've been spiraling about whether she was conscious right up til the very end, which somehow turned into me reading about panpsychism at 3am instead of sleeping. And honestly I kinda like it? Saying that matter has some spark of experience all the way down feels less insane to me than saying consciousness just pops into existence once you arrange atoms brain-shaped. But there are two things I can't get past and I want to know if they're actually fatal or if I'm misunderstanding the view.

1. If everything is conscious — electrons, quarks, whatever — then my thermostat is conscious, and rocks are conscious, and a bowl of soup is conscious. But experience is only knowable from the inside, so there's no possible experiment that could ever show the rock ISN'T having experiences. Doesn't that make panpsychism unfalsifiable and therefore not even a real theory? Is that why basically no working scientists take it seriously, or am I missing something?

2. The combination thing. If billions of tiny micro-experiences in my neurons combine into one big me, why don't me and my roommate, who sit on the same couch basically every night, combine into some shared consciousness? What's so special about being inside one skull — is it just proximity? Because if its proximity, everyone crammed into a subway car should briefly be one mind, and pretty clearly they're not.

I'm not trying to dunk on anyone here, I genuinely want panpsychism to work out, I just can't see how it gets around these two problems. So please tell me straight: am I attacking a version of the view that nobody actually holds?
