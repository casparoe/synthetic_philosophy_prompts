I'm a bioethics grad student and I've gotten a bit obsessed with anti-doping rules as a case study for the therapy/enhancement distinction, and the more I dig the stranger the pattern looks. A swimmer with asthma gets a therapeutic use exemption and can take a drug from a class that would get anyone else a two-year ban; painkilling and cortisone injections have been routine in pro sports for decades; a golfer can get LASIK and come out with better than 20/20 vision, and nobody blinks; distance runners can sleep in an altitude tent, which triggers basically the same physiological cascade an EPO injection does, and that's totally legal. But EPO itself, or re-infusing your own stored blood — banned. Officials always gesture at some vague phrase about the spirit of sport, but nobody ever cashes it out. My friends in the philosophy department keep telling me the whole thing is just accumulated historical accident, but that seems too quick — the pattern does feel like it's tracking *something*, maybe something about interventions that bypass effort and training rather than ones that repair or support them? I honestly keep flip-flopping.

So here's what I want: what theory of enhancement, or of what counts as the athlete's "own" performance, do these rules actually presuppose, if any? Where is the line implicitly drawn, and is it consistent — e.g., is the altitude tent plus LASIK combo really distinguishable from EPO in terms of the underlying picture? Or does the practice turn out to be underdetermined, where several different theories would license the exact same pattern of allowances and bans, so the rules don't strictly presuppose any one of them?
