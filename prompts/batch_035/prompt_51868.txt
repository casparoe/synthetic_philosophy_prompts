I'm stuck in a running argument with my brother-in-law, and I think I've finally hit the point where I need actual philosophy instead of just stubbornness.

He's religious, I'm not, and for years he'd say things like "everything happens for a reason" and I'd give the standard reply: reasons are things minds have, and before there were minds there were no reasons, just causes. He'd usually concede that. But last weekend, watching a nature documentary, I said — totally on autopilot — that the reason the polar bear's fur is white is camouflage, so it can sneak up on seals. He turned to me and asked: who's the "so it can" for? No bear decided anything, nobody designed it, there's no mind anywhere holding that reason. So wasn't I doing exactly what I yell at him for — smuggling a reasoner in where there isn't one?

I tried "it's shorthand for natural selection," and he said fine, but then either natural selection counts as a reasoner (which I'd never claim — it's not even a thing, it's a process nobody runs), or the shorthand is doing real work and I owe him an account of when it's licensed. And honestly I can't give one. I dont want to say hearts aren't for pumping blood — that sounds insane. But I also dont want to concede that the universe hands out reasons, because then his "everything happens for a reason" starts being true in some watered-down sense, and I can't tell if that sense is worth anything.

A few cases that seem to pull apart, in case they help:

- My buddy's doctor told him the appendix might be "for" reseeding gut bacteria after illness, but my buddy insists it's just a leftover that does nothing. Who's talking sense here? Does it matter whether the appendix got shaped by selection for doing that job, versus just happening to do something useful now?

- Last year my company did a reorg that turned out to save a bunch of money, but it later came out that nobody planned that — it started as two managers covering for each other during a hiring freeze and just stuck. Can I say the reorg happened in order to cut costs? Feels wrong, but I can't say why, and it feels uncomfortably close to the bear.

- The nastiest one: if there's no bear-shaped mind and no designer, but the camouflage story is still somehow true, what exactly is it true OF? Where does the reason live?

Is there a principled line here, or do I just have to pick a bullet to bite? The line that feels right to me is something like "there has to be a history that favored the thing doing the job" — but I cant tell whether that covers the appendix and the reorg, and I suspect my brother-in-law's next move is that if reasons can exist with no reasoner anywhere, he'll take one reasoner with no location, please.
