Here's my cluster of views on the trolley stuff and I want to know if it hangs together before I teach it as my line next semester: (1) diverting the trolley onto the one to save the five is permissible, pushing the large man off the footbridge is not, and that asymmetry is a real feature of the cases, not just an artifact of our discomfort with doing harm up close; (2) the doctrine of double effect is the correct explanation of the asymmetry -- killing someone as a means is out, foreseen side-effect deaths can be ok; (3) I'm pretty firm that in the loop variant (the track loops around so the trolley is only stopped *because* it hits the one guy, and that's what saves the five) you may still divert; (4) I think permissibility is intention-independent -- two agents doing the same thing in the same situation are equally permitted regardless of what's going on in their heads, intentions matter for blame, not permission; (5) terror bombing civilians to break morale is impermissible, bombing the munitions factory knowing civilians will die is permissible, and that's the same distinction as in (1); and (6) I'm skeptical that fancy redescriptions of intention ("I'm merely redirecting a threat," "his death is incidental to my deflecting the trolley") should be able to change what we may do -- if the distinction can be gamed by description then it's not doing real work. Which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. I genuinely can't tell which one I'd give up, and I'd rather know where the pressure points are than have the class find them for me.
