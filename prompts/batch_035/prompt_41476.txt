If two labs prepare systems independently — no conspiracy between them — then quantum states that assign different probabilities must correspond to genuinely different underlying realities, since if a single underlying situation were compatible with both, joint measurements on independently prepared pairs would sometimes yield outcomes that quantum theory itself assigns probability zero.
