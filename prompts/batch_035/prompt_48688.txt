In three weeks I'm giving a workshop paper to a formal epistemology group — about twenty people, mostly epistemologists and a few Bayesian statisticians who treat conditionalization as constitutive of rational credence, plus two philosophers of science who know the Adams thesis and the triviality literature cold. I want a Q&A prep sheet built around the following case and thesis, so let me lay both out precisely.

The case: a soldier, call her Sergeant Benjamin, parachutes into a swamp and, before any other information arrives, is confident to degree 1/3 each that she is in Blue territory, in the Red Army's headquarters area, or in the Red Army's reserve area. A radio operator then tells her: if you are in Red territory at all, you are in the headquarters area rather than the reserve area. She cannot ask questions. The classical puzzle is what her posteriors should be. If we conditionalize on the material conditional (Blue or headquarters), her credence in Blue jumps from 1/3 to 1/2 and the two Red options fall to 1/4 each; if we instead shift the reserve credence over to headquarters, Blue stays at 1/3 and headquarters rises to 2/3.

My thesis: the second update is the rational one, and the case therefore shows that conditionalization is not the universal update rule; the right rule for testimony of this form is a nearest-possibility rule in the spirit of imaging, where the probability of the "offending" option (the reserve area) migrates to its nearest acceptable neighbor rather than being spread over all survivors of the conditioning event. My argument sketch: (i) the intuition that Blue should be untouched is strong, shared even by people who have never seen the case; (ii) the material-conditional reading misrepresents the operator's report — she said something about Red territory, not a disjunction — and the misrepresentation objection generalizes beyond Gricean niceties to cases where the testimony is explicit and literal; (iii) nearest-possibility updating handles the case without inflating Blue, and inherits at least a prima facie justification from the way conditional assertibility behaves.
