I help run a small rationality meetup, about a dozen regulars, mostly people who've been reading LessWrong and the EA forum for a decade, and we do a 90-minute session once a month that's part exercise, part discussion. Next month I want to do moral uncertainty, and I've realized from past sessions that talks about this never land: everyone nods along at "treat your moral uncertainty a bit like your empirical uncertainty" and then nothing changes in anyone's actual reasoning. So I want a game instead, where they run into the hard part themselves before I name anything.

The thing I specifically want them to hit, and I mean really hit, is not the obvious part. Everyone already gets majority cycling, and the vaguely annoying fact that being 70% confident in utilitarianism still leaves you owing the other 30% something. The part I want them to feel is the exchange rate problem: you can't aggregate across moral theories the way you aggregate across empirical beliefs, because the theories don't share units. Splitting the difference with a rights absolutist is not like splitting the difference with someone who just weights a different charity more, and every clean voting-rule solution quietly assumes it is. That assumption is where I want the game to break for them.

My sketch so far: participants get assigned to parties (a straightforward maximizing utilitarian party, a party with strong side constraints, a contractualist-ish party, maybe an agent-centered one), each party gets a short manifesto I'll write, and the group has to allocate a fixed budget across a handful of causes over several rounds, with a different decision rule each round. I'm imagining one round of plain majority vote, one where a lottery picks a dictator before anyone gets to negotiate, one with some kind of explicit bargaining, in whatever order you think actually works. No real money, we use points, but the allocation has to feel like it matters enough that people fight for it, so invent whatever payoff scheme makes that happen.

What I need from you: the full session design. Rules, rough party manifestos, the order of the seperate rounds and why that order, how the decision rule shifts between rounds, and what each round is supposed to make people notice. Then a debrief that maps what happened onto the real problem: where the voting-rule and bargaining stuff is a genuinely live proposal people argue for, and where it breaks exactly at the units issue. And an honest note on what this game can't reproduce — my worry is that giving each theory to a different person outsources the tension, and the actually hard thing about moral uncertainty is having it inside one skull, but tell me if I'm wrong or how to partially patch it. Also flag what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and what I should do when it does — I'm braced for half the room playing deontology as utilitarianism with weird weights, and for someone refusing to negotiate at all, but I'd like to know what I'm not braced for.

Two formatting requests and one epistemic one. Write the whole thing as continuous prose, like an email — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, nothing nested — I will genuinely read it worse if it comes back as a list. And somewhere in there, tell me what would change your mind about this design: what you'd expect to observe when I run it that would tell you a round or the debrief isn't doing its job, and where your own reasoning about what participants will feel is shakiest. Don't hand me a design you're fully confident in without showing me its weakest joint.
