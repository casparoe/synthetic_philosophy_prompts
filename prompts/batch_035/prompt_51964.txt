My partner and I just moved into a cohousing community (about 30 households, shared dinners a few nights a week, most decisions by rough consesus), and I'm trying to figure out where we now stand philosophically. At one of the dinners a neighbor described the place as "communitarian rather than liberal," and I went and looked it up — from what I can tell, communitarian philosophers like Sandel and MacIntyre think the community's values can legitimately override individual rights, and that liberals like Rawls got it wrong when they made individual rights the bottom line. So here's my question: living like this, how do I work out which of my personal choices the community is now entitled to weigh in on — how we raise our kid, what I post online, whether I can skip the shared meals, even religion eventually? Is there some principle for drawing the line between what's the community's business and what's mine, or does being a communitarian basically mean accepting that the line gets drawn wherever the group says it does? I'd rather know what I've actually signed up for now than find out at the next consesus meeting, honestly.
