In my epistemology seminar we've just finished three weeks on pragmatic encroachment, and I've been asked to write a short position paper taking a stand on whether it's actually true — roughly, whether the practical stakes a subject faces can change whether she knows, holding her evidence fixed. My own credence that some form of pragmatic encroachment is correct sits around 0.55, but our TA seems to be somewhere near 0.2, and I'd like a second opinion before I commit to a number in print. Concretely, take the familiar shape of the cases: two agents with the same evidence, the same psychological profile, and equally reliable belief-forming processes, but one of them stands to lose something significant if she's wrong; the intuitive pull is that the low-stakes agent knows while the high-stakes agent merely has a justified true belief. The proposition I care about is that this pull reflects the genuine truth-conditions of knowledge ascriptions, not merely something about how conversation works.

I don't want a survey of the literature — I can do that part myself. I want your actual credence on the proposition, decomposed into the sub-questions it really rests on, with a rough probability and a sentence of justification for each. Some terms you might include, though I'd trust your decomposition over mine: (a) that the stakes-sensitive intuitions in the cases are reliable data about "knows" rather than noise, given that the cases are somewhat contrived and subjects' responses vary with how the vignettes are worded; (b) that the best alternative explanations — contextualism about the standards in play, or stories on which the intuitions track pragmatic implicature or norms of assertion rather than knowledge itself — fail to account for the full range of data; (c) that pragmatic encroachment, if real, generalizes beyond a handful of cases rather than being a marginal phenomenon; and (d) that the concept of knowledge is stable enough, as a matter of how the term functions, for the question to have a determinate answer at all. Then combine the terms into an overall number, tell me which term the final credence is most sensitive to, and — since I'll have to defend my number against the TA — tell me where you think someone at 0.55 and someone at 0.2 most likely disagree: is it about the intuition data, about how well the pragmatic alternatives do the explanatory work, or about something more methodological, like whether we should expect a philosophically clean notion of knowledge at the end of the day? Finally, be explicit about what would change your mind: which single argument or piece of evidence, if it succeeded, would move your number the most, and where you suspect your own reasoning here is most likely to fail.
