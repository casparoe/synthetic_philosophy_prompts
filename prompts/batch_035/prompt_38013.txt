Write a satirical product-catalog page for a company that sells precommitment devices to people with akrasia — think SkyMall crossed with a self-help bestseller's marketing copy. Maybe 600-700 words total. The catalog should have maybe 5 or 6 products, each with a name, ad copy, and a short customer review, and the humor should come from getting the actual psychology of weakness of will right: hyperbolic rather than exponential discounting (so your preferences literally reverse as the reward gets closer), the way a personal rule works by bundling a whole class of choices together so one lapse costs you the rule and not just the lapse, the credibility problem where every exception you grant yourself is evidence to future selves that exceptions are grantable, and the arms race between your now-self and your soon-self, where each side is fully rational and neither is the "real" you. Some ideas you could work in: a timed snack lockbox whose one-time emergency override gets renegotiated within the week; a gym-membership escrow scheme; a "Odysseus package" that lets you pre-order things to be tied to masts; a subscription that emails your past self's motivational speeches to your present self, who experiences them as nagging from a stranger; a premium tier that fails on purpose because being defeated fair and square is the only outcome both selves accept. At least one review should be from a customer who has realized they're now akratic about installing the akrasia devices (the meta-lapse). And somewhere a philosopher-type reviewer should complain, in one or two lines, that the whole catalog assumes the discounted-to-near-zero distant self is the "true" one with standing to rule — why should tonight-morning get to be the boss just because it's earlier? Keep it deadpan — the catalog genuinely wants to sell these things — and make sure a behavioral economist would nod along rather than wince. don't let it become a lecture, the concepts should show up in the products and reviews, not in an explainer paragraph.
