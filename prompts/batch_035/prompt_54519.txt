Can you build me a thought experiment on acausal trade where the usual crutches are taken away? Two agents, no causal influence possible in either direction (outside each other's light cones, say), and the trade should be something concrete — each permanently forgoes a benefit in order to confer one on the other. Constraints: the agents aren't superintelligences, they don't simulate each other, and their mutual predictions are no better than a shrewd person's guesses about a stranger; and you can't just stipulate that they reason identically, the rationality of the trade has to come out of the structure of the case. The snag I want you to confront rather than hand-wave: agents with no causal connection presumably can't even know of each other's existence, since no information ever passed between them. If you have to relax one of my constraints to get a coherent case at all, say which one and why — dont quietly relax two. After the scenario: walk through each agent's decision-theoretic reasoning, identify which feature is load-bearing (logical correlation? a shared decision procedure? mutual modelling?), and then build a twin case that differs in exactly that one respect where the trade is no longer rational. Finish by saying whether a causal decision theorist can brush the whole thing off as just evidential reasoning about someone who doesn't exist, and whether that brush-off actually sticks. Answer in British English please.
