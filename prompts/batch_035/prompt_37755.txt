been reading interpretability stuff since the old "which neurons light up" days, through the superposition hypothesis era, to whatever we're calling it now, and I've noticed the arguments in the comments never move. someone posts "we found the circuit for X", half the replies are "you're reading tea leaves, that's just a story you're telling about the weights" and the other half are "we ablated it and the behavior went away, it's causally real, checkmate." and nobody ever says what would actually settle it, because I don't think anyone has written down what we're even asking for when we ask whether a network *has* a representation of X.

so before I read another one of these, I want the spec. write me the desiderata: what does a notion of "representation" in a neural network have to deliver before interpretability claims count as discovery instead of projection? I can think of candidate requirements but I don't trust my own list:

- detectability -- there's some stable structure (direction, subnetwork, whatever) you can point to
- causal necessity -- knock it out and the X-behavior degrades, and ideally not much else
- causal sufficiency -- activate it in a context that never had X and you get X-ish behavior out
- portability -- the same feature shows up in other models trained on other data, or is it fine if it's idiosyncratic to this net?
- faithfulness to the concept's inferential role -- the classic problem that the thing you called the "dog detector" also fires on parks and fur and snow, so did you find "dog" or a grab bag of correlates? which means the requirement is something like: the feature relates to other features roughly the way the concept relates to other concepts
- and whatever it is has to survive superposition, where one set of weights carries a dozen things and a "feature" might not line up with any natural unit of the network at all

for each requirement, say what case or consideration is behind it, and mark which ones are load-bearing for realism about representations vs which are just conveniences of current technique (e.g. maybe "features are directions" is a technique artifact, not a commitment). and here's the part I most want: flag any pair of requirements you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. my hunch is fine-grained causal specificity and "the code is distributed/superposed" are in tension, and that maybe detectability and faithfulness pull apart too, but maybe I'm wrong.

then, briefly, run the list against (a) the deflationary view where interp is just a lossy compression that helps you predict the model and there's no fact of the matter about what it represents, (b) full feature realism where the net has a genuine world model with discrete concepts in it, and (c) the pragmatic in-between. keep that part short, the criteria are what I care about. also: if you think the whole question is confused -- that "does the network really have representations" is a wrong question, like asking whether a thermostat really believes it's hot -- then say that, but tell me what the better question is, because I'll keep reading these papers either way and I'd like to know what I should be checking for.
