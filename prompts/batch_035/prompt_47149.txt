I do grantmaking at a small family foundation -- we give out roughly $1.5M a year, mostly in $50-150k chunks, and the docket runs maybe 60 proposals a year across a stupidly wide range: local homelessness, global health, a youth literacy program, emergency preparedness, some AI thing last quarter, environmental stuff. Two program staff plus me, no in-house economists, and a board of family members who have recently gotten very into the importance/tractability/neglectedness framework after one of them read about it somewhere. The problem is that when it comes time to actually rank proposals, everyone just vibes: something gets called "highly neglected" if nobody on the board had heard of it before, "tractable" if the grantee sounds confident, and "important" if it involves children or death. Then the framework gets invoked after the fact to justify whatever we were going to do anyway.

I want the opposite: an actual procedure. Please turn ITN into something I can run on a stack of 10-15 proposals in an afternoon, at our scale. Concretely:

- The questions to ask of each proposal, in order, with what counts as a sufficient answer at each step -- including how to handle it when I have no numbers and no time to get any (most of our applicants can't even tell me how many people their program serves).
- How to score or rate scale, tractability, and neglectedness without pretending to a precision we don't have. Does multiplying three fuzzy scores together make sense at our size, or does it just launder vibes through arithmetic? If it's the latter, what's the honest alternative?
- Where the procedure says no on its own, and where it has to stop and hand the decision to the board because the remaining question is about values, not facts. Our board cares about the local community; the framework, applied straight, will probably keep telling them to send money overseas. I need the procedure to flag that as a values question rather than pretending ITN settles it.
- The failure modes I should watch for. The one I already suspect: neglectedness can mean "nobody's working on this" or it can mean "people looked at this and it's a bad idea," and those score identically if I just ask who else is funding it. What other ways does this framework misfire, and which misfires are cheap to catch and which aren't?

Then run it on three from a recent-ish docket, which I've slightly anonymized: (1) a food bank expansion in our own county, very well run, everyone knows the director; (2) a small org doing early literacy interventions that claims a big effect per dollar but has one unpublished pilot; (3) a pooled fund for pandemic preparedness where our $100k is a rounding error but the org argues that's exactly why it counts -- huge scale, almost no private funders. At least one of these should come out with an answer the board will hate, and I want to see how the procedure handles that rather than smoothing it over.

I don't need the history of the framework or who invented it or anything -- just the thing my team can actually run, and an honest accounting of where it stops being a procedure and starts being judgment with better posture.
