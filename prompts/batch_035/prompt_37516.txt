My housemate and I have been having a running argument about what "freedom" is and we decided to do it empirically, sort of — instead of trading theories we've been trading cases where I have a confident gut verdict, and now I want to know if there's a principle under all of them or whether I'm just being inconsistent. So here are my verdicts. Stopped at a red light at 3am on a completely empty street: no one is stopping me, nobody would even know if I ran it, and I don't feel remotely unfree — not unfree. My month-to-month lease where the landlord could evict me at any time for any reason, no appeal: he's friendly and has never done it, but I feel less free than a friend with the same apartment on a two-year contract — unfree, and more so than the light, which already seems weird since in both cases nobody actually interfered. My friend whose husband is warm, generous, and never restricts a single thing she does, but everyone including her knows which of her choices would end the marriage, and she steers around them without ever admitting that's why — unfree (I half remember some philosopher using a contented-slave or beloved-wife example like this, and I buy it). Someone on a visa that can be revoked at an official's discretion with no appeal: unfree even during the years they sail through and never once get stopped. Too broke to afford the train to the coast — no person is doing anything to me, prices just are what they are — and yet I still say unfree, which is exactly where my housemate starts yelling at me.

And then the one I keep flip-flopping on: a couple where one partner always eventually gets their way, not by threatening anything, just because the other one gives in to avoid the mood. No explicit threat is ever issued, nothing is ever taken away, and I genuinely can't settle whether that's the landlord case in slow motion or just... a relationship being a relationship. There's also the newer one that keeps nagging at me: a recommender app that has learned me so well that when I scroll I take whatever it gives me. Nobody is stopping me from doing anything, all the options are "mine," and yet I want to say I'm being steered in a way the red light doesn't steer me.

So what I want is this: lay out exactly five candidate principles that could plausibly sit under my verdicts — things like "freedom is absence of interference" or "freedom is absence of interference plus something else" or whatever else you can construct — as a numbered list, exactly five items, no intro and no conclusion. Each item should be two sentences: first the principle itself stated precisely enough that it actually decides cases, then one sentence saying which of my verdicts it captures, which it has to throw out (especially the broke-at-the-station one, since I suspect that's the spoiler), and what it says about the sulking-partner case. If one of the five saves everything including the sulking one, I want to know which, and what its two-sentence cost is. I'd rather be told I'm holding two irreconcilable ideas of freedom than get some mushy principle that only sounds like it covers everything.
