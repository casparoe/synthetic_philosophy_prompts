I'm on the historical commission in my small town and I need to write something up before our meeting next month, and honestly I'm stuck. Quick background: we have this bronze statue in the town square of our founder-ish figure, a guy who built the mill that the whole town grew up around, donated the land for the library and the park, etc. But it came out recently (someone digitized the old town records) that he also ran on child labor well past when it was normal, and personally pushed a covenant that kept the "wrong sort of families" (his words, apparently) out of the good neighborhood. Now there's a petition to take the statue down, another petition to leave it alone, and the commission has to actually decide something.

The keep-it side mostly says it's not our job to judge a guy from the 1890s by today's standards, and that tearing it down is like erasing the town's history. I take that second part seriously but I also can't quite believe it — we still have the records, the library has his portrait, there's a whole shelf about him; the statue seems like it's doing something different from remembering, more like... continuing to say well done? But then I'm not sure what to do with the first part either, because there's a difference between condemning him and just deciding who we put on a pedestal now, or at least I think there is and I can't fully argue it. Some other options people floated: add a big explanatory plaque, move the statue to the cemetery where his grave already is, commission a second statue of the mill kids facing him (which I weirdly like but can't defend), or my neighbor's idea of just letting it weather and not ever cleaning it again, letting it slowly fall apart on its own, which everyone agrees is a bit much.

Could you draft the memo I'd circulate to the other commissioners? It should recommend one specific course of action (I'd rather not do the "study the issue further" cop-out unless you really think that's right), lay out the reasoning in a way that could actually change minds at the meeting, and deal fairly with the strongest version of the "don't judge the past" argument instead of steamrolling it. If your recommendation involves conditions (like a plaque or a relocation target), say what the condition is doing and why it matters, not just that it's a compromise for compromise's sake. Keep it to something I could read out loud in about five minutes.
