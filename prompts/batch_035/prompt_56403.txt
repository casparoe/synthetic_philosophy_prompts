Question: "Equality of what? Lay out the difference between welfare egalitarianism, resource egalitarianism (Dworkin), and the capability approach (Sen). Then say which of the three best handles (a) expensive tastes and (b) adaptive preferences, and defend your choice."

Student's answer:

"Equality of welfare says the distributive target is people's well-being, so utilitarianism is the natural starting point here since it also takes welfare as the currency — the egalitarian just insists it be spread equally rather than maximized. Equality of resources, developed by Dworkin, says we equalize what people have, not how well off they feel. Dworkin's famous auction has everyone bid for bundles of resources until no one envies anyone else's bundle, which means everyone ends up equally happy with what they got. He also distinguishes brute luck from option luck, so misfortunes you insured against are your own problem. Sen's capability approach is best seen as a technical refinement of resource equality: a capability is just a resource combined with a conversion factor, so once we know how a person converts resources into functionings, the two views collapse into one, and the choice between them is mostly pragmatic. On expensive tastes, the welfare view is embarrassed because it has to compensate people who develop champagne tastes, while the resource view says no — your tastes are your own, so resources do better there. On adaptive preferences, the resource view struggles because someone who genuinely wants less (the oppressed contentment cases) shows no resource deficit, and utility metrics are useless for the same reason. Capabilities handle this best because we can ask what people are actually able to do and be, independent of what they've learned to want. Notably, Rawls himself later accepted Sen's point and revised his own view to use capabilities as the metric of justice. Overall I'd say capabilities win on adaptive preferences, resources win on expensive tastes, and the capability approach wins overall since it inherits the strengths of resources without its blind spots."
