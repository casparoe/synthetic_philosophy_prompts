Trying to work out what I actually think "equality" should be measured in, going off my gut reactions to cases rather than theory. A colleague lost the use of her legs and needs her car and bathroom adapted: obviously the state owes her help beyond the same cash everyone else gets. A guy who can only enjoy life when drinking vintage champagne and eating at fancy places: no, I'm not subsidizing that, even though his day-to-day satisfaction is genuinely lower than most people's. Two workers with identical pay, one of them just constitutionally gloomy: I don't think he gets a top-up to cheer him up. But if the gloom is clinical depression: yes, treatment on the public purse. A gambler who blew his money and comes back for more: no. A motorcyclist who was warned, rode uninsured anyway, and is now disabled: I wobble here — part of me says he chose the risk, but refusing him feels way harsher than refusing the gambler. Someone illiterate because his schools were hopeless: obviously yes to literacy programs. Someone illiterate despite decent schools that he skipped: much less yes. So: do welfare, resources, or capabilities (or some mix with a responsibility cut) capture all this, and if one of my verdicts has to go, which would you drop and why?
