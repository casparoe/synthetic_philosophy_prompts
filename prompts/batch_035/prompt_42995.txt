I hold a strict representationist view of utility, and I want to know where it is exposed. The view is this: utility attributions have no content beyond what the representation theorems give them. An agent has a utility function just in case her choices satisfy certain coherence conditions; the function is then a mathematical repackaging of the choice data, unique only up to positive affine transformation; and statements like "she prefers A to B" or "she maximizes expected utility" report no further psychological or evaluative fact, any more than saying a body moves so as to minimize action reports an inner striving in the body. Utility is bookkeeping, not measurement of a hidden quantity — on my view there is nothing there to measure. Tell me what would count against this. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want them sorted by kind: formal results (which coherence axioms, once weakened, leave the representation so permissive that the view says nothing), empirical findings (preference reversals and related inconsistencies — or do these only threaten expected utility specifically, leaving my thinner ordinal view untouched?), and counterexamples of principle (Sen's point that choice reflects the menu: a person who never takes the apple when other fruit is available, but gladly takes it when it is the only option — if choice is not a reliable guide to preference, what exactly is the representation representing?). Consider two further cases I find uncomfortable but can't evaluate myself. First, adaptive preferences: if circumstances shape what people come to prefer — the sour-grapes pattern — my view is committed to saying the adapted preferences are fully genuine, with no principled sense in which they are the wrong ones; is that a defect or just an honest implication? Second, suppose we found agents whose choices are perfectly coherent, whose self-reports systematically invert their choice ordering, and whose physiological satisfaction measures track the reports rather than the choices: my view is committed to siding with the choices and calling the reports noise, and I want to know whether that commitment is a real prediction — something the world could have gone the other way on — or a definitional refusal to engage. Relatedly, would evidence of a neural common currency encoding value cardinally and comparably across persons refute the view, or merely give utility a realization base without giving the semantic claim any content? For each defeater, say whether a committed defender in the behaviorist lineage would concede it as counting against the view or dismiss it as aimed at something else, and be blunt about whether the view has been insulated by qualifications into unfalsifiability — and, if so, whether that is a problem for a view whose ambition is precisely to say what utility statements mean. Finally, tell me what the view predicts that its rivals (preference understood as a psychological attitude, utility as hedonic quantity) do not, since a view that predicts nothing distinctive is hard to lose and worth little.
