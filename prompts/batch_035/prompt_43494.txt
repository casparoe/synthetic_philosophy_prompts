Is there a name for this? I grew up in a town with an annual commemoration of an old mill disaster, and the story everyone tells has the owners locking the doors so the workers couldn't get out. A while back I dug through the local paper archive out of curiosity, and the contemporary reporting says nothing about locked doors — it describes the fire and the deaths plainly, doors never come up. When I mention this to people they don't argue. They'll say "huh, interesting," and then go right back to telling it with the locked doors at the next commemoration. And so do I, honestly. It's not that anyone thinks the archive is lying — it's more like the two versions live side by side, the ritual version gets told at the ritual and the archival version at the archive, and nobody feels any tension between them. I've since noticed the same shape elsewhere: a family that keeps a story about an ancestor that one cousin disproved with documents, and the family didn't dispute the cousin, they just kept telling the story and left the documents at the cousin's house. So the pattern isn't denial, and it isn't anyone editing the record — the record just loses every contest it doesn't absolutely have to win, and everyone involved concedes it won, and that concession somehow changes nothing. I know this is somewhere near collective memory, but that seems to name shared remembering in general, not this specific bit where the community *knows about* the discrepancy, grants the record is right, and keeps performing the other version anyway. It also doesn't seem like historical revisionism, since nobody touches the record. Is there an established concept that covers exactly this, and if not, can you coin a term and define it well enough to distinguish it from revisionism, invented tradition, and ordinary forgetting? Please keep it under 350 words, and don't just give me a bare label — I want to know what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, and where its edges are.
