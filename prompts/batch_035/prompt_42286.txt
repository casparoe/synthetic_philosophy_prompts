They came over for bbq saturday and spent 3 hours arguing about whether to shut it down or not, and they want me to referee but honestly i got lost, so heres the argument.

(1) Dana said the obvious thing: every dollar they already put into the truck is gone whether they quit or not, so the only question is whether the NEXT year is worth doing, and on the numbers alone the answer is no. Keeping it going just because they've sacrificed so much is the textbook sunk cost mistake.

(2) Rick said wait, the past isn't actually gone, its sitting in the truck. The equipment, sure, but also stuff you cant sell: they finally have the permit process figured out, the commissary kitchen relationship, the regulars who know them. A stranger starting the same truck today would be 3 years behind, so the "sunk" years are quietly changing what the future looks like. Its not a mistake to count that.

(3) Dana said that's sneaky but its really just agreeing with her — if the customer base and know-how make the future look good, then continue FOR THAT REASON, not because of the money already spent. Rick's point about the value of past investment is still just future value wearing a costume.

(4) Rick said maybe, but heres the thing she keeps skipping: quitting has a cost you cant see on a spreadsheet. If they fold now, in two years when they get old and bored and want to start some little business again, rick says dana will bring up the truck every single time, and he knows it because she already does. The version of their marriage where they pushed through one bad stretch is worth something, and people who bail whenever year 2 gets ugly never build anything. He said its like, you dont keep a promise because keeping it pays, you keep it because you made it, and same here, they made a promise to the business and to the regulars.

(5) Dana said thats exactly how people talk themselves into losing their retirement. She said promises to people are one thing, a promise to a money-losing food truck is just stubbornness with a nicer name, and "we're finishers" is the same logic a gambler uses at 3am. Also she pointed out that by his rule theyd have to keep the truck FOREVER, because there will always be a next hard stretch that quitting would make them quitters.

Then they both looked at me. Can you go through it move by move and tell me whos right about what? Im espescially confused about whether ricks step 2 actually beats danas step 3 or not, and whether his step 4 about promises is a real point or just emotional. And who comes out more right overall?
