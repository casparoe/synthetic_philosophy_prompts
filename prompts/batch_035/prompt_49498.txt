I'm a graduate student in cognitive science, and for our seminar on theories of consciousness I've been asked to write a short position statement laying out where I stand on IIT and its neighbors, which the other students will then critique. Before I submit it, I want a consistency audit, because I've been assembling these views from different papers and arguments over two years and I suspect some of them were never meant to live together. Here is the set. (a) I'm a physicalist: consciousness is fully constituted by the physical organization of a system, with no extra ingredients. (b) I think integrated information theory is broadly on the right track: consciousness just is integrated information, and the quantity Phi, whatever the ultimately correct measure turns out to be, tracks both the presence and the degree of experience. (c) I accept the exclusion postulate: when candidate systems overlap in the brain, only the one with maximum Phi is conscious as such, so while I am conscious, my visual cortex, my individual neurons, and any mere aggregate of my neurons are not. (d) I hold organizational invariance: what matters is the fine-grained causal structure a system physically instantiates, not the substrate, so a system with the same causal structure implemented in silicon would be exactly as conscious as I am. (e) I accept a weak, graded panpsychism: any system with nonzero integrated information has some experience, however minimal -- a photodiode, a small network of logic gates. (f) I have a strong intuition that no two systems could match in all input-output dispositions over time, under any test you like, and still differ in whether there is something it is like to be them; in particular, I cannot accept that a perfect behavioral duplicate of me could be a philosophical zombie. (g) I think facts about consciousness should be invariant under redescription at different spatiotemporal grains: whether and how much a system experiences should not depend on which spatial resolution or timescale an investigator happens to pick.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs (or larger subsets) are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be in tension and dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe each real tension is. For each genuine one, say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me whether there is a single revision that would clean up the most conflicts at once. I'd also like to know, where relevant, whether a tension I've created is a problem for integrated information theory itself or only for my particular gloss on it -- I'm prepared to learn that some of these commitments are things I imported from functionalism that IIT was never supposed to accommodate. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as an undifferentiated list, and don't hesitate to tell me that a view I expressed confidently is actually two different views wearing one label.
