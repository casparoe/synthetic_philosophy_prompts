In my senior seminar we watched a documentary that ended with a segment on the simulation argument. The narrator's summary went roughly like this: a philosopher proved that if it's ever technologically possible for a future civilization to build convincing simulations of past human minds, then simulated people must vastly outnumber real ones, and since we have no way of telling from the inside which kind we are, simple statistics says we are almost certainly characters in some future civilization's simulation. The narrator added that the philosopher himself concluded we should just accept this and get on with life, on the assumption that our world, right down to its laws of physics, is literally being computed by someone, the way a video game computes its world. My teacher described the segment as a proof that we live in something like the Matrix, though she admitted she had never read the original paper. I was a little suspicious of how confident the narrator sounded, and my classmate insisted the whole thing is just modern skepticism about being a brain in a vat, which the documentary never mentioned. Is the documentary's summary fair? Please go claim by claim: tell me what the philosopher actually argued, what the narrator simplified in an acceptable way, and what was genuinely misleading — and in particular whether the conclusion that we are probably simulated follows from the premises the documentary actually stated, and whether the brain-in-a-vat comparison my classmate made captures what is going on.
