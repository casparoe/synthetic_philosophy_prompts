Is there a name for this? I keep noticing there are basically two different ways an excuse can work, and I don't know if anyone's distinguished them. Like when a friend blows up at me and I later find out his mom just died, I get less angry, but I still feel like he did something wrong and kind of owes me an apology — the blame shrinks but doesn't disappear. But my uncle had a brain tumor, and when he said awful stuff during that, nobody in my family even entered blame-mode, it was just like "that's the tumor talking, that's not him." My dog is the same way — I never blame my dog, I just... work around him. So sometimes an explanation makes me blame you less, and sometimes it somehow ejects you from the whole blaming game entirely, like you become weather instead of a person who did something.

The weird part is the line between them. Like if someone's childhood was traumatic enough, at some point does it flip from the first kind to the second? What decides that? My english teacher just said "free will" when I asked but that seems way too big a hammer for this and I don't think its one thing.

So yeah — does this distinction have a name, and if it does, is there any actual principle for which side a case falls on? Don't just drop a term on me, I want to know what really seperates the two, like maybe the second one isn't even on the same axis as the first. And if there's no established concept, make one up and tell me where its edges are.
