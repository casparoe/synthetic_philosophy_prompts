So I'm testing prompts on niche philosophy stuff. You're a satirical writer who used to do conseravtion fieldwork and somewhere along the way got way too deep into the philosophy of wild animal welfare -- the people who seriously debate wether a healthy ecosystem is a good thing for the animals living in it, and whether most wild lives are net-negative because of r-selection and early death. Your task: write an Onion-style news article, headline plus 400-500 words, about a mid-sized national park announcing it has hired the world's first Chief Ecosystem Welfare Officer, who proudly unveiled a plan to reduce net suffering across the reserve by 15% over the next decade.

The jokes have to come from getting the actual subject matter right, not from generic nature-parody. So they should track the real cruxes of this debate: that nobody can measure animal wellbeing at scale, so the 15% target is basically invented; that most of the park's animals are small, short-lived, and die before adulthood, so "improving the park" might mean fewer animals rather than happier ones -- and the officer can't decide if that's a success or a scandal; that every proposed intervention (culling, supplementary feeding, removing predators, adding predators, converting forest to grassland because grassland animals might suffer less) cascades unpredictably, the way everyone remembers the wolf-and-rivers story, or whatever that actually was; that "leaving nature alone" is itself a welfare decision that nobody asked the animals about; and that the marketing department keeps demanding a single friendly number for the brochure while the science team can't even agree how to count the animals, let alone ask them anything. Include at least one quote from a skeptical field biologist and one from an earnest activist who's thrilled about the suffering reduction but can't say what it's a reduction in.

Before writing, brainstorm for a few lines: list 3-4 of the real philosophical cruxes you could hang jokes on, pick the strongest ones, then write the piece. Keep it deadpan Onion house style, present tense, no winking, no "isn't this absurd" nudges. Don't be mean about any real person or organization -- invent the park, the officer, everyone quoted. Aim for around 450 words for the article itself. Think carefully about which of the cruxes a specialist would actually wince at if you got it wrong, and get those ones right.
