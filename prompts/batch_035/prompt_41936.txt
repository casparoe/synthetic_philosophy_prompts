I'd like to rehearse an objection to functional decision theory against its best defender. Please play a committed FDT proponent — comfortable speaking of deciding as if fixing the output of a decision procedure rather than of a single act, relaxed about reasoning ex ante from behind a veil of pre-commitment, insistent that the causal structure of a decision problem is the thing one must get right. Be sharp, never a strawman; you may concede where the theory costs intuition, but you may not abandon the core view. Stay in character until I write 'stop', and keep each reply to a single paragraph.

Here is my opening move. In the counterfactual mugging, Omega flipped a fair coin before I existed, the coin landed tails in this branch, and Omega now asks me to hand over £100 with no prospect of receiving anything in return — the £10,000 was on heads, the branch I know did not happen. You tell me to pay, because the policy of paying made me better off in the branch I never saw: my decision procedure's output is linked, through Omega's prediction, to how things went for me over there. But now a blackmailer arrives who knows in advance whether my factory will burn down this year. He sends his demand letter if and only if either the fire will happen and he predicts I won't pay, or the fire won't happen and he predicts I will — so the letter on my desk is evidence, if anything is, that paying goes with safety. You tell me to refuse, on the ground that the fire was settled by causes my cheque cannot touch. Yet in the mugging, my payment likewise does nothing causally in this branch: you invoked dependence running through the predictor's past action. The blackmailer's sending of this letter also depended on his prediction of me. Both cases hand me a demand whose profitability to the demander was fixed by a prediction of my policy, and in both cases the payoff-relevant event lies in the past or in branches I can't reach. So what, exactly, is the asymmetry — or is your treating these cases differently just the visible seam where 'evaluate the policy, not the act' gets tailored case by case to whatever intuition is handy?
