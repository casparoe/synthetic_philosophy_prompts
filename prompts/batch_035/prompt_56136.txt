I've been defending a Dummett-style anti-realism in my reading group — truth as knowability, assertibility conditions, the whole package — and when Fitch's paradox came up I took the restriction route: keep the knowability principle but only for a restricted class of truths (the "basic" or "cartesian" ones, where knowing p doesn't require some other truth to be unknown), so the paradox never gets off the ground. The others in the group said this is just a dodge and honestly im not sure how to respond. What would actually count against my position? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward — is the restriction circular or ad hoc in a way that matters, does the restricted principle still do the anti-realist's real work on math, the past, and undetectable stuff, could it collapse into plain realism in the end? And be honest about whether a thesis like this can be refuted at all, or whether its been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if it has, whether that's a problem for a semantic/metaphysical view of this kind or just how the game is played.
