An autonomous reasoning system must set betting odds on statements of the form "the fixed machine M outputs 1 on input n," for many values of n on which M has not yet been run. Each statement is in principle decidable by computation, but not yet decided in practice. Rate each of the following credence policies on a scale of 1–10 as a model of rational logical uncertainty, with a one-sentence justification for each score:

1. Assign 1/2 to every statement whose truth value has not yet been computed.
2. Assign 1/2 initially, but revise as though newly computed input–output pairs were ordinary empirical evidence.
3. Require full probabilistic coherence not just over the computed facts but over all of their logical consequences, ignoring how expensive it is to derive those consequences.
4. Suspend credence entirely and refuse all bets on undecided statements.

Then rank the four policies by how well they avoid being exploited by an opponent who is merely faster at deriving consequences than the system is, and state in one or two sentences which policy you would actually deploy and what its most serious remaining defect is.
