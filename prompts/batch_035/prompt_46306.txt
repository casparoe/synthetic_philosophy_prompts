My mom (74, still sharp) needs knee surgery after a fall. Routine op, good success rates, she understands all of that, and she says she'd "rather just take it easy." She's also been saying stuff like she's too old to make a fuss over herself. Here's my problem: my sister is the family's designated referee on whether someone "really" wants something, and whoever she sides with at sunday dinner wins. So before that dinner I want to predict her verdict, because if she says it's a real preference we all drop it forever, and if she says it's a bias then it's surgery-talk time.

Her track record, from years of arguments:

1. Our uncle quit a good finance job at 52 to keep bees. Everyone said midlife crisis, she said its his preference, respect it.
2. My cousin keeps going back to a boyfriend who runs her down. Pressed, my cousin admits he manipulates her but says she still wants to stay. Sister: not a real preference, that's him talking, not you.
3. My aunt refuses vaccines because of things she heard online. Sister: that's a bias, she's misinformed.
4. I only got into hiking because an ex dragged me up a mountain ten years ago, now I do it every weekend. When I joked that my hobby is just leftover from a relationship, she said obviously it's a real preference, who cares how it started.
5. Grandpa refuses to sell the farm even though it's eating the family alive financially. Sister: his preference, respect it.
6. A family friend's daughter announced she's not going to college because "school isn't for people like us." Sister: that's a bias, not a preference.

So: what do you think she'll say about mom? I can see more than one rule fitting all six — something like "if they'd still want it with the facts straight, it's a preference," something like "it only doesn't count if it was planted in them by manipulation or by people keeping them down," and something like the sour-grapes test, where what matters is whether they'd still want it if they weren't worn down (which would mean the daughter's college thing and mom's surgery are biases but my hiking is fine, since it just happens to have come from an ex rather than from being ground down). Those rules seem to split on mom: she has all the facts, which points one way, but "too old to make a fuss" sounds a LOT like "school isn't for people like us," which points the other. And the deliberation part is weird too — when I asked whether she'd want the surgery if she hadn't fallen, she got annoyed and said she doesn't need interrogating, so I genuinely can't tell if she's refused to think it through or already thought it through.

Give me your best guess with reasons, say which of the six precedents is the closest analog, and tell me what principle she'd be committing herself to either way. Also: what case would actually split the surviving rules? The one I keep chewing on is someone who knows exactly where their desire came from, knows it's bad for them by their own standards, has thought it all the way through, and keeps it anyway — but maybe there's a better one.

For what it's worth, I'd rather she respect grandpa's farm verdict than my cousin's boyfriend verdict, whatever that costs me on mom.
