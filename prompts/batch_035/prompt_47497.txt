I'm an undergraduate taking an intermediate course in normative ethics, and we've just reached the debate between deontological constraints and consequentialism. Rather than argue at the level of theory, I've been trying to pin down my own verdicts on cases, and I'd like to know whether they add up to a coherent principle — and whether that principle can survive what I gather is called the paradox of deontology. My verdicts so far:

- Diverting a runaway trolley from a track holding five onto a track holding one: permissible.
- A surgeon harvesting a healthy patient's organs to save five dying patients: impermissible — and I'm confident of this even though the arithmetic looks the same as in the trolley case.
- The loop variant, where the lone person on the side track is needed as a physical buffer to stop the trolley from circling back onto the five: I keep going back and forth. It feels closer to the surgeon than to the plain switch case, and I can't say why.
- Torturing one person in order to stop a regime from torturing five: impermissible. But this is the verdict that bothers me most, because it seems to commit the agent to ensuring that more torture occurs overall — as if the rule forbids him to minimize the very thing it forbids.
- Lying to a murderer at the door about where the intended victim is hiding: permissible.
- A sheriff framing and executing one innocent man to prevent a riot that will kill several others: impermissible.

Is there a single principle — the doctrine of double effect, a doing/allowing distinction, or something else — that delivers all or most of these? In particular, does anything cleanly separate the plain switch case from the loop case, or should I stop pretending they come apart? And where does the torture case leave me on the paradox: does my verdict there force me to accept that constraints can require allowing more of the prohibited thing to happen, or would consistency require giving up one of my verdicts instead? If you propose a principle, please say what it predicts for a case I haven't listed — say, bombing a munitions factory in a civilian area to shorten a war, as against firebombing the residential district to break the population's will — and be honest about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate.
