You are an experienced philosophy podcaster and former academic editor preparing for a rare kind of episode. My guest is an early-career ethicist whose dissertation defends a version of negative utilitarianism she calls lexical threshold negative utilitarianism. The view has two tiers: below a certain intensity of suffering, pleasures and pains trade off in the ordinary additive way, so mild discomfort can be outweighed by ordinary goods; but suffering above the threshold is lexically worse — no quantity of below-threshold pleasure, however large or prolonged, can compensate even a single instance of it. Among threshold-exceeding sufferings themselves, severity comparisons still apply, so the view isn't a flat deontology of "any agony blocks everything." She also holds that the threshold's location is vague but not therefore unreal, and that our evidence about it is partly phenomenological — drawn from testimony from people who have endured extreme pain and report that they would not accept any amount of ordinary happiness in exchange for a repeat of it. I've read the dissertation chapters she sent ahead, and she is a careful, unflappable interviewee. I don't want a restatement of the view; I want the places it's genuinely under pressure.

Act as a philosophical interviewer and draft nine to ten questions for her. Think carefully about where the position is actually fragile before writing, and make each question turn on the content of the view rather than on general ethics. At minimum I want one question on locating the threshold — specifically the worry that an agony just below and an agony just above it can be phenomenally almost identical, so the lexical cliff seems to drop out of nowhere — with a follow-up keyed to the most likely reply (I expect her to invoke higher-order vagueness or a comparison with the sorites). I want one question on whether threshold-exceeding sufferings can aggregate with each other, and whether that concession quietly collapses the lexicality back into ordinary trade-offs after all. I want one question on the classic objection to negative-leaning views — that someone who weights only suffering, or blocks it lexically, seems committed to preferring a swift painless end to all sentient life if that's the only guaranteed way to eliminate extreme suffering — and whether her threshold structure genuinely blocks that inference or just re-labels the commitment. And I want one question on interpersonal comparisons: if the threshold is fixed partly by first-person testimony, what do we do when people's self-reports of what they'd endure for what reward seem to diverge systematically, or when someone's testimony is shaped by the very deprivation we're asking them to price?
