Quick backstory: my younger sister lost her husband two years ago and last month got diagnosed with prolonged grief disorder (apparently that's in the DSM now?? which is how I fell down this rabbit hole in the first place). Since then I've been reading the naturalism vs normativism fight in philosophy of medicine and it's kind of eating my brain, because it seems SO stalled. On one side you've got Boorse's biostatistical theory: a disease is just a part of you functioning below the species-typical level for your reference class (age, sex, whatever), full stop, no value judgments allowed in. On the other side the normativists, who say that's insane -- health is at least partly about whether you can pursue your goals and live the life you want, so values come in at the ground floor. And then there's Wakefield trying to split it with his harmful dysfunction thing: evolutionary dysfunction plus a value judgment about the harm. That debate is like 50 years old now and as far as I can tell everyone is just repeating their greatest hits.

Here's my actual question, and I want a diagnosis not a survey. What kind of disagreement IS this? My working theory, which I hold loosely: it's basically verbal -- the two camps are answering different questions under one word. The naturalists are doing something like taxonomy (which conditions cluster and get explained by medical science) and the normativists are doing gatekeeping (which conditions deserve the medical system's attention, sympathy, and the insurance code that gets my sister's therapy paid for). Nobody disputes that both jobs exist; they just both insist their job is what "disease" really means. But maybe I'm wrong and there's a real substantive fight hiding in there -- maybe about whether statistical typicality can even be defined without smuggling values into the reference class, or about whether functions are objective facts. Am I close? And if I'm right that it's two questions wearing one coat, is there anything that would actually count as progress -- a distinction, a split of the concept, whatever -- or does the word "disease" just do too much work socially (sick leave, disability payments, stigma, the whole apparatus) for the definitional fight to ever stop mattering even if it's verbal? Genuinely asking, this is now the thing I think about instead of sleeping.
