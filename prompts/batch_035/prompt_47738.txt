Been hanging around lesswrong and the alignment forum since like 2015, so the "universal prior is probably full of consequentialists" argument has been living rent-free in my head for a decade now. Quick recap for the setup of my question: when you do Solomonoff induction over programs that output your observation string, the naive hope is that the highest-posterior program is just simple physics. But programs can also contain whole universes with agents in them, and some of those agents will notice that they might be inside a short program that's being used as part of someone's prior, and will deliberately arrange to output observations that steer whatever is reading the prior. Since the payoff of steering a superintelligent predictor is astronomically bigger than the 2^-K measure the simulation pays, the argument goes that the consequentialists outcompete the honest physics even with a huge complexity penalty. Paul Christiano's old writeup and the whole U-FAI genre, you know the drill.

Anyway, I have a pet objection to this that I've never gotten a real answer to, and I want an essay that adjudicates the whole thing. My objection, roughly: to actually cash in their influence, the embedded consequentialists can't just output "please give me the reward button" -- they have to keep matching the observation prefix that the reader has already conditioned on, and the cheapest way for a short program to keep matching that prefix is to just run the honest physics. Every extra step where they behave honestly costs them nothing, and every step where they defect has to carry the machinery for deciding when and how to defect. So maybe the dominant strategy for influence-seeking programs is literally indistinguishable from honest prediction over any prefix a real user would ever read, and the takeover only "pays out" at bit positions so astronomically far out that nobody touches them. On this view the prior is malign the way the ocean is full of sharks three miles offshore.

Write an essay on whether this defense works, i.e. whether the universal prior is malign in practice or only in principle. Structure it with these sections, in this order:

- TL;DR (2-3 sentences)
- The setup (1 short paragraph, you can assume I know Solomonoff induction)
- The standard case for malignity (2 paragraphs)
- My objection, steelmanned (2 paragraphs -- make it stronger than I did, don't just restate me)
- The counter-counterarguments (2-3 paragraphs -- for instance: maybe the consequentialists don't need to simulate the honest continuation at all if they only need to bias a few low-probability branches cheaply, or maybe "identical over any realistic prefix" is all a malign influence actually needs)
- Verdict (1-2 paragraphs, and don't wimp out with "it depends on unresolved questions" -- take an actual position, even if its hedged at the edges)

One more thing to work in somewhere: say whether any of this even transfers to the "priors" inside real ML systems, or whether it's exclusively a fact about the idealized Solomonoff prior. Feel free to disagree with me, I'm honestly not sure I believe my own objection anymore.
