At next month's departmental workshop I'm defending a trimmed-down version of Lewis's modal realism: possible worlds are concrete and of the same ontological kind as ours, but I individuate them by causal isolation rather than spatiotemporal separation, and I stay deliberately neutral on how many worlds there are. The audience contains no sympathetic modal theorists: two neo-Quineans who hold that ontology should answer only to natural science, a philosopher of mathematics who regards abstract-object talk as eliminable by paraphrase, a pragmatist who treats modal locutions as bookkeeping devices, and a graduate cohort raised on deflationary accounts of modality. Prepare my Q&A sheet: the eight or so objections this audience will actually raise, ordered by how much damage each would do, each phrased the way they would put it, with a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft. Indicate what I should concede up front, what I must hold for the thesis to survive, and the one objection I am hoping nobody asks.
