My phil of science reading group has been stuck on the Humeanism vs anti-Humeanism fight about laws of nature for the better part of a year, and I'm starting to think the stall isn't just us being slow. On the Humean side you've got the Lewis picture: the world is just a vast mosaic of local matters of particular fact, and the laws are nothing over and above whatever pattern-claimer would score best on a competition between true systems that balance simplicity and strength and fit. On the anti-Humean side you've got the governing conception -- Armstrong's laws as necessitation relations between universals, Maudlin's laws as primitive entities that literally push things around, whatever the flavor. Every argument feels like it's on the table: the anti-Humean says a law that merely supervenes on the facts can't explain those facts without going in a circle, and that Humeans owe us an account of why the mosaic is regular in the first place; the Humean says the "governing" relation is mysterious, does no work, and that unification is all explanation ever needed to be. Then someone brings up the inference problem -- whether we can even infer governing laws from the regularities we observe without begging the question -- and around we go again. Meanwhile the physics people in the room bring up whether quantum mechanics (especially on Everett-type readings) plus whatever ends up being true about spacetime threatens the whole mosaic picture the Humean needs, and the philosophers mostly shrug.

Here's my working hypothesis, which I want pressure-tested rather than just affirmed: the real fight isn't about ontology at all. It's about what an explanation has to be. The anti-Humean quietly requires that explaining something means identifying whatever constrains or produces or makes-necessary the thing explained, and the Humean quietly requires only systematization, and since neither ever states this requirement as a premise, every exchange ends with each side staring at the other like they've been handed a non sequitur. Layered on top of that, maybe, is a second fight about whether parsimony is a truth-tracking virtue or just an aesthetic preference one side happens to have.

Verbal, substantive-metaphysical, two different questions under one heading ("what do our law-statements assert" vs "what makes the world lawful"), or something else entirely?
