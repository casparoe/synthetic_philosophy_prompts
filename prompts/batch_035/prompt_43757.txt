I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and for the past few years I've been reporting on the argument over whether human violence has been in long-term decline. The two researchers I quote most have now agreed, at my urging, to run an adversarial collaboration, and my publication gets to print the joint statement when it's done. One of them — call her the declinist — holds that the record of war deaths, homicide, and state killing shows a genuine, sustained drop in the probability that a randomly chosen person dies violently, and that this is among the most important facts we know. The other — call him the skeptic — holds that the apparent pattern is largely an artifact of what gets counted, what could be counted, and how one aggregates episodes that differ by orders of magnitude. Draft the statement they could both sign.

I want the claims they agree on, including about the underlying data: which series are more trustworthy, which comparisons across centuries are legitimate, and what the twentieth century's concentration of enormous death tolls into rare episodes does and does not show about any trend. I want their disagreements stated precisely, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording — especially on per capita versus absolute counts, on whether a world with more total violent deaths but lower per-capita risk is better, worse, or neither, and on whether state-administered killing deserves separate treatment from decentralized killing. I want the cruxes: which sub-questions, if settled, would actually move one or both of them. For each crux, record a test, argument, or case both agree would bear on it — for instance, what each would predict if a newly catalogued dataset of pre-modern conflicts or historical homicide archives shifted baseline estimates substantially, or if the post-1945 pattern in great-power conflict broke one way or the other — with each side's prediction written down in advance so I can check later who called it. Finally, since I have to convey this to readers, include their joint verdict on whether any part of the dispute is verbal rather than substantive — whether "is the world becoming less violent" has a determinate answer at all before someone fixes an index, and if not, say plainly which parts of the disagreement survive once the words are straightened out. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table with cruxes and predictions. Do not include citations; I will handle sourcing myself.
