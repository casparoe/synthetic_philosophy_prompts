I want a procedure I can actually run in the forty minutes before the family meeting that sorts these into withholding vs withdrawing vs something I shouldn't call treatment at all. Ground it in whatever the philosophy actually says about doing vs allowing and killing vs letting die — acts vs omissions, whether the bathtub cases should scare me off the distinction entirely, double effect if you think it survives, ordinary vs extraordinary means if you think that cut helps. The procedure needs the questions in order, what counts as enough of an answer to each, where it hands off to the attending or the ethics committee chair because the remaining question is values not facts, and where it just fails and I'm on my own. Then run it on three cases: a family asking to discontinue a ventilator on a patient who might breathe on her own in a week, a resident wanting to quietly not escalate on a patient whose proxy is unreachable untill morning, and a nurse titrating pain meds in a dying patient where the dose needed for comfort could plausibly also depress respiration. Be honest about where the procedure gives an answer I won't like, and what it flat out can't settle.
