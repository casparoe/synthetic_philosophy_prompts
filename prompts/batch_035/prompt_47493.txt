(1) Maya said the liberal principle just is what liberalism means: on anything that's basically about yourself -- what you read, whether you smoke in your own home -- you should be socially decisive, no vote needed. (2) Sam said that's exactly the rule Sen showed is incoherent: take the prude who most wants nobody to read the dirty book but would rather read it himself than have the libertine read it, and the libertine who most wants the prude to read it -- if each man is decisive over his own reading, you can end up with an outcome that both rank below an alternative, so the liberal rule can force everyone to lose by everyone's own lights. (4) Sam said "self-regarding" is doing all the work with none of the credit: Sen's setup never tells you where to draw the personal spheres, and in real life nearly every preference is partly other-regarding anyway (people legitimately care about pollution, about cruelty, about what their kids are exposed to), so purging "meddlesome" ones just means smuggling in a full moral theory and calling it a technicality. they went around on this for another forty minutes. can you go through the four moves and say who's right at each step, and whether there's some point both of them are missing entirely?
