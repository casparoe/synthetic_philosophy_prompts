I keep encountering proposals for solving the ontology identification problem in AI alignment — getting an advanced model to track what some human concept like "happiness" or "doesn't want to be coerced" refers to when the model represents the world in its own learned ontology — and I've realized I don't have a clear sense of what would even count as a solution. Write the desiderata: what any adequate method for ontology identification has to deliver, with the case behind each requirement. At minimum I want you to address: whether the method must work when the model's ontology is alien to ours rather than merely different in granularity; whether it must survive the human's ontology being revised (e.g., a concept anchored in a folk theory that science later displaces, where the referent seems to both survive and change); whether the output has to be checkable by the human whose concepts are being identified, given that the motivating scenario often involves the model possessing inaccessible information that no human could verify; and whether identifying a concept requires also capturing its normative role (what the human counts as a mistake in applying it) or only its extension. For each desideratum, say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular research program, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I care most about whether the human-checkability requirement and the inaccessible-information motivation are compatible at all, since if they are not, that tension is itself the main result. Then, briefly, score interpretability-driven concept mapping and debate-style elicitation against the list; keep that part short, since the criteria are where the thinking is.
