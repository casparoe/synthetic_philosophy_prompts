I teach an upper-level course on non-classical logic and I've drafted the essay question below for the final. I don't want a full model answer -- I'll write that myself -- but I do want the marking scheme. Write it as a numbered list of exactly five must-hit points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion and nothing before or after the list.

Here's the question:

"A paraconsistent logician and a dialetheist can use the exact same logic (say, LP) and yet disagree about everything that matters. Explain the difference between paraconsistency as a claim about which inferences are valid and dialetheism as a claim about truth. Then evaluate the Liar-based argument for dialetheism: does deriving a contradiction from the Liar sentence (plus disquotation) show that some contradiction is actually true, or only that our best theory of truth is inconsistent in a way we could tolerate instrumentally, like we tolerate an inconsistent database or an inconsistent set of regulations? In your answer, discuss 'classical recapture' -- the dialetheist's recovery of classical reasoning for consistent fragments -- and say whether it helps or hurts the case that contradictions are genuinely true. Defend or attack: paraconsistency without dialetheism is a stable resting place."

Students get 90 minutes and it's worth 25 marks. They've all read Priest and they've all memorized the phrase "a true contradiction," so the scheme has to reward answers that go past the labels. Each of your five points should name one specific move a top answer must make, and briefly say what a B answer typically does instead.
