Design a minimal pair of variants of the surprise examination paradox that isolates a single variable: whether the students know the teacher's announcement to be true, as opposed to justifiably believing it while it happens to be true. The two scenarios must be as alike as possible in every other respect, so that any difference in how the backward induction runs can be traced to that variable alone.

Specifications:

1. The examination period runs exactly three days — Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday — so the induction can be laid out in full.
2. The announcement, fixed word for word and identical in both variants, says that an exam will be held on exactly one of the three days and that on the morning of the exam the students will not be able to deduce, from the announcement and everything else they know, that the exam is that day.
3. Surprise is to be used in exactly that one sense throughout; the diagnosis may not trade on an equivocation between did not in fact predict and could not have deduced.
4. The teacher is fully truthful and never forgetful, fixes the exam day before making the announcement, uses no randomness afterward, and holds the exam on the announced terms.
5. Neither variant may stipulate its verdict — for example, by declaring that the students cannot deduce the day; if they cannot deduce it, that must be earned by the construction. If you conclude that one variant cannot be coherently completed without violating some specification, say so explicitly, identify the violated specification, and treat that infeasibility as a finding rather than a failure — but be sure it is genuine and not an artifact of your setup.

After presenting the pair, do the following:

(a) Run the students' backward induction explicitly through all three days in each variant, flagging the exact step at which it first loses validity and which premise of that step fails.
(b) State which structural feature the knowledge variant has that the mere-belief variant lacks, what work that feature does in the induction, and what happens to the induction in each variant if the feature is deleted.
(c) Give the strongest reply available to someone who holds that the induction is sound all the way down and that the announcement is simply self-refuting, and say whether your pair forces that position to be abandoned or merely redescribed.
(d) Tell me what the pair is for: which proposed resolution of the paradox it is built to test, since that fixes what counts as cheating. A pair in which the two variants collapse into the same case, or in which the difference in verdicts is imported by fiat rather than derived from the students' epistemic situation, does not count.
