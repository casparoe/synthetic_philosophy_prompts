I run a small politics-and-philosophy newsletter and want to build a "so do you actually think you have a duty to vote?" quiz for readers ahead of the election. 8-10 questions, each one a concrete scenario with two or three answer options, and then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the real positions in that literature: the civic duty view (voting as something citizens owe each other whether or not it's fun or convenient), the expressive theory where voting is closer to cheering at a game and can't be judged by whether your single ballot tips the outcome, the "no duty to vote but you can still vote badly" view, the pure expected-consequence position that just flips on the numbers, and outright abstentionism. If the lesser-evil camp and the "vote your conscience, never reward the lesser evil" camp come apart as separate leaves, include both.

Scenarios I want in there: voting for the lesser evil in a state that's guaranteed to go one way anyway; voting third party in a genuine swing state; going to the polls specifically to cancel out your spouse's vote for a candidate you think is awful; the "no single vote has ever decided a national election so nothing is at stake in yours" argument — I want at least one question where the positions genuinely disagree about whether that argument even bites; and voting while honestly clueless about the candidates, the case that makes the "bad voting is worse than not voting" people sweat.

The whole point is to sort readers by what they say about cases, not by which label they like, so no question may just ask "do you believe voting is merely expressive?" or similar — every question should be something like "here's a person in this situation, what should they do / were they wrong?" And at the end, flag which pairs of positions the quiz can't really separate (e.g. can it honestly tell the expressive theorist apart from the conscience voter?), and if some answer combos map onto positions nobody actually holds, note whether that's because the view is incoherent or just unexplored.

Please start the response with the quiz itself — question one on the first line, no intro paragraph, no comentary about your approach before it. Scoring key, the separation caveats, and the unoccupied-combo notes after. typos in the quiz itself are fine since its for a blog but keep the scoring key clean.
