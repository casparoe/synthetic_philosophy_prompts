I got into a running argument with a friend of mine who's very into mind uploading. Her whole position rests on one analogy, repeated like a mantra: the brain is hardware, the mind is software running on it. Software doesn't care what the hardware is made of — silicon, vacuum tubes, whatever — so a faithful simulation of my mind is my mind, and I'd survive being run on future computers, and simulated people generally are just people whose substrate happens to be cheaper. I used to just nod along, but then a while back I read a hard sci-fi novel (I believe it was Greg Egan's Permutation City, though I'm probably mangling the details) that contained a thought experiment I can't stop thinking about, and I want to use it to stress-test her analogy.

The thought experiment, as I remember it: imagine a universe whose entire physics consists of billiard balls careening around and colliding — call this world the careenium. Nothing ever happens there except collisions and rebounds, by perfectly ordinary rules. Now suppose that somewhere in the careenium there's a pocket of balls whose positions and momenta, read the right way, happen to encode a running simulation of a little society of conscious beings — call these the simmballs. From the inside, the simulated beings seem to act: they deliberate, choose, build things. But zoom out, and the striking fact is that nothing is happening except careenium dynamics. Every motion of every simmball is fully caused by prior ball-collisions; the simulated people's "choices" never push the substrate around in any way that isn't already entailed by the collision rules. The pattern rides along; it doesn't reach down. And here's what bugs me: when I decide to raise my arm in *this* universe, I have a powerful intuition that a symbol-level fact — my decision — reaches down and pushes the substrate around: neurons fire, muscles contract, matter moves. In the careenium that is flatly false. So the question I keep chewing on is whether my friend's software picture quietly smuggles in exactly this downward, symbol-to-substrate causation that a simulation doesn't have — whether the mind-software analogy gets its intuitive grip from us *not* being the careenium, and whether that's true, or whether we're a careenium too and just don't like admitting it.

Can you audit the analogy for me? Please structure your answer as: (1) "The correspondence table" — what plays the role of hardware, software, the programmer (if anyone), the fan spinning up when the program runs hot, and the simmballs' choices, in both the computer case and the brain case (~150 words); (2) "Where the picture breaks" — the disanalogies, being explicit about which ones threaten my friend's uploading conclusion versus which ones are idle (~300 words); (3) "The careenium question" — say straight whether, on physicalism, we differ from the simmballs in any way that matters, i.e. whether symbols in our universe push the substrate around or only seem to, and what follows for the reality of simulated people either way (~250 words); (4) "Verdict" — does the software/hardware analogy survive in some repaired form, or should my friend find a different argument, and if so, sketched in two sentences, what would a better one look like (~150 words). Feel free to be disagreeable with the framing if you think the careenium comparison itself is confused — I'd rather know that than get a polite audit of a bad question.
