My housemate and I have a long-running argument about whether big groups are wiser than the people in them, and over the past year she has built up a set of firm calls that I've been writing down. I'll give you her track record, and then three new cases we argued about last night, and I want your prediction of what she'll say. Her calls so far: (1) if a million voters each have a 55% chance of getting the right answer, and their mistakes have nothing to do with each other, the majority is right almost beyond doubt; (2) if the same million voters are each right only 45% of the time, the majority is almost certainly wrong; (3) if a million voters are each 55% reliable but nearly all of them just follow the same three newspaper columnists, so their votes move together, the size of the crowd adds almost nothing and the country collectively is about as reliable as a single columnist; (4) a twelve-person jury whose members are each right 60% of the time will probably convict/decide correctly, but with a real chance of error, which she put at roughly one chance in four of getting it wrong.

The three new cases from last night: (a) a referendum with a million voters, each right just 50.1% of the time, fully independent — does she trust the majority here? (b) a hundred-person expert panel, each member right only 40% of the time, fully independent — she'll presumably say the majority is probably wrong, but the part I'm curious about is whether she'd then say the minority position is almost certainly the right one, or whether she'd say a group that's worse than a coin flip can't be used at all, even upside-down; and (c) a contest with three options, where in any head-to-head matchup between two options each voter picks the better one 55% of the time, but everyone just votes for their single favorite and the most votes wins.

We never resolved any of these before she left for the weekend, so I'd like your prediction of her verdicts on all three. What I'd find most useful isn't just the answers: it's the rule she seems to be running underneath her earlier calls, the other rules that would fit everything she's already said just as well, and where those rival rules come apart on my three cases. Then give me your best guess with your reasons, and suggest what further case — one I could actually pose to her — would best settle which rule she's really using. For what it's worth, I never studied statistics past school, so please explain any assumptions you're making about how these majorities behave in plain terms.
