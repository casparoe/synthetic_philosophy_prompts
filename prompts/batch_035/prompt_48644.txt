I'm a PhD student in STS, mostly philosophy of technology, and I keep running into a tension I can't put my finger on. On one side, nearly every professional code of engineering ethics I've seen treats artifacts as neutral instruments: the engineer owes duties to clients, employers, and the public, but the artifacts themselves carry no values -- values enter only at the point of use, which is why the "guns don't kill people" line sounds like common sense to most engineers. On the other side, the very same governments and professional bodies that treat tech as neutral also regulate design directly: accessibility law mandates curb cuts and screen-reader compatibility, and privacy law in Europe now requires "privacy by design" -- rules that only make sense if a design itself can embody an exclusion or a leak, before anyone uses the thing well or badly. So my question: what view of the relation between artifacts and values does this combination of practices presuppose? Is it flatly inconsistent, or is there a coherent implicit theory there -- maybe something like a weak embeddability thesis, where values can be built into a design as reliable but defeasible tendencies rather than as settled political facts? Also tell me which parts of the practice are strictly forced by that theory and which are underdetermined, and what regulation would actually have to look like if lawmakers fully adopted the stronger determinism you find in Winner's 1980 Daedalus essay -- would our current design mandates be recognizable under that assumption, or would they need a whole different shape? Answer in American English please, and don't be afraid to get into the weeds -- I'd rather have precision than a survey.
