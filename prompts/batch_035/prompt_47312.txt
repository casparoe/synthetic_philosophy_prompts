I help run a small grant program that can fund roughly six researcher-years over three years, and we've decided to focus on conceptual rather than empirical work: philosophy, formal theory, and evaluation design, not training runs. The area we're considering is eliciting latent knowledge from AI systems — the cluster of questions around whether a model "knows" something it doesn't report, whether a model that is misbehaving also knows it is misbehaving, what techniques for surfacing such knowledge are actually measuring, and when knowledge that exists somewhere in a system can be treated as accessible in practice. I've read the usual high-level discussions, but for allocation purposes I need something more disciplined than "this is an important problem," so I'm trying to work out what the actual open questions are and which of them this particular budget should buy.

To make this concrete, my colleagues and I have been arguing about whether three projects that get run together in this space are really separable. First, a measurement project: treating elicitation techniques as instruments and importing the psychometric way of thinking — reliability, convergent and discriminant validity, the problem of defining a construct when you have no behavioral ground truth for whether a probe has succeeded. Second, a metaphysical project: whether the knowledge-like states we're trying to elicit are best described as beliefs, dispositions, features of a simulated persona, or something the belief vocabulary distorts — and whether any answer here changes elicitation strategy or is just conceptual housekeeping. Third, a strategic project: the arms-race structure between training pressure, which can select for systems whose knowledge and reports come apart, and evaluation-time probing, which is supposed to detect the gap — whether this is fundamentally an iterated adversarial setup in which "elicitability" degrades by design rather than by accident. One colleague holds the measurement project is upstream of everything, since without a validity story we cannot distinguish genuine elicitation from theater; another says ontology is upstream because instrument design presupposes some account of the thing measured; a third thinks both are hygiene and the strategic question is where the stakes live.

What I want from you is a prioritized agenda for this budget. Rank the open questions — the three projects above, their subdivisions, or questions we're failing to list at all — and defend the ordering against explicit criteria: importance for safety-relevant decisions about real systems over the next decade, tractability for researchers who cannot run large experiments, how neglected each is relative to the empirical ML work already happening, and how many other questions in the cluster depend on each one. Tell me where you think my colleagues' "upstream" claims are right and where they smuggle in a false dependency — in particular, whether the measurement framing genuinely needs the metaphysics settled, or whether that's an illusion of dependency common to construct debates. I'd also like to know what would change your ranking: which plausible developments over the next two or three years, whether technical results, public evaluation failures, or imported ideas from other fields, would move a question sharply up or down. Finally, be willing to say which much-discussed questions in this space are overrated for a budget of this size and type, and why — I'm more interested in the reasoning behind the cuts than in the cuts themselves.
