I've been reading about van Fraassen's Reflection principle and I think I've either found a knockdown objection or badly misunderstood it, and honestly I can't tell which. The principle says your credence now in some claim should line up with your expectation of what your credence will be later, right? So here's my problem. Say I'm 60% confident it'll rain saturday. But I know that between now and then I'll check the forecast, and my credence will jump around as new info comes in — maybe up to 90%, maybe down to 20%. It seems like Reflection is telling me my current credence should already be the average of wherever I'll end up. But then... what's the point of checking the forecast at all? If my expected future credence is just my current credence, learning looks like a complete waste of time, like I already implicitly know everything I'm going to find out and the checking is theater. That feels wrong but I can't see where it goes wrong.

And theres a seperate thing bothering me. A friend of mine takes medication that he says makes him paranoid for a day — he'll beleive all sorts of crazy stuff while it's in his system. If Reflection says defer to your future self's opinions, is he obligated to adopt the paranoid opinions NOW, before he even takes the pill?? Surely not. But I gather the standard fix is that you only reflect toward future selves you consider rational, and that strikes me as circular — you need a prior handle on which future beliefs deserve deference, which was the whole question Reflection was supposed to answer.

Can someone untangle this? Which parts of my complaint are real objections that people take seriously, and which parts are me confusing something basic?
