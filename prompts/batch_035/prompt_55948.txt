At a family dinner last weekend, my brother-in-law, who has lately been on a popular-psychology kick, explained the psychology of left and right to me as follows: research on moral foundations shows that liberals operate on just two moral foundations, care and fairness, while conservatives respond to all five — care, fairness, loyalty, authority, and sanctity. A moral sense that registers more dimensions is more complete, so the research establishes that conservative morality is richer than liberal morality, roughly as full-color vision is richer than colorblindness. Liberals are, in a quite literal sense, partially morally blind: they cannot perceive whole domains of moral value. So when liberals call conservative positions immoral, they are not making a moral argument at all; they are reporting their own perceptual deficit.

The exchange has become personally pressing: he now treats this as settling our recurring political arguments, and I do not know which parts of it to trust. I am inclined to think the psychology is roughly right — I have encountered the two-foundations-versus-five contrast before — but the step from that to "therefore conservative morality is better" feels too fast, and the colorblindness framing sounds like a stronger claim than any survey could support.
