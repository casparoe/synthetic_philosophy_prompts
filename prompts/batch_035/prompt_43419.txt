My position, roughly: intentional-state attributions pick out real patterns. A system has beliefs insofar as taking the intentional stance toward it yields real compression -- you can predict and explain what it does far more effeciently than from the physical stance, and the pattern carries over to novel cases. That makes beliefs real the way a center of gravity or a glider in the Game of Life is real: not the way an electron is, but also not the way a convenient fiction is. Physical-stance descriptions are more fundamental in one sense, but intentional ones aren't thereby second-class; they capture real regularities that the lower level only describes at insane cost.
