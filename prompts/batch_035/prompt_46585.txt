I have no academic background — my brother and I argue about these things on long drives — so I apologize if my terms are imprecise. Here is the argument he left me with, and I cannot find my way out of it.

(1) A statement about someone's experience is meaningful only if there is some conceivable way, however indirect, of finding out whether it is true.
(2) For any dream, the only possible evidence is the report the dreamer gives after waking.
(3) Nothing that happens during sleep can serve as a check on the dream, because anything the sleeper does that would count as describing their experience — answering questions, giving an account, correcting themselves — would just prove they are awake. There is no state in which you are asleep and reporting on your sleep at the same time.
(4) A report produced after the fact, with no possible check, attests only to the fact that the dreamer is now inclined to tell, say, a falling story. It is evidence of a present disposition, not of anything that went on last night.
(5) So when I say I dreamt I was falling, I am not describing an occurrence during the night. My dream is, at most, the story I produced at breakfast.
(6) Therefore dreams are not experiences that happen while we sleep.
