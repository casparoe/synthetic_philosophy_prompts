I'm grading a philosophy-of-language final and would like a second opinion before I finalize the marks. The exam question read:

Grice distinguishes what a speaker says from what a speaker implicates. Explain what a conversational implicature is, including the role played by the Cooperative Principle and the maxims, and explain how cancellability is supposed to distinguish conversational implicatures from entailments and from conventional implicatures. Illustrate with examples.

Here is one student's answer:

Grice claims that whenever people speak they follow a Cooperative Principle — make your contribution one that is required — together with four maxims: Quantity (be as informative as possible), Quality (be truthful), Relation (be relevant), and Manner (be clear). Because the speaker follows these maxims, the hearer can work out what is implicated beyond what is literally said. For example, if I say "Some of the students passed," I implicate that not all of them did, since if I had believed that all passed, the maxim of Quantity would have required me to say so.

Implicatures are distinguished from entailments by cancellation. An entailment cannot be cancelled without contradiction: "Some of the students passed — in fact none did" contradicts itself. A conversational implicature can be cancelled without contradiction: "Some of the students passed — in fact all of them did" is perfectly consistent. Conventional implicatures behave the same way. In "She is poor but honest," the word "but" conventionally implicates a contrast between being poor and being honest, and that too is cancellable — one can go on to add "not that there is anything surprising about that combination." So the mark of an implicature, whether conventional or conversational, is cancellability; entailments alone resist cancellation.

The implicature from "some" to "not all" is a conventional implicature rather than a conversational one, because unlike a genuine conversational implicature it arises on every occasion of use and does not depend on any special features of the context.
