A friend of mine is deep into microhistory — the whole "history from below" thing, reconstructing ordinary lives out of parish records, court depositions, wage disputes, that kind of stuff. At a party a guy kept shutting her down with 'history is written by the victors' — as if everything she dug out of an archive was somehow pre-filtered by whoever ended up on top, so the whole enterprise was naive. I had the feeling the slogan was doing like four different jobs at once and nobody was pinning down which one. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that sentence could be making, from boringly true to genuinely radical? I'm thinking it could mean anything like: victors just produce more of the surviving accounts; they actively destroy or supress the losers' records; our basic categories and periodizations inherit the victors' framing (what counts as "the" event, where the story starts); all the way to a relativist reading where there's no fact of the matter about the past at all independent of who won. For each reading, say whether it's plausible, whether it's actually true historically (plenty of victors' own records survive precisely because they were bureaucracies that lost control of their archives), and crucially whether it would support using the slogan to dismiss what she does. I suspect the history-from-below tradition is partly a response to exactly this worry, but maybe I'm missing something. Lay it out as a list I can bring to the next party.
