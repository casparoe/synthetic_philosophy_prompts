So my department has a little reading group on philosophy of art that I've gotten dragged into, and after falling down the van Meegeren rabbit hole (the forger who conned the nazis with a fake Vermeer and then had to paint another one in custody to avoid a treason charge, you probably know it) I've been asked to write up "where I stand" on fakes and originals so the others can poke holes in it. Before I do, I want a consistency check, because I suspect there's something rotten in here but I genuinely can't tell which part. The package is roughly:

(1) I'm a pretty thorough formalist about painting — aesthetic value supervenes on what you can perceptually get from the object, so two visually indistinguishable canvases can't differ aesthetically, full stop. (2) The buyer of a perfect forgery is a victim of fraud but NOT of aesthetic fraud — they were lied to about provenance and market facts, but the experience they paid for is the one they got. (3) A museum putting a perfect facsimile on the wall while the original sits in a vault loses nothing that matters, and I'd honestly be fine with never seeing the "real" one. (4) BUT I also hold that expressive properties run through the physical performance of the mark — a stroke that looks free and was made in one confident gesture is expressive of spontaneity in a way that a stroke that looks free but was laboriously traced stroke-by-stroke off the original is not, even if they're visually identical. (5) I'm more moved standing in front of an original than a facsimile even when I couldn't tell them apart in a blind test, but I chalk that up to my beliefs doing the work, not the object. (6) I think forgeries made to deceive are a genuinely interesting art form of their own — a fake displayed and labeled AS a fake grips me in a way an honest minor pastiche by the same hand wouldn't — but I insist that's a non-aesthetic, historical kind of interest, like being into the story of the heist.

One guy in the group already told me that (2) sits badly with my conviction that forgery is a serious wrong and not just breach of contract, but I don't really see it — is he right? More broadly: go through the set, tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly, which only look like they are, and for each real tension say what would have to give and what that costs me. Rank them by severity rather than listing everything as equally damning — I want to know which single revision cleans up the most, and I honestly don't know which one I'd give up. Please don't lecture me on the literature, I just want the audit. (Also if there's some well-known name for the position I've stumbled into holding, mention it, but only if it actually fits.)
