Popper famously wobbled on Darwinism: at one point he grouped natural selection with Marxism and psychoanalysis as failing his falsifiability criterion, though he also insisted the theory was scientifically valuable and later partly softened the claim. Meanwhile, in his philosophy of social science he leans on the rationality principle — roughly the assumption that agents act appropriately to the situations they're in — which he openly admitted isn't testable, yet treated as indispensable for his situational analysis. Compare these two cases. Both are unfalsifiable frameworks that he allowed could guide testable science, so what exactly distinguishes his tolerant verdict on Darwinism and the rationality principle from his harsh verdict on Marxism and psychoanalysis? Is it just whether the programme keeps generating refutable output over time, and if so, hasn't he quietly swapped falsificationism for a pragmatic story about research productivity that his demarcation criterion was supposed to replace? And do you read his softer later view of natural selection, recast as a metaphysical research programme, as the endpoint his epistemology was pointing at all along — or as an unprincipled retreat?
