my brother in law and I keep circling the ship of theseus thing and I finally wrote out where I actually stand, but looking at it as a set it seems like a mess. here's the list:

(1) a ship that gets maintained plank by plank over decades of service is still the original ship.
(2) if someone collected every discarded plank and rebuilt the ship out of them, that second ship is a replica — the continuously maintained one is the original, period.
(3) but if a ship is wrecked in a storm and later rebuilt in a drydock from her salvaged original timbers, with new wood filling in whats missing, that can still be the same ship. a gap in being afloat doesn't erase identity.
(4) for artifacts I think identity is partly interest-relative — theres no deep metaphysical fact about which ship is "the original", it depends on our sortal and what we care about.
(5) persons are different: in a body-swap type case I'd say the psychological continuer is me, not the one with the body.
(6) and no branching: if the salvaged planks got split between two new ships, neither one is the original.

my gut says (2) and (3) can't both be right, but honestly they both seem right to me and I don't know which one to drop. I also can't tell if (4) is even consistent with how flat my verdicts are in (2) and (6), or whether (1)-(3) sitting alongside (5) is a double standard. go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, rank them by severity, and give the cheapest fix for each. then tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
