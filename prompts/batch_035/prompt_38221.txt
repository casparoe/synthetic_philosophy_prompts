My friend Leah has been sorting out her diet according to her views about animals, and her verdicts so far are these: she eats beef but refuses chicken, on the grounds that a single cow covers her meals for months while chicken means a bird dies for every dinner; she eats ordinary beef but not veal or foie gras, because what she's buying is how the animal lived, not just whether it died; she eats mussels but refuses shrimp and octopus, so smallness is no excuse, though some sentience line is clearly being drawn; she pays extra for grass-finished over grain-finished beef, citing everything that dies when feed grains are combine-harvested — she counts wild animal deaths from crop cultivation as real deaths on her ledger; and she eats eggs and dairy without fuss, since nothing dies for them directly.

Next month our food co-op starts stocking pasture-raised broiler chickens from a small local farm — birds that live genuinely good ten-week lives outdoors, fattened mostly on forage and food waste, handled and slaughtered carefully — alongside the grass-finished beef she already buys. Which does she pick for the cookout? I'm less interested in the answer itself than in the rule she seems to be running. At least two principles appear to fit her track record equally well: one that counts deaths per meal, direct and indirect, above a sentience threshold, and one that weighs total suffering, treating a quick death after a decent life as nearly costless. The trouble is that these seem to come apart on the new case: on the first rule the chicken loses badly, since one bird still dies per dinner while the cow's death amortizes across hundreds of meals, while on the second the pasture broiler's short good life and painless-enough death might make it the better buy, or at least permissible. Tell me which candidate rules explain all of her verdicts, what each one predicts for the chicken-versus-beef choice, your best guess about what she'll do and why, and what further case I could put to her — insect flour, wild-caught salmon, whatever you think works — that would most cleanly distinguish between the rules. And if her track record genuinely underdetermines the answer, say what principle she'd be committing herself to with either choice.
