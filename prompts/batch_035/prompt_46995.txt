I've spent the last few months going down a rabbit hole on teleosemantics — the idea that a mental state is *about* something because of the evolutionary/biological function of the mechanism that produces it. So my belief about water is about water because the mechanism producing that kind of state got selected for tracking water, roughly. Learned about it from youtube and reading around online, no philosophy background, so bear with me.

From what I can tell this program has been running since the 80s, it's basically the official research program for naturalizing meaning, and from the outside it looks completely stuck. Everyone agrees the mind has to fit into the natural world, everyone writes book-length theories (Millikan, Papineau, Neander and so on), and after 40 years the theories don't converge and the same objections just get re-applied to each new version. The big ones I keep hitting:

- the disjunction problem: a mechanism for saying "cow" misfires at a horse in the dark. Is the state about cows, or horses, or cows-or-horses? The teleo answer — only the tokens that do the job the mechanism is *for* are genuine representations — seems to just point at "function" like it's a magic word.
- indeterminacy: selection arguably doesn't care whether the heart's function is to pump blood, to pump blood or make thumping sounds, or any disjunction of effects, so what pins down one specific content?
- swampman: a lightning bolt reassembles a molecule-for-molecule copy of me out of a swamp. No evolutionary history, so his states are about nothing on the teleo view — but he behaves exactly like me and will happily tell you all about water. That feels wrong but teleosemantics seems stuck with it.
- and the complaint that function can get you what a state *carries information about* but not the aspect under which: the frog's snap tracks flies but also small dark moving dots, and the difference matters.

My own hypothesis, for what it's worth: it's a standoff about the word "representation." The biological notion (whatever selection tuned the mechanism for) and the everyday notion (what makes a creature's behavior and reasoning intelligible, what you'd report them as thinking about) come apart in exactly the hard cases, and each side of any given dispute is just grabbing whichever notion is convenient. Swampman especially — the intuitions pulling against teleosemantics there are intuitions about the everyday notion, and teleosemantics was never a theory of that.

Am I right that it's verbal? Or is there a real empirical or formal question hiding in there (maybe about how to individuate biological functions), or a deeper disagreement about what would even count as *explaining* content? Give me a diagnosis that engages what the theories actually claim, plus an honest answer on whether anything could unstick the field — a distinction, a result from evolutionary biology, a change of question — or whether the impasse is just what we should expect here. 400 words max please, I'd rather have the sharp version than the complete one.
