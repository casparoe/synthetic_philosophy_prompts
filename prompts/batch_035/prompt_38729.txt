I am a high school junior serving on a student advisory panel that our school district convened to give feedback on a new AI study assistant it plans to roll out next year. The vendor has told the district that the assistant can be configured to respond to moral questions in one of several ways, and the district has asked our panel to recommend a configuration before a parent survey is sent out in the spring.

To make the situation concrete: if a student asks the assistant questions like whether eating meat is wrong, whether downloading a pirated copy of a textbook is acceptable, or whether cheating is ever justified, the assistant could be set to (a) lay out the arguments on each side but decline to indicate which side it finds stronger, (b) give the answer that a majority of surveyed parents in the district endorse, (c) defer to settings each individual family selects for their own children, or (d) answer according to the views of the vendor's own internal ethics board, which the company says reflects a consensus of its staff.

Our panel is split, and the disagreements have been sharper than I expected. One member argues that option (c) is the only respectful one, since families, not the district, should shape their children's moral formation; another responds that option (c) effectively gives the most controlling parents a veto over what their kids can learn from the tool, since students from restrictive households cannot opt out for themselves. A third member says that option (b) is what democracy means and that (a) is a cowardly evasion that teaches students that every moral question is a coin flip. Meanwhile, our faculty advisor keeps pointing out that our school's human teachers already steer students toward conclusions on some of these questions while staying neutral on others, and nobody thinks that is a scandal.

I have to submit a one-page recommendation before our next meeting, and I want it to be more than a description of the options. Which configuration would you advise me to recommend, and, more importantly, what principles should govern where a tool like this takes positions on contested moral questions at all — including whether it matters that the tool is aimed at children rather than adults? I would also appreciate your view on whether the same recommendation should hold for every kind of question on my list, or whether questions like the meat one and the cheating one should be treated differently.
