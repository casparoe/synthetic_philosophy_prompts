I help run a little philosophy-and-film night at my apartment — we're maybe eight people, mostly grad students and a couple of curious neighbors, and the whole conceit is that we watch a film and then argue about it with one philosophy paper's worth of machinery. Next month it's Memento (the Nolan one, Leonard with the anterograde amnesia hunting his wife's killer), and I've been given the job of picking the lens. I've spent the last few months obsessing over the Sleeping Beauty problem, so my instinct is obvious to me but I want to pressure-test it before I commit: Leonard looks like a walking case study in self-locating belief. He can't form new memories, so every time he "wakes up" in a motel room or a parking lot he doesn't know *where in his own life he are* — which date it is, how many John G.s he's already killed, whether he's mid-plan or at the end of one. That's basically Beauty's situation without the coin: the relevant uncertainty isn't about the world but about which centered world he's currently in, and you'd think the halfer/thirder toolkit would have something to say about how he should apportion credence.

But here's where it gets interesting and where I'd love a careful reading. Beauty can't do anything between awakenings, but Leonard leaves himself evidence — the Polaroids, the tattoos, the notes (including the "don't believe his lies" one). That's like a Beauty who gets to write notes to her future self before each waking, which I gather people have actually discussed as a variant. And the film's whole tragedy is that this channel is corruptible: Teddy tells him he's already found and killed the real attacker, and Leonard chooses to burn the photo and write down Teddy's license plate, manufacturing false evidence so his future self will have a clear mission and a reason to exist. So is that self-deception at the level of credence (he's rigging his own evidence function), or something the centered-worlds framework can't even express? Natalie manipulating him mid-scene, and Leonard reassembling who to trust from a Polaroid scribbled five minutes ago, seem like live demonstrations of how self-locating uncertainty gets exploited.

What I want for the session is a reading that takes both sides seriously: read Memento through self-locating belief and the Sleeping Beauty debate — does the film dramatize what it's like to have amnesia-as-epistemic-position better than the thought experiments capture it, where does the analogy break down (his procedural memory and skills survive, unlike Beauty's reset-to-prior, and he accumulates physical traces even when memories fail), and is there something in the film — the ending's self-directed lie, say, or Sammy Jankis's wife and the insulin test — that adds to the halfers-vs-thirders fight that argument alone doesn't? Also please tell me honestly where the film resists my lens, since one of the film-night regulars is a contrarian and will come at me with "it's a movie about grief and self-deception, not probability theory" and I'd rather concede those points up front than get caught flat-footed.
