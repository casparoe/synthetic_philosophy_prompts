So, bit of backstory: I'm a backend engineer by trade, zero philosophy training, but a few months ago I got sucked into a wiki spiral that ended at Lakatos and his stuff about research programmes, and I've become genuinely convinced it's the best lens anyone's offered for understanding what's actually going on in deep learning. Next month I'm presenting this to our internal reading group and I need to be ready for the pile-on.

My thesis, roughly: deep learning is a textbook Lakatosian research programme. The hard core is something like "scale up models that learn from data and capabilities will emerge" — nobody inside the programme is allowed to question that. Around it sits the protective belt: architectures, attention variants, regularisation tricks, data augmentation, whatever. Through the 2010s the programme was clearly progressive in Lakatos's sense — it produced predictions nobody expected, like scaling laws and in-context learning, which is the "novel predictions" bar. But now I think we're watching the shift to degenerating in real time: pure scaling keeps failing to deliver reasoning, and each time the response is a new bolt-on — chain of thought, RLHF, tool use, retrieval — added after the fact to rescue the core, predicting nothing in advance. That's his signature of a programme that's generating patches instead of discoveries. The extra thing I want to argue, and this is the part I've not seen anyone say: our own benchmark habits are the clearest degenerating move of all. Every time a model fails an eval, the eval gets quietly retired and a new one with a scary name appears, so the programme can never lose. Lakatos would recognise that trick immediately.
