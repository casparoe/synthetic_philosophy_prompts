I write about science and economics ideas for a living, and somewhere along the way I got seduced by the Keynes picture of uncertainty — the line he develops in his Treatise on Probability and then drags into the General Theory, that a lot of the time there is no scientific basis on which to calculate any probability at all. Not "low confidence", not "wide error bars" — just no number, because the situation is genuinely unlike anything that came before and gives you nothing to count. On this view you weigh arguments qualitatively, you rely on convention and animal spirits when you must act anyway, and the whole risk-management industry is quietly performing a category error when it treats a genuinely uncertain future as if it were a lottery. I've more or less adopted this as my working frame when I write about financial crises and forecasting, so I'd like to know what would count against it, honestly ranked rather than strawmanned.

Some things I already know I should face up to, so tell me how much they hurt. Ramsey's old objection that Keynes's objective "degrees of rational belief" relations just aren't there to be perceived — that when people claim to just see that one argument supports a conclusion better than another, they're reporting psychology, not logic. The superforecasting literature, where people forced to put numbers on wildly uncertain geopolitical questions do noticeably better than chance and better than pundits, over years, out of sample. The Dutch book / betting argument that if you act at all, your actions reveal coherent numbers whether you admit to them or not. And on the other side, I worry my position has been insulated into unfalsifiability: whenever a Bayesian does well, I say "ah, that domain was measurable all along", and whenever they fail I say "genuine uncertainty, you see". Is that a principled restriction or just an escape hatch? Could anything — a formal result, a track record, a specific kind of empirical success or failure — actually force someone holding the Keynes view to give it up, and what would a thoughtful defender of it concede counts against them versus dismiss as beside the point?

Give me the full analysis in prose first, then afterwards a JSON object with this schema: a field "one_line_verdict" (string, your overall assessment), a field "defeaters" (array of objects, each with "name" string, "severity" one of "fatal", "serious", "awkward", "what_it_show" string, and "defender_response" string), a field "insulation_assessment" (string — has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that a problem for a view of this kind), and a field "concessions_expected" (array of strings — things a serious Keynesian would themselves grant count against the view). Rank the defeaters from most to least dangerous, definately don't pad the list with weak stuff to seem balanced.
