Okay so I want a designed policy case — a thought experiment, not a real event — where French-style laïcité ends up *defending* a religious accomodation, and Anglo-style multiculturalism ends up *blocking* one. The reverse of their usual positions: the secular republic makes the case for granting a religious exception, and the multiculturalist makes the case for refusing it.

Constraints:

- Realistic setting: a school, a clinic, a public pool, a library — somewhere ordinary, nothing far-fetched.
- The laïcité side's defense must not come from an obviously bad version of the principle, and not from them suddenly going soft on religion. It should fall out of something they genuinely hold — the state not taking sides between religious and non-religious ways of life, evenhandedness. You'll probably need a secular version of the same thing to already be allowed; that's your engine.
- Same deal for the multiculturalist: their resistance must flow from commitments they actually have — like who inside the group gets to decide, and who bears the cost — not from them suddenly turning assimilationist.
- Don't use the worn cases: no headscarves, no burkinis, no halal menus. Pick something alot less travelled.
- The practice itself should be facially benign, so the resistance can't just be "this is harmful" — it has to be about a pattern, not the act.

This is for testing whether a model can run these two frameworks in unfamiliar configurations instead of reciting the usual culture-war lines. So don't stipulate the verdicts by making one side dumb — the verdicts have to be generated by each side's own principles, and you have to show that working.

Format: exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two (run-ons are fine for the scenario), no intro or conclusion. 1) The scenario itself. 2) The laïcité verdict, plus the reasoning that gets there. 3) The multiculturalist verdict, plus its reasoning. 4) The single feature of the case that's doing the work, and what happens to each verdict if you delete it. 5) The strongest pushback a defender of each view would give, and whether it's a principled resistance or just a bullet-bite.
