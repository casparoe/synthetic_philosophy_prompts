quick background: i'm a philosophy of mind PhD student who hangs out with the comparative cognition crowd, and one of my labmates (let's call her Dana) is completely obsessed with Dennett's ladder of mind-kinds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian creatures. you know the scheme: hardwired creatures shaped only by selection over generations, creatures that learn by individual trial and error (reward and punishment), creatures that can test behavioral hypotheses internally before acting on them instead of flailing around, and creatures that borrow actual tools of thought — language, maps, other people's hints — to upgrade their own anticipation machinery. Dana files every animal she hears about onto this ladder. at departmental mixers. it's insufferable but she is weirdly consistent about it, so I've been keeping notes, and honestly her rulings are a cleaner dataset than half the stuff our lab publishes. Her verdicts so far:

- E. coli doing chemotaxis: Darwinian, obviously
- a rat learning a lever press on a reinforcement schedule: Skinnerian
- an octopus that needed a dozen attempts to learn to unscrew a jar, pure trial and error: Skinnerian
- a chimp from one of the classic insight setups, who sits there apparently doing nothing and then suddenly stacks boxes to reach a banana, no flailing: Popperian — she says it pre-screens moves in its head before committing
- a scrub jay that re-caches its food after being watched by another jay, but only if it has itself stolen from caches before: Popperian, and not one rung higher, which the bird people hate
- a honeybee colony: Darwinian, full stop. her reasoning is that nothing in the colony learns anything within a lifetime, the foraging decisions are all selection-tuned instinct, and being a superorganism doesn't move you up the ladder
- her own nephew, about 18 months old, who points at a picture book and says something approximating "more": Gregorian, instant

so next week a visiting speaker is coming through with video footage, and Dana has already promised on-the-spot rulings like some kind of taxonomy psychic. a few of us want to game out her answers in advance. the new cases on the docket:

1. a border collie that knows proper nouns for something like a thousand objects, and when told to fetch "the new one," brings the item it has never seen before, by exclusion
2. a corvid that, when it isn't even hungry in the evening, caches food in a spot where it has never been fed, apparently because it will be hungry there tomorrow morning
3. a chimp from an old language study that uses a lexigram keyboard to request tools that aren't currently visible
4. our lab's own chatbot, because someone will absolutely ask

predict her verdicts, but the interesting part is the reasoning: spell out the rules that fit her past rulings. i can see at least two — a strict version where Gregorian requires symbols that actually restructure the creature's thinking (not just very fancy fetch behavior), versus a looser version where any learned symbol use gets you the top rung — and I suspect there's also a "foresight" rule lurking there, where what earns the Popperian rung is acting on anticipation rather than on feedback, which might slice things differently again. say what each rule predicts for the four new cases, where the rules diverge, give me your best guess per case, and then tell me what extra case we should feed Dana to figure out which rule she's actually running — ideally one where her instinctive answer won't embarrass her in front of the speaker. she's going to be smug about this regardless.
