I'm a PhD student in epistemology finishing up a prospectus chapter on assertion, and my advisor keeps telling me the literature talks past itself because people self-identify with a norm label ("I'm a knowledge theorist") without actually getting sorted by cases. So for a talk I'm giving to our department's work-in-progress group, I want to make one of those slightly obnoxious "which norm of assertion do you *actually* hold?" quizzes as an icebreaker before the Q&A, and I want it to sort people by their verdicts on cases, not by which label they like.

Please write me the quiz: around 8-10 questions, each a concrete case with 2-3 answer options, plus a scoring key at the end mapping answer patterns onto positions. The positions that need to appear as possible outcomes are the knowledge account, the truth account, the reasonable or justified belief account, and the belief/sincerity account (assert what you believe, never mind whether it's true or warranted). The cases should be ones where the positions genuinely come apart: stuff like lottery propositions (very probably true, no knowledge), Gettiered assertions where the speaker has justified true belief but not knowledge, "selfless" assertions where someone sincerely asserts something they don't believe because the evidence in front of them points that way, chaste/therapeutic assertions (a doctor telling a scared patient something they don't believe), and retraction behavior — do you owe retraction when your assertion turns out false but you were perfectly justified, and does that show the norm was truth rather than justification? One subtlety I care about: distinguish between the norm for *properly* asserting and the norm for being *blameworthy* — some people think you can assert improperly without being blameworthy (the nervous swimmer who truthfully yells "there's a shark" without knowing it), so at least one question should probe whether someone separates those.

Also please flag honestly where the quiz can't discriminate: e.g., the knowledge account and the justified-belief account agree on almost everything except Gettier-style cases, so if someone's answers there are inconsistent the quiz should just say "unclassified, you're in Lackey territory" or whatever rather than force a verdict. And I'd like one question where the answer options are designed so that nobody who thought about it for more than a second would pick one, just to see if my colleagues are paying attention. Keep the whole thing answerable by someone who hasn't read the literature — no question should say "as a knowledge theorist, do you agree that..." — and don't worry about citations, I'll do the references myself. My talk is in two weeks and I want to test-drive it on my flatmate first, who's a linguist and will be annoyed if the cases are too philosophy-insider-y, so keep them vivid.
