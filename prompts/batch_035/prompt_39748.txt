I'm a novelist, and my current book is built as a pair of interleaved lives — a lepidopterist in contemporary Vancouver and, alternating with her chapters, a second person whose life is hers as she half-remembers it from dreams, shifted and misfiled. I've been structuring the whole thing as an argument with the Zhuangzi butterfly passage: not the pop-culture "what if we're all dreaming" version, but the actual text's quieter, more unsettling move, where Zhuang Zhou wakes and doesn't merely wonder which state he's in but notes that in the dream there was no Zhou at all to do the wondering — the dreamer doesn't dream that he's a dreaming man who is a butterfly; he's just a butterfly, contented and complete. Waking, by symmetry, gives him no marker either, since waking life feels exactly as self-evident from the inside as the dream did.

I'd like a poem to serve as the book's interlude, printed between the two halves, and I want the form to carry the philosophy rather than the diction. Specifically: a chain poem in which the last line of each stanza returns, slightly altered, as the first line of the next, and the final line of the poem is the opening line's closest variant — so the poem is a loop with no privileged entry point, and a reader who opened to any page of it couldn't say which stanza is the "waking" one. Within that constraint, I want the stanzas to trace the actual phenomenology in the passage: the dream's self-sufficiency, the forgetting, the waking that arrives not as a revelation but as just another state asserting itself, and the final indistinguishability that isn't distress but something nearer to amusement.

Two cautions. Please don't lean on the stock sci-fi vocabulary of simulation and glitch; the text's worry is older and stranger than that. And in the closing movement I'd like you to gesture at Zhuangzi's suggestion that the whole thing may be less a skeptical problem than a "transformation of things" — the possibility that there is no fact of the matter to recover, that asking which state is real is the mistake — without resolving it, since my novel refuses to resolve it either. Someone who knows the passage should recognize every beat; someone who doesn't should still come away with a good, strange poem. Roughly 30 to 40 lines, free meter, no rhyme required.
