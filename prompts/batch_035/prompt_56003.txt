Two philosophers of punishment — one a retributivist, who holds that the deserved response to culpable wrongdoing is the fundamental justification of punishment; the other a consequentialist, who holds that punishment is justified only by its effects (deterrence, incapacitation, the anchoring of social norms) and that inflicted suffering without such effects is cruelty — have argued past each other for a decade. I have been asked to facilitate their adversarial collaboration, and I need the joint statement they would produce and both sign.

Draft that document. It must contain:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims — substantive, not platitudes — covering at least: what distinguishes punishment from mere harm (intentional infliction of a hardship, for an offense, by an authority claiming the right to inflict it); what any adequate theory of punishment must deliver (some proportionality constraint, an account of why the state rather than the victim punishes, an explanation of why deliberately punishing the known-innocent is worse than letting the guilty go unpunished); and their joint verdicts, insofar as they have any, on the three stipulated cases below.

2. A disagreements table: for each point on which they still differ, one formulation the retributivist would accept as a fair statement of the retributivist view and one the consequentialist would accept for its own. Nothing slanted toward either side.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one party. I have guesses — whether the connection between guilt and deserved suffering is constitutive or merely instrumental; whether proportionality is fixed by the seriousness of the offense alone or may properly track detection rates and behavioral responses; whether punishment can be justified in part by what it does for the person punished — but I want you to make the call about which of these are genuine cruxes and which are artifacts of wording. For each crux you keep, give a concrete case or argument both parties would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction about what it shows recorded in advance.

Use as shared material the following three stipulated cases (invented for this collaboration; no real cases, statutes, or named authors should be invoked):

- The lottery scheme: to economize on prisons, a jurisdiction convicts everyone but sentences only a randomly selected tenth of offenders, at ten times the severity; expected punishment is unchanged.
- The hermit: an offender who, after his crime, moves permanently to a remote valley with no further contact with society; incapacitation and specific deterrence are moot, and the probability his punishment deters anyone else is stipulated to be negligible.
- The sunset clause: an act has been legislated decriminalized as of a fixed date six months out, and someone commits it five months before that date; by the time his sentence ends, the law itself no longer condemns what he did.

Finally, state which parts of their residual disagreement are verbal — for instance, whether the two sides could agree on the right outcome in every case while disputing only the word "justification" — and take a definite position on that question, as on every drafting decision above: where a call is close, make it and give your reason, rather than laying out the options and leaving the choice to me. Everything needed is in this prompt; do not cite or attribute anything.
