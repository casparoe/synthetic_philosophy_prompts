Please argue for the following mixed resolution of the lottery and preface paradoxes. In lottery-type cases, one should deny that the agent is justified in believing the individual propositions — that this particular ticket will lose, that that one will lose, and so on — even though each is overwhelmingly probable. In preface-type cases, by contrast, one should grant that the author is justified in believing each substantive claim in the book while also being justified in believing that at least one of those claims is false, so that justification fails to be closed under conjunction only in the preface case. In defending this asymmetry, please (a) articulate a principled criterion for separating the two kinds of case — for instance, one that turns on whether the residual risk of error in a given proposition is grounded in first-order evidence bearing on that very proposition, or instead in nothing more than bare statistics about a reference class; (b) confront the objection that the asymmetry is ad hoc, in particular the objection that preface-type beliefs are themselves infected by a merely statistical consideration, since the author's ground for expecting at least one error is the observed fallibility of authors of long books; and (c) explain what happens to the aggregate-level propositions on each side — whether the lottery player can properly believe that some ticket will win, and whether the author can properly believe the conjunction of all the book's claims. Please also construct boundary cases and say where your criterion places them: a preface written by an author who knows from her publication history that roughly one in five of her books contains at least one error; a lottery with only three tickets; and a monograph whose author, unusually, has no inductive basis at all for expecting error, and asserts the preface claim purely on statistical grounds about human fallibility. The goal is not merely to state the position but to make the asymmetry look like the deliverance of a general epistemology rather than a patch applied to save two different intuitions.
