I am a novelist, currently planning a book, and I would like your help in determining whether a philosophical intuition I keep running into has a name.

The premise of the novel: astronomers announce, with certainty, that an asteroid will extinguish all human life — but only fifty years from now, by which point everyone currently alive would in any case have died of natural causes. My protagonist, a composer in her sixties, receives this news, and I find that I cannot write her grief as being about anyone's death. No one dies a day earlier than they would have; the number of deaths is exactly what it always was. And yet everything she does — finishing her opera, training her students, planting a grove — sours in her mouth, and I intend to write it that way. While drafting her monologues I noticed the same thing in myself: when I imagine my own death, I am quietly comforted by the thought that my books will be read, quarrels settled, cities repaired after me; when I imagine the world simply stopping, the value seems to stop with it.

So my question concerns this asymmetry. There is, I gather, a literature on why an individual's death is bad for her, and a contrary literature suggesting that never dying would be a curse rather than a prize. What I have been unable to locate is work on what future people — none of whom I will meet, most of whom will never read me — do for the value of my present activities. It looks embarrassingly close to simple egoism, the desire to be remembered; but it does not feel like egoism, because I also care that the cathedral be finished, though I would never live to see it even if humanity continued.

I would like you to take the role of a professional philosopher and to think carefully before answering: first canvass the nearest candidate concepts and say why each fits or fails, and only then settle on an answer. Tell me whether this configuration has an established name, what that line of thought actually claims, and how it differs from its neighbours — the badness of death for the one who dies, the fear of oblivion, and the general idea that meaning depends on continuation. If nothing established fits exactly, say so plainly, and propose a term of your own, with a definition and at least one edge case. I am not looking for a reading list, and I am not looking for a bare label; I want to understand what the phenomenon is, and where its edges are. Please keep the entire answer to 450 words or fewer.
