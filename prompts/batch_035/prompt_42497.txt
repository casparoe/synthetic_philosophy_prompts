For a reading group on bounded rationality I've drafted a statement of my own position, and before circulating it I'd like a consistency check. My commitments are these: (1) as a matter of fact, human agents satisfice — they search until an option clears an aspiration level and then stop, and this is the right descriptive model of actual deliberation; (2) the mechanism by which aspiration levels rise after easy successes and fall after failures is itself part of rational behavior, not a deviation from it; (3) satisficing does not collapse into constrained optimization — the satisficing stopping rule is not the rule an optimizer with search costs folded into the objective function would run, and absorbing satisficing into optimization misses what Simon was pointing at; (4) the optimizing model of the agent nonetheless remains a legitimate and useful idealization in the social sciences, and I would not want economics to abandon it; (5) normatively, "good enough" is the right standard for bounded agents like us — demanding full comparison of all options is a mistake about what rationality can ask of creatures with our limits; (6) for the biggest one-off decisions of a life — career, partner, where to live — one should resist settling for the first acceptable option and survey a wide field before committing. Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look to be, and what the cheapest revision would be in each case. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and for each genuine one say exactly what would have to give and what giving it up costs me elsewhere in the set. I'm more interested in the audit than in a lecture on any single commitment.
