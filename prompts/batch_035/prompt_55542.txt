For the final session of my philosophy of mind seminar, each of us has to bring in something that makes multiple realizability feel less like a slogan and more like something one can see working. I would like to bring a pantoum. In a pantoum, the second and fourth lines of each stanza return verbatim as the first and third lines of the next, so every line recurs without ever occupying the same setting — which seems to me exactly the situation of a functional state, constant in its relations while its realization changes underneath it. Write that pantoum. The recurring lines should carry the role — pain as whatever sits in the causal nexus between tissue damage, withdrawal, and future avoidance — while the changing material around them should be the realizers: neuronal firing in a mammal, an octopus guarding an injured arm, a silicon system running through the same transitions. The poem should close on the line it opened with, but only after that line has meant something slightly different in each stanza, and it should leave the reader genuinely unsure whether the recurring line is one line appearing many times or many lines that merely share their words — because that unresolved question is precisely what separates the functionalist from the type-identity theorist. Please make it philosophically exact enough that my professor would find the view correctly rendered rather than name-dropped, and good enough as a poem that the form feels like an argument rather than a stunt.
