I just moved from Lisbon to Munich and it's making me question everything I believe about rules. Five years in Portugal and I basically internalized that rules are suggestions -- jaywalking, splitting restaurant bills however, friends letting you park wherever, nobody blinking. Three months in Munich and I've already had a neighbor knock on my door because I ran the washing machine at 10pm on a Sunday (quiet hours, obviously). And honestly my gut reactions are all over the place, so help me figure out what I actually think. Here's where I land on cases:

- Yelling at a tourist for jaywalking on a completely empty street: ridiculous, the rule has no point there.
- Neighbors enforcing Sunday quiet hours: I've come around, I actually like that everyone can count on the silence.
- A friend underreporting income on a small freelance gig: not fine, even though basically everyone there does it.
- A nurse letting a dying patient's family stay way past visiting hours: fine, even good.
- My German colleague reporting a coworker to HR for expensing a personal taxi: honestly kinda admire it, though my Lisbon self wants to call her a snake.
- Zero-tolerance drug policies in schools: awful, mindless.
- The strict exam proctoring culture at the university here, where even looking away from your desk is noted: I hate it, but I also can't deny it seems to keep the degrees meaning something.

Is there some principle about how much rule-following a society should demand that gets all of these? Because it feels like I believe both "loose cultures are more humane" and "tight cultures actually work," depending on which case you put in front of me. Give me the principle or the best couple of candidates, what each implies for cases I haven't mentioned (e.g., should Munich relax or should Lisbon tighten, and what about a society that's tight about corruption but loose about everything social), and tell me straight which of my verdicts has to go.
