I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, but I've fallen down a rabbit hole on how medicine decides what counts as a disease, and I'd like a satirical piece that plays in that space. Here's my understanding, so we're on the same page: the naturalist camp holds that disease is a purely factual matter — a part of the organism functioning below the level standard for the species, the way a function that silently returns wrong values is just a bug whether or not anyone has noticed; the normativist camp holds that disvalue is constitutive of disease, so that nothing is a disease unless it is unwanted or harmful to someone; and there are hybrid views, one well-known compromise requiring both a failure of a naturally selected function and harm in the person's actual circumstances.

With that as background, write an Onion-style news article — headline plus 400–500 words — reporting that a large health insurer has announced it will no longer cover conditions whose policyholders report being unbothered by, with the actuarial department citing the normativist definition of disease as settled science. The humor should come from getting the philosophy right rather than from caricature: e.g. the insurer's denials applying the disvalue criterion with the same rigor they apply to formularies; a quoted complaint from a naturalist (in the biostatistical-theory mold) that the company is being selective, since on its own logic it should also start covering ordinary unhappiness whenever enough policyholders disvalue it; a spokesperson sidestepping the observation that the same reasoning that lets them deny coverage also obliges them to pay for treatments patients want for conditions their doctors consider trivial; and an interview with a customer whose claim was denied on the grounds that she had, in the adjuster's words, come to terms with her condition, while a second customer's claim was approved only after she submitted an affidavit asserting she was genuinely miserable. If it fits naturally, work in one real-world style of example that cuts against simple naturalism — conditions whose disease status has drifted over time as social attitudes changed, or the phenomenon sometimes called disease-mongering, where ordinary states get recategorized as disorders to sell treatments — so the satire lands on both camps, not just the insurer.
