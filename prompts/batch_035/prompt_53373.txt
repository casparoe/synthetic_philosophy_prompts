longtime lurker here (OB then LW since like 2010, plus way too many arguments in comment sections about history), and I've finally tried to write down what I actually believe about the whole "lessons of history" genre — Munich analogies, Vietnam, Thucydides trap, that stuff. I'd like an audit of whether my views hang together:

1. Historical analogies are mostly motivated reasoning with extra steps. Every foreign policy debate gets a Munich or a 1914 SerbIA retro-fitted onto it, and people pick the analogy after they've already decided the conclusion.
2. That said, I'm fairly confident we're in a genuine Thucydides Trap situation with US/China, and that the Allison-style reference class (rising power vs. established power, the dozen-ish cases he counts) does real predictive work, it's not just branding.
3. I'm a base rates guy in general: reference-class forecasting beats narrative reasoning, Tetlock showed this, and I try to apply it everywhere.
4. I think nuclear weapons are an actual ontological break. "This time is different" is usually the most expensive phrase in finance, but here it's true, so pre-1945 great-power war data is close to useless for thinking about a US-China war.
5. I think the Vietnam lesson — don't launch interventions without a clear national stake, occuppation/counterinsurgency has a terrible track record — is one of the few historical lessons that was actually learned, and I apply it pretty mechanically whenever an intervention debate comes up.
6. I also believe "generals always fight the last war": the lesson everyone extracts from each conflict is systematically the wrong one for the next conflict, so lessons-of-history reasoning keeps failing in a predictable direction.

Rank the tensions by severity instead of flagging everything equally. Tell me which pair is actually load-bearing for the rest, which tensions dissolve on inspection, and what the cheapest revision would be. I honestly don't know where the rot is — my vague suspicion is #3 vs #4, or maybe #1 vs #2 since I give the Thucydides thing a pass, but I can't tell whether reference classes can just have a boundary past which they stop applying or whether that's special pleading I'd never accept from anyone else.
