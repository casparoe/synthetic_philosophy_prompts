I write on science and ideas for a national weekly, and I am preparing a long-form interview with a researcher who has spent the last decade developing what the field calls impact measures: penalty terms added to an agent's objective function, intended to keep it from disturbing the world beyond what its task requires, without anyone first having to specify everything in the world that we care about. Her central claim, as she states it in her talks, is that low impact can be defined in structural terms — in terms of what the agent could otherwise have achieved, or how far the consequences of its actions reach — rather than as a list of protected values. I find the program intriguing but am not yet convinced, and my readership mixes AI researchers with a general audience, so each question must be intelligible in plain language while still pressing on the technical substance.

Please draft nine or ten interview questions, each with a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. At least one question should press on offsetting — the worry that a penalty on impact gives the agent no reason not to cancel good it would itself have done, as in the parable of the office assistant that keeps the office immaculate by quietly preventing anyone from ever working in it. At least one should press on the choice of baseline: impact is measured relative to what would have happened otherwise, and it is far from obvious whose "otherwise" is meant — the agent held idle from the beginning, or the agent paused mid-task — and I would like her to say what stops the choice from being gerrymandered case by case. A third should press on the charge I find hardest to dismiss: that any definition of "impact" smuggles in a judgment about which differences in the world matter, so the advertised value-neutrality is a bookkeeping convention rather than a discovery. For that one, include the follow-up for the likely reply that there is a principled distinction between a small perturbation and a good outcome — I want to know, in her own terms, what that distinction is made of if not values. Close with one question about what result, within her own research program, she would regard as evidence that the whole approach is a dead end.
