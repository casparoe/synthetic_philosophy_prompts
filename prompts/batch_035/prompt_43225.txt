My college roommate and I have a standing tradition of arguing about foreign policy over dinner, and after fifteen years her verdicts on humanitarian interventions have gotten weirdly consistent. She still defends the Kosovo bombing in 1999 even though it never got Security Council authorization — her line is that stopping the expulsions of Kosovar Albanians mattered more than the paperwork. She was against the Iraq invasion from day one, and not just because of the WMD intelligence; she thought the whole project was rotten through and through. She backed the Libya intervention when Resolution 1973 passed in 2011, though she gets visibly irritated if you point out that it ended with Gaddafi dead in a ditch, which was not exactly the mandate anyone voted on. She calls Rwanda the great moral failure of our lifetime and insists she'd have supported even an unilateral intervention there at the time. And she's always had a soft spot for the small British deployment in Sierra Leone in 2000, the one where a relative handful of troops shored up the elected government against the rebels and it actually seemed to work.

The one case I've never gotten a straight answer out of her on is Darfur. Whenever it came up she'd change the subject, and it's been bugging me for years. So here's what I want from you: if I put her on the spot about whether some military intervention in Darfur would have been justified — a no-fly zone, or troops protecting the displaced on the ground, whatever — what would she say? I care less about the answer itself than the reasoning behind it. What rule is she implicitly running that fits her whole track record — Kosovo without authorization yes, Iraq without authorization no, Libya with authorization yes, Sierra Leone with actual ground troops yes — what other rules would fit that same record just as good, and which of those rules actually come apart on Darfur? My sense is that they disagree there, because an intervention in Darfur would have had a clean humanitarian motive but no realistic shot at Security Council authorization given Chinese and Russian resistance, and no obvious way to make it work with air power alone in the middle of that terrain. And once you've narrowed the candidate rules down, tell me what hypothetical case I should pose to her at dinner to force her to reveal which one she's really running — I want to finally win one of these arguments.
