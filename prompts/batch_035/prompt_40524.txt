Hi! I'm writing a short philosophy piece on Jeffrey conditioning for a workshop that's mostly formal epistemologists, and I'd like comments on my notes before I commit to a full draft. Working title: "Frozen conditionals: what rigidity costs you".

The idea: Jeffrey conditioning lets you update on uncertain evidence by redistributing credence over a partition E1...En while keeping each conditional P(H|Ei) fixed — that's the rigidity condition. Commentators treat rigidity as a technical assumption you check like a hygiene requirement, but I want to argue it carries the whole epistemological content of the rule: it says your views about how you'd react to possible observations are exempt from the very learning that changes your views about everything else.

Consequences I want to draw out:

1. Rigid updates don't commute — two pieces of uncertain evidence, applied in different orders, can give different posteriors. This is usually presented as an embarrassment, with the fix being to massage your partition until the updates mesh. I want to argue it's actually a feature: it's how the formalism records that learning is historically extended, that the second observation is made by an agent already altered by the first.
2. So the real normative question isn't when Jeffrey conditioning is valid but when you're entitled to hold your conditionals fixed — and the honest answer is basically never in the long run, only locally. Rigidity is a short-range idealisation, like treating a small patch of curved spacetime as Euclidean.
3. Tie this to evidence that bears on your own reliability: once the evidence makes you doubt the conditionals themselves, you're outside Jeffrey's framework entirely, and I suspect that's where most real scientific learning actually lives.

What I'd like from you: (a) an accuracy check — have I got the noncommutativity point right, and is my framing of the partition-massaging fix fair; (b) is the spacetime analogy doing real work or is it just decoration; (c) does point 3 overreach? And be blunt about whether this is just the standard dogmatism-of-conditioning worry (the old-evidence adjacent stuff) warmed over, or whether the uncertain-evidence angle genuinely adds something new. Thanx!
