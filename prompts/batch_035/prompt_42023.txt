ok so here's my situation: my roommate is a panpsychist and keeps telling me the combination problem is "basically solved, people just haven't read the right papers," while my philosophy of mind prof apparently thinks it's the single worst problem in the entire literature. I'm writing a term paper on it and I don't want to just rehash the standard intro paragraph where somebody says micro-experiences bond into a big one and then everyone gasps. What I actually want to settle first is what would even COUNT as solving it. So: write me the desiderata -- the full spec of what an adequate account of phenomenal combination has to deliver.

Here's how I want you to think about it. People throw around "the combination problem" like it's one thing, but as far as I can tell there are at least these strands: subject-summing (how do many micro-subjects yield one macro-subject rather than just a pile of subjects sitting next to each other), the palette problem (how do simple qualities in the micro-constituents get you the richness of, like, a sunset experience if the simple ones don't contain the complex ones in miniature), and the boundary problem (what determines that the neurons in my head compose one subject and not, say, me-plus-the-chair, or seven overlapping subjects in my skull). Plus there's what happens to the original micro-subjects -- do they get absorbed, annihilated, or do they keep having their tiny experiences inside me? Maybe you'll find more strands, maybe some of these collapse into each other -- that's part of what I want your judgement on.

For each requirement, tell me: what case or consideration motivates it, whether it's non-negotiable or just something one camp cares about (I suspect the "no new fundamental laws" requirement that physicalist-leaning panpsychists impose is doing a lot of sneaky work, and maybe constitutive panpsychists don't need it), and crucially whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable -- if it is, that's basically my thesis, so be honest and tell me if the whole list can't be met at once.

And one more thing, because my roommate ALSO likes to argue that if combination works for neurons it should work for groups, so a corporation or a nation or the two of us in this apartment might literally be a conscious subject above and beyond us. That seems like it should be a test case for any solution: if your account of combination is so permissive that it makes Google conscious, is that a reductio, or just an honest consequence? Include in the desiderata whatever it takes to handle that -- or argue that a solution shouldn't have to handle it at all.

Please answer in American English, and don't give me a reading list -- I have the reading, I need the specification. A table at the end summarizing the requirements and which are non-negotiable would be great.
