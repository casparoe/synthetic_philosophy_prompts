"Explain the role that the thesis of multiple realizability plays in the case for functionalism and against the mind-brain identity theory. Then discuss one objection that challenges either the truth or the evidential significance of the multiple realizability thesis, and assess how damaging it is."

The student's answer, reproduced in full:

"Multiple realizability is the claim that one and the same mental state can be realized by very different physical states. Putnam used it against the mind-brain identity theory: if pain is identical to a brain state, the theory needs to say which brain state, but pain appears in mammals, octopuses, and possibly silicon-based systems, so no single neural state can be the one that pain is identical to. The identity theory therefore fails, and functionalism explains why: a mental state is defined by its causal role, and any number of physical states can play that role. Since functionalists accept that each particular token of pain is identical to some physical state or other, multiple realizability tells against every form of the identity theory, both the claim that pain as a kind is identical to a neural kind and the claim that my pain right now is identical to a particular neural state of mine.

Bechtel and Mundale object to multiple realizability by appeal to actual neuroscientific practice. When neuroscientists study vision in the macaque and apply the results to humans, they rely on the assumption that the relevant brain structures are conserved across species; brain atlases map visual areas across mammals precisely on the assumption that they are homologous. This shows that multiple realizability is false: if the same brain areas realize the same psychological capacities in different species, then psychological states are not in fact realized by wildly different neural states, and the original argument never gets started.

Finally, Kim's causal exclusion argument shows that the autonomy of psychology is safe. If a neural state realizes pain, one might worry that the neural state, not pain, does the causal work. But because a multiply realized property like pain is distinct from each of its realizers, it contributes causation over and above them, so functionalist psychology keeps its own causal-explanatory domain and reduction to neuroscience is unnecessary."
