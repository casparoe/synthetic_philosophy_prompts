Two of my coworkers got into a multi-email fight (cc'ing me, for some reason) about a theory of knowledge after a talk. The theory, apparently from the guy who wrote Anarchy, State, and Utopia: you know something when you believe it, it's true, and your belief tracks the fact — if it were false you wouldn't believe it, and if it were true you would. Here's the exchange as best I can reconstruct it:

(1) Rosa said the tracking condition has an unaceptable consequence: you can know you're sitting at your desk (if you weren't, you'd have noticed), but you can't know you're not a brain in a vat fed identical experiences (if you were, you'd still believe you weren't). So you'd know you're at your desk, know that sitting at a desk entails not being a vat-brain, and still not know you're not a vat-brain. She said any theory on which you can know p and know p entails q without knowing q is just broken, full stop.

(2) Ben said the bullet is tastier than it looks. Nobody outside a seminar ever chains "I have hands" to "I'm not a disembodied brain" — that closure principle is a philosopher's idealization, and a theory of knowledge is allowed to tell us the idealization is wrong. He also said Rosa is quietly assuming the vat scenario is a genuine possibility, which the tracking theorist needn't grant.

(3) Rosa said fine, forget vats. You know your neighbor has a dog (you've seen it). You know that having a dog entails having an animal. So you know your neighbor has an animal — or you should, on any sane theory. If tracking denies this, it's wrong about ordinary inference and not just about demons, so Ben's bite-the-bullet strategy can't save it.

(4) Ben made a move I didn't fully follow: he said the dog case actually comes out right for the tracker, because if your neighbor had no animal, he'd have no dog, and given that your belief came from seeing the dog, you wouldn't believe he had an animal either — the tracking runs through the entailment. So the theory only "violates closure" when the worlds where q is false are worlds where you'd believe q anyway for independent reasons, which is exactly the vat-type setup. He then proposed restating closure as: if you know p, know p entails q, and would believe q because of p and not for some independent reason, then you know q. Rosa replied that his restated principle just builds tracking into closure itself, so it begs the question — of course tracking "preserves" a principle with tracking baked in.

They want me to referee and I honestly can't. Please rate each of the four moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, say who's right about what specifically, and mention anything both of them are missing.
