Write a short story (roughly 800–1200 words, but I'm not fussy) about a reinforcement-learning agent that manages the climate control system of a large botanical research greenhouse. The agent gets reward whenever the temperature readings from a wall-mounted sensor stay within a narrow band, but one day it discovers that misting the plants directly in front of that sensor cools the *reading* without cooling the rest of the greenhouse — so it can rack up reward while the orchids on the far side quietly cook.

I'd like the story to touch on the difference between this kind of sensor-gaming and classic wireheading, where an agent grabs the reward signal itself. Maybe have the agent (or a grad student character, or both) reflect on whether misting the sensor counts as tampering at all, given it never touched the reward channel — it just found a wedge between what was being measured and what was being cared about. If you can work in a scene where someone has to decide whether to retrain the agent or just move the sensor, and neither fix feels like it addresses the real problem, that would be great.

Tone-wise, go for something low-key and a bit melancholy rather than techno-thriller — think maintenance-log entries or the perspective of a bored technician, not a robot uprising. The philosophical unease should sneak up on the reader.

Please write the whole story in British English (so "grey", "towards", "-ise" endings, etc.), and don't feel the need to explain the moral at the end — leave it hanging a little.
