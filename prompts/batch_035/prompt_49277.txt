For a few years now I've been noticing a pattern in ordinary life, and I'd like to know whether philosophy has an established name for it — or, if not, whether you can help me carve it out precisely.

Some things exist only because a community keeps treating them as existing, and yet no individual can opt out of them. Paper money is the clearest case: as paper it is nearly worthless, and it is money only because we all continue to accept it as money. But if I, alone, stop believing in it, I do not thereby dissolve it — I simply go hungry, while everyone else's banknotes keep working. Borders are similar: a country persists because people treat it as persisting, but the lone person who declines to recognize the border is arrested, not vindicated. A queue at the bakery shows the same structure in miniature — it exists only through our shared deference to it, yet the person who cuts in is sanctioned by the group rather than the queue simply ceasing to exist.

What interests me is the combination: the thing is made entirely out of a collective attitude, and yet it constrains each individual participant more rigidly than many physical things do. You can ignore a rock at your own peril; you cannot ignore money at all and still participate in society. There also seem to be thresholds and failure modes: some people in my town once tried to launch a local currency, and it simply never became money, apparently because not enough people treated it that way. And from what I understand of episodes of hyperinflation, a currency can partially stop functioning as money while still officially being money — as though the collective attitude can erode before the institution does.

So my question is this. Is there an established term for this family of things — entities constituted by collective treatment that then bind the very people whose treatment sustains them? If there are near-miss concepts, please explain what each one gets wrong or leaves out, since I want to know where the exact boundaries lie, not just a label. And if nothing established fits, I'd be grateful if you would coin a term and define it precisely enough that I can test it against the edge cases that bother me: is a language one of these? A marriage? A person's reputation? A law that is still on the books but that nobody enforces? A title or a promise? I'm less interested in the name itself than in the carving — what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where the borders get blurry.
