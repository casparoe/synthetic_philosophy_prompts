i'm a phd student in HPS writing a seminar paper on Popper's views on Darwinism, and I keep running into the same move from my supervisor and half the commentaries I've read, and I can't tell if it's deep or a trick.

Background in one paragraph: everyone knows Popper griped that the theory of natural selection is close to tautological — that "survival of the fittest" just means the survivors survive, so it risks failing his falsifiability criterion — and everyone also knows he more or less took it back later in life. The move I keep seeing is this: natural selection is itself falsificationism in action. Mutation is conjecture, the environment throws the crucial experiment at it, extinction is refutation. Lineages are little scientists; the biosphere is running a giant distributed test. Hence Darwinism can't be vacuous, because the very method Popper demanded of science is literally what evolution does — and this is supposed to both dissolve the tautology complaint and explain why Popper warmed up to the theory (there's a whole evolutionary-epistemology crowd that treats the growth of knowledge as a continuation of organic evolution, so on this reading Popper's epistemology and Darwinism were never really rivals).

Can you audit this analogy for me? I want the full correspondence table — what plays the role of the theory, the prediction, the crucial test, the refutation, the scientific community, even the journal — and then where the mapping breaks. My instinct is that the breaking points that actually matter are these: the environment isn't trying to refute anything, it just is what it is, so there's no severity in the Popperian sense; there's no advance prediction, since the "hypothesis" is already out in the world doing its work before anything tests it; and crucially there's no discussion — nothing in the system can step back and criticise the criterion of fitness itself, which seems to be the whole point for Popper when he contrasts the amoeba with Einstein. The amoeba's rejected conjecture is stuck to its body, so a failed trial kills the theorist; Einstein can let the theory die instead of him. If that contrast is real, the analogy seems to break exactly where it was supposed to do its work.

But I don't want a survey, I want a verdict. Decide: does the analogy, patched where it can be patched, still carry the conclusion it's being used for — that selection theory is methodologically respectable on Popperian grounds? Or is the respectability actually coming from somewhere else entirely (Popper's own line that natural selection is a metaphysical research programme that's heuristically fertile, not a falsifiable theory) with the evolution-is-Popperian picture as a decoy? If there's a better analogy available, one that carries the weight the original was supposed to, give me that instead of a hedge. And flag anywhere you think I'm misreading Popper — he wrote so much and revised so often that I honestly can't tell anymore, and my supervisor has a habit of quoting the late Popper at me as if the early one never existed. no "there are several ways to think about this" endings please, I have enough of those in my drafts already.
