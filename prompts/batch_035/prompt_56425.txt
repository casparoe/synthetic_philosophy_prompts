My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about why humans crave fatty food, and by the end we had stopped talking about food entirely. He kept insisting that evolution doesn't produce anything for nothing: if a trait is widespread across a species, it must have done something useful for our ancestors, and if we can't see the use, that just means we haven't found it yet. I thought that was too quick — surely some widespread traits are just side effects of other things selection built, or leftovers from earlier ancestors that nothing ever bothered to remove. He accused me of giving up on explanation altogether, of answering every "why" with a shrug. So I'd like to know: is there a principled way to decide, for a given trait, whether selection actually shaped it for a job or whether it's just along for the ride? And how demanding should the evidence be before we accept the "it must be good for something" story — is telling a plausible story enough, or does the story have to make predictions that could have come out otherwise?
