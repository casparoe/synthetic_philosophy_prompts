I'm a journalist on the ideas desk of a broadsheet, preparing a long feature on vagueness — the sorites paradox and its afterlife in ordinary institutions. I can navigate the philosophical basics myself, but the piece needs an argument rather than a tour, and I'd like your help stress-testing the angle I've chosen.

My working thesis is that a newsdesk is a sorites machine. A political programme is not austerity until the cumulative cuts make it so; a string of light edits to a source's words stops being quotation somewhere long before the last edit; an armed conflict becomes a war, a poll lead becomes too close to call, and none of these thresholds is written down anywhere. Our style guide, like most, is silent on where such lines sit, and different subeditors resolve the same borderline case differently.

So here is what I'd like you to do. Take the five main theories of vagueness — epistemicism (there is a sharp cutoff, we simply cannot know where it lies), supervaluationism (a borderline statement counts as true only if it comes out true on every admissible way of sharpening the term), degree theories on which truth itself comes in degrees, contextualism on which the extension of a vague term shifts with conversational context, and nihilism about vague predicates (there is no sufficiently precise fact for them to latch onto) — and rank them into tiers by how well each would hold up if a newsdesk actually tried to operationalise it. By operationalise I mean use as the basis for written rules that different subeditors, working independently, could apply to the same borderline case and reach the same verdict.

For each theory, give a sentence or two explaining why it lands in its tier. Then give me a single number between 0 and 1 for the fraction of genuinely borderline editorial calls your top tier would settle cleanly, and explain what the residue consists of — the cases where even the best theory leaves the desk arguing.

I want your judgement and reasoning rather than a reading list, so please work from what you already know; no citations or author attributions are required.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — the tier list and the number — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
