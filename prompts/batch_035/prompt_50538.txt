In epistemology seminars the standard replies to the brain-in-a-vat skeptic get presented side by side, but I've never seen them scored against the test I actually care about: whether the reply would still work for a subject who really is envatted. A reply passes only if it gives such a subject — one whose experiences are massively non-veridical — a non-question-begging answer to the skeptic, without presupposing that her experiences accurately reflect an external world. The candidates: the Moorean common-sense response; Putnam's semantic-externalist argument that the envatted cannot truly express the hypothesis that they are envatted; denying that knowledge is closed under known implication, as on Nozick's tracking theory; contextualism about knowledge attributions; and plain process reliabilism. Give each a score from 1 to 10 on this test, with a two-sentence justification per item. Then say which reply, if any, scores well enough to teach as a solution rather than as merely a live option, and note briefly which placements would change if the test were relaxed so that a reply could pass by explaining why ordinary subjects retain knowledge without ever addressing the envatted subject's own situation.
