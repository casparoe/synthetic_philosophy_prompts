I TA a reading group on origins-of-life stuff and I want to make a fun "which definition of life are you actually using?" quiz for our little website. The quiz has to be case-based: about ten questions, each one a concrete scenario where the standard definitions of life come apart, two or three answer options each, and then a scoring key at the end mapping answer patterns onto the actual positions: autopoiesis (life as self-producing systems that make and maintain their own boundary), the NASA-flavored evolutionary one (roughly: a self-sustaining chemical system capable of evolution by natural selection), the thermodynamic view that goes back to Schrödinger's idea that living things feed on order and export entropy, the metabolic/homeostatic view, and the deflationary option that life is just a cluster concept with no hidden essence.

Cases the quiz should probably draw on, though swap in better ones if you have them: a virus, a prion, a sterile worker ant and a mule (these two do different work, keep both), the HeLa cell line, a desiccated tardigrade that's been inert for thirty years, a glider in Conway's Game of Life, a self-replicating robot factory someone left on the moon, an oil droplet that crawls up pH gradients and buds off smaller droplets, a virus spreading through a simulated digital world, and a hypothetical protocell that replicates perfectly with zero mutation so that no evolution can ever occur. The diagnostic logic should be things like: how someone answers about the mutation-free protocell separates the Darwinian from the autopoiesis crowd, the mule separates people who define life at the individual level from those who define it at the lineage level, and the tardigrade probes whether continuous metabolism matters.

Hard constraint: no label questions. Nothing like "do you believe viruses are alive" — a reader should be able to answer every single question without ever having heard the word autopoiesis, and get sorted by what they say about cases, not by which school they think they belong to. Also, at the end please flag which pairs of leaves your quiz genuinely can't separate (e.g. if the metabolic and thermodynamic people will end up with identical answer patterns, say so), and note any answer pattern that dumps the reader somewhere weird or nowhere at all.
