I'm a second-year grad student and for our value theory seminar we have to turn in a "position paper" laying out our actual views on the good, which then gets torn apart in discussion. Mine is due in two weeks and I'd rather find the problems now than in front of twelve people. So here's roughly where I've landed, and I want to know if it hangs together:

(1) I'm basically a hedonist about welfare, and I think pains aggregate in the obvious way: the badness of a pain is a function of its intensity and its duration, trading off against each other additively, no weird interaction effects between them.

(2) "Better than" is transitive. If X is better than Y and Y is better than Z, then X is better than Z, full stop. My go-to defense is the money-pump argument — anyone whose evaluations cycle can be drained of everything they have, so cycles must be irrational — and I want to extend this to betterness itself, not just to preferences.

(3) Picture a ladder of headaches. Top rung: a truly blinding headache lasting ten minutes. Next rung down: a headache that's just barely less intense but lasts a whole hour. Below that, slightly less intense still but lasts an entire evening, and so on, all the way down to a faint background ache that stays with you for years. Looking at any two adjacent rungs, my judgment is rock solid: the less intense headache is better than its neighbor above it. I would trade down the ladder at every single step, and I've tried and failed to talk myself out of the stepwise judgments.

(4) And yet I'm completely certain that ten minutes of blinding pain is better than years of a faint nagging ache. I'd take the ten minutes without a moments hesitation.

(5) I also think that for tons of close comparisons — two similar experiences, two similar lives — it can be true that neither is better than the other even though they're not equally good. They're just incomparable, or "on a par" or whatever you want to call it. I don't accept that everything must be better, worse, or exactly equal.

(6) Finally, I'm fine with mere indifference being intransitive: coffee sweetened with 99 vs 100 vs 101 grains of sugar — I'm indifferent between any adjacent pair but I'd still pick 101 over 99. Seems totally rational to me, and I don't think it violates anything else I believe.

My own suspicion is that the trouble, if there is any, is hiding in (5), since that's the most exotic-sounding commitment, and one of my friends claims my advisor muttered something about me "smuggling something in," though he wouldn't say where. Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix — and what does that fix cost me? Rank the tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give me the reasoning, no preamble please.
