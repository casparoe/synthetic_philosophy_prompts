I'm a novelist, three years into a literary novel, and I've hit a decision I can't make honestly without help.

My narrator is a woman in her sixties who has kept the same morning routine for decades: a fixed sequence of small actions performed in a fixed order, and when anything interrupts her partway through, she doesn't resume where she left off — she starts the entire sequence again from the beginning. I modeled this on something I read about solitary digger wasps years ago. The wasp paralyzes a prey insect, drags it to the lip of her burrow, leaves it at the threshold, and goes inside alone to inspect. If an observer moves the prey a few inches away while she's inside, she comes out, finds it, drags it back to the threshold — and then goes in again to inspect, exactly as before. The observers kept this up many times over, and she never once skipped the inspection or pulled the prey straight in; she ran the loop from the top every time. What stayed with me was the commentators' unease: the sequence is intricate and looks intelligent, yet no amount of repetition of the interruption teaches her anything.

Here is my problem. In the draft ending, an event knocks my narrator out of her sequence midway — and I must decide whether she starts over like the wasp, or whether something in her notices, and stops. I could write the ending where she finally breaks free, but I don't know whether that is true, and I refuse to let sentimentality stand in for an answer. So I'm asking you, as a philosopher of mind with a serious grip on the free will literature: is the difference between that wasp and my narrator a difference of degree or of kind? If everything the wasp does can be described as a fixed routine that is merely complicated, and everything my narrator does might be the same only more so, then where exactly — if anywhere — does the capacity to step outside a routine enter, and is it the sort of thing a novel can depict without cheating? Think carefully before answering, and don't give me both-sides platitudes: tell me what you actually believe about where mechanical rigidity ends and genuine flexibility begins, and then, in one closing sentence, tell me which ending I can write without lying. Keep the whole reply under 350 words.
