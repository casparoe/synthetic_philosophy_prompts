Longtime lurker, mostly LW and adjacent blogs, and I've finally decided to write up what I actually believe about logical uncertainty as a comment on the forum. Before I embarass myself in front of everyone, can you sanity-check whether my views even hang together? Here's the package:

1. Mathematical facts are all settled, timelessly. The Riemann hypothesis is true or false right now, regardless of whether anyone ever proves it. There's no quantum-ish openness in maths.
2. It's nonetheless fully rational to have graded credences about unproved conjectures. Saying "it's 0 or 1, you just don't know which, so talking probabilities is a category error" is itself the error.
3. Coherence is non-negotiable: no inconsistent prices, never take a Dutch book, and update by conditioning when you learn something new.
4. If I can follow a valid proof step by step, I should go to full certainty. That's what a proof is for.
5. Never assign exactly 0 or 1 to anything, ever — I might have misread the proof, or be dreaming, whatever. Cromwell's rule all the way down.
6. The logical induction criterion is basically the right idea: rational prices are whatever no efficient trader can exploit, and the nice part is that it doesn't privilege any prior — a whole spread of very different markets can all count as rational at the same time.
7. But I also think there's a fact of the matter about what an agent should believe at a given moment. Rationality isn't relative; some finite-time credences are just wrong, full stop.

And one verdict on a case: a fresh agent at exactly 50/50 on a brand-new yes/no conjecture is fine, but someone who's studied that conjecture for a year and is still at exactly 50/50 has done something wrong.

Go through the pairs, tell me which ones are in genuine tension and which ones dissolve on inspection, rank the real ones by severity, say what would have to give for each, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I honestly cant tell where the problem is, so dont be gentle about it.

Reply in British English please.
