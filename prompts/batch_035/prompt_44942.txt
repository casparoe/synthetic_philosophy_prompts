My brother-in-law moved here from a country where tipping is basically seen as insulting, and he flat out refuses to tip in the US. His argument: it's a made-up custom, not a moral rule, so skipping it is rude at worst, not wrong the way stealing is. We argue about this every time we go out to dinner and I've realized the family splits into like four or five camps, but I can't figure out what the actual underlying question is that separates them. Help me build a "which position do you secretly hold" quiz that sorts people by how they answer concrete cases, not by which label they'd pick for themselves.

Cases I want included: (1) jaywalking at 3am on a completely empty street where it's technically illegal; (2) stiffing a waiter who was genuinely terrible, in a place where waiters depend on tips to make a living wage; (3) my brother-in-law not tipping as a visitor who fully knows the local custom; (4) cutting in a queue when nobody will notice or confront you; (5) visiting a country where nobody queues at all — are you off the hook from queueing, or should you still do it? And one more axis I keep going back and forth on: does it matter whether the convention exists because it fixes something real (tipping because restaurants underpay staff) versus being pure arbitrariness (which fork you use)?

I want you to build it from the logical space first — the questions, and which answer combinations they allow — and only then place the actual positions as leaves of the tree. Positions that should show up somewhere: conventions are just etiquette and never genuine obligations; they become binding through fairness once you're inside the system and other people rely on them; they bind only when the convention is doing moral work, like carrying someone's livelihood (so really it's the underlying injustice that binds you, not the custom itself); you're bound by whatever norms you grew up with wherever you are; you're bound by wherever you currently are, visitor or not. If some combinations of answers don't correspond to a position anybody holds, say so and tell me whether that's because the combo is incoherent or just because nobody's thought of it. And if two of the camps can't actually be separated by any of my cases, be honest about that instead of pretending my quiz catches everything. Eight to ten questions max, each with two or three answer options, and a scoring key at the end.
