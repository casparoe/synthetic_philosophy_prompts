I keep getting into it with my brother-in-law about redistribution every time politics comes up (he's basically a libertarian, long story, usually over Christmas dinner), and last month it hit me that I don't actually have a principle, just gut reactions. So I sat down, made up a bunch of cases, and wrote down what my gut says for each one. Now I want to know whether there's a single idea behind all of them or whether my gut is just a mess.

Here's the list. Taking £1,000 off a millionaire and giving it to a family on minimum wage: obviously good, even if the paperwork eats £50 of it on the way. Taking £1,000 off the millionaire and just burning it, so the gap between him and the family shrinks but nobody is a penny better off: not an improvement, it feels almost petty, though I can't say why since the gap shrinks either way. Two societies to pick between: one where everyone has £10k, and one where the worst-off person has £12k but someone else has £100k — I prefer the second, the worst-off person doing better matters more than the size of the gap. But then a third one: everyone at £10k each, versus a society where the worst-off has £9k and one guy has £5 million, and here my gut flips and says the equal one is better, even though the worst-off person is now worse off than in the unequal one. I can feel those last two pulling in opposite directions and I don't know wich one to give up, or whether both can be saved.

A couple more. A fixed pot of £500 can go either to a household living on £10k or one living on £25k, everything else about them the same: it should go to the £10k household, no question. But if the £10k household got there through years of gambling and the £25k household is only at £25k because of medical bills, my gut wants to flip it, though I feel a bit ashamed of that. And one I honestly can't settle: a rich person and a poor person break the same leg in the same way and there's only one ambulance — my gut says poor person first, but when I ask myself why, all I've got is "he needs it more," and he doesn't, they need it exactly the same. So: is there a principle, or maybe two principles with some rule for when each applies, that gets all of this? What would it say about a case I haven't thought of — say, the £500 pot again, but where the worse-off household would genuinely enjoy the money less (long depression, money just doesn't do much for them right now)? And please be honest about which of my verdicts you'd throw out. My own guess is the £9k-plus-£5m-guy one is the odd one out, but I'm not sure, and I'd rather hear it straight than have you bend the principle to flatter me.
