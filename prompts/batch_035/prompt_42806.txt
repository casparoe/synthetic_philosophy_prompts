I've always been kind of obsessed with art forgery scandals, the Han van Meegeren fake Vermeers, that Elmyr de Hory guy from the F for Fake documentary, all of it. And a while back I noticed something that's been bugging me ever since. When a painting gets outed as a forgery, the museum takes it down or buries it in storage, and the auction value goes from millions to basically zero -- even though the physical object hasn't changed at all. Same canvas, same paint, same image on the wall. But here's the thing: I have a print of the exact same painting hanging in my apartment and nobody thinks I'm committing fraud. Orchestras play Bach on modern instruments and nobody calls it fake Bach. My local theater does Shakespeare with the wrong genders and accents and it's just "a production." So the art world seems to be doing something weird: it treats paintings as if the exact physical object and who actually made it matters enormously, but treats music, theater, and literature as if a decent copy or performance delivers the real thing just fine.

And it's not just the copying thing. A living painter can make an intentional "Vermeer-style" painting, totally honest about it, and it's worth nothing even though it might be indistinguishable to an average viewer standing in front of it. Meanwhile the "real" one next to it is roped off and insured for a fortune. Also there's the weird middle cases -- workshops where the master painted like 10% of it and assistants did the rest, and we shrug and call it a Titian. So even the "the master's own hand" thing seems to get applied really inconsistently.

So what I want to know is: what does this whole set of practices actually presuppose about why original paintings are valuable? Is it some idea that the value comes from the object's history (it was touched by this person, it was present in 1650), or from something about how the work was made (it was an authentic creative act vs a deception), or is it just a purity status thing collectors made up to keep prices high? And is the painting/music split coherent, or is the art world just being arbitrary -- and if they're being arbitrary, why does almost everyone, including me honestly, FEEL the difference?
