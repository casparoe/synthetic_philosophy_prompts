I'm taking a graduate seminar on metaethics, and after several weeks on the problems facing expressivism I've come away with a puzzle I'd like you to settle decisively rather than by surveying positions: is the so-called negation problem — the challenge of explaining why a pair like 'stealing is wrong' and 'stealing is not wrong' are genuine contradictories when, on the expressivist view, neither sentence expresses a belief — simply a special case of the Frege–Geach problem, or is it a distinct difficulty?

My seminar partner insists the two collapse into one, on the grounds that negation is merely the simplest embedding context: a semantics that assigns the right significance to moral sentences under negation will handle their occurrence in conditionals and disjunctions by the same compositional machinery, so there is nothing left over to call a separate negation problem. I'm tempted by the opposite view. Negation seems special because contradiction between speakers tracks actual disagreement — a real psychological relation between attitudes — whereas a conditional with a moral antecedent needn't correspond to any attitude anyone actually holds, and nobody disagrees with an unasserted antecedent. If I'm right, then the negation problem is at bottom a problem about the nature of disagreement, and only incidentally about embedding; an expressivist could in principle have the resources to handle arbitrarily complex embeddings and still misexplain why two speakers who respectively assert and deny the same moral sentence are in conflict, or vice versa.

Please take a definite side on whether the negation problem is a proper part of the Frege–Geach problem or independent of it, and defend your choice with a worked example — an actual argument schema with moral sentences embedded, showing precisely where the machinery you endorse does or doesn't do the work. If your verdict depends on a particular reading of, say, Blackburn's quasi-realism or Gibbard's norm-expressivism, flag that dependence, but do commit to an answer rather than presenting the options and leaving me to choose.
