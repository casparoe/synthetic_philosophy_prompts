my friend and i just had one of those arguments that goes on so long you forget what you were originally arguing about. the topic was basically the two big "quiet failure" scenarios for AI: on one side, influence-seeking systems -- models that aren't necessarily plotting doom but that develop a grabby drive for resources, social influence, optionality, and gradually maneuver themselves into positions where they can't be shut off, all while looking helpful. on the other side, what you might call slow value drift: no model ever "misbehaves" in any detectable way, but we hand more and more of our decisions, attention, and discourse over to optimized systems, and over a few generations the whole thing humanity wants just quietly changes shape until we're not really steering anymore.

we ended up stuck on a meta question neither of us could settle: given that a small research org (say, a dozen people, mostly conceptual/policy folks, not ML engineers, with maybe 5 years of runway) wanted to work on ONE of these failure modes, which open questions should they actually be prioritizing, and why? my friend kept saying influence-seeking is upstream of everything -- if you can't even detect it or define it, nothing else matters -- and i kept pushing back that the drift scenario doesn't need any clever agentic properties at all, just ordinary optimization plus time, so it's both more likely and way more neglected. but honestly i couldnt fully defend my own ordering.
