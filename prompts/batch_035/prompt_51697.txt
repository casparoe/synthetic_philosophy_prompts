I'm the TA for a second-year game theory course, and next week I lead the fifty-minute section that sits between the lecture on dominance by pure strategies and the lecture on Nash equilibrium. My section is supposed to cover the refinement in which dominance is defined over mixed strategies: first the relatively easy point that a strategy's payoffs must be compared not just against each of the opponent's pure strategies but against every mixture of them, and then the part I expect to be hard — the fact that a pure strategy can be strictly dominated by a mixture of the player's *own* other pure strategies even when no single pure strategy dominates it, and that this justifies deleting it just the same. I also have to say something about iteration: why deleting one strategy can make a previously undominated strategy dominated, and why this procedure is legitimate under strict dominance in a way students should not assume carries over to weak dominance. Don't explain the material to me; I know it. What I want is a plan for the section: the order the pieces have to come in and what each later piece presupposes, the misconceptions the students will walk in with or form along the way, and exactly where in the sequence each one should be headed off. My guess about one of them: they'll conflate "dominated" with "never a best response to any pure strategy of the opponent," which I believe is actually false — a strategy can fail to be a best response to every pure strategy and still be a best response to some mixture, and so survive — but I'm not sure where in the sequence that confusion bites or how to head it off without a detour.

For the carrying example I'm inclined to use a game I built for the purpose. The row player has four strategies; against the column player's three pure strategies they yield, respectively, (6, 0, 0), (0, 6, 0), (0, 0, 6), and (1, 1, 1). The first three are symmetric, and the fourth is strictly dominated by an equal mix of the first three (which yields (2, 2, 2) against every column) while being dominated by none of them individually. I'd leave the column player's payoffs generic. Tell me whether this game is the right vehicle — whether the equal-mixture symmetry makes the point vivid or lets students pattern-match to "average the rows" without understanding, whether the fourth row should instead be dominated by a *weighted* mixture so they can't find it by symmetry, and whether I need a column side with its own dominated structure or whether one-sided deletion is cleaner at this level.
