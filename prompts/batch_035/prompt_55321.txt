So our econ-psych club at school (14 people, one 45-minute meeting) wants me to run something on escalation of commitment. Problem: everyone already knows the dollar auction, its all over youtube, so if I just run one nobody will be surprised by where they end up. What I want instead is a game where the game itself does the work of isolating *why* people escalate — like rounds where the usual suspect (sunk costs) gets taken off the table somehow: refunds of spent bids, fresh anonymous budgets each round, bidders swapped out mid-auction so you're escalating on behalf of someone else's hole. If people keep bidding anyway when the sunk-cost excuse is gone, they've run into something about face or competition or not-looking-like-a-quitter, and that's the thing I want them to notice before anyone names it. Can you design the actual rules, a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money, and a debrief that maps each round onto one candidate explanation (sunk costs, beating the other guy, reputation in front of the group)? Also tell me which round people will predictibly try to argue "doesn't count," and what will go wrong the first time I run it. The take-away I'm after is that escalating isn't just a math error — the same dumb bidding survives when you take the math excuse away.
