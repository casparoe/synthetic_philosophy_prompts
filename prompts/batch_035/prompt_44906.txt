I'm taking a philosophy of mind seminar this semester and we just finished the dreaming unit, which is basically a 60-year-old knife fight between two papers that never really touched each other. On one side you have the Wittgensteinian line (Norman Malcolm's Dreaming, 1959) that dreams can't literally be experiences happening while you sleep, because the whole concept of a dream is tied to the report you give on waking, and that report is just an unverifiable memory-impression — there's no such thing as checking whether you really dreamt what you say you dreamt. On the other you have Dennett's 1976 paper where he floats the "cassette theory": that the brain might compose or select the dream content at the moment of waking, and all the "experiencing" would then be a false implication of the memory. His favorite example is the old anecdote about the 19th century guy who dreamt an elaborate French Revolution narrative that ended with the guillotine falling — and then woke up with the bed canopy collapsed on his neck. Dennett's point is that whether the narrative played out over minutes or was slapped together in the last half second, the waking report is identical, so how could we ever know?

Here's the thing: our seminar has been arguing about this for three weeks and it's obvious to me the two camps are constantly talking past each other, but I think there's actually a real disagreement buried in there and I want to force us to make it exact. Please write that document.

1. A numbered section of claims both could sign — about dream reports as data, about the logic of verification, about what any theory of dreaming has to explain, whatever they genuinely share. Not vacuous filler like "dreams are interesting."

I *think* one genuine disagreement is whether "I dreamt X" reports from inside an ongoing dream (see below) count as dream reports at all, but maybe that's actually verbal and you should say so if it is.

To me this looks like it should be devastating for BOTH sides — the Malcolmian said reports from within sleep are conceptually impossible, and the cassette theory says content gets composed at waking, yet here's someone doing arithmetic inside the dream and answering on the spot.
