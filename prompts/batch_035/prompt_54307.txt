I've got this ongoing thing with my brother-in-law where we argue about what the word "for" actually means when you say a body part is for something. He's a nurse, I fix agricultural machinery, so between us we know a bit about bodies and a bit about parts that have jobs, and it started because he said my old tractor's engine is "for" turning the wheels in a way my heart isn't "for" pumping blood, which annoyed me even though I couldn't say why. So I've been collecting cases and going with my gut on each, and I want someone to tell me if there's a rule underneath it all, because right now I feel like a hypocrite.

Here's my list. Hearts are for pumping blood, obviously. Eyes are for seeing. But even though I'm using my eyes to read this right now, eyes are not for reading — that one feels solid. The heart also makes a thumping sound that a doctor uses to tell if you're healthy, but hearts are definitely not for making noise. Wings on a bird are for flying, but I read somewhere that the earliest feathered things couldn't fly and the feathers were probably keeping them warm, so were those early feathers for warmth, or for flying, or for nothing? And here's the one that keeps me up: if some animal is born with a slightly thicker coat than its parents, just a random mutation, and there happens to be a freak cold winter that year and the thick coat is the only reason it survives — I feel like the thick coat is for keeping it warm, right then, in that animal, even though it's brand new and there's no history behind it. But my brother-in-law says no, "for" has to come from the species having been shaped by that job over generations, otherwise a rock that happens to block a doorway is "for" blocking the doorway. And I can't answer that, except that it feels totally different from the rock.

So what I want is: is there one principle that gets all of these, or close to it? Please state it properly, tell me which of my gut verdicts it can't fit and why, and then tell me what it says about two cases I haven't made my mind up on — first, whether the appendix (which seems to do basically nothing but maybe once helped our ancestors digest stuff) is for anything at all, and second, whether a monkey that picks up a stick and uses it to fish termites out of a hole means the stick is for termite-fishing in a way that's relevantly different from the coat case. If my coat verdict has to go, I want to know that honestly rather than being let down gently.
