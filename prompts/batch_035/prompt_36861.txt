I've got no academic background, just a lot of time spent on our city council and a head full of half-formed ideas, but here's the view I've landed on after years of watching how things actually get decided: democracy IS an experiment, or at least it's supposed to be. Policies are hypotheses, citizens are the ones running the test, and the whole point of free speech and regular elections is to keep the results honest — you need disagreement the way a lab needs a control group. I've even got a pet scheme where every new law comes with a five-year sunset clause, and before renewal we actually look at what happened, the way you'd check a trial outcome. I found out later that John Dewey said something along these lines a hundred years ago, which felt like vindication, but I want to stress-test MY version instead of just feeling good about it. So: what would count against it? Not the standard complaints about voters being dumb — I mean what would we have to observe about real democracies, or about real science for that matter, for the "politics as inquiry" picture to be flat out wrong rather than just aspirational? Like, in science a failed hypothesis gets dropped and nobody spends twenty years defending it publicly, but in politics that's basically the norm — does that show my analogy is broken, or just that we've messed up the experiment? And here's the thing I'm honestly worried about: is there a version of my view that's been so qualified ("well, the experiment never really concludes...") that it stops meaning anything at all? Be blunt about whether I'm holding a real position or a vibe.
