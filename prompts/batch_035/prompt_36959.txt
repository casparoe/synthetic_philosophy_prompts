I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I'm fluent in the vocabulary of my trade: build systems, dependency resolution, container images, compilers, CI pipelines, version control, and formal verification. I've lately gathered that Wittgenstein's late work On Certainty argues something along these lines: some of the things we are most sure of are not items of knowledge sitting at the bottom of our belief structure, duly checked and then used to check everything else. They are more like the substrate the activity of checking runs on top of; calling them into question is not refuting them but disabling the very practice of verification, so that the doubt, whatever it feels like from the inside, has nowhere to land.

Restate that position in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and without smuggling in assumptions of my framework and presenting them as translation. The following materials suggest themselves to me, though you need not be limited to them: pinned and transitive dependencies that no one ever audits because everything upstream of them assumes they work; the compiler and the base container image, which we must trust in order to build anything at all, and which we would have to use in any attempt to verify themselves; the fact that you cannot test a checksum utility using only that utility; invariants the type system enforces globally and that therefore never appear in any test case, since every test presupposes them; and the difference between a bug report, which is a specific checkable claim that can be triaged and fixed, and a suspicion that arithmetic itself is broken somewhere in our toolchain, which does not open a ticket but makes the whole practice of opening tickets unintelligible.

Then give me the residue report, in three parts. First, which of Wittgenstein's claims translate cleanly, with the build-system phenomenon each one maps onto. Second, which claims you had to distort to make them fit, and exactly how. Third, which claims have no expression in my vocabulary at all. For that last category, I want your considered view on one thing in particular. Wittgenstein appears to insist that these hinge certainties are not propositions we hold true but something manifested in how we act. In my world, an untested assumption remains testable in principle — you can cross-check a compiler with a second compiler, bootstrap a verified toolchain, or push verification down a level — it is only impractical to test. So the residue seems to come down to this: is his claim that hinge commitments are not even testable in principle, or not testable in the same sense, something my vocabulary genuinely cannot express? Or does it dissolve once I have the notion of a verification stack in which every level presupposes all the levels beneath it, so that no total check is even well-defined? Distinguish carefully between those two diagnoses, and tell me whether the untranslatable residue points to something his view has that my framework lacks, or to something my framework cannot say. I ask because I cannot tell from the inside, and I suspect that inability is itself part of what he was describing.
