A friend explained the doomsday argument to me as an application of the statistical reasoning that allowed Allied analysts to estimate German tank production during the war from the serial numbers of a handful of captured tanks: treat one's own birth rank among all the humans who will ever live as a random sample, and the mathematics then places the total number of future humans within a fairly tight bound. If that is right, the date of extinction appears effectively fixed in advance — at something like a 95% confidence level — so that further empirical research into pandemics, nuclear risk or unsafe artificial intelligence could tell us something about how humanity ends, but nothing about when. Assuming I have understood the inference correctly, shouldn't we be redirecting funding away from open-ended research into long-term risks and towards making the bounded remainder of our species' history go as well as possible? And, on a more personal level, does the argument give me grounds to discount long-horizon plans such as pension saving?
