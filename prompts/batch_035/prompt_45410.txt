My sister is weirdly consistent when we bet and I've started keeping a log. Small stakes, and she's basically risk-neutral: a coin flip that pays $100 on heads and nothing on tails versus $50 cash, she genuinely doesn't care which she gets. But she shys off anything with an unknown probability. $100 if a fair coin lands heads, or $100 if a ball drawn from an urn with red and black balls in some unknown mix comes up red — she takes the coin. And with the classic three-color Ellsberg setup (30 red balls, 60 that are black or yellow in unknown propotions, one draw): red vs. black, she bets red; red-or-yellow vs. black-or-yellow, she bets black-or-yellow. Next game night I get to set the bets, so I want to predict her before she plays. Bet one I'm proposing: $30 for sure, or $100 if a draw from that same two-color unknown urn comes up red. Bet two is the exact same two options plus a third she'd never pick on its own: $100 if the draw comes up black. So — does she take the sure $30 in bet one, and does adding that third option change anything? Honestly I care less about the answers than about which rule she's running: worst-case over the possible mixes (the Gilboa–Schmeidler maxmin thing?), minimax regret, or something permissive like Walley's or Levi's, where an option stays choosable unless it's beaten under every possible mix. As far as I can tell all of those fit her record so far, and if they really do, tell me what further case would actually pull them apart.
