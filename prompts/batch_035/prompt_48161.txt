I'm an undergraduate currently taking a philosophy of mind course, and we've just finished a unit on the three stances (physical, design, and intentional) and on the idea that beliefs attributed from the intentional stance are real patterns rather than useful fictions. The unit has been swimming around in my head all week, and during yesterday's study group two of the other students — I'll call them Elena and Yusuf — got into an argument about it that I was entirely unable to referee. I'd like your help adjudicating it.

It started over a chess engine. Elena claimed that when the engine sacrifices its queen, we can say flatly that it believes the sacrifice wins, with no hedging "as if": the intentional-stance description latches onto a genuine regularity in its behavior, one that supports prediction and counterfactuals, and a pattern that carries that explanatory weight is as real as the patterns we file under physics — objecting that its internals are "just" search trees and number comparisons, she said, is like objecting that a planet is "just" a swarm of particles. Yusuf replied that this waters the concept of belief down to a bookkeeping convenience: on that account a thermostat believes the room is too cold, and if you push it, the wall ends up believing whatever makes prediction cheapest, so the distinction between a genuine believer and a merely as-if one dissolves — and with it, the very distinction the account was supposed to illuminate. Elena answered that the wall is not a counterexample but the expected output of a graded view: a pattern counts as real only when it gives predictive purchase that no cheaper description supplies, and for a wall the intentional stance buys nothing over the physical stance, so the wall sits at the degenerate bottom of a continuum rather than refuting anything — and besides, she added, Yusuf's demand for a bright line presupposes that belief must be all-or-nothing, which the pattern idea never promised in the first place. Yusuf's last word was that a continuum running from adult humans down through thermostats still owes us an account of where the differences that matter set in, and that making reality hinge on which description compresses best makes what counts as real depend on our computational budgets — a fact about us, not about the engine or the wall.

Could you work through their exchange move by move — saying who is right at each step, not just who comes out ahead overall — and then tell me what, if anything, both of them are missing? Please write your answer in American English.
