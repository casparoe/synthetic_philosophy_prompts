My closest friend and I have been arguing for years about who deserves a vote. It started small — whether seasonal renters in our lake community should get a say in the association's rules, whether a factory one town over entitles the people downstream to a vote on its permit — and it grew into a standing quarrel about the point of voting itself. Neither of us has any academic background; we worked this out in the car and over coffee. I've since read enough to learn that political philosophers call this the boundary problem, and that the two camps we stumbled into on our own have names: one says the demos should include everyone whose interests are affected by a decision, the other that it should include everyone actually subjected to the decision's authority or coercion.

Please draft the joint statement the two of us could finally sign, treating us as parties to an adversarial collaboration. I want the claims we can both accept — including about the concrete cases that set us off, like the downstream neighbors, the tourists passing through on election day, the children, the unborn, and the people who might move here next year; the exact statements of where we still differ, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; and the cruxes. I suspect one crux is whether being affected a little counts as being governed at all, and another is what to do about people who can't yet or can never cast a ballot, but I leave that to you. For each crux, give a case or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and if you conclude that some of our quarrel was only about the word "affected," say which part and why.
