so I got into an argument at a party (of all places) about bug spray and it left me wanting a proper consistency check, because I walked away suspecting my positions don't actually hang together. here's roughly where I've landed over the years, a mix of things I've read and gut reactions:

1. insects probably can suffer. the science has been moving in this direction for a while and I'm past the point where I'd bet against it.
2. moral status comes in degrees. a bee's suffering counts, but way less than a dog's, and a tiny fraction of a person's.
3. causing significant suffering for a trivial benefit is just wrong. this is the whole reason I'm against cosmetics testing on animals and, tbh, its basically why I stopped eating meat — taste pleasure is a trivial benefit and factory farming is significant suffering.
4. ordinary pest control is fine. spraying crops, ant bait under the sink, wasp spray on the nest by the porch — I have never once thought twice about any of it and I don't plan to start.
5. for insects, what's wrong with killing one is only the suffering involved in the dying. a creature with no projects doesn't lose anything by being dead, so a quick painless death is basically morally free.
6. when I see a kid burning ants with a magnifying glass I don't just feel grossed out, I think he's doing something wrong, and I think that's a moral reaction, not an aesthetic one.

here's what's nagging me. my neighbor sprays her roses every spring so they look perfect for garden club, and the guy at the party told me I have no grounds to object to that given everything else I believe. then on the way home I remembered that ant bait doesn't kill quickly — the ants carry it back to the nest and it works over days. and I can't decide whether these are the same problem or two different problems, or which of the six things has to give. I'm not going to tell you which one I'd drop because I genuinly don't know, though I'd rather keep 1 than not.
