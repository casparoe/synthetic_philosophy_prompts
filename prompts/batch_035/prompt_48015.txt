For many years I have been a regular reader of the rationalist blogosphere, and somewhere along the way I noticed a structural oddity that I have never been able to put a name to. I am hoping there is an established term for it, and if there is not, I would like help carving the concept precisely, because my attempts to describe it to others keep collapsing into vague gestures at higher-order reasoning.

The paradigm case is the blue-eyed islanders puzzle, which circulated widely on the blogs years ago and has been re-litigated in comment sections ever since. A visitor publicly announces, to the assembled islanders, a fact that every one of them already knows: that at least one islander has blue eyes. Nobody learns the fact itself. What changes is that everyone now knows that everyone knows (that everyone knows...) the fact, and in the standard resolution the blue-eyed islanders all depart on the night whose number equals the size of the blue-eyed group. What interests me is not the puzzle but a specific feature of its mechanics: the depth of nested knowledge functions as a countdown, so that the announcement is completely inert for ninety-nine days and then, on the hundredth, empties the island — and the rounds of visible inaction are themselves what advance the clock, since each day of nobody leaving tells everybody something, but only because everybody can see that nobody is leaving.

I keep meeting what seems to be the same structure elsewhere. When two agents with common priors repeatedly and publicly announce their posterior beliefs until their disagreement evaporates, each announcement seems to peel off exactly one layer of knowledge-about-the-other's-knowledge, so that the number of rounds needed is readable off the depth of the initial divergence. In the electronic mail game that Rubinstein used to exhibit the fragility of common knowledge, introducing an arbitrarily small probability that the chain of confirmation messages has silently broken down does not gradually degrade the coordinated outcome — it destroys it outright, a cliff rather than a slope. And in everyday settings: a rumor that everyone has already privately heard behaves very differently from the same rumor announced from a podium, even when the propositional content is identical.

So, my question: does this mechanism have a name? I mean the deterministic unwinding, over discrete and mutually observed rounds, of a finite tower of mutual knowledge, where the height of the tower fixes the number of rounds, and where each round of publicly observable non-action is itself informative and thereby shortens the fuse. I know the general neighborhood is common knowledge in the Lewis/Aumann tradition, backward induction, and iterated dominance, but as far as I can tell none of those names the temporal structure I am after: nested knowledge behaving like a burning fuse whose length you can count off from the number of levels, with the fuse advanced by public silence. If no established term exists, I would be grateful for a candidate name and definition, together with the boundary cases that any good definition must handle — for instance, what becomes of the fuse when the tower is infinite rather than merely tall, i.e., genuine common knowledge rather than n-level mutual knowledge, and whether the same countdown mechanism survives when the rounds cannot be publicly observed, as in the email game. Please do not simply tell me that this is about common knowledge; I am after the carving of this specific unwinding — what it is, what it is not, and where its edges lie.
