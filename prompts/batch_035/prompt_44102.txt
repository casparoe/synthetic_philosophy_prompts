everyone in my metaphysics seminar leans on the knot analogy for ontic structural realism and I want to know if it can actually carry the weight: objects are like knots in a fishing net. A knot is just where threads cross, there's no extra knot-thing there besides the threads, and if you pull the threads the knot slides along or comes undone but nothing in the world has been destroyed. My prof uses this to argue we should drop objects from the ontology entirely and keep only structure -- the net without the knots -- and says it fits quantum physics nicely since identical particles aren't distinguishable anyway. Audit the analogy for me: what plays the role of the threads, the knot, the material the net is woven from, then where the mapping breaks (e.g. knots have identity and location in ways particles famously don't, and can you really have a net whose threads are all there is when the threads look suspiciously like relata-less relations...), which disanalogies actually matter for his eliminativist conclusion and which don't, and whether "no objects, just structure" survives once the breaks are on the table or whether the analogy was doing all the work. If there's a better picture for what OSR wants to claim, say what it would be.
