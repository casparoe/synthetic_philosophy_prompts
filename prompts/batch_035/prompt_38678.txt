Got into a huge argument with my friend last night about illusionism — the Dennett-ish view that consciousness is a kind of user illusion, the interface the brain shows itself — and now I cant stop thinking about it. I wrote up some notes for an essay and want feedback before I actually try to write the thing.

Her position: if consciousness is an illusion, then someone has to be experiencing the illusion, and the theory just helped itself to the very thing it claims to explain away. A magic trick that nobody watches isn't a trick. She thinks this is a knockdown argument and that the illusionists basically dodge it.

My notes so far:

1. The standard reply is that the "user" is itself part of the model, the way a desktop icon is part of the interface — there's no leftover spectator we failed to locate. But I suspect this reply proves too much: if both the contents of experience and the subject of experience are just more model, the theory stops saying our experience is misleading and starts saying it's fictional, more like a narrator in a novel than an illusion in a lab.

2. So maybe the analogy should be fiction rather than perceptual illusion. A fictional narrator isn't deceived about anything, and nothing is deceived on its behalf. Question: does calling consciousness a fiction instead of an illusion actually change any argument, or is it just swapping vibes?

3. My real point, and I'm not sure it holds up: a self-model can be wrong about what it *is* without being wrong about everything else it represents. The model misdescribes its own status (it presents itself as direct contact with reality rather than as a model) while still carrying accurate information about the world. If that's right, then "consciousness is an illusion" is true in the same boring way that "a map is not the territory" is true, and both the knockdown argument and the flashy theory lose their drama.

Things I want to know: does point 3 actually work or am I just equivocating between consciousness and self-consciousness (my friend says I am)? Is the fiction vs illusion distinction in point 2 doing any real work? And is this interesting enough to write an essay on, or am I rehashing the standard back-and-forth with new words? Be honest, my friend certainly was.
