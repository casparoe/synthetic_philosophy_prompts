So full disclosure I never went to college and philosophy stuff I mostly just think about on my own, but a couple years ago I read The Brothers Karamazov (ok, mostly, I zoned out during the long monk parts) and the Grand Inquisitor chapter has been living rent free in my head ever since. The way I understand it is the old cardinal tells Jesus that people don't really want the freedom Jesus gave them, freedom is too heavy, what people want is bread, and miracles, and someone to bow down to and someone to take the burden of choice off their shoulders. The Inquisitor says basically we fixed your mistake, and Jesus doesn't even argue, he just kisses him.

Anyway. Dostoevsky died in 1881 so he never saw any of this, but I keep wondering what he would say if you sat him down with a smartphone. Like specifically TikTok-style feeds and those AI girlfriend/boyfriend apps. The temptations the Inquisitor lists almost map onto it one by one — the algorithm hands you bread (endless entertainment), performs a kind of miracle (it knows what you want before you say it), and takes authority over your choices (you don't decide what to watch, the machine decides). And people seem happy, or at least they keep scrolling.

Here's my actual question though, and I want you to push back on my take because I might be full of it. In the poem the Inquisitor is terrifying but he at least claims to love mankind, he says he took the burden on himself out of pity. My gut says Dostoevsky would notice that the algorithm is like an Inquisitor with nobody home — it doesn't love you, it doesn't even want your soul, it just wants your attention. And I can't decide if that's worse or actually better than the real thing. A fake shepherd you can walk away from vs. a shepherd-shaped vacuum that nobody is even steering on purpose.

So: what would he say? Would he think the Inquisitor basically won, or would he say it doesn't count as the same thing at all? And who's supposed to be the Jesus figure in this version — is there even one, or did Dostoevsky think that kind of silent kiss only works on someone who chose to burn you at the stake?
