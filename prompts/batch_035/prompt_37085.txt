Every so often my mates and I go back through our old group chat and take the mick out of what we believed ten years ago. Lately it's got me thinking. I've basicly the same brain now as five years ago, same intelligence and all that, and back then I was dead certain about some things I'm now dead certain the opposite way about. Best example: five years ago I was sure renting was throwing money away and buying a place was the only sensible move. Now I'm sure the opposite is true for our area. And here's the thing — the evidence hasn't really changed. House prices, wages, interest rates — I knew all that then and I know it now, nobody proved me wrong in between, I just sort of drifted.

So here's the thought I can't shake off. The rule everyone seems to accept is that if someone exactly as clever as you, with exactly the same information, disagrees with you, you ought to take that seriously — maybe even meet them halfway. Fine. But the person who disagrees with me is me, five years ago. He wasn't stupid. If a mate held exactly his views I'd have to count him my equal. So shouldn't I count him as my equal too? And if I do, doesn't that mean I should only be half-confident on the renting question, and by extension half-confident on everything I've flip-flopped on without new evidence? Worse still, me-in-five-years will look back at current me the same way, so maybe I should be shaving confidence off everything I believe right now, before I've even changed my mind.

Please describe the most important implications of taking my past self as seriously as a present-day equal for how much I should trust the views I hold now — specially the ones I've already changed my mind on once. And don't just lay the options out and leave me to pick, actually take a position: tell me whether the half-confidence thing really follows, and if it doesn't, exactly where my reasoning goes wrong. Then tell me what I should actually do about it in practice — like right now a real decision rides on it, whether we sink our savings into a flat or keep renting.
