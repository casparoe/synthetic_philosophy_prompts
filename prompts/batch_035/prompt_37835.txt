As the senior member of a reading group on the philosophy of physics, I have been asked to circulate a written profile of my commitments on Bell's theorem and locality, so the others can probe it line by line. Before it goes out, I would like an audit of whether the package hangs together. Please tell me which of my commitments stand in genuine tension and how severe each tension is; which pairs only look to be in tension but dissolve on inspection; and, where something must be revised, what the cheapest revision is and what it costs. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally serious, and close by identifying the single revision that would restore consistency at the least cost — noting, where relevant, whether a commitment needs only rewording rather than abandonment.

The profile:

1. Locality. Nothing exerts a causal influence on anything outside its past light cone. This is not an artifact of the particular equations we happen to have; it is what the spirit of relativity requires, and a theory that violates it is, to that extent, in conflict with relativity, whatever form its dynamics takes.

2. Bell's result, taken with the experiments. The Bell inequalities are violated just as quantum mechanics predicts, in tests whose detection and locality loopholes have been closed; the only remaining loophole is the one item 3 shuts by fiat. Accordingly, the class of theories Bell targeted — local hidden-variable theories — is empirically excluded.

3. No superdeterminism. The settings are chosen independently of the relevant prior variables; the correlations are not to be explained by a common cause reaching forward into the choices. I regard superdeterminist evasions as a reductio of experimental method itself, and I will not consider them further.

4. No retrocausation. Causes do not operate backward in time; in particular, a later choice of setting does not affect the earlier state of the pair.

5. Completeness. The quantum state ascribed to an individual system, together with the laws, is a complete description of it: there are no further variables that, jointly with the state, settle how a measurement on that system will issue.

6. Anti-realism about the wavefunction. The wavefunction is not a field laid out in physical space; it is a representation of information — roughly, of the results one should expect for the various possible measurements.

7. No-signaling, and its sufficiency. Neither wing's marginal statistics depend on the setting chosen at the distant wing, so the correlations cannot be exploited for communication. I hold that this is precisely what keeps quantum mechanics consistent with relativity.

8. A verdict on the central case. In a spin pair of the EPR-Bohm kind, when both parties measure along a common axis, the outcomes, though individually chancy, always come out opposite. I hold that this requires no explanation over and above the formalism: there is no pre-established agreement between the wings, and none is called for.

That is the profile. Two cautions. First, I do not know where, if anywhere, it breaks, and I have no attachment to any particular item; do not assume I would sooner give up one commitment than another. Second, I do not want a tour of the interpretations or a verdict on which is true; treat the eight items as a closed set, test it for joint tenability, and report the results.
