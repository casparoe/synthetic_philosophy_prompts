I do security engineering for a living -- ACLs, capabilities, the discretionary-vs-mandatory access control distinction, privilege escalation, sandboxes, revocation, the confused deputy problem. Recently I got pulled into the old three-way fight in political philosophy about what liberty even is: negative liberty (freedom as absence of interference), positive liberty (freedom as actually being able to do things, self-mastery), and the republican one (freedom as not living at the mercy of someone's arbitrary will -- the slave with a kindly master is still unfree even though the master never actually interferes).

Restate all three positions in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. My rough instinct is that negative liberty looks like the permissions actually on my account, positive liberty looks like the gap between nominal permissions and effective ability to do anything (I "own" the file but there's no execute bit on anything, or my resource quota is zero), and republican liberty looks like whether some admin holds discretionary root over my box and whether revoking my access requires a process he can't just skip. But don't just bless my sketch -- do the translation properly, and then give me the residue report I care about most: which parts of each philosophical position carry over cleanly, which parts have to get distorted to fit access-control terms, and which have no expression at all. The piece I keep chewing on is the republican claim that what makes domination domination is that interference *would be arbitrary*, that it lives in the master's will, not in any interference that occurs -- is that just "admin has discretionary revocation and no appeal"? Or is there something about it that doesn't fit, something about status among persons, or about what it even means for a system to contain a "will" rather than just a policy? And wherever something fails to translate, tell me honestly whether that's because my vocabulary is missing something or because the philosophers' claim was less clear than it sounded. One warning: don't smuggle in assumptions from my world and call it translation -- e.g. don't quietly assume that liberty has to bottom out in something like a policy table if that assumption is itself one of the things being contested.
