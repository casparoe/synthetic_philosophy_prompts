I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy, and over the past few years I've drifted into a deflationary take on the Libet experiments. My position runs as follows: on the accumulation-to-bound account proposed by Schurger and colleagues in 2012, the readiness potential is not the signature of an unconscious decision being made in advance of awareness, but the signature of ongoing spontaneous neural fluctuations that occasionally cross a threshold, with the movement occurring at the crossing. If that's right, then the famous finding that the readiness potential precedes the reported urge to move by some hundreds of milliseconds says nothing about when the decision was made — the averaging procedure builds the apparent early buildup into the data — and the popular "your brain decides before you do" reading is an artifact. My working conclusion is that the whole paradigm never bore on free will in the first place. I'd like to know what would count against this deflationary position. Concretely: which experimental results, replications, or reinterpretations would pull me back toward thinking Libet's setup does reveal something about the timing of conscious will, and how decisive would each be? Sort the defeaters from strongest to weakest, indicate which of them the accumulation model's own proponents would concede as counting against their view and which they would dismiss, and give me an honest assessment of whether the deflationary position has been qualified so many ways — artifact here, threshold there — that no conceivable result could touch it, and whether that insulation is a problem for a position of this kind.
