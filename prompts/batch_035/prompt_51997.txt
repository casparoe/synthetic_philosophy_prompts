I teach political philosophy, and every couple of years the grad students in our department put out a symposium issue where each faculty member has to write a short where-I-stand piece — no hiding behind exegesis, you have to actually own your positions. Mine is on distributive justice. I've got my list of commitments drafted but before I send it to thier editors I want an audit, because I have a sinking feeling theres at least one pair in there that doesn't fit and I genuinely can't see which. The set:

(1) The luck egalitarian core: an inequality is unjust if and only if it traces to brute luck; inequalities tracing to genuine choices aren't unjust. (2) The metric is resources, Dworkin style — we compare what people can do with what they've got, not how satisfied they end up feeling. (3) On that metric, expensive tastes get you nothing, even when the taste is unchosen and you'd shed it tomorrow if you could — someone who just can't enjoy cheap food has no claim to extra. (4) Disabilities do get compensated, with the amount fixed by hypothetical insurance: what people would have insured themselves against if they didn't know whether they'd be born with the condition. (5) The voluntary risk-taker gets nothing beyond what her actual insurance pays — the climber who ends up paralyzed knew exactly what she was signing up for. (6) Same for the idle surfer: if you choose leisure over work, that was your resource allocation, done, no top up later. (7) But I also hold that the state owes a real safety net — emergency medical care, relief of actual destitution — even to people whose dire straits are entirely their own doing, and that this is owed as a matter of justice, not charity.

So: which of these actually fight each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix for the real ones? I don't want a literature lecture, I want an audit of my list, ordered by severity. Give me the whole thing as a JSON object with these fields: "tensions" — an array with one entry per genuinely conflicting pair, each entry having "commitments" (the numbers), "severity" (one of "fatal", "serious", "minor"), "diagnosis", "cheapest_fix", and "cost_of_fix"; "apparent_tensions" — an array of pairs that look like conflicts but dissolve on inspection, each with "commitments" and "why_it_dissolves"; "single_best_revision" — the one change that would clean up the most; and "confidence_note" — an object with "overall" (how confident you are in the audit as a whole) and "least_sure_of" (the single diagnosis you're least certain about, and why). Please discriminate rather than hedge — don't flag every pair as tense just to cover yourself.
