My friend Dana has been issuing rulings on hypotheticals about threats and negotiation for as long as I've known her — I actually keep a list on my phone, which she finds hilarious. Her track record so far: (1) a kidnapper who only abducts people his simulations predict will pay the ransom — never pay a cent, she says, because if you're the kind of person who doesn't pay, this simply never happens to you; (2) nuclear second strike — it's rational for a country to maintain the doctrine and to actually follow through once attacked, even though by then retaliation can't undo anything and just adds destruction; (3) a sealed, one-shot blackmailer — a stranger who already stole her data regardless of anything she does, will sell it unless she pays $5k against $50k of damage, will never interact with anyone she knows, and will never meet her again — she'd pay without hesitation, since the damage is locked in and paying is just buying the least bad outcome that's left; (4) an ordinary real-world ransom demand, no sealing-off — she refuses, because every payment feeds the market for hostages. Two new hypotheticals came up at dinner last week and I want to predict her answers before I ask her, mostly so I can look smart.

The first: a vandal in a small town sprays one house per night, chosen from the residents he's predicted will NOT pay his cheap protection fee — payers are never bothered, and his predictions are known to be near perfect. Sealed world, one shot, no reputation anywhere. Does she pay the fee? The second: before any adversary exists at all, she's offered the chance to adopt what the decision-theory corner of the internet calls a surrogate goal — she publicly takes up, say, collecting porcelain, and commits to responding to any threat against the collection with exactly the stubbornness she'd show if her family were threatened, plus never telling anyone which of her values are the load-bearing ones. Upside: no threatener ever finds a cheap lever on her, since everything she cares about is priced like the dearest thing. Cost: she'll sometimes eat real losses defending trinkets — including against someone who never threatens anything and just quietly steals the porcelain, because if she lets non-threats slide the whole arrangement unravels. Does she sign up? Predict her ruling on both. Then tell me what rule or rules actually fit her earlier track record — I can see at least two that fit all four of her past rulings, and I'd like you to spell each one out and say what it predicts for these two cases — then give me your best guess with reasons, and design the one further hypothetical that would most cleanly split those candidate rules apart.
