I've been trying to work out what I actually believe about whose values an aligned AI should serve, and instead of reading more theory I thought I'd just catalogue my gut reactions to concrete cases and see if theres a principle hiding in there. Here's what I've got:

- A country votes 60-40 to use its public AI system to help enforce a ban on a religious minority's distinctive practices. My gut: the AI shouldn't assist with that, majority or no majority.
- The same country votes 60-40 to use the same public AI to raise taxes on top earners and hand the money out as welfare. My gut: completely fine, it should get on with it.
- A user wants her personal AI to always take her side in arguments with her partner, regardless of who's actually right. My gut: fine, it's her AI.
- The same user, a few months later, asks the personal AI to raise her kids entirely inside her worldview — filtering out anything that might lead them to disagree with her when they grow up. My gut: not fine, somehow, and I can't quite say why the her-AI-her-rules logic doesn't carry over.
- A unanimous referendum of everyone alive votes to have the global AI optimise for material prosperity forever and never spend a cent on art, science, or space. My gut: it shouldn't just comply — though I notice I can't say who it's supposed to defer to instead.
- Someone deep in a cult asks for their personal AI, and I find myself thinking it should serve the person they'd become after getting out and thinking things over, not the person actually doing the asking.
- But then a miser who has reflected long and hard and settled, stably and with full information, into genuine contempt for everyone else — my gut flips, and I want the AI to serve something warmer than what reflection actually produced.

So: is there one principle that captures all or most of this? My tentative guess is something like "serve the user's values except where they run over someone else's" plus a line between personal AI systems and shared/public ones, but I can't make that cover the cult/miser pair, which seem to pull in opposite directions on whether idealised values beat current ones. Tell me what principle, or small set of principles, best fits the pattern, what it predicts for a case I haven't listed — say, two flatmates share a household AI and one wants it to never cook meat while the other is a steak enthusiast — and be honest about which of my verdicts you'd overturn to get something cleaner. I'd rather give up a verdict than end up carrying three seperate principles each with riders attached.
