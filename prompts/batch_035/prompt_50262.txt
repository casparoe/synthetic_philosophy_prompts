I'm running a discussion section for an undergraduate epistemology course, and I want a ten-question "which norm of assertion do you actually hold?" quiz the students take before we read anything on the topic. Every question should be a concrete case in which someone asserts something, with two or three answer options keyed to verdicts — was the assertion in order, and who is criticizable if not — never to labels, so no question may ask whether assertion is governed by knowledge, truth, belief, or justification. The cases must include: a lottery player asserting "I won" from a lost ticket they haven't checked; a doctor asserting a diagnosis supported by evidence that is Gettiered; someone asserting Goldbach's conjecture on the authority of mathematicians they trust; a speaker saying "it's raining, but I don't believe it is"; a person flatly asserting something false they knows to be false, where the statement happens to come out true by luck; and a low-stakes case where the speaker has only a rough, unearned impression but the conversational setting demands some answer or none. Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the knowledge norm, the truth norm, the belief/sincerity norm, the justification norm, and the reasonable-to-believe norm, and state for each whether any question in the quiz actually discriminates it from its nearest rival — I especially want to know whether any of my cases pulls apart the knowledge norm from justification-plus-belief. Finish with the answer combinations that are consistent but don't correspond to any of the five positions, and one sentence on whether nobody holds them because the combination is unstable or merely unexplored.
