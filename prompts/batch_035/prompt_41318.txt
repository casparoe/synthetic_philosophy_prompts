For an upcoming metaphysics workshop, I've been asked to circulate a one-page position statement, and I'd like a consistency check before I send it. Here are my five commitments:

1. Truth is deflationary: "true" is a device for expressing infinite conjunctions and the like, not the name of a substantive property.
2. Quantifier variance: there are multiple equally good meanings of the existential quantifier, such that "There are tables" and "There are no tables" can both be correct relative to different languages, and the dispute over composite objects is resolved by choosing a language rather than by discovering facts about the world.
3. Scientific realism: "There are electrons" states a mind-independent fact, and the dispute over electrons is not in any way verbal — it was settled by physics, and settled correctly.
4. Joint-carving: it is an objective, language-independent matter which of our predicates carve nature at the joints — "electron" does, "bald" does not.
5. The variance claim in (2) is itself a substantive, factual thesis about the relation between language and the world — something that could turn out to be wrong, not a mere verbal maneuver or stipulation.

Go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how badly, ranking the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs; also flag any pair that only looks to be in tension and explain why the appearance dissolves. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most overall. Please answer in American English, and end with a final line of the form "Verdict: " naming the single commitment I should give up, or "Verdict: no revision needed" if you think the package stands.
