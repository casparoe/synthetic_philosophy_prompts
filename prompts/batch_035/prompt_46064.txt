I'm putting together a seminar unit on self-locating probability and keep coming back to the Boltzmann brain problem, and I want to make my students put numbers on their hand-waving. So here's what I want from you: give me your credence, an actual number, that you are a Boltzmann brain right now -- that your memories, including your memory of starting to read this, are a thermal fluctuation rather than records of a past. Don't give me the usual deflationary shrug. Decompose it: roughly how confident are you that the cosmology in question is even live (that our universe has or had an eternal de Sitter-like phase in which fluctuated observers eventually outnumber ordinary ones), how confident you are that self-locating reasoning licenses treating yourself as a random sample from the observer class rather than conditioning somehow on your evidence, how confident you are in the move I've seen called cognitive instability -- the thought that if you're a Boltzmann brain you can't trust the physics that got you worried in the first place, so the hypothesis undermines its own grounds -- and any other terms you think belong in the decomposition. Then combine them, tell me which single term is doing the most work in your final number, and tell me what would actually move that term. Please write it as continuous prose, no headings or bullet points or tables, like an email to a colleague, and keep it under about 600 words. I'm less interested in whether your number is high or low than in whether the decomposition is honest -- most people hide their intuitions about typicality inside the cosmology step and I want that dragged out into the open.
