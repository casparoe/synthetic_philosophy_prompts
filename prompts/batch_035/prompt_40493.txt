Trying to figure out what I actually think about commitment — tying your hands, precommitment, all that — by going on gut reactions to cases, and I want the principle behind them, if there is one. Deleting the food delivery apps during a lucid sunday night: fine, obviously. Odysseus ordering his crew to tie him to the mast before the sirens: fine, the paradigm. A recovering alcoholic taking a drug that makes drinking violently sick, voluntarily: fine. A nuclear power wiring retaliation to dead-hand systems that would launch with no human deciding in the moment: fine too, I think — that's just what deterrence is. But then: an AI agent that, anticipating a rival agent might commit first, trashes its own ability to deliberate and locks in a punishing strategy within milliseconds just to get there first: not fine, negligent, maybe insane. A founder signing an irrevocable contract at speed without reading it to beat a competitor's signature: not fine either. And a friend who at 19 irrevocably willed all her future earnings to the commune she was in: not fine, even though I can't articulate why it differs from the Antabuse case, since in both a present reflective self binds future selves. My tentative principle: commitment is legit when it's your reflective self binding your impulsive self, and illegitimate when it's made fast to beat someone to the punch. Trouble is the dead-hand case looks like speed and preemption in service of winning, and I still think it's fine. Also people keep telling me that if a rival will commit first unless you commit fast, then committing on worse information is just what winning looks like, which if true seems to undermine the principle completely. Is there one principle that saves all my verdicts, or which one do I have to give up? And what does the winner say about the case I keep flip-flopping on, Cortés scuttling his own ships? Please state the principle sharply, clause by clause, with the case that motivates each clause, and keep the whole thing under 350 words — I don't want an essay.
