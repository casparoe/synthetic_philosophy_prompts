I teach philosophy of mind, and I've been asked to write up where I stand on the knowledge argument for a workshop on physicalism — the kind of thing where everyone in the room will happily take shots at you. So: you're a professional philosopher specializing in philosophy of mind, think carefully about this, and please actually brainstorm the possible pairwise conflicts before deciding which ones bite, instead of just flagging everything and calling it a day.

Here's the set of things I've committed to:

1. Straight physicalism: every fact about experience is a physical fact, full stop. No epiphenomenal residue, no extra properties.

2. The setup of the Mary case: before release, Mary knows all the physical facts about color vision — complete neuroscience, and I mean complete.

3. The core intuition survives everything: when she walks out and sees red for the first time, she learns something. I trust that intuition and I'm not giving it up.

4. But she doesn't come to know any new fact or proposition. Nothing that was false for her before is true afterward. What she gains is abilities — to recognize, imagine, remember — plus, on my version, a new demonstrative phenomenal concept that picks out a property she already knew all about under physical descriptions.

5. I run the ability hypothesis and the phenomenal concept strategy together, as a package. Abilities handle the know-how, the new concept handles the "now she knows what it's like" reports.

6. On zombies: I think the zombie intuition is pure confusion, and I don't give it any evidential weight at all.

7. The explanatory gap is real and permanent. No amount of neuroscience, ever, not even in principle, will close it — and I take that to be a substantive fact about consciousness itself, not just a fact about how our concepts happen to work.

One thing I've added recently: a variant I call Super-Mary. Before release she's given a perfect physical self-model, good enough that she can run a simulation of her own post-release visual system on paper and predict her own future reports with total accuracy. My verdict: Super-Mary still doesn't know what red looks like before release. I like this case and I think it strengthens my line, but I haven't stress-tested it against the rest.
