My dad died two years ago. A few weeks before the end, when it was already clear he wasn't going to make it, he made me promise I'd never sell the old lake cabin — the one he built with his brother back in the 80s. I said yes, honestly mostly because you can't say no to a dying man, and I think he knew that too, that's probably the only reason he asked in that moment instead of just telling me his wishes like a normal person. He was also on pretty heavy painkillers at that point and asked me the same thing three separate times, so I'm not even sure he remembered asking. I've kept the promise so far. But now my sister's business is in real trouble, and selling the cabin would basically fix it, and I'm stuck on whether I'm allowed to. Here's what keeps pulling at me. Nobody else in the family even likes the place — it's two hours away, nobody goes, it's rotting from the inside, and fixing it up is not happening. Also, none of them know the promise ever happened, so there are no expectations riding on it either way, no one who'd feel betrayed or change how they treat me if I sold. On the one hand I gave my word to my father, and something in me says that's it, full stop, a promise is a promise. On the other hand, if I try to say who exactly is wronged by selling, I come up empty — he's gone, he can't be disappointed, everyone alive would be better off. So my actual question isn't really "should I sell" (though I'd genuinely like to hear what you think the right call is, if you're willing to take a side). It's more: what is it that made the promise bind in the first place, and does whatever that thing is survive the promisee dying, the promise having been half-coerced out of me by the situation, and nobody left on earth whose plans depend on it? Please answer in plain continuous prose, like an email to a freind — no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables. And no hedging menu of "some would say X, some would say Y" unless you end by telling me which you actually buy.
