I run a small horror film night at my place — tenish people, a mix of grad students and civilians, we watch a movie and argue about it till 2am. Next month is A Quiet Place, and I want to run the discussion through Lewis on convention (the 1969 book) and the evolution of norms, which I half remeber from one undergrad course. Set up the reading for me before I open the floor.

The hook I want to explore: the family's whole life — the barefoot sand paths, eating with their hands off leaves so nothing clatters, the felt pieces in the board game, the sign language, the colored warning lights on the stairs — looks exactly like a web of Lewisian conventions. Recurring coordination problem, everyone better off if everyone conforms, and everyone knows that everyone knows it. But here's the thing that bugs me: for Lewis, conventions persist because each person conforms given the expectation that others conform and prefers to conform given that. In the movie the enforcer isn't the mutual expectations — it's the blind monsters. Lee would have to tiptoe even if he were alone on the farm. So is the silence regime a convention at all, or just fear? Does the Lewisian analysis survive having its enforcement outsourced to a predator? I want the group to actually chew on this, not nod along.

Then the specific things I want the reading to hit: which single scene should carry the whole interpretation (the toy space shuttle at the start? the family dinner? the bathtub birth?) and what that scene needs to show for the Lewis lens to grip; whether the family's rules are arbitray in Lewis's sense — driving on the left vs the right counts as arbitrary because the alternative is equally good, but are there equally good alternatives to silence the family could have converged on instead, and does it matter that the alternatives are worse?; what to do with Regan's deafness and the family's pre-existing sign language, which looks like a convention that just happened to become load-bearing once the world changed; and what the ending, where the hearing aid feedback turns out to disable the creatures, says about how norms get revised — is that a new convention being invented in real time, or is it something Lewis's framework simply doesn't cover? And I want the honest version: where does the film resist the reading, because I don't want to turn it into an illustration and one of the grad students will call me on it if I try.

Give me the full reading as prose first, then close with a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields: "verdict" (one of "lewisian-convention", "merely-enforced-norm", or "hybrid", plus a one-sentence reason); "carry_scene" (the scene that carries the reading and why it beats the alternatives); "arbitrariness_call" (whether the rules are arbitrary in Lewis's sense and what turns on it); "scenes" (an array of up to five objects with "scene", "what_it_shows", and "resistance" — where the reading strains against that scene); "strongest_counterpoint" (the best case that the Lewis lens fails here, one or two sentences); "discussion_questions" (three questions that will actually start an argument among people who've had two beers); and "what_the_film_adds" (one thing the movie dramatizes about convention and norm evolution that Lewis's formal treatment itself doesn't).
