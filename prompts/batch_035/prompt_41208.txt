Like many people here, I've spent years on LessWrong and adjacent forums, so the vocabulary I actually think in is the house one: utility functions, revealed preferences, optimization targets, budget constraints, agent-environment framings, coherent extrapolated volition, QALY-style aggregation. I'd like the "equality of what?" debate restated in those terms. Specifically, take the three main answers — equality of welfare, Dworkin's equality of resources (including his distinction between brute luck and option luck, and the envy test), and Sen's capability approach (resources versus what people can actually do with them) — and translate each into my vocabulary. Welfare egalitarianism presumably becomes something like equalizing a utility scalar across agents, resource egalitarianism something like equalizing the budget set each agent faces before optimization begins, and the capability view something about the size and shape of the feasible action space, or the mapping from resources to outcomes under heterogeneous architectures. Do this as faithfully as you can, flagging where you have to distort to make it fit.

Then I want the residue report. Which parts of the philosophical debate translate cleanly, which survive only in distorted form, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? I'm especially interested in whether the following are expressible on my side: expensive tastes (one agent's utility function prices the same goods much higher), adaptive or preference-formation problems (an agent whose utility function was shaped by scarcity, so equalizing measured utility ratifies the scarcity), and Dworkin's insistence that we compensate people for unchosen endowments but not for the results of chosen gambles. And finally, the diagnostic question: where there's residue, is it because the rationalist framework is missing something real, or because the philosophical position was less crisp than it looked on paper? I'd rather know which of my commitments I'd have to give up than get a diplomatic answer.
