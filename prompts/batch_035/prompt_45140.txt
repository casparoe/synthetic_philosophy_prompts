I'm an undergraduate currently taking a philosophy of mind course, and our unit on the epiphenomenalist picture of consciousness has kept returning to one objection: the problem of phenomenal judgment. The worry, as we've formulated it in seminar, is that if qualia are generated by the brain but causally idle with respect to the physical world, then when I sincerely say "that looks red to me," the utterance is produced entirely by neural processes that never touch the quale itself, so my introspective reports cannot count as evidence about what my experiences are like — which seems plainly absurd, since our reports about experience are about the most reliable things we have. The standard epiphenomenalist reply, as our course presented it, is that the same neural machinery produces both the quale and the report in lawful correlation, so the reports are reliable even though the quale contributes nothing to them.

For my term essay, I want to argue that this objection is not uniquely a problem for epiphenomenalism, and I need a designed case to carry the argument. Please construct a thought experiment in which it is interactionist dualism, rather than epiphenomenalism, that has the harder time explaining the reliability of first-person phenomenal reports. The asymmetry I want the case to exploit is this: on the interactionist picture, the causal chain from stimulus to verbal report runs partly through the quale itself, so the report's reliability is hostage to the reliability of qualia as causal intermediaries — an extra assumption the epiphenomenalist never needs, since on their view the reports inherit their reliability from ordinary neural mechanisms while the qualia are merely correlated passengers. That is the reverse of the usual direction of the objection, which is exactly the point of the essay.

Constraints: the scenario should be realistic — a laboratory or clinical setting, and no demons, perfect predictors, zombie twins, inverted spectra, or possible-world duplicates; it should be presentable in a single paragraph; and the interactionist's difficulty must emerge from the causal efficacy of qualia as such, not from stipulating that the qualia in the case are deceptive, malfunctioning, or otherwise abnormal. After presenting the scenario, do the following in order. First, walk through what each theory predicts and explains about the reliability of the subject's reports, making sure the asymmetry is generated by the theories rather than baked into the description — my instructor will be checking precisely for whether the verdicts are derived or smuggled in. Second, identify which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to the asymmetry if that feature is removed. Third, give the interactionist's best defense: where in the setup they would most plausibly dig in and refuse to concede, and what it would cost them. Finally, tell me whether the case survives a simplification pass — if I strip out every detail a critic could call question-begging against interactionism, does it still generate the asymmetry, or does it collapse back into an ordinary case where both theories explain reliability equally well? I would rather learn that the case cannot be made to work than submit one that wins by rigging the description.
