I'm writing a paper section arguing that Sen's liberal paradox — the result that no social decision procedure can both honor the Pareto principle and give each of two individuals decisive say over at least one pair of social states differing only in that individual's own sphere — has been made to look like a pathology of nosiness. In the canonical illustrations (the prude, the Lewd, and Lady Chatterley's Lover, or the neighbors with strong views about the color of each other's houses), the other-regarding preference that closes the cycle is censorious or competitive: somebody wants somebody else to have less of something. My thesis is that the valence of the intrusion is doing no real work, and I want a case that proves it. Build me one.

Construct a thought experiment in which the paradox closes even though every other-regarding preference in the case is benevolent: each meddler wants the other person to have more of a good, not less, and would bear a real cost to secure it. The benevolence must be sincere rather than paternalism in disguise — include at least one concrete detail, some action the meddler takes or forgoes, that shows it. Specifications:

1. Ordinary, contemporary setting; at least the two decisive individuals the construction needs (bystanders allowed if they earn their place); each decisive person has a recognizable personal sphere — but not the reading of a book, since the classic's shadow is too long. Something like a garden, a recipe, a Saturday, a move, a tattoo.
2. No exotic psychology. The preference profile must be one you could find in two people you know, and each person's ranking must be a genuine ordering: transitive, self-consistent, and explicable in a sentence or two of ordinary motivation.
3. Derive the cycle; don't stipulate it. Enumerate the alternatives, mark which pairs of them differ only in one person's sphere, state each person's full ranking over the alternatives, and then walk the social preference edge by edge: which liberty-based choice generates which social preference, which unanimous preference generates which, and where the cycle closes. Confirm explicitly that the Pareto edge you use is unanimous — both rankings, not one.
4. The sting must survive the reader's warm feelings. By the end, no one should have done anything a decent person would regret, and yet respecting each person's say over their own sphere and honoring unanimity must be jointly impossible. If the case needs someone to be a little awful in order to work, it has failed my purpose.
5. Address the obvious exit: two people this fond of each other could just talk it over and revise their preferences until the conflict dissolves. Either build the case so that exit is unavailable (decisions made privately, in parallel, on a schedule that forecloses renegotiation) or so that it remains available but is what it is — giving up on the procedure rather than satisfying it. Say which you did.
6. It must not be the prude-and-Lewd scenario with the valence flipped and the book swapped for something else. At least one structural feature — the number of decisive pairs in play, the location of the Pareto edge, the shape of the self-regarding preferences — must genuinely differ, and you should name the difference.

After the case and its derivation: (a) identify which feature of the profile is doing the work — is it the benevolent intrusions themselves, or something structural that benevolence happened not to touch, and how you can tell; (b) say what happens if that feature is removed: does the case collapse back into the classic structure, dissolve entirely, or turn into a mere coordination problem with a negotiable outcome; (c) give the strongest one-paragraph reply available to a defender of unrestricted Pareto (unanimity is unanimity, and other-regarding preferences still count) and to a defender of the liberal condition (a sphere is a sphere precisely so that what others want there is irrelevant), each as deployed against your case specifically.

Format: the scenario itself in one paragraph, before any analysis. Then whatever structure you need for the derivation and the replies. Your final line must be exactly "Verdict: " followed by one of four labels — UPHOLD LIBERTY, RESTRICT PARETO, REVISE PREFERENCES, REDEFINE SPHERES — naming the escape route you judge best for the case you built, with nothing after it.
