I would like a villanelle on the paradox of fiction, written in the voice of a reader partway through a novel. The two refrains should carry the two halves of the paradox between them: one refrain states the flat cognitive verdict — that the person on the page never existed, that nothing is actually happening to anyone — and the other reports the grief anyway. Each of the five tercets should give one standard resolution of the paradox its turn and show it failing: the appeal to make-believe (I am only pretending she is real, so nothing is amiss), the appeal to thoughts without belief (I can entertain the thought of her death without believing anything), and the dismissal of the emotion as a shallow, pale imitation of the real thing. I am content for you to choose which resolutions fill the remaining tercets, as long as each is a recognizable position in the literature. The closing quatrain should put the two refrains side by side without letting either cancel the other, since the point of the form is that the paradox reasserts itself every time the reminder returns. Please follow the villanelle form strictly — five tercets and a concluding quatrain, with the refrains alternating and then joined in the final couplet — but keep the meter loose enough for a contemporary voice. Someone who knows the literature on the paradox of fiction should be able to tell which tercet corresponds to which resolution, and someone who does not should still find it a good poem about reading. After the poem, append a JSON object with the following fields: "refrain_one" and "refrain_two" (the two refrain lines, verbatim); "tercet_positions" (an array of five strings, each naming and giving a one-sentence characterization of the resolution of the paradox dramatized in the corresponding tercet); "turn" (the line number at which the poem's central turn occurs, with a one-sentence gloss on what turns); and "unresolved" (one sentence on exactly what tension the final couplet leaves standing).
