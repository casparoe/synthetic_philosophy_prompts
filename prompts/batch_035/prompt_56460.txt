I'm in a bioethics elective this semester (philosophy dept, mostly juniors/seniors) and we just started the unit on organ sales and repugnant markets. I keep staring at the actual pattern of american law and I can't reverse-engineer the principle behind it, so I want to run it past you.

Here's the pattern as I understand it. Federal law since the 80s makes it a crime to sell a kidney (or any organ), but you can donate one for free, including to a complete stranger, and there are actual ad campaigns encouraging you to do it. Meanwhile, in the same country: you can be paid to donate plasma, and the clinics literally run promotions like new donor bonuses; egg donors are paid five figures; you can be paid a few grand to enroll in a phase 1 drug trial where the entire scientific purpose is to find out whether the drug hurts you; and paid surrogacy is legal in a bunch of states. Hair, you can sell. Blood, mostly not, except plasma. So the line can't be about bodily risk, because giving up a whole kidney for nothing is fine and getting pumped full of an experimental molecule for money is fine — it's the combination of that particular body part and that particular reason (money for a lifesaving transplant) that's supposedly intolerable.

And then the thing that really gets me: I've read that most countries that ban paying their own plasma donors still import massive amounts of plasma products from the US, so their health systems are buying paid-for plasma, just with the payment laundered through the American supply chain. Their citizens receive the commodified thing; they just don't have to watch the transaction.

My professor keeps pushing us to "name the principle the practice is committed to" and honestly I can't tell the practice is committed to anything. Candidate principles I can think of all seem to explain one slice and blow up on another: (a) some body parts are so person-involving that money demeans them, but then why are eggs ok and kidneys not; (b) markets in body parts exploit the desperate poor, but a phase 1 trial population is about as desperate as it gets; (c) certain exchanges are only legitimate as gifts and payment destroys the meaning, but plasma is bought every day and nobody seems to think the meaning died. My paper is due in two weeks and I don't want to do the standard survey where I summarize Sandel and Satz and Debra Satz's constraints and end with "there are tensions here" — that's what everyone in my section is doing.

Also flag whether you think the whole thing is philosophically underdetermined, i.e. several different theories could license the same patchwork and there's no fact of the matter about which one the law "really" presupposes — but if you say that, still tell me which reconstruction you'd bet on and why.
