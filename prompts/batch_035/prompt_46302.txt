I'm preparing a position statement on standing to blame for a workshop on moral responsibility, and before circulating it I want an external check on whether the theses I've committed to on paper actually fit together. Here is the set as I've stated it:

(1) Hypocritical blame is not merely in poor taste or dialectically awkward; it is wrongful, and wrongful because the hypocrite lacks standing to blame — a normative status that can come apart from whether the target deserves blame at all.

(2) Standing is lost only through the blamer's own conduct — paradigmatically, culpably doing the very thing one blames another for — and never through anything the target of blame does, believes, or consents to.

(3) The wrong in blame without standing is best explained on the model of unfairness: the hypocrite holds another to a standard she is herself flouting, and so treats herself as exempt from a demand she is pressing on an equal.

(4) A hypocrite can recover standing through sincere acknowledgment of her own fault — naming it herself, in the same breath as the blame — even if she has not yet changed her conduct.

(5) Blame can also be wrongful for lack of standing when it is deployed manipulatively — to deflect attention, to bargain, to establish dominance — even when the blamer is no hypocrite and the blame is accurate.

(6) Standing attaches to persons and their relations rather than to individual acts of blame: the problem with the hypocrite is not that this or that utterance was inappropriate, but that she is, for a time, the wrong kind of party to press the demand.

My own working sense is that (4) and (5) are where I'm least secure, but I don't want to prejudice the audit. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look to be, and in what order of severity — please rank the tensions rather than presenting them as equally grave. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give, and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the set. Finish by identifying the single revision that would restore the most coherence overall.
