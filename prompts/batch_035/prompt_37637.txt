I teach a mid-level political philosophy seminar and I want an in-class sorting quiz -- "which luck egalitarian are you actually?" -- because half my students picked up a label in week 3 and have been coasting on it since. So: about ten questions, each one a concrete case with two or three options, phrased so you can answer without knowing any of the names, and no question that just asks "do you think people are responsible for their choices?" because that's pattern-matching to the label, which is exactly what I'm trying to diagnose.

I want the cases to be ones where the internal disagreements show up, not just the ones that separate luck egalitarians from everyone else. So make sure you include: the gambler who squanders their income versus the person born with a disability they could have hypothetically insured against; someone whose religious commitments require expensive or risky observances; a person with genuinely expensive tastes they never chose; the able-bodied surfer who could work but prefers not to; and someone who "chose" the only dangerous job in a town where that was the only job going. Maybe a talents case too, where the question is whether we compensate the person who could have developed a talent but never got the chance.

Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto actual positions: Dworkin's resource version with its hypothetical insurance market, Arneson-style opportunity for welfare, the variant that treats even preferences and character as brute luck, a plain Rawlsian who never buys the responsibility apparatus in the first place, and -- this is important -- the escape branch for students whose answers reveal they never really accepted that responsibility cuts claims at all, and who should come out as relational or democratic egalitarians rather than any flavor of luck egalitarian. Flag the answer combinations no actual philosopher defends, so I can ask in class whether each empty corner is empty because the view is incoherent or just because nobody bothered. One of my grad students did something like this for distributive justice generally and it was recieved really well, so the format works -- I just need this one done properly for the luck egalitarian debate.
