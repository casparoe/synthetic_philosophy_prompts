I write novels, and for the last three of them Odile has been my French translator; our friendliest arguments have always turned on her decisions about form. Her track record, as I would summarize it: in my first book a rhymed epitaph closed the story, and she kept the rhyme scheme while letting the sense of two lines drift, telling me the rhyme was the lid on the coffin. In the second, a character named Wright carried a running right/Wright joke, and she renamed him outright — altered a settled fact of the novel — so that a pun would land in French. A third book buried an acrostic in its chapter openings, spelling the dead sister's name; she preserved it, twisting several French sentences to manage it, while conceding that most readers would never notice, on the grounds that the acrostic was for the book rather than for the reader. And where two teenagers traded improvised insults in dense internal rhyme, she kept the rhyme and let the specific jibes go generic, because the speed of the exchange was the scene. We have both read Hofstadter's Le Ton beau de Marot, his book built around endlessly varied translations of a small rondeau Marot sent to a sick young woman, and I have never managed to settle whether Odile takes from it the lesson that form is the music or that the tone is. My fourth novel now contains a set piece I am anxious about: a chapter narrating a boy's paper round in mock-heroic rhymed couplets, the comedy living entirely in the gap between the lofty form and the trivial errand. Odile warns that in French the corresponding couplet form reads as solemn, so the gap may collapse into plain pomposity. Her options, as I understand them, are to keep dignified rhymed couplets and trust the bathos to survive, or to abandon the couplets and rebuild the mock effect in free prose — epic vocabulary and periphrastic grandeur applied to bicycles and dogs. Can you predict what she will decide? Please lay out the rule or rules her record actually supports — including any that fit it equally well but would diverge on this case — say which she is probably running and why, and name the further case that would best tell those rules apart. And be blunt with me: if her verdicts don't cohere enough to predict anything, or if I have framed her options badly, say so plainly rather than conjuring a tidy theory on my behalf.
