and to be clear, i'm not fishing for a generic list of anti-utilitarian intuitions (pushing the fat man off the bridge and so on) — i mean objections specific to the theorem-as-argument. e.g. i've gathered that the ex ante vs ex post distinction matters somehow, that Diamond had this example with two people where the setup seems to force indifference between a coin flip deciding who suffers and one person suffering for sure twice, and that the theorem needs interpersonal utility comparisons that expected utility theory by itself can't actually deliver. does any of that sink the argument, or is it a fixable gap?

also curious how much the whole thing quietly rests on treating each person's claim as entering the picture only through their own utility function. if you thought people's interests were genuinely entangled — caring about each other's welfare as part of your own, shared projects — does the aggregation still go through, or does the theorem secretly assume seperability of persons and just never advertises it?
