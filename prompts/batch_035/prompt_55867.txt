I'm a psychiatry resident and I keep getting dragged into arguments about what "dysfunction" means when we say a disorder reflects a dysfunction of some mental mechanism — everyone waves at "the function the mechanism was designed for" but nobody says what function means, and it turns out there are two big camps in the phil of bio literature that can't agree either. Before I go read them, I want the spec first.

Write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of biological function has to deliver, with the case behind each requirement. Make sure the list covers at least the things I'd want it for — deciding when a trait is malfunctioning vs just doing something I don't like, telling me what the function of a trait is when natural selection built it for one thing and it now does another (like feathers maybe starting as insulation and ending up as flight), handling vestigial stuff like the appendix where the original function is long gone, and not saying something absurd about pest insects evolving resistance in response to a pesticide (did the resistance mechanisms have the "function" of resisting DDT before DDT existed? seems like the etiologists have to say something weird here and I want the requirement stated so that this is a real test).

Also include whatever constraints you think the theory has to respect that come from elsewhere — can't contradict evolutionary biology, has to handle cases where a mechanism is working exactly as selected for but the output is harmful to the organism, etc.

Then flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied — I have a feeling that "the function must explain why the trait is there" and "we must be able to attribute functions to newly useful traits with no selection history" pull against each other, and if some subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable, say so plainly, because that's the part I most want to see.

Don't leave me with a menu. Pick which desiderata you'd actually hold firm on for the purpose I care about (grounding dysfunction claims in clinical contexts), say which ones you'd drop as prejudices of one camp, and commit to which theory — the selected-effects side or the causal-role side — best satisfies your trimmed-down list, and own the counterintuitive consequences of that choice. Max 550 words, and no reading lists, I want your verdicts not a tour of the literature.
