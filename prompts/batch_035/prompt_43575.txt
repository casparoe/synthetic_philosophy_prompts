(a) The procreation asymmetry says it's wrong to bring into existence a person whose life will be worth not living, but there's no obligation to bring into existence a person whose life would be worth living. If you don't create a happy person, you haven't wronged anyone, since there's no one there to be deprived of the happiness. But creating a miserable person is a harm to that person. Peter Singer introduced this in Reasons and Persons.

(b) The argument comes from the person-affecting restriction, which says morality is about making things better or worse for particular people, and this is basically the same idea as the non-identity problem. Since the person who would have been born happy doesn't exist and never suffers from not existing, there's nothing bad about not creating them. The person who is born miserable does exist and does suffer, so creating them is bad for them. This explains why we feel worse about the one than the other.

(c) A total utilitarian could accommodate the asymmetry pretty easily with a small tweak: only sum up negative welfare, ignore positive welfare. Then creating a miserable person lowers the total and is forbidden, while creating a happy person leaves it unchanged, so there's no duty to do it. This is just the total view with a minor adjustment, so the total utilitarian is basically fine. It also follows that a couple who has a 50% chance of having a child with a bad life definitely shouldn't have the child, since the asymmetry forbids creating a miserable person.
