I am a novelist four years into a book set at the edge of the Neolithic transition — a valley where the last foraging band watches its neighbors break soil, fence off fields, and store grain against the winter. My narrator, an old woman of the band, has lately taken to contempt for the farmers, and I have let her borrow a line that keeps surfacing in my research: the title of Jared Diamond's 1987 Discover essay, which pronounces agriculture "the worst mistake in the history of the human race." The essay's evidence I can manage — skeletal health, work hours, the Bushman who asked why he should farm "when there are so many mongongo nuts in the world." What unsettles me is how easily the title has detached itself from the essay and hardened into a proverb, repeated now by people with entirely different grievances — against civilization, against bread, against the alarm clock.

Before I let my narrator lean on it, I need to know what the sentence is actually asserting. Please lay out, as a numbered list, the distinct precise claims "the worst mistake in the history of the human race" could be expressing. Worst for whom — the first farmers, their immediate descendants, everyone since, or humanity considered as a single agent making a single choice? Measured how — health, freedom, equality, daily contentment, or welfare summed across all lives ever lived? Over what timescale, and against which counterfactual: a world in which no one ever farmed, or one in which the transition came later or unfolded differently? Some of these readings should turn out to be near-trivial, some substantive and defensible, some demonstrably false, and some not really claims about agriculture at all. Give each a one-line verdict.

Then tell me plainly which reading my narrator requires if her contempt is to be earned rather than inherited, and whether she can have it without the novel quietly endorsing the sentimental idea that her band's world was better because it was about to disappear. Keep the whole thing under 650 words; I will read every one of them.
