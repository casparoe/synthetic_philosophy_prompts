I spent thirty-seven years as a condensed-matter physicist, and since retiring I have been sitting in on a philosophy reading group — retired scientists and a few academics, meeting fortnightly over a decade's worth of texts we should have read long ago. This term we have reached philosophy of history, and last session the discussion turned to Hempel's idea that historical explanation works by subsuming events under general laws. I confess the model struck me as naive from the outset, but before I could say why, one of our members — a retired diplomat, fond of closing arguments rather than opening them — pronounced that history is just one damn thing after another, and everyone nodded as though the matter had been settled. The famous quip was left unattributed and unexamined, which bothered me for the rest of the evening.

I want to know what that one-liner actually claims. In my field we also deal with regularities that hold only ceteris paribus, and only above some scale of description; nobody says the rheology of a suspension is "just one damn thing after another" merely because it resists tidy microreduction. So I suspect the slogan is doing several quite different jobs, only some of which would support what the group wanted it to support. Please lay out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing — from the trivial (whatever happened, happened after what happened before it?) through readings about the absence of exceptionless generalizations, the role of ceteris paribus laws, the idea that narrative rather than subsumption is the vehicle of historical understanding, and whatever anti-determinist or anti-predictive claims it might carry. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or simply false, and — this is the part I most want — say which of the readings would actually license dismissing the covering-law model for history while leaving the likes of geophysics, epidemiology, and evolutionary biology intact. It seems to me that several natural readings prove far too much: if history is one damn thing after another because its events are unrepeatable, then so is every extinction event in the fossil record, and I would want to know what the historian's dismissal has left standing. Present the readings as an ordered menu I can bring to our next meeting, with your verdict on each and on which one the people who repeat the line most likely have in mind, whether they know it or not.
