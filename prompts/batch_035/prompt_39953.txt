writing a term paper for my phil of science seminar and want to sanity check my overall position on laws of nature before I commit to it. here's roughly where I stand:

(1) I buy the best-system view: laws are whatever theorems come out of the systematization of the total mosaic of particular facts — all the local, qualitatively specific stuff spread across spacetime — that best balances simplicity, strength, and fit. nothing governs anything, nothing necessitates anything, there's just the pattern and our summary of it.

(2) laws genuinely explain their instances. when I ask why this sample of gas expanded when heated and someone cites the relevant laws plus initial conditions, that answer is doing real explanatory work — it's not just restating the facts in compressed form.

(3) laws are fully contingent. a world with a different distribution of matter just automatically has different laws, and there's no further fact about which laws it "had to" have.

(4) fundamental physics involves objective single-case chances — when a radium atom is sitting there, there's a real fact that it has roughly a 50/50 chance of decaying within one half-life, and that's not just a statement about our ignorance.

(5) counterfactuals are grounded in our world's laws plus the actual facts — what would have happened if I'd knocked this cup off the table is fixed by the actual laws of this world, no more and no less.
