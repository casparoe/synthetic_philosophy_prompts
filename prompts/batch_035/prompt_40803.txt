the absent-minded driver problem has been living rent free in my head ever since someone necro'd one of the old decision theory threads. quick setup so we're on the same page: a driver passes two identical intersections and can't remember whether he's already passed one, so at whichever intersection he's at he doesn't know if it's the first or the second. exiting at the first gets him payoff 0, exiting at the second gets 4, driving past both gets 1. ex ante, the optimal plan is to continue with probability 2/3 at each intersection, expected payoff 4/3. the fun part: if he's standing at an intersection and takes his strategy-implied credences at face value (3/5 it's the first, 2/5 it's the second, if he sticks to the 2/3 plan), then exiting now looks better than continuing — which is exactly piccione and rubinstein's original point that the optimal plan is unstable under interim reflection, and then aumann, hart and perry wrote a response arguing the interim reasoning is fallacious and the driver should just stick to the plan, and a whole literature about self-referential probabilities and multi-selves followed.

my friend and i have been fighting about this for weeks. he's a dyed-in-the-wool one-boxer type and thinks it's trivially solved by updatelessness: your decision is a policy over observations, there's one driver, so evaluate policies ex ante, pick continue-2/3, and the fact that it "looks bad" from inside one node is just the same illusion as two-boxing. he's pretty sure one of the early UDT posts on lesswrong even used this exact problem as a warm-up. i keep feeling this is too fast: the driver's self-locating probability literally depends on his own strategy, "what should i do NOW" seems like a question that deserves an answer rather than a policy shrug, and i don't actually know if the aumann-hart-perry argument goes through or just quietly assumes the conclusion.

so give me your actual credence that the correct advice to the driver at the intersection is "continue with probability 2/3" — i.e. that the planning-optimal answer is also the right interim answer — as opposed to "exit", "deviate to some other mix", or "there's no well-defined answer". for reference i'm at maybe 0.55 and my friend is at like 0.9. then decompose: how much rides on whether policy/updateless decision theory is the right framework for agents who can't distinguish their decision nodes, how much on whether the strategy-implied self-locating credences are well-defined or viciously circular, how much on whether the stick-to-the-plan argument actually works or is a modeling artifact, how much on whether "what should i do now" even has determinate content for an agent with amnesia, plus anything else you think belongs. number and one line of justification for each term, then the combination, then tell me which single sub-question, if i could settle it tomorrow, would move your final answer the most. and if you think my friend and i are actually disagreeing about some third thing neither of us has noticed, i defintely want to hear that too.
