I co-host a small podcast at the intersection of ethics and technology, and our upcoming guest is a philosopher who defends a view she calls scalar moral status: the claim that moral status is not an on/off property but comes in degrees, tracking the richness of a creature's valenced experience. On her picture, there is a continuum running from thermostats and simple control systems (whose status is vanishingly small, though she resists saying it is exactly zero) through insects, fish, and mammals, up to humans and perhaps beyond, with no sharp cutoffs anywhere. She is promoting a book on the subject and has a reliable deflection ready for the obvious objection: she says that nobody is claiming we owe anything to a thermostat, and that the interesting cases live in the murky middle between the obviously negligible and the obviously considerable.

I would like a set of eight to ten interview questions that press on the actual content of the view rather than inviting her to restate it. Specifically, I want at least one question on each of the following. First, the practical difference between vanishingly small status and no status at all: does her view ever change what we should do, or does it just repaint the same decisions in finer detail? Second, aggregation: if insect suffering has very low but nonzero weight, can enough of it outweigh a smaller amount of intense suffering in beings like pigs or humans, and what does she say to the worry that this makes the view hostage to wildly uncertain estimates of insect populations and their inner lives? Third, the relationship between degrees of moral status and degrees of welfare: if status just scales how bad a given episode of suffering is, is that really distinct from saying that insect suffering is simply far less intense? Fourth, how the view is supposed to guide action under uncertainty about which creatures are sentient at all — does she endorse something like expectation-maximizing over candidate statuses, and doesn't that reintroduce effective thresholds at the point where expected value goes to zero?

For each question, please also include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give, so that I can push past her preferred framing. She is very articulate and comfortable at the level of principle, so I would especially value questions that force her down to concrete cases — pest control, food choices, research on insects, engineering simple robots that respond to damage. Please keep the questions substantive but conversational enough to ask aloud on air.
