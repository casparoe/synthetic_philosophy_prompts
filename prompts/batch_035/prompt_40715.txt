Write a press release, roughly 650 words, from the Classification Committee of a fictional psychiatric association announcing the upcoming ninth edition of their diagnostic manual. The big announcement: the manual will now ship with a mandatory companion volume, updated annually, documenting the people the previous edition created. The companion volume describes how the edition-8 criteria produced a patient population that didnt exist before those criteria did — support groups with their own terminology, symptom reports that suspiciously track the exact wording of each criterion, forums lobbying for lower thresholds — and the committee treats all this as a complication for their reliability statistics rather than any kind of conceptual problem. Their proposed fix is field trials in which the raters are themselves members of the classified population. Then, since the companion volume is itself a classification, it too will need its own companion volume, and the press release should let the committee handle this regress with total bureaucratic serenity (a projected publication schedule is fine).

The satire has to be deadpan and from inside the committee: they never for a second doubt that they are discovering kinds rather than manufacturing them, and every sentence should quietly demonstrate the opposite. Land the actual content of looping effects without ever naming them or naming Hacking: classifications of people interact with the people classified; the classified organize and push back (think of how self-advocates have reshaped the categories they inhabit, or how a category can be retired because the people it described made that happen); each revision creates the population that forces the next revision, so the manual is always chasing its own tail; and the committee's response to any of this is always just another edition. Somewhere in there the committee should note, wistfully, that chemists never have this problem — the contrast between kinds of people and kinds of beetles or quarks should be visible in the piece without anyone explaining it. Maybe include a warm boilerplate quote from the fictional committee chair about how this edition they finally got the categories right.

The humor should come from getting the philosophy right, not from making the committee villains — the joke is that their own evidence keeps showing them the loop and they keep responding with more classification, and they should seem competent and well-meaning throughout. A reader who knows the looping-effects / making-up-people literature should find the satire fair and find themself laughing rather than wincing; a reader who doesn't should still find it a funny sendup of a medical bureaucracy. Format it like a real press release — FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE at the top, the chair's quote in the middle, "About the Association" boilerplate and media contact info at the bottom.
