I'm a software engineer at a company that operates a large video-streaming platform, and I sit on the team that owns the recommendation model. I have no philosophy background, so please write for an intelligent layperson rather than assuming I know any technical terminology. Please also answer in British English.

For years our ranking objective has been simple: maximise predicted watch time. Six months ago a product manager on our team proposed we revisit it. Her argument, roughly, is that we can see in the data that a meaningful fraction of users binge low-quality content late at night, then disengage for weeks, and report in surveys that they didn't enjoy much of what they watched. On her view we are not ranking by what people want but by what they are susceptible to. A senior engineer pushed back hard: surveys are noisy, people are famously bad at saying what they want, and watch time is the only honest signal we have. Second-guessing engagement, he argues, means quietly substituting our judgement for the user's — which is arrogant, and a business risk besides. The debate has gone in circles for half a year, and it turned heated when someone accused the other side of thinking they know better than users what is good for them.

I've now been asked to write a design document summarising the arguments and recommending a direction, and I've realised I can't even state the disagreement cleanly. Both sides accept the same behavioural data; they diverge on what it means. So my questions: is there any principled way to distinguish a preference from a bias when the only evidence available is behaviour? Is this dispute secretly about a fact we could in principle discover — what the user really wants — or about the ethics of overriding choices people freely make, or does it sit somewhere the concept of preference itself simply gives out? I don't want product strategy advice as such; I want to understand the structure of the problem well enough to write an honest document. Concretely: what would each side have to demonstrate for the dispute to be resolved, is there any study or experiment that could actually settle it, and if not, why does it resist settling?
