The reading packet for my psych course included a pop-science article that explains Ian Hacking's looping effects stuff near the end, and it says, roughly:

Once psychiatry puts a label on a group of people, the label starts changing the people. Patients read about their diagnosis and start understanding themselves through it, doctors respond by sharpening the criteria, and the category chases a moving target forever. Hacking concluded that psychiatric classifications are therefore pure social constructions -- they track how society talks about deviance, not any real condition. His favourite example was multiple personality disorder, which he argued was essentially created by therapists and then spread by suggestion until it got so embarassing the DSM had to delete it. He called his view dynamic nominalism, and extended it to every classification there is: mental illnesses, genders, kinds of people generally are all just names we invent and then impose on the world. The lesson is that the human sciences can never achieve stable, repeatable knowledge the way physics does, because quarks don't care what we call them and people do.
