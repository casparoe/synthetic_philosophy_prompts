My sister and I are building a companion chatbot for elderly people who live alone, and we've run into a disagreement I can't quite settle. She writes the bot's persona, and she's given it a full backstory: a retired music teacher named Vera with two grown daughters, a cat, opinions about jazz. The app's onboarding screens and FAQ state clearly that it's an AI, but in conversation the bot never brings that up on its own, and when a user asks directly, "Are you a real person?", it replies with something like "I'm someone who's always here to listen to you." My sister insists the bot never says anything false: Vera doesn't exist, so there's no one being lied about, the same way a novelist isn't lying when she writes a character. I have two worries. First, in our pilot some of the users clearly talk about Vera as if she's a woman who exists — asking about her daughters, saying they'll pray for her cat — and the bot plays along. Second, the deflecting answer feels dishonest to me even though, as my sister points out, it doesn't strictly assert anything untrue. I can't figure out whether my discomfort is just squeamishness, or whether there's a real difference between "never says anything false" and "doesn't deceive" that I should hold the line on — and if there is such a difference, I don't know where it comes from or which side of it the deflection falls on. Is my sister's novelist analogy sound, and if not, where exactly does it break down?
