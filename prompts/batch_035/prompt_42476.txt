I'm a PhD student in bioethics, currently stuck on my dissertation chapter on advance directives, and I've realized my problem is that I keep arguing about cases before I know what a theory of them is even supposed to do. The literature is mostly case-slinging -- everyone has their favorite dementia patient or their favorite religious conversion and they wave it at the other side -- and I want to write the spec first, the way you'd write desiderata for any theory: what any adequate account of the authority of the earlier self over the later self has to deliver. Some of the constraints I can see already: it has to say something about the standard example from my grad seminar of the patient who is now demented and seems content but left clear instructions refusing treatment, and it has to do better than just pick the earlier or the later self by fiat; it has to distinguish a directive from a mere statement of current preference (why is writing it down in advance supposed to matter?); it has to handle the fact that values change, sometimes for reasons the earlier self would endorse (I'm thinking of my grandmother, who wrote a fairly stern directive at 65 and then mellowed enormously, and nobody in the family can decide if the directive or the mellowed version counts); and it has to extend (or explain why it doesn't extend) to psychiatric advance directives and other Ulysses-style arrangements, where the whole point seems to be that the binding self expects to resist at the time.

What I'd love from you is the full requirements list: the cases it has to handle, the distinctions it can't collapse -- e.g., between the directive being *evidence* of what the person would want versus being *authoritative* regardless, or between self-government across time and being ruled by a now-dead stranger's preferences -- and whatever it has to stay consistent with in the personal identity literature (if the later demented self is a different person, does that rescue the directive or dissolve it? I genuinely don't know which way that cuts). For each requirement, give me the case or consideration behind it, mark which ones you think are non-negotiable and which are just prejudices of one camp, and flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- that's the part I most want to see, because I have a hunch the whole area is unstable in exactly that way and I'd rather know now than in my defense. Then, briefly, test two framings against the list: the one where the earlier self is like a trustee acting for the whole life, and the one where the directive is basically a contract between successive selves, and say which requirement each one fails. No need to cite anything, I have the sources; I need the structure, not a bibliography. Feel free to be opinionated about which single requirement knocks out the most theories.
