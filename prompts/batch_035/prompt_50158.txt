my epistemology seminar just spent two weeks on the surprise exam paradox and our prof is letting us do a creative response for the final assignment instead of another essay. I want to write a poem but I'm tired of poems that just name-drop a paradox, I want the form itself to do some of the philosophical work.

quick recap in case it helps: prof announces there will be an exam next week (monday through friday) and that on no day will you know the exam is coming until it actually arrives. then some clever student reasons: it can't be friday, because if we get to thursday night without an exam we'd know it has to be friday, so no surprise. but once friday is out, it can't be thursday either, since we'd know by wednesday night. and the elimination cascades back through the whole week, so they conclude the exam can't happen at all, kick back and relax — and then the exam happens on tuesday and they're genuinely surprised. exactly as promised.

so my idea: a sestina where the six end-words are monday, tuesday, wednesday, thursday, friday, and surprise. what I like is that a sestina keeps shuffling the same six words through every stanza without ever letting them settle — the days move around but it's the same five days being ruled out over and over, and the argument keeps circling without landing. then the envoi is the exam actually showing up. one specific thing I want: in the envoi, surprise should fall in the middle of a line instead of at the end, so it arrives somewhere in the line you couldn't have predicted — which is kind of the whole point of the announcement, right?

things I need you to get right: (1) the backward induction has to be rendered properly, each elimination depending on the one before it, not just "friday seems unlikely" vibes. (2) the student can't be a strawman — they're genuinely smart, and the twist is that being convinced by their own proof is precisely what makes them get blindsided. (3) don't have the poem settle where the argument goes wrong, because honestly our class didn't either — half the room thinks the problem is the announcement being self-referential, the other half thinks it's the assumption that the students *know* the announcement will hold. maybe the envoi can leave that open somehow without being mushy about it.

also please actually follow the real sestina rotation (I know each stanza's end-words follow a fixed permutation of the previous stanza's, but I don't remember it exactly — you should). it shouldn't rhyme since sestinas don't. if someone who knows the paradox cold would nod along, and someone in my class who's still confused would understand the argument better after reading it, that's the target.
