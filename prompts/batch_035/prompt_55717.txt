Trying to wrap my head around two different ways of defusing the grandfather paradox, and I keep feeling like they're answering different questions.

The first is David Lewis's old move: going back to the past is fine as long as you don't do anything there that didn't happen. You "can" kill your grandfather in the ordinary sense — you have the skill, the gun, a clear shot — it's just that you won't, and if you try you'll fumble, the gun will misfire, you'll get distracted, whatever. It's an accident of history, not a metaphysical ban. The second is the self-consistency idea out of physics (Novikov's thing): the whole timeline is one fixed global solution, your trip was always part of it, and the paradox event just has probability zero, full stop.

So my question: are these actually two different views, or the same view in different clothes? Lewis wants his "can" to be a normal ability claim, compatibilist-style. The consistency view sounds like it makes the failure constitutive rather than coincidental. Here's the scenario that's bugging me — call him the lazy assassin. A traveler goes back to kill grandfather, but only half-heartedly: doesn't check the gun, no surveilance, plans the hit around lunch. On Lewis's story his failure is just his own mediocrity, same as any botched job in the present day. On the consistency story, is the failure somehow guaranteed by the global constraint — was his mediocrity itself "selected for" by the requirement that he fail? And if consistency does imply that, doesn't it leak fatalism backwards into the ordinary present? How do I know my own botched projects aren't being kept consistent by a constraint I have no access to?

So three things I want: (1) a clear statement of where Lewis's relative-modal treatment and the fixed-timeline treatment genuinely come apart, if they even do, (2) whether the lazy assassin forces one of them into saying something weird, and (3) whether the backwards leak into present-day fatalism is real or just a trick of the framing. Keep it conceptual, please — no citations or reading lists, I just want the argument straight.
