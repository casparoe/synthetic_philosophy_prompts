I've been reading a popular philosophy book whose chapter on the ethics of having children ends with a summary of Benatar's so-called asymmetry argument for antinatalism. The author puts it roughly like this: 'Benatar begins from the plain fact that life contains far more suffering than happiness — for most people the bad outweighs the good even if they refuse to admit it. From this he derives his famous asymmetry: since being born guarantees pain but offers pleasure only as a possibility, coming into existence is always a net loss for the person born. Because it is wrong to impose a harm on someone without their consent, procreation is morally impermissible; indeed, Benatar's view commits him to holding that humanity should be wound down as quickly as possible. The obvious reply — that most people are glad to have been born — is waved away as wishful thinking, though our own gladness is surely the best evidence about whether our lives have gone well.' The chapter then concludes that once you point at a single genuinely happy life, the asymmetry collapses.
