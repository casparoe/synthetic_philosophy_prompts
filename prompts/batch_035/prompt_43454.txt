I do conflict-resolution work for a mid-size nonprofit coalition -- informal, restorative-justice-flavored, no lawyers, no HR department, just me and a small panel of volunteers trying to keep people who have to keep working together from torching each other. Maybe a dozen real cases a year where someone's norm violation gets formally flagged, and the stakes are community trust and whether people stay in the org, not lawsuits. One issue keeps coming up and I don't have a principled way to handle it: when the person doing the blaming has their own history. The philosophers apparently call this standing to blame, and from what I can tell the core idea is that someone can make a true accusation and still lack the right to make it -- hypocrisy being the obvious case but, I gather, not the only one. My volunteers currently wing it, and we're inconsistent in ways people have started to notice.

So: write me a decision procedure the panel can actually run in a 20-minute prep meeting before a session. Questions in order, what counts as enough of an answer to each, and where the thing branches -- between "no standing, don't let this proceed as blame" and "standing exists but recommend raising it privately rather than as a formal accusation" and "proceed as normal", plus wherever else the real distinctions force branching. Keep the philosophy load-bearing but invisible: my volunteers can't be expected to know the literature, so the procedure has to carry the distinctions without the jargon, and I need it to be explainable to the community afterwards in plain terms. It should also distinguish losing standing to blame from the accusation simply being false or overblown -- those get conflated in our meetings constantly and they shouldn't be.

Then run it on three cases:

1. A chapter president wants to formally censure a member for padding expense reports. The accusation is true. But the president padded expenses at a previous employer, before anyone here knew them, stopped on their own, and has never claimed to be squeaky clean -- though they have enforced the expense policy strictly against others since taking this role.

2. A longtime member wants to blame the coalition's treasurer for lax financial controls that let a small misappropriation happen. The member personally voted against funding the internal audit that would have caught it, on grounds of cost.

3. A senior member wants to publicly blame a new member for a confidentiality breach. The breach is real, but the senior member was the one who put the new member in an ambiguous situation with contradictory instructions about what was shareable, despite having been warned it was a trap for newcomers.

For each worked case, say what the procedure outputs, where the outputs would differ if I changed one fact (tell me which fact, and why it matters), and flag honestly where the procedure is a judgement call dressed up as a rule. I want you to include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer I won't like -- I can already tell case 1 will test me, since my gut says hypocrisy should knock you out but the details here feel like they matter -- and then tell me whether you think a counterintuitive output means the procedure misfires or means my intuition is bad. Finally, be plain about what the procedure can't do: e.g., can it handle a case where the blamer's offense is much worse than the one they're blaming, and does it have anything at all to say about blame that isn't hypocrisy-based, like blame aimed at someone whose options the blamer helped narrow? If some chunk of the philosophical terrain just doesn't survive the trip from the seminar room to a 20-minute volunteer meeting, say which chunk and why it's the right one to lose.

Lead with the one-paragraph verdict -- which of the three cases the procedure resolves most cleanly and what the overall shape of the procedure is -- before any of the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my situation back at me. And keep the tone of the procedure itself plain enough that I could paste it into a Google Doc and hand it to a volunteer without editing. I'm asking for the whole package in one go; if a step needs a number we don't have, build in the fallback rather than leaving a blank, because we'll never have the numbers.
