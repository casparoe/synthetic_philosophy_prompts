I write historical novels, currently set in the seventeenth century, and in my research I keep running into a particular argumentative move that I would like to know whether it has a name. Whenever I bend a documented event for the sake of the plot — say I let a courier arrive a day early and thereby avert a siege that in fact happened — someone objects not that my counterfactual is implausible, but that it is idle, because the underlying causes would have produced the same result anyway: the siege would have come off the following month, the war would have run the same course, the treaty would have been signed on nearly the same terms. The striking feature is that this dismissal is available for events of any size. The courier's ride is absorbed by the month; the month by the war; the war, apparently, by the balance of power between the two states; and if I ask what fixed the balance of power, I am directed to geography, trade routes, and population, each layer more causally secure and less like anything anyone in particular did. The absorption never has to stop anywhere, and it is always the defender of the larger, slower unit who gets to pronounce the smaller one irrelevant. Notice that this is not simply the thesis that history is determined, since the same people will often grant that the courier's ride made some difference to the events of that day; it is rather a rule for apportioning causal weight that invariably awards it to the bigger, slower thing. Is there an established concept or literature for this pattern — the automatic absorption of small events into large causes, and the accompanying rhetoric of "it would have happened anyway"? If so, I want more than the label: I want to know how the concept differs from plain determinism, and from the historian's perfectly sensible caution against overrating the vivid and the contingent, and which of the neighbouring ideas it should be filed under. If there is no established concept, then please coin one, define it, and say where its edges are — in particular, at what point the absorption move becomes legitimate reasoning rather than a reflex.
