Is there an established concept for the following configuration? A system whose original job is to represent things other than itself — a bureaucracy whose ledgers record grain and taxes, a brain that models its environment, a language model trained on text about the world — eventually acquires the capacity to represent itself, and from that point on the self-representation quietly takes over: forms about forms become the bureaucracy's main output, self-directed planning and self-narration become the mind's busiest activity, and text about models becomes a growing share of the model's training data. The puzzling stage is not the self-reference itself but what follows: the self-representation becomes the system's most central structure, the one through which the rest of its activity is organized, and — in the case of a mind — the place where "I" seems to reside. I know Hofstadter has a notion of strange loops, but I gather that concerns self-referential paradoxes of the Gödel and Escher kind, whereas what I am describing is something more like a structural takeover by the self-model. Does this specific configuration have a name, and how does it differ from mere recursion, from cybernetic feedback, and from whatever Hofstadter actually meant? If no single term exists, coin one and give its boundary conditions — say whether a thermostat counts, whether a corporation counts, whether a chatbot that merely talks about itself counts. I am not after a bare label; I want the carving. Please write your answer in American English.
