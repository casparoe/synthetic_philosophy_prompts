The speaker is a computationalist: he holds that cognition just is the brain's implementing the right computation, and that this is what makes mental states scientifically tractable. He also has a stock deflection of triviality worries — he says that objections in the style of Putnam's mapping argument attack a strawman, and that genuine implementation requires a counterfactual-supporting causal organization among physical states, not an arbitrary formal mapping. It is precisely that deflection I want interrogated. Please draft my questions in five sections, in this order and rough size: 'Warm-up' (two short questions pinning down what he means by computation, so he cannot retreat to a looser sense mid-session); 'The triviality gauntlet' (three or four questions pressing the mapping problem, the intuition that a wall suitably described seems to implement any automaton, and whether causal constraints themselves are only read off the physics under an interpretation); 'The counterfactual reply and its costs' (two questions, including whether demanding counterfactual structure quietly imports semantics — whose counterfactuals, at which grain of physical description); 'Spillover' (two questions on whether his reply, if it works, also licenses or undermines the everyday neuroscientific claim that neurons encode features); and 'The question he cannot deflect' (a single closing question about what would genuinely count against his view). For each main question, include the follow-up that lands if he gives the answer he is most likely to give.
