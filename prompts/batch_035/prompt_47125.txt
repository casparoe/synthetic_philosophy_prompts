I sit on our city's naming review board — two years now, mostly street names and a couple of park statues — and I've had to write up my actual position for the chair. I have a feeling a couple of my points pull against each other, but I honestly can't see which, so before I send it I want an audit. Here's roughly where I stand:

(1) A statue or a street name isn't a historical record, it's an honor — its a present-tense thing the community says about somebody, and whatever authority confers it can take it back. I have no patience for the idea that removing a statue erases history; the history lives in books and archives, not in bronze.

(2) When the question is whether somebody deserves the honor, I want a whole-life judgment — I'm against canonizing a person for one big acomplishment and just as against damning them for one stain. Neither the single-achievement fan nor the single-vice prosecutor has it right.

(3) When we do judge a historical figure, we should hold them to the standards of their own era, not ours — you can't fault someone for failing norms they couldn't have known about. One exception I'd carve: if their own contemporaries were already condemning the very thing we object to, that person is fair game, no era shields that.

(4) I'd actually like commemorative honors to sunset automatically, say every 50 years, unless the community affirmatively renews them. Like a lease. If nobody cares enough to renew, it comes down with no ceremony and no finding of disgrace.

(5) Renaming a street should be a higher bar than taking down a statue. A statue sits in a park; a street name is people's addresses, their mail, maps some of them have used for decades — ripping it out imposes real costs on residents who had nothing to do with the honoree, so it should take more to justify.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are really in tension and which only look like it. Rank the tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad — for each real one, say what would have to give and roughly what it costs to give it. I'm not attached to any particular one, so don't try to guess which I'd sacrifice; just do the accounting straight. Also, at the end, tell me how confident you are in each of your calls, and single out the one point in your audit you're least sure of — I'd rather know where the audit itself is shaky than get a confident answer to a shaky question.
