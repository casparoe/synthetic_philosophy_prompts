I do deployment safety at a lab that trains big models, and I've been keeping a running list of my gut reactions to control measures -- the ones that make sense especially if the model turns out to be misaligned -- and I want to know whether there's a principle behind them or whether I'm just pattern-matching.

The verdicts, roughly in order of how sure I am:

- Full sandbox, tools that can't touch anything real: fine, obviously, that's just a firewall with extra steps.
- Logging everything it says and having humans or other models review the logs for signs of instruction-dodging: fine.
- Tripwires -- deliberately leaving what looks like an unguarded way out (a shell it thinks reaches the internet, say) and seeing whether it takes it: fine, honestly I think alot more places should be doing this.
- A hard off switch: fine.
- Not telling the model it's in an evaluation: fine. We don't announce audits to employees either.
- Quietly comparing how it behaves when it plausibly thinks nobody is reading against how it behaves when it knows someone is: fine, that's just auditing.
- Telling it a flat lie about its own situation to test its honesty -- say, convincing it that it's running unmonitored when it isn't: not fine. Feels entrapment-ish, and it seems to dirty the very thing I'm trying to measure.
- Shaping its general picture of the world (fake physics, fake moral facts) because a model with those beliefs is easier to handle: obviously not fine.
- Using monitor flags as negative training signal: fine in principal, but it goes bad the instant the monitors have false positives, and monitors always have false positives.

So the tension I can't get out of: the tripwire and the flat lie both involve constructing a false situation and watching what the model does in it, and I think one is great and one is rotten. And on the full "escape room" -- a whole staged fake production environment where even the coworkers it messages are simulated -- I go back and forth, because it's a tripwire at scale but also the biggest lie on the list.

My tentative principle is something like: a measure is okay iff it would still be a reasonable thing to do if the model were perfectly aligned -- you still lock your door even when you trust your houseguest. But I suspect this kills the tripwire, which I like. Does it? Is there a principle that keeps everything I want to keep, or something close? And what does it say about the two cases I'm genuinely split on: running two copies side by side with different cover stories about their training and watching for divergence, and rewarding the model for reporting its own sketchy-seeming plans before it carries them out? Tell me straight which of my verdicts you'd throw out rather than build a principle around.
