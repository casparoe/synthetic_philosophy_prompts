I've been working through the knowability paradox and want to write up where I stand, but first I need a sanity check, because something feels off and I can't tell if it's real. My package, roughly:

(1) I'm a realist about truth: bivalence holds without exceptions, and plenty of true propositions will simply never be known by anyone — details of the deep past, the exact state of some region of spacetime, whatever.
(2) But I still buy the knowability principle in spirit: no truth is off-limits in principle — for any truth there is some possible epistemic situation, maybe involving knowers far better placed than humans, in which it is known.
(3) I've gone through Fitch's derivation and I think it's just valid. Factivity plus knowledge distributing over conjunction — the whole thing works for any knowledge operator whatsoever, nothing in it turns on the knowers being human or cognitively limited.
(4) So my official line is the Tennant-style restriction: the principle covers only basic propositions, and the Moorean conjunctions (p and nobody ever knows that p) are knowably unknowable, so they were never really what the principle was about in the first place. I don't think this is ad hoc.
(5) Meanwhile I reject the Dummettian background entirely — meaning is not verification conditions. My reason for caring about knowability is just epistemic optimism: inquiry is open-ended, and nothing true is forever beyond reach.
(6) And for mathematics I'd want the unrestricted claim: every arithmetical truth is knowable in principle. Math has no blind spots, that's kind of the point of it.

Which of these actually fight, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix for each? Please rank the tensions by severity instead of handing me a flat list, and be specific about what exactly has to give. And don't just run through the standard positions — I want to know what *my* particular set commits me to, since I can't tell whether the problem is (2), whether (4) is quietly incompatible with how I motivate it in (5), or whether I'm imagining the whole thing.
