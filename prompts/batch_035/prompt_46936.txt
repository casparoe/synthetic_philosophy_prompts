I've been reading decision-theory threads on LessWrong and adjacent blogs since roughly the Scott Alexander sequence era, and after a long period of squirming I've settled on a view I'll state as bluntly as I can: expected utility maximization over aggregate welfare is the correct decision rule, and the correct version of it is fanatical. That is, I accept that for any certainty of a merely enormous payoff, there is a sufficiently tiny probability of a sufficiently larger payoff that the gamble wins — a one-in-a-googolplex chance that some physics-flouting stranger's coin flip creates 10^(10^20) flourishing lives outweighs the certain creation of a trillion flourishing lives, and I should choose accordingly. I take the standard package that comes with this: I would pay Pascal's mugger in principle, I should direct existential-risk effort toward astronomically cheap longshots rather than solid marginal gains, and the fact that a norm this shape selects an infinite series of ever-weirder bets doesn't by itself bother me, because dominance reasoning and the money-pump arguments for expected utility seem to me to leave no room for risk aversion over morally relevant stakes. A friend in my local decision-theory meetup thinks I've stopped noticing how much I've insulated the view — I've said that money pumps only bind over finite sequences, that boundedness worries are about my psychology rather than the mathematics, and that intuitionist objections to longshot dominance are just scope insensitivity wearing a lab coat.

What would actually count against this? I want a map of the view's exposure, not a defense of it. Which formal results would genuinely wound it — say, representation theorems under which risk-weighted expected utility satisfies the same compelling axioms while licensing the sane verdicts, impossibility results about extending a social welfare ordering to infinitary lotteries, or Dutch book or dynamic-consistency arguments that cut in favor of bounded utilities rather than merely failing to bind against them? Which of my claimed predictions — pay the mugger, prefer the longshot, skip the insurance — are ones its serious defenders would themselves concede a view should not make, and which only strike outsiders as absurd? Where does the view risk something at all: is there any observation, argument, or consequence that could move someone who has already accepted dominance and the von Neumann–Morgenstern setup, or has the position effectively become a theorem from axioms I refuse to reexamine — and if it has, is that a defect for a normative view of this kind, or just what mathematically derived views look like? Finally, tell me what has arguably already happened that should make me uneasy: the complaints, the cases, the formal wrinkles that a committed fanatic should acknowledge as counting against them, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. In under 500 words, please — I plan to bring your ranking to the next meetup, so lead with your bottom line.
