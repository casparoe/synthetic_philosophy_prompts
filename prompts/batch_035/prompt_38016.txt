Suppose Arthur Schopenhauer could be shown the results of the Libet-style experiments: subjects asked to flex a wrist at a moment of their own choosing show a slow ramping of cortical activity beginning well over half a second before the time at which they later report having first become aware of the urge, and some researchers add that in a final fraction of a second the act can still be withheld, even after the urge is felt. Given his doctrine that the will is a fixed, indivisible striving whose intelligible and empirical characters leave no room for libertarian freedom, and that the felt freedom of choice is an artifact of the intellect observing its own appetitions, predict his reading of the data. Specifically: which parts of the experiment would he take as confirmation, and why; whether he would embrace the reported last-moment veto as a genuine remnant of freedom or instead explain it away as the intellect, in his sense, merely declining to execute an urge that was never going to be acted on; and how he would respond to the later suggestion that the early ramping reflects ongoing spontaneous neural fluctuation rather than an unconscious decision-in-preparation. Conclude by saying whether Schopenhauer would be more likely than a present-day determinist to overclaim what the experiment establishes, and what in his own philosophy would tempt him to do so.
