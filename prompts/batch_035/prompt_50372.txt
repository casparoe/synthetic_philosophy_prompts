I'm a PhD student working on the ethics of biomedical enhancement, and I've been defending a version of the therapy/enhancement distinction that I believe survives the standard objections. My version: the line is fixed by species-typical functioning — therapy restores a capacity to the normal human range, enhancement raises it above that range — and it carries normative weight only via justice: falling below the normal range is the kind of disadvantage fairness requires institutions to mitigate, while merely being above-range-impaired is not. This lets me say that society has stronger obligations to provide cochlear implants than memory enhancers, without implying that enhancement is inherently suspect.

I don't need the familiar objections rerun (I know normality is contestable and that aging and death are statistically normal). What I want from you is a map of what would actually count against my view, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Two things I especially want your judgment on:

First, the moving baseline problem. If a majority of a population takes a cognitive enhancer, the statistical norm shifts, and yesterday's enhancement becomes today's subnormal functioning — which my view then counts as therapy, generating a duty to subsidize it. Does this make the view self-undermining, in that it comes over time to license exactly what it was designed to exclude? Can the baseline be anchored to something historical (the range at the time of a policy's adoption) or biological (what the genome unassisted supports) without reintroducing arbitrary line-drawing? Second, which costs would defenders of my own kind of view concede as real — say, that it misclassifies individual tragedies just above the normal range — and which would they rightly dismiss as confusion?

Also tell me what empirical developments, such as germline interventions becoming routine or the normal range drifting upward generation by generation, would strip the distinction of its normative work. What I would personally find most persuasive is a case my view cannot even describe, not a rival theory. And give me an honest verdict on whether my qualifications have insulated the view into unfalsifiability — and if so, whether that is a defect in a view of this kind, whose job is to guide policy rather than predict anything.
