I lead a small backend team and after years of dashboards and KPIs I've accumulated a bunch of views about measurement that I've never actually checked against each other. a friend at a meetup said half of them probably contradict each other and now it's bugging me. here's the set, roughly as I'd say them out loud:

(1) the strong goodhart thing — any metric that gets leaned on hard enough eventually diverges from what anyone actually cared about, and this is structural: it happens even when nobody is cheating, and it hits ML systems optimizing a reward function just as much as humans gaming a KPI. no exceptions. (2) the old management line that you can't manage what you can't measure — I basicaly run my team by this; if something's not on a dashboard I assume it's drifting. (3) the fix for goodhart is to measure the thing you care about as directly as possible — if you care about code quality, count production defects, not lines of code or story points. get the measure close enough to the real thing and the gaming problem mostly goes away. (4) when I actually look at cases where a metric got gamed, it's almost always a person with an incentive exploiting the gap on purpose — so the real fix is incentives and culture, not cleverer metrics. (5) metric hygiene: keep several metrics and read them together, rotate them now and then, and never tie more than a sliver of anyone's comp to any single number. (6) our sprint velocity metric is fine even though it lives on a dashboard, because nobody's pay or promotion depends on it — it's just for planning.

some of these feel fine together and some feel off and I genuinly can't tell which is which, so don't go easy on me. give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion: items 1 through 3 should be the three worst tensions among my views, in order of severity, each saying what would have to give; item 4 should be one pair that only looks like a tension and why it dissolves; item 5 should be the single cheapest revision to my set that would clean up the most overall.
