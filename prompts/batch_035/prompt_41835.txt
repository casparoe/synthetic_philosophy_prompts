I've spent over a decade reading rationalist blogs and forums — the Sequences, the sprawling LessWrong threads about priors and induction, the occasional flare-up about whether Occam's razor can be justified without circularity. I thought I understood where the bodies were buried. Then I put together the following argument, and I can't find my way out of it. I'd like you to act as an expert epistemologist with a serious working knowledge of machine learning theory, and diagnose it for me: either name the step where it goes wrong and explain why that step looked fine, or, if the argument is valid, tell me which premise I should give up and what that costs.

Background, stated informally so we're on the same page. The no-free-lunch results from 1990s machine learning theory (Wolpert and Macready are the names usually attached to them) show, roughly, that if you average a learner's performance over all possible worlds — over all ways a function could map histories of observations to future observations — then every learning algorithm ties with every other. Expect tomorrow to resemble today, expect tomorrow to be today run backwards, or guess randomly: identical average accuracy. Any advantage a method enjoys in some worlds is exactly offset by its failures in others. Call this NFL, and grant it as stated.

Now the argument:

(1) By NFL, no inductive method is better than any other when assessed prior to all data; there is no a priori ranking.
(2) Whatever advantage a method has comes from the fit between its biases and the actual world, not from the method itself.
(3) Whether the actual world fits our biases can only be learned from experience, and learning it from experience presupposes some inductive bias. So the defense of any inductive bias is either circular — it uses the bias to vouch for itself — or nonexistent.
(4) Therefore no inductive bias is rationally preferable to any other without first assuming some inductive bias.
(5) A prior just is a packaged set of inductive biases.
(6) So the choice of prior is arbitrary from the epistemic point of view: nothing rational favors mine over yours.
(7) Consider an agent with a prior dominated by a cream-cheese hypothesis: the sun will rise normally every day until some distant day N, on which it will turn into cream cheese. Before day N, no possible observation can count against this hypothesis — it predicts everything the ordinary hypothesis predicts, plus cheese. This agent updates by Bayes on exactly the same history I have, is exactly as well-calibrated as I am on every observed occasion, and predicts cream cheese at day N.
(8) Given (6), the cream-cheese agent is just as rational as I am. Our priors differ, and per (4)–(6) nothing rational breaks the tie.
(9) But rationality is supposed to be systematized winning. If the label rational attaches equally to me and to the cream-cheese agent, it has lost its content; a concept of rationality that certifies dispositions predictably doomed in the world we actually occupy is not the concept I signed up for.
(10) So either the cream-cheese agent is rational, or something rationally preferable about my prior exists — and (4) says it can't.

I can't accept that the cream-cheese agent is rational. I also very much want to keep the thought behind (9); it's close to the core of everything I've taken from years in this corner of the internet. And I already know the local stock reply — the universe is simple, weight hypotheses by description length, prefer compressibility — but the choice of machine that defines description length seems exactly as arbitrary as the choice of prior, so if anything that move instantiates (5) through (6) rather than rebutting them. Please don't just hand it back to me. My vague suspicion is that the trouble lies in the slide from no non-circular justification, in (3) and (4), to arbitrary, in (6) — that some distinction is being erased there — but I can't name it, and I'd be grateful if you either named it or convinced me no such distinction holds.

If you conclude the argument is valid, price the premises against one another and tell me which is cheapest to abandon. Please be precise and, where you introduce a distinction to break the slide from (3) to (6), state it in full sentences and test it against the cream-cheese agent rather than leaving it as a slogan.
