My philosophy-of-mind reading group has two members who've been arguing about mind uploading for literally years, and they finally agreed to do one of those adversarial collaboration writeups, and I got volun-told to draft it. One of them holds that gradual replacement — nanorobots taking over the function of individual neurons, one small batch at a time, while you stay awake and conversational throughout — gets you survival: the person who walks out of the clinic is you. The other says it's a copy: you effectively die during the procedure and something else, however similar, walks out. Draft the joint statement they could both sign. I want the claims both sides accept (about what the procedure physically does, which facts about brains and function are relevant, and which parts of the "is it you?" question are empirical vs. conceptual), the residual disagreements each phrased so its own side would accept the wording of its own view, and the cruxes. One crux I suspect they'll hit: the dual-run variant, where each synthetic batch runs in parallel with the biological neurons it's slated to replace for a while before the originals are switched off — so for a stretch there's both a gradual-replacement chain and an untouched biological original side by side, each causally connected to the pre-procedure person. For each crux, give a concrete thought experiment or argument both sides would agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts they'd conclude from it. And if part of their dispute is basically verbal — say, whether "survival" means numerical identity or something weaker like psychological continuity — say which part and why. Format as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a table for the disagreements.
