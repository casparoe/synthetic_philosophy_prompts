A few friends and I run a tiny nonpartisan voter-engagement thing ahead of local elections, mostly first-time voters who are completely turned off by every party on the ballot. Whenever the "should I even bother voting, and for whom" conversation comes up, people talk past each other because they're using the same words for totally different views: one person means "voting is a duty, period," another means "vote for whoever best expresses what you actually want, outcomes be damned," another is doing cold expected-value math and wondering why anyone votes at all. I want to build a "which view of voting do you actually hold?" quiz that sorts people by what they say about concrete cases, not by which slogan they like, so the questions have to be cases and scenarios they can answer without knowing the philosophical labels.

Please write eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. Some cases I'd like in there (add or swap others if you think they're better diagnostics): a safe-seat/landslide district where your vote has effectively zero chance of mattering to the outcome; a ballot where every candidate is genuinely awful and you're choosing the least bad; whether to vote for a small party you actually like versus a big one you merely prefer to the other big one; deliberately spoiling your ballot or staying home as a protest; a friend who says voting is pointless because one vote never decides anything and yet you feel they're wrong somehow; and whether you'd support making voting compulsory with a small fine, like some countries do. No question should just ask "do you think voting is a civic duty?" — that's the label, not the diagnostic.

Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions: roughly the consequentialist/instrumental view (votes only matter through outcomes, which typically leads toward "vote where stakes are close, abstain or flip a coin elsewhere"), the civic-duty view, expressive voting, the tactical lesser-evil view, the sincere/protest-spoiling option, and the compulsory-voting-supporter type. If two of these come apart on none of my cases — I suspect expressive voting and sincere third-party voting get sorted into the same bucket no matter what — please flag that explicitly instead of pretending the quiz separates them, and suggest what case WOULD separate them if you can think of one. Also fine to note combos of answers nobody gives, and a quick guess at why (incoherent, unappealing, or just rare). Keep the whole thing usable on paper, so no interactive logic needed — just questions, options, and a scoring key at the end.
