Context: last weekend two of my friends, Keisha and Dan, got into it at a barbecue over the Sleeping Beauty problem. In case you don't know it offhand: Beauty agrees to an experiment. A fair coin is flipped while she sleeps Sunday night. Heads: they wake her Monday morning, interview her, done. Tails: they wake her Monday, interview her, wipe her memory, and wake her again Tuesday with exactly the same kind of interview. The room, the grogginess, everything is identical, so on any awakening she can't tell which day it is. At every interview they ask her: how confident are you that the coin landed heads?

Dan went first: "Easy, it's 1/3. Run the experiment 1000 times. Roughly 500 heads runs produce 500 awakenings, 500 tails runs produce 1000 awakenings. She's equally likely to be in any one awakening, so on waking, it's tails 2 times out of 3. Thirders win."

Keisha: "You're counting awakenings, but the experiment happens once and there's one coin. Sunday night she'd say 1/2, and when she wakes up she notices nothing she didn't already forsee Sunday — white room, interview, foggy head, she knew all of it was coming. No new information, so no update. 1/2. Halfers win."

Dan: "Fine, forget frequencies, look at money. Suppose at each interview the staff offer her this: pay 40 cents now, win $1 if the coin landed heads. If she values heads at 1/3 she'll refuse, and she should refuse: over many runs of that offer, tails experiments make her pay the 40 cents twice and get nothing back, and that eats more than the heads experiments pay out. Break-even sits at 33 cents, which is the 1/3 again. Thirders win twice."

Keisha: "That's a trick. The break-even price there is a fact about the deal's structure — when it's tails she has to buy the bet twice, so of course the package only comes out fair at a lower price. You've computed what the house should charge for a bundle, not what she believes. If they offered the bet only once per experiment, the fair price would be different and her confidence wouldn't have budged. Halfers still win."

Dan: "Then explain this. On your view, if the staff truthfully told her 'by the way, this is Monday,' she'd have to update to 2/3 heads. She becomes more confident in heads than a fair coin ever justified, just from finding out which weekday it is. That's a bullet, and you're chewing it."

Keisha didn't swallow that, and instead pulled this move, which is where they lost me: "Here's why I think the whole fight is about time, not probability. Suppose the staff flip the coin Monday evening instead of Sunday night, after Monday's interview, same protocol otherwise. Then during the Monday awakening the toss literally hasn't happened yet — there's no fact of the matter for her to be wrong about — so her Monday confidence should just be 1/2, it's a fair coin toss about the future. But everything she can observe on Monday is identical whichever night they schedule the flip. So either the timing of the flip matters while being undetectable, which is bizarre, or your whole framing is off."
