I'm a graduate student in economics, and I keep running into a dialectical move that I can't put a name to. A model built on obviously false assumptions — perfect foresight, frictionless adjustment, representative agents who differ only in their endowments — predicts reasonably well within some domain, and its defenders cite those predictions whenever anyone attacks the assumptions: the falsity of the assumptions, they say, is simply irrelevant to the model's worth; that's just what a good model does, the way a point mass or a frictionless plane never stopped anyone from using mechanics. Fine so far. But when the same model is then used to answer a counterfactual question — say, what would happen under a tax or a policy regime the model was never estimated on — the very same people quietly start treating the assumptions as load-bearing: the model's answer must be reliable, because agents are assumed to optimize, and optimizing is a real feature of the world. The assumptions are declared irrelevant when the model is under attack and essential the moment it is applied, and nobody seems to register the switch.

Is there an established name for this pattern? I know about Friedman's position that the realism of assumptions doesn't matter, and I know the general label of instrumentalism, but neither of those names the switching itself, which is what I'm after: the fact that the same assumption is assigned opposite methodological roles depending on which conversation it's in. If there is no established concept, please coin a term for it, define it precisely, and distinguish it from nearby things — plain inconsistency, an ordinary motte-and-bailey, and the legitimate point that a model's assumptions can be dispensable for one purpose and essential for another. Also tell me where the edges are: is it still the same phenomenon when the two roles are played by different people, so that the field as a whole, rather than any single arguer, holds both positions at once? And is there a principled way for the model's defenders to redraw the boundary — declaring in advance which questions the false assumptions are licensed for — that would dissolve the problem rather than just making the switch more explicit?
