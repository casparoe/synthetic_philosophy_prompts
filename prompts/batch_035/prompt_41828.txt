I've been keeping a list of my gut reactions to dream cases and I want to know if they add up to a position or if they're just a mess. Here they are. When I wake up remembering a long involved dream about being late for a flight, my strong feeling is that I had an experience, last night, while asleep -- it happened then, like a perceptual episode. Lucid dreams feel even clearer: I knew I was dreaming, I decided to fly, that was me acting in the moment. Sleep lab stuff pushes the same way: wake someone up mid-REM and they tell you an ongoing story, and my gut says the story was being lived, not composed on the spot at wake-up. But now the hard ones. If a hypnotist, or some futuristic neuroscientist, just implants a fake memory of a dream, I'd say no dream occured at all -- the experience matters, not the report. Yet I also recall reading about the idea that dream content might get written into memory in one burst at the moment of waking, and for nightmares I somehow find that more tolerable than for the long flight-panic dream, where the felt length of the thing seems like evidence it unfolded in real time. And then night terrors: I thrashed and screamed for ten minutes, remember nothing, and everyone tells me it looked like I was terrified -- did I have a fear-experience I can't report, or did my body just run a program with nobody home? That one I flip on constantly. Same flip with the totally ordinary case: every night my brain is presumably doing something dream-ish that I forget entirely by morning -- was that experience, or does experience require the memory that survives waking? So: is there one principle that captures all these verdicts (something like: a dream is an experience iff such-and-such happened during sleep rather than at the boundary), and what does it say about the night terror and the forgotten nightly dreaming, which is where I need it most? If saving all my verdicts requires some principle about memory and report, say so plainly, and tell me which verdict you'd overturn instead. Oh and please answer in American English.
