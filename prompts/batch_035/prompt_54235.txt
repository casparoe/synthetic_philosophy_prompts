Write a deadpan parody of a technical RFC — the formal "request for comments" documents used to propose software standards — for an open-source project that maintains a daemon enforcing a fair distribution of tokens across a network. I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, but I've been reading about Nozick's entitlement theory, and I want the piece to be a joke about its Wilt Chamberlain argument: the maintainers deploy the daemon to restore a distribution everyone has agreed is fair, then discover that users keep voluntarily spending their tokens to watch a star athlete, so the daemon can never just run once at startup — it has to intercept every transfer, forever, because free exchange keeps regenerating the very inequality it was built to remove. Give it the usual RFC furniture (motivation, terminology, protocol, security considerations), and work the three parts of Nozick's theory in as subsystems: an acquisition module gated by a proviso check, a transfer module, and a rectification module that has been marked "TODO: future work" since version 0.1 with no assignee. Let the satire come from the engineering decisions rather than editorializing — nobody in the document should say that continuous redistribution is wrong; the maintainers should just keep calmly writing status reports about it — and include at least one closed bug in which a contributor asks whether "voluntary" transfers under the protocol are really voluntary, dismissed as out of scope. Around 700 words: funny to someone who knows the Nozick literature, readable to someone who doesn't.
