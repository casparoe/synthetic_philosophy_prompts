I help run the facebook group for my neighborhood, few thousand members. Lately someone has been posting rants calling one of our city council members corrupt and a "puppet of developers." On its own whatever, people trash local politicians all the time. But the last post went further: it listed the cafe where she has breakfast every morning, the gym she goes to and the hours she's usually there, and ended with something like "if you see her, let her know what you think." A bunch of people have been showing up there to confront her, and yesterday a small crowd basically boxed her in outside the gym until her husband came. Nobody touched her, but she's visibly shaken and asked me directly to take the posts down.

The guy who posted is now accusing me of censorship if I remove it. His claim is that it's all true and all public info — she really does eat there, and everything he posted he found from her own social media. He says if people choose to be hostile that's on them, not on his posts, and that a "free exchange of opinions" was kind of the whole point of the group. Some members back him up and say slippery slope: today I delete "criticism of a politician," tomorrow I'm deleting anything I personally disagree with.

I'm torn. I do think he should be allowed to say she's corrupt, that's normal political speech to me. But listing her routines and telling people to go find her feels different in a way I can't fully articulate — like the same words matter differently depending on who's reading them and what they can do with them. Is there an actual principled line here, or am I just making a gut call and dressing it up? And does it matter that he didn't explicitly tell anyone to threaten her? Help me figure out where the line is and how to explain the removal (or non-removal) to the group in a way that isn't just "I felt like it."
