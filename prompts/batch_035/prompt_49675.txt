I would like your credence that perdurantism is true — the view that persisting objects are extended in time as well as space, so that persistence consists in having distinct temporal parts at distinct times — as against endurantism, on which a persisting object is wholly present at every moment it exists. Please give a number rather than a survey, and decompose the derivation. My own credence sits around 0.65; a colleague of mine is closer to 0.25, and I would like to know whether we most likely disagree about the arguments or about how much arguments can be expected to settle matters here. As I see it, the decomposition should at least include terms for the following: the probability that the problem of temporary intrinsics is best solved by temporal parts rather than by tensing properties, or by treating properties as relations to times; the probability that the argument from vagueness for unrestricted mereological composition is sound, and how much of that verdict should propagate from composition over space to composition over time; and the weight that relativistic considerations deserve, given that in Minkowski spacetime there appears to be no frame-independent present at which an object could be wholly present. Two complications I want priced in explicitly. First, several of these arguments support the stage theory about as readily as classical perdurantism, so say whether you fold that into the proposition perdurantism is true, treat it as a third live outcome, or assign it its own probability. Second, purely as a hypothetical: suppose physicists tomorrow announced a well-confirmed fundamental ontology consisting entirely of distributions of properties over spacetime points, with no objects at any scale. Would that raise your credence in perdurantism substantially, or do you hold that perdurantism was never really hostage to the physics, since it is a thesis about ordinary middle-sized objects rather than about what fundamental physics quantifies over? Close by telling me which single term your overall credence is most sensitive to, and what would actually move it — for example, a fully worked-out endurantist treatment of fission cases in personal identity, or a decisive objection to the vagueness argument.
