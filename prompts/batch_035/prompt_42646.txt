I've been reading up on the great famines of the last two centuries — Bengal in the 1940s, Ireland in the 1840s, China after the Great Leap Forward — and one comparison keeps coming up, sometimes from governments themselves: a famine is basically a natural disaster, like an earthquake or a flood. The way I see the argument go, if it's a natural disaster, then the deaths are a tragedy the way earthquake deaths are a tragedy — no one is really at fault, the best you can do is send relief afterward, and blaming the rulers is like blaming the weather. The Irish case bugs me, though, because I've read that food was being shipped out of Ireland while people were dying, and I've seen people say the economist Amartya Sen found something similar in Bengal — that there wasn't really a shortage of food overall, just masses of people who'd lost the ability to buy any of it. And in China, I gather the government's own explanation for years leaned heavily on bad weather, which some historians now dispute. So the natural-disaster comparison seems to be doing a lot of work in letting people off the hook, and I want to know how far it actually holds. Please give me exactly five numbered points, each only a sentence or two long, with nothing before the first number and nothing after the last: first, a straight mapping of the famine onto the earthquake analogy — what corresponds to what (the cause, the victims, the government, the aid, the rebuilding); second, the places the analogy breaks that genuinely matter for whether anyone is at fault, including anything from the entitlement idea and the Irish and Chinese cases; third, the places it breaks that don't actually matter for the blame question, so I don't get distracted by them; fourth, what the analogy quietly smuggles in about blame and predictability — an earthquake gives no warning and no one benefits from it, so what does the analogy assume about famines that needs arguing rather than stipulating; and fifth, your verdict on whether the nobody's-really-at-fault conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and if there's a better comparison available from ordinary life (I've half-thought a bank run or a riot might fit, but I'm not sure), say which and why.
