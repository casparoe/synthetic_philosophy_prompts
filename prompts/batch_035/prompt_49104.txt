quick consistency check before I present to my reading group please. we've been on Lakatos this term and I've written out where I've landed, and I want someone to poke at whether it actually hangs together as a package. here are my positions:

(1) I'm not a naive falsificationist. I think the Duhem point is just correct — when a prediction fails you can always pin the blame on some auxiliary assumption instead of the theory, and Popper's "reject on clash with observation" was never a rule anyone could actually follow. this is a lot of why I find Lakatos appealing in the first place.

(2) I buy the basic machinery: a research programme has a hard core (protected by fiat, you don't test it directly) and a protective belt of auxiliaries that does the absorbing of counterexamples, and that arrangement is legitimate rather than cheating.

(3) I think the unit of appraisal is the whole programme over time — progressing when it keeps predicting novel facts (Mercury's perihelion counts for relativity even though the anomaly predated it, since the theory wasn't built to fit that one) and degenerating when it only patches things after the fact (late Ptolemaic epicycles, in my view, though people argue about that one).

(4) BUT I also think the hard core is revisable in principle. if a programme has been degenerating for decades I think scientists are entitled to go back and reopen the core itself, not just reshuffle the belt — and I think they've historically done exactly this, whatever the official methodology says.

(5) I think appraisal is essentially retrospective: you can only really tell whether a programme was progressive after the fact, over the long run. but I also want to be able to tell a PhD student TODAY which programme to join, and I want that advice to be rationally grounded, not a coin flip weighted by fashion.

(6) I'm quite confident astrology is pseudoscience and I'd say so now, flat out, without waiting for the long run to tell me how the astrology programme turns out.

(7) I think rival programmes can be genuinely hard to compare because they carve the world up differently — they use their central terms in their own ways, and what counts as a problem for one isn't quite a problem for the other. some mild incommensurability, nothing mystical. and yet I still want there to be a fact of the matter about which programme is more progressive, since (3) says there is.

so: which of these pairs are in real tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix for each of the real ones? rank the tensions by severity rather than giving me a flat list, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most if I made it. also please actually deliver a verdict on my epicycles claim in (3) while you're at it, since I keep going back and forth on it. one more thing — I'd like the whole answer in British English, I know that's probably your default anyway but humour me. and don't just summarise what Lakatos said to me, treat the list as MY position, however close to or far from the historical Lakatos it ends up being.
