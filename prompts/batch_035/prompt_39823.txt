quick sanity check on my keynes views before I present to my reading group — do these hang together?

(1) Day to day I'm a straightforward Bayesian: I allways have a precise credence in every proposition and I update by conditionalisation.

(2) I think Keynes was right in the Treatise that lots of probabilities just aren't numerical — on the evidence we actually have, many propositions get no precise probability at all, only a partial ordering at best.

(3) On the big fight, I side with Ramsey against Keynes: there are no logical probability relations between propositions sitting out there waiting to be perceived; probability is degree of belief.

(4) I read Keynes's 1937 reply to his critics as the Treatise continued rather than a break — the uncertainty he talks about there just is non-numerical probability plus low weight of argument, so he never really changed his mind.

(5) In Ellsberg's two-urn problem I'd pay a premium to bet on the urn I know is 50/50 rather than the unknown one, and I'm confident that's fully rational, not a bias to be explained away.

(6) I think weight of argument matters in its own right — I'd sometimes pay for extra evidence even when I'm sure it won't move any of my probabilities or change any decision, and I reckon that can be genuinely rational rather than a taste for curiosity.

Go through the pairs, tell me which are in real tension, which only look like it, and for the real ones what would have to give. Rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them all the same, and say which single revision would clean up the most. Keep it under 500 words, my slot is short and the others will have done the reading.
