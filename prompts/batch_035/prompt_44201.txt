I've been reading LessWrong, the EA Forum, and the blogs orbiting them for something like a decade, and I've lately realized that my views on Bostrom's singleton hypothesis have accreted piecemeal from a hundred different posts without ever being checked as a package. Here is roughly where I've landed, in decreasing order of confidence:

(1) A singleton — a single decision-making agency operating at the civilizational level, in Bostrom's sense — is more likely than not to be the eventual structure of Earth-originating intelligence, and the most plausible route there is a sufficiently capable AI system under the effective control of some small group.

(2) On balance, a singleton would be a good outcome, or at least far better than the multipolar alternative: uncoordinated competition among powerful agents is the mechanism behind arms races and the Moloch-style coordination failures I fear most, and a well-designed singleton could suppress those permanently rather than winning the same battles over and over.

(3) I take the value fragility thesis seriously: human values occupy a small and contingent region of the space of possible value systems, so processes that reshape values even slightly — or freeze them at the wrong moment — can lose almost everything.

(4) I also think moral progress is real, ongoing, and nowhere near complete: people in two hundred years will probably regard some of my settled moral convictions with the same horror I feel toward historical apologists for slavery, and I cannot reliably say which of my convictions those will be.

(5) The historical engine of moral progress, as far as I can tell, has been adversarial — clashing factions, competing institutions, arguments sharpened by opposition — rather than isolated deliberation converging on truth.

(6) The right design for a singleton would therefore include a long reflection: an extended period in which humanity, under the singleton's protection and with existential risks suspended, works out its values before anything is locked in.

(7) I support aggressive global coordination to slow AI development right now, precisely because I think such coordination is feasible.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and for each real tension what is the cheapest revision and what does that revision cost elsewhere in the set? Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as an undifferentiated list, and at the end tell me which single change would clean up the most. I have a nagging sense that some of these commitments don't sit well together, but I genuinely can't locate the fault lines myself, so don't hold back on anything I haven't spotted — and equally, don't manufacture conflict where a moment's thought dissolves it.
