I got into a long argument with my housemate (neuroscience PhD student, huge Dennett fan) after she showed me the color phi phenomenon at her lab's open house. In case you don't know it: you flash a red dot on the left side of a screen, then a green dot on the right, and people report seeing ONE dot that moves across and changes color partway through — often saying it turned, like, yellowish or blended in the middle, a color that never actually appeared anywhere. The puzzle isn't the illusion itself, it's the timing. The brain can't have known the second dot's color when the first one was flashed, so the motion and the color-change were only determined after the second flash. Two classic stories about what happened: on the "Stalinesque" story, consciousness is broadcast on a delay — the brain holds the feed, edits it (inserts the motion, the intermediate color), and only then does it "air," so you really did experience the moving, color-shifting dot, just later than you think you did. On the "Orwellian" story, you experienced red-here then green-there, no motion, and the memory of the illusion was rewritten moments later — you're misremembering your own experience, smoothly and without noticing.

Here's the thing: I'm at maybe 0.6 that the Stalinesque version is basically right (delayed broadcast, real fill-in before the fact), and she's at like 0.25 — but for a weird reason, because she doesn't think the Orwellian story is right either. Her view is closer to Dennett's: there's no single moment when the experience "happened," no place in the brain where it all comes together for an audience, so the question "was the motion experienced at time t or falsely remembered at t+200ms" has no fact of the matter — the brain just drafts and redrafts, and asking which draft was the real one is like asking which of two conflicting eyewitness accounts is the true recording when there was never a camera running.

So I want your actual credence, as a number, and not just "it depends on your theory of consciousness." Decompose it: the probability that there IS a determinate fact of the matter about when and with what content the experience occurred (vs. her Dennettian no-fact view); given there is such a fact, the probability that any experiment could in principle distinguish Orwellian rewriting from Stalinesque editing, since both predict identical reports and identical confident-but-wrong memories; the probability that postdiction — later input determining earlier-seeming content — is a real feature of experience rather than just a memory effect; and whatever other terms you think belong in there. Give me a rough number and a one-line justification for each, then combine them. Then tell me which term you're least confident in, and what kind of evidence (some clever experiment, lesion data, a drug that selectively blocked memory rewriting, whatever) would move that term the most. Lead with your final credence in the very first sentence, then walk me through the decomposition — no preamble, no re-explaining the setup back to me.
