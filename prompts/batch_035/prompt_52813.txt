I spent most of last evening arguing with a friend about democratic boundaries, and the conversation ended without resolution — partly because, as I discovered while trying to defend myself, my own positions kept sliding around. I've now written down what I actually believe, and I'd like someone to audit the set for coherence. Here is my profile:

1. I accept the all-subjected principle: a democratic body is legitimate only if its demos includes all, and only, those who are actually subjected to its coercive authority. The "and only" part matters to me — I don't think being affected by a decision, however gravely, is by itself a claim to a vote, because the all-affected principle blows the demos up to include literally everyone touched by a trade or climate policy.

2. I think long-term resident noncitizens — people who have settled, work, and raise children in a country — have a claim to full enfranchisement, not just local voting rights.

3. I think citizens who have permanently emigrated and settled abroad should nevertheless retain the right to vote in their country of origin, through overseas registration.

4. I believe future generations deserve institutionalized political representation — something like a standing commissioner or assembly for future people with real blocking power over long-horizon legislation, particularly on climate.

5. I do not think tourists, seasonal workers, or business travelers should be able to vote in elections where they happen to be present while in the country, even though they are obviously subject to its traffic laws, criminal law, and so on for the duration of their stay.

6. I think children, though more fully subject to the legal order than almost anyone, are rightly excluded from the franchise.

Having stared at this list after the argument, I can see there must be trouble somewhere — my friend certainly thought so — but I honestly can't tell which of these commitments are genuinely incompatible and which merely look that way. Please go through the set and tell me: which pairs or clusters are in real tension, which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and how severe each problem is? For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what that concession would cost me elsewhere in the list. Then, if you had to pick one revision that would clean up the most, which would it be? I'd rather have a rigorous audit than a defense of the positions — I'm prepared to give something up, though I don't yet know what.
