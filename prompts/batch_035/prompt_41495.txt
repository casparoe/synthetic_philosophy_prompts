I host a podcast on formal epistemology and the foundations of game theory. Next week's guest is a theorist who has spent his career defending the common prior assumption — the idea that rational agents in a strategic setting must, in the relevant sense, share one prior and differ only in their information. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking him to restate the view. I want at least one question pressing on whether he takes the assumption as a normative claim about rationality or merely as a modeling convention that buys tractability; one on the redescription worry — since any disagreement in priors can be re-described as differing information by expanding the state space, it is not clear what the assumption actually rules out, so ask him what empirical or conceptual content it retains; one on whether he bites the bullet of Aumann's agreeing-to-disagree result, namely that persistent political or religious disagreement between apparent epistemic peers implies that at least one party is being irrational or the common knowledge conditions quietly fail; and one on the timing of the prior — at what point in agents' lives is the shared prior supposed to be located, and what does he say to the observation that people know they acquired their most basic convictions from their upbringing, in ways that feel neither rational nor irrational but simply prior to assessment. For each of these, include a follow-up keyed to the answer he is most likely to give, anticipating his standard reply rather than merely requesting elaboration.
