I'm an undergraduate taking a seminar in philosophy of art, and I'm drafting a position paper on whether immorality — the artist's or the work's own — can bear on artistic value. Before I commit anything to paper, I want to know whether my actual views hang together. Please take on the role of a professional philosopher of art and audit my position set rather than lecture me on the literature: first brainstorm the possible tensions between my commitments without prejudging them, then tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and, for each real conflict, what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision costs. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad.

Here is where I stand:

1. Separatism about the artist: whatever a maker has done — however monstrous — it cannot itself lower the aesthetic value of their works. A painting isn't worse because its painter turned out to be a murderer.
2. Ethicism about works: when a work prescribes an immoral attitude — when it invites the audience to endorse cruelty where it ought to resist — that is an aesthetic defect in the work, not merely a moral stain sitting outside it.
3. Moderate intentionalism about interpretation: facts about an artist's life and character are legitimate evidence for which attitudes a work prescribes. A scene I read as pitch-black satire becomes hard to read that way once I learn its author was in earnest.
4. I hold that a technically brilliant film that glorifies atrocity can still be a great film, full stop.
5. From the armchair I defend (1), but in practice, learning what a certain novelist did to his students has completely soured his books for me: they now strike me as worse novels than they did before, and I can't seem to read them any other way.

My questions: Which pairs of these are actually inconsistent, and which are compatible once the right distinctions are drawn? In particular, is (5) a genuine counterexample to (1), or am I confusing how a work strikes me with what it is worth? I'd prefer to keep (2) if possible, but I'm prepared to hear that I can't have everything. Please reason through this carefully before answering, and be specific about which single revision would clean up the most.
