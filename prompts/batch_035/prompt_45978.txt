Ever since I read about "responsibility gaps" with autonomous systems I've been confused about something. The idea, as I understand it, is that when say a self-driving car kills somebody, nobody is really to blame: the engineers didn't intend the harm, the passenger wasn't driving, the company can point to all the testing they did, and the AI itself can't be blamed because it doesn't have intentions. So the responsibility just... vanishes. And if that's right, then a death caused by an autonomous vehicle is morally more like getting struck by lightning than like drunk driving — tragic, but no one's fault, just bad luck. Which would mean we should shrug it off the way we shrug off storm deaths. And by the same logic, deploying a buggy autonomous system is no more wrong than going for a walk in a thunderstorm, since neither will be anyone's fault when someone dies. I feel like something has gone wrong here but I can't locate the exact step where the argument breaks. Can you help?
