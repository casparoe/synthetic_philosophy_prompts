A close friend of mine, a theist, has spent the past year adjudicating cases of evil one at a time: for each, she rules on whether God's permitting it is defensible. She rules from intuition honed by argument, not from any system she can name, so her track record is all I have to go on. Her rulings so far:

1. The risk of betrayal by a trusted friend, as a structural feature of relationships of real trust: defensible.
2. The capacity for physical pain across animal life, understood as a warning and protective system: defensible.
3. A child's terminal illness that deepens the child's own courage and the family's love over a final year: defensible.
4. An animal born with a congenital degenerative condition, dying slowly and unwitnessed, with no effect on any person, population, or ecosystem: indefensible.
5. Chronic pain that continues in a patient after it has destroyed his capacities for patience, courage, or any further growth — even though the pain's onset decades earlier did occasion real gains in him: the ongoing condition is indefensible.
6. She refuses appeals to goods too far beyond our ken to characterize: that we cannot rule out unknown goods is, for her, not a defense of any specific evil but an abdication of the exercise, and some gesture at a good we can make sense of is required.

She has now agreed to rule on a case I have constructed for her: predation, parasitism, and the ordinary carnage of animal life in ecosystems no person will ever observe or affect — suffering that is functional only at the level of the system, regulating populations and driving selection, while doing nothing, in the individual dying animal, beyond accompanying its death.

Predict her verdict. In doing so, identify at least two rules that each fit all six of her rulings, say what each predicts on this case, and give me your best guess together with your reasons for preferring it. I would also like the further case — one she has not yet ruled on — that would most cleanly separate those rules, including at least one on which they issue in opposite verdicts. Finally, tell me how confident you are in the prediction and in each rule-attribution, and single out the point in your reasoning about her that you are least sure of.
