In the metaphysics seminar I'm designing, I want students to practice diagnosing when a dispute is merely verbal, as opposed to substantive. To that end, I've come up with a working distinction that I'd like you to apply, and I'm curious how you'd triage a set of classic debates under it.

My distinction is between three ways a dispute can be "verbal":

1. Quantifier-verbal: the parties agree on all the ordinary facts, but their quantifiers range over different domains, so each side's existence claims come out true on their own usage (e.g., one side says "there is a table" while the other says "there are only particles arranged table-wise," and neither is disagreeing about the arrangement itself).
2. Criterion-verbal: the parties agree on the underlying facts but implicitly fix the criteria for applying a predicate differently, so the fight is over which criteria are built into a word, not over what the world is like.
3. Substantive: the disagreement persists no matter how one tinkers with words; some possibility is genuinely open or closed.

Please evaluate the following six debates: (a) whether a statue is identical to the lump of clay that constitutes it; (b) whether mereological composition ever occurs (the composition question); (c) whether the Ship of Theseus, planked over year by year, remains the same ship; (d) whether holes exist; (e) whether a person survives teleportation with all memories and traits preserved; (f) whether there are objective joint-carvings in nature or only useful groupings of individuals into kinds.

For each debate, give a verbalness score from 0.0 to 1.0, where 1.0 means you judge the dispute to be entirely verbal (in either the quantifier-verbal or criterion-verbal sense) and 0.0 means entirely substantive. For any debate you score above 0.5, say explicitly whether it is quantifier-verbal or criterion-verbal (or a mix, with the mixture's rough proportions). Then produce an overall ranking from most verbal to least verbal, and for each item in the ranking give a one-to-two-sentence justification. Finally, tell me which single debate you'd pick for the seminar's first session, given that I want the clearest possible illustration of the difference between the quantifier-verbal and criterion-verbal categories, and why.
