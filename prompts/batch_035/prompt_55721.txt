But when I pushed her on what it actually claims, she gave me at least three different answers over the course of the argument, and I don't think she noticed she was switching.

It could be the claim that selves are literally fictional, narrated into existence the way an author creates a character. For each one, tell me whether it's actually defensible, and -- this is the part I care about most -- whether it's strong enough to do the work my friend wanted it to do in our argument, which was to reject my claim that if the self is just an abstraction, then "I" don't really make decisions, the brain does, and personal responsibility is a convenient fiction.

And don't just hand me the menu and shrug. Pick a winner: tell me which reading is the most charitable and most defensible version of what Dennett was getting at, whether you buy it, and what my friend would have to concede if she accepted that reading. If it turns out the best reading makes her right about some things and me right about others, say exactly which. I want a verdict I can bring back to her, with your reasons, not a "there are many perspectives here" cop-out. It's fine to flag where you're less sure, but commit to the main calls.
