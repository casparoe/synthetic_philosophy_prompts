I'm taking a philosophy of mind course and we just did the Malcolm vs. Dennett thing on whether dreams are experiences. What really got me was Dennett's alternative -- the idea that the dream gets composed at the moment of waking, that there's a library of pre-recorded dream "cassettes" and the thing that wakes you up somehow selects which one gets played back, so it feels like you dreamt it all night even though nothing was experienced during sleep. I'm meant to write a satirical piece responding to it and I'd rather do something more fun than another mock op-ed, so here's my idea, and I'd like you to write the full thing for me as a draft.

Write a corporate FAQ / customer service notice, about 700 words, from something called the Bureau of Dream Distribution (or invent a better name) -- the fictional agency that operates the cassette library. Deadpan bureaucratic voice throughout, like an actual telecom or streaming company FAQ, with numbered questions. The jokes should come from getting the philosophy right: the claim that nothing whatsoever was experienced during sleep and that playback begins only at wake-up; the probing stimulus selecting the cassette that best "explains" what woke you (so the alarm clock is effectively commissioning content); the point that no customer can ever verify when their dream was composed, since the memory of having dreamt it overnight is exactly what a wake-up composition would deliver; lucid dreamers treated as users reporting "playback during business hours" which the Bureau insists is a category error or possibly fraud; and a section on refunds, where the Bureau explains that dream reports are the only product evidence they accept, so any complaint filed is itself proof of successful delivery. Near the end, include one letter from a furious correspondent in the Wittgenstein/Malcolm tradition -- someone insisting the whole operation rests on a grammatical confusion, that "I dreamt while asleep" only makes sense because of how the report works, and that the Bureau has confused the criteria for having dreamt with a theory about when it happened. The Bureau's dismissive reply should actually engage his point rather than strawman it, since that's the joke -- their rebuttal is unfalsifiable, and they should be visibly proud of that. Don't quote any real text verbatim, obviously, and keep it legible to a reader who only half-remembers Descartes' dream argument.

Please write the whole thing in British English (so "authorised", "colour", and the like). It can run a bit long or short of 700 words if the jokes need it, but don't pad. And one typo in the FAQ itself is fine if it looks accidental -- but keep the surrounding text clean.
