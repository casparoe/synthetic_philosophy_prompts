"Skolem's paradox is that ZFC, if consistent, has a countable model, even though ZFC proves Cantor's theorem that there is no bijection between the natural numbers and the reals. The downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem says that any first-order theory with an infinite model has a countable model, so if ZFC has a model at all, it has a countable one, call it M. Since M is countable, the reals of M are countable too — there are only countably many elements to go around. But M satisfies 'the reals are uncountable', since Cantor's theorem is a theorem of ZFC. The trick is that the bijection witnessing countability lives inside M: M can count its own reals, it just can't see that its doing so. So what 'uncountable' really means, as Skolem showed, is 'no bijection in some model', which means uncountability is relative rather than absolute. Skolem took this to refute Hilbert's formalism and to show that set theory could never capture the intended uncountable infinities, and in this respect the paradox anticipates Gödel and Cohen's later proof that the continuum hypothesis is independent of ZFC. Cantor's theorem is therefore not contradicted: there is no bijection in the universe, only from outside M. The lesson is that first-order logic cannot pin down uncountability, which is why we need second-order logic to talk about the reals."
