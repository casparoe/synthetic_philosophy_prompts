My philosophy of art seminar has spent the whole semester on the fakes and forgeries debate and the two camps have basically been talking past each other. One camp holds that if a duplicate is visually indistinguishable from the original, the original has no extra aesthetic value; the other camp holds that the history of the thing — who made it, when, under what constraints, as an act of its time — is part of what we see when we look at it. Write the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: a numbered section of claims both accept (including about the Van Meegeren Vermeers and about perfect digital copies of a painting), then a disagreements table where each sides position is phrased so that side would accept the wording of its own view. Lay out the cruxes — I suspect one is whether seeing the original and seeing a perfect copy differ in experiential content, and another is whether the relevant value attaches to objects or to our experiences of objects — and for each crux give a concrete case or test both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. One more thing they got stuck on that the statement has to address: an AI trained only on a famous painter's surviving works completes her final unfinished canvas, and the workshop sells it as authentic with a disclaimer saying who actually put the brush down. Both camps should say, in wording the other side accepts, whether that object is a forgery, a completion, or a genuinely new category. Please write it in American English.
