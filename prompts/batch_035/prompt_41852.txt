I'm an undergraduate in a metaphysics seminar that has spent the past month on fundamentality and grounding. Last week a classmate asked, in something close to innocence, whether tables really exist, given that physics seems to need only particles and fields. Our TA cut the discussion short by saying that a table is nothing over and above its particles arranged table-wise, and everyone nodded as though the question had thereby been answered. As far as I can tell, it hadn't been. Since then I've heard the same phrase of art — "nothing over and above" — used by the same TA to characterize the relation of pain to neural firing, and of a contract to the behavior and legal history of the parties, and I've begun to suspect that it is several different claims traveling under one label.

Please prepare something I can bring to office hours: a list of the distinct, precise theses that "x is nothing over and above y" could be expressing in the table case, ordered from the most trivial to the most radical. For each reading, tell me (i) whether it is a platitude, a substantive but defensible claim, or flat-out false; (ii) whether, if true, it would actually settle my classmate's question, and in which direction — that is, whether it would show that tables exist, show that they don't, or show that the question rests on a mistake about what "exist" demands; and (iii) whether that same reading would carry over to the pain case, where my own suspicion is that the phrase has to be doing considerably stronger work. Please close by saying which reading the TA most likely had in mind when she used the slogan to shut the discussion down, and whether that reading, on your verdict, actually licensed shutting it down. Write in American English throughout, and please avoid citing specific papers or authors; I want the distinctions themselves, not a reading list.
