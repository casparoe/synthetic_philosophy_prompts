I run a small online reading group on population ethics and I want a "which view do you actually hold?" quiz to give them before we start. Like 8 or so questions, each one a forced choice between two concrete futures — e.g. ten billion extremely happy people vs. ten trillion people whose lives are just barely worth living, or a policy that makes future people different people than they'd otherwise be and slightly better off (so no one is harmed by the alternative), etc. Answer options should always be verdicts on the cases ("B is better", "neither is better, they're incomparable", "B is worse even though no one in B is worse off"), never school names — I want it to sort by what people say, not by which label they like. And one question needs to test the average vs. total thing where the two come apart (fewer happier vs. more, less happy).

Then a scoring key. But here's the part I care about most: build the key from the logical space first — list the combinations of verdicts the questions actually allow, then locate the named views (total utilitarian, average, person-affecting, critical level, leximin, whatever else the cases force into existance) inside that space, rather than starting from a list of famous positions and fudging the fit. Flag the unoccupied combos too — like people who accept mere addition but won't bite the bullet on the repugnant conclusion, or people who refuse the sadistic conclusion but accept its mirror image — and say for each whether nobody holds it because its incoherent, psychologically too hard, or just unexplored. Also be honest about which leaves the quiz genuinely can't separate and say so rather than pretending it sorts cleanly.

audience is smart non-philosophers so keep the cases vivid, but don't dumb down the scoring key.
