I teach political philosophy at a mid-sized state school, and through one of those service traps none of us ever learn to avoid, I've ended up as the token humanist on our state's Criminal Code Review Commission. We're working through the old morals provisions this cycle — most of them unenforced for decades — and the big fight is the adultery statute, which is still on the books and still occasionally charged, pretty much always as leverage in ugly divorce/custody fights and never on its own. Two commissioners want it repealed outright; one wants it kept and actually enforced, because in his words, if the criminal law doesn't stand for the family it stands for nothing; and the rest just want to avoid a news cycle.

I have to circulate a written recommendation before the next meeting, and I'd rather not phone it in. Draft me the memo: a concrete bottom line (straight repeal, repeal plus something, keep-but-narrow, whatever you actually think is best), with reasons the other commissioners could engage with. The temptation on our side of these debates is to treat Devlin as a punching bag and the harm principle as obviously sufficient, but the guy who wants enforcement isn't stupid, and the memo is worthless if it doesn't give the strongest version of his position a real run for its money.

One thing I want you to deal with head on: a bunch of laws we all accept enforce conventional morality well past any harm boundary — I can't sell myself into slavery, can't sell a kidney, can't duel a consenting partner — so the mere fact that adultery between consenting adults is 'victimless' can't settle the question by itself, and if the memo pretends it does, the enforcement guy will rightly tear into it. Also please don't lean too hard on constitutional arguments — the commission's charge is what the code *should* say, not what a court would let it say. If it helps, we also have a lesser-known provision on the docket this cycle (public 'lewdness' applied to a nude beach that's been tolerated for 40 years), so if you want to sketch how your recommendation's logic would handle that one too, that would actually be useful.
