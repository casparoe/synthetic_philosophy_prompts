Draft the joint statement that two historians could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: one who holds that the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki were justified military acts that ended the war at lower cost in lives than the alternatives, and one who holds that they were grave moral crimes that violated the laws of war as then understood. They have argued for years without converging, so the document must do real work, not paper over differences. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims about the historical record both can sign — covering, e.g., the state of Japan's capacity to continue the war in mid-1945, the diplomatic signalling through Moscow, the role of Soviet entry into the war, and the decision process on the American side; (2) a disagreements table in which each remaining disagreement is stated in wording the side holding that view would accept as fair, including the counterfactual cruxes I suspect divide them — whether Soviet entry plus blockade would have produced surrender without either invasion or the bombs, whether the timing of the second bombing allowed Japan's leadership any genuine window to decide, and how the civilian deaths are to be treated morally given the firebombing campaign that preceded them; (3) for each crux, the evidence or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance; and (4) an explicit separation of what is settled historical fact from what remains genuinely contested among specialists, so that neither side smuggles a contested reading in under cover of consensus. Finally, where you judge that part of their dispute is verbal rather than substantive — for instance over labels like terror bombing, war crime, or military necessity — say which part and why.
