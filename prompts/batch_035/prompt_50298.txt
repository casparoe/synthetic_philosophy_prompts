I'm in my school's animal welfare club and we've been having basically the same fight for four months now about what food to serve at our fundraiser events, and I'm starting to wonder if it's even the fight we all think it is. Quick background so this makes sense: everyone in the club agrees factory farming is terrible and that animal suffering matters, so we're NOT arguing about the basics. The fight started when one of our members, Maya, argued we should serve beef instead of chicken at our BBQ fundraiser, because a single cow provides hundreds of meals while a chicken is basically one meal, so per meal you're responsible for a tiny fraction of a cow's death instead of an entire chicken's — multiply that by everyone eating and the death count isn't even close. My friend DeShawn pushed back that this ignores all the animals that die growing feed for cattle, like rodents shredded by combines and insects killed by pesticides, and that if you count those, beef might actually kill more animals per meal than chicken, especially since broiler chickens convert feed super efficiently. Then a third guy argued pasture-raised beef changes the math again because grassland doesn't need as much tilled cropland, and someone ELSE said pastures might even be decent habitat for wild animals compared to a monoculture corn field. And one girl says the whole thing collapses because we have no clue whether insects or mice even suffer, so those deaths can't go on the scale at all and we're just waving our hands.

The annoying part is that everyone still shows up to meetings, everyone still agrees on the core stuff, and nobody has moved an inch since October. My working theory is that this is secretly an empirical disagreement — how many animals actually die per calorie of each food, and whether small animals feel anything — that we keep having as if it were an ethics disagreement. But DeShawn thinks it's the opposite: the numbers only look decisive because we've never decided what the metric even is (deaths? days of suffering? does one cow's death really count the same as one chicken's?), so we could get perfect data and still be stuck. And maybe it's a third thing entirely, where Maya is really answering "what should our club publicly serve" as a statement, DeShawn is answering "what diet minimizes harm if everyone adopted it," and they're just talking past each other.

So that's my question for you: what kind of disagreement is this actually — a hidden empirical question, a fight about the metric that we haven't noticed we're having, two different questions under one name, or something else I haven't thought of? Diagnose it using what each side is actually claiming, don't just survey the arguments. And then tell me honestly whether anything would count as progress — better numbers on crop deaths, a settled answer on insect pain, an agreed-upon metric, some distinction someone could draw — or whether the stall is telling us something, like that the question we're asking is the wrong one. Please answer in American English.
