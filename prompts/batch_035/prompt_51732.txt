Construct a case in which an agent's preferences form a strict cycle over three options — it strictly prefers B to A, C to B, and A to C — and yet the agent cannot be turned into a money pump, even though trades are free, the counterparty is patient, fully informed of the agent's dispositions, and under no time pressure, and there is no commitment technology and no outside option. The options should be concrete and everyday (holiday packages, used cars, investment portfolios — anything but trolleys). The blocking feature must not be an external restriction on trading; it must live in the agent's own choice dispositions. It also must not be the degenerate solution of refusing every trade: the agent should accept at least the first two steps of the standard pump before the exploitation stalls.

After presenting the case, run the standard money pump step by step and show exactly where and why it stalls; identify which feature of the agent's dispositions is doing the work; and assess whether that feature can be defended on independent grounds or merely mimics rationality while violating some other coherence condition. Then address the philosophical upshot: is non-exploitability the ground of preference rationality or only a symptom of it? Should a defender of coherence arguments for expected utility maximization concede that your agent is rational — perhaps on the grounds that money-pump arguments only ever supported acyclicity, not full conformity to expected utility — or should she instead argue the case away, either by exhibiting a cleverer pump that circumvents the blocking feature or by charging the agent with a distinct incoherence?
