Hi, I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm working on a column about whether pleasure is even one thing. Philosophers apparently call this the heterogeneity problem for hedonism: the taste of a ripe peach, the click of finally getting a proof, the warm afterglow of a nap — they don't seem to share any felt quality at all. So what exactly do they have in common that makes them all pleasures? A philosopher I interviewed swears the common thread is attitudinal rather than phenomenological: a pleasure is just any state you endorse and would rather continue than stop, however it feels. That seems suspiciously thin to me — by that logic a pain would just be any state you'd rather stop, and surely dreading a boring meeting doesnt make the meeting painful. Can you explain the heterogeneity problem properly, and then assess whether the attitudinal move actually solves it or just relabels it? Bonus points if you can say whether it handles oddball cases like relief or the pleasure of remembering something, where the felt quality and the attitude seem to pull apart.
