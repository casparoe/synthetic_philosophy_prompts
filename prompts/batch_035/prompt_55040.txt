A few months ago my adult son wrote to tell me he no longer believes in God. This was not rebellion or reaction: for years beforehand he prayed honestly, kept up the practices he was raised in, asked repeatedly for even a modest sense of God's presence, and — by his account, which I have every reason to trust — received nothing. He eventually concluded that the silence was itself the answer. I have been a theist all my life and intend to remain one. My own prayer life has felt genuinely reciprocal; at two or three decisive moments I received what I can only describe as felt guidance that I did not manufacture, and my faith rests partly on those episodes. Before I reply to his letter I want to know whether what I actually believe hangs together, so I have written out the full set as I hold it:

1. God exists and is perfectly loving.
2. In my own case God has been perceptibly present: the felt guidance I received at those decisive moments was real, not something I produced.
3. My son's nonbelief was nonresistant: he sought sincerely and persistently over years, and did not find.
4. God's hiddenness is deliberate policy rather than neglect: overwhelming evidence would leave no room for a free response, and God wants relationship freely given rather than coerced, so God deliberately stays just beyond proof.
5. God is impartial: divine love does not play favorites among those who seek him.
6. No one's ultimate fate turns on a mere accident of evidence: God does not allow anyone to be finally separated from him simply because they happened not to be convinced.

I am asking for an audit, not a sermon or an expression of sympathy. Please go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which merely appear to be; for each genuine tension, rank it by severity, say what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair would cost me elsewhere in the set; identify the apparent tensions and explain exactly where the appearance dissolves; and then tell me which single revision, if any, would clean up the most. I have stated each commitment exactly as I hold it, without loading the dice, and I am not looking to be told to drop whichever one is most awkward given my son's situation — I genuinely do not know where, or whether, the problem lies.
