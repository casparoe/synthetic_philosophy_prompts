A friend of mine who works on formal epistemology has produced an argument that concludes I am permitted to be flat-out certain that I will lose next week's lottery while my evidence makes me 99.9 per cent confident I will win. He claims it is just the sorites with the serial numbers filed off, and that since everyone already knows how to handle the sorites, I should know how to handle this. I do not. Here is the argument:

(1) If your evidence makes you 99.9 per cent confident that p, then believing outright that p — treating it as settled in your reasoning, assertion and action — is epistemically permissible.
(2) A difference between two total evidential situations too small for you to detect by reflection cannot by itself flip outright belief from permissible to impermissible: if believing p is permissible at 99.9 per cent confidence, it is permissible at 99.8999 per cent, since you could not tell those conditions apart from the inside.
(3) This step may be iterated: by a chain of undetectable decrements we walk from 99.9 per cent confidence down to 0.1 per cent, and no single step does the flipping.
(4) So it is permissible to believe outright that you will lose the lottery while being 99.9 per cent confident that you will win — and, generalising, to be fully convinced of a proposition your own evidence renders almost certainly false.
(5) That is absurd.
(6) So something before (4) is false.

I can see three suspects. Rejecting (2) buys sharp cutoffs in permissibility between evidential situations that no one could ever distinguish, which strikes me as a stranger purchase than sharp cutoffs between heaps and non-heaps — but I cannot say exactly why. Rejecting (3) means conceding that each imperceptible difference is harmless in isolation while the accumulated difference is not, which sounds like saying the chain is sound at every link and broken as a whole. Rejecting (1) seems cheapest until I notice that the argument needs (1) only as a foothold: deny it and I owe a story about when high confidence does suffice, and why that story cannot be walked down. Please tell me which premise to give up, why it looked fine, and what each rejection costs — and please tell me whether the sorites parallel genuinely transfers, since rational belief looks like a normative property in a way heapedness does not, and I want to know whether that difference does any work or is just comfort.
