My friend and I got into a long argument about how science textbooks are organized. Every chapter explains the upper level by the level below — chemistry gets explained by molecular structure, molecular structure by atomic physics — and you never once see a section going the other way, explaining why electrons behave as they do by appeal to chemistry. My friend says this is just a writing habit inherited from how the fields historically got split up, and that nothing metaphysical is implied. I think the convention presupposes more: at minimum that explanation between levels is asymmetric, and maybe that there's a well-founded ordering with no cycles. Which of us is closer? And if I'm right, does the practice also commit you to an absolute bottom level (a fundamental physics), or is local well-foundedness enough — and what exactly would break, in the textbooks or in science, if some pair of levels mutually grounded each other? Answer in at most 130 words.
