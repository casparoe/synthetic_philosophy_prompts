I'm a PhD student in environmental history, which means I'm constantly caught between the geologists and the humanities people, and the fight over when the Anthropocene started has gotten weird. The stratigraphers spent 15 years on this, the Working Group eventually landed on the mid-20th century with Crawford Lake as the reference site (plutonium in the sediment, golden spike and all), and then in March 2024 the Quaternary subcommission just voted the whole proposal down -- and now some of them are accusing each other of procedural irregularities over the vote itself. Meanwhile in my own corner of the field people treat the question as basically settled, except they settle it differently: some like 1610 (the CO2 dip after the collapse of indigenous American populations, the so-called Orbis spike), some like the Industrial Revolution, some want to push it back to early agriculture, and a few think any of these choices smuggles in a Eurocentric story about which humans count.

My working hypothesis is that this was never really a factual disagreement about rocks. The geologists are answering "is there a formally definable stratigraphic unit with a sharp lower boundary here," which needs a single golden spike and maybe nothing satisfies it, while everyone else is using the Anthropocene to mean something like "humans became a planetary force," which doesn't need a sharp start date at all. So the two camps might not even be disagreeing -- they might just be running one word on two different definitions. But I'm not fully convinced by my own diagnosis, because the fight got genuinely bitter, and people don't usually get bitter over a verbal dispute. So: is there a hidden substantive question in here, maybe about whether the mid-20th century really is qualitatively distinct from earlier human impact, or about who has the standing to define a term that half the academy now uses? Is it telling that geologists tried to settle it *by vote* -- does the fact that the vote went against the Anthropocene mean it "isn't real" in any sense beyond the bookkeeping of stratigraphic nomenclature? I'd love a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is, and what, if anything, would count as progress -- not another tour of the candidate start dates, we've all read those essays.
