For a reading group on social norms I've written up my position on convention, roughly in the spirit of David Lewis's *Convention* (1969), and I'd like a consistency check before I circulate it. Here is the package: (1) a convention is a solution to a coordination problem — a regularity that nearly everyone conforms to, expects others to conform to, and would rather conform to provided nearly everyone else does, in a situation where some alternative regularity would have served about as well; (2) conventions require common knowledge: unless the parties expect the conformity of others, expect the others to expect it, and so on, there is no convention, only a habit; (3) driving on the right is a paradigm convention, even though no driver has met, or could enumerate, most of the people she is coordinating with; (4) punishment-backed norms such as dueling or foot-binding, where the practice persists because deviation is sanctioned, are conventions in the full sense and not merely regularities propped up by penalties; (5) conventions are arbitrary, in that among the available alternatives the payoffs do not favor the one actually adopted — and this arbitrariness is compatible with our actual driving side having a straightforward historical explanation; (6) language is conventional in the same sense as driving on one side of the road, so that meaning is just one more regularity of this kind; (7) conventions by themselves generate no obligations, so unilateral deviation from one is at most imprudent, never wrong; and (8) breaking a promise is wrong, and promising is itself a convention. Go through these and tell me which pairs are in real tension and how badly, which only appear to be, and what the cheapest revision would be for each genuine tension — please rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally serious, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I genuinely don't know where the problem lies, so don't read into my ordering a preference about what should be dropped.
