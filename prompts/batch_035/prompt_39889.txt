So I'm a phil of mind PhD student and my labmate Elena (dissertation on affect, very opinionated) has this running list of verdicts about whether particular pains are bad for the person feeling them, and I want to figure out what rule she's actually running before I grill her about it at reading group. Her verdicts so far: an ordinary tension headache — bad for you; the pain of a pain asymbolia patient (the folks with cingulate damage who report that it hurts but seem basically amused by it, don't reach for painkillers, don't withdraw) — not bad; a masochist's pain during a session they explicitly asked for — not bad; that same masochist's unwanted soreness the next morning — bad; a soccer player who doesn't notice a shin injury until after the match — not bad at the time, bad once noticed; and pure spinal nociception in someone under anesthesia — not bad, not even really a pain. All of this seems consistent with at least two rules and I can't tell which she's using. Rule 1: a pain is bad for you iff the sensation itself is unpleasant, where asymbolia somehow strips the unpleasantness while leaving the feeling. Rule 2: a pain is bad iff the subject would rather not have it, so asymbolics don't mind it and masochists want it, hence not bad.

Predict Elena's verdict, spell out the candidate rules that fit her track record just as well as each other, say what each one predicts about the admin, and tell me which further case would seperate those rules more cleanly than this one does.
