My older sister is halfway through an evo-bio PhD and I just started vet school, so family dinners have turned into her auditing my anatomy lectures. Everytime the lecturer says "the function of X is Y" she has opinions, and to keep myself sane I've started writing down her actual verdicts like she's a case law I can reverse-engineer. Problem is I can't figure out the rule. Here's her track record so far: the kidney's function is filtering blood, and she'll say that even about a cat with congenital renal dysplasia whose kidneys filter basically nothing — they still have the function, they're just terrible at it. But the pelvic bones inside a whale skeleton have NO function, full stop, and when I offered that maybe their function is walking but they just fail to perform it, she looked at me like I'd put ketchup on her ice cream. On a goat farm she visited there's a gene variant present in maybe 4% of the herd that protects against a parasite when it's present, and she says yes, that's genuinely its function, even though most goats don't have it. On the other hand a dog with a benign tumor that secretes some hormone detectable on bloodwork — the tumor cells are not "functioning" to secrete it, that's just something they do, not a function, whatever the lab report implies. And she's fine saying the scent gland on some deer's leg has the function of marking territory even though it apparently started out as a byproduct of something else ages ago.

So now here's the thing, she texted me last night about a mouse in her lab. It was born with a brand new mutation, single individual, no siblings, no offspring yet, nothing selected for anything — and it can digest a sugar that the rest of the strain can't touch. She wants my guess before she tells me her verdict (there's a milkshake riding on this). What do you think she'll say — does that mutation have the function of digesting the sugar? More than the yes/no, I want to know what rule she seems to be running given the verdicts above, what other rules would fit those same verdicts just as well, and where those rules come apart on the mouse. I can half-see a backward-looking story (functions come from some kind of history) and a forward-looking one (functions are about what a part contributes to the animal right now), and I suspect the mouse is exactly where they split, but I don't trust myself to say which one explains her other verdicts better — like, does the failing kidney or the whale pelvis sit closer to the mouse case?

And if her track record really is consistent with more than one rule, tell me what I should spring on her next to settle it — I get one more dinner before she claims the milkshake, so I want the question that actually discriminates between the surviving candidate rules, not just another case they'd all answer the same way.
