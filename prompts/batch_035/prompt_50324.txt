I manage day-to-day operations at a sanctuary for rescued farm animals — around 80 animals, mostly pigs, cows, goats and chickens, plus a few horses. Every month our welfare committee has to make small husbandry and spending calls, and the arguments go in circles, so we want a standing procedure we can reuse. The principle the board settled on (a trustee who does ethics talked us into it, and I honestly can't reconstruct the whole argument) goes like this: for pain above some intensity, no amount of pleasure can ever compensate it — that pain just has to be dealt with, full stop, it can't be bought off with pleasant things. For pain below that line, pleasure and pain can be weighed against each other somehow, though we're pretty vague on how. Thats it — thats the whole theory we've got.

Write me a procedure the committee can actually run at a monthly meeting: our vet gives rough pain estimates, nobody on the committee is a philosopher, and decisions can't sit for more than a month. It should say what order to ask the questions in, what counts as a good enough answer to move on, and where it stops and hands off to a straight vote because the remaining question is a value question no procedure can settle — like where exactly the threshold sits, since the board refuses to give a number and I suspect no number exists that isn't just someones gut. The procedure also has to handle the case where the animal itself seems to choose the painful thing — a horse that lives for hard running that's wrecking her joints — since I don't know if her choosing it counts as weighing on the scale or not.

Then run it on three real-ish cases we've argued about: (1) new foraging and play enrichment the animals very obviously love, versus paying for extra overnight staffing after surgeries, which cuts moderate post-op pain; (2) a pig with a chronic pressure sore, constant but mild at any given moment, versus a one-off procedure involving several days of strong pain that would end the sore for good; (3) integrating two new goats into the main herd, which means a few weeks of fighting and real injuries but unlocks years of what looks like genuine social pleasure. That last one worries me: if I understand our principle, the years of pleasure can't be put on the scale against the intense pain of the integration period at all. If the procedure honestly says the goat integration isn't justified, or is only justified by some argument on the pain side, then say so plainly, including if it gives answers the committee won't like. And tell us what the procedure can't do — where it'll misfire, not just where it's slow.
