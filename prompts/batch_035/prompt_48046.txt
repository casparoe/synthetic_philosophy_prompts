Draft the joint statement that a hard incompatibilist and a reasons-responsiveness compatibilist could both sign after a two-year adversarial collaboration on free will and basic-desert moral responsibility.

Some constraints on the document:

1. It must open with a glossary both parties have signed off on, fixing the senses of "free will," "moral responsibility," "basic desert," "leeway," "sourcehood," and "reasons-responsiveness" — because both parties suspect that some of their past disputes turned on using "free will" to mean different things (e.g., the ability to do otherwise versus being the genuine source of one's action), and the statement should say explicitly which residual disagreements, if any, they diagnosed as merely verbal and which as substantive.

2. The agreed-claims section (numbered) should include only claims each party would endorse under the glossary's terms — including claims about what Frankfurt cases and manipulation arguments each establish and fail to establish, what determinism itself does and does not imply, and what would follow if indeterministic noise were discovered in human decision-making.

3. The disagreements section must be a table in which, for each disputed point, the compatibilist's position and the incompatibilist's position are each worded so that its own side would accept the phrasing, without the drafting side's slant. At least one disagreement must concern the manipulation argument's central move: whether the difference between an agent whose deliberation is covertly engineered and an agent shaped by ordinary deterministic causes is morally relevant, and if so, what it is.

4. The cruxes section must identify the subquestions such that settling them would move at least one party, and for each crux specify an argument, case, or finding that both parties agree would bear on it, with each party's prediction recorded in advance. At minimum, include as a crux-test the following exercise both accept as probative: a continuum of cases running from an agent whose every choice is dictated moment-to-moment by a hidden designer, through an agent engineered once at birth with no further intervention, to an agent shaped by ordinary heredity and upbringing in a deterministic world. Each party must state, before the exercise, the exact point on the continuum at which they predict their opponent's responsibility judgments will become unstable or ad hoc, and both must agree in advance on what pattern of marking would count as evidence against each of them.

5. The statement must close with a jointly authored paragraph on what, if anything, both parties now agree would actually move the debate, as distinct from what merely restates it.

Begin with the joint statement itself, with no preface or commentary.
