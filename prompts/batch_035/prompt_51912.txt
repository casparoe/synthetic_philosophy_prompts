I've committed to a particular version of the fine-tuning argument and I want stress-testing, not the standard tour of objections. My version goes: the fact that the constants and initial conditions fall in the narrow life-permitting range is strong evidence for design, because design raises the probability of a life-permitting universe relative to chance; the multiverse-plus-anthropic-selection response commits an inverse gambler's fallacy, since positing many universes can't explain why *this* one is fine-tuned, any more than seeing double sixes on this table is explained by learning the casino has thousands of tables; and the argument survives the denial of objective probabilities over the constants, because the evidential point still goes through with epistemic probabilities over a range of possible values. I'm not moved by the slogans "you can't put a probability distribution on the constants" or "the design hypothesis predicts nothing" — I want to know what would specifically count against this package, not hear the slogans again.

So give me the defeaters. What observations or arguments would be fatal if they held — e.g. what if further physics showed the life-permitting region is actually broad, or what if something like many-worlds quantum mechanics independently delivered a multiverse for free, so the multiverse response sheds its ad-hocness penalty: does that change the evidential situation itself or only the prior weights? Which defeaters are merely awkward? I'm most interested in the internal pressure points: the objection that design predicts a universe *optimally* tuned for life rather than barely permitting it (why so much empty space, why is so much of the habitable parameter range unused); the worry that the "range" of possible constants is ill-defined, so the whole "narrow range" claim smuggles in an arbitrary measure and normalizability is doing undisclosed work; and whether the inverse-gambler charge can be flipped back on me — whether positing a designer is itself a way of positing "many attempts" in some disguised sense, or whether observer-selection effects really do legitimately alter evidential force elsewhere in cosmology in a way that would license the multiverse responder's move.

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones serious defenders of a view like mine would themselves concede as genuine exposure versus dismiss as confused, and — the part I care most about — give me an honest verdict on whether my version has been insulated into unfalsifiability by the retreat from objective chance to epistemic probability. If it has, is that disqualifying for an evidential argument of this kind, or is that just what evidential arguments about unique events have to look like? No citations or reading lists please, just the arguments.
