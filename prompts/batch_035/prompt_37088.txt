I am a final-year secondary school student writing an extended essay in philosophy, and I want to stress-test a thesis before I commit to defending it. My view is that patriotism is a virtue — but not in the vague "love of one's country" sense. The version I want to defend is this: partiality toward compatriots is structurally like partiality toward family. Both, I claim, grow out of a relationship of shared formative dependence. Just as one's obligations to parents and siblings are not contracted for but arise from having been formed by them, so one's obligations to fellow citizens arise from having been formed by a shared language, shared institutions, and a common civic life. From this I conclude that the patriot's special concern for compatriots — preferring their welfare, defending their interests, accepting sacrifices for them that one would not accept for strangers — is a virtue rather than a prejudice.

What I need is a map of what would count against this view, so that I know in advance where my essay is exposed. Please sort the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be explicit about three things. First, where the family analogy might break — for instance, whether a country, unlike a family, can act as a single responsible agent, so that loyalty to it can be loyalty to a wrongdoer in a way that family loyalty rarely is, and whether this difference matters to my argument. Second, what my view predicts that its rivals do not: does it commit me to holding that a citizen should prefer modest benefits to compatriots over greater benefits to foreigners, and is there a principled place for me to stop, or only an arbitrary one? Third, which of the objections a defender of the view would himself concede as counting against it, as opposed to those that only its critics press. I would also like an honest verdict on whether the view, once qualified in the usual ways — that it holds only where the country is broadly just, only where the partiality stays proportionate, and so on — has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and whether such insulation would be a defect in a view of this kind. Finally, the most useful thing you could give me, if it exists, would be a case in which the two claims I have been running together — that partiality toward compatriots is permissible and that it is virtuous — come apart.
