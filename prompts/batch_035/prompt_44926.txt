I'm a PhD student working at the border of metaphysics and philosophy of biology, and I've spent the last month stuck on whether origin essentialism about organisms is actually true — not whether it's popular, but whether the thesis itself survives contact with the developmental biology I've been reading. The precise proposition I care about is: the concrete organism I am could not have developed from a numerically different sperm or egg than the ones I actually came from; had a different sperm from my father fertilized my mother's egg, no one would exist who is me, only a non-identical sibling. My supervisor, a fairly orthodox Kripkean, puts his credence in the thesis around 0.9 and treats my hesitation as a failure of nerve; I keep drifting toward 0.5–0.6 and can't tell whether I have a real argument or just an aversion to modal metaphysics.

What I'd like from you is your own credence, with the reasoning fully decomposed rather than asserted. The terms I'd expect in the decomposition are things like: whether de re modal claims about unoriginated-origin individuals are even in order, or whether counterpart-theoretic or conventionalist accounts make the question ill-posed rather than false; whether Kripke-style arguments from conceivability failures actually establish necessity of origin for organisms, or only for artifacts and their makers; where my origination is located in the first place — the zygote, or the later fetus, or the person once consciousness arrives — and whether the thesis changes strength depending on that choice; and how the messy biological cases cut, which is what's been gnawing at me. Specifically: monozygotic twinning, where two people originate from one zygote and it seems impossible for both to be identical to whatever originated at conception; tetragametic chimerism, where two fertilized eggs fuse into a single organism, so the individual appears to have two origins; and the case of sibling gametes — a different sperm from the same father carrying nearly the same genome, which tests whether the necessity is numerical or merely qualitative. Assign each term a rough probability with a line of justification, say how the terms combine into your final number, and tell me which term the number is most sensitive to.
