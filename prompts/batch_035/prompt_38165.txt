retired chemist here — 30-odd years in pharma, before that a stretch in academic labs — and since I stopped working I've gotten proper deep into philosophy of science, especially this inside view / outside view stuff. I've ended up holding a bunch of positions that each felt obviously right at the time, but my son (a philosopher, insufferable) says they can't all be true, and honeslty I've lost track myself. Here's roughly where I stand:

1. Inside my own field I always trusted my mechanistic hunches over any base rate. When I actually knew the chemistry, "compounds like this fail 90% of the time" told me nothing I cared about.
2. Outside my field I'm an out-and-out epistemic modesty guy: I defer to the climatologists, the economists, the whole consensus apparatus, and I think people who don't are mostly fooling themselves.
3. I think the biggest turns in the history of science happened because somebody ignored the consensus — Wegener with continental drift, Marshall and Warren with ulcers — and I think those people were doing the epistemically *right* thing, not just the lucky thing.
4. I think you can tell, from the inside, when you've earned the right to your own view on a topic. There's a felt difference between genuinely understanding a domain and just having opinions, and I'm pretty confident I can make that call about my own case.
5. I think the outside view is basically a forecasting tool. It's great for telling you your project will run late and your company will fail, but it has nothing to say about which theory is true.
6. I've come around to thinking calibration — being right X% of the times you claim X% — is the gold standard for rationall belief.

So which of these hang together, which ones clash, and how badly? My son won't rank them, he just says I contradict myself all over the place. Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion: order the clashes worst-first, say what would have to give in each one, and flag any clash that only *looks* like a clash — I suspect there's at least one of those in the pile, and I'd like to know which.
