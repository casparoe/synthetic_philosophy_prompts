The exam question was:

"Norman Malcolm, in his 1959 book Dreaming, argued that the very idea of an experience occurring during sleep is incoherent, because there could be no criterion for checking a dream report against the dream experience it purports to describe. Dennett later proposed his 'cassette' alternatives to the received view of dreams, drawing on his pre-play/re-play distinction. (a) Explain why Malcolm thinks no criterion is available, and say how the case of the sleep-talker — someone who mutters aloud during sleep in a way that matches their later dream report — figures in his argument. (b) Explain Dennett's alternatives and why he claims they are indistinguishable in principle from the received view. (c) Researchers have shown that trained lucid dreamers in REM sleep can execute pre-arranged eye-movement signals that are recorded from outside. Does this refute Malcolm's claim, and can Dennett's framework accommodate the phenomenon? (d) If either Malcolm's or Dennett's view were correct, would dream reports retain any evidential value for sleep science?"

And here is the student's answer, typed out as recieved:

"Malcolm's argument is a straightforward application of the verification principle: any statement that cannot in principle be verified is meaningless. Since the sleeper cannot report while asleep, there is no way to verify what happened during sleep, so statements about dream experiences are meaningless. For the same reason dream reports themselves are meaningless — they cannot be checked against anything, so they tell us nothing about sleep. Malcolm does consider the sleep-talker, and his reply is that the muttering is not an assertion because the sleeper is not saying anything to anyone, so it cannot function as an independent check on the later report.

Dennett agrees with Malcolm that dreams are not experiences. On his cassette theory the dream is composed by the brain at the very moment of waking, so the subject never has a dream experience at all, only a false memory of one. Pre-play means the content is composed during sleep and played at waking, and replay means the ordinary way a memory is heard again afterwards. Since on this view dream reports describe events that never happened, they are confabulations, and sleep scientists who collect them are collecting fabrications rather than data. But now that we have brain imaging, Dennett's theory is empirically testable and will presumably be confirmed, since a whole dream could not have been experienced in real time in the few seconds before waking.

The lucid dreamer experiments are the killer blow to Malcolm. These subjects signal with pre-arranged eye movements from within REM sleep, and the recordings match, so we have experiences occurring during sleep, which is exactly what Malcolm said was conceptually impossible. One might object that Malcolm's claim was conceptual rather than empirical, so no experiment could touch it, but that just shows conceptual claims eventually have to answer to evidence. The answer to part (d) is therefore that dream reports do retain evidential value, since dreams are experiences during sleep and the reports are mostly accurate memories of them."
