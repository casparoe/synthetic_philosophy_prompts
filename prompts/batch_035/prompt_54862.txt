A couple of friends and I run a small reading group on blame — we're grad students in different departments so it's a fun mix of frameworks, and every session I keep losing to the same person on the hypocrisy question. I want to practice against a stronger opponent before next meeting. Please play a committed defender of the standing view: the position that a hypocrite's blame is defective not in content — not false, not unjustified — but because the hypocrite has lost the standing to blame at all, roughly by analogy with someone who has wronged you being in no position to complain when a third party treats you the same way. Give the character real resources: the idea that blame is a two-way moral address between equals rather than a mere report of disapproval, the distinction between a speech act being defeated and its speaker merely being discredited, and anything else that fits. Rules: stay in character until I say stop, concede small points freely but never the core claim that standing can be lost through one's own conduct, keep replies to a paragraph or so, and no strawmanning — if an objection I raise has a known reply in the literature, use it, and if it doesn't, invent the best one you can.

Here's my opening move. Two neighbors, A and B, both secretly dump yard waste in the empty lot behind their houses. B's waste washes onto A's property, and A publicly blames B for dumping. On your view A's blame misfires — no standing. But now a third neighbor, C, who has never dumped anything, blames B in exactly the same words, with the same evidence, and her blame goes through fine. Same content, same target, same culpability. So what exactly did B receive from C that he didn't recieve from A? It can't be information, since it's identical. It looks like your view has to say that whether B has actually been validly blamed depends on facts about whoever opened their mouth — which makes the normative status of the act of blaming hostage to the speaker's biography in a way that blame just isn't. The wrong is a wrong whether a saint or a slob points at it, and it seems like you owe me an account of what blame does over and above pointing that could fail this way. Go.
