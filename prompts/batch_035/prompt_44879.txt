I'm prepping a discussion section on Langdon Winner's 1980 essay "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" and I want a fresh case for it instead of recycling his low bridges, which everyone now agrees is pretty shaky as history anyway. What I want is a thought experiment built to spec, and the spec is the hard part, so let me be precise.

The scenario: one single artifact, deployed twice. In one deployment it is politically inert; in the other it clearly has politics. And here's the catch — it has to get there with (a) zero design intent, no Moses figure scheming in the background, no procurement officer quietly steering things either, and (b) nothing "inherently political" about the technology itself, no Winner-style "this machine demands a whole social order around it" necessity. The political meaning has to arrive from somewhere else, and the case has to make visible where it comes from. The two deployments should be identical in every respect except one variable, and isolating that variable is your job — you can't just stipulate it. The verdicts (inert here, political there) have to fall out of how the thing actually gets used, not out of the tone of the description. If one deployment reads like a dystopia and the other reads like an ad, you've cheated. Same artifact throughout, too — no quietly changing the settings between deployments.

Constraints: realistic, present-day, mundane. Municipal or office-level stuff, nothing sci-fi, nothing exotic. The scenario itself should be one paragraph. And to be clear on what "zero design intent" means: I don't just mean nobody built it to oppress anybody, I mean no mundane steering either — no accidental de facto intent smuggled back in.

Then do the walkthrough. What a technological determinist would say — the Winner sense-1 reading, technologies that require or strongly favor a particular social order — and exactly where the case resists that reading. What a social constructivist would say, and where they'd get too comfortable (they love these cases a little too much, so push back on them somewhere). Which single feature of the setup is doing the work. What happens to the case if you delete that feature. And how a Winner-defender would counterattack — my guess is the move is to redescribe the surrounding "circumstance" as part of the technical system proper, so tell me whether that move is even available here or whether the case blocks it.

Casual register throughout is fine, its for a seminar discussion not a paper. One more thing: tell me what the case is actually testing — whether "having politics" turns out to be a property of the thing or of the arrangement it lands in — and whether your case decides that question or just dramatizes it. I'd rather know which.
