You are a political philosopher with expertise in territorial rights, the ethics of immigration, and the ethics of refuge. Please think carefully and begin with some brief brainstorming before committing to your audit.

Context: I work at a policy institute, and a colleague is drafting a position paper arguing for broad state discretion over asylum admissions. The backbone of every draft is an analogy he refuses to give up: a political community is like a private household. Homeowners may decide whom to invite over, and they do nothing wrong when they simply don't answer the door; a house is a place where a family pursues its shared life, and strangers have no claim to be let into it. In the current draft he extends the analogy in two directions I want to stress-test before the paper goes out. First, he says the analogy covers not only the front door (exclusion at the border) but also the removal of guests: just as a host may end the party and ask a guest to leave, even into the cold, a state may deport. Second, he concedes one limit — a homeowner may not push someone off the porch into a blizzard — but insists this maps only onto the rule against returning people to danger, generating at most a duty of temporary shelter: the stranger sleeps in the garage until the storm passes, but acquires no claim to a place in the household itself.

Please audit the analogy. Give me a correspondence table (house, homeowner, family members, door, guest, blizzard, garage, and anything else doing real structural work); then say where the mapping breaks; then distinguish the disanalogies that matter for his intended use from the ones that don't — for example, whether the fact that a state's territory is not owned the way a house is, that the "homeowners" found the door already placed rather than placing it themselves, or that the guests of states are typically people with no home to go back to rather than people with one, bear on exclusion, on deportation, or on both. Assess whether his two extensions are genuinely supported by the analogy or are being smuggled in past it, and whether the blizzard exception, as he reads it, captures the actual limits of the duty it gestures at. If there is a better analogy for the moral situation of a state facing asylum-seekers, name it and say what it gets right that the household picture gets wrong.
