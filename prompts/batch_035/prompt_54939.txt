So I'm a novelist, and my next book is set in a fictional alpine canton that runs its affairs Swiss-style — citizens' initiatives, mandatory referendums, the whole apparatus, scaled down to one valley of about four thousand people. There's a pivotal scene where the village votes on whether to sell a parcel of communal alp to a hotel developer, and I don't want to write it from vibes. I want to know how people actually behave when they're handed real agenda-setting power, not just a yes/no box someone else wrote for them.

So here's my plan: before I write the scene, I'm going to run a simulation with my writing group — about a dozen adults, we meet thursday evenings, I can get two hours out of them. Design the simulation for me. It should have the actual moving parts of Swiss-style direct democracy, compressed into a living room: a phase where small groups draft ballot measures themselves (I suspect the interesting part is who ends up deciding what goes on the ballot and in whose words, so build that in), a rule reproducing the double-majority thing where a measure needs both a majority of voters and a majority of districts to pass, and districts of deliberately unequal size so the weighting actually bites. I want at least one designed moment where the popular-vote winner loses overall, and the room has to sit with whether that's a bug or a feature. Final vote by secret ballot, no show of hands.

Then a debrief mapping what happened onto the real distinction people gloss over — democracy as deliberation versus democracy as aggregation — tying each phase of the evening to the part of the idea it dramatized. And be honest with me about two things: what will predictably go wrong when I run this with novelists, who are professionally incapable of staying on topic, and which feature of actual referendum politics the living-room version just cannot reproduce, so I know where my scene is allowed to cheat.
