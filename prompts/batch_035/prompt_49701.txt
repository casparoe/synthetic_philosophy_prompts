So a bit of background on me: I'm a second-year grad student, mostly doing phil of language, but I sat in on a metaphysics seminar this spring where we spent a good chunk of the semester on how objects persist through time, and I came out of it genuinely puzzled in a way I wasn't with other debates. On one side you have the perdurantists, who say a persisting thing is spread out through time the way it's spread out through space -- you and your infancy are related as temporal parts of one four-dimensional worm. On the other side the endurantists, who say no, the very same whole object is fully present at every moment it exists; it doesn't have temporal parts, it just is at different times, the way I'm at different places. This fight has been running basically since the early eighties, and from where I'm sitting it looks completely frozen: every move has a countermove that everyone already knows. The endurantist's big weapon is change: how can one and the same thing be straight at noon and bent at noon-fifteen without contradicting itself? The perdurantist answers that the straight thing and the bent thing are different temporal parts, no contradiction. The endurantist then says you've explained away change rather than accounted for it -- nothing really changes on your picture, relations between parts do all the work, which is why some endurantists grumble that the worm view is "static" in a way they can't quite pin down. The perdurantist counter-counter is that the alternatives get ugly fast: either relativize the property to a time, or pull some trick like saying the object "bends bentwise" instead of having a bent part, and endurantists themselves admit these look ad hoc. Then there are the side skirmishes: perdurantists like pointing out that their view is what relativity seems to want anyway, since there's no fact about which spacetime slice is "the present"; endurantists retort that ordinary counting and ordinary speech ("Socrates is sitting") are on their side, and the perdurantist has to tell us we count by kind rather than by object, which feels like changing the subject. Both camps have smart people, the same data as it were, and nobody has moved in decades.

My working hypothesis, offered as just a hypothesis: this isn't a substantive dispute about how the world is at all, but a standoff between two different conceptions of what a metaphysical explanation owes you -- the perdurantists are optimizing for a clean, unified, relativistically friendly ontology where nothing is primitive, and the endurantists are insisting that a theory of persistence has to vindicate the manifest fact that things genuinely change and that the thing here now is numerically the thing that was there before. If that's right, the debate is like two groups grading exams with different rubrics, and it will never terminate. But I'm not confident -- maybe there's a hidden substantive question I'm not seeing, something about identity conditions or about what makes the earlier part and the later part parts of one thing rather than two things, and one side just hasn't found the argument yet.

Support whatever you say with what each side actually claims, including the stuff I mentioned above. And then tell me honestly what, if anything, would count as progress given your diagnosis: some new distinction, a way of restating the question, results from physics, a formal result about identity -- or an argument that this one is just permanently stuck, and why. Throughout, please be explicit about how confident you are in each claim, and especially flag the single point in your diagnosis you're least sure of, since that's presumably where I should push next.
