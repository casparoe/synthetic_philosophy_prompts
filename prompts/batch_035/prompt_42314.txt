ok so for a philosophy of maths reading group I've been asked to write down where I stand on constructivism, and before I send it round I want to check that my position actually hangs together. here's roughly the list I've got:

(1) I fully buy the constructive reading of the connectives: proving an existence claim means producing a witness or a method for finding one, proving a disjunction means proving one of the disjuncts, etc. this just seems like what the words *mean* to me. (2) that said, I'm not a Brouwerian -- choice sequences, the creating subject, all that stuff about mathematics being a free creation of the mind feels like mysticism to me. I want the sober proof-theoretic kind of constructivism, the Bishop / Heyting arithmetic flavour, and I'm happy treating infinite totalities as completed linguistic objects, at least as a façon de parler. (3) I use excluded middle freely in everyday reasoning and in finite combinatorics, with zero guilt. (4) I also don't feel guilty using it in ordinary number theory -- for something like Goldbach it seems like each instance is decidable in principle, so the general statement is just all the instances bundled together, and saying "Goldbach or not-Goldbach" feels harmless. (5) I think the classical intermediate value theorem and Bolzano-Weierstrass are basically fine as stated -- obviously true, it's just that sometimes the classical proofs are a quicker route to a true fact. (6) and I take a fair bit of comfort from the double negation translation thing: classical arithmetic is consistent exactly when intuitionistic arithmetic is, so all that classical reasoning can't be leading me into actual falsehood -- at worst it's "nonconstructive", whatever exactly that costs me.

so: go through the package and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly, which ones only look like they're in tension, and what the cheapest fix would be for each real tension. then tell me which single revision, if I made it, would clean up the most. I genuinely don't know where the problem is, so please be specific rather than just telling me "constructivism and classical habits clash sometimes". also please answer in British English (analyse, behaviour, etc.).
