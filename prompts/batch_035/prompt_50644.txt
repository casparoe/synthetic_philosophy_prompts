here's the exam question as written on the test:

"A large study estimates the heritability of a personality trait, X, at 0.7 in a particular population. (a) State as precisely as you can what this number does and does not tell us. (b) Explain why, on its own, this figure does not settle whether a difference in average levels of X between two populations is genetic in origin. (c) Describe, concretely, a situation in which the heritability of the same trait would come out different in two different populations, and explain why."

and here's the student's answer, typed out verbatim:

"(a) A heritability of 0.7 means that 70% of the variation in trait X within this population is due to genetic differences among its members, with the remaining 30% due to environmental differences. So genes are the stronger influence on X overall. For any particular person in the study who scores high on X, roughly 70% of where they land on the trait is coming from their genes.

It also tells us something about malleability: if 70% of the variance is genetic, then environmental interventions can only work on the remaining 30%, so 30% is the ceiling on how much you could shift the population's average through environmental change.

(b) Heritability is estimated within a single population, so it explains why members of that population differ from each other, not why one population's average differs from another's. Two populations could differ in average X entirely because of some environmental difference even with heritability at 0.7 in each. For example, take two countries with different norms about expressing trait X: within each country, genes do most of the work of explaining who is high and who is low, but the difference between the countries' averages could be entirely cultural. Since heritability is a within-population statistic, it simply doesn't speak to between-population comparisons at all.

(c) Heritability would differ between populations when the amount of environmental variation differs. Consider height: in a country where everyone is well fed, the environment is doing a lot of work — everyone gets pushed toward their maximum height — so more of the differences between people are due to environment, and heritability comes out lower. In a country where malnutrition is common, genes become the main reason some people end up taller than others, so heritability is higher."
