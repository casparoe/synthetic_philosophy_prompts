I'm a backend engineer with no philosophy background, but I keep ending up in arguments about this stuff at work and at the pub, and there's one I can't get out of my head: the "culture is an operating system" thing. My coworker (minor in anthropology, never lets you forget it) uses it like this — culture gets installed early in childhood, it runs in the background so you never notice it, it determines your defaults (how direct you are, how bad you feel about being late, what counts as interrupting), and you basically can't do a clean reinstall after a certain age. On that picture, national character talk is fine: saying "Germans tend to be punctual" is like saying "machines running this distro handle memory this way" — it's a claim about the software, not about anyone's soul, and it's not bigoted, it's just stats about a build.

My friend thinks the analogy is doing all the work and is secretly just stereotypes with a tech gloss. Her complaint is that it smuggles in determinism and uniformity that real cultures don't have: there's no single vendor shipping a German OS, no release notes, no checksum, huge variance within any "build," and people can actually inspect and rewrite parts of their own stack — immigrants don't just run the old OS in a VM forever. But when I ask her what's left of national character once you drop the OS picture, she kind of shrugs, and honestly so do I. Like, is there anything coherent there at all, or is "the French national character" in the same bucket as "the average user"?

So audit the analogy for me. Give me the correspondence table — what's supposed to play the kernel, the system calls, the update/patch mechanism, backwards compatability, whether someone can be "running an outdated version" — then show where the mapping genuinely breaks. Which of the disanalogies actually matter for what it's being used to license (is it ever acceptable to generalize about a people, the way we generalize about machines on the same build?) and which are just noise? And is there a better comparison — a language, a codebase, a protocol stack — that keeps whatever is true in the OS picture without importing the determinism and uniformity? I mainly want to know whether the "you can generalize if you talk software, not essences" conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether it was riding on the broken parts the whole time.
