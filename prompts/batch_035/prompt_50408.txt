Quick background: I'm in a law-and-philosophy reading group that just finished the Hart/Devlin debate (the 1950s one about enforcing morality, not the later offshoots), and I've volunteered to write something for our end-of-term zine — yes, we have a zine, it is exactly as nerdy as it sounds. The piece I want is a satirical FAQ page published by the Ministry of Public Morals of a fictional country that has just constitutionalized Devlin's legal moralism: the state's official job there is to uphold the shared public morality, not merely prevent harm, and the FAQ is meant to explain the new arrangements to citizens.

I want the humor to come from getting Devlin right rather than from making him look dumb. So the answers should track his actual moves: the reasonable man as the standard for what counts as intolerable (with the awkward fact that you can go out and survey him), the thought that society needs a shared morality roughly the way it needs a government, the idea that immorality is a kind of treason against society so even private, consensual, harms-literally-nobody conduct is the state's business, and the integrity thread — the claim that society may legislate against an immorality that hardly anyone actually practices anymore, because morality is all of a piece and you can't pick which bits to keep. At least one Q&A should quietly concede Hart's points — the reasonable man turning out to be a mid-century man with mid-century prejudices, or the slide between a society changing and a society dying — without ever saying so out loud.

Format: around 700 words total. Start with a minister's foreword of about 60 words, then six Q&As of roughly 100 words each — please include one on the lines of "can I be prosecuted for something I did alone, at home, that harmed no one?" (answer: yes, and the FAQ has to give Devlin's actual reason, not a made-up one) and one on "what if the reasonable man himself turns out to be unreasonable?" — and close with an 80-word section titled Feedback and moral drift, which should be some kind of complaints procedure. Deadpan throughout, like a real government FAQ: the reader should be able to tell what's being ironised without the piece winking at them, and a philosopher of law who disagrees with Devlin should still find it fair to him. No real names of living people, and don't bother with a bibliography, the zine doesn't do those.
