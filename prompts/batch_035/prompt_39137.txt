The view: deflationism about truth. I came to it reporting a long read on the liar paradox — interview after interview, and the people I found most convincing kept saying some version of: there is no deep property of truth to analyze. Saying that snow is white is true just amounts to saying snow is white, and the whole function of the truth predicate is bookkeeping — it lets you endorse a sentence you can't or won't spell out, like everything in the auditor's report is true, or say that all the laws of thermodynamics are true without listing them. Truth isn't a natural kind, it doesn't have an essence, it's a logical device. As a journalist I found this extremely congenial — I spend my life around people who think TRUTH is capitalized, and deflationism feels like the grown-up take. But my editor says if I present it as just obviously correct, I'm gonna get letters.

So, role assignment: you're a professional philosopher whose specialty is theories of truth specifically — you've sat through every version of this fight since the correspondence theories, you know the deflationist literature and its critics equally well, and you have no horse in the race.
