My friend and I burned an entire evening on this arguement and I can't let it go, so here I am. We were fighting about whether modern democracies are reliving the last century of the Roman Republic — she's all in on the analogy, I keep saying every generation thinks it's Rome, yawn. But somewhere in the middle we noticed something in the actual Roman sequence that neither of us had a term for, and that's what I actually want to ask about.

So the sequence: Tiberius Gracchus shoves a land reform through the popular assembly while sidestepping the senate, and gets killed for it. His brother Gaius goes further and dies in a purge. A generation later Marius holds consulship after consulship in flagrant violation of the term limits and builds himself a personal army. Then Sulla marches a Roman army on Rome itself — twice — and publishes kill lists. Then Caesar, going on about how his side had been treated, crosses the Rubicon with his own army. And the thing I keep turning over: each man's defense, to the extent he bothers with one, is a truthful report on the previous man. Caesar points at Sulla, Sulla pointed at Marius and at what the assembly had just done to him, and so on back. Nobody argues his own move was good on its merits; everyone argues that the standard has already been reset by the other side's last violation. The baseline for acceptable conduct is always the most recent crime, and it never snaps back.

There's one more detail that I think makes this not just ordinary escalation: by Caesar's day, the people doing the citing weren't even alive when the Gracchi were murdered. It's not tit-for-tat within a feud — it's inherited precedent, cited the way a lawyer cites case law, except every case on the books is itself a violation.

Is there a name for this exact configuration? Whataboutism doesn't fit — that's a rhetorical dodge inside a single exchange, and here the counter-accusation is true, happens across generations, and actually licenses the next step rather than just deflecting blame. The stuff my friend reads on how democracies backslide talks about norms eroding, and that's close, but it's a description from the outside, like observing decay, and it doesn't capture the adversarial structure where each side's justification is genuinely accurate and each step is individually defensible if you accept the previous one.
