I am a PhD student in health economics, currently writing a thesis chapter on incentive schemes for organ donation, and there is a recurring pattern I keep running into without finding a proper term for it. Across several literatures, a transaction seems to be treated as entirely acceptable at a price of zero and seriously wrong at any positive price — even when the parties are informed, uncoerced and roughly equal in bargaining position. So: donating a kidney to a stranger is admirable whilst selling one is a crime; giving bottled water away after a hurricane is heroic, selling it at the pre-storm price is ordinary commerce, and charging a premium is a moral scandal; volunteering for a clinical trial is civic-minded, whilst paying subjects beyond their expenses raises 'undue inducement' alarms at ethics committees; sex freely given is one thing and sexual surrogacy quite another. The size of the fee often seems almost beside the point — a pound is nearly as bad as ten thousand.

I know some neighbouring concepts. Roth's 'repugnant transactions' are too broad: his examples include things like dwarf-tossing, where there is no gift–sale contrast at all. Walzer's 'blocked exchanges' block the transfer outright, whereas here the transfer itself is welcomed; only the price is the problem. The Titmuss blood debate concerns an empirical crowding-out effect, and much of the Sandel-style commodification literature rests on claims about character or inequality that my hypotheticals are built to strip away. What I am after is the structural discontinuity at zero itself: the wrong that appears only once money changes hands, as though the price itself expressed something the transfer does not.

Does this gift–sale asymmetry have an established name, whether in the commodification literature, in contract law's treatment of inalienability, or elsewhere? If so, how does it relate to the neighbouring concepts I have mentioned, and if not, could you coin a term for it, define it precisely, and tell me where its edges are — for instance, does it cover nominal payments, 'expense reimbursement', or the regulated kidney market in Iran?
