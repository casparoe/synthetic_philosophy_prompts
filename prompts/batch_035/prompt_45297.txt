I work on causation, and I've come to think the debate about causation by omission -- dormant rather than dead since the wave of papers in the 2000s -- has stalled in a way nobody bothers to diagnose. Here is the pattern as I see it. Nobody actually disagrees about the cases: everyone agrees the gardener's failure to water killed the plants and that the queen's failure to water them didn't. The overgeneration problem -- McGrath's dilemma that either omissions never cause or far more of them do than common sense grants -- was supposedly answered by relevance conditions of various kinds: Lewis's appeal to default courses of events in "Void and Object," Hall's division of labor between production and dependence, Beebee's line that absences are never causes at all, and the norm-sensitive, contextualist approaches Hitchcock and Knobe later pressed. These answers more or less work; the flood of problem cases dried up; and yet the debate did not resolve. It just migrated into a lower-grade fight about whose normality facts do the selecting, with each side suspecting the others of changing the subject from causation to conversational pragmatics. My working hypothesis is that the residual disagreement is not about causation at all. Omission-causation claims are inherently contrastive and context-sensitive -- 'the failure to water' picks out an omission only against a backdrop of what was supposed to happen -- and the two camps differ on whether a theory of causation may model that context sensitivity (which makes it partly a theory of how causal talk works) or must abstract away from it (which makes it a theory of the world's causal structure, at the price of failing to predict our verdicts). Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a hidden substantive question keeping the dispute genuinely alive -- about the causal relata, about whether there are such things as negative events, about how normative facts could make causal claims true? And given your diagnosis, tell me what would count as progress here, if anything: new kinds of cases, a formal treatment of contrast, or an admission that the question was ill-posed from the start. I want a diagnosis with reasons, not another tour of the positions.
