Otto Neurath spent much of his career arguing that pictures could function as an international language: his Isotype project, developed in interwar Vienna with the graphic artist Gerd Arntz and later in Oxford with Marie Neurath, used standardised, simplified figures and strict conventions for depicting quantities and comparisons, initially in the service of civic education through social and economic statistics. Now suppose Neurath were somehow presented with the visual-communication landscape of the early twenty-first century.
