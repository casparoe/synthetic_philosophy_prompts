At my company (mid-size, we build developer tooling) we changed how we hire a few years back, and something about it has been bugging me. It used to be unstructured chats: someone senior would talk to a candidate for an hour and go yeah, sharp guy, good energy, hell fit in, and that was bascially it. Now it's a fixed script — everyone gets the same questions and exercises, scored against anchored rubrics, and we deliberately shuffle who meets whom, because we noticed the individual interviewers' gut read on a candidate predicted almost nothing about how they actually did the job once here.

But then look at everything else we do. People still get let go for "bad attitude" or "not a culture fit", and the writeups talk like there's a stable thing called his character that came out. Promotions go to people managers describe as having grit or being self-driven. Every offsite we run some strengths-finder workshop that assumes people have durable traits you can coach around.

So here's my question: what view of people do each of these practices presuppose? Does the hiring side quietly assume the behaviour-is-mostly-situational crowd are right — that there's no reliable character to read off an interview, so we control the situation instead of trying to spot virtue? And if that's the presupposition, is it actually consistent with still firing and promoting on character grounds, or is one half of how we run the company just philosophically confused? Please take a definite position — tell me which practice is wrong and what it would have to change if we took the presupposed view seriously. Don't give me a menu of "it depends which theory you prefer", I'd rather flat-out disagree with you than have to pick. Keep it under 350 words, firm limit, and don't hedge in the last paragraph either.
