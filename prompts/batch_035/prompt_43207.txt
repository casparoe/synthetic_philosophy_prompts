I'm teaching intro Chinese phil again this fall and the Mencius vs Xunzi lecture always slides into students just picking the team whose name they like better. So I want a "which theory of human nature do you actually hold?" quiz for them, 8-10 questions, each one a concrete case with two or three options, and the options should never use the labels Mencius/Xunzi/Gaozi -- students should get sorted by what they say about cases, not by which philosopher they already decided to stan. Cover at least these positions as leaves: Mencius's view (the sprouts are really there and just need cultivation), the harder Xunzi reading (human nature actively tends bad, order is imposed from outside like a carpenter steaming wood straight), the softer Xunzi reading (nature is just raw material, neither good nor bad, like an untouched board), Gaozi's neutrality claim, and the naive optimist leaf that I suspect nobody actually occupies once the cases get hard (humans are good enough that not much corrective structure is needed at all). Use the classic thought experiments where they actually separate the views -- the child and the well, the willow cups, water flowing east or west, whether ritual propriety grows out of us or is worked into us -- but rephrase them as fresh cases so students can't pattern-match from the reading. Flag any pair of leaves your quiz can't really pull apart. Then, at the very end, run the quiz on me and give me a verdict in the form \answer{...} with a short label -- here are my answers: I say anyone would feel alarm at the well before thinking, that our inborn reactions are raw and shapeable but not pointed anywhere, that a society's norms can't just be excavated from what's already inside people, but also that the carpenter metaphor is too harsh because the wood kindof wants to grow straight. Where do I land, and be honest if I land somewhere embarrassing or incoherent.
