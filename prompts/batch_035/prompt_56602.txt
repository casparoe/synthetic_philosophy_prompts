1. i think sex work is work: the person selling can genuinely consent, the payment doesn't automatically void the consent, and there's no deep moral difference between sex for pay and other intimate/emotional labor for pay (care work, etc.)
2. i endorse the Nordic model as the best actually-existing policy: buying sex is criminal, selling is not, sellers can't be prosecuted and can access health and legal services
3. i hold a pretty standard millian harm principle — the state shouldn't criminalize consensual adult exchanges absent harm to others
4. i beleive the evidence (such as it is) supports that criminalizing demand shrinks the overall market and that this reduces trafficking and coerced entry. as far as i can tell this is the load-bearing empirical claim for #2
5. i think the asymmetry of the Nordic model is principled, not just enforcement pragmatics — criminal law should target the buyer because the buyer is the one doing the wrong, the seller is at most the victim of the transaction
6. separately, i'm fairly convinced by the New Zealand decrim results: sex workers can report violence to police, sue employers, work indoors legally — that all seems unambiguously good for the sellers themselves
