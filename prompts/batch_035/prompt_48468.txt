I run a philosophy-of-science reading group and want a one-page handout that sorts members by what they actually believe about whether the sciences carve nature at its joints. Please design it as a decision tree built entirely from cases rather than labels, so that no node asks anyone directly whether they are, say, an essentialist. The tree must, at a minimum, send its branches through these cases: jade, which artisans and markets treated as a single stuff for millennia before chemistry revealed it to be two distinct minerals, nephrite and jadeite; the question of whether a biological species is a kind at all, given the lineage view on which it is more like an individual organism extended in time; lithium, whose isotopes differ enough in pharmacological and nuclear behavior to make one wonder whether sameness of atomic number suffices for sameness of kind; and a deliberately gerrymandered but perfectly regular grouping of one's own invention, to test what a responder does when projectibility and naturalness come apart. Each leaf should carry the name of the position that actually sits there -- microstructural essentialism, homeostatic property cluster realism, promiscuous or pluralist realism, conventionalist or deflationary views -- or else be marked as unoccupied, with a sentence diagnosing whether nobody holds that combination because it is incoherent, unattractive, or simply unexplored. Please also flag any pair of leaves your cases cannot genuinely separate, rather than pretending the tree does more work than it does. Finally, describe the whole tree in continuous prose only, the way one would write a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just sentences that walk a reader from the trunk out to the branches and say at each fork what question separates them.
