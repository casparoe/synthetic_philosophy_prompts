I run a weekly metaphysics reading group, and after a month spent on de re modality we are ready for a diagnostic exercise: a one-page decision tree that sorts each participant, by their answers, into a position in the counterpart theory versus transworld identity debate. The response should be exactly five numbered branch questions and nothing else — no introduction, no conclusion, no separate roster of leaves. Each item should consist of the question itself plus at most one sentence saying where each answer routes the answerer, with the destination position named inline: Lewisian counterpart theory, an actualist counterpart theory that keeps the counterpart relation while denying Lewis's concrete possible worlds, Plantinga-style haecceitism on which transworld identity is grounded in non-qualitative thisnesses, Kripkean deflationism on which worlds are stipulated rather than peered into, and whatever you judge the best "none of the above" leaf for someone who holds that de re modal claims require no cross-world apparatus at all.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a syllabus, and order the questions so that every one can be answered by someone who has done the reading but does not know which label it feeds. The first question should turn on the Humphrey-style case: whether, when a losing candidate insists that he himself might have won, he is talking about himself or about a distinct individual who merely resembles him. After that, include at least one question about whether possible worlds are as real as this one, at least one about whether which individual in another world is the candidate is settled by a further non-qualitative fact or by our stipulations in describing worlds, and at least one question that cuts across the earlier branches — for instance, whether the truth of a de re possibility claim requires any entity at any other world in the first place, or whether some answer combinations land on no position anyone actually holds. Phrase these as concrete cases a participant can react to, not as questions about the labels. Finally, within the five items, be candid about the instrument's limits: wherever two leaves cannot honestly be separated by these questions, say so inside the relevant item rather than papering over it, and within one of the five items tell me which pattern of answers, if our group actually produced it, would force you to redraw the tree.
