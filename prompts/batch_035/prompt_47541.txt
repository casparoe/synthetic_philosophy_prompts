I'm a novelist working on a book where the main character, a retired ferry mechanic, checks the stove exactly seven times before leaving the house, and if you interrupt him mid-count he starts over from one. I keep thinking about that wasp — the one that drags its prey to the edge of the burrow, pops down to check the burrow is safe, and if you nudge the prey an inch away she drags it back and rechecks the whole burrow agian, endlessly, like the first time never happened. My mechanic is like that. The trouble is my editor says readers will either file him under OCD or read him as a robot, and either way they'll stop believing his choices are choices. But the book is supposed to sit right on that edge — the mechanic himself insists the rituals are chosen, that it's just how he does things, and I want the reader genuinely unsure whether that insistence is self-knowledge or self-deception. So my question, I guess: is there any principled line between mechanical rigidity and free agency, or between rigidity that expresses a self and rigidity that just bypasses one? And do I have to land on one side of it for him to work as a character, or can I get away with never resolving it?
