I'm a PhD student in philosophy working on truth, and my department has two faculty members who have been arguing about deflationism for as long as I've been here. One holds that truth is a thin, non-substantive notion whose entire content is captured by the equivalence schema — 'p' is true iff p — and that asking for a deeper account of what truth is amounts to a confusion; the other holds that truth is a substantive property whose nature demands explanation, roughly along correspondence or representational lines, and that deflationism, whatever its dialectical merits against grand metaphysical theories, can't explain what truth does for us — why true beliefs generally get us around the world better than false ones, why convergent inquiry is truth-aimed at all. Their exchanges at proseminar and colloquia go in circles: the deflationist accuses the other of manufacturing a mystery out of a logical device; the inflationist accuses the deflationist of changing the subject when the subject is hard. This year they agreed, half in exasperation, to attempt an adversarial collaboration, and as the department's rapporteur-drone I've been assigned to produce a first draft of the joint statement for them to mark up.

Please draft that document. It should contain: (1) a numbered section of claims both parties can sign without embarrassment — I'd expect things like the unlimited validity of the equivalence schema (each instance, no exceptions needed for ordinary discourse), that truth is a single property of propositions rather than a scattered plurality, and that some traditional disputes about truth were purely verbal; (2) a disagreements table stating exactly where they part ways, with each disagreement phrased so that the deflationist would accept the wording of the deflationist's side and likewise for the inflationist — this is where I most need the drafting skill, since formulations that quietly favor one side are exactly what has kept this going; and (3) the cruxes, which I suspect include at least: whether the correlation between true belief and successful action requires truth to be explanatory rather than merely disquotational; whether the Liar and its revenge cases are a distinctive problem for a purely schematic treatment of truth, or a problem for everyone that deflationism handles no worse than its rivals; and whether truth is constitutively normative for belief in a way that goes beyond the equivalence schema. For each crux, give a concrete case, argument, or test both would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance, so the collaboration has something to come back to.

Since I have to hand this to two people who will each read it looking for slant, I'd like you to append a short note on how confident you are in the various formulations — especially any you suspect one side might refuse to sign as worded — and to single out the single point in the draft you're least sure about and say why. Where you think a residual disagreement is actually verbal rather than substantive, say so explicitly and justify the diagnosis rather than hinting at it.
