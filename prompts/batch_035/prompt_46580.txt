My mom has mid-stage Alzheimer's and lives in a memory care facility. Twenty years ago she signed a living will saying that if she ever had dementia and couldn't recognize her family anymore, she didn't want antibiotics or tube feeding, just comfort care. Here's the complication: she doesn't recognize us, checks that box exactly, but she's honestly kind of content? She jokes with the nurses, loves the music sessions, eats happily when someone helps her. Maybe one day out of ten she gets agitated and says she wants to die, but the rest of the time she seems fine. My brother says we follow the directive the next time she gets pneumonia — that was the real her speaking when her mind was intact, and this version isn't the person she wanted kept alive. But it feels crazy to let someone die who laughs at jokes. My sister takes a third position, that we should just ask mom now what she wants and take whatever answer comes out seriously, even if it's garbled and different from week to week. We have to settle on something before the next infection instead of scrambling like last time. Which of us is actually tracking the thing that matters here?
