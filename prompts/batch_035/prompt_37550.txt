I'm a high school senior, and my philosophy club has asked each member to write up their views on one big question so the rest of the group can challenge them. Mine is on whether this world could have been better. Here is where I've landed: (1) I believe in a God who is all-powerful, all-knowing, and perfectly good; (2) I've been persuaded by Leibniz that a being like that would create the best of all possible worlds, so this world is the best one there could be; (3) I think the value of free will goes a long way toward explaining why God permits evil, since a world of genuinely free creatures beats a world of puppets even if the creatures sometimes choose horribly; (4) I also believe we have a real duty to fight suffering — disease, poverty, cruelty — and to leave the world better than we found it; (5) I suspect that when something looks like pointless evil, that reflects our limited viewpoint, rather like someone with their nose against a canvas who cannot judge the whole picture; and (6) I take possible worlds to be just ways of talking about how things might have been, not parallel universes that really exist. I don't know where the weak points are, so please don't pull your punches. Please structure your response as follows: a section titled "The genuine tensions, ordered by severity" in which each tension is traced to the specific beliefs that generate it (about 400 words total); a section titled "Apparent tensions that dissolve on inspection" covering at least one pair of my views that seem to conflict but do not (about 150 words); and a closing section titled "The single best revision" naming the one change to my list that would resolve the most trouble and what it would cost me to accept it (about 150 words). Throughout, tell me what my beliefs commit me to rather than lecturing me about the history of the problem.
