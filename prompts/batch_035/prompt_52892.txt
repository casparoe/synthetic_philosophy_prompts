I write science-and-ideas features for a magazine, and I'm deep into a piece on what makes ordinary people participate in atrocities — basically the old Browning versus Goldhagen fight. Browning's line is that the Reserve Police Battalion 101 findings show you could take almost any conscript unit, put them in that situation — an authority figure giving the order, peer pressure, gradual escalation, everyone around you already brutalized by war — and you'd get participants; the men were ordinary, the circumstances were not. Goldhagen's counter was that this whitewashes the perpetrators: what mattered was what those particular men believed about their particular victims, and the eliminationist antisemitism they'd absorbed meant killing felt, if not exactly good, at least not like murdering a person.

Here's my problem, and it's the reason my editor keeps pushing back on the draft: for basically every case I look at, the two stories predict the same observable outcome, and each side just folds the evidence in after the fact. Which raises the fair question of why I'm writing this at all. So what I want from you is a concrete example — real or constructed — of a case where the situationist explanation and the culture/ideation explanation actually make different predictions, such that the outcome would count as evidence for one over the other. You'd have to change something: who the victims are (say, a unit ordered to kill a group its members had recieved no propaganda about and if anything had mildly positive priors toward), or who the perpetrators are (volunteers who signed up eagerly versus conscripts handed the same orders), or what the opt-outs look like (an open refusal you can act on alone versus a quiet transfer you have to know to ask for). Design it however makes the contrast sharpest.

But don't just give me the setup — for your example, spell out what Browning's story predicts, what Goldhagen's predicts, where the predictions differ, and which possible outcomes would count against which theory. I want a case where the theories can lose, not another both-fit story. I've been sketching candidates myself and I honestly can't tell anymore whether they discriminate or whether I'm smuggling in my own conclusion about which explanation is doing the work, so if you think my candidates are confounded or trivially biased toward one side, say so and do better.
