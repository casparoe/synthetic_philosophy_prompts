Please argue that the conditional amnesties administered by South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission — granted in exchange for full public disclosure of politically motivated crimes — are best understood not as an escape from punishment but as a distinctive punishment in their own right, one whose burden lies in the perpetrator's compelled confession, the public destruction of the heroic narrative under which many perpetrators understood their own violence, and lasting social exposure. In the course of the argument, please assess whether this reframing can answer the standard objection from victims that such amnesty delivers less than justice.
