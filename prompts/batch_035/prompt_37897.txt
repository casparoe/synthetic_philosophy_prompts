I was recently appointed to the safety review council of a mid-sized AI development lab, and the first consequential decision of my term has just landed on my desk. Our next frontier model is scheduled for release in four months. The engineering team estimates that the full safety package the council provisionally endorsed — extended red-teaming, independent dangerous-capability evaluations, staged deployment with restricted API access, secured model weights, and a published written safety case — would delay release by roughly five months and cost an amount equivalent to several percent of annual revenue, a burden people in the field have taken to calling the alignment tax. At the same time, credible internal reporting indicates that a well-funded competitor will ship a comparably capable model within six months, and our executive team argues that losing that race would erode the very commercial position that funds our safety research: a standard that bankrupts the lab, or cedes the frontier to a demonstrably less careful competitor, makes the world less safe, not more. They have therefore proposed a reduced package that keeps automated evaluations and basic access controls but drops the independent audits, the staged rollout, and the published safety case, allowing release on schedule. The council is split roughly down the middle. The minority faction replies that if this reasoning is valid for us it is valid for every lab, and that universal capitulation to it is precisely the race-to-the-bottom dynamic our charter exists to resist; they add that the tax is partly a one-time fixed cost — the evaluation infrastructure, once built, serves future models — and that if relative position is what matters, a world in which all serious labs bear comparable taxes leaves nobody worse off competitively, so the real question is defection and whether unilateral restraint can ever be credible rather than whether safety is affordable. Please draft the decision memo I will circulate before next week's session. It should recommend one concrete course of action — the full package, the reduced package, or a conditional structure of your own design, such as a phased release tied to audit milestones or a public commitment timed to invite reciprocal restraint — with a time frame and conditions the other council members can actually act on. It should give the strongest argument on the losing side its full weight rather than caricaturing it, and it should be candid about what our decision can and cannot achieve given that we control only our own conduct. Treat the stipulated figures and timelines as given; I am not asking you to verify them.
