Write me an analysis of South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission -- the mid-90s hearings after apartheid ended, with Tutu chairing, perpetrators trading full public disclosure of politically motivated crimes for individual amnesty, victims testifying about what happened to them and theirs, and mass prosecutions essentially off the table -- through the lens of Strawson's reactive attitudes framework from Freedom and Resentment. Not a book report on either; I want the mapping done seriously and then an honest account of where it breaks, in both directions.

Some things I'd want the analysis to actually work through. Is amnesty best understood as a suspension of the reactive attitudes (resentment, indignation, and their vicarious analogues) while keeping the participant stance toward the perpetrator, or as a switch to the objective attitude -- treating the perpetrator as a problem to be managed for the sake of civic peace rather than as someone addressed? Strawson thinks we modulate reactive attitudes all the time via excuses and exemptions, so one option is to read the TRC's disclosure-for-amnesty structure as a giant institutionalized excuse/exemption process: does full public confession work like "he couldn't help it" or "he's been through enough" in Strawson's scheme, or does the transactional quality of it (testimony buys amnesty, whether or not the will was reformed) resist that mapping? Strawson also insists the reactive attitudes are natural, non-optional commitments we can't just decide to drop wholesale -- so does his picture predict the TRC should have collapsed, or does his own allowance for exemption and mitigation explain how it held together?

Then the pressure points where I suspect the framework is silent or stretches: reactive attitudes are interpersonal and felt by individuals, but here a commission and a state were doing the forgivng on behalf of everyone -- who's the "we," and is official forgiveness on behalf of victims coherent in Strawson's terms, especially given that many victims testified that they felt the deal traded away their resentment without their consent? Related: does the vicarious sharing of resentment Strawson talks about survive being routed through an institution?

And run it the other way too: what does the episode show about the theory? Does the fact that a political design could deliberately restructure a society's blaming practices (with at least partial success) threaten Strawson's upshot that our responsibility practices need no external rational justification because they're just woven into human life -- or actually vindicate his claim that the whole metaphysical debate about free will missed the point, since the interesting questions turned out to be about how we hold and release each other, not about determinism? Where would you say the theory was never designed to speak at all, and where does the TRC work as an anomaly?

Feel free to bring in the obvious rejoinder that reconciliation-as-policy was partly cynical statecraft and only partly a moral undertaking, if you think that distinction matters for the analysis. It probably does.
