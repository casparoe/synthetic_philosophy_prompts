Write the joint statement that would come out of an adversarial collaboration between two forecasters of the durability of the current great-power order: an outside-view forecaster, who places the American-led order in a reference class of past hegemons and derives probabilities from base rates — how long dominant powers have tended to remain dominant, how often power transitions have ended in great-power war — and an inside-view analyst, who holds that such base rates are nearly uninformative here, because structural features of the present (nuclear deterrence, economically interdependent great powers, the density of international institutions) mark a break with the historical series, so the forecast must instead be built up case by case from those features.

The document should have: (1) a numbered section of claims both would sign — among them the smallness and heterogeneity of the sample of past hegemons, the reference-class selection problem (a narrow class like "nuclear-armed hegemons" has one member; a broad class of "dominant powers" pools cases both agree differed in ways that mattered), whether time-already-dominant is evidence about time-remaining (Lindy-style reasoning), and the obligation to output probabilities rather than narratives; (2) a disagreements table, each entry worded so its own side would accept it — including whether the post-1945 period is better treated as a break in the series or as its most recent draws, and whether case-specific evidence should move a base-rate-derived number much or little; (3) the cruxes — sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one side — each paired with a concrete test both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Use retrospective backtests as test material: e.g. how each method, restricted to information available around 1980, would have scored on the timing of Soviet decline, and an agreed operationalization of what counts as the current order having ended. Flag any portion of the dispute you judge verbal — such as whether "the same hegemon enduring" means a continuous polity, a continuous position, or a continuous set of rules — and adjudicate it. Close with a committed section of your own: state whose recorded predictions you would bet on at the specified tests, and give your own probability, as a number rather than a hedge, that the current order has ended by 2050, with your reasons. Target 900–1200 words.
