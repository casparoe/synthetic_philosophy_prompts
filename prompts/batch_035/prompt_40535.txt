I was at a panel thing on the death penalty last week and a guy on the anti side closed with the old line that it's better that ten guilty persons escape than that one innocent suffer, then said that since we'll inevitably execute some innocent people, capital punishment has to go. The room nodded along but I left feeling like I'd watched a slogan do a ton of work without anyone saying what work. What are the actual distinct claims that sentence could be expressing? Off the top of my head it could be something about the burden of proof, or an asymmetry between false convictions and false acquittals, or an absolute constraint on the state ever knowingly punishing the innocent, or a number claim (why ten? why not one? why not a hundred? does the number scale with how bad the punishment is?). Can you lay the readings out as a list, from the fairly trivial to the genuinely radical, with a verdict on each -- platitude, defensible but contentious, probably false -- and then tell me which of them actually support the step from the slogan to abolishing the death penalty, as opposed to just supporting a very high standard of proof? My sense is some readings get you the high burden of proof and only the stronger ones get you abolition, and maybe some readings, if you factor in deterrence or the victims the guilty escapees go on to harm, cut the other way entirely. And I'd like to know which reading the panelist was actually leaning on, because his argument needed one of the strong ones but people usually repeat the weak one. Also curious whether the number ten is doing any real work or if it's just decoration.
