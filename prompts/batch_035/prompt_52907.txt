Im putting together a "which dream-skepticism position do you actually hold?" quiz for the members of a small philosophy newsletter I run — the hook being that most readers seem to think the Zhuangzi butterfly passage is just Descartes in a silk robe, and I want to show them the space of positions on "how do I know Im not dreaming right now" is way more scattered than skeptic vs. not-skeptic. So the deliverable is the quiz itself, not an essay about dream skepticism. Please write it in American English since I paste answers straight into drafts.

Format: around 8 questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, phrased so someone can answer without knowing any of the labels — no "do you agree with contextualism?" type questions, I want people sorted by what they say about cases, not by which name they like. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions, plus a note on any two leaves the quiz cant actually seperate.

Cases that need to show up, because I think they do real diagnostic work:

- Lucid dreams, where you figure out mid-dream that it's a dream. Does the existence of lucid dreaming help against the skeptic, and how?
- Reality checks like rereading a page of text (it famously shifts or blurs when youre dreaming). If these checks mostly work, does that deflate the dream argument or just push it back a level?
- The butterfly's side of the story: Zhuang Zhou dreamed he was a butterfly who knew nothing of Zhou. Was the butterfly, from inside, wrong about anything? This one should be answerable in a way that separates people.
- A neuroscientist friend of the newsletter claims dreaming is basically your offline predictive machinery running unconstrained — same machinery as waking perception, just not being error-corrected by the senses. Does that support the skeptic or dissolve the problem?
- The old observation that you can only seriously entertain "maybe I'm dreaming" while awake — wondering whether youre dreaming, done inside a dream, doesnt seem to count as the real thing somehow.

Leaves that must appear: (a) the full Cartesian global skeptic; (b) the Moorean "I know I'm awake at breakfast, so the skeptical premise is just false" line; (c) the Malcolm-style view that dreams arent experiences occurring during sleep at all, so asking whether youre dreaming right now is confused from the start; (d) the Dennett-style view that dream content gets composed or confabulated on waking rather than experienced in real time; (e) the reading of Zhuangzi on which the passage isnt an argument for skepticism at all — the closing move about the transformation of things suggesting theres no privileged vantage from which being Zhuangzi is more real than being the butterfly, so "which one am I really" never had the grip it seemed to; (f) contextualism, where "I know I'm awake" is true at the breakfast table and false in the seminar room. If some other position occurs to you while building the tree, add it.

Two things I want you to be honest about at the end: first, whether the Malcolm-style leaf is even reachable by answering case questions, given that someone who holds it might reject the premise of every single question I've listed; second, any combination of answers that maps to no live position, and whether nobody sits there because the combo is incoherent or just because its unattractive or unexplored. Dont sand those edges off — the flagging of dead branches and unseparable leaves is honestly the part I care about most, since its what makes the quiz more than a horoscope.
