I've been following the mech interp literature for a while and there's this recurring fight that never actually gets anywhere: some people talk about neural networks as genuinely having representations — real features, real circuits, directions in activation space that mean something — while others basically treat all of that as a convenient story we project onto a big pile of matrix multiplications, fine for engineering but not a discovery about the network itself. And what bugs me is that both sides agree on basically all the technical facts: probes can linearly decode concepts, activation steering works, sparse autoencoders turn up features that look consistent across models and layers. None of that is disputed. Yet the disagreement about what any of it MEANS hasn't moved an inch in years, and the two camps mostly just... publish in parallel.

My working hypothesis is that it's not a technical disagreement at all but a smuggled-in metaphysical one about what makes something count as a representation: the realist thinks that if a decoder reliably extracts "dog" from the activations, and steering that direction changes dog-related outputs, then the network has a dog feature, full stop. The anti-realist thinks you've just demonstrated a useful correlation and that "the network represents dog" adds nothing beyond "this decoder works." If that's right, the field has no tools to settle it because it's not an empirical question.

But I'm not totally sure it's that clean either. It seems like there could be a genuinely empirical version lurking underneath — something like whether the features get used downstream the way a representation would be used, e.g. whether the "dog direction" is causally load-bearing in the model's actual computation across lots of different tasks, versus being an artifact of where we happen to point our instruments. And superposition makes this worse, honestly: if the model is packing many more features than it has neurons, it's a bit odd to say the features are just projections of ours — projections of what, onto what? You don't get to store a projection.

So: diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is. Verbal, substantive, two groups answering different questions under one heading? Where does the burden of proof sit, and does the anti-realist owe an account of why interpretability tools transfer to new models and tasks at all if there's nothing there to track? And given your diagnosis, tell me what would actually count as progress — a sharper definition, some class of experiments, or nothing, and the field should just pick a convention and stop litigating it. I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of the debate.
