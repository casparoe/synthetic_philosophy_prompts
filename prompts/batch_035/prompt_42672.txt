So last semester I took a comparative politics course with a big unit on corruption, and since then I keep arguing about this stuff with a friend who works in compliance at a multinational. Over break I tried to write down my actual positions, partly for a term paper and partly to win the argument with her. I showed the list to my TA and she said it "looks like one definition doing two different jobs" but refused to elaborate, which was annoying. So now I want a proper audit from someone else. Here's roughly where I stand:

(1) The essence of corruption is someone using the discretion their role gives them in a way that subverts the purpose that discretion exists to serve. It's about the role and its purpose — not about money changing hands, not about who's public or private. That's the definition I want to defend. (2) Because of that, private-sector bribery is real corruption, not just analogous to it: a purchasing manager at a private company who takes kickbacks from a supplier is corrupt in the strict sense, because the point of his discretion was getting the best deal for his employer and that's being subverted. "Abuse of public office" definitions are too narrow. (3) Clientelism, on the other hand, is NOT corruption. The old city-machine politician who gets your traffic ticket fixed or finds your nephew a municipal job in exchange for your vote does it in the open, the voters consent, and the voters are the boss — the principal approves, so nothing is betrayed. I find machine politics ugly but calling it corruption has always seemed like a stretch to me; it's a degraded form of responsiveness. (4) Grease payments — slipping a clerk a small amount to issue the permit you're already legally entitled to — basically aren't corruption either. At most it's queue-jumping; the clerk does what he was going to do anyway, so nothing gets subverted. (5) Gratitude gifts are fine: the doctor who gets an expensive watch from a family after saving their father, with nothing expected afterward, hasn't done anything corrupt. (6) Last thing: a definition like this can't bend to local norms. The same act is corrupt or not regardless of whether it happens in Lagos, Lisbon, or Tokyo.

Go through these for me pair by pair. Which ones are actually in tension, which only look like they clash, and for the real ones rank them by severity and say what the cheapest fix would be. And then tell me: if I had to make one single revision, which one cleans up the most? Don't pull your punches just because I clearly like some of these.
