The number where your fasting glucose becomes "diabetes" instead of "pre-diabetes," the threshold at which we're allowed to say a heatwave was "caused" by climate change, whether the red wolf still counts as a species given all the coyote interbreeding, that kind of thing. At some point I did a background interview with a philosopher who kept using the word "sorites" and now I'm ruined. I've ended up with a set of positions on vagueness that each seem obviously fine to me on their own, and I want somebody to audit the package, because I genuinely cannot see where the pressure is. Here's the list. (1) The sorites argument is valid, full stop — it's just induction plus modus ponens, and I refuse to be the guy who says the inference pattern itself is broken. (2) The conclusion is obviously false — a man with 90,000 hairs is not bald — so some premise is false, and I assume it's the induction step, the "if n hairs is bald then n+1 is bald" thing. (3) I am firmly NOT an epistemicist: there is no secret exact number where baldness stops, and it's not just that we can't know the number, there's no number to know. Vagueness is in our words, not in the world. (4) I want to keep classical logic completely intact — excluded middle, modus ponens, everything. Every time I try to read about fuzzy logic or truth-value gaps my eyes glaze over and it feels like refusing to bite a bullet. (5) I hold the T-schema with no exceptions at all: "snow is white" is true iff snow is white, same for every other sentence, no asterisks, ever. (6) When I picture a borderline guy, Tom, with maybe 8,000 hairs, "Tom is bald or Tom is not bald" still sounds plainly true to me — I can't imagine a third thing Tom could be — I just also think you can't say which side he's on.

So here's what I want. Go through these commitments pair by pair (or in whatever groupings make sense) and tell me which ones are genuinely in tension, which only look like they're, and rank the real conflicts by severity rather than presenting them as all equally bad. For each genuine one, say what exactly has to give and what it costs me. A few things I keep going back and forth on, in case they help you find stuff I've missed: if I end up saying some induction-step conditional is flat-out false rather than weird or half-true, have I quietly become the epistemicist I keep assuring editors I'm not? Also does my "no cutoffs, it's all in the words" stance (3) somehow infect mathematics — the sorites uses the same induction pattern mathematicians rely on, and I definetely don't want to be committed to math being vague? And is the Tom "no third option" intuition (6) actually load-bearing for anything in my package, or is it just a verbal tic that I can drop without noticing? I honestly don't know which of these I'd give up, which is sort of the point of asking — I'd rather know where the real pressure is before I drop anything.
