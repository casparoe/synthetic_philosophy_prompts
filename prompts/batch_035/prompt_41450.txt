Last month I was reporting a piece on disinformation and fact-checking, and I called up a philosopher to get the "what even is truth" background quote. He told me, half off the record, that the whole "nature of truth" debate is kind of a scam -- that saying "it's true that p" just says p, and the truth predicate is basically a grammatical device for generalizing, so I can say "everything in the report is true" instead of reciting the whole report. Fine. But when I pushed on why fact-checkers even exists, or what's going on when someone says "it's true that smoking causes cancer" and it seems to do more than just assert the content -- like it's vouching, or certifying, standing behind it -- he waved me off with "that's pragmatics, not semantics." I hung up feeling like I don't actually know what to demand of a theory of truth in the first place, and I'd rather find that out before I write another word on this beat.

So give me the spec, not the theories. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of truth has to deliver -- the jobs the truth predicate does (the generalization job, the endorsement/vouching job my fact-checking question was about, the norm-of-belief job where truth is supposed to be the aim of believing), the cases it has to get right ("that's not true" seems to do more than merely fail to assert; the liar and other self-referential sentences; whether statements about fiction or matters of taste are truth-apt at all), the distinctions it can't collapse -- each with the case or consideration behind it. Then tell me which of these are non-negotiable and which are just camp preferences (deflationists vs correspondence people vs the pragmatist crowd), and be straight with me about whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable -- I've heard the liar plus the idea of a fully transparent truth predicate is exactly where deflationists get squeezed, and if that's real it seems like the kind of thing my readers should know. Last thing: flag the single requirement that does the most to knock theories out of the running. If I understand that one, I can hold my own next time a philosopher tells me the truth debate is a scam over the phone.
