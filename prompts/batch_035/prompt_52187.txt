I'm on the bargaining committee of a dockworkers local. The terminal operator wants to automate the truck gates in the next contract — OCR cameras reading container numbers, remote checkers instead of the guys in the booths. Half the committee wants a hard no-automation clause written in; the other half wants to trade acceptance for retraining, a no-layoff guarantee, and a cut of the productivity savings. One member keeps bringing up the Grundrisse, saying something like: once machines do the work, labor time stops being the real measure of wealth, but capital still runs on labor time, so we're just fighting over a shrinking pot — and I honestly can't tell if he's against any deal at all or if he's arguing for something bigger. Draft the memo I'll circulate to the committee before thursday: it should recommend concrete contract language we could actually bargain at the table, and it should take seriously his (and I guess Marx's) worry that automation structurally weakens the union and the wage relation itself, not just this round of jobs.
