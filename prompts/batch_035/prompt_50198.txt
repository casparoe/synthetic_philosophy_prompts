I'm a high school student in a maths-and-philosophy reading group, and last week we discussed Benacerraf's dilemma. I want to put a puzzle to the group at our next meeting, but I suspect my own reasoning about it is flawed somewhere, and I'd rather find out now than in front of everyone.

Here is how I understand the dilemma: if numbers are abstract objects, existing outside space and time, then they cannot enter into any causal chain; and if knowledge of something requires some causal contact with it, then we could never know anything about numbers. But mathematicians plainly do know things about numbers — there are proofs of the infinitude of the primes, and of Fermat's Last Theorem, that generations of mathematicians have checked and built upon. It seems to me that this successful practice simply refutes the dilemma: given that we demonstrably acquire knowledge of numbers, numbers must somehow be able to affect us after all, perhaps through some channel that physics has not yet described. So my question is: why is Benacerraf's dilemma still taken seriously at all, rather than being regarded as an argument that was empirically discredited by the ongoing success of mathematics? Is the standard reply really that there is a special, non-physical route by which numbers influence mathematicians' brains?

I'd be grateful for a careful answer that does the following. First, tell me precisely where my reasoning goes wrong — I'm especially unsure about the step from "we know about numbers" to "numbers must affect us," and about whether my statement of the dilemma itself is accurate. Second, please be candid about the parts of this that philosophers still disagree about, rather than presenting one resolution as settled. Third, state explicitly what would change your verdict — for instance, what a satisfying account of mathematical knowledge would have to accomplish, and which of my assumptions such an account would have to reject. Finally, indicate where you think your own answer is most likely to go wrong, so that I can raise that with the group too.
