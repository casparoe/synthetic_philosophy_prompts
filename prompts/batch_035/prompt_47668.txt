here's where I've landed on modality after way too much reading group time, and I want an audit before I put it in my thesis intro. (1) I'm on board with Lewis-style modal realism: other possible worlds are real concrete places, exactly as real as ours, just not causally or spatiotemporally connected to us, and there are a lot of them. (2) "Actual" is indexical, like "here" or "now" — our world has no property the others lack, it's just the one we're in. (3) I never literally exist in other worlds; when I say I could have become an architect, what's true is that some architect in another world resembles me closely enough to count as my counterpart. (4) I still believe in deliberating: when I'm choosing between two job offers, I think the matter is genuinely open, the future is unsettled until I decide, and it makes a real difference which one I pick. (5) I take ontological parsimony seriously as a virtue — it's part of why I don't believe in numbers as abstract objects. Does this hang together? It feels fine to me but I have a nagging suspicion that (4) is doing something the others quietly forbid. Go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which just look like it, rank them by severity, and say what the cheapest fix costs. don't just tell me to add epicycles — if something has to go, I want to know which one and what I'd be giving up.
