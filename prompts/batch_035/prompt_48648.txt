My best friend and I have been arguing for about two years now (mostly in a group chat, sometimes over beers) about AI safety via debate — the idea where you let two AI systems argue opposite sides of a question in front of a human judge, and safety comes from the human only needing to tell who's right, since checking a claim is supposed to be easier than coming up with it. The specific thing we fight about is obfuscated arguments: cases where a sneaky debater takes some true-but-confusing fact and builds a case for a false conclusion on top of it, so every individual step checks out but the whole thing misleads you. He's the optimist: he thinks that with enough rounds, cross-examination, and letting the judge demand any sub-step be broken down further, the sneaky side eventually runs out of places to hide, because the honest side can always demand verification one level deeper and there's some limit to how deep a lie can stay hidden. I'm the pessimist: I think there are arguments where the misleadingness isn't located in any one step — it's in the overall structure, like a writeup that's technically accurate everywhere but arranged so the reader walks away with the wrong picture — and drilling down doesn't catch that, because every isolated step the judge examines looks fine, and knowing when to drill down is itself exactly the thing the sneaky debater is attacking.

We've decided we're done talking past each other, and we want to do one of those adversarial collaboration writeups I've read about, where two people who disagree produce a joint document they can both actually sign. Please draft it for us — you're an expert on argumentation and AI alignment, and you've seen how these collaborations are supposed to work. Think carefully and do some brainstorming before writing, becuase the hard part is finding wording neither of us would balk at.
