I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar on the ethics of extinction — fifteen students, one 90-minute session. We have just finished Samuel Scheffler's Death and the Afterlife, so the afterlife conjecture — the claim that many of our values and projects depend on the assumption that humanity will outlast us, on what he calls the collective afterlife — is already on the table, along with its two canonical scenarios: fertility fails and no one is born after the announcement, and an asteroid that ends everything a short while after a fixed date. My students can recite all of that. What they cannot do is feel it, and the standard classroom treatment has a second weakness: it treats the conjecture as a single undifferentiated claim, when in fact it leaves at least three distinctions wide open. I want an activity that delivers both at once.

Design an in-class deliberation game in which participants re-price their own future commitments under a series of sealed scenario cards, and in which the three open distinctions surface as disagreements in the room rather than as points in a debrief lecture.

The three distinctions:

1. Distance and route. The conjecture as usually stated is insensitive to how far off the end is and how it arrives: the collective afterlife is over, and that is that. But an end that arrives in thirty days, an end that arrives only after the natural deaths of everyone now alive, and an end that arrives through a slow failure of fertility seem to call for different reactions, and it is not obvious which differences in reaction are rationally required and which are artifacts of terror or grief. Include scenario cards that vary the distance and the route, so that divergent responses across the room register as data rather than noise.

2. Whose continuation? Include a successor card: humanity ends, but our practices, archives, and institutions are carried forward by entities we would hesitate to call our descendants — engineered successors, say, or a digital continuation of our culture maintained without us. If the re-pricing under this card differs from the re-pricing under ordinary human continuation, the dependence at work runs to biological descent, or to cultural continuity, or to something even less specific, and the activity should leave the participants able to say which.

3. Fact or confidence. The conjecture is ambiguous between our values depending on the fact of continued human existence and on our confidence in that fact. Build a round in which, after participants have re-priced their commitments under their assigned card, some cards are revealed to have been false — the world continues after all. Whether their re-priced commitments snap back, persist, or drift is the interesting result, and the design should make that visible.

A fourth card should isolate the deaths from the ending: the participant dies next month in an accident while humanity continues untouched. This gives the debrief its anchor: the question of what, if anything, remains bad about extinction over and above the deaths it involves.

Constraints and deliverables: no real money — use a token allocation scheme, perhaps a fixed budget of continuation units that participants distribute across their own real projects, relationships, and practices under each card. The participants' own commitments should be the material; the scenarios should remain sealed until the appropriate moment within the activity; the facilitator should not name what is happening until the debrief. Then provide: full rules and materials, a minute-by-minute timeline, the allocation mechanism, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto specific parts of the conjecture — including an honest account of where the activity produced distinctions the book itself does not draw. Tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — students playing it for laughs, strategic exaggeration of the re-pricing, the participant who refuses to move any units under any card, subgroups comparing cards too early — and how to handle each. Finally, be frank about the feature of the conjecture the activity cannot reproduce: everyone in the room knows the scenarios are stipulated, whereas the conjecture concerns confidence that is real, sustained, and load-bearing over a whole life. Say what the facilitator must supply in the debrief to compensate, and which residual gap no debrief can close.
