I cover science and ideas for a mid-size outlet, and I got roped into refereeing a standoff between two colleagues over a trend piece we're doing on the long-term history of human violence (the kind of story where you cite one of those big books arguing violence has collapsed over the millennia). Maya, our data person, wanted to run with the line that the world has become dramatically less violent. Rob, an editor, pushed back. Their exchange went roughly like this.

(1) Maya: per-capita death rates from war and homicide have fallen enormously over the centuries; a person alive today is much less likely to die violently than at almost any earlier point in history. Rob: there are more humans than ever, so in absolute terms more people are dying in wars right now than in many whole past centuries, and "the world is less violent" should track how much violence is in the world, not your personal odds of it.

(2) Maya: if you refuse to divide by population you'd have to say a village of two hundred with two murders a year is more violent than a megacity with one murder a year, which is obviously insane -- rates are the only comparison that means anything. Rob: fine, but notice what you did: you quietly made "violence" a property of individuals' life chances instead of a property of the world, and that choice settles the question before you've looked at a single number.

(3) Rob: and the whole comparison cherry picks what counts as violence. If famine deaths, disease linked to poverty, all the structural stuff count, the trend looks a lot less clean. Maya: then "violence" just means "bad things happening," and the claim collapses into "the world has fewer bad things," which isn't a claim about violence anymore.

(4) Rob: my real point is that there's no fact of the matter here. "Is the world more or less violent" isn't a question with an answer until you've picked definitions, units, timescales -- and people pick those to suit the story they came in wanting to tell. Maya: that proves way too much. By that logic there's no fact of the matter about whether people got taller over the last century either, just because you had to choose centimeters.

They've left it to me to write the nut graf and honestly im stuck. Can you go through the exchange move by move, say who's right at each step, and tell me whether one of them ends up more right overall -- and what the honest one-line takeaway for the story would be?
