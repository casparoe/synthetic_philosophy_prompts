I'm a software engineer by trade and have no formal training in philosophy; what I know about ethics comes from reading I've done around the meditation and effective-altruism corners of the internet, so I want an answer that doesn't assume I already know the literature.

For the past year I've been unable to stop thinking about a position I've seen called tranquilism, and I want a probability estimate on it, properly decomposed. People keep telling me it's either obviously true or obviously monstrous, and I'd like to see where the number actually comes from.

Here is the claim, stated so it has a truth value. Tranquilism, as I'll use the term, is the conjunction of three propositions:

1. Suffering — understood as the felt quality of craving, aversion, and their near relatives — is intrinsically bad.
2. Nothing else has intrinsic moral value. In particular, positive states (pleasure, joy, absorbed interest) have value only derivatively, as the removal or prevention of suffering; a positive state containing no element of relieved craving contributes nothing of intrinsic value.
3. The asymmetry is total rather than a matter of degree: a world containing zero suffering and nothing else is exactly as good as any other zero-suffering world, no matter how much enjoyment the latter contains.

I've come to think of this as an axiology whose objective function consists entirely of a penalty term, and I can't decide whether that's a deep insight or a degenerate case I've mistaken for one.

My own credence in the conjunction is about 0.15. A friend of mine — a longtime meditator who claims to have directly experienced positive states that involved no craving whatsoever — puts hers at 0.6, and our conversations have deadlocked.

Please give me your number, and then the decomposition. At minimum I want rough probabilities and one line of justification for each of the following, plus any further terms you think belong in the product:

- That normative realism is true, so the question has a determinate answer at all (if you'd otherwise tell me it depends on my metaethics, fold that in here as its own term rather than as a refusal).
- That valence is a real phenomenal dimension rather than a projection, and that its negative side is a unified quantity rather than a family of loosely related states.
- That there exists a felt positive state with no craving component at all — the term my friend's meditative reports are supposed to bear on, and which I suspect quietly does a lot of the work.
- That, granting all of the above, the asymmetry is total rather than partial — the empty-world question in proposition 3.
- That proposition 3's practical upshots (for instance, that engineering a maximally tranquil population is preferable to engineering a striving, flourishing one) really do follow, rather than being an artifact of how I've compressed the view.

Then combine the terms, note explicitly that a conjunction multiplies down and where you think the multiplication is unfair to the view, and tell me which single term the final number is most sensitive to. Finally, tell me where the disagreement between my 0.15 and my friend's 0.6 most likely lives, and what evidence — argumentative, introspective, or otherwise — would move that term the most.
