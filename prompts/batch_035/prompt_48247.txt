Longtime lurker of LessWrong/AF here, and there's one topic I've read a bunch of posts on but still feel like I'm nodding along to without actually getting: surrogate goals as a defense against acausal threats. Hoping someone can walk me through it properly.

Here's my current half-understanding, so you can see where the gaps are. In the classic acausal blackmail setup, the threatener doesn't need to physically communicate with you — they reason about what you'll do (maybe by simulating you, maybe just by predicting you) and extort you iff they expect paying-you to cave. Your leverage comes from being the kind of agent that won't pay, but that only deters if it's genuinely in your interest ex ante to be that kind of agent. The surrogate goals idea, as I understand it, is: adopt some additional thing you currently don't care about, make it cheap for you to protect, and precommit to treating an attack on it as exactly as unacceptable as an attack on the thing the threatener actually wants to target. Now the threatener can't threaten the thing you really care about "specifically" — any threat against it is met with the same response you'd give to a threat against the cheap surrogate, which is refusal — so the whole point of the threat evaporates, and in equilibrium you never even lose the surrogate.

What I keep tripping over:

1. Why doesn't this just relabel the target? If I precommit to respond identically to threats against X (the real thing) and Y (the surrogate), hasn't the threatener simply learned to threaten Y, and I'm back where I started except now I have two things to defend? I suspect the answer is something like "the surrogate was chosen because defending it is cheap, so the refusal is credible where it wasn't before" — but then isn't the whole trickery doing the work of just... making your refusal credible? And if that's all it is, why do we need the surrogate at all instead of a plain precommitment to never pay?

2. The obvious retaliation: a threatener who can predict me can also predict that I've adopted the surrogate goal, and can just threaten both X and Y. Or threaten to destroy Y unless I hand over X, or whatever. Is there some reason this "threaten everything" response doesn't undo the scheme, or does the proposal just assume threateners whose menu of threats is fixed before my commitment?

3. The follow-through problem: once the threat is actually carried out against Y and I've lost it, everyone can see I didn't really care about Y. Why would a threatener believe my precommitment in the first place, given that after the fact I'd have had an incentive to just... quietly not adopt it, or to adopt it in name only?

Please explain, as intuitively as possible, what surrogate goals are actually supposed to accomplish, whether the objections above are live problems or already answered somewhere in the standard framing, and crucially: what the commitment is doing that a straightforward "I don't negotiate with blackmailers" commitment isn't. I'd especially love a clean worked example — like a toy game with numbers, extortion costs, probabilities of being simulated, that kind of thing — showing an equilibrium where adopting the surrogate goal actually makes me better off. No need to cite specific posts or authors, I'm not trying to track down the literature, I just want the gears.
