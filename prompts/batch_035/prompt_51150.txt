I've been reading the rationalist blogosphere since the old Sequences-era comment wars over Sleeping Beauty, and after all these years I think I've converged on a stable package of views on self-locating belief and anthropics. A friend is compiling a document of positions on anthropic reasoning for our reading group, and before I submit mine I want a proper consistency audit — I'd rather find out now than have the group find it for me. Here is the package:

1. In the classic setup — fair coin, awakened and interviewed on Monday only if heads, on Monday and Tuesday with amnesia in between if tails, no other clues — Beauty, interviewed upon waking, should give 1/3 to heads.

2. In the variant where, upon waking, Beauty is told that it is Monday, I think her credence in heads should be 1/2 — once the day is fixed, the coin becomes effectively fair again.

3. On the general question of anthropics, I lean toward the Self-Indication Assumption over the Self-Sampling Assumption: ceteris paribus, finding yourself in a world is evidence for worlds with more observers.

4. But I think the Presumptuous Philosopher gets it wrong. If two cosmological theories start at equal priors and one implies, say, a trillion times more observers, I don't think anthropic reasoning alone should make you nearly certain of the bigger-universe theory before any ordinary evidence is in; running the experiment should still matter enormously.

5. I think the Doomsday Argument fails: our birth rank shouldn't move us toward expecting imminent extinction.

6. I think anthropic reasoning is doing real, non-conventional work in the Fermi paradox: that we find ourselves existing before any clear evidence of other civilizations is genuine evidence about where the Great Filter sits, and it legitimately feeds into estimates of existential risk.

7. Lately I've also come to suspect that the halfer/thirder dispute in the classic problem is largely verbal — a disagreement about whether the tails awakenings count as one trial or two, with no underlying difference in what either party expects to observe. On that suspicion, neither halfers nor thirders are making an error about the world; they are keeping different books.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what it costs; for pairs that only look like they conflict, say why the appearance dissolves. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and at the end tell me which single revision to the package would clean up the most.
