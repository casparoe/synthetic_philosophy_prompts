Here's my opening move. I'm not going to start with Aumann — you have your counterexamples to agreeing-to-disagree memorized and I don't want to spend the whole rehearsal there. Instead: picture the two of us locked in a seminar room for a decade, pooling every scrap of evidence either of us ever had, both updating like saints, and at the end of it we still disagree about some contingent proposition p, purely because our priors differed going in. Tell me what that residual disagreement is made of. It can't be evidence — we've shared all of it. It can't be logic. So it's some extra ingredient that never touches the world, never gets washed out by anything, and yet somehow fixes what each of us is willing to bet on p. That doesn't look like a taste to me — it looks like a belief that has exempted itself from every rational standard we apply to beliefs. And here's the part I actually want to see you squirm on: if your prior really were like a taste in ice cream, you'd be roughly indifferent between having yours and having mine, the way I don't mind eating your favorite flavor. So — would you trade priors with me? If not, what exactly are you attached to?
