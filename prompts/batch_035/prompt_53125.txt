So I've been circling this Dennett-adjacent fight for like two years and I want to see it done properly for once. Quick setup so we're on the same page (paraphrasing from memory, don't treat this as a quote): Dennett has this thought experiment with two coffee tasters. One has been drinking Maxwell House for years and slowly, without noticing, stopped liking it — his tastes drifted. The other liked coffee, got a brain lesion (or something like that), and still reports liking it, but his pleasure response is actually gone. Both of them, when asked, sincerely say "I liked coffee then and I don't now" — and Dennett's point is that if the old introspective reports were just as firm before the change as after, you can't locate the moment where the "way coffee tastes" itself shifted vs. where the judgment about it shifted vs. where the liking got disconnected. Introspection underdetermines which of these happened, which is supposed to show the supposed intrinsic, directly-given properties of experience ("qualia") can't do the work they're recruited for. He wants to "quine" them — show the concept is incoherent the way you'd deflate any confused posit.

Now: write the joint statement that a qualia realist (think someone sympathetic to Chalmers or even just Nagel-style "there is something it is like") and a committed Dennettian eliminativist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration about exactly this. I want:

- a numbered section of claims both sides can accept (about the thought experiment itself, about what introspection can and can't discriminate, about which of Dennett's four properties — ineffability, intrinsicness, privacy, immediacy — are actually load-bearing);
- a disagreements section where each remaining disagreement is phrased in wording its own side would actually endorse (no strawmen);
- the cruxes — I suspect one is whether "phenomenal character without any functional difference" is even a coherent possibility, and another is whether the Chase/Sanborn underdetermination shows qualia are incoherent posits or just that introspection is a bad instrument, which are NOT the same thing and the debate constantly slides between them;
- for each crux, a concrete case, tweak to the thought experiment, or argument both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about what it would show written down in advance;
- and flag explicitly which parts of their dispute you think are verbal (e.g. maybe both agree coffee tastes changed, and the fight is only about what "taste" ranges over?) vs. which are genuine.

Hard limit: the whole document should come in under 900 words, numbered sections format. And at the end, add a short "how this could be wrong" note — say which formulations in the document you're least confident are actually signable by both sides, and what argument or consideration would most likely force you to redraw the agreed/disagreed line. Don't pad the agreed section with trivia just to hit the structure; if a claim is vacuous, leave it out.
