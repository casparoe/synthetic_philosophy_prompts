I am contributing a chapter to an edited volume on the philosophy of death, and the editors have asked each contributor to begin with an explicit statement of basic commitments. Before I submit mine, I want an audit of whether they jointly cohere. The list:

1. Death can be bad for the person who dies: it is a harm to the deceased, not merely a loss to the survivors or a worse state of the world.
2. When death is bad, it is bad by deprivation, and only by deprivation: it is bad because, and only because, the life cut short would otherwise have contained more good than the life actually lived.
3. The harm of death is not the process or experience of dying; a sudden, painless death in one's sleep can still be an enormous harm.
4. Nothing can be bad for a person at a time at which that person does not exist.
5. How well or badly off a person is at any given time is fixed entirely by that person's experiences at that time; so far as wellbeing at times goes, I am an experientialist.
6. There was nothing bad for me in the long stretch before I was born, even though coming into existence earlier would presumably have afforded me decades of additional good life; I take the prenatal case to be disanalogous to the posthumous one, though I have never managed to say exactly why.
7. Whatever is bad for someone is bad for them at some particular time.

I realize several of these echo familiar positions associated with Epicurus, Lucretius, and Nagel, but my question is not exegetical; the object of the audit is my list, not the literature. A colleague has already told me the list does not hang together, but she could not say where, and I am beginning to suspect she was bluffing. So please go through the commitments pair by pair: identify which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give, and what giving it up would cost elsewhere in the list. Then tell me which single revision would restore the most consistency at the lowest price, and, finally, whether my colleague was right that there is a problem at all or right only in the weaker sense that some of the commitments merely look unstable. Please write your answer in American English.
