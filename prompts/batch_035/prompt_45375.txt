A philosophy podcast I listened to offered a summary of G. E. Moore's views on the ideal, roughly as follows. Moore identified the two greatest intrinsic goods as friendship and beauty, and he established this by what the presenter called his method of isolation: mentally strip away everything else and see whether the candidate still shines as valuable in itself. On beauty, the presenter said Moore held that a world of beautiful objects with no minds to enjoy them is exactly as good as one in which they are admired, since beauty's value is entirely independent of any consciousness of it. On friendship, we were told that Moore thought the value of loving someone is wholly borrowed from the beloved's admirable qualities — the affection itself adds nothing beyond what the friend is already worth — and that loving a bad person is therefore simply worthless. Finally, since the isolation method weighs components one at a time, Moore supposedly concluded that the value of any whole is just the sum of the values of its parts. Something about this seems off to me — I recall Moore being associated with so-called organic unities, which sounds like the opposite of part-by-part addition — but the presenter sounded knowledgeable. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what Moore would accept, where the distortions lie, and whether any of the presenter's further conclusions collapse once they are corrected.
