hey, I want a custom thought experiment built to spec, on the dreams debate. Background (roughly): Norman Malcolm argued ages ago (Dreaming, late 50s) that dreams are not experiences at all — since there's no way to check a dream report against anything, "what happened in the dream" talk really just tracks dispositions to report stuff while awake. Dennett pushed back: dreams are experiences, we're just terrible at remembering them, like remembering a movie you only half watched. The usual verdict people give is that Malcolm's line is bonks, and honestly that's my verdict too. So build me the reverse case: a thought experiment where Dennett's side comes out looking worse, and Malcolm's behaviourist line handles the data more naturally. I want to see whether his view can be made to earn its keep, that's the point of this.

Constraints: no science fiction. No dream-recording machines, no fancy neurotech, nothing that just builds the answer into the world. Ordinary sleep phenomena only — alarm clocks going off, being shaken awake, grogginess and sleep inertia, external noises getting woven into the "dream", false awakenings, that sort of thing is fair game. The scenario itself should be one paragraph. And no cheating: you can't just stipulate "and there was no experience"; the pressure on Dennett has to fall out of the structure of the case, from things that are already true of sleep.

After the scenario: walk through what each of the two would say about it, tell me which feature of the case is doing the work, what happens if you delete that feature, and how a Dennettian would try to break the case. And be honest with me if you don't think the case can be built to this spec — I'd rather hear that than get a fudge.
