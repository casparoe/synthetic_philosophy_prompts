I've been writing about the effective altruism world for a science-and-ideas outlet for a few years now — long enough that I've accumulated opinions that are actually mine rather than recaps of what other commentators say. My editor wants a first-person piece on where I've landed after all that coverage, and I like the idea, but I really don't want to publish five views that quietly contradict each other and then get dunked on in the replies. So before I draft the thing, I want to run the full package past someone who'll be ruthless about it.

Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) I think the institutional critique of EA is basically correct: treating global poverty as a problem individual donors solve by allocating their giving like a portfolio obscures the fact that it's sustained by political arrangements — trade rules, IP, immigration — that no amount of donor optimization touches, and the whole frame quietly depoliticizes things. (2) At the same time, when I actually meet people who took a finance job specifically in order to give away half their income, I think they're doing something genuinely admirable, and alot of the sneering coverage of earning to give strikes me as status games dressed up as politics. (3) There's a real measurability bias in how EA allocates: cost-effectiveness estimates built on randomized trials systematically channel money toward things you can count — pills, bednets — and away from diffuse, high-upside stuff like policy advocacy or institution-building, where the evidence will never be that clean. (4) Despite all that, I think EA's actual lasting contribution is epistemic — the discipline of comparing interventions against each other on evidence rather than warm glow — and I'd defend that core against critics who claim the whole quantified, spreadsheet-brained mindset is rotten from the start; that version of the critique always sounded to me like an excuse for not thinking hard about what your money does. (5) And I'm fairly relaxed about EA's drift toward longtermist projects and AI risk: to me that looks like cause-neutrality working as designed rather than a betrayal of the global poor, even though it means moving money toward causes about as far from measurable as you can get.

So, audit this for me. Which of the five pull against each other, which pairs only look like they're in tension, and what's the cheapest revision that would make the set hold together — plus a note on what that revision costs me in the piece (e.g., whether I'd have to water the institutional critique down to something much weaker, or give up on defending earning to give altogether). Rank the tensions by severity instead of treating them all as equally fatal, and don't be charitable where I wouldn't notice it myself. And since you're doing me a favor here: be upfront about how confident you are in each diagnosis, and flag the single part of your audit you'd least trust if a proper EA-skeptic went over it — that's the part I'll definately hedge in the essay.
