I'm a senior site reliability engineer with about fifteen years of distributed-systems experience and essentially no philosophy background -- I design and run systems made of hundreds of services, and I spend my days thinking about failure behavior, redundancy, and graceful degradation. A philosopher friend told me about the literature on dispositions, finks, and masks, and I've read enough now to know the shape of it: a glass is fragile because it's disposed to break when struck, the classic conditional analysis says having the disposition is a matter of the if-then holding, and then finks and masks are meant to be counterexamples -- a fink is something that would remove the disposition if the trigger were about to manifest it (the guardian who swaps the glass for an unbreakable duplicate just before impact), while a mask is something that lets the disposition and its basis persist but blocks the path from manifestation-condition to manifestation (the carefully packed glass that is struck but doesn't break because of the bubble wrap). I gather there's also a further wrinkle about whether the mask case should really count as retaining the disposition, and some debate about antidotes versus masks and about so-called reverse finks.

Here's my suspicion, and what I want from you. In my world, this all looks like familiar territory: retries and circuit breakers wrapping flaky downstreams, a load balancer draining a sick instance before users notice, a health check that makes a service's bad state invisible to callers, a fallback response that keeps the API contract satisfied while the real backend burns. My instinct is that the fink/mask distinction maps onto something like the difference between interventions that change the component's intrinsic state (finks, whatever else they are, seem to remove or replace the causal basis) and interventions that leave the component alone and interpose elsewhere in the path (masks), and that the philosophers' residual worry -- whether the masked glass is really still fragile -- is a special case of something we deal with constantly, namely that what a component is disposed to do and what the overall system is disposed to do can come apart layer by layer, and every masking layer quietly becomes part of a new disposition ascription at the level above it.

What I'm asking for is a careful translation, not a pep talk for my analogy. Restate the fink/mask debate -- the conditional analysis it attacks, the fink cases, the mask cases, the antidote cases, the reverse-fink cases, and the main response strategies (whatever philosophers actually say when they try to patch the conditional analysis or replace it with something about causal bases) -- in the vocabulary I actually use: services, dependencies, error budgets, health checks, retries, circuit breakers, failovers, synthetic traffic, the distinction between the process state and the externally observed contract, the way we reason about nested SLAs. Do it case by case, and do it as precisely as you can, resisting the temptation to make my side of the translation look good by fiat.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Specifically: (1) which parts of the philosophical debate survive the translation cleanly, and does my intrinsic-state-versus-interposed-layer reading of the fink/mask distinction hold up, or does it distort what the philosophers mean; (2) whether anything in the debate has no counterpart at all in how I talk -- my guess is the intuition that the masked glass is still fragile, with its basis intact, might be one of these, since in software a disposition is typically something we ascribe relative to a boundary we've drawn, and we redraw the boundary without ceremony whenever we add a retry layer, whereas the philosophers seem to think there's a fact about whether the glass is really still fragile that isn't just a fact about which boundary we picked; and (3) whether any residue cuts the other way -- something their debate handles that my layered-ascription picture can't express, rather than something I handle better. On that last point, be as hard on me as you can: if my picture is just the conditional analysis with extra steps, and my layered talk is the kind of thing fink and mask counterexamples were designed to break, say so, and show me where the breaking happens.
