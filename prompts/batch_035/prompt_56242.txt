I'm a grad student working on laws of nature and I've ended up with what's meant to be a Humean package, but people keep telling me parts of it don't fit together and I can't tell which of their complaints are real. Here's the set, roughly as I'd put it in a paper:

(1) Humean supervenience: everything about the world supervenes on the the distribution of local qualities across spacetime, full stop.
(2) Laws of nature are whatever the best systematization of that distribution says — the system that best balances simplicity and strength. Nothing governs anything; there are no necessitation relations out there in nature.
(3) Laws genuinely explain things. The inverse-square law's being a law is part of why the planets move the way they do, not just a tidy summary of how they happen to move.
(4) There are objective single-case chances. This atom had a real 50% chance of decaying in the next minute, and if you know that, having a credence far off 50% is irrational.
(5) Laws are contingent — there are other worlds with quite different laws.
(6) The laws are observer-independent: ideally completed physics would land any sufficiently rational community of investigators on the same system, so laws get discovered rather than projected from our tastes.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture: go through the commitments, tell me which are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what the cheapest fix costs in each case. Rank the tensions by severity instead of listing everything as equally bad. Then take a side — if the package is untenable as it stands, come out and say which single revision cleans up the most, because honestly I can't see where the problem is supposed to be.
