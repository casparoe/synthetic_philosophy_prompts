A colleague of mine teaching historiography claims to have an argument that historical objectivity is not merely hard to attain but incoherent, and each step sounds like something I have taught with approval. (1) The past contains no story: it is a swarm of events with no beginnings, emphases or endings in it. (2) Any historical account must select some events and omit infinitely many others, and the selection is the historian's, not the past's. (3) Any selection presupposes a judgement of which events matter, and mattering is not a property events possess independently of the questions we bring to them. (4) The selected events must then be arranged into a plot — tragedy, progress, decline, ironic repetition — and these plot forms are cultural inheritances the historian brings to the archive before examining a single document. (5) So every historical work is partly a product of something other than the past itself: of selection, significance and plot. (6) A work whose content is partly a product of something other than its object is not a representation of that object but a construction. (7) Constructions are answerable to taste and politics, not to truth. (8) Therefore no historical account is more true than any rival, and Ranke's ambition to show how it actually was is incoherent, not merely unachievable.

The trouble is that the argument appears to prove far too much. A seismograph trace is also selected and plotted; a map omits almost everything and presupposes what counts as significant; a forensic report must judge which traces matter before it can describe a crime. If (6) and (7) are sound, they seem to show that geology, cartography and forensic science have no facts either — and my colleague does not want to say that. Yet if I reject (6) I seem to throw away everything Carr and Hayden White taught us, and I would much rather keep (1) through (4), which strike me as genuine insights about the historian's situation. Tell me which step to give up, why that step looked fine, and what rejecting it costs me in the rest of my views about history. Please answer in continuous prose only — no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables — the way one would write a careful letter.
