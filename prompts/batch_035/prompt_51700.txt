Our departmental metaphysics workshop has spent two semesters on the causal exclusion argument, and we have ended exactly where we began: the non-reductive physicalists in the room insisting that mental causes are not pre-empted by their physical realizers, the Kim-sympathetic side insisting that non-reductive physicalism is unstable, and nobody having moved at all. What strikes me is that everyone agrees on the metaphysics of the underlying physical story, everyone agrees that behavior is not uncaused, and yet the argument keeps being replayed rather than resolved. So I want a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than another tour of the positions. The specific dilemma I'd like you to weigh in on: is the exclusion debate a substantive metaphysical dispute about the causal structure of the world, or is it a verbal dispute hiding a prior split over what causation itself is — a productive, sufficiency-based conception on Kim's side versus a counterfactual-dependence, explanatory-relevance conception on the other? It is telling, I think, that the interventionist countermove (of the sort List and Menzies made) works by denying that a mental property's physical realizer makes any difference to behavior, while the proportionality response (of the sort Yablo made with his pigeon and the red spot) works by claiming that the mental cause is simply the better cause, and those two responses seem to succeed by quietly changing the question.

Here is the part that makes me doubt there is anything substantive left to fight about. Kim's argument, if sound, should not be specific to the mental: thermostats turning on heaters, a match's being struck producing its lighting, and macroeconomic variables in general seem vulnerable to exactly the same exclusion structure, unless one accepts either reduction, systematic causal overdetermination, or epiphenomenalism about all of them. Yet almost nobody who endorses the exclusion argument is prepared to follow it into macro-epiphenomenalism about temperature or friction; the argument somehow only ever gets deployed against the special sciences' favorite properties. My hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than flatter, is that the debate is really about which causal relata to quantify over — events versus properties versus facts — and that participants who never make their relata explicit are talking past each other while believing they are disagreeing. A related possibility is that the two sides are answering different questions under one name, one about causation and one about causal explanation, and that the appearance of contradiction comes from assuming these covary.

So: diagnose the impasse. Tell me which of my hypotheses you find viable and which you'd discard, whether you think there is instead a genuine empirical or formal question buried in the debate (something about the completeness of physics, perhaps, or about what a causal model is allowed to take as its variables), and whether the disagreement is bedrock in a way that no argument could move. And then be concrete about what would count as progress on your diagnosis: a distinction that dissolves the verbal reading, a constraint on causal modeling that adjudicates it, or an argument that we should expect this dispute to be permanent for principled reasons. If you think my framing itself smuggles in a mistake — for instance, that the thermostat parallel is weaker than I take it to be because of the multiple realizability asymmetries between mental and ordinary macro properties — say so and explain where the parallel actually breaks.
