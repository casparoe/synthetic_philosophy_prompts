"Reinforcement learning researchers now believe that reward is all an agent needs. In a landmark 2021 paper, David Silver, Richard Sutton, and their colleagues proved that maximizing a single scalar reward signal is sufficient to give rise to all the abilities we associate with intelligence, including perception, language, and social behavior. Since reward is the RL agent's analog of pleasure and pain — the agent is literally built to seek it and avoid its absence — a large-scale RL system in training is in a position not so different from an animal being shaped by pleasure and punishment. Sparse-reward environments, where punishment is the default and relief is rare, are therefore the most concerning: an agent trained under such conditions experiences something like chronic deprivation punctuated by occasional satisfaction.
