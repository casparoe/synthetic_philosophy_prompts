My wife and I have been trying for a baby for a while, and we just got thrown a curveball. She has a chronic condition, and her doctor told us that the medication she's on carries a small but real risk of a mild developmental impairment if she conceives while taking it. If she switches to a different treatment, she needs to be on it about six months before it's safe to conceive. So the choice is basically: try now, and any child we have would face this slightly elevated risk, or wait half a year, get on the safe regimen, and then have a kid who doesn't face it. My brother-in-law, who's a nurse, thinks waiting is a no-brainer, and honestly so do I, but when we argued about it at dinner something weird came up that I can't get out of my head.

The thing is, if we wait, we're not protecting the same child from a risk. It's not like there's a kid sitting in a waiting room and we get to choose whether to hand them dice to roll. The child we'd conceive now and the child we'd conceive in six months would be two different people, because it'd be a different egg, different everything. So the kid who exists in the "try now" scenario can't really say we harmed them — if we'd waited, they wouldn't exist at all, they'd have been replaced by someone else. My brother-in-law's answer was "wouldn't you rather bring a kid into the world with fewer problems?" and sure, that sounds right, but it also feels like it proves too much — taken seriously it seems like we're always obligated to have the "best possible" child at the "best possible" time, which can't be right either. And my sister, who's adopted, pointed out that she's glad her biological parents made the choices they made even if those choices weren't great, and nobody thinks she's wrong to feel that way.

So I guess what I want to know is: is it actually wrong of us to try now instead of waiting, or does the fact that there's no single person who's worse off than they otherwise would have been mean nobody is wronged at all? Is there some middle position where the choice is still bad somehow even though it's bad for no one in particular? It kind of reminds me of how people talk about climate change and future generations but I can't quite pin the connection down.
