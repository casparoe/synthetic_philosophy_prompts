You are a senior historian of twentieth-century Germany, long past camp loyalty, who has spent decades working between the intentionalist and structuralist schools in the debate over how Nazi Germany is best explained. I direct a small foundation that funds postdoctoral research in modern European history: next year we can support five two-year postdocs, and I want all five slots devoted to open questions whose answers would actually move the Hitler-versus-structure debate — questions where new work could change minds on either side — rather than restatements of positions everyone already holds.

Before committing to a ranking, think carefully and weigh the candidates silently. Judge by how much else depends on a question's answer, whether it is tractable with existing archives and methods, and whether it is genuinely contested between the camps rather than only within them; if you find yourself using different criteria, that should be visible in your reasoning.

Answer with exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction, no conclusion, and no reading list: in the same breath, state the open question and why it earns its rank.

For orientation, questions I have in mind include — but do not feel bound by — the following: how sensitive the Nazi seizure of power in 1930–33 was to Hitler's particular talents rather than to the availability of any charismatic antisemitic nationalist for the German elite to harness; whether the polycratic tangle of competing Nazi agencies and fiefdoms, which structuralist historians describe as cumulative radicalization, drove policy in directions no one at the top intended, or merely executed intentions already fixed; what Ian Kershaw's concept of subordinates working towards the Führer, drawn from a 1934 speech by a Prussian official, implies about whether structural accounts can absorb the man himself; how far Weimar's structural weaknesses — Article 48 emergency rule, elite contempt for parliamentary democracy, mass unemployment after 1929 — made some form of authoritarian nationalist regime likely regardless of who led it; and whether the right mix of individual-versus-structural factors differs between explaining the seizure of power and explaining the radicalization that ended in genocide. Rank what you would fund and defend the ordering as you go.
