So I've been sold on the standard rationalist-adjacent story about time's arrow for basically forever: the dynamical laws are (more or less) time-symmetric, the reason eggs don't unscramble and we remember the past but not the future is that the universe started in a spectacularly low-entropy state — the past hypothesis — and everything else (causation, records, our ability to do induction at all) is downstream of that one boundary condition. No flow, no passage, just an entropy gradient plus symmetric microphysics. I recite this as settled whenever it comes up in discussions of Boltzmann brains or decision theory or whatever.

Lately I want to actually stress-test it instead of just repeating it. What would count against it? Not just the usual "where did the low-entropy initial condition come from, isn't that fine-tuned" hand-wave — I mean what would genuinely move me off the position. Things I'd especially like you to chew on:

- If the fundamental laws turned out to be time-asymmetric in some real ingredient (neutral kaon decay is the standard example, or some future theory with an honest T-violating term baked in), does that undermine the boundary-condition explanation or is it just orthogonal? Is there a version where micro T-violation could do actual explanatory work for the macroscopic arrow, and what would the world have to look like for that?
- Does spontaneous-collapse stuff (GRW-style, where collapse injects genuine irreversibility into the microdynamics) count as evidence against the pure boundary-condition story, or as a freindly amendment? What would a defender concede vs. dismiss here?
- The Penrose-flavoured point that the early universe being *smooth* isn't quite the same as being low-entropy, since gravitational entropy apparently lives in lumpiness (black holes and so on). If that's right, how much of my confidence was misplaced all along — did I conflate two different claims and only defend one of them?
- And the thing that actually bugs me most: every explanation of the boundary condition I've ever encountered — inflation, cyclic cosmologies, baby-universe stories — seems to just relocate the fine-tuning rather than remove it. At what point has the view been qualified into unfalsifiability, and is that a problem for a claim of this kind (a boundary condition at the beginning of everything being whatever it is), or is it just what you get, and should I be okay with it?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which ones a committed defender would themselves concede as counting against the view versus which they'd explain away, and be honest about whether this position risks anything at all. If the answer is "it's very well insulated and that's fine because X", say that rather than manufacturing drama. Also, please write the whole answer in British English — I know "behavior" and the like keep slipping into my own writing, don't let it slip into yours.
