My friend Maya and I have been arguing about the problem of evil all semester — we're both in a philosophy of religion seminar and it has basically consumed our friendship, in a mostly good way. Maya's the religious one (grew up evangelical, now more of a generic theist) and I keep bringing her cases and writing down her verdicts about whether God would be justified in permitting each evil. So far: on a dictatorship torturing dissidents, she says justified — free will is valuable enough that God won't prevent even horrendous choices. On a child's terminal illness where the family and community clearly come out more compassionate and tightly knit, she says justified — suffering builds character, soul-making and all that. On a lightning strike that instantly kills a hermit nobody knows about (nobody grows from it, nobody even finds out) she surprised me: justified again, because a world with stable, exception-free natural laws is better than one where God keeps intervening, and you can't have those laws without their occasional bad effects. But when I asked about a case where God directly inflicts pointless suffering — no free creature involved, no law involved — she said flat out not justified.

Next week's seminar is on animal suffering and I want to walk in with a prediction I can lord over her. The case on the table: hundreds of millions of years of animals suffering before humans ever existed — like a dinosaur dying slowly of an infection, with no humans around to grow from it, no souls to make, and no free creature responsible. Predict her verdict. But more than the verdict, I want the analysis: what are the candidate rules that fit her track record so far (free-will stuff, soul-making, the law-regularity idea, whatever else fits all four of her answers), what does each rule predict for the dinosaur case, and which do you actually think she's running and why? I have a suspicion that at least two of the rules that fit her record come apart on the dinosaur case, but I can't tell which.

Also — and this is the part I'll actually use — give me one further case I could pose to her over coffee that would settle which of the top two candidate rules she's really committed to. Something concrete, not a hypothetical about angels. I keep thinking the law-regularity rule is her weak spot for some reason I can't quite articulate, so if that's a live option tell me what would expose it.
