I've basically convinced myself that Malthus was right, just early — the mechanism is sound (population grows geometrically, food supply doesn't, so the crunch has to come eventually), and the fact that we've dodged it for 200+ years only shows the dodges were temporary. My friend calls this unfalsifiable doom-mongering and says the theory has been "rescued" so many times it predicts nothing at all.

What would actually count against my view? I want you to take this seriously rather than just listing stuff. Specifically:

- Which observations would be decisive against "right but early" as opposed to merely awkward? E.g., does the global demographic transition — fertility falling below replacement almost everywhere, population possibly peaking and declining this century — kill the mechanism, or is it consistent with it? Where does Boserup's story (population pressure driving agricultural intensification, i.e. the causation running the other way) fit in, and does it defeat Malthus or just re-describe him?
- What about the rescue moves themselves? The Green Revolution, the opening of new land, trade, the demographic transition — at what point does "the crunch was postponed again" stop being an explanation and start being epicycles? Is there a principled difference between a long-run prediction with an unspecified date and a prediction that can't fail?
- My own attempt at a defense: I'd say a fair test is whether the theory ever committed to a no-later-than date, or to which resources are incompressible — if Malthusianism implies *some* resource must eventually bind, then persistent real price declines across basically everything (metals, food, energy) for a century or so looks like genuine counter-evidence, not just delay. Does that work, or have I just chosen a test the theory passes on a technicality?

Give me a committed verdict at the end: is "right but early" a defensible epistemic position or a respectablized way of never conceding, and if you think I should give it up, tell me straight. And if you think the view survives, tell me what observation, even hypothetically, would make *you* abandon it — I don't want a position I can't lose.
