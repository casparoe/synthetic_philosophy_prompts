So I'm getting married in October, and at the rehersal dinner my best man -- philosophy grad student, insufferable in the best way -- walked me into an argument about whether the vows actually bind me, and now I can't get it out of my head. Here it is as he laid it out:

(1) A promise can't conjure an obligation out of nothing; whatever makes it wrong to break a promise has to be something the promise actually did to the world.
(2) The only thing the promise does to the world is change your side of it: now you have reason to rely on me, build plans on me, pass up alternatives.
(3) If your reliance is what makes breaking it wrong, then the duty tracks the reliance, not the promise itself.
(4) But then anything else that produces the same reliance should produce the same duty: a confident lie, my track record of doing this every year, even a third party correctly predicting me if word gets back to you and you plan around it.
(5) And if you never relied at all -- you forgot the promise, made no plans, maybe you'd honestly be relieved if I broke it -- then there's nothing wrong with breaking it, and plausibly no obligation left at all.

So he concludes the vow is just a really effective machine for generating reliance, and the wrong of breaking it is the ordinary wrong of letting down someone who depended on you -- no special promissory duty, just garden-variety harm through dependence. He kept comparing it to Hume's line about promises being useful fictions the way money is.

Which sounds almost plausible until you notice what it proves. On his view my wedding vows would bind me to nothing if my fiancee were a total skeptic who never beleived a word of them, while a used car salesman's patter would bind him completely if some poor guy genuinely hung his whole year on it. And a promise extracted at gunpoint would bind you, as long as you actually relied on it. I really don't want to accept that I'm obligated to someone exactly insofar as they took me seriously.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? My instinct is that it slips somewhere between (3) and (4) -- something about the difference between reliance making a breach *worse* and reliance being *all there is* -- but I can't make it precise. If a premise has to go, tell me which one, why it looked so innocent, and what rejecting it costs (he warned me that every way out has a price, like ending up with the skeptic conclusion anyway or with obligations I never consented to). And if you think his conclusion is actually survivable, tell me how much of it you'd keep and what weird residue you're left holding. I have about a month of quiet panic left before the wedding, so take your time but be thorough.
