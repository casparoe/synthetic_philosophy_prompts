Is there a name for this? I organize a weekly pickup game and handle the logistics over a group chat. When I need to change the time, the change only really "sticks" if I announce it when everyone is together, or in the main chat where everybody can see everybody else reacting to it. If instead I tell people one by one, and each confirms, and I even confirm back ("ok, but you know that Alex also knows, right?"), coordination still falls apart way more often — someone shows up at the old time because they weren't sure the others had actually switched. And the weirdest part: it doesn't get much better with more rounds. Telling everyone, then confirming that everyone got it, then confirming that everyone knows that everyone got it — after a few rounds it feels like it should become equivalent to the public announcement, but it doesn't behave like that, its like there's a qualitative jump that no number of layered confirmations bridges. I vaguely remember the phrase "common knowledge" being used for something like this, but what I'm after is the specific thing: why layered one-on-one confirmations never add up to the public version, and whether theres an actual formal result or literature on that gap. So: does this have a name, and if "common knowledge" is the nearest concept, what exactly is the difference between it and the layered-confirmations setup I'm describing? Please don't just give me the label — I want to understand where the edges of the concept are.
