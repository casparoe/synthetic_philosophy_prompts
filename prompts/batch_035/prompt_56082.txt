In the sentience debates I follow — particularly around insect welfare and how animal advocacy funders rank candidate beneficiaries — people reach again and again for the same image: sentience is not an on/off light but a dimmer switch, a matter of degree rather than presence or absence. The conclusion the image is used to license is that moral status likewise comes in degrees, so that a bee sits low on the scale, a pig higher, and a thermostat at zero, with our concern to be proportioned to the brightness. Please audit the analogy. Set out what maps onto what: the current, the brightness, the switching mechanism, the person watching the light. Then identify where the mapping breaks — for instance, whether sentience is really a single continuous quantity like luminosity or a cluster of dissociable capacities (valenced experience, nociception, self-modelling) that need not dim together; whether there is a genuine zero point or only a contested threshold below which we simply cannot tell whether there is anything it is like to be the creature; and whether the intensity of experience tracks the strength of a moral claim at all, or merely some property correlated with it. Separate the disanalogies that matter for the intended use from those that do not, then tell me whether the graded-moral-status conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether a better comparison — a mixture of kinds rather than a spectrum of intensities, separate channels rather than one volume knob — would carry the weight more honestly. If the dimmer switch was doing more of the argumentative work than the conclusion deserves, I want to know that too.
