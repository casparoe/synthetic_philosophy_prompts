I'm in a forecasting group, and last week two members got into a standoff about how to generate a probability that our city's planned light-rail line will open by the agency's announced date, and I couldn't referee it. The exchange, as best I can reconstruct it:

(1) Priya said to use the outside view: take the reference class of comparable urban rail megaprojects, which are notorious for cost and schedule overruns, and let the class's base rate set the probability. (2) Davi said the class should instead be rail projects delivered by this same agency in this region, whose record is much better; pooling in projects from countries with different construction practices and land-acquisition regimes just contaminates the estimate. (3) Priya called that cherry-picking: with a free choice of reference class you can dial in whatever probability you want, and the regional class is tiny anyway, so its base rate is mostly noise. (4) Davi answered that "use the broadest class" is just as arbitrary, that relevance should beat breadth, and that nobody would predict his commute time by averaging over the class of all trips, including transatlantic flights. (5) Priya conceded the relevance point but pushed it back at him: if you sort the class by causal relevance, every matching feature you demand shrinks it, until the fully relevant class contains exactly one member — this project — in which case the base rate of on-time opening is undefined, and the outside view has devoured itself. (6) Davi said that may just be the truth of it: there is no neutral probability waiting to be discovered, and any number reflects modelling choices. Priya replied that if that's so, forecasters' claimed discipline is theater.
