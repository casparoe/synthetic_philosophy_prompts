My two friends and I go hiking most saturdays, and last weekend the whole climb turned into an argument about whether the world is getting less violent, and neither of them would drop it, so now they've both asked me to get a verdict from someone. The exchange went roughly like this. Maya said the answer is obviously yes: your chance of dying a violent death, per capita, is way lower than in basically any earlier era — fewer war deaths per person, less homicide, etc. Dev said that's a statistical trick: in absolute numbers more people die violently every year now than at most points in history, because there are eight billion of us, and telling the family of someone killed in a war that, adjusted for population, this is a remarkably peaceful era, is monstrous. Maya replied that per capita is the only sane measure, because the question is about the risk facing a typical person — otherwise you could make the world "less violent" simply by having fewer children, which is absurd, and conversely the absolute-count measure makes the distant past look peaceful just because hardly anyone was alive to be killed. Dev said moral weight isn't carried by risk but by total suffering: a world with the same per-person risk and eight times the people contains eight times the corpses, and he refuses to call that no worse. Then he added a second front: "violence" itself is doing sneaky work in Maya's graphs — if you count famines, preventable disease, and the domestic abuse that was never recorded anywhere, the decline might be partly a bookkeeping illusion, and anyway the answer flips depending on whether your baseline is the 1940s or the 1990s. Maya said if you let "violence" stretch to cover everything bad, the question stops having an answer at all — which, she admitted, might actually be Dev's hidden conclusion. Can you adjudicate this move by move: who's right about which specific point (per capita vs. absolute, the definition of violence, whether the trend depends on the window chosen), whether there's a genuine fact of the matter once you fix those choices, and who comes out more right overall? Please answer in American English.
