Here's what the book says, more or less: "The Nash bargaining solution picks the agreement that maximizes the product of the two players' utility gains over their fallback, and it's the unique solution satisfying a short list of axioms: efficiency, symmetry, invariance to rescaling of utilities, and independence of irrelevant alternatives. Kalai and Smorodinsky's fix is to normalize each player's gain by the best they could possibly get, and pick the agreement where the two normalized gains are equal: each player gives up the same fraction of their dream. And because the gains are normalized this way, the solution effectively splits the difference — in practice it amounts to an even split of the surplus. Better yet, the KS solution keeps every one of Nash's axioms except independence of irrelevant alternatives, which it trades for individual monotonicity, and since IIA was always the suspicious axiom, nothing of value is lost: the KS solution is also resource monotonic, so whenever the pie grows, both players weakly benefit.
