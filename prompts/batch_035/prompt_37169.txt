Please explain, in an intuitive rather than a purely formal way, the phenomenon of dilation in imprecise probability theory. Set the stage briefly: an agent's credences are represented by a non-singleton set of probability functions, and imprecision in the agent's attitude toward a proposition is measured by the spread of probabilities the set assigns it. Dilation occurs when conditioning on some event E makes the set's probabilities for an unrelated-seeming proposition A more spread out than they were before -- so that acquiring evidence E leaves the agent strictly less precise about A, even though every probability function in the set may have assigned A exactly one-half both before and after.

I would like you to do three things. First, construct a simple two-coin example: one coin is known to be fair; about a second coin the agent is unsure whether it is fair and independent of the first or perfectly correlated with it, and this uncertainty is represented by a set of priors. Walk through, step by step, why conditioning on the outcome of the first coin dilates the agent's interval for the second coin landing heads. In particular, I want the mechanical intuition for why an event can be evidentially relevant to A for some members of the credal set and evidentially irrelevant for others, so that the set as a whole disperses upon conditioning even though no single member becomes unsure.

Second, explain why dilation is widely regarded as a problem for the sets-of-probabilities view of imprecise credence. It seems to show that representing ignorance as a set of sharp probabilities has an unwanted structural consequence: the framework manufactures apparent learning and unlearning of correlations out of what is really just indecision between hypotheses about correlation. Address whether the phenomenon reflects a genuine feature of rational imprecise attitudes or an artifact of the representation -- for instance, whether an agent in the two-coin case should really condition on the observed flip at all, given that she does not know which conditioning hypothesis (independence versus correlation) governs it.

Third, briefly connect the phenomenon to the broader dialectic: critics take dilation to support the view that something is wrong with treating imprecision as a set of precise posteriors, while defenders reply that the dilating event is not genuinely evidence for the agent, or that the intuition against dilation illicitly assumes the agent's credences must behave like a single probability function. Say which reply you find stronger and why. Assume I know the formal definition of a credal set and of conditioning applied member-wise; what I want is the working intuition, the diagnosis of what the example shows, and a clear statement of where you come down.
