Longtime LW-adjacent reader here, been through every quantum immortality thread since forever, and I've finally written down where I actually stand. I want a proper consistency check because I have a nagging feeling I'm smuggling something. The package:

(1) Everett is just right. Unitary evolution, decoherence, every branch equally real, no collapse postulate, no Gods peeking at the detector.

(2) Quantum immortality is a crock. If I rig a device to a quantum coin that kills me in half the branches, I should be exactly as scared as a Copenhagenist, maybe more. The QI argument confuses "some future branch-people continue" with "I continue," and I'd say anyone who runs the device anyway is basically suicidal, not rational.

(3) Anthropic selection effects are real and you should update on your own existence. I'm a thirder in Sleeping Beauty, I think multiverse + selection genuinely raises the probability of finding ourselves in a life-friendly universe, and I take self-sampling style reasoning seriously in general.

(4) Personal identity is psychological continuity, full stop. I'm signed up for cryonics and I'd count a revival that only preserves, say, 80% of my pattern as still being me. No extra thread of identity beyond the pattern.

(5) FDT is the right decision theory. I one-box, I pay the driver in Parfit's hitchhiker, the whole deal.

(6) But I won't pay Pascal's mugger, and I think that's fine — an uncorrelated claim with an absurdly tiny probability shouldn't dominate no matter the stakes.

Do these hang together or not? I genuinely can't tell anymore after years of arguing, so don't assume you know which one I'm attached to. Go through it properly: which ones actually fight each other, which only look like they do, and if something has to give, what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost me elsewhere? Rank the tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and be specific about which pair is the real problem, not a vibes-level "anthropics is weird."
