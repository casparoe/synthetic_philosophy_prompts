I'm taking a free will seminar this semester and my TA has this ritual every section where she throws out little thought experiments and gives her own verdict after we argue for a while. I started keeping a list of her calls (me and another girl in the class have a running bet on predicting the next ones). Next week is supposedly the "hard cases" section and I want to show up with predictions. Here's her track record so far, all about moral responsibility:

1. Jones is going to vote Democrat anyway; a neuroscientist has a device that would have forced the vote if Jones showed any sign of switching, but Jones never wavers, and the device never fires. Her verdict: responsible.

2. Same setup, but Jones does start to waver, the device detects it and quietly suppresses the wavering, and Jones ends up voting Democrat for the same reasons he had all along. Her verdict: still responsible. She said the zap didn't make him do it, it just kept the road clear.

3. A guy whose entire motivational system was designed by neuroscientists from birth, and he now acts exactly as the programming dictates. Her verdict: not responsible.

4. A normal person with full alternatives doing something ordinary for her own reasons. Responsible, obviously.

5. Someone voting with a gunman literally standing behind them. Not responsible.

So she's clearly given up on "could have done otherwise" as necessary. But I genuinely can't tell what she's running instead, since at least two rules fit everything above. One is like: you're responsible iff the decision comes out of your own reasons and no one else's device is doing actual causal work on the decision itself, while messing with alternatives is fine. Another is more like: you're responsible iff you're the source, meaning the character the decision flows from is one you had a real hand in shaping. As far as I can tell both of those agree on 1-5, and maybe there's a third I havent thought of.

Here's the preview she posted for next week:

(a) The device is scheduled to fire this one time no matter what, as a test run. It fires. But at that exact moment Jones is, for his own reasons, independently deciding to vote Democrat. Either the device alone or Jones's own reasons alone would have been enough to produce the vote, and both actually happen. Responsible or not?

(b) Programmed-from-birth guy again, but decades later the neuroscientists lose remote access, the implant degrades, and he spends 30 years deliberately rebuilding his values. Nowadays roughly 90% of what moves him is stuff he built himself, 10% is leftover programming, and the act in question flows partly through that leftover 10%. Responsible or not?

(c) The device fires again but a split second too late -- Jones's own decision was already made before the zap lands, so the zap does nothing to the vote. Responsible or not?

So: what's your prediction for her verdict on each of a, b and c? Which rule do you think she's actually running, and where do the candidate rules that fit her track record come apart on these?
