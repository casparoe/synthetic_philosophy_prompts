My sister and I got into it about whether she should take a free genetic test for a condition there's no treatment for. She said she'd rather not know, and I told her that's straightforwardly irrational because Good's theorem proves information always has non-negative expected value — as long as the test is free and accurate, refusing it can only leave her worse off in expectation. She said the theorem doesn't apply to this case but couldn't really explain why, and tbh neither can I. So who's right? If she's just being irrational I want to keep pushing her, but if the theorem doesn't actually cover a case like this, what exactly breaks?
