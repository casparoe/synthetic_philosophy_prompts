I'm a novelist, three books in, mostly literary fiction with a slightly uncanny streak, and for my fourth I'm building the plot around the old Death in Damascus thought experiment — you probably know it: a man runs into Death in the Damascus market, Death looks suprised to see him and tells him he's coming for him tomorrow, and the man reasons that Death, being infallible, must already have predicted where he'll be, so he bolts for Aleppo — and of course that's exactly where Death is waiting, because fleeing to Aleppo was what Death predicted all along. My protagonist is a very literal-minded rationalist, an actuary by trade, and I want the novel to take his reasoning seriously rather than wave the prophecy away as magic. So I've arranged to interview a philosopher, a friend of a friend who works on decision theory, over Zoom, probably 45 minutes. He's a committed defender of causal decision theory — the two-boxing sort — and apparently has a line he likes to repeat, something to the effect that causal decision theory was never meant to make you rich, only to stop you paying for outcomes you couldn't have caused. Which is fair enough, but my character dies, so I need more than that.

I've done my homework but alot of the literature goes over my head, so I want the questions to do the pressing for me. Draft me eight to ten interview questions that go past asking him to restate the view. I especially want one on what his theory actually recommends my character do — I've gathered the answer involves randomising, flipping a coin between Damascus and Aleppo, so press him on whether Death can predict a coin flip, and what it even means to choose randomly if Death has already modelled your decision procedure rather than just your final act. I also want one on the awkwardness that in this case everyone's intuitions seem to flip relative to Newcomb's problem — the same people who cheerfully two-box seem to think the man was a fool to run — and one on whether it can ever be rational to do what you're confident you predictably won't do, since my character's whole arc turns on that.

For each main question, give me a follow-up keyed to the reply he's most likely to give — he's a fast talker and I'll lose the thread if I don't have something ready. And if you can slip in one question about what would have to be true about Death — how good a predictor, exactly — for his answer to change, that would help me decide how literally to write the prophecy. Please keep it conversational; I'll be reading these off a notebook on camera.
