I'd like an audit of a package of views I've accumulated on historical explanation — mostly from Braudel and the Annales school, filtered through my own reading — because I've slowly realized I may be committed to things that don't fit together. Here is where I've landed:

(1) In the big macro-historical questions — why Europe industrialized before China, why some regions became durable cores of the world economy and others peripheries — the decisive causal factors live in the longue durée: geography, climate, agrarian structures, demographic regimes, the slow accumulation of trade networks. Not decisions, not inventions, not individuals.

(2) Braudel's picture of nested temporalities is basically right: event-time, the medium conjunctural band of roughly twenty to fifty years, and the longue durée are genuinely distinct registers, and the deeper layers constrain the shallower ones without dictating them.

(3) I'm nonetheless repelled by explicit geographic and climatic determinism when I meet it in popular big-history form — continent axes, resource endowments, and the like strike me as crude, and I don't accept those books' arguments.

(4) I believe some event-level contingencies altered trajectories in kind, not merely in timing — that certain individual decisions in, say, the July crisis of 1914 changed what the twentieth century was, not just when things happened in it.

(5) I think counterfactual history as a method is largely idle: the "holding everything else fixed" that a counterfactual requires is precisely what history never gives you, so I distrust that style of reasoning whenever I see it practiced.

(6) I also believe deep structures are, in principle, revisable within a generation when there is sufficient will — the Meiji transformation being the obvious case, where a state deliberately rewrote its economic geography and institutional order in a few decades.

What I want from you: go through these six commitments, identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, and rank the real tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damaging. For each genuine tension, tell me what would have to give and what it would cost to give it up — including whether the cost infects any of my other commitments. For the merely apparent ones, show me the dissolution rather than just asserting that one exists. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most of the package, and whether there's a coherent position left standing that keeps the spirit of most of it.

One request: if you think the package is incoherent in a way that no revision of the six items can fix — that the problem is with how I've set up the question rather than with any particular commitment — say so plainly instead of patching over it. I'd rather hear that the audit can't be done as posed than get a polite damage report on a framework that should have been abandoned.
