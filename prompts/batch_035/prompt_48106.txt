By day I write backend systems, and lately I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole that started, slightly embarrassingly, from work. I was rereading Lamport's old paper on distributed systems — the one about clocks and ordering events in a network — and realised the problem it solves is basically the same thing special relativity says about simultaneity: there's no total order on events unless you impose one, just a partial order of who-could-have-influenced-whom. Two events that are simultaneous in one reference frame arent in another, so there seems to be no objective "now" slicing across the whole universe. From there I stumbled into the presentism vs eternalism debate: presentists say only the present exists, the past is gone and the future isnt there yet; eternalists say past, present and future events are all equally real, the universe is a four-dimensional block, and "now" is like "here" — just an indexical, no more metaphysically deep than my current location. And there's apparently a third option, the growing block, where past and present are real but the future isn't, which to me sounds exactly like an append-only log with no writes yet to come.

Here's what bugs me: this debate seems to have been going for decades without anyone moving. The relativity argument against presentism is old news, presentists have their stock replies (priviledge a frame even if no experiment could detect it, or deny that physics settles what's metaphysically real), and eternalists still can't give a convincing account of why time *feels* like it passes if everything just tenselessly is. My working theory, as a database person, is that it's at bottom a disagreement about what "exists" means — the eternalist's "exists" is like every row in an immutable events table, the presentist's is like whatever row is currently in a mutable working set, and both could be true in their own sense of the word. But maybe that's too quick and there's a genuine substantive question hiding in there, or the two sides are answering different questions entirely — one about the structure of spacetime, one about the phenomenology of experience — under a single label.

So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Diagnose it using what each side actually claims rather than caricatures, tell me whether my "it's about the word exists" theory holds up, and — this is the part I care most about — what would count as progress? A result in physics, a better theory of temporal experience, some conceptual distinction nobody has drawn yet, or nothing short of one side dying out? And is the impasse a sign the question is ill-posed, or just that it's genuinely hard? Please answer in American English, and don't be gentle with my database metaphors — if they're doing more harm than good, say so.
