I teach a 300-level seminar on social ontology — 14 students, one 75-minute session — and we're coming up on the unit on whether doing something *together* is anything over and above each of us doing it near the others. Design an in-class experiment that makes them feel the difference instead of just reading it. My rough idea: pair students off on some task that genuinely needs two people, where some pairs have explicitly agreed to be a team (signing a silly team sheet, picking a team name, whatever) and other pairs are just seated next to each other doing the same task with no agreement. Then, partway in, one member of each pair gets a secret offer — bonus points if they abandon their partner mid-task and switch to a solo scramble for points instead. The debrief should map who felt they had to *announce* they were quitting versus who just quietly walked away, and press on whether that felt "I owe you notice" thing is a moral obligation or some other kind — that's the distinction I want them walking away with. They shouldnt hear any of the technical vocabulary until afterwards. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I assume a few students will defect with zero hesitation and others will explain everything via plain everyday morality), and be honest about which part of the idea the activity genuinely cannot reproduce and I'll have to just supply in the debrief.
