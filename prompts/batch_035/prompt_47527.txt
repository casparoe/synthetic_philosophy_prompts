Please write a philosophical essay, in British English, of roughly 1,200 to 1,800 words, on the questions below. The essay should weigh the arguments on both sides honestly, but it must end by defending a definite verdict, not a refusal to choose.

The case. On Sunday evening, while Beauty sleeps, a fair coin is tossed. If it lands heads, she is woken once, on Monday, interviewed, and then returned to ordinary life with the week's memories erased. If it lands tails, she is woken on Monday, interviewed, and put back to sleep; that night a second fair coin is tossed. If the second coin lands heads, there is no further awakening; if it lands tails, she is woken a second time, on Tuesday, and interviewed. All memories of each awakening are erased before the next one, or before she returns home. Beauty is told the whole protocol in advance, believes it, and wakes each time in the laboratory, so she always knows she is at an experimental interview; there are no other awakenings during the experiment. At each interview the experimenters begin by announcing the day of the week, and they always tell the truth about it.

The questions. (1) When Beauty wakes, after the drug has worn off but before the day has been announced, what credence should she give to the proposition that the first coin landed heads? (2) Once she has been told that it is Monday, what credence should she give to that proposition then? The case is a test of self-locating belief: what, if anything, a person can learn from facts about which of her own possible situations she is in.

Two lines of answer suggest themselves, and the essay should develop both. According to the first, her waking provides no information: she knew she would be woken at least once whatever the coins did, so the answer to (1) is 1/2. According to the second, her present awakening might have been the Tuesday one — an awakening that had only a one-in-four chance of occurring at all — and she should treat herself as randomly located among all the awakenings that might have come about; this yields a different figure for (1), which the essay should derive rather than assume. The essay should examine what each line commits Beauty to at both stages, including any point at which a line would oblige her, before the day is announced, to assign probability zero to a Tuesday awakening, and what becomes of that assignment if the Tuesday awakening in fact occurs and she wakes in it. It should also examine the awkward middle course — retaining 1/2 as the answer to (1) while accepting ordinary conditionalisation throughout — and decide whether that course is stable here.

The verdict may be that one line is right, that a principled hybrid is right, or even that one of the two questions lacks a determinate answer; but whichever it is, it must be defended, and the recommended credences must be stated explicitly. Please do not turn the essay into a survey of the literature on Sleeping Beauty, and do not append a bibliography or footnoted citations; argue from the protocol as given, on its merits. The labels 'halfer' and 'thirder' may be used as shorthand for the two lines, but every claim made under a label must be checked against this protocol, not against the textbook version of the problem.
