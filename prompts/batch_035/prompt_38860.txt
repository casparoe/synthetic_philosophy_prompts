For a while now I've been trying to work out, entirely on my own, what an emotion actually is, and I've gotten stuck in a way I can't resolve by introspection alone. It started on a flight: I know, and genuinely believe, that flying is far safer than driving, yet my hands sweat and my stomach drops for the whole ascent. When I describe this to people, three different pictures of emotion come out, and I can't tell which one is true. My brother says the fear must be a kind of judgment — that some part of me really does believe the plane will crash, and the sweaty palms are just that belief showing itself in the body. My sister-in-law says it's more like a perception: the fear is detecting something (danger, or the possibility of disaster) roughly the way eyes detect a table — a quick, crude read that my rational thoughts can then overrule but not switch off. And a friend of mine insists both are overcomplicating it: an emotion is just the feeling itself, the racing heart and the churning stomach and the dread, and if you strip the bodily sensations away there's simply nothing left to call fear.

I keep testing these pictures against cases and each one keeps half-failing. The perception story fits the flight case — my fear resists my better knowledge the way an optical illusion resists measurement — but then I think about crying at a sad song, feeling genuinely moved and even enjoying it, and I can't tell whether the feeling and the evaluation have come apart there or whether one of the three views just has no story for it. The judgment story seems to struggle with my dog, who is unmistakably terrified of the vacuum cleaner but presumably isn't judging anything; the pure feeling story seems to struggle with the fact that I can be afraid of something happening next year, which no bodily sensation seems to be about.

So here is what I'd like from you: give me your actual credence that each of the three pictures is basically right — emotions as judgments, emotions as perceptions (of value or danger or significance, however you want to gloss it), emotions as just feelings — plus a credence for "none of the above cleanly." Then decompose the reasoning: how much turns on whether emotions represent the world at all (can fear be correct or incorrect in the way a belief can), how much on whether a state deserves to be called a perception when it systematically ignores what the person knows, how much on whether animals and infants having emotions tells against the judgment picture, and how much on cases like the sad song where the feeling seems detachable from the evaluation. Give me a number and a one-line justification for each component, then show how they combine into your headline credences. Finally, tell me which single component you are least confident in, and what kind of case or consideration would move it the most — because that's where I suspect my own thinking is weakest, and I'd like to know where to push.
