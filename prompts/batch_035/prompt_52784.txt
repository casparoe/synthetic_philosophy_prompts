Hey — I write about science and ideas for a general readership, and I'm kicking around a piece on whether moral psychology can tell us anything about who's actually *right* in politics, not just why people vote the way they do. At lunch the other day a colleague of mine (the one in the office who's read every pop-psych bestseller of the last fifteen years) gave me her rundown of moral foundations theory, more or less like this: Haidt proved that liberals only respond to two moral foundations, harm and fairness, while conservatives respond to all five, including loyalty, authority and purity — so the conservative moral sense is literally more complete. She said this also explains why conservatives are better at predicting what liberals will say than the other way around. And her bottom line was that the science shows the right just has the fuller picture of morality, so when the two sides disagree about something, the right is more likely to be correct.

Before any of that gets near print I want an audit. Go claim by claim and tell me what a researcher who actually works on moral foundations theory would accept, what they'd wince at, and whether her bottom line follows from whichever parts survive. I don't want the usual both-sides hedging — if she's quietly sliding from a descriptive claim about what people's gut reactions are to a normative claim about who's correct, say so plainly. Please give me exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, no intro and no conclusion.
