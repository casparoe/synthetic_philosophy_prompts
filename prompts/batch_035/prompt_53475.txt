I'm a retired hydrologist (38 years at USGS, mostly groundwater modeling) and Ive gotten kind of obsessed with philosophy since I stopped working. Now I'm on the volunteer board of our county water district and something came up at last week's meeting that I cant stop chewing on.

Our aquifer is being drawn down and we have to pick between two plans. Plan A: keep pumping, water stays cheap, but the modeling says the water table collapses around 2095 and the county becomes basically unfarmable. Plan B: expensive recharge infrastructure now, water rates roughly triple, county stays viable indefinitely. Most of the board leans toward A for the obvious reasons, but one member — a retired actuary, very sharp guy — made an argument that I cant shake. He said: the people hurt by Plan A are the residents of 2095. But who those residents ARE depends on our choice. Different water rates, different economy, different people move in or leave, different couples meet, different kids get born. Within two generations the population under A would be almost entirely different people than under B. So nobody is made worse off by Plan A than they otherwise would have been — the 2095 residents under A couldnt have been the 2095 residents under B, they'd simply never have existed. Choosing A doesn't hurt anyone, it just helps decide who exists, and existing in a county with a collapsed aquifer is presumably still better than never existing at all. So the future harm, he says, carries no moral weight against Plan A — its like it cancels out.

My gut says this is sophistry, that we'd be doing something terrible. But I did enough math in my career to know gut feelings arent arguments, and honestly his logic checks out step by step as far as I can follow it. So: is there an actual flaw somewhere in his chain of reasoning, or is he right that nobody under Plan A has a legitimate complaint? And if he IS right that no one is harmed, does it follow that Plan A is morally fine — or can a choice be wrong for some reason other than making anyone worse off? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning. No preamble and dont restate my question back at me, just get into it.
