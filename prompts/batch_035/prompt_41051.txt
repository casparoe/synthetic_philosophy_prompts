I have no academic background in this; what follows comes from years of thinking about it on my own, mostly while reading history and the news and arguing with a friend who is a sociologist. I have slowly assembled a set of convictions about how social things should be explained, and I would like an honest audit of whether they can all be true at once. I genuinely do not know where the problem is, if there is one, so please do not spare me.

Here are my convictions, as precisely as I can state them:

1. Only individual people really exist. "Society," "the market," "the culture," "the ruling class" are names for patterns in what many people do, not for additional things over and above people. There is no extra entity anywhere with its own mind or its own hands.

2. A proper explanation of anything social must, at least in principle, be traceable through individual decisions — what particular people wanted, believed, feared, and did. If an explanation cannot be cashed out at that level even in outline, I take it to be a placeholder or a metaphor, not an explanation. When someone tells me "the system produced this," I always want to ask: which people, moved by what, doing what?

3. And yet some facts seem to be genuinely about collectives and about no member. A crowd can be in a panic while no one in it panics — each person is just trying not to be crushed. A traffic jam can persist for hours with no accident at its head. A bank run can destroy a bank every depositor individually believes is sound. And things like money and language are real constraints: I cannot by myself pay with something else or mean "cat" by "dog," and this is not merely a fact about my own mind. I do not want to deny these facts, and I do not think I should have to.

4. Some of the explanations I find most illuminating are ones that explain practices by what they do for a group rather than by what anyone intended: that a norm of burying the dead survives in part because groups that abandon it fall apart, or that certain wasteful rituals persist because they screen out halfhearted members. I believe explanations of this shape can be correct. But I also despise exactly this shape of explanation when it is used lazily — "religion exists to keep people docile," "the schools are designed to produce obedient workers" — and I want a principled line between the legitimate use and the incantation, not just a feeling about which ones I happen to like.

5. I believe most of what people want is absorbed rather than chosen. My ambitions, my taste, my sense of what is shameful were stamped on me by my time and place before I was in any position to consent, and this is true of nearly everyone. Who I am was substantially made by my social world.

6. At the same time, I think the right way to explain what a person does is by their reasons — what they wanted and believed — and that this kind of explanation should be treated as complete and legitimate, not as something to be explained away. When I ask why my neighbor changed jobs, "because he wanted more money and believed the new firm was stable" is a real answer, not a confession of ignorance to be dissolved into class position or upbringing.

My request: go through these and tell me which parts are in genuine tension and how badly; which tensions only look real and dissolve once you say things carefully (I suspect at least one does, but I am not sure which); and for each real tension, what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair would cost me elsewhere. Then tell me which single revision, if any, would clean up the most. Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally damning, and if some of what I have said simply restates a well-known position under another name, say so plainly.

One further request about the answer itself: where you can, please distinguish what is broadly settled among people who study this from what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested position as if it were the consensus. If my convictions force me onto contested ground, I would rather know that than be given a tidy verdict that borrows authority it does not have. And where you are uncertain about the best repair, say so and explain what the uncertainty turns on, rather than picking one for the sake of a clean conclusion.
