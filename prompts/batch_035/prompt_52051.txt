I'm on the grantmaking board of a mid-sized charitable fund — think a few hundred million a year. Every cycle we allocate between roughly two kinds of programs: direct present-day stuff (malaria prevention, vitamin supplementation, farm animal welfare audits) and what our research arm calls long-horizon grants, mostly work aimed at reducing catastrophic risks. Since forever we've ranked everything by expected value per dollar, cost-effectiveness estimates, the usuall.

This cycle one of our advisors, a decision theorist, circulated a paper arguing we should scrap the whole framework. His argument in a nutshell: if the universe is infinite — spatially, or because the future never ends — then the total amount of value at stake is infinite, and when you aggregate over infinite domains your sums either diverge or, worse, depend on the order you add things up in (he invokes the thing about conditionally convergent series being rearrangeable into any total you like). So expected-value comparisons between our programs aren't just noisy, they're basically undefined, and any ranking we produce that way is arbitrary. His proposed fix: rank grants only by their effects inside a finite bubble — a bounded chunk of spacetime around us, say the next few hundred years within some distance — and treat everything outside the bubble as off the books entirely.

Half the board thinks this sinks the long-horizon portfolio outright (why fund far-future stuff if it's off the books), the other half thinks the paper is a curiosity with no practical upshot, and the chair wants a decision before the December meeting. Please draft the memo I'll circulate: it should recommend one concrete decision rule for this funding cycle — adopt the bubble rule, keep expected value with some modification, something else entirely — with actual reasoning, and it has to engage the advisor's math rather than waving it off. I also want it to be honest about the strongest point on whichever side loses.

One more thing, important: keep separate what's basically settled among people who actually know this literature from what's genuinely contested. My understanding is it's not really disputed that naive infinite sums are order-dependent, or that some comparisons survive even in infinite worlds (if one outcome is at least as good everywhere, that settles something). But whether truncating, discounting, or something else is the right repair seems much more open. Please flag wherever the recommendation leans on a contested view, so the board doesn't walk out thinking it's the consensus. Casual tone is fine, this is an internal memo not a philosophy journal.
