for my stats study group i have to write a dialogue, and i want it to actually get the philosophy right instead of the usual simpson's paradox hand-waving. here's the setup i've concocted:

a clinical trial of a drug for chronic fatigue. the drug beats placebo on the primary outcome when you pool all patients. but the trial had two follow-up protocols — an intensive one and a standard one — and patients weren't randomized between them. which protocol you got was decided after the first week, partly on how the clinicians thought you were responding. and when you split the data by protocol, placebo wins within both protocols.

one character, Dana, takes the standard simpson's lesson: pooled comparisons across non-comparable groups can invert, the within-strata numbers are the real ones, so the drug actually loses and the aggregate result is just a mixture artifact. she can lean on the classic cases where disaggregating turned out to give the right answer (the kidney stone surgery one, the berkeley admissions one).

the second character, Marcus, isn't just a contrarian — his point is structural. in those classic cases the stratifying variable (stone size, department) existed before treatment and was a genuine confounder, which is exactly why disaggregating fixed things. here the protocol assignment happened after treatment started and was partly based on patient response, so the strata are downstream of the treatment. conditioning on them may be scrambling real information, and he thinks the pooled comparison isn't obviously the wrong one in this case.

the dialogue should make both of them look sharp. Dana should get Marcus to admit that the pooled estimate also compares groups that differ systematically and can't just be read causally either. Marcus should get Dana to admit her rule was derived from cases where the third variable sat upstream of treatment, and that she'd be nervous applying it blind to a stratification variable that's downstream. they should end without a cheap resolution — maybe landing on what evidence would settle it (re-randomizing protocol assignment? stratifying only on pre-treatment variables?) — but they have to actually argue their way to that landing, not just agree to disagree.

aim for 600ish words of dialogue. they're colleagues who are a little exhasperated with each other, and they talk like humans, not textbook narrators. also please dont let either of them get a monologue longer than ~6 lines.
