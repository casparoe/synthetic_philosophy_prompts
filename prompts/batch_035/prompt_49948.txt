I'm in a philosophy of history seminar this semester (it's a weird mix of historiography people and metaethics people and we mostly argue past each other) and I have to write a term paper on presentism -- the question of whether historians can escape the standards of their own time. My professor keeps saying the literature is a bunch of seperate debates that all wear the same word, and she's pretty right honestly. So before I commit, I'm trying to figure out which of the open questions in this area are actually worth working on vs. which are just terminological turf wars.

The candidates we've been circling in class:

(a) the argument that even choosing what counts as significant to study smuggles in present values, so the real question was never whether historians are presentist but which kind -- is that argument right, and does it prove too much? By the same logic every discipline would be "presentist" in some sense, and then the word stops doing anything.

(b) whether judging the actor and judging the practice come apart. Condemning slavery from here is easy, but what about the person raised inside the institution -- and can that distinction between "person was bad" and "world was bad" be made principled at all, or is it always ad hoc?

(c) whether there's any coherent middle position between Butterfield-style detachment and just applying today's morality wholesale. People gesture at standards that were "available at the time" (like abolitionist voices in the period), but doesn't that just let you cherry-pick the most sympathetic contemporaries and call their view the era's?

(d) whether the presentism charge as it shows up in public fights -- statues, school curricula, commemorations -- is doing any philosophical work at all, or whether it's just a rhetorical move to shut down moral judgment that everyone would happily drop when the judgment is one they like.

So: rank these for me (or swap one out if you think there's a bigger question I'm missing), given that I've got ~15 pages and one semester, not a research career. What I want defended is the ordering itself -- which questions are genuinely unresolved rather than just under-argued, which one the others quietly depend on, and which one a philosophy student with basically no history background beyond survey courses could say something new about instead of restating the standard positions for the nth time. And tell me which one is most likely a dead end, and why.
