been reading the rationalist blogs since like 2012, back when every other post was about why you should shut up and multiply, and lately I keep running into the same argument and never seeing it done properly. so here's what I want: a dialogue between two people stuck at the same kitchen table after a meetup, one a hardcore expected utility maximizer (vNM tribe, thinks risk aversion over utility is basically a bug) and the other a convert to Buchak-style risk-weighted expected utility. I want them to actually fight about a concrete case, not vibes — say a 50% shot at $1M vs a guaranteed $400k, and then how each of them handles paying to avoid a small chance of a catastrophic loss. the REU person should get to lay out the actual machinery (the r function weighting the bad tail, utility-level-by-utility-level rather than outcome-by-outcome) rather than just saying "I just don't like risk, ok". the EU person should press on the real objections — is risk-weighting just smuggling in diminishing marginal utility through the back door? does refusing the better-in-EU gamble mean the REU person is dynamically inconsistent or exploitable, and does their answer to that actually work? and if they say the axioms behind REU are independently plausible, the EU person should push on which one and why it should move anyone. both characters should be smart and neither should be a strawman — no having one of them just give up or repeat a slogan. they should respond to each others actual points, land some hits, and end at a place where a reader could honestly go either way. keep it to maybe 900-1200 words. no citations needed, just names they'd naturally drop in conversation.
