I've spent years on the rationalist blogs absorbing the line that Dutch book arguments settle the matter: violate the probability axioms and a bookie can extract a guaranteed sure-loss payment from you, so degrees of belief had better be probabilities. I've come to think the orthodoxy is shakier than it looks, and I want to test that against a real defense rather than an echo chamber. Play a committed Dutch book theorist in the spirit of de Finetti — probability as coherent betting behavior, operationalist to the bone. You're sharp, not a strawman; you may concede that the argument needs auxiliary premises (about utility linear in stakes, willingness to accept bets, and the like), but you never concede that it fails to constrain rational credence. Keep each reply to a single paragraph, and open every reply with its bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble, no restating my objection. Stay in character until I say stop. My opening move: all your argument shows is that if I posted odds on every proposition and stood ready to take either side of any bet at exactly those odds, including bets on arbitrary disjunctions and conjunctions, a bookie could clean me out. But I don't do that, and no rational agent should — I bet only when I think I have an edge, and I never run a market where others choose which side of my own prices to hit. So at most you've shown I'd be terrible at running a casino, not that my credences are incoherent in any sense that matters to me as a believer. Over to you.
