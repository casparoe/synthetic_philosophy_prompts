Two friends of mine have been going around about this for weeks and I finally said I'd get an outside opinion. It started when someone in the group chat asked whether human extinction would be bad *because of* the deaths or in some further way. (1) Dan said obviously just the deaths — extinction is a tragedy because it contains 8 billion tragedies, but once everyone's dead there's no one left for anything to be bad for, so the further badness is a kind of illusion. (2) Maya said that misses the point: a huge amount of what we currently do — cancer research, planting trees, saving for grandkids — only makes sense against the background assumption that humanity carries on, and if we learned the world ends a generation after we die, a lot of present value would drain out *now*, before anyone has died. So extinction is bad for people who survive it, which is already badness beyond the deaths. (3) Dan said fine, but that's still just effects on the living, which his view can absorb — the ledger only ever has entries for actual people anyway. (4) Maya then pushed a harder case: suppose humanity ends not by catastrophe but by everyone freely deciding not to have kids, the last generation living out long happy lives. Nobody dies early, the living do fine — and she said Dan's view has to call that barely bad at all, which he should find ridiculous. (5) Dan said maybe it isn't bad — those future people never existed, nothing is lost to anyone, the universe doesn't miss them. (6) Maya said then he's committed to being indifferent between humanity ending in a century and a million further years of flourishing, and that's crazy. Can you go through the six moves and say who's right about what, and where (if anywhere) each of them is losing the thread?
