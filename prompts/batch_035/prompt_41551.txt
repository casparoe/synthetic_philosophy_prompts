She tried to sketch the main claims for me: that archives actively produce silences rather than just suffering them, and that the decisive moment is when the record is made, not when it's later lost; that what a collection holds reflects, and then reinforces, who had power back when the collecting was done; and that the people most likely to be missing from the records are precisely the ones least able to complain about being missing. Translate those claims into my vocabulary — schema design, event definitions, the difference between something that gets no log line, something that's an implicit NULL, and something that never gets a column at all, sampling rates, retention policies, curation of training data, survivorship bias in downstream dashboards. Do it as faithfully as you can, and please don't smuggle the archival theorists' assumptions in as if they were part of the translation.

Which of their claims map onto something I already have a word for — the never-logged column obviously smells like structural missingness, but tell me whether that's a real mapping or just a vibe? Which claims only translate with distortion? And is there anything in their position that has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all — and if so, is that because my vocabulary is missing something, or because their claim is vaguer than it looks? There are practical stakes here: this quarter I have to write down which events we log at 100%, which we sample at 1%, and which we don't record at all, and I'd like to know whether the archival literature says anything that be careful about downstream bias doesn't already cover.
