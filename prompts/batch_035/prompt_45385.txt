I do computer vision research for a living — mostly detection and segmentation, and lately multimodal models where a text encoder feeds into the visual backbone through cross-attention — so when a philosophy friend of mine started going on about "cognitive penetration of perception" and how it supposedly matters for whether perception can justify belief, my honest reaction was that this sounds like a convoluted way of asking whether the visual system is a purely feedforward stack or has top-down connections. In my world that's just an architecture question, and a pretty boring one at this point: everything is connected to everything, gradients and activations flow both ways, and nobody thinks you can cleanly seperate "perception" from whatever uses its outputs. But my friend insists I'm missing the point, so I want a real translation.

Here's the ask: restate the cognitive penetration debate in the vocabulary I actually use — feedforward vs. recurrent pathways, learned priors, conditioning and prompting, attention as query-driven selection, end-to-end training vs. modular pipelines, in-distribution generalization, shortcut learning — as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report. Which parts of the philosophers' disagreement map cleanly onto architecture and training questions, which parts you had to distort to make them fit, and which parts have no expression in my vocabulary at all. Some specifics I'd want handled: there's apparently a screening-off condition, where cognition only "penetrates" if it influences experience without being a backflow of information that itself came through the senses — does that map onto anything real for me, like the difference between a prior baked in during training versus a text prompt supplied at inference time, or is that a fudge? There's also the epistemic downgrade idea (I think this is Siegel's) that experiences shaped by your desires or expectations give you less justification for the beliefs they support — my models literally condition on prompts, so if a caption biases a segmentation mask, is that penetration, and is there any coherent sense in which it makes the mask "less justified"? And the old Fodor modularity stuff about informational encapsulation — I want to map that onto a frozen encoder with a fixed output vocabulary, but tell me if that's honest or if I'm smuggling in my assumption that modularity is about the graph structure rather than about information flow under the hood.

The part I actually care about is the residue. Once you grant top-down connections exist and that beliefs can drive them — which no one who's looked at cortex or at a modern architecture would deny — is there a genuine disagreement left over, or does the whole debate live somewhere my vocabulary can't reach? If it does live there, I want your judgement on which side is at fault: is my vocabulary just missing the normative notions (justification, rationality, what a perceiver is entitled to conclude), or were the philosophers' distinctions blurry in a way that becomes obvious the moment you try to operationalize them? My friend bets it's the first, I half suspect it's the second, and neither of us has the background to say definately.
