I want a thought experiment constructed to probe a suspicion of mine about supererogation. On the classical analysis, an act is supererogatory when it is neither obligatory nor forbidden, is good, and merits special praise for going beyond duty. But I suspect that what actually drives our "beyond the call" judgments is sacrifice — net cost to the agent — rather than any deontic boundary, and I want a case built to isolate that variable. Construct a pair of scenarios in which two agents perform the same kind of act (helping someone in genuine need), with identical outcomes, identical effort, skill, and risk, and no difference in special obligation to the beneficiary; the only difference is that for one agent the act is a real sacrifice — she would rather be doing anything else — while for the other it is something she loves doing and would happily pay to do.
