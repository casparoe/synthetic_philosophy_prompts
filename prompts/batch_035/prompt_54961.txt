Since my daughter was born last spring I keep catching myself doing the doomsday argument in my head (I'm apparently around the 100-billionth human, so, uh, get ready for the end), and after a few months of reading around it I've noticed the debate basically froze in the early 2000s. You've got the side that treats your birth rank as ordinary evidence about humanity's total lifespan, the side that says conditioning on your own existence changes everything, and instead of anyone converting, they just pick SSA or SIA and stay there forever. My working theory is that this isn't really a fight about probability at all — it's about whether "I find myself at rank n" is the kind of thing that can be evidence in the first place, whether being an observer is like drawing a ball from an urn or whether existence just isn't a sampling process you can conditionalize on. Is that right? Or is there a hidden formal question doing the actual work — reference classes, self-locating probability, whatever — and the philosophical framing is a distraction? I'd like a diagnosis with reasons, not a tour of the arguments, and an honest answer about whether anything could move this, since the Presumptuous Philosopher stuff seems to have just made everyone dig in deeper. Also happy to hear that the whole question is ill-posed, if it is, but you'd have to say why in a way that explains why such smart poeple have been stuck on it for so long.
