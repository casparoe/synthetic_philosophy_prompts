Quick context: I'm a science-and-ideas journalist, and I'm working on a piece about triage — who got the last ICU bed during covid, who gets the last dose of antiviral, that kind of thing. My editor keeps steering me back to Taurek's old "should the numbers count?" setup: you can save five strangers or one stranger, you know nothing about any of them, no one involved knows anyone. Taurek's line was roughly that each of the six could reasonably ask "why him and not me?", that flipping a coin between the one and the five is the only way to give everyone an equal chance, and that it's far from obvious you should just save the five. Alot of people in bioethics still argue about whether he was even allowed to say that.

Act as a professional philosopher working at the intersection of ethics and decision theory, and think carefully before answering — I'd like you to actually brainstorm first: just list out, in a couple of sentences, what the moving parts of this question really are. Then give me your credence, as an actual number, that morality *requires* saving the five in the simple case (not merely permits it). And decompose it for me, since the number alone tells me nothing — something like: probability that each person has an equal claim to rescue, probability that equal claims aggregate so one averted death counts on each side of the ledger no matter whose it is, probability that a lottery rather than a threshold rule is the right way to handle ties in claims, probability that "separateness of persons" or fairness considerations do real work blocking the addition — plus any other terms you think belong, each with a rough probability and one line of justification, and then show how they combine into your final number.

Two more things. Tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or thought experiment would move that term the most. And then tell me whether your number survives scaling: five million strangers versus five. My own gut says yes for the big numbers and gets wobbly around five-versus-one, and I want to know if that's a real feature of the view or just incoherence on my part — because if I put it in the piece, someone's going to check.
