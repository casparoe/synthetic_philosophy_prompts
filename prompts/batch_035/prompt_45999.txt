i teach a comparative law seminar and want a decision tree that sorts students on where they actually stand about criminalizing hate speech, rather than which side of the atlantic they happen to live on. build it from the logical space, not from a list of country names: each node should be a concrete question or case a student can answer without knowing which jurisdiction it points toward. some nodes i'd want in there: whether a cross burned on a family's lawn is "speech" or intimidation-by-conduct; whether a march chanting for a minority's expulsion inflicts a *harm* or just offense; whether a dignity-based harm — living as a second-class citizen in your own town, the sort of thing theorists who defend the european model lean on — is the kind of harm that justifies coercion; whether "counterspeech is the remedy" holds up when the targets can't realistically answer back; and whether trusting the government to draw the line is itself the bigger danger. leaves should be named positions: viewpoint-neutral first amendment near-absolutism, content-but-not-viewpoint regulation, the dignity/european model, and whatever else falls out of the space, with unoccupied combinations noted and diagnosed (is nobody there because the combo is incoherent or just unattractive?). also flag which pairs of leaves the tree can't actually seperate — e.g. whether mill-style counterspeech optimists and pure state-distrust libertarians get lumped together. around 8-10 nodes, phrased so a student answers with their gut, not with a label. typos in the cases are fine since i'll edit, just make the branching logic clean.
