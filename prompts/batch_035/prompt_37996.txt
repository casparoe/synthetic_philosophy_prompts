My friend has a running hobby-horse about whether it's ever fair to call a long-run prediction "right but early". Her verdicts so far: Malthus she calls wrong-but-respectable -- the geometric/arithmetic logic was sound, the conditions just didn't hold, so he wasn't a crank, he was wrong. Ehrlich's Population Bomb she grades flat-out wrong, no charity: the mass famine deaths were supposed to hit in the 1970s, they didn't, and the mistake was treating a snapshot of yields and fertility as a law of nature. Hubbert's original US peak-oil call she counts as right -- production peaked on schedule, and she won't let later fracking count against it, because "he was predicting about geology, not about what markets would then do with the geology." Keynes's line about his grandchildren working 15-hour weeks she splits: right about the wealth, wrong about the hours. And the Club of Rome's Limits to Growth she refuses to grade at all -- conditional scenarios, she says, so right/wrong is a category error, and she's just as annoyed at people who call it vindicated.

Her colleague is now going around claiming that fertility collapse means world population will be well under two billion by 2150 and the central economic problem of the century after next is managing contraction. Predict her verdict. What I actually want is the rule she's running. As far as I can tell she does something like: rescue a predictor's reputation when the mechanism was real but the system adapted around it, while grading the prediction itself wrong whenever the stated window passes without the event. Except she also seems to just side with whoever treated the mechanism as real and the adaptation as the story -- which is how Hubbert gets to be "right." Those readings fit her past verdicts about equally, and I can't tell wether they come apart on this one. The demographic case looks Ehrlich-ish (extrapolating current fertility as if it were a law) but also Hubbert-ish (the next few decades of births are already locked in by people who exist). So: what would each reading predict she says, which is the better bet and why, and what further case would most cleanly split the readings apart?
