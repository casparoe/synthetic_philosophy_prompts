I help run a small decision theory reading group (six people, we meet over video, mostly lapsed grad students). Next session is on the Death in Damascus family of cases — the Gibbard and Harper one where you run into Death in Damascus and he mentions he's expecting you tomorrow in Aleppo (or however the retelling goes, you know the one), and it dawns on you that whereever you decide to go, that very decision is evidence Death will be there, so fleeing seems pointless. I want to open the session with a flowchart that sorts everyone into the position they actually hold based on gut reactions to variants, before anyone gets to say a theory's name out loud.

What I want exactly:

- The flowchart should start from the base case and then perturb it one knob at a time. Knobs I care about: whether the omen/seer is infallible or just right, say, 9 times out of 10; whether you're asked before or after you've half-made up your mind to stay put (does having tentatively decided change what you wish you'd done?); whether Death's appearance is a prediction of you specifically or just a fixed fact about tomorrow that has nothing to do with your psychology; and whether fleeing costs you something small, like most of your money.

- Every branch node should be a question a normal person can answer in five seconds without knowing which position it feeds into. Absolutely no "are you a causal decision theorist" type questions — I want to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like the sound of.

- Leaves I want on there: something in the causal decision theory neighborhood, something evidential, something updateless/FDT-flavored, the view that the choice is genuinely unstable (the one that motivated the ratifiability machinery Gibbard and Harper themselves built for cases like this), and plain old fatalism (whatever you do it makes no difference, so nothing is asked of you). If some path through your tree lands nowhere — like a person who stays put in the infallible version but flees in the 9-out-of-10 version — give that leaf an "unnamed" tag and one sentence on why you think nobody lives there (incoherent? just unattractive? maybe nobody's bothered to explore it?).

- Explicitly flag any pair of leaves your tree can't separate. My own suspicion is that on the base case as I've described it, the evidential, updateless, and fatalist leaves all recommend roughly the same thing (don't bother packing), so tell me whether that's right, and if so what extra variant — if any — would pull them apart, or whether they're genuinely just the same view in this particular corner of the garden.

No citations or reading lists needed btw, this is just for our own session. Keep the tone a bit light, one of our members falls asleep when things get too formal lol.
