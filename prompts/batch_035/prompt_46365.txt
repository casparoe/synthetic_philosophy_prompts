You're a professional philosopher specializing in formal epistemology, and I'd like you to run a consistency audit on my current views. Please think carefully -- maybe start with a quick brainstorm of where the tensions could be before ranking anything -- and write your answer in British English.

A bit of background: I'm a retired physicist (solid state, thirty-some years at a national lab) who only got into philosophy properly after retiring, so go easy on the jargon. My reading group has been doing philosophy of science, and we just worked through a 1984 paper by Bas van Fraassen -- the constructive empiricism fellow -- where he defends a principle he calls Reflection. The way I'd put it in my own words: if I know for certain what degree of belief I'll have in some claim at some later time, then my degree of belief in it now should already match. When I first read this it struck me as obviously right -- it's just trusting your own future inquiry, like how I can plan an experiment around trusting tomorrow's me to read the instruments properly.

So here's roughly where I've landed, and I'd like to know whether it hangs together: (1) I endorse Reflection as I stated it above. (2) I've always held, and drilled into every graduate student I ever supervised, that you believe things on the evidence you have now -- you don't write a conclusion in your notebook because of what you expect to figure out next week. Credences move for reasons, and if theres no reason on the table today, the number stays put. (3) Here's a case I feel strongly about: suppose I took a pill that I knew for certain would make me dead convinced of something unlikely -- say, that I'll win our club chess tournament, which on current form I have maybe a 2% shot at. I know that an hour after swallowing it I'll be at 99%. Reflection seems to say I should already be at 99% *before* swallowing, and I think that's just wrong -- that future certainty is definately noise, not information. (4) I don't accept the obvious patch of saying Reflection applies "except when future me is being irrational." I can't see any non-circular way of sorting my future beliefs in advance into the trustworthy ones and the corrupted ones -- deciding which future beliefs are the rational ones would just be doing the whole inquiry now, which is exactly the work Reflection was supposed to let me defer. (5) Finally, I routinely overturn my past self's judgments and think nothing of it, even though my past self, if he'd applied the same principle, would have expected his views to evolve more or less as they did. Somehow that doesn't strike me as inconsistent in the same way, but I can't put my finger on why, so maybe you can.

Which of these commitments are in real tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest repair? Rank the tensions by severity rather than giving me a flat list, and for each one say what would have to give and roughly what it costs.
