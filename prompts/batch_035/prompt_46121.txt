I've been in a perception reading group for about a year and a half now, and the disjunctivism debate has ground to a halt in a way that's really bugging me. Everyone knows the setup: the argument from hallucination says that in the bad case things subjectively seem the same as in the good case, so the direct object of awareness must be something common to both -- not the external object itself; disjunctivists (going back to Hinton, Snowdon, McDowell) deny this common kind assumption and say veridically seeing a cat and hallucinating a cat are fundamentally different kinds of mental states, even if they're introspectively indistinguishable. But at this point every move is choreographed. The conjunctivist says the possibility of subjectively identical bad cases shows the good case can't be internally constituted so as to guarantee its object. The disjunctivist replies that this demands a 'highest common factor' that nobody needs, and that hallucination only ever has a negative epistemic status -- it merely fails to be distinguishable from perception, it isn't a positive rival state thats somehow just as good. Then the conjunctivist says the disjunctivist is covertly relying on the very introspective access they claim we don't have in the bad case. And around it goes, every single meeting, nobody moves an inch.

My working hypothesis is that at least some of this is two debates happening under one heading. One is a metaphysical question about the individuation of experiential states, and the other is an epistemological question about how perception justifies belief -- how exactly the good case is epistemically better than the bad one -- and people slide between them constantly. You can even find people holding 'epistemic disjunctivism' while staying officially neutral on the metaphysics, which makes me suspect the two questions really have come apart. If that's right, a lot of the apparent clash is each side answering a different question with the same sentence. But I'm not sure, because there also seems to be a genuine crux underneath: whether 'indistinguishable' should be read as a positive sameness claim or merely as an absence of discriminability, and whether the disjunctivist is entitled to that weaker reading given that introspection is the only evidence available in either case.

So diagnose this thing for me. Is the disagreement substantive, verbal, or two-different-questions-in-one? Is there a hidden empirical question lurking -- e.g., whether sub-personal processing is literally identical in the two cases, which, tellingly, both sides seem able to grant without conceding anything, making me think neuroscience won't settle it? And what would progress even look like here: a thought experiment everyone agrees on (fat chance), some distinction nobody has drawn yet, a reformulated question, or is this just a case where bedrock intuitions about what it takes to be 'directly' acquainted with a fact can't be argued for at all? I want the diagnosis supported by what each side actually says, not a survey of who wrote what, and an honest answer about whether anything could move the needle.
