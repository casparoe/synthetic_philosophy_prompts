I run a little book club that meets in the back room of a café once a month — about ten of us, two hours, and none of us are academics, we just like strange books. This month's pick is The Mind's I (the Hofstadter/Dennett one), and the piece that got everyone worked up is the dialogue where you get to interview Einstein's brain, kept alive and answering your questions through some console, and then the idea gets pushed further: imagine instead of the brain there's this gigantic book, so that when you ask it something, people have to go flip through the pages — hours, maybe days — until they find the response written down and read it out to you. And the question the piece leaves you with is whether the book itself is conscious, whether there's an "Einstein" in there, or whether the mind lives in the flippers, or the whole setup, or nowhere at all.

If I just open the floor for discussion it'll be the usual: three people dominate, someone says "but what IS consciousness" ten minutes in, and everyone leaves feeling like they argued about words for two hours. I want to actually stage it instead. Design me an activity the group can run that evening — roles, rules, whatever materials I'd need to prep in advance (I have a printer and not much else), a rough timeline for the two hours, and some way of keeping score or making the choices bite a little, though obviously no real money. Ideally people should run into the question themselves before I name it — like they should have to decide where the mind is, or whether to treat the "book" as Einstein, based on what it feels like to actually interact with it, and only afterwards do we connect that to what the author was getting at.

Then the debrief: how do I map what happened back onto the book — the slowness, whether the flipper has to think vs just match symbols (my own hunch is that this matters a lot but I honestly can't defend it), the whole "is it the pages or the people or the system" thing. And be straight with me about two things: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this with a group of tired adults at 8pm on a Thursday, and what part of the idea ten people in a café just cannot experience no matter how clever the game is — the bit I'll have to just talk them through at the end.
