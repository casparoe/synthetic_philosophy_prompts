I've never studied philosophy — my training is in dental hygiene — but for the past two years my friend Elena and I have been arguing about abortion, and somewhere along the way we started making up scenarios instead of arguing about the real thing, because the made-up ones strip off the politics. Elena has given verdicts on a bunch of them, and I've noticed I can't predict the next verdict from the earlier ones, or at least I can predict it two different ways and can't tell which she's running with. I'd like your help.

Her verdicts so far. One: you wake up in a hospital bed surgically attached to a stranger whose organs are failing; if you unplug, he dies; you must stay attached for nine months, after which he recovers fully. Elena says you may unplug, full stop. Two: in a region where drifting seed-pods occasionally blow through window screens, take root in a person's body, and then need that body for nine months or die, you keep your screen in good repair, know the risk is small but real, and one takes root anyway. Elena says you may remove it. Three: you volunteer, deliberately and knowingly, to be surgically attached to a dying stranger for nine months — you wanted to do something noble — and a month in you change your mind. Elena says you must stay attached, health permitting. Four: your own young child, whom you chose to have, will die unless you give up your evenings and a large part of your income for nine months — no bodily intrusion at all, just sacrifice. Elena says you must do it. Five, back in the real world: she thinks a first-trimester abortion after failed contraception is clearly permissible. Six, also real: she thinks aborting a healthy pregnancy at eight months, for ordinary convenience reasons, is wrong — not just distasteful, wrong.

The new scenarios we can't resolve. In the first, the seed-pod case returns, except there was a storm warning that day, you left the window wide open anyway figuring it wouldn't be you, and a pod took root. In the second, the pod doesn't grow into another dying stranger; it grows into a person gradually, conscious only in the final few weeks, and you want it removed in month seven — your precautions, though, were as careful as in the original case.

What I want from you: predict Elena's verdict on both new cases, and give me your reasoning. It seems to me at least two different rules fit everything she's said so far — one that turns on whether you deliberately took the burden on versus merely risked it, and one that turns on how careless you were about creating the dependency, with maybe a third that keys on what the attached thing is, or is about to become, rather than on anything about you. Do those fit her record as well as I think? Which predicts what on the new cases? Give me your best guess with your reasons, and — because I suspect we'll be arguing about this for another two years — tell me what further scenario, or what question I could put to her directly, would best reveal which rule is actually hers.
