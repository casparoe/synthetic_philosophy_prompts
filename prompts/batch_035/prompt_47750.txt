I'm a software engineer, and I've gotten drawn into an argument about supertasks that started, embarrassingly, from a while loop. A colleague and I were debugging a program that toggles a flag on each iteration of a loop that never terminates, and he asked half-jokingly: if the loop ran infinitely fast, what would the flag be afterward? That led us to Thomson's lamp — a lamp that is switched on at one minute to midnight, off at half a minute to midnight, on at a quarter minute to midnight, and so on, with the switching completed by midnight. What is the lamp's state at midnight? My colleague says the thought experiment is just incoherent, on a par with asking about the value of a non-convergent sequence — the state history has no limit, so there is no fact of the matter, and anyone claiming the lamp must be either on or off is presupposing that the state function is continuous at midnight when nothing in the setup guarantees that. I'm inclined to say something slightly different: the setup is consistent, it merely underdetermines the answer, which suggests the question is like asking what color the lamp is — true or false answers both extend the description without contradicting it.

Here is what I want from you: a credence, as an actual number, that the proposition "the lamp is on at midnight" has a determinate truth value in the scenario as described — that is, that the scenario as standardly stated fixes a fact of the matter, as opposed to being contradictory or merely leaving the question open. Then decompose that number into the sub-questions it rests on: whether the supertask is metaphysically coherent enough to reason about at all (as opposed to physically impossible, which I already grant); whether "underdetermined by the description" and "no fact of the matter" are actually different positions or the same one dressed up differently; whether the state function's lack of a limit settles anything or is an artifact of modeling the lamp's state as a pointwise-defined function; and whether the obvious extension to infinity the sequence of switchings makes it on or off is admissible or just one arbitrary extension among many, like adding a value at a removable discontinuity. Assign a rough probability to each term, give one line of justification per term, and show how they combine to your final number.

Finally, since I want to know how much weight to put on your answer: tell me how confident you are overall, single out the sub-question you are least sure about, and say what argument or consideration would move your final number the most — for example, whether a physically realizable variant (a dimmer ramp with a limiting brightness, or a switch whose positions converge) would change your answer, and if so why the discrete toggle case should be treated differently. I care less about matching any particular philosopher's position than about seeing where the reasoning is load-bearing and where it's hand-waving, so please be explicit about that rather than presenting the decomposition as more solid than it is.
