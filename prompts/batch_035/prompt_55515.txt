I am on the governing board of a small research institute, and we have a recurring structural fight about how the board should make decisions. Our procedure de facto works like this: a proposal (say, whether to fund a project) is accepted only if two premise propositions are each accepted — for instance, P1: the methodology is sound, and P2: the project addresses a question that matters; conclusion C: fund it. Each board member votes on P1, P2, and C, and each member is individually coherent: she votes for C if and only if she votes for both P1 and P2. Yet the pattern that keeps arising is that a majority accepts P1, a (possibly different) majority accepts P2, and a majority rejects C — so the board's "view" across the three votes is collectively incoherent, and different board members accuse the procedure of being either unfair or irrational depending on which outcome they wanted.

Before our next governance meeting, I want a specification, not a solution. Please write the desiderata that any acceptable rule for aggregating the board's judgments over an interlocking set of propositions must meet. At minimum, work through conditions of the following kinds, though I want you to add, split, or reformulate them where you think the standard formulations are crude: (a) conditions on the output — that the collective judgment be complete and logically coherent on the whole agenda, not just on each issue taken separately; (b) conditions on the input — what distributions of individual votes the rule must be prepared to handle, including whether the rule may presuppose anything about how members' votes on premises hang together with their votes on conclusions; (c) conditions on the rule's shape — whether it must treat every proposition on the agenda by the same principle, whether the collective verdict on one proposition may depend on how individuals voted on other propositions, whether votes must count equally regardless of who casts them, and whether adding a vote for a proposition can ever hurt it.

For each desideratum, state the consideration that motivates it and be explicit about whether it is genuinely non-negotiable for any acceptable rule or is instead the prejudice of a particular conception of what the board is for — I have one faction that sees the board as an epistemic instrument for tracking the truth about P1 and P2, and another that sees it as a fair procedure for registering already-formed views, and I suspect they will rank your desiderata differently. Then do the part I most want: identify which subsets of your desiderata are jointly unsatisfiable, prove or at least exhibit the impossibility in the concrete three-member case sketched above, and say which single condition, if relaxed, opens up the most space for workable rules — and what the price of relaxing it is, both for the epistemic faction and the fairness faction. Finally, in a short closing section, assess briefly how three candidate rules fare against your list: voting independently by majority on every proposition, deriving the conclusion from majority votes on the premises only, and deriving the premises from the majority vote on the conclusion — but keep that section brief; the desiderata and the impossibility are the substance of what I need.
