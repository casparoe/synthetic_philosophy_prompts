So for a couple years now I've been chewing on the free will question on my own, no philosophy background really, I just think about it on my commute and I've read enough wikipedia to know the basic names (compatibilism, hard determinism, all that). What got me started was reading about this wasp, the sphex wasp, that drags a paralyzed cricket to its burrow, goes in alone to check the nest is ok, and if you move the cricket a few inches while she's inside, she comes out, finds it, drags it back to the exact same spot at the threshold, and goes in to check the nest AGAIN. You can do this to her ten times and she checks ten times, like she's stuck in a loop and physically cannot notice the loop. Apparently people call this being sphexish, and I've kind of adopted that as my whole mental model: the wasp isn't free because when her routine stops working she can't tell, and I'm free (or at least freer) because when my routine stops working I can notice and do something different. Flexibility is the whole thing for me.

Anyway I tried writing down everything I actually beleive and I want you to audit it. Here's the list:

1. The universe is basically deterministic, whatever happens follows from what came before plus the laws, and there's no ghost in the machine steering me around.
2. When I turned down one job offer and took another a few years back, I genuinely could have taken the other one. Not "it feels like I could," I actually could have. That's what choosing means.
3. Freedom is flexibility: an agent is free to the extent it can notice its usual move is failing and switch to a different move.
4. Me and the wasp differ in degree, not kind. Same sort of machinery, mine just has more layers of noticing-when-it's-not-working stacked on top. There's no magic line where freedom switches on.
5. But my GPS reroutes the instant I miss a turn and it is obviously, completely, zero percent free. A chess engine that adapts to anything I do is the same, its just doing math. No amount of flexibility makes a machine free.
6. Praise and blame only make sense for someone who could have done otherwise. That's why we don't put the wasp on trial.
7. My brother is an alcoholic and over the years I've come around to thinking his worst behavior genuinely wasn't up to him, the flexibility machinery itself is what's broken, that's what addiction is. But I still can't drop the resentment, and I think the resentment is tracking something real about him, it's not just a habit of mine that hasn't caught up with my opinions.
8. Also, and this one confuses me, if it turned out quantum randomness jiggles my neurons I don't think that would make me freer either. Random twitching isn't choosing.

Does this hang together? Go through the pairs, tell me which ones are actually in tension and which only look like they are (I suspect some of these just feel like they clash because of an ambiguity in "could have" but I can never keep it straight). For each real tension rank it by how bad it is and tell me what would have to give and what the fix costs me. Then tell me if there's one single revision that would clean up the most. Don't give me a lecture on the standard positions, I've heard those, I want to know what MY list commits me to. And be honest if 7 is just me refusing to feel bad about hating my brother, I can take it.
