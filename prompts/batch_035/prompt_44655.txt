At a workshop in the spring I've been asked to contribute a short position statement on the axiom of choice and the theory of the continuum, and before drafting it I want an outside check on whether what I actually believe adds up. Here is the package.

(1) I accept the axiom of choice without reservation, on indispensability grounds: mathematics as practiced needs it — every vector space has a basis, every proper ideal extends to a maximal one, Tychonoff's theorem in topology — and I take that to settle the matter.

(2) I regard Banach–Tarski as simply a theorem. It surprised people, but so did space-filling curves, and nobody revised analysis over those; I see no reason the duplication result should count against choice.

(3) I think the continuum hypothesis has a determinate truth value. Its independence from ZFC shows only that ZFC doesn't settle everything, not that there is no fact of the matter; I am not a formalist about set theory, and I am not a formalist about CH in particular.

(4) On the theory of the continuum, I side with the descriptive set theorists. The large-cardinal results that give projective sets their regularity properties — Lebesgue measurability, the Baire property, the perfect set property — and projective determinacy feel to me like the truth about sets of reals, in a way that the wild choice-generated sets, a Vitali set or the pieces in Banach–Tarski, do not. My gloss: the right theory of the continuum is one on which its sets are as regular as consistency permits.

(5) On justification: I hold that axioms are warranted only by their mathematical consequences — fruitfulness for existing practice, unification, unexpected explanatory connections — and I am suspicious of any appeal to intuition about the set-theoretic universe as carrying justificatory weight.

Two loose credences, held lightly: I would bet the continuum has size ℵ₂, on forcing-axiom grounds; and while I accept full choice in the universe, the arrangement whereby determinacy holds in L(ℝ) while choice holds outright strikes me as how things are in fact arranged.

What I'd like from you: tell me which of these commitments genuinely pull against each other and which conflicts are only apparent, ranking the real ones by severity rather than presenting a flat list; for each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere in the package; for each apparent one, show how it dissolves. Close by telling me which single revision would clean up the most. I don't know in advance where the problems are, so don't soften the audit on my behalf — but don't manufacture conflicts either: if a pair only looks unstable, I want to know that, and why.
