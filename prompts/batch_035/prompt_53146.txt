I hold that verisimilitude is a real, objective relation: some false theories genuinely are closer to the truth than others — Newtonian mechanics really is closer than Ptolemaic astronomy — and the aim of science can be characterized as increasing this closeness, not merely accumulating unrefuted conjectures. I also believe a formal account of the notion is achievable. The Tichý–Miller results of the 1970s showed that Popper's own definition, in terms of a theory's truth-content and falsity-content, collapses: it can never rank one false theory as closer to the truth than another. But I read the decades of successor proposals since — content-based orderings, similarity measures over possible worlds, closeness of a theory's models to the true model — as evidence that the concept can be salvaged. Tell me what would count against my view. I'm particularly interested in the following candidate defeaters: the language-dependence results, on which redescribing the same two theories in different vocabularies can reverse which one comes out closer to the truth; the fact that rival measures, each individually plausible, disagree on ordinary cases, such as a theory that conjoins a rival with an unrelated true claim plus some extra falsehoods; the charge of circularity, that judging closeness to a truth we don't have presupposes exactly the access we lack; and the pragmatist fallback that predictive accuracy and problem-solving are all the notion of progress that science actually needs, making verisimilitude idle. Rank the objections from fatal to merely awkward, distinguish what a defender of verisimilitude would concede as counting against the view from what they would resist, say whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if so, whether that is a defect in a position of this kind or a reasonable demand for revisability — and note what the view commits us to that its rivals do not. The circularity objection is the one I would personally find hardest to shake, so treat it with particular care. Please answer in American English.
