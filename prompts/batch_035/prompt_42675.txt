A friend and I have been arguing for months about whether long-run history is "locked in": whether, if the tape were rewound and replayed with small perturbations, the broad outcomes would re-arrive or whether we are living in one of countless equally possible histories. To make it concrete: suppose history were rerun from 1500 CE with only micro-level changes — different individuals conceived and born, the same geography, resource distributions, technologies, and institutions — while my credence is about 0.65 that something recognizably like the scientific-industrial transformation (sustained growth in productive capacity driven by systematic empirical inquiry) would occur somewhere within three centuries, his is about 0.2. Please give your own number, but more importantly decompose it into terms like: (i) the probability that the underlying dynamics are more convergent than contingent — that functional pressures such as interstate competition and the returns to useful knowledge reliably push societies toward that attractor rather than toward any of many stable alternatives; (ii) the probability that the relevant state space contains few rather than many locally stable outcomes, i.e., that the road to industrialization runs through wide gates rather than pinch points that most runs would miss; (iii) the probability that the phrase "recognizably like" isn't doing all the work — that you would count a transformation powered by, say, improved water and wind rather than coal, or centered in East Asia rather than Europe, as the same outcome rather than a different one; and (iv) whatever adjustment you think is owed to selection effects, given that we necessarily observe the one history in which industrialization happened. Assign a rough probability and one line of justification to each, combine them into your final credence, then tell me which term you are least confident in and what evidence would move it most — comparative history, independent "natural experiments" such as the multiple origins of agriculture and writing, or formal models of increasing returns. Last, given our two numbers, tell me whether my friend and I most plausibly disagree about the dynamics (term i), the ontology of outcome-sameness (term iii), or about what "rerun with micro-perturbations" even means.
