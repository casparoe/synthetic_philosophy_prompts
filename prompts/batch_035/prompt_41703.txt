I'm preparing a position statement for a graduate seminar on deontological ethics, and before I circulate it I want a consistency audit. Here are my commitments, stated as precisely as I can:

(a) There are at least near-absolute agent-centered constraints: I may not intentionally kill an innocent person even to prevent several comparable killings that would otherwise occur — no matter how many.

(b) I reject agent-relative prerogatives. I don't accept that morality grants me special license to favor my own projects, interests, or attachments over greater goods. If anything, I find prerogatives harder to defend than constraints.

(c) In the standard trolley switch case, diverting the trolley is not merely permissible but required: standing by and letting five die is itself a moral failure.

(d) It is wrong to push the large man off the footbridge, and wrong to kill one patient and distribute their organs to five, even though each act would avert five deaths.

(e) Impersonally speaking, a world containing more rights violations is worse than a world containing fewer, other things equal. Accordingly, I would support a state's bombing campaign that foreseeably, though not intentionally, kills some civilians in order to stop a larger massacre.

(f) The deepest flaw in consequentialism is that it licenses treating persons as mere means — as raw material for the production of good outcomes.

(g) If an unjust aggressor is about to kill five innocent people and the only way to stop him is to kill him, then I am required to kill him; mere permission would be too weak.

What I want from you is an audit, not a lecture. Tell me which pairs among (a)–(g) are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damaging. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what that concession would cost me elsewhere in the set. Then address two things specifically: first, whether the package is exposed to the objection sometimes called the paradox of deontology — that constraints like (a) commit me to holding that my killing one person is somehow worse than five other people dying, which sits badly with my refusal in (b) to let agent-relativity do any other work for me; and second, whether my acceptance of aggregation at the level of the state in (e), and of requiredness in (c) and (g), can be reconciled with the constraint structure in (a) and (d) without appealing to something I have already ruled out. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I have no settled view on where the problem is, so don't spare any commitment for my sake.

As you go, please flag how confident you are in each of your verdicts — which tensions you'd stake a lot on versus which you're calling as you see it — and single out the point in your audit you are least sure of, with a sentence on what kind of argument or case would move you on it.
