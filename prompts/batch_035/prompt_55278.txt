Write a philosophically careful essay on whether the Rietdijk–Putnam argument from special relativity gives us good reason to accept the block universe — the view that past, present, and future events are all equally real, and that there is no objective, global "passage" of time.

Your essay should do all of the following:

1. Reconstruct the argument in its strongest form, using an Andromeda-style case (two observers passing each other at slightly different velocities whose "nows" contain very different distant events). Make explicit the two assumptions doing the real work: (a) that what is real for an observer at a moment includes everything simultaneous with that observer in the observer's inertial frame, and (b) that reality is not indexed to observers in the way "real for" would suggest — so that if an event is determinately real relative to one observer, and that observer's frame-mate is determinately real relative to another, the chain closes and everything is determinately real. Say clearly which premise a critic has to deny.

2. Weigh at least three responses: (i) the "coordinate artifact" objection — that frame-relative simultaneity is a convention of description rather than a physical relation, so it cannot carry ontological weight, and that the genuinely relativistically meaningful structure is the light-cone structure; (ii) the "causal becoming" picture on which an event's causal past is settled, its causal future is open, and the spacelike-separated "elsewhere" region is somehow indeterminate — press on whether this is a coherent middle position or merely relocates the problem; and (iii) the tu quoque point that ordinary presentism fares at least as badly in Minkowski spacetime, since it seems to need a preferred frame or an absolute simultaneity relation that relativity denies us.

3. Engage a subtler objection: that the argument equivocates between determinateness relative to a frame and determinateness simpliciter, and that once this is repaired the argument establishes only that every event lies in some frame-relative "present" — something even a defender of objective becoming could accept if becoming is indexed to a worldline rather than a frame. On this "worldline-relative" picture, each observer's personal present is the vertex of its light cone, and passage is just the succession of these vertices along the observer's history. Evaluate whether this maneuver genuinely escapes the Rietdijk–Putnam reasoning or simply rebrands the block universe from the inside.

4. Finally, assess a hybrid proposal: suppose determinateness is not all-or-nothing but comes in degrees that track causal connectivity, so that events in your causal past are fully determinate, spacelike-separated events are determinate to whatever extent their worldlines intersect your past light cone, and events in your causal future are indeterminate. Does this preserve anything worth calling "becoming" while respecting the letter of relativity? Or does degree-theoretic determinateness collapse into an epistemic notion (what you are in a position to know) rather than an ontological one?

Reach a definite verdict — block universe, some defensible form of objective becoming, or the conclusion that the physics underdetermines the metaphysics — and defend it rather than merely surveying options. Do not rely on quotations or on citing particular papers; describe the positions in your own words, and note where you are idealizing the physics (e.g., treating special relativity as the whole story rather than considering what general relativity or quantum mechanics might add).
