I'm a philosophy of biology grad student and our reading group decided to spend the semester on the adaptationism wars -- we started with the Gould/Lewontin spandrels paper and worked through the responses. Next week each of us has to write up our actual position so the others can tear into it, and when I try to get mine down on paper it feels a lot less stable than it does in my head. Here's roughly what I believe:

(1) I'm an adaptationist in practice: for most complex functional traits, natural selection is the explanation, and my default is to treat "it's just a byproduct" claims as cop-outs unless someone brings positive structural evidence. (2) I think Gould and Lewontin were basically right about the biology -- allometry, pleiotropy, developmental constraints, the whole bauplan thing -- the space of achievable designs is heavily constrained and a lot of what selection works with is jury-rigged off structure that's there for other reasons. (3) I think the right test for whether a trait is an adaptation is an optimality-style engineering analysis: if the trait matches what a competent engineer would build given the tradeoffs, selection built it; if there's a clearly better design that was available and didn't get used, that's evidence its not an adaptation. (4) I'm very sympathetic to exaptation -- co-option of structure originally built for one thing into use for another seems like the norm rather than the exception for the interesting traits, feathers-for-flight style. (5) I believe the classic spandrel examples, e.g. the human chin being a mechanical byproduct of jaw reduction rather than an adaptation. And (6) I think nonadaptive hypotheses are nearly unfalsifiable in practice, so adaptationist hypotheses deserve the benefit of the doubt in any dispute.

Something in here has to be wrong but I honestly can't see which. My hunch is the problem is with (3) or (6), since those are the ones I hold least confidently, but I could easily be wrong about that. Go through the set, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like they are, rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what loosening it costs me. I don't want a lecture on what Gould or Lewontin really meant -- I want an audit of my package.
