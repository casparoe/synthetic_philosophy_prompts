my advisor keeps saying i should turn my dissertation chapter on origin essentialism into a paper, and instead I've talked myself into an argument that the view is self-refuting. it's supposed to be presented at our metaphysics workshop next week and i genuinely can't figure out which step is the bad one, so halp.

setup, standard kripkean background: i couldn't have originated from a completely different sperm and egg. the thing that makes me me, on this view, is originating from the particular zygote that formed when that particular sperm met that particular egg. fine so far. now:

(1) i couldn't have originated from a different zygote — that's origin essentialism, the thing we're supposedly defending.
(2) my zygote could have split during the first week and become identical twins. nobody thinks twinning is metaphysically impossible.
(3) twinning is symmetric — the zygote doesn't "continue as" one twin while merely "producing" the other; both twins originate from it on equal footing.
(4) so both twins originate from the very zygote i actually came from.
(5) if originating from that zygote is what makes something me (which is the essentialist's whole story about identity across possible worlds), then both twins would be me.
(6) two distinct people can't both be me.
(7) so my zygote could not have split — twinning was impossible for it specifically, even if its possible for zygotes in general?
(8) that's nuts, so origin essentialism is false.

and theres a second thing that came up when i workshopped this. even if you block the twinning argument somehow, the same guy pressed me with this: (a) i could have come from a sperm differing from my actual sperm by a single molecule. (b) if a one-molecule difference is compatible with being me, then a one-molecule difference from THAT sperm is compatible too. (c) iterate and i could have come from a completely different sperm, even one from a different father entirely. (d) no essentialist believes that. (e) so actually NO molecular difference is allowed — my origin had to be molecule-for-molecule identical, which seems just as crazy (gamete formation is noisy; on that view almost no conception of "my parents having another kid" produces a sibling rather than a stranger).

so the menu seems to be: twinning was metaphysically impossible for my zygote specifically, or my origin is fixed down to individual molecules and i was astonishingly lucky to exist at all, or origin doesn't matter and i could have had different parents. all three look bad to me but obviously they can't all be the price — some premise up there is doing something illegitimate. which step do you actually reject, and what does rejecting it cost? for what its worth i'd love to keep (2), and my instinct is the trick is hiding in (3), but every time i try to say why (3) is false it comes out as "one twin is the real continuation and the other is a newcomer," which is exactly what the symmetry was supposed to rule out. and i have no idea what to say about the molecule argument except mumbling something about possibility not being transitive, which sounds like special pleading until i can actually explain it.
