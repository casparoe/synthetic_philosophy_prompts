I'm a software engineer (backend, zero philosophy training, so keep the vocabulary in plain English where you can). My company ships LLM features and I sit next to the interpretability team, and something about their workflow keeps bugging me. Concretely, here's what they do: they poke around in the model's activation vectors, find some direction that lights up when the model is being confidently wrong or flattering the user, then they either write a paper saying "we found the deception feature" or, more seriously, wire it into production so that when that direction fires above some threshold, the output gets flagged or blocked.

When I ask them point blank whether the model *really has* a deception feature in there, or whether that's just a convenient label for a direction that happens to predict bad outputs, I get two different answers from the same team. One guy says it's basically like a good variable name — a compression trick for us humans, don't get spooked. Another says no, it's like finding out my service has a cache in it: a real component that was there all along, doing causal work, and we just located it. And the safety engineers talk as if the second guy is right, because their whole mitigation assumes blocking that direction is blocking the *lying itself*, not a correlated symptom.

So here's what I want: what does this practice actually presuppose about representation? Like, what would have to be true of the network for the "real component" view to be right versus the "useful fiction" view, and does the production-safety use (as opposed to just publishing descriptive findings) commit the team to the stronger one? Is the practice maybe underdetermined — several different views of what a representation is would all license the same workflow? And if the team bit the bullet and adopted the variable-name view, what concretely would have to change in how they talk, what they claim, or what they ship? Also tell me if the two camps are even disagreeing about anything real or just talking past each other. Please answer in American English.
