The practice exam question was:

"Sleeping Beauty volunteers for the following experiment, and every detail of it is explained to her in advance. On Sunday she is put to sleep, and a fair coin is tossed. If the coin lands heads, she is woken, interviewed, and returned to sleep once, on Monday. If the coin lands tails, she is woken, interviewed, and returned to sleep on both Monday and Tuesday, with a drug erasing her memory of the Monday interview overnight. When woken she cannot tell which day it is or whether she has been woken before. At each interview she is asked: what is your credence now that the coin landed heads?

(a) State the halfer and thirder positions, and present the strongest argument for each.
(b) Explain the halfer's claim that Beauty gains no new information upon waking, and present the most serious objection to that claim.
(c) Say which position you find more defensible, and defend your choice."

Here is the student's answer, verbatim:

"Beauty knows the whole protocol in advance, so the only uncertainties she ever has are the coin flip and, if the coin lands tails, which of the two awakenings she is currently experiencing.

(a) The thirder position says her credence in heads upon waking should be 1/3. The argument runs by indifference over centered possibilities. From her point of view upon being interviewed, there are exactly three situations she could be in: heads-and-Monday, tails-and-Monday, and tails-and-Tuesday. The Monday and Tuesday awakenings in the tails branch are subjectively indistinguishable, so she must give them equal credence. And given that the coin is fair, none of the three candidate situations is privileged over any other, so each gets credence 1/3. Hence her credence in heads is 1/3.

The halfer position, originally argued by Adam Elga in the paper that introduced the problem, says her credence should remain 1/2. The argument is that on Sunday Beauty already knew everything there is to know: she knew she would be woken at least once and interviewed, and nothing that happens at an awakening goes beyond that. Since her Sunday credence was 1/2, and she anticipated being in exactly this epistemic position, her evidence upon waking is identical to her Sunday evidence, and her credence should be unchanged at 1/2. David Lewis replied to Elga defending the thirder view on the grounds that Beauty's credences should match the long-run frequency of heads among all awakenings in repeated trials, which is 1/3.

(b) The most serious objection to the halfer's no-new-information claim is that waking does give Beauty new self-locating information: she learns that this awakening is occurring, and on the tails branch there are two candidate 'nows' rather than one. But this objection is weaker than it looks. Self-locating information is information about which temporal part of the experiment she occupies, and that can't rationally shift her credence in an uncentered proposition like how the coin landed, since the coin's outcome is fixed independently of which day it is. So the halfer can grant the new self-locating evidence and still deny that it is relevant.

(c) I find the halfer position more defensible. The indifference principle in (a) looks less innocent than it does in ordinary cases, because the three 'equally possible' situations are not symmetric: heads-and-Monday is one coin outcome paired with one day, while tails gets matched with two days, so treating the three as on a par effectively counts the tails branch twice. The halfer view also has an attractive stability. Suppose Beauty is told, during an interview, that today is Monday. Since the day of the week is causally independent of the coin toss, learning that it is Monday gives her no reason to move her credence in heads, so it stays at 1/2, just as it was upon waking. So the halfer picture gives 1/2 before and after being told the day, which is exactly the sort of stability you would expect from a fair coin. The main open cost of the halfer view is that it must deny that a fair coin's outcome and the day of the week, which are independent, produce a uniform distribution over the three centered situations — but that is a cost the thirder incurs in mirror image, by insisting on a uniform distribution in a setting where the underlying chances are not uniform over days."
