For a metaphysics reading group I've been asked to write up my position on dispositions, finks, and masks so the others can probe it. Before circulating it, I'd like a consistency check from you. Here is where I stand.

(1) Dispositions are intrinsic properties of their bearers. Two perfect intrinsic duplicates cannot differ in what dispositions they have; whether a glass is fragile depends on its microstructure, not on what is happening around it.

(2) The simple conditional analysis is false: a fragile glass need not be one that would break if struck, since a fink — a mechanism that would remove the fragility before the strike could take effect — can make the conditional come out false while the disposition remains. But I believe the conditional analysis can be repaired. My version: something is disposed to M when S if, were S to obtain, M would occur, holding fixed the actual intrinsic basis of the disposition and assuming conditions suitable for its operation.

(3) Masks and antidotes do not remove dispositions. A vase packed in bubble wrap is still fragile; the wrap blocks the route from basis to manifestation, but the disposition itself is intact. Similarly, someone who has taken a drug that would prevent aspirin from working still has the disposition to have her headache relieved by aspirin.

(4) In reverse-fink cases I side with the conditional verdict. A wire that is in fact dead, but hooked to a device that would make it live a moment before anyone touched it, is not genuinely disposed to electrocute. It would conduct, but only because its basis would be switched in just in time; the disposition itself is absent, and I think that is the right thing to say.

(5) A disposition is individuated by its stimulus and manifestation conditions alone. The causal basis is a realizer of the disposition, not a constituent of it: two glasses with quite different microstructures can be fragile in exactly the same sense, and what makes them both fragile cannot include either basis.

I can feel some friction among these five commitments, but I honestly cannot tell where it is real and where it is mere appearance. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and how badly; which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection; and, for each genuine one, what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost me. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please write continuous prose only, as in an email or letter — no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — and do not restate my views back to me at length before getting to the audit.
