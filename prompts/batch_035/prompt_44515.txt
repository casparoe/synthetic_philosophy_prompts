i'm a phd student in moral philosophy and i help run a thing in our department where instead of discussing a paper we do a hands-on session on one concept (about 14 grad students plus whoever shows up from faculty, 75 min). next session i want to do moral trade — the toby ord idea that people with genuinely conflicting moral views can cut deals that both sides count as wins by their own lights, like the vegetarian and the meat eater who agree that one will go veg and the other will fund animal welfare work, so each thinks the world got better even though they never converged on the object-level question. i don't want to open with a lecture, i want them to run into the gains from trade themselves.

design the activity for me. rough shape i'm imagining, but you can push back: each participant gets a secret "moral portfolio" at the start — a few weighted commitments (a fairly strict deontologist, a total utilitarian, someone who only really cares about factory farming, a partialist who cares way more about people they know, etc.), each portfolio expressed as point values over a menu of possible actions they can spend a fixed budget of "activist hours" on. then a baseline round where everyone allocates unilaterally, then open bilateral/multilateral trading of allocations, side payments, whatever they can negotiate. no real money obviously — points on cards or a shared spreadsheet.

the crucial part: tell me how to build the payoff tables so real gains from trade are baked in but not obvious — i want some pairs or coalitions to find them and others not to, so the debrief has natural variation to work with. also the mechanics need to be simple enough that a room of philosophers can run it without a rulebook longer than a paragraph.

then the debrief: map the rounds onto the idea — pareto improvements under settled disagreement, why they never had to resolve the disagreement to benefit, why moral uncertainty makes you a more natural trading partner than you'd expect. but also the hard parts: one of the portfolios should probably be nasty on purpose (someone whose weights put a lot of value on something most of the room will hate), so the debrief has to face the question of whether trading with them is complicity or whether refusing makes the world worse by literally everyone's own lights including yours. and tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time i run it, plus what part of real moral trade a classroom game just can't reproduce — be honest about that part because i'll have to say it out loud at the end and i don't want to hand-wave it.
