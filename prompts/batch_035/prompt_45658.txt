I teach a graduate seminar in the philosophy of mathematics, and at the end of each term I poll the students on platonism versus nominalism. This year's results were instructive in a frustrating way: the numbers ranged from 0.15 to 0.9, and when I asked each student to justify their figure, nearly everyone argued at the level of the whole question — one big consideration for, one big consideration against, and a number that seemed to come from temperament. I want to train next year's cohort to argue at the level of the parts, and for that I need a worked example better than any I can construct from the textbook presentations, because those presentations all decompose the debate along the same two fault lines — the epistemic problem of access, and the indispensability argument — and I have become convinced that those two lines cross a more fundamental one which they obscure: the distinction between whether there exist abstract mathematical objects and whether our mathematical assertions are true under their ordinary reading. A nominalist can hold that arithmetic sentences are true while denying that they are made true by objects, via paraphrase or a figurative semantics; a platonist can, in principle, hold that the objects exist while denying that our theorems refer to them in any robust sense. Most seminar arguments, and every poll I have run, fail to separate these.

So here is what I would like from you. Give me your credence that platonism is true, where I stipulate platonism as the conjunction of two claims: first, that there exist mind-independent, non-spatiotemporal mathematical objects; second, that at least some standard mathematical assertions are literally true in virtue of those objects. Then decompose that credence into sub-questions, each with its own probability and a one-line justification. The decomposition must include, at minimum: (i) a term for the semantic question described above — the probability that the truth of mathematical assertions requires objects, i.e. that every account on which mathematical truth is secured without objects fails; (ii) a term for the identification problem that Benacerraf raised for set-theoretic reductions of arithmetic — the probability that there is a fact of the matter about which set-theoretic object, if any, the number 3 is, together with a judgment on whether the absence of any such fact favors structuralism over platonism or merely restates the platonist's commitment in different words; (iii) a term for epistemic access — the probability that creatures like us could know substantive truths about causally isolated objects, conditional on such objects existing; and (iv) any further terms you think belong, named and defended by you. Then combine them, and tell me explicitly whether your final figure is best read as the probability of a conjunction or as something looser, given that the terms need not be independent.

After that, three further things. First, a sensitivity analysis: which single term, if you came to believe it should be 0.05 lower than you placed it, would move your final figure the most, and why. Second, resolution criteria: state what would count as settling each of the two headline claims by, say, 2100 — a completed nominalization of applied mathematics of the sort Field began for Newtonian gravitation, a workable account of how our symbols reach abstracta, or something else you specify — and say whether your credence differs at all depending on whether you think the question will be settled by argument rather than by the slow drift of what philosophers find tolerable to assert. Third, and most useful to me as a teacher: two of this year's students — a mathematician at 0.85 and a metaphysician at 0.2 — disagree more than the overall poll spread suggests, and I suspect their disagreement is concentrated rather than diffuse. Tell me which of your sub-questions it most likely lives in, and give me the one question I could put to each of them in seminar that would best expose it. Please give real numbers throughout; an answer of the form that it all depends on what we mean by platonism is precisely what I am trying to get away from, and the stipulation above was meant to have foreclosed it.
