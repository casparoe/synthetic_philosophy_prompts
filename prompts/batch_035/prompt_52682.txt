My husband died in March, and by June I was what my therapist calls depressed: not sleeping, dropped alot of weight, quit my book club, some days I don't get out of bed till noon. She wants me on antidepressants and says this has become major depression rather than ordinary grief. My sister thinks that's absurd -- of course I'm wrecked, anyone would be, and being destroyed after your husband dies isn't a disease, it's just being a person. She says the medical label is a way of pretending normal human suffering is a malfunction, and that taking pills for it means I'm agreeing with that. A friend mentioned that doctors apparently used to have an actual rule about this -- if your depression started right after a death they wouldn't even diagnose you, you had to be depressed for no reason at all -- but they scrapped it at some point, so even the people who decide what counts as a disease can't seem to settle it. Part of me sides with my sister: the label feels like it lets everyone off from actually sitting with me while I mourn. But honestly I also just want to function again. So: is there a real fact of the matter about whether I'm sick here, or is it just a choice about how we talk about suffering? And either way, does the answer change whether I should take the meds?
