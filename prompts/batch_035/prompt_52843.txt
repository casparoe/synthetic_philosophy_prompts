My decision theory reading group is doing an end-of-term open mic (dont ask) and I drew the Death in Damascus problem. Write a sestina for it. The six rotating end words should be something like Damascus, Aleppo, death, flee, expect and meet, and I want the rotation itself to do the work: as the end words keep coming back in new positions, it should feel like the traveller rerouting his reasoning and just relocating the appointment. Ideally by the later stanzas the reader can sense the instability — staying in Damascus feels right if Death expects you in Aleppo, fleeing feels right if he expects you here, and each resolution to be clever is evidence for the opposite move. The envoi should land on the fixed-point horror without spelling out the moral: the man and death are both trying to be consistent with each other, and that shared consistency is the trap. Someone in the group who knows the problem should recognise which stanza is the two-boxer analogue (staying = taking what's predicted), and someone who doesnt should still get a creepy little poem about running from something patient. Please write in British English.
