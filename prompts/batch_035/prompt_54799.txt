I audit a reading group on later Wittgenstein, and last week two of the regulars — Rosa and Jules, both philosophy graduate students — spent the entire session locked in a dispute about the status of hinge commitments. The occasion was Moore's famous gesture of holding up his hands to prove the external world, which had pushed the group into On Certainty. The argument unfolded in five moves, and since I could follow every step of it but settle none of it, I told them I'd put it to someone knowledgeable. (1) Rosa opened by insisting that hinges are a genuine special class of knowledge: the metaphor is that the door of empirical inquiry swings while the hinges stay fixed, so hinges are certainties that are not themselves grounded but that make grounding possible elsewhere; on her reading, Wittgenstein's verdict on Moore was that Moore was right to claim to know such things and wrong only to think he could prove them — knowing a hinge differs in kind from knowing an ordinary fact, but it is still knowing. (2) Jules replied that this empties the word: if, by hypothesis, no doubt and no justification is even intelligible for a hinge, then a knowledge-attribution to it does no epistemic work; on his reading Wittgenstein treats such sentences as rules of grammar — expressions of the framework rather than true descriptions within it — and rules are not the sort of thing one knows, any more than one knows the rules of chess from observing that the pieces always move that way.

(3) Rosa countered that Wittgenstein does allow the ordinary knowledge-attribution in special cases, pointing to the passage where he grants that someone who says they know they have never been on the moon is saying something correct, even though no grounds are possible; and she argued this is not grammar masquerading as knowledge but knowledge whose certainty is inherited from its role — it is because some things must hold fast that anything can be tested, and what holds fast is thereby known, though not by evidence. (4) Jules pressed two objections: first, that Rosa equivocates between certainty as a psychological, almost animal state and certainty as an epistemic status carrying truth-responsibility, and only the former survives her own characterization; second, that her hard category cannot survive the riverbed passages, where Wittgenstein concedes that the distinction between the shifting bed and the flowing water is itself fluid and may alter — so a hinge is a hinge only relative to a time and a community, and when the bed shifts, a former hinge turns back into an ordinary revisable belief, which suggests the category is perspectival rather than a real ontological divide. (5) Rosa closed with what she called her strongest point: that the riverbed metaphor cuts for her, not against her — the bed shifts, but at any moment there is a bed, and Wittgenstein's lesson is precisely that some propositions must be immune from doubt at a time for testing to get any grip at all; and she ended by challenging Jules to explain how, on his grammatical reading, a hinge could ever be false rather than merely abandoned — for if hinges are rules, revising one looks like changing the subject, and yet we do say of communities in the past that their fixed certainties were wrong, which seems to require that hinges have truth-values after all. Neither would give an inch.

They have asked me, as the neutral party, to bring back a verdict, and I would honestly like to know where the balance of truth lies. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: for each of the five numbered moves above, say who is right about what, and to what degree — including, where apt, what both of them may be missing. Present your adjudication as a numbered list of exactly five items, one per move, each item a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion.
