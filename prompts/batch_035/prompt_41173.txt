Camp A holds that the Enlightenment — reason, criticism, universal rights, roughly the 18th century movement — was on net the source of moral and material progress, and that the bad stuff (colonialism, scientific racism) was a betrayal of its own principles. Camp B holds the opposite: universalism licensed empire, reason turned instrumental, and whatever progress happened was paid for by people outside Europe, so the 'betrayal' framing is the whole mistake. Please draft the joint statement both camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: the claims they can genuinely agree on (not vacuous ones — e.g. maybe they can both accept that Enlightenment language was used on both sides of the abolition debate, if worded right); the precise disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; and the cruxes — sub-questions where settling one would move at least one camp — with, for each crux, a concrete case or body of evidence both sides would agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance. For instance I suspect one crux is whether Enlightenment principles were internally revisionary enough to make abolitionism possible, and a test case might be enslaved people and anticolonial figures wielding the Declaration's language against the powers that issued it — but use your judgment on which cases actually split the camps. Format: numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Also please flag any residual disagreement you think is verbal rather than real. Finally, be honest about your own draft: say where you think the wording is most likely slanted toward one camp, and what argument or piece of evidence would change your mind about which disagreements are genuine.
