I am a science and ideas correspondent who also sits on the editorial board of our publication's longform section, and I need a decision memo drafted for our next board meeting. The case: we are deciding whether to commission a planned season of longform pieces built around profound suffering — a documentary series following a hospice, an interactive essay on a famine's aftermath, a true-crime serial on an unsolved murder. Our engagement team supports the season on the grounds of morbid curiosity and the paradox of tragedy, reasoning that audiences demonstrably seek out sad and disturbing art, and they have retention figures from our previous crime and disaster coverage to support them. A faction of the board counters that the classical treatment of the paradox — from Hume's puzzle about why audiences take pleasure in sorrow, and the various answers on offer, whether in terms of the artistry that converts distress into aesthetic pleasure, or in terms of the rich mixed experience of being moved — was framed around fiction and theatre, and that documentary portrayals of real suffering are a different animal entirely; on their view, the worry is not that readers will disengage but that they will engage in a way that amounts to consuming real anguish as entertainment.

Please draft the memo the board will circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action — commission the full season, cut it, or proceed with conditions — with reasons the editors can actually act on, and it should give the losing side's strongest argument an honest hearing rather than a token paragraph. In the course of the reasoning, I want the board to see clearly where the philosophical accounts of the paradox apply to our case and where they break down: does the traditional machinery, built for fictional tragedy, carry over when the suffering is real and the reader knows it, or does something in that boundary do the work? Feel free to draw on relevant empirical research on why audiences seek out sad or frightening material, but do not pad the memo with reading lists or citations — the board wants the substance, not a bibliography. Please write the memo in British English.
