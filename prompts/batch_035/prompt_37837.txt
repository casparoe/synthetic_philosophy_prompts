For the last year or so I've been chewing over this question on my own time: which animals have inner lives, and whether it's more of a dimmer switch than an on/off thing. No training, just reading and thinking, and I've settled on a picture where the distribution is gradual and messy rather than a ladder with humans at the top. Now I've gone and agreed to give a 20-minute session at my local skeptics meetup -- regular folks, no biology background, and half of them will walk in sure that animals "just run on instict." I don't want you to write the talk, I want a plan for it: what has to be in place before the dimmer-switch idea even makes sense to them, which single animal comparison should carry the whole thing (I keep going back and forth between fish feeling pain and octopus arms, but I have a feeling octopuses just seduce people into "they're basically aliens" mode), the misconceptions they'll arrive with or form along the way -- I'd bet on "either it's conscious or it's a robot" and "more neurons = more conscious" -- and exactly where in the sequence each one should get headed off. Also what I should openly refuse to tackle in 20 minutes (the hard problem itself? insects?) and say so, and one question I can ask at the end to check whether they actually got the gradient idea rather than just leaving with "wow, nature is amazing."
