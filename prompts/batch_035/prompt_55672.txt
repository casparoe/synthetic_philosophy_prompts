A couple of friends of mine got into it after one of them read that IQ test scores are around 80% heritable in wealthy countries. (1) Dan said that means genes explain 80% of any given person's intelligence, so the tutoring program our city is debating is basically doomed -- you can't move something that's mostly genetic. (2) Maya said that's a category error: heritability is about what explains *differences* in a population, not what built any individual's intelligence, the same way saying height differences are mostly genetic in a well-fed population tells you nothing about whether better nutrition would make everyone taller. (3) Dan said fine, but if heritability is about differences, then a figure of 80% says the IQ differences in these countries really are mostly genetic, which was his point all along. (4) Maya said no -- heritability is measured within whatever range of environments happens to exist, and in countries where schooling and nutrition are pretty uniform, the genetic share gets inflated; the same population could come out much lower under different conditions. (5) Dan said that's a dodge: if uniform environments are what make genetic differences loom large, then the high heritability itself shows our environments are already uniform, so spending more money equalizing them should accomplish roughly nothing. Neither would budge. Can you go through the exchange and say who's right at each step, and what (if anything) they're both missing? I genuinely couldn't referee this one myself.
