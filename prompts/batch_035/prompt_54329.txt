My friend Maya has very firm opinions about what makes one explanation better than another, and she hands out verdicts constantly. A sample of her rulings: when petty cash kept vanishing at her old job, she was sure it was one bookkeeper rather than three employees each skimming a little, since one person covered everything. When both our smoke alarms started chirping the same night, she insisted it was one small kitchen fire rather than two batteries coincidentally dying together. But when a mutual friend credited the universe looking out for him for surviving three close calls in a month, she said that wasn't even a candidate explanation, no matter how neatly it unified all three. Same reaction when her upstairs neighbor blamed the utility's smart meter for her headaches, the flickering lights, and the dog's restlessness — one tidy cause covering everything, and Maya still rolled her eyes. On the other hand, when a string of car break-ins hit our street along with pry marks on a shed door, she went with one burglar, and said the shed marks actually counted as evidence for the burglar since he'd produce them too.

So now something happened at my office and I genuinely can't predict her. All week, food has been disappearing from the shared fridge, the plants by the window keep getting knocked over, and there's been a weird smell in the copy room. Option one: a raccoon is living in the drop ceiling and roams at night — one cause, explains all three things, and you could just set up a camera to check. Option two: a coworker is taking lunches, the night janitor is bumping the plants with the vacuum, and the copier has a burnt part — three causes, but every single one of them is boringly common. What's her verdict going to be? What rule is she actually running from those earlier rulings, what other rules fit her track record just as well, and which further case would actually tell those rules apart? I care less about the raccoon answer than about the pattern.
