I spent thirty-six years as a polymer chemist in industrial research, sorting matter into bins for a living: distinguishing one copolymer family from another, deciding when a change in tacticity made a material a new thing rather than a worse version of an old thing. Since retiring I have been reading philosophy of science, and the literature on natural kinds strikes me as written by people who have never had to maintain a classification under pressure from the instruments. Before I read any further in it, I want to know what I should be demanding. Please write the desiderata: what any adequate account of natural kinds has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement.

Let me seed the list with the cases that keep occurring to me, so you can see the level of specificity I want. Isotopes: a chemist can ignore them, a nuclear physicist cannot, and neither is wrong — so is being a natural kind somehow relative to an inquiry, or is one of us classifying a non-joint? Allotropes and polymorphs: graphite and diamond share a composition and differ in packing, and in my industry a polymorph transition could halt a production line while regulators still called it the same active ingredient. Species: my biologist friends tell me the species category itself is not one kind of kind. Reclassification: when the crystallographers had to accommodate quasicrystals, they redefined what a crystal is, and when astronomers demoted Pluto, they did something that looked on the surface like the same operation but felt completely different — one seemed forced by nature, the other by convenience, and I would like an account of kinds on which that difference is visible rather than declared. And then the awkward cross-cutters: jade, which is two minerals and one marketplace kind; clay, which is at least partly defined by a grain-size convention; functional kinds like keystone species, which are not held together by any shared intrinsic property at all.

Two further requirements I suspect are jointly unsatisfiable, and I want your verdict on this in particular: first, that a kind's boundaries should be indifferent to improvements in instrumentation (a kind map that redraws itself every time a spectrometer gets better is not carving anything); second, that the account should defer entirely to the classificatory practice of working scientists, who redraw exactly those maps for exactly those reasons. If some subset of the desiderata cannot be jointly met, say so plainly — that would itself be the result I most want.

For each requirement, note whether it is non-negotiable or a prejudice of one camp. Then, briefly, say how classical essentialism, homeostatic property cluster views, and anything you would call deflationary or pluralist fare against the list, and which single requirement does the most to eliminate candidates. I care about the criteria and the motivating cases; the scorecard matters much less. Do not offer me a reading list — I want your best current construction, not a survey of who said what.
