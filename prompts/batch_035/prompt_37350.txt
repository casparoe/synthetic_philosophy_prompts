Here's something I've been going back and forth on, and I want you to tell me what I actually believe. It's about what probability statements mean. I've got a bunch of specific claims plus my gut verdicts on each, and I want to know whether there's one interpretation of probability -- frequentist, subjective, propensity, whatever -- that captures all of them, or whether I'm stuck being a pluralist, or whether I just have to throw some verdicts out.

The cases:

1. "The probability this die comes up 4 is 1/6." Fine, obviously. Though I confess I don't know whether what makes it fine is the long-run frequency, the symmetry of the die, or my ignorance, and I'd sort of like to know.

2. "The probability that the billionth digit of pi is 7 is 1/10." I'm happy to say it and happy to bet at those odds, but it feels like it's 100% about my ignorance. There's nothing chancy in the world; the digit is what it is.

3. "The probability Caesar was assasinated in 44 BCE, given everything I know, is about 1." Totally fine, and clearly just credence. Nobody thinks there's a chance process operating there.

4. "This particular radon atom has a probability of about 1/2 of decaying over the next 3.8 days." This one feels different in kind from 2 and 3. It's a single case, unrepeated, and yet I feel like the 1/2 is out there in the atom, a real tendency, not a fact about me. Even if nobody ever knew or asked, there'd still be this fact.

5. "The probability this photon passes through the polarizer is cos² of the angle." Same feeling as 4, even stronger. Real chance in the world.

6. "The probability a randomly selected voter supports the incumbent is 0.45." Fine, and it feels like it's about a frequency in a population -- though I notice the population is finite and the election is a one-off, so if you push on it it gets weird.

7. "The probability I'd have gone into academia if I'd gone to a different college is maybe 0.3." And here I stall. It can't be a frequency (there's one me), it can't be a propensity (nothing in me is disposed to exactly 30%), and yet it also doesn't feel like mere ignorance the way the pi digit does -- it's not like there's a fact out there that I'm missing. But maybe that's exactly wrong and it IS just like the pi digit.

And then two general commitments I have that are probably in tension with the above:

A. I'm drawn to the thought that there's no such thing as THE probability of anything independent of an information state -- probabilities are always somebody's, always conditional on what they know. That feels right for every case above except 4 and 5, where it feels totally wrong.

B. I think that if physics turned out to be fully deterministic (no quantum indeterminacy anywhere), there would be no objective chances at all, and every probability would be like case 2. But I'd still say "the probability the coin lands heads is 1/2," and I'd still think that was TRUE.

So: what's the principle? Is there a single story -- one interpretation, or one principled hybrid (chances as credences conditional on some privileged body of evidence, propinquities [sorry, propensities] only where physics supplies them, whatever) -- that gets all or most of my verdicts? State it precisely enough that I can apply it myself. Tell me which of my verdicts it can't save, and which of those should go. And then tell me what it predicts for two cases I haven't listed: (i) "the probability the universe ends in heat death is around 0.9," and (ii) a doctor telling a specific patient "there's about a 30% chance this treatment fails" when the trial data are the only evidence anyone has.
