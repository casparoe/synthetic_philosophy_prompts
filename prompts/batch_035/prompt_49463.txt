I'm giving a talk in two weeks at a small workshop on how to allocate an animal welfare budget across species, and I need help prepping for the Q&A because the audience is going to be rough. My thesis: brain size (and neuron count, which is what people actually proxy with it) should be dropped as a direct input into moral-weight estimates. It's not that brains don't matter, it's that brain size is so tangled up with body size through allometric scaling that when you put "number of neurons" into your spreadsheet, you're mostly measuring how big the animal is, not how much it can suffer. If an elephant has more neurons than me because it's a big animal, and a crow has way fewer but solves problems my cat can't, then raw neuron counts can't be doing the work the spreadsheet says they're doing.

The audience: roughly half grantmakers who came up through the effective altruism ecosystem and currently use neuron-count-weighted moral weights to decide between funding hen welfare, shrimp welfare, and (increasingly) insect welfare programs. There's a nocicection researcher who studies pain circuits in crustaceans and thinks the EA people abuse her field's data. One veterinarian who thinks all this number-crunching is a distraction from obvious welfare wins that don't need a model. And two philosophers of mind who mostly came to watch. A couple of the grantmakers have a real stake in this — their current portfolio allocations assume shrimp moral weight is meaningfuly lower than hen moral weight, mostly on neural numbers.

Draft me a prep sheet: the eight-ish objections I'll actually get, ordered by how much damage they could do, phrased the way each of those people would actually phrase it (the neuroscientist's objection will sound nothing like the grantmakers'). For each one give me a short reply I can deliver in under two minutes, and a note on where the reply is soft. Be honest about which objections I should just concede — I'm fine admitting that within-clade correlations carry some signal, what I can't concede is the cross-species comparisons the funding decisions actually rest on. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks, which I'm pretty sure is some version of "fine, so what number do we use for shrimp on Thursday when the grant is due" — if it's actually a different question I should be dreading, tell me what it is and why.
