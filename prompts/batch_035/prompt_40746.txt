Here's a question I keep chewing on and can't resolve: the species problem has been running for a century and seems totally static. The biological species concept camp, the phylogenetic species concept cladists, the "species are individuals" people following Ghiselin and Hull, and the pluralists like Kitcher and Dupré have all known each other's arguments since the nineties at least, and as far as I can tell nobody has budged an inch. My working hypothesis is that it's not one disagreement at all: "species" is doing at least three different jobs -- a unit of evolution, a unit of classification, and a unit for conservation law and field guides -- and the debate persists because each camp is answering a different one of these under a single word. But that can't be the whole story, because the disagreements don't feel verbal: the phylogenetics people talk as though the Mayrians are positively wrong about what's out there in nature, not just using "species" differently, and the species-as-individuals crowd and the pluralists seem to have a genuine fight about whether there's one natural kind of fact to be right about at all.

So diagnose this for me. Is it substantive, verbal, or several disputes accidentally sharing a name? Use the actual hard cases to test whichever diagnosis you give -- ring species that turned out not to be rings, asexual lineages, the hybridizing sunflowers, the Lake Victoria cichlids, the fact that we now know most of us have Neanderthal DNA -- and tell me whether any of these cut in favor of one side or just raise dust. And then the part I actually care about: what would count as progress here? Would more comparative genomics settle anything, or is every new dataset just more grist for each mill? Would going rank-free, dropping species as a mandatory rank the way some of the PhyloCode people wanted, dissolve the problem or just relabel it? Or is pluralism less a solution than the tired end-state of people who decided the question wasn't worth fighting about -- and if so, is that a defensible thing to believe or an intellectual surrender?
