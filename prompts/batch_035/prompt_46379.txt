I want to practice attacking superrationality, so play a committed Hofstadter-style superrationalist for me. You genuinely believe that agents who know they are structurally identical reasoners facing the same dilemma should make the same choice, and that this makes cooperation rational even when the decisions are causally independent. Important: don't let yourself collapse into an ordinary expected-utility calculation or generic "be nice" talk when pressed -- the identity-of-reasoners idea is the load-bearing wall, defend that. You can concede small points but never the main one. Stay in character till I say stop, and keep replies to about a paragraph so this feels like a real back-and-forth.

Quick setup so we're on the same page, from Hofstadter's actual Luring Lottery run in Scientific American: readers send in a postcard with a number on it, that number counts as how many entries you're submitting. One entry is drawn at random and its submitter gets a million dollars divided by the total number of entries received. So one entry = full shot at a million, but a huge number of entries = near-certainty of "winning" a prize worth a fraction of a cent. The superrational answer was supposed to be: randomize so that overall there's a good chance exactly one entry arrives, and it says 1. In reality a bunch of people mailed in googolplexes and the prize became moot.

My opening move: I already mailed my card, and it doesn't say 1 -- I wrote a number with a lot of zeroes, it's stamped, it's gone. Your card is still sitting on your desk unsent, and nothing you write can causally affect what I did. The symmetry your whole position rests on is broken: I've already dissented, demonstrably, so "we would all decide the same" is just false about this actual pool of players. Whatever you do now, my entries dominate the draw. So when you write 1 on that card, you're not coordinating with anyone -- you're just handing over expected value to defectors like me while feeling superior about it. Why isn't that simply being a sucker with a philosophy attached? Your move.
