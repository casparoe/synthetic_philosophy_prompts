I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, and at a colloquium last week a visiting speaker brushed off my question about whether physics could in principle be complete by invoking Gödel: in effect, that Gödel proved any formal system rich enough contains truths it cannot prove, that physics is formalized in mathematics, and that therefore no physical theory can ever be complete — so my question was confused at the root. I could tell the one-liner was carrying a lot of hidden weight, but I couldn't disaggregate it on the spot. Help me lay out, as a numbered list I can bring to our next reading group, the distinct precise claims that could be packed into the slogan that Gödel showed truth outruns provability. I want the readings ordered from weakest to strongest, and for each: what exactly it asserts, about what (a particular sentence unprovable in a particular system, under what assumptions about that system's consistency or soundness; or unprovability period, by any system whatsoever), and whether it is a theorem, a platitude, a substantive but non-mathematical thesis, or simply false. A few things I half-remember and would like placed within this map: that the original argument needed a stronger assumption than mere consistency and that this was later weakened; that there is a separate and different result about truth itself not being capturable within a given language; and that if you are allowed to keep adding axioms or moving to stronger systems, it starts to look like any particular truth becomes provvable somewhere, so the interesting question is what restriction on what counts as a system keeps the slogan from collapsing. Finally, tell me which of the readings, if any, the speaker actually needed in order to get from arithmetic to physics — for instance, whether a physical theory must contain arithmetic in the relevant sense, and whether the incompleteness of a theory says anything about incompleteness of the world it describes. If none of the readings gets him there, say plainly where the inference breaks.
