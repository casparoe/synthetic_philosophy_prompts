I'm a graduate student finishing a term paper for a seminar on philosophy of mathematics. My draft defends a version of platonism against Benacerraf's dilemma from "Mathematical Truth" (1973), and I'd be grateful for detailed comments before I revise. The core of the draft is this: the dilemma is standardly taken to have an epistemological horn (if numbers are causally inert abstract objects, and knowledge requires some causal link between knower and known, then mathematical knowledge is impossible) and a semantic horn (if we instead adopt a combinatorial or "syntax-based" account on which mathematical truth is internal to a formal system, we can explain our access to mathematical truths but lose the distinction between mathematics and an arbitrary game like chess, whose propositions aren't truth-apt in the same way). My paper argues that both horns fail for structuralism-plus-platonism, and I want to know whether the arguments actually hold together.

The first half of my draft contends that the epistemological horn rests on a causal theory of knowledge that nobody should accept anyway, since it struggles with familiar counterexamples involving inference to unobservables: we apparently know plenty about things that have never causally interacted with us in the right way (events before our birth, posited particles too weakly coupled to detect directly), and if inference can secure knowledge in those cases, I claim it can in the mathematical case too. The second, more speculative half argues that the chess horn is answered by a modal gloss: mathematical statements are implicitly claims about what holds in *every* structure of a given type, whereas chess propositions depend on the contingent stipulations of a particular rule set — and this difference in modal force, not causal contact, is what underwrites the objectivity of mathematics. The draft runs about four thousand words; the sections I have real doubts about are the causal-counterexample section and the modal-force section.

Please comment on the following. First, accuracy: am I representing the two horns of the dilemma fairly, or am I conflating Benacerraf's original presentation with later epistemologized versions of the argument (e.g., Field-style worries about reliability)? Second, do my two counterarguments actually work? In particular, does the modal-force suggestion evade the chess horn, or does the platonist about structures simply inherit the same access problem one level up? And does the appeal to unobservables beg the question against the causal theorist, given that posited particles are at least in principle causally connected to us while numbers are not? Third, flag any point where a grader sympathetic to the dilemma would say I've changed the subject rather than answered it, and tell me which section most needs strengthening if I can only substantially revise one. Please do not supply a reading list or citations; I'm set on sources and just want engagement with the arguments themselves.
