my game theory course just ended the semester on the centipede game and I've been chewing on it all week. Quick setup in case it's been a while: two players alternate turns, and at each node the player whose turn it is can either "take" — the game ends immediately and the taker gets the larger share of the current pot — or "pass," which grows the pot and hands control to the other player. If they both keep passing the pot gets very big, but backward induction says a rational player takes at the final node, so the player before that, anticipating it, takes one node earlier, and so on down the line, so with common knowledge of rationality the first player should take right away and the huge pot is never even glimpsed. Except real people playing for real money pass for a while, and there's a deeper puzzle underneath: the induction seems to prove passing can never occur, yet if my opponent ever does pass, that very pass refutes the premise (rationality as common knowledge) the induction was run on. My friend from class thinks the whole thing is just an artifact of nobody having common knowledge of rationality in real life, but I think the puzzle is more interesting than that, and I'd rather not have the poem just take her side.

so: write me a villanelle out of this. the two refrains should be the two voices — one the voice of the induction itself (something in the spirit of each rational player taking at the node before the last, though make your own lines, don't use my phrasing, and they should scan), the other the voice of the player who passes anyway, trusting or testing. then the tercets should walk backwards through the game tree: the first tercet sits at or near the final node, and each tercet after it lands one node earlier, so that the poem itself performs the backward induction, refrain by refrain, until the closing quatrain arrives at the first node and the two refrains finally meet as a couplet. and I want that meeting to feel like the paradox, not a resolution — the induction is valid at every node, and yet there the two of them are, still passing. someone who knows the literature should be able to tell which line is which at each step and why the argument keeps reasserting itself, and someone who doesn't should just find it a good villanelle about greed and trust.

one last thing: please get the game-theoretic content right, the structure of the payoffs at the last node is what drives everything, so a game theorist shouldn't wince. villanelle rules are strict about the refrains and the ABA tercets, but you knew that, you're the poet here — I'm just the guy who keeps passing when he should take.
