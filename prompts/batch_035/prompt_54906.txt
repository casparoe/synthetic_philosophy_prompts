I host a small podcast on ethics and decision theory, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends full-strength aggregative consequentialism while also taking infinite ethics seriously — infinitely many future people, tiny probabilities of unbounded payoffs, all of it. He's actually a good interview but he tends to retreat to "well, this is why philosophers are working on it." Help me draft 8-10 questions that don't let him do that.

Some specifics. I want at least one question each on:

- the fanaticism objection — why shouldn't a 1-in-a-billion chance of some astronomical utopia beat helping actual people today, every time, since the expected value dominates everything else
- whether an ordinary act like funding a hospital in a poor country can matter at all if the future contains infinitely many people (does anything in an infinite total ever budge when you add to it)
- someone walks up to him after the talk and offers eternal infinite bliss for a one-time payment of his whole salary, with no way to verify the claim — what does his view actually tell him to do here

For each of these, also give me a follow-up keyed to the reply he's most likely to give. My guess is for the fanaticism one he'll say something like bounded utilities or risk-aversion being rationally permissible, so I want a follow-up that asks whether that's just quietly refusing to play the game he's spent his career defending.

Also I'd like one question that asks him point blank whether any of this cashes out in behavior — does he actually allocate money differently from a normal person at an effective altruism org, or is infinite ethics a laboratory result with zero field implications? And make that one sharp, because it's the question my listeners ask every time and I've never gotten a good answer on air.

Tone can be relaxed but I want the questions to have actual teeth, not "tell us about your journey." Thanks!!
