I sit on the editorial board of a mid-tier interdisciplinary journal (psych plus evolutionary biology, that sort of thing), and we keep getting papers that go like this: humans in the ancestral environment faced problem X, therefore trait Y is an adaptation for X, and here's a questionnaire on 200 undergrads thats consistent with Y existing. The latest one argues that gossip is an evolved free-rider-detection mechanism, resting mostly on the finding that people report enjoying gossip about norm violations more than gossip about, say, the weather.

My co-editor has had enough and wants a blanket policy of desk-rejecting anything in this mould — adaptationist psychology isn't science, she says, until it makes risky predictions, it's just stories with a lab coat on. Another board member points out that we happily publish adaptationist work on peacock tails and finch beaks, and nobody calls those just-so stories, so what exactly is the difference? That we can't run experiments on humans? That we can't observe the ancestral environment? Or just that these hypotheses happen to be about us, which makes everyone squeamish?

Draft the memo I'll circulate before thursday's meeting. It needs to reccomend something concrete: blanket rejection, case-by-case judgement, or a stated evidential bar — and if it's the bar, say what the bar is in terms a reviewer can actually apply to a manuscript, not "must be more rigorous". I want the co-editor's worry engaged with seriously, not waved off with the word falsifiability. The hard question, as I see it: is there a principled line between the gossip paper and a perfectly respectable behavioural ecology paper, or is it just degrees of confidence and tidier study design? If some of these papers are salvageable, say what they'd actually need — cross-cultural sampling, phylogenetic comparison, developmental signatures, whatever you think genuinely does the work. And be honest about whether the sceptics are right that the core move, inferring function from the fit between a trait and a hypothesised ancestral problem, is circular in a way the finch work isn't.

Also, no reading lists or name-dropping of authorities please — I want the reasoning, not the bibliography.
