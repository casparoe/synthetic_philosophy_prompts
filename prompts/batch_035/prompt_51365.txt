Write an essay on whether Nozick's tracking theory of knowledge can live with its denial of epistemic closure. Quick setup so we're on the same page: on the tracking view you know p roughly when you'd believe p if it were true and wouldn't believe it if it were false. That handles ordinary cases but breaks closure -- you can know you're looking at a zebra, and know that a zebra entails not-a-painted-mule, yet fail to know it's not a painted mule, since if it were one you'd still believe zebra. Nozick bites the bullet. Here's a twist I want you to deal with specifically: imagine a nature park that, unknown to visitors, has a standing policy of quietly replacing any deceased zebra with a painted mule rather than admit the loss -- but they've never once had to do it, and the paint jobs are detectable under proper lighting. A visitor glances at a zebra standing in the shade and thinks "that's a zebra". Intuitively they know; on tracking, whether they know turns on how near the worlds are where it's a mule, and the park's policy makes those worlds uncomfortably close, even though they never actually instantiate. Weigh the abominable conjunction ("I know it's a zebra, but I don't know it's not a painted mule") against the case for denying closure -- e.g. that closure plus our inability to know we're not deceived skeptically would otherwise sink all knowledge. Also consider whether some restricted closure principle survives on the tracking view, say closure under deductions whose conclusion you'd also track when using the same method, and whether that restriction is principled or ad hoc. Reach a verdict: is denying closure a fatal cost, a manageable bullet-bite, or maybe even a feature? Lead with your conclusion in the very first sentence and then give the reasoning -- no preamble, no restating the question.
