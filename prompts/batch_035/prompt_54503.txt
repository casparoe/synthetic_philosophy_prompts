Write the FAQ and complaints page for the Universe's Thermodynamics Complaints Department — the office where you go to formally object to the Second Law. Deadpan bureaucratic voice throughout, like a local council's website crossed with Ofsted, maybe 700 words, formatted as numbered entries (Q1, Q2, etc.) each being a complaint from a member of the public followed by the Department's reply. british english for the piece please.

The complainants should each embody a different way people get the thermodynamic arrow wrong, and the humour has to come from the physics being right rather than mocked: the replies should never deny anything that's actually true. Suggested letters (you can reshuffle but hit all of these notes):

1. A man who has thoroughly cleaned his flat and now wants the Department to acknowledge that he has reversed entropy, demanding retroactive credit. The reply should concede the local decrease and then itemise the invoice — the Sun underwrote it, your fridge did some subcontracting, the overall books still balance.

2. A Boltzmann brain filing a complaint about the general unreliability of its memories, asking whether it is owed a refund on its past. The reply should struggle here, because the Department's records are themselves just low-entropy traces and the clerk knows it.

3. Someone citing the fluctuation theorem as if it were case law establishing precedent that entropy sometimes decreases, and therefore their iced americano staying cold is not unreasonable but merely improbable. The reply should agree with the maths and gently explain what a waiting time is.

4. A gentleman invoking time-reversal symmetry — he read that the microscopic laws don't prefer a direction, so he'd like the reversal he is owed, in writing, by Friday. The reply should explain that the asymmetry is statistical and that reversing his tea would require the cooperation of roughly every particle in the room.

5. A wellness-life-coach type complaining that the Department keeps rejecting her trademark application for a negentropy smoothie. This one is the target of the satire proper — the replies to her should be the most savage while remaining scrupulously polite.

Somewhere in there the Past Hypothesis has to show up, ideally as an obscure piece of legislation that nobody in the Department can locate the original text of — the low-entropy initial condition everyone works from but no one can produce. Maybe a footnote, maybe a clerk going quiet when asked about it. Don't over-explain the joke.

Constraints: no winking narrator, the piece stays in universe to the end; the clerk is exhausted but never wrong; and at least one reply should make the reader feel the actual vertigo of the Past Hypothesis rather than just laugh at the complainant. If a physicist reads it they should nod along, not wince. Around 700 words, and use proper complaints-department british spellings (procedure, programme, licence etc.) where natural.
