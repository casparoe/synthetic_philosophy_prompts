I'm wrapping up a seminar week on technological determinism. We read Langdon Winner's "Do Artifacts Have Politics?" — the Robert Moses overpass story — and then the rejoinder from the critic who actually went back and checked the bridges and found the whole thing kind of shaky (turns out the numbers don't really support it, buses barely used those roads, there were other ways to the beach). For the last session I want a sonnet in the voice of one of the low overpasses on the Long Island parkway. The octave is the bridge telling Winner's version with total confidence: I decide who fits under me, I keep the buses — and whoever rides them — away from the beaches; politics poured straight into the concrete. The volta is were the historians show up: turns out I maybe wasn't built for that purpose at all, maybe people just told that story about me afterwards. And the closing couplet is the bridge wondering whether it *has* politics or just had politics read into it, and realizing it can't tell the difference from where it stands — since either way the same cars pass underneath it every day. Please make it sound like a bridge talking, not a lectern, and no smugness in either half.
