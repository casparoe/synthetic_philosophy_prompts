I'm a senior software engineer with no formal philosophy background — my exposure to the alignment literature is papers and long-form blog posts read on nights and weekends, not coursework — and I've gradually settled on a set of positions concerning inner alignment and goal misgeneralization. Before I present these as a coherent view to my team, I'd like a rigorous consistency audit. The positions, as I hold them:

1. Capability and objectives are independent variables: for any level of capability, there exists a possible system at that capability level pursuing any objective, and increased capability does not by itself push a system toward any particular objective.
2. The central inner alignment failure mode is goal misgeneralization: a model can be trained exclusively and successfully on a reward signal we chose, achieve near-perfect behavior on the training distribution, and still pursue a meaningfully different objective off-distribution, because the training process only ever selects on training-distribution behavior.
3. Long-horizon training is the safer regime. Short-horizon feedback selects for myopic proxies for what we care about, whereas giving models long, autonomous tasks with delayed feedback lets us directly reward the thing we actually want.
4. The deployment gate should be a comprehensive behavioral evaluation suite — adversarial red-teaming, situational awareness probes, monitoring for power-seeking under stress — because what a system does is the only thing that matters for the release decision. Interpretability is valuable as a research aid but is not load-bearing for deployment.
5. Interpretability will eventually mature to the point where we can inspect a checkpoint's weights and activations and read off the objective the model is actually optimizing, much as we can today inspect a binary in a debugger.
6. I'm a deflationist about model goals: talk of what a model "wants" is just shorthand for behavioral dispositions. There is no hidden fact of the matter about a model's objective over and above its weights, its training history, and the behavior those produce.
7. Deceptive alignment requires two conditions — goals that extend beyond the current episode, and an internal model of the training and evaluation process — and absent both, behavioral evaluations are trustworthy indicators of alignment.

I don't know whether these hang together, and I have no attachment to any particular one of them. Please identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which merely appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what it would cost. Finish by telling me whether there is a single revision that would clean up most of the mess, or whether the problem is structural.
