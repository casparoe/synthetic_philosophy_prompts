Background: I'm a software engineer, zero philosophy training, and I've been going back and forth on what to eat for like two years now. I've read enough random internet arguments about this stuff (the kind where someone in the comments does the math on how many animals die per calorie) that I have actual gut reactions to specific food choices. What I don't have is any idea whether my gut reactions add up to a consistent position. So here are my verdicts, tell me what principle, if any, is behind them:

1. A chicken sandwich is clearly worse than a beef burger. You're killing like a couple hundred chickens worth of animal per cow, and broiler chickens seem to have pretty miserable lives even before slaughter. So beef over chicken, pretty confident.

2. Oysters and mussels are basically fine. I eat them without a second thought. They're barely even animals.

3. Dairy feels okay to me, way better than eating chicken. I know intellectually that dairy cows need to keep having calves and the male ones become veal, and I can feel this verdict wobbling as I type it, but my gut still says milk is fine.

4. Wild-caught fish feels fine, better than farmed. The animal lived a free life, the only bad part is getting caught. Though someone once told me about bycatch in trawling and I didn't like that at all, so maybe trawled fish specifically is bad?

5. Oatmeal, beans, vegetables — obviously fine, no guilt whatsoever. Even though I've seen estimates that harvesters and pesticides shred an absolutely enormous number of field mice, insects, etc. per field per year. This one I cannot reconcile with #1 and it bugs me. If my reason for picking beef over chicken is "more animals suffer," why doesn't that same logic make my oatmeal a small atrocity? Every meal is downstream of agriculture that kills things.

6. Steak from a cow that grazed on pasture that used to be wild habitat feels worse than steak from a cow in a feedlot? No wait, that's not right, the feedlot one feels worse to live in... but the pasture one destroyed a whole ecosystem for cows. Honestly I don't know what my verdict here is, which itself seems like information.

7. Meat from the trash, or roadkill — fine. No new demand created, that animal's suffering already happened, eating it changes nothing.

So: is there one principle that gets all (or most) of these? My friend suggested "minimize total suffering your food causes" but that seems to wreck #5 — or does it, if insects suffer less per individual? Maybe there's a threshold thing, like suffering only counts above some cognitive complexity line, but then #1 might partially collapse if a cow suffers way more per individual than 200 chickens. Another candidate I've half-formed myself is something like "don't pay into systems whose whole business model is breeding suffering into existence," which saves #5 and #7 but might say weird things about #3.

Please actually pick a principle (or two competing ones and tell me which wins), state it precisely enough to make predictions, show me which of my verdicts it captures and which it has to throw out, and then tell me what it says about two cases I haven't given you: eggs (given the male chick culling thing), and whether I should order the beef or the chicken for a big BBQ where I suspect a lot of it's going to end up in the trash anyway. If one of my verdicts has to go, I'd rather know which one and why than keep the whole set by torturing the principle into pretzels.
