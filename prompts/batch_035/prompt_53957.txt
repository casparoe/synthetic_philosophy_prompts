so my roommate Dana chairs the eligibility subcommittee for our city rec league (she got roped into it two years ago and nobody else wanted the job), and last month I got drafted onto it too, so now I actually have to care. most of it is paperwork, but every so often we get a real case, and Dana has this way of ruling on them that everyone on the committee trusts even though nobody can write down the rule she's actually using. we've got a new one coming up in two weeks and I want to walk in with a prediction.

her verdicts so far, as I understand them:

1. a trans woman, 47, transitioned at 30, on hormones 17 years, testosterone solidly in the female range for over a decade — wanted into the women's Premier division (the one with standings and regional playoffs). Dana said no.
2. a trans girl, 15, socially transitioned at 12, puberty blockers from 13, already playing on the girls' Premier squad. Dana said yes.
3. a trans woman, 60, wanted into the women's walking football group — that one's explicitly no scores, "for fun and fresh air" in their own words. Dana said yes.
4. a cis guy, gentle soul, asked to join the women's social badminton because the men's sessions were too agressive for him. Dana said no.

the new case: a cis woman, 33, has been playing women's Premier for years, got flagged when the league started doing blood screening for that division — her testosterone came back way above the female reference range, in the range the medical officer said she'd expect to see from the men's Premier players. she's never transitioned, never been anything but a woman, and as far as anyone knows her puberty was unremarkable. one person on the committee wants to bar her from Premier, another says that's insane, she's not even trans. Dana will have a view and I'd like to predict it before the meeting.

so what rule is she running? or rules — I suspect more than one fits her record, and I'd like those laid out, since they probably disagree about this case. what does each one predict here? give me your best guess at her verdict, but also tell me where that guess is most likely to fail — like, what fact about her record or the new case, if I've gotten it wrong or am about to get it wrong, would flip the prediction. and what further case would actually split the candidate rules apart? we'll get another one of these eventually and I'd like to know what to watch for.

for what it's worth I do NOT want the standard opinion-having about trans people and sports, I can get that anywhere — I want the prediction. if it helps: Dana is the least ideological person I know, she'd rule the same way with the parties swapped.
