everyone in AI safety talks past each other about the orthogonality thesis and I can't tell what the actual thesis is supposed to commit me to. so before I go read another round of takes, I want the spec: what would a theory of the relationship between intelligence and goals in advanced agents have to deliver to count as adequate? Like, it presumably has to say whether the claim is "any level of intelligence is compatible with any goal in principle" or only "goals and intelligence are uncorrelated in practice" — those come apart once you remember we only ever observe agents whose goals came from evolution or human design, so our sample is biased in ways that matter. It has to handle the instrumental convergence stuff without cheating: self-preservation, resource acquisition, goal-content integrity etc. are supposed to follow from almost any goal, but the theory needs to distinguish an instrumental drive from a terminal one, and say why that distinction survives an agent smart enough to revise its own goals. It has to deal with the strongest counterview — that sufficient intelligence somehow constrains the goal space, whether via coherence pressure, or via the idea that a truly general learner trained on our world will end up valuing roughly what we value — and not just dismiss it as anthropomorphism. It has to say something about the coherence objection that expected-utility maximization is itself an idealization that may not describe real agents. And it probably needs to be testable enough that we could in principle tell if it were wrong, or else admit it's not that kind of claim.

So: write me the requirements list, with the motivating case or puzzle behind each one, mark which are non-negotiable and which are basically just what one camp happens to care about (Bostrom-style safety people vs their critics will weight these very differently I suspect), and flag any pair of requirements you suspect can't be jointly satisfied — that's the part I most want to see, since if the list is jointly unsatisfiable that's itself a result about the debate. Then briefly score the orthogonality thesis itself and the best "intelligence constrains goals" counterview against the list, a couple sentences each, I don't need the full scorecard. And since I gather there's real disagreement here even among people who've thought about it for years, tell me how confident you are in the requirements you ended up with, and which single requirement you're least sure actually belongs on the list at all.
