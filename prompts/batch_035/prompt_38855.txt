Michel Foucault died in 1984, long before recommender-driven feeds, gamified productivity apps, and workplace analytics platforms that score employees on metrics like "collaboration," inferred from calendar and communication metadata rather than direct observation. Suppose he were asked to comment on these systems using the conceptual toolkit he had already developed by the early 1980s — disciplinary power, hierarchical observation, the examination and its accompanying documentation, normalization, biopower. Which features of these systems would he most plausibly seize on as vindications of his framework (e.g., individuals rendering themselves legible and comparable without any identifiable watcher), and where would the framework strain? In particular, how might he handle the fact that the standards being enforced are often statistical outputs of optimization processes that no human designer explicitly endorses — does disciplinary power still apply when the "norm" is produced by an opaque mechanism rather than a normalizing institution? Would he be more inclined to side with contemporary critics of corporate surveillance, or to find their privacy-centered framing too juridical for what is actually going on? Please distinguish as clearly as you can between predictions grounded in his documented positions and claims that require extrapolation beyond them.
