I would appreciate help diagnosing an argument for the block universe that a physicist friend pressed on me. It appears to be a version of what I gather is called the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. Each step looks individually unimpeachable to me, yet the conclusion seems to prove far too much: it settles the truth of every future quantum measurement using kinematics alone, with no appeal to dynamics at all. Here is the argument as he gave it to me.

The setup: tonight at midnight I stand still on Earth while a jogger passes me at about 10 km/h. The Andromeda galaxy is roughly 2.5 million light-years away. In special relativity simultaneity is frame-dependent, so the hyperplane of "now" through me at midnight intersects Andromeda at different points in my rest frame and in the jogger's. By the standard tilt arithmetic (velocity times distance over c-squared), his "now" at Andromeda sits roughly nine days later than mine — or earlier, depending on which way he runs.

(1) If an event is determinate for one inertial observer, it is determinate simpliciter. Determinacy cannot vary from frame to frame: to allow that would be to make one frame's verdict authoritative where they disagree, and relativity recognizes no privileged frame.

(2) In my frame, the Andromedan events simultaneous with my midnight are present, and whatever is present is determinate.

(3) So the events of my now-slice at Andromeda are determinate; but the jogger's now-slice, containing Andromedan events nine days later, is present for an observer standing right beside me at the very same moment, and so those events are determinate too.

(4) By chaining observers — each passing me at midnight with a somewhat larger velocity, but every one of them co-located with me at that instant — the "now" at Andromeda can be slid forward by arbitrarily large amounts, up to the light-travel time of about 2.5 million years. Events on Andromeda arbitrarily far in what I must call its future — including the outcomes of quantum experiments its inhabitants have not yet performed, and which no dynamics, deterministic or chancy, is needed to fix — are therefore already determinate.

(5) Nothing in the reasoning was specific to Andromeda. Any event whatever, however far in the future of any given observer, lies on the present hyperplane of some suitably boosted observer somewhere. So every event in spacetime is determinate, and the world is a block in which the future, including each future quantum outcome, is already settled.

The conclusion strikes me as too strong to be established this cheaply: if steps (1) through (5) stand, then quantum indeterminism is an illusion and we have refuted it a priori, without ever looking at a Schrödinger equation. But I cannot find the defective step. My suspicion falls on (1), since that is where the frame-relativity of simultaneity gets converted into frame-independent determinacy. Yet rejecting (1) seems to mean that when two co-located observers disagree about which distant events are present, one of them is simply wrong about a matter of fact — which sounds like a privileged frame under another name, and I want to keep relativity exactly as it is. Rejecting (2) instead looks ad hoc, since the premise that the present is determinate seemed like the one thing everyone from the presentist to the eternalist could agree on.

Which step should I give up, why did it look innocent, and what does rejecting it cost? If you think the argument is actually sound and the block universe simply follows, I would want to hear what I am buying with that — including how to live with the idea that this was settled before I asked.
