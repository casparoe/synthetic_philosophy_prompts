(2) We ship interface changes all the time knowing that rare visual glitches will reach some users, and I don't regard that as a precaution failure. (3) I think the years of delay in deploying golden rice over speculative ecological concerns were indefensible, given that vitamin A deficiency was blinding children the entire time. (4) I think retiring working nuclear plants while coal plants keep running is precaution applied to the wrong risk. (5) At the same time, I think drug regulators are right to demand strong safety evidence before approval even though the delay itself predictably costs lives — and I don't think this is just cases (3) and (4) repeating. (6) I think we should not attempt large-scale solar geoengineering now, even though climate change is doing ongoing damage and this is exactly the sort of ongoing-damage situation that made me side against precaution in (3) and (4). (7) And when a single weak epidemiological study links a food additive to cancer, I think banning it immediately "to be safe" is the wrong reaction. Is there a single principle that captures all of these? If not, say which of my verdicts has to go and what the cheapest repair is, rather than treating them all as equally vulnerable. Please state any principle you propose precisely enough to decide the case I keep going back and forth on: releasing a feature that lets an AI agent send emails on a user's behalf, where we cannot enumerate the failure and misuse scenarios in advance. Finally, tell me honestly whether whatever principle saves my verdicts is still meaningfully a precautionary principle, or just expected-value reasoning with extra steps bolted on. Please answer in American English.
