So I help run a fortnightly meetup that's basically LessWrong-adjacent people in a pub backroom — about fourteen regulars, mostly programmers, plus one poor soul who actually did a philosophy degree and gets dragged into everything. I've been volunteered to run the session next month, and I want to build the whole 90 minutes around the MU puzzle from Gödel, Escher, Bach. Not the puzzle as a puzzle, exactly — the point I want them to actually *feel* is jumping out of the system: that grinding harder inside the rules never gets you to MU, that the only way to settle the question is to step outside and reason about the system rather than within it, and that this move feels like cheating or giving up the first time you make it. Which is, I assume, exactly why Hofstadter built the thing.

So you don't have to guess at my setup, here's the material as I understand it (correct me if I've got a rule wrong): strings made from the letters M, I and U; you start from the axiom MI; four production rules — if a string ends in I you may append a U; if you have M followed by some string x you may extend it to Mxx (doubling the tail); you may replace any occurrence of III with a U; and you may delete any occurrence of UU. The goal is to derive MU, which is provably impossible — the standard proof being that no sequence of moves can ever make the count of I's divisible by three, and MU contains zero I's. Fine, I know the answer. What I don't know is how to *stage* it.

Design me the full session: rules and materials, how to split the time so that people spend a genuinely long stretch grinding inside the system first (the frustration is the point, I don't want to spoil it early), and then how to engineer the moment where someone is allowed or nudged to step outside without me just lecturing at them about metasystems. Include the debrief — how to map what happened onto reasoning within the system versus reasoning about it, and ideally a second act where they apply the invariant trick to some fresh question so it doesn't feel like a one-off party trick. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong when I run it: my bet is that at least two of these people are absurdly fast and will smell the divisibility thing within five minutes, and I need a plan for that which doesn't ruin it for everyone else. And be honest about which feature of JOOTSing a 90-minute session structurally can't reproduce — real formal systems don't come with a printed rulebook, and in actual life you don't get told which system you're currently inside — because I want to know exactly what I'll have to just assert in the debrief rather than demonstrate.
