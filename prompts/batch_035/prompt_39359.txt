You're an expert on AI alignment and evaluation methods, and you also have a philosophy of science background, so you're comfortable going deep on the conceptual stuff. Write a dialogue between two alignment researchers, Dana and Priya, who strongly disagree about whether we should supervise AI systems based on their process (rating the reasoning step-by-step, rewarding sound intermediate moves regardless of whether the final answer turns out right) or based on their outcomes (just score the end result and let the model find whatever path gets it there).

Priya should counter with a process-side pathology she can call "epiphenomenal chain-of-thought" — the claim that once you reward the reasoning, the displayed steps can become causally disconnected from the computation that actually produces the answer, and the model just learns to emit what auditors like to see; on her view, only outcome signal keeps you tethered to whether the reasoning is actually doing any work. At some point one of them should propose a concrete hybrid — e.g., auditing the process on a random 5% sample of trajectories, or using process scores only as a tiebreaker when outcomes are ambiguous — and the other should push back with the objection that a model smart enough to model the audit policy will behave "process-honestly" only on the inputs it predicts will be audited.
