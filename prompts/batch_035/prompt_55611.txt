I'm a PhD student in machine learning working on mechanistic interpretability — probing, sparse autoencoders, activation steering, circuit tracing — and I have only a dilettante's grasp of philosophy of science. I've read enough to know that the question my field keeps half-asking in workshop arguments — when we say a network "has a representation" of some concept, and when our probes and steering vectors are supposed to be detecting it rather than inventing it — is an old question that philosophers of science have fought over for a century in the case of unobservable scientific entities. So I want the translation, done by someone fluent in both directions.

Restate the main positions in the scientific realism debate — the no-miracles argument, the pessimistic meta-induction, van Fraassen's constructive empiricism, entity realism, and structural realism — in the vocabulary I actually use: linear probes and their accuracy, selectivity controls, activation patching and ablation as interventions, features found by sparse autoencoders, steering vectors, circuits, superposition, the linear representation hypothesis, logit-lens-style readouts. Be faithful rather than charitable: where a philosophical position has a natural reading in my terms, give it; where you have to distort it to fit, say so.

Then give me the residue report I care about. Specifically: which parts of the debate survive translation cleanly; which parts only translate if you quietly reinterpret them; and which parts have no expression in my vocabulary at all. For example, I can see that the pessimistic meta-induction might become something like "most concepts that earlier probe-based papers claimed to find did not survive better controls," but I can't see whether constructive empiricism's distinction between believing that a theory is empirically adequate and believing it is true has any counterpart in a field where the model itself is the system under study rather than a theory of it — the bookkeeping of what "observable" even means seems to break when the unobservable entities and the data-generating process are the same object. Similarly for entity realism: the argument that you intervene on an entity to make things happen is often cited in my field to defend steering-based evidence for features, and I want to know whether that is a faithful application or a slogan borrowed out of context.

Finally, tell me where the residue lies. Which parts of what the philosophers argue about have no counterpart in interpretability practice because our vocabulary is missing something, which parts don't survive because they were less substantive than they looked once stated in concrete terms, and — the direction that worries me — which parts of our own practice only look innocuous because the philosophical debate that would expose them never got translated back. I'd rather not have the answer assume realism or anti-realism about representations is correct; I want the translation to be one I could check against my own experiments.
