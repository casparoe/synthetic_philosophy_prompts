So this all started because a friend of mine who grew up in a pretty observant Hindu household keeps claiming that karma is just "moral desert, but consistent." Her point, as best I can reconstruct it: everyone who isn't a total consequentialist has intuitions about desert, but western philosophy basically leaves them hanging — desert is real but the universe never pays out, so it's all promissory. Karma says no, the ledger actually gets settled, maybe over multiple lives. I'm a longtime rationalist-adjacent reader, I think reincarnation is false, and yet I've been finding her framing annoyingly hard to dismiss, so I did what I always do and catalogued my gut verdicts on cases to see if they even form a pattern. Here they are. A war criminal who escapes to another country, lives comfortably, and dies peacefully at 94: something in me insists he didn't get what he was owed, that an injustice occurred, not just an unfortunate distribution. A child born with a painful degenerative disease: she does not deserve it, full stop, and any theory that says otherwise is monstrous. A lottery winner: his windfall isn't deserved, but it also doesn't offend me the way the child's illness does, and I can't fully explain the asymmetry. Two identically drunk drivers, one of whom kills a pedestrian who happens to step out and one of whom gets home fine: I keep wanting to say the first is more blameworthy, even though I know the control principle says otherwise and I mostly believe the control principle. Someone who works hard with mediocre talent and does well: he deserves some of it, but the talent itself was luck, so the desert is diluted in a way I can't make precise. And a person who does something terrible but was genuinely formed by horrific childhood abuse: blame shrinks but never hits zero, no matter how causal the abuse story gets.

So, what's the principle, if any, that captures this mess? I want it stated precisely enough to make predictions, not just vibes. The thing I keep noticing is that my war-criminal intuition seems to require exactly the machinery my friend's karma provides — a real ledger where suffering can be *owed* and unpaid — while my congenital-disease intuition absolutely forbids the other half of that machinery (past-life deeds explaining birth circumstances). Can I keep the "owed suffering" verdict without a cosmic debt collector, or does it collapse into an aesthetic preference for tidier universes once nothing enforces it? Is there an asymmetry between deserving punishment and deserving reward that survives scrutiny, or is that just salience bias? And there's a case I honestly can't call: someone who inherits a fortune their parent stole, gives most of it away, lives modestly on the rest — did they ever deserve any of it, and does it matter that giving it away doesn't restore it to the victims? Tell me which of my verdicts has to go, because I suspect at least one does, and I'd rather know which one than keep asserting all of them. Also if the best principle happens to be "your desert intuitions are all downstream of being a social ape tracking reciprocity," I'll take that answer too, but then explain why the war-criminal case feels metaphysical to me instead of just socially useful.
