I am defending my dissertation in three weeks before a committee and an audience of mainstream analytic metaphysicians, and I need a Q&A prep sheet built around the objections this specific audience will actually raise. My thesis: Bradley's regress is valid as an argument but its conclusion should be embraced rather than avoided. When a fact's constituents are unified by a relation, that the relation unifies them is itself a further fact, and so on without end — and this regress is constitutive rather than vicious. Unity is not conferred by any terminal unifier but by the infinite chain taken as a whole, on analogy with what epistemic infinitists claim about justification: no level is self-supporting, yet the structure is not thereby unexplanatory. The demand for a final unifier that itself needs no unifier — whether a primitive tie, a non-relational exemplification nexus, or a bare "that such-and-such" — rests, I argue, on confusing the demand for analysis with the demand for a terminating item.

The audience matters here. The committee includes a committed truthmaker theorist who thinks infinite chains of facts are exactly the wrong ontology for truthmaking; a Quinean nominalist who will regard my facts as hypostatized true sentences and my regress as a grammatical illusion; a trope theorist who will say compression solves what my infinitism multiplies; and a philosopher of language who suspects the unity problem belongs to representations, not to worldly items, so that my entire project is aimed at nothing. There will also be a junior faculty member deep in the grounding literature, looking for the technical question.

Prepare me accordingly: the objections roughly in the order they will arrive, phrased in the vocabulary each of these people would actually use, ordered by how much damage each could do; for each, a two-minute reply I can deliver at a podium, plus a candid note on where the reply is soft. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks — and I suspect it is the one about whether an infinite chain of facts unifies anything at all, or merely describes an infinite plurality wearing the costume of a single fact.
