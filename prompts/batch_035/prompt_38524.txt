In my philosophy elective we recently covered epiphenomenalism — the view that conscious experiences are real but have no causal influence on the physical world — and I've become stuck on what I've seen called the paradox of phenomenal judgment: if my experiences don't affect anything physical, then my statements about my experiences are produced entirely by physical brain processes, and the experiences themselves seem to do nothing to bring about the very reports that are supposed to be about them. For a creative assignment, I have to write a short story that dramatizes this problem rather than explaining it, and I would like your help. I have in mind a protagonist who is a neuroscientist connected to a machine that predicts, a few seconds in advance, every word she will say about her own conscious states — including her insistence that her experiences are what make her say such things — but you may take a different direction as long as the same problem remains at the center. Please keep the story to roughly 800–1200 words, and do not resolve the problem at the end: no last-minute conversion away from epiphenomenalism, and no convenient discovery that experiences do after all push neurons around. I want the ending to leave the reader as uneasy as the problem leaves me.
