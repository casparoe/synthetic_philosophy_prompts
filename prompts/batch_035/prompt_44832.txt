so my friend and i spent basically the whole evening arguing about schopenhauer's thing where music is the highest of all the arts, and i need you to settle it with a poem. he thinks its just schopenhauer being a gloomy romantic who happened to love music and dressed up his taste in metaphysics. i kept trying to explain that the claim is actually much weirder and more specific than "music is so profound": for schopenhauer every other art copies the world at arm's length, through the Ideas, through images of things, but music skips the middleman entirely -- it's a direct copy of the will itself, the blind striving underneath everything, with no concepts in between. and there's all this concrete structural stuff in the famous music section (§52): the bass as the inorganic ground everything else stands on, the inner voices as the middle grades of nature, the melody up top as one individual human life wandering off, getting delayed, always eventually pulled back toward the tonic. even dissonance is doing metaphysical work for him -- a dissonance straining toward its resolution is basically what wanting-and-briefly-getting feels like from the inside.

anyway, he was not buying any of it. his position was that the metaphysics does no actual work, its just a metaphor a music-lover makes after the fact, and i want to prove him wrong by showing the idea can carry a structure. so: write me a sonnet about it. use the octave to build up the ordinary picture of the arts, each of them once removed from the world, images of images, copies of copies -- and then make the volta do the real work, the turn where music gets excepted from all of that, where it stops depicting and starts being. the closing couplet should land the specific claim -- a copy of the will itself, not of any Idea -- and ideally get the dissonance/resolution thing in there so a dissonance resolving literally enacts willing and satisfaction rather than just illustrating it.

two constraints. first, i want someone who has actually read the music section to read this and nod, not wince -- the bass-as-ground and melody-as-one-striving-life stuff should come through accurately, not just vibe. second, someone who has never read a word of schopenhauer should still be able to read it as a genuinely good poem about music, no required reading. and please dont let it collapse into "life is suffering, the will is bad, ahh" -- that was my friends other cheap shot and honestly the least interesting part of schopenhauer, and this poem shouldnt be about resignation at all, its about the strange fact that one art gets to be the thing itself instead of a picture of the thing.
