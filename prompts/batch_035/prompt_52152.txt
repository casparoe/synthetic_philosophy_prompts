Pretend you're a philosopher of science who moonlights writing satire for consumer-review sites, and you've read your Dennett very closely. Write a Yelp-style review page, around 600 words, for a fictional company called "Skyhooks & Cranes Ltd." that sells metaphorical lifting equipment for explaining complexity — minds, life, language, the eye, whatever. The one-star reviews should come from crane customers complaining about assembly being required: the crane arrives as a heap of parts, has to be assembled by earlier cruder cranes, comes with a warranty clause about "gradual accumulation over deep time," no shortcuts, no results overnight. The five-star reviews come from skyhook devotees: the product arrives fully assembled, hangs from nothing, needs no origin story, and explains design in one step with no questions asked — one of these reviewers should be recognizably an intelligent-design proponent, though don't name any real person. Include a deadpan "response from the owner" defending the skyhook product line (e.g., that their premium skyhook is the only tool that can explain the cranes). At least one review must be from a customer who used the crane greedily — skipped straight from particle physics to chess openings — and got told by customer support that the intermediate levels are load-bearing, so greedy use voids the warranty. The humor has to come from actually getting the cranes-vs-skyhooks distinction and greedy vs. non-greedy reductionism right, so a philosopher who knows Darwin's Dangerous Idea laughs rather than winces. Start by brainstorming four or five review premises and pick the best ones, keep the whole thing deadpan, and don't wink at the reader anywhere.
