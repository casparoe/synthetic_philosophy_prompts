I am a postdoc in behavioral economics putting together a one-year grant application, and I need you to turn a half-formed idea into a proper proposal. Here is the idea. The classic results on information cascades and rational herding rest on an assumption nobody spends much time defending: that agents act in some given order and observe only what their predecessors did, not when or why they did it. But in many real settings the order of action is chosen, not imposed. A consumer deciding whether to adopt a product, a depositor deciding whether to withdraw, a reviewer deciding whether to post a rating can all simply wait. And if others can see that they are waiting, the waiting itself becomes a signal: someone who hangs back when acting is cheap may be revealing that their private information is weak or absent. My suspicion is that once timing is endogenous and observable, the logic of cascades partly undoes itself, since the very silence that sustains a cascade becomes informative, and forward-looking agents should account for that. What I want funded is a combined theory-and-experiment project asking whether information cascades are robust to endogenous timing, and, if they are not, what institutional details, such as deadlines that force simultaneous decisions, opaque order of action, or visible queues, restore them or break them further. Please write the two-page proposal. It should state the question precisely enough that it has a determinate answer, explain why the standard cascade results leave it open, describe what I would actually do month by month, including the theoretical model I would build first and the laboratory experiments that would follow, with the treatments contrasted so that a reader can see what would count as the cascade surviving and what would count as it collapsing. Include an honest account of what could make the project fail or turn out to be trivial, and a short note on why the answer matters outside the lab, for instance for product review systems or runs on financial platforms. Do not include citations; I will fill those in myself. Please write the proposal as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the style of a formal grant application.
