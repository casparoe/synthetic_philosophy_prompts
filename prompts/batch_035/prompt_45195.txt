My friend from a philosophy of physics reading group has been issuing verdicts on laws-of-nature cases all term, and at the next session I get to pose the new case. Her track record so far:

1. A world exactly like ours except for a single collision in which momentum fails to be conserved, with no ripple effects thereafter: its laws are the same as ours.
2. A world containing no magnetic monopoles, where the cheapest completion of the systematization adds the claim that no monopole carries charge above the Dirac value: that uninstantiated claim is a law of that world.
3. A world whose regularities switch wholesale halfway through its history: it has laws, indexed to epochs, rather than no laws.
4. A world whose history is a gerrymander through and through — no compressible structure at any scale, no stable frequencies anywhere: it has no laws at all.

The case I'm planning to pose: is that fourth world — the very mosaic she has already called lawless — possibly a world whose laws are just our probabilistic laws, with every chance misfiring in one colossal cosmic fluke? Could the frequencies of a world sit nowhere near its chances at any point in its history?

I care less about her eventual yes or no than about the rules she might be running — a best-system view, a primitive governing-laws view, a necessitation view, or something else — and about which of her four verdicts fit which rule. Please predict her answer to the fluke case under each rule that fits her record, give your best guess at what she will actually say along with your reasons, and identify the single further case that would best tell the surviving rules apart.

Please answer as a JSON object with the following fields: "candidate_rules", an array of objects each with "name", "statement", and "fit" (which of verdicts 1-4 it accommodates and any it violates); "predictions", an object mapping each surviving rule to its verdict on the fluke case with a one-line rationale; "best_guess", an object with "verdict", "rule", and "reasons"; and "discriminating_case", an object with "description" and "divergence" (how the surviving rules come apart on it).
