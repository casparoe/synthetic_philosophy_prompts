I am a retired physical chemist who has lately taken up philosophy of well-being in retirement, and I find that my old professional habit of decomposing an uncertain estimate into separable error terms has followed me into the new subject. Here is the proposition I want you to put a number on: a person voluntarily enters a flawless experience machine as a young adult, never suspects for a moment that anything is artificial, and lives out a full life inside it; that life contains, subjectively, exactly the experiences — love, work, discovery, daily pleasures and travails — that the corresponding real life would have contained from the inside. My question: what is your credence that this person's life contains strictly less well-being than the corresponding veridical life would have?

The standard verdict is that the machine life is worse, and this verdict is presumably what powers the argument against hedonism: if experiential quality were all that mattered, the two lives would be equal, and almost nobody says equal. But I have read that when survey respondents are asked whether they would stay in the machine if told they are already in one — with the "real" world on offer being the one they believe they have always known — many people's preferences weaken or even reverse. That looks to a scientist like a confound: the intuition may be tracking attachment to the status quo rather than any insight about the good, which would discount its evidential value considerably.

So I want a credence with its derivation, not a bare number. Please decompose the question into the terms you think it depends on — at minimum something like: the probability that a broadly hedonistic theory of well-being is correct; conditional on hedonism being false, the probability that whatever else matters (achievement, authenticity, some relation to how things actually are) is genuinely diminished by non-veridicality rather than only by how things appear to the subject; and the weight the anti-machine intuition deserves given the possibility that it is a psychological artifact rather than a datum about the good. Assign each term a rough probability, give one line of justification for each, combine them, and state the resulting credence. Then tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or finding would move that term the most. Please keep the entire response under 400 words.
