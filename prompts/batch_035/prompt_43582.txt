I keep running into a pattern in democratic theory and I can't find a name for it. On the board of my housing cooperative I've noticed that whenever we make a decision that touches outsiders — expanding onto the neighboring lot, changing rules that our subtenants live under — the question of whether those outsiders should get a say is itself settled by a vote of current members, so the existing franchise always ratifies itself. Reading up on this, I learned that democratic theory has a "boundary problem" about who belongs to the demos, and that one popular answer is the all-affected principle: everyone affected by a decision should be entitled to vote on it. But the principle seems to turn on itself. A decision about who is affected — and hence who votes — affects people too, since being excluded from the vote is itself a harm, so the meta-decision about the franchise should presumably be made by an even wider circle, and widening the circle affects yet others in turn. Is there an established name for this regress, where self-applying the all-affected principle yields a franchise that keeps expanding and may never terminate, and for the mirror-image fact that any actual procedure has to truncate the regress at some arbitrary point, which then determines everything? If there is no established concept, please coin one and define it precisely, and distinguish it from the general boundary problem, from constitutional circularity (the people who ratify a constitution are constituted by that very constitution), and from the chicken-and-egg character of constituent power. I'm not after a bare label; I want to know what exactly the regress consists in, whether and how it can be stopped without arbitrariness, and where its edges are.
