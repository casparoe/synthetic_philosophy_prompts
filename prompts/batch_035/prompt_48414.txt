I'm hoping you can give me some feedback on an essay I've drafted. To set the scene: I never studied philosophy — I run a small hardware store and my last formal course of any kind was a community college accounting class — but for the past two years I've been chewing on a question about food ethics, and I've written up my conclusions as a piece I'd like to publish on my personal blog, which a few dozen friends and regulars read. Before I post it, I want someone to tell me honestly whether the reasoning holds up, because I've convinced myself of something that sounds like a rationalization, and I'd like to know if it is one.

The essay's thesis is that if you care about animals and are going to keep eating meat anyway, you should eat beef and give up chicken, and that this might even beat some plant-based options. My notes run roughly as follows. First, the arithmetic of lives: one cow yields hundreds of pounds of meat, so a family eating only beef for a year might account for a fraction of a single animal's death, while the same family eating chicken at the same rate would account for dozens of birds — so per meal, chicken is far worse on any count-of-animals basis. Second, the arithmetic might need adjusting for the animals themselves: a cow lives longer and may have a richer mental life than a chicken, so maybe one cow's suffering counts for more than one chicken's, which would eat into beef's advantage, though I doubt it eats in enough to flip the ranking. Third, the part I'm least sure of: crop cultivation kills wild animals — animals crushed by harvesters, poisoned by pesticides, or displaced when land is plowed — so a vegetarian diet isn't bloodless either, and I suspect that grass-finished beef, raised on pasture that isn't repeatedly tilled and sprayed, might involve fewer deaths per calorie than a diet built on intensively farmed grains and vegetables. Fourth, a wrinkle a friend raised: most beef is grain-finished, meaning cows are fattened on farmed crops for months, so the crop deaths I was crediting against the vegetarian diet are hidden inside beef too, and I have no idea how the numbers balance out. And fifth, a doubt that runs under everything: I've heard it said that one person's purchases are too small to change what farmers produce, since a supermarket reorders in batches, so maybe nothing I choose matters at all.

What I'd like from you are comments in the style of a careful reader rather than a referee: tell me which parts of the argument are sound, which are shaky, and where the single weakest link is if I had to fix one thing. In particular, I'd like your view on whether the wild-animal part is doing real work or whether I'm using it as a convenient excuse to keep eating what I like; on whether my friend's point about grain-finishing collapses the beef-versus-vegetables comparison; and on whether the "my choices are too small to matter" doubt is a reason to ignore the whole essay or just a caveat to add to it. Please write your comments in American English, and feel free to be blunt — I would rather publish something defensible than something flattering to my current habits.
