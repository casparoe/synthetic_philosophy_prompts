my friend hit me with this after philosophy club and I've been stuck on it for a week. we were talking about Moore's paradox, the whole "it's raining but I don't believe it" thing, and he says the paradox actually proves way more than people admit. his argument:

(1) Nobody can believe "p, but I don't believe p." Not because it's false — the sentence could totally be true, it's true of me right now about tons of stuff I don't believe. It's that believing it would mean believing p while also believing that you don't believe p, and those two can't live together in one head.
(2) So nobody who believes p can also believe that they don't believe p.
(3) Now take anything I believe, like that dogs bark. Either I believe that I believe it, or I believe that I don't believe it, or I've just never formed any belief about whether I believe it.
(4) The second option is ruled out by (2).
(5) The third option is out too: if I really believe that dogs bark, then the second you ask me "do you believe that dogs bark?" I answer instantly, and someone who had genuinely never considered the question couldn't do that. So a real belief always comes with an opinion about its own existence.
(6) So whenever I believe anything, I believe that I believe it. Beliefs are self-transparent.
(7) And if beliefs are self-transparent, introspection can't be wrong about what I believe — asking myself is just reading off the belief itself. So nobody can ever be mistaken about their own beliefs.
