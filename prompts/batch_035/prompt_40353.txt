I'm a grad student and next month I'm giving a talk at a regional hospice and palliative care conference. The audience: mostly chaplains (the majority Christian and trained in CPE, plus a rabbi, an imam, and one Zen chaplain), a bunch of grief counselors, and a few palliative docs who show up to these things for the CEUs. My thesis is that Zhuangzi's response to death is more than a fun bit of comparative religion — it's a usable pastoral and clinical resource for secular and doubting patients, not just a curiosity for the philosophy crowd.

Sketch of my argument, so the prep sheet can target it: (1) the scene where he drums on a tub and sings after his wife dies is almost always retold as cold detachment, but the text itself has him say he grieved like anyone else at first, and only eased off after tracing her back through the phases of the qi transformation, life and death coming on each other like the seasons. So read properly the point isn't suppressing grief, it's letting it move through and out — which is basically continuing-bonds grief work minus the metaphysics, and I want to say that's a resource, not a rival. (2) The skull dialogue, where being dead comes out sounding nicer than a lifetime of anxiety and grinding, can be reframed as a death-anxiety exposure exercise, and the chaplains already do something structurally similar with psalms about sleep and rest.

Give me a Q&A prep sheet: the eight or so objections this audience will actually raise — from the Christian chaplains, both the worry about what happens to hope when you strip out resurrection and the suspicion that I'm evangelizing for secularism under another name; from the Zen chaplain, the line that this is watered-down Buddhism and that Zhuangzi never had to sit with a dying patient at 3am; from the grief counselors, the request for outcome evidence; from the docs, probably something short and pointed. Order them by how much damage they'd do, phrase each the way that person would actually put it, give me a two-minute reply for each, and flag where the reply is soft. Also tell me what I should concede up front for credibility. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks.
