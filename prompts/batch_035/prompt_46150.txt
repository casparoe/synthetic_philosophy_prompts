Retired physical chemist here — spent 35 years on reaction dynamics and nonequilibrium statistical mechanics, and now that the lab is someone else's problem I've fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole. Been reading about Newcomb's problem and I can't shake the feeling I've seen this movie before. You've got a predictor that scans you, fills box B conditionally, and then you choose one or two boxes. The "evidential" folks say one-box because people who one-box tend to walk away rich — that's just conditioning on an observation, fine. The "causal" folks say two-box because your choice now can't causally reach back into what's already in the box — fine too, that's an independence assumption. And they've been fighting about this for decades, which honestly baffles me, because in my world we settled this fight and it wasn't even that hard.

So here's my ask. Restate the one-boxer/two-boxer debate (evidential vs causal decision theory, and throw in whatever the "functional" or "updateless" positions are if they matter) in the vocabulary I actually use: ensemble averages versus single realizations, correlation functions versus perturbing the system, measurement back-action, preparing a state versus measuring it, open versus closed systems, that whole toolkit. Be as faithful as you can. In particular I want to know whether the predictor's accuracy is best thought of as a measurement that back-acts on me (in which case one-boxing is like not measuring a fragile state you want to preserve... or something, I haven't fully worked this out), or whether it's more like the choice being correlated with the box contents through a common cause, which would make the two-boxer right that the correlation is spurious for purposes of acting.

Then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what you had to fudge, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. And tell me which way the residue points — is my toolkit missing something the philosophers can see, or does the decades-long fight mean their disagreement is less substantive than it looks? I have a suspicion it's the latter, that if you forced everyone to write down a proper partition function over (my choice, predictor's prediction, box contents) with the right conditional independencies, the disagreement would evaporate — but I'm aware that's exactly the kind of thing someone says when they learned their hammer at 25 and are now seeing nails everywhere at 70. So push back on me where I deserve it. And be honest about whether "me at the moment of choosing" is even a well-defined system boundary in this setup, because that's the part that keeps me up at night.
