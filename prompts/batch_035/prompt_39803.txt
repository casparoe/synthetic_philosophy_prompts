Been arguing with a friend from my EA-adjacent rationalist discord about longtermism, and at some point he dropped the Keynes one-liner "in the long run we are all dead" to argue that worrying about the far future is basically a category error -- if humanity won't be around to enjoy it, the far future can't matter much, so resources should go to people who exist now. I know this line is associated with Keynes (I believe it's from his Tract on Monetary Reform, where if I remember right the surrounding passage is about economists who tell you the storm will pass and the ocean will be flat again -- he was making a point about short-run stabilization policy, not about the moral status of future people). But in the wild it's fully detached from all that and gets used for everything from YOLO-ish personal hedonism to dismissing existential risk work to justifying short-termist corporate governance. It feels like a slogan that's doing five different jobs at once and nobody notices the switches.

I want to treat this the way people on LessWrong do with "the map is not the territory": take the sentence, lay out the distinct precise theses it could be expressing, from the totally trivial to the actually radical, and then check which readings are true, which are false, and which would actually support the conclusion it's being used for in my friend's argument. Some candidate readings I half-see: (a) a descriptive claim that economists' long-run equilibrium analysis is useless for policy because the short run is where people actually live; (b) a moral claim about the non-identity or the irrelevance of un-lived-in futures; (c) a prudential claim about discounting in decision-making; (d) something like a tu quoque about how even longtermist forecasters are stuck in their own short run. But I suspect I'm missing some and mis-slicing others.

Please act as a patient philosopher-economist who knows the Keynesian context but is primarily a precisification specialist. Think carefully and maybe brainstorm a bit before committing, then give me the full menu of readings as a list I can literally paste back into the discord thread, each with: what it claims, whether it's trivial/substantive/defensible/false, and -- crucially -- whether it supports my friend's anti-longtermist use or whether the argument was quietly coasting on a reading Keynes wouldn't have endorsed. Also flag which reading is probably the one people *actually* mean when they say it in arguments like ours. And if some reading is interesting but wrong in a way worth being wrong, say that too.
