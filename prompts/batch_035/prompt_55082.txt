My brother-in-law Dave is the family's designated rationality guy — reads all the decision-theory books, and over the years he's issued rulings on basically everyone's dilemmas. Well now it's my turn to bring him one, and honestly I'd like to walk into that conversation already knowing what he's going to say.

His track record, as best I can reconstruct it: he walked out of a movie he'd paid $18 for and said anyone who sits through a bad movie because of the ticket price is being a sucker. He told my cousin, in year five of her six-year PhD, to just drop out rather than "finish what she started" — the four years are gone either way, he said, and every extra miserable semester is a brand new cost you're actively choosing. When our uncle kept pouring money into a hardware store he'd spent twenty years building even though it had been losing money for a while, Dave told him to close it, that the twenty years of sweat don't obligate him to sweat more. BUT: Dave has gone to the same Thursday trivia night for nine years now, even though he freely admits it's gotten kind of boring, because "we've built something there" — his team, the bartender, the whole ritual. And when my aunt floated finally selling the lakeside cabin her late husband poured fifteen summers into (the family barely uses it and it costs like $4k a year to keep), Dave was weirdly adament that they hold onto it.

Okay, my case. Two years ago I prepaid a nonrefundable $2,600 for three years of classes at a jiu-jitsu gym (long story, promo rate). My enthusiasm has cooled and I've gotten seriously into woodworking, which is what I actually want to do with my evenings now. The gym won't transfer or refund anything. There IS a belt test in five months though — if I keep showing up, even at maybe half my old frequency, I'd likely make blue belt, which was literally the goal I signed up with. My wife says I'll hate myself for letting the $2,600 "be for nothing" and that Dave will say the same thing.

So before I call him: what's Dave going to rule on the gym thing? What rule do you think he's actually running, and what other rules fit his whole record just as well — and do those rules agree or disagree about my case? Give me your best guess with your reasoning, and then tell me what case, real or hypothetical, would best split the candidate rules apart, because I want to test him with it over thanksgiving.
