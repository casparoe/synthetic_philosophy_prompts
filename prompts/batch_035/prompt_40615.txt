In my philosophy of economics seminar the debate over revealed preference and the meaning of utility has stalled in a way my students find genuinely puzzling, and I want them to see the structure of it rather than take sides. The economists' line is that utility is nothing over and above an index representing choices: ordinal utility is whatever quantity rationalises an agent's demand behaviour, and asking what utility 'really is' beyond that is a confusion left over from cardinal, hedonistic days. The critics reply that if utility is defined purely by choices, welfare economics quietly becomes descriptive bookkeeping — the claim that a policy makes people better off reduces to the claim that people choose it, which is normatively interesting only if choices track well-being, and the theory goes idle at exactly the point where it was supposed to earn its keep. Behavioural economics has sharpened rather than settled this: the gap Kahneman draws between decision utility and experienced utility, together with the framing and inconsistency results, hands the critics their best ammunition, but it also hands economists tools for reconstructing an agent's 'true' preferences from distorted choices — a move critics accuse of smuggling in a substantive standard of well-being the theory was meant to do without. My working hypothesis, which I offer as a hypothesis, is that the two parties mean different things by 'preference': on one side stable dispositions to choose, on the other settled judgements about what is good for the agent, and that once this is noticed the disagreement is partly verbal but leaves a substantive residue about which notion, if either, welfare economics should be built on. Please assess that diagnosis. Using what each side actually says, tell me whether the dispute is verbal, substantive, or a case of two projects — a representation theorem and a theory of well-being — sharing one vocabulary; identify what, if anything, would count as progress (a distinction, an empirical finding, a reconstruction of welfare economics itself); and say honestly whether the impasse reflects a question that is ill-posed or merely one that is hard. Please write in British English.
