I'd like your credence, decomposed into its component parts, on a central claim of Schopenhauer's aesthetics: that music differs in kind from all the other arts because it does not, like them, merely copy the Ideas — the grades at which the Will objectifies itself in the world — but is a copy of the Will itself, so direct that Schopenhauer can suggest the world should be called embodied music just as much as embodied Will, and that a completed philosophy of music would amount to a completed philosophy of the world.

To be precise about the proposition I want a number for: not the full metaphysical thesis, but the structural claim that does the real work underneath it — namely, that the internal dynamic of music (striving, tension, dissonance resolving into consonance, the rising and falling shape of a melody as a portrait of desire and its satisfaction) is isomorphic to the structure of willing itself, in a way the representational arts necessarily cannot be, and that this isomorphism explains music's peculiar power over the listener.

Please decompose your credence along at least the following lines, giving a rough probability and a one-line justification for each:

1. That Schopenhauer's underlying metaphysics — the world as representation, plus a blind, striving Will as its inner nature — is roughly true.
2. That the copy claim is even coherent: whether something non-spatial and non-temporal can be copied at all, or whether the thesis necessarily collapses into a metaphorical analogy between formal structures.
3. That the weaker structural claim — an isomorphism between musical dynamics and the phenomenology of desire, tension, and release — is correct, and that this is what Schopenhauer's thesis is genuinely pointing at, independently of whether his metaphysics survives.
4. That music's exceptional status among the arts is best explained by that isomorphism, rather than by rival naturalistic explanations: conditioning, learned schematic associations, physiological entrainment, or the statistical learning of expectation.

Then explain how the terms combine — in particular whether the strong thesis can fail while the structural claim stands in weakened form, and what the conditional structure should be. State which term you are least confident in and why.
