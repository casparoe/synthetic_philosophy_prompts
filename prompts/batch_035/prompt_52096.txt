ok so for a Wittgenstein reading group I've been asked to write up where I stand on the family resemblance / language games stuff so the others can poke holes in it, and before I send it out I want to know if it even hangs together. here's my position:

1. The family resemblance diagnosis is just correct: there's no common essence shared by everything we call a game, and it's not just "game" — art, religion, democracy, basically our whole everyday vocabulary works this way.
2. That said, I don't take Wittgenstein to have been offering a general theory of concepts. The point was local and therapeutic — aimed at a specific picture (the Augustinian one, and the craving for generality he complains about) that traps philosophers, not a contribution to cognitive science.
3. My reason for believing (1) is basically inductive: people have been trying to define "game" for a long time and every attempt has failed, so at some point you conclude the concept just has no essence to define.
4. Family resemblance concepts are NOT vague concepts. "Game" is not like "heap" — chess is clearly a game, solitaire is clearly a game, and I don't think there are serious borderline cases at all. Lacking an essence is a different thing from having fuzzy boundaries; you can draw a boundary for a special purpose, the point is just that none was already drawn.
5. I also think prototype theory in psychology is pretty much the same insight arrived at independantly, so in a way the empirical work vindicates Wittgenstein.
6. And on applying it: I'm still pretty confident a slot machine session isn't really a game, and neither is one of those walking-sim videogames with no objectives. They can resemble paradigm games as much as they want — resemblance isn't everything.

Some of this feels like it might be pulling in different directions but I genuinely can't see where. Which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest fix for the real ones? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad.
