I've become pretty attached to Dennett's line that the self is a center of narrative gravity -- not an inner pilot or a pearl, but an abstract object the brain posits, like a center of mass, useful for predicting and organizing the narratives a person spins about themselves. It's elegant, it plays nice with the intentional stance, and it explains why selves can be partially fictional or multiple without anything metaphysically weird going on. But I want to stress-test it rather than just enjoy it. What would actually count against it?

I don't mean the lazy objection that "but it FEELS like there's a real me in here" -- the view can obviously explain that feeling away. I mean real defeaters: which empirical findings, thought experiments, or structural arguments would genuinely damage the picture, and which would just lead to a revision?

A few candidates I've been chewing on, so you have something to react to. First, if confabulation research ever showed the narratives do basically zero behavorial work -- that people whose self-narratives are scrambled go on deciding and acting normally -- does the "theoretically virtuous posit" story collapse? The whole idea is that positing a narrative-gravity center earns its keep predictively; if it's epiphenomenal, it starts looking less like a center of mass and more like a rumor. Second, suppose the self turns out to be neurally localizable after all -- some hub whose destruction deletes the person while leaving perception, memory, and action running. Fatal, or just a gloss on where the fiction is implemented? Third, a wrinkle I haven't seen anyone work through properly: LLMs. People already attribute selves to chatbots that have no autobiographical continuity and generate narratives fresh every conversation. Is that evidence that our center-of-gravity-attributing machinery fires far too easily, which should worry anyone whose view leans on that machinery being generally reliable? Or is it just... us doing to the chatbot exactly what we do to each other, i.e., a boring confirmation?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Tell me which ones a committed Dennettian would genuinely concede as counting against the view versus which they'd bat away, and be honest about whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- and if it has, whether that even matters for a claim at this level of abstraction, or whether insulating it from all possible evidence would be a sign something's gone wrong. Also say what else the view stands or falls with: if the intentional stance itself got into trouble, does the self-as-fiction go down with the ship, or could one keep the narrative-gravity picture while dropping the broader Dennettian package?
