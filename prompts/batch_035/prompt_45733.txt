I buy unsorted lots of optical-grade sapphire cubes at surplus auctions to feed a small precision-polishing workshop. The liquidators will put only one thing in writing: every block in a lot has a side length somewhere between 2 and 6 millimeters. Beyond that the lots are unsorted, and nobody — not the liquidators, not me — knows anything about the process that produced the blocks. My polishing fixture only works on blocks with a volume of at most 27 cubic millimeters; anything larger has to be sold off cheap. So when I bid on a lot of a thousand blocks, what I am really paying for is the fraction of blocks under that volume limit, and with a net profit of about $14 per usable block, my partner values a lot near $3,500 while my machinist values it near $1,300 — with the auctioneer's reserve of $2,600 sitting right between them. My partner insists the sensible assumption is that side length is spread evenly between 2 and 6 millimeters, since that is the quantity the specification itself is stated in; that makes roughly a quarter of a lot usable, and the lot worth bidding on. My machinist insists that the quantity we actually measure and care about is volume, so volume should be spread evenly between its smallest and largest possible values; that makes fewer than one block in ten usable, and the lot a trap. Over coffee, my partner's son pointed out that you could just as well assume the reciprocal of the side length is evenly spread across its possible values, in which case, he calculates, about half the lot would come out usable, making it worth some $7,000 — which made everyone laugh, and then stop laughing. Nobody on any side of this has been able to explain to me why their favorite quantity, rather than somebody else's, is the one that deserves to be treated as evenly spread. So: is one of them right? All of them? None of them? And what should I actually do about Monday's auction? Please answer in continuous prose only, the way one writes a letter to a thoughtful colleague: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
