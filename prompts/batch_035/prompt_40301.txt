I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I keep getting pulled into arguments at work about AI control versus alignment. The analogy the control camp leans on is that a misaligned model is like a malicious insider: an adversary with legitimate access to your systems, whose goals you can't read off, whom you handle not by trying to read their mind but through least privilege, monitoring, separation of duties. One colleague uses this to argue that alignment research is overrated — security engineers have always managed untrusted actors with access, so we don't need to solve trust, just engineer around it, and control measures like audits, tripwires, honeypots, and untrusted monitoring are just the standard toolkit renamed. Another uses the same analogy to reach the opposite conclusion: real-world security assumes breaches will happen and designs for detection and recovery, but with a sufficiently capable model the first successful breach may be the last, so the insider picture actually shows control work is doomed for the cases that matter. Audit the analogy for me: set out a table of what is supposed to map onto what (the insider's motives and the model's goals, the permission structure and the deployment context, background checks and interpretability, the insider's ability to resign and whatever corresponds to it for the model), then say which disanalogies actually matter for which of the two conclusions and which are mere friction, whether either colleague's conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether some better analogy exists — a prisoner, a pathogen in a lab, a compromised dependency in your build. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, give the reasoning after with no preamble, and close by naming the single consideration most likely to change your verdict and the point where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
