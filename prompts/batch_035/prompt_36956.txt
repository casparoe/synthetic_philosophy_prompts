I am a philosophy professor who teaches a graduate seminar on theories of history. In a former career I was an applied mathematician working on nonlinear dynamics, and I am more fluent in limit cycles, bifurcation parameters, relaxation oscillations, hysteresis, and basin boundaries than I am in German idealism. I want the great cyclical theories of history — Polybius's anacyclosis, Ibn Khaldun's dynastic cycle driven by group solidarity ('asabiyya), Spengler's civilizational morphology, and Toynbee's challenge-and-response — restated in my dynamical-systems vocabulary, one by one, as faithfully as that vocabulary allows.

For each theory, do the following. Identify the state variables: what, exactly, is the quantity that grows and decays? Identify the attractor structure and the control parameters, and say what mechanism drives the return trip — is this a genuine limit cycle, a relaxation oscillation with slow accumulation and fast release, a transient that merely looks periodic, or something with no dynamical-systems counterpart at all? Where a theory is built from discrete qualitative stages rather than continuous variables — Polybius's sequence of six regimes, Spengler's seasons of a culture — say whether the stage structure maps onto anything in my vocabulary, or whether discretizing a trajectory is a distortion of the theory rather than a translation of it.

Then give me the residue report. Three items in particular. First, numerology: Ibn Khaldun ties the dynasty's arc to roughly four generations from hard-won solidarity to decadence, and Spengler assigns his high cultures a lifespan of about a millennium. Does specific periodicity translate as anything respectable in a dynamical model, or is it precisely the part that evaporates on contact — and if it evaporates, what is left of a cycle claim with no period? Second, agency: Toynbee's creative minority responds to a challenge, and the response is in some sense not determined by the stimulus. Where does that go in a phase-space picture — is it a noise term, a parameter the system itself sets, or nothing at all? Third, reflexivity: Polybius's readers can locate themselves in his cycle and legislate against the stage they see coming; Ibn Khaldun's dynasts can, and did, read Ibn Khaldun. Can a dynamical-systems description say anything about a system whose trajectories depend on the agents' beliefs about the trajectories, or does the vocabulary silently assume an observer standing outside the system?

Finally, the judgment call, which is what I actually care about. Does the residue show that my vocabulary is missing something the cyclical theorists were genuinely tracking, or that their claims were never as precise as they sounded — extended organic metaphors doing the work of models? I want the cases distinguished, not lumped: my working suspicion is that Polybius and Ibn Khaldun are closer to being proto-dynamical models, sparse but real, while Spengler and Toynbee are morphological metaphors wearing the costume of mechanism. Argue with that suspicion rather than ratifying it. Do not give me a reading list or cite chapter and verse; reconstruct the positions from their structure and argue from that.
