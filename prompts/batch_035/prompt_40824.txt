I'm a TA for a large intro philosophy of mind course, and every year the unit on the Cartesian theater goes the same way: the prof demos the color phi phenomenon (a red dot flashes, then a green dot appears a bit to the side, and subjects swear they saw a red thing move across the screen and change color midway, at positions where literally nothing was displayed), and half the class decides Dennett is right mostly because he's funnier than everyone else. I want to do better this year with a decision-tree handout that sorts students by what they actually say about the cases, not by which author they like the sound of.

The tree should be built from cases, not names. The phi case, plus the cutaneous rabbit (rapid taps at the wrist and elbow get experienced as a regular sequence marching up the forearm, with the "in-between" taps felt at locations that were never touched, and apparently before the later physical taps even happen), plus the general Dennettian question of what a probe at different points in time would reveal about when the experience "occurred." Each node should be something a student with zero reading can answer from their own intuitions — e.g., "in the phi case, is there a fact about when the color change you experienced actually happened, in the brain or anywhere?", "if the subject's later reports are our only access, does that settle what was experienced, or can the reports themselves be wrong about the subject's own experience?", "is there any difference between the experience and what the subject would sincerely report about it?" The leaves I need are: Cartesian dualism (the show happens in a nonphysical place), the "second theater" view Dennett is really attacking (some later, central brain event is where it all finally comes together for an audience), his multiple drafts model (no fact of the matter about the time or place at which content "becomes conscious"), and global workspace theory (there is a genuine place and moment of broadcast, but nothing gets assembled there). Somewhere the Orwell vs. Stalinesque split should be reachable (memory gets rewritten afterwards vs. the show is edited before it airs), and I specifically want you to flag every pair of leaves the tree can't cleanly separate — I suspect Orwell vs. Stalinesque is one of them, since Dennett's whole move there is that the two tellings of the phi story are empirically indistinguishable, but push back if you disagree, and find others if they exist.

Keep every node phrased so nobody needs to have read a word to answer it, and no node should just ask "do you believe in the Cartesian theater?" If some region of the logical space ends up unoccupied (like "there's a fact of the matter about the timing but no place where it happens"), note it and guess whether nobody's there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Two pages max for the handout, and it's for undergrads, so don't assume they know any neuroscience beyond "there's a brain in there." Also if you could mark which nodes you'd expect a naive subject to answer inconsistently (like saying there's no fact of the matter in the abstract but then insisting the in-between rabbit taps were really felt), that would be super useful for the discussion section — I got burned last year assuming the intuitions were more stable than they are.
