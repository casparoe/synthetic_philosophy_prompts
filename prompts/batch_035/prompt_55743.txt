For the past two years I have been deliberately trying to stop resenting my mother. After a round of family therapy, I assembled what I find a genuinely persuasive causal story about her: her own cold and chaotic childhood, an almost certainly untreated mood disorder, a marriage she experienced as a trap from which there was no honorable exit. My therapist's view was that fully internalizing this story should dissolve the resentment, and when I narrate it to myself, it does quiet down. The difficulty is what happens in her presence. She makes some dismissive remark at dinner and I am flooded with the old anger before I have time to apply the story; the explanation always seems to arrive too late to stop the reflex. Stranger still, the pattern of what I do and don't resent has shifted in ways I never chose: a cutting remark from a stranger barely registers, the same remark from her still stings, while her failure to visit me during a week in hospital — objectively the worse offense — now leaves me oddly flat, almost charitable.

I would like advice on three things. First, is my project even coherent: can one decide to adopt a purely explanatory stance toward a person one is also in an ongoing relationship with, or does living with someone call up responses of a more personal kind that explanation, however complete, cannot switch off? Second, if the residual resentment is a conditioned reflex that fires before any deliberation, does it still carry any significance about whether she is actually to blame, or have I been treating a symptom as a judgment? And third, is there a stable middle position between "she couldn't help any of it" and "she chose it" — something that fits the fact that my feelings respond differently to different facts about her, and if so, what exactly are those feelings tracking?
