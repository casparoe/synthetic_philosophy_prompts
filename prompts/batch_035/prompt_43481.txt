A documentary I watched last week set off a running argument between two friends of mine, Dan and Mara, which they still haven't settled, and since I lost the thread somewhere in the middle I said I'd write it up and get an outside opinion. The documentary covered the famous experiments from the 1950s in which rats with an electrode implanted in a spot near the middle of the brain would press a lever, sometimes thousands of times an hour, to deliver a small current to that spot — apparently preferring the stimulation to food. Afterwards the exchange went like this:

(1) Dan said the stimulated rat is, if anything is, happy. The activation of that system is what pleasure is, or at least the only thing pleasure could be; the rat has it in unlimited amounts and will give up everything else for it. If a creature pressing the lever with all else abandoned isn't happy, the word has no application anywhere.

(2) Mara answered that the system isn't pleasure itself but pleasure's tracker: it evolved to fire when good things happen to the rat, so as to steer the rat toward them. The current delivers none of the good things; it just sets the needle swinging. The rat is in the position of someone who discovers a vending machine that plays the winning jingle without dispensing the candy, and keeps pressing the button for the jingle all the same. Being fooled that way isn't happiness — it's being robbed with your own cooperation.

(3) Dan said the candy-versus-jingle picture assumes what it needs to prove: that there's a real good the signal is merely announcing. But the rat can't want anything except through this system's pointing; nothing is a good for the rat independently of what makes the system fire. So there's no standpoint from which the electrode's reading is a counterfeit: for the rat, "all is well" just is the state that system reports. If Mara insists the goods are something over and above the signal, she has to say what they are without using the signal — and no one can, for a rat.

(4) Mara replied that a built-in tracker can of course misreport; that's what malfunction means. A thermostat stuck at "warm" in a freezing room doesn't make the room warm, and you don't need an independent theory of warmth to say the reading is wrong — the shivering inhabitants supply one. Dan's requirement would prove too much: on it no creature could ever be systematically deceived about its own good, and the very idea of a defective monitor becomes incoherent, since the monitor's word is always final.

(5) Dan's last word was that the thermostat works as an analogy only where there's an independent quantity for the reading to be right or wrong about, and for warmth there is one — the shivering, the ice, the physics. For happiness, the only handle anyone has, from the inside, is the very machinery in question; there's no further fact to consult. So either the electrode rat is happy, or nothing is — unless Mara wants to ground the rat's good in the goods natural selection "meant" to track, which is a strange thing to call happiness, since a mechanism's historical function is invisible to the creature living inside it.

They broke off there, each convinced the other had begged the question. I honestly couldn't tell, and I'd rather not just pick the one who sounded better. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move. Give me exactly five numbered items, one per move, each one or two sentences rating that move on a scale from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with the reason. No introduction, no conclusion, nothing after item five. Attach a confidence level (high, medium, or low) to each item, and in whichever item you are least sure of, say so explicitly and say what makes you unsure.
