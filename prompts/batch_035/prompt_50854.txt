I am working on an automated theory-evaluation pipeline: a symbolic-regression system that generates hundreds of candidate laws from a fixed dataset, and I want to rank the candidates by how close they are to the truth rather than by fit to the sample alone. Before anyone proposes a measure, I want the specification any measure of verisimilitude must meet for this particular use, since adequacy here is relative to the pipeline and not to the seminar room. Write exactly five desiderata as a numbered list, each item one or two sentences with no introduction or conclusion; each desideratum must state its motivating case, and within the relevant items flag which pairs of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, since that is the part I most want to see. The desiderata must respect the known negative results — notably that Popper's original definition in terms of truth and falsity contents collapses when applied to pairs of false theories, and that comparative verdicts delivered by later content-based measures can turn on the vocabulary in which the theories happen to be stated — and must get the canonical cases right: a theory whose consequences are all true but which is trivially weaker than its rivals (the child's play problem), and a bold falsehood that is nonetheless near the mark, such as Bohr's 1913 model of the hydrogen atom. Two further constraints come from the pipeline itself and should shape the desiderata: the measure must be computable on finitely axiomatized candidates within the system, and — hardest of all — it must be usable when the truth is precisely what we lack, since I cannot rank candidates by their distance from a target I do not have, so any desideratum presupposing an accessible truth function belongs on the list only if you can defend its role here.
