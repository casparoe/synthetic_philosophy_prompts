ok so I fell down a rabbit hole a few months ago (started with Newcomb's problem videos, then it got worse) and I've ended up with a set of views about rational choice that my mate reckons can't all be true at once, but neither of us is smart enough to work out which one has to go. There's this idea I came across — I think it's called superrationality or something — where if everyone in a situation is reasoning the same way, you should act as if you're choosing for all of them. Anyway, here's what I actually believe, numbered so you can poke at each bit:

(1) In a one-shot prisoner's dilemna against a perfect copy of me, or even just someone who I know for a fact reasons exactly like I do, I should cooperate — we're going to end up doing the same thing either way, so the only real outcomes on the table are both-cooperate and both-defect, and one of those is clearly better. (2) But against a clever stranger who I know is rational in the ordinary sense yet has never thought about any of this, I should defect — there's no point cooperating with someone who's just going to defect. (3) In Newcomb's problem I one-box, and honestly I don't get how anyone two-boxes. (4) I think voting in a general election is basically irrational for an individual — one vote has essentially zero chance of deciding anything, and I get a bit annoyed at people who guilt-trip others about it. (5) And yet I did vote last time, and I don't think that was stupid of me — it felt right given that loads of people I know were separately deciding whether to bother, and we mostly all went.

So: which of these are genuinely in tension, and which only look like they are? My mate says (1) and (2) flatly contradict each other, but I have a feeling the line between a copy and a stranger can do real work there, so I'd like you to actually adjudicate that rather than just agreeing with him. And if (3) commits me to some "my choice is evidence about what other choosers do" way of thinking, does that rescue (5), or undermine (4), or both? Please order the tensions by how severe they are, and for each one tell me the cheapest fix and what it costs — don't just list everything as equally bad, and don't tell me what I want to hear.
