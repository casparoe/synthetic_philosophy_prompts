Restate the dark forest answer to the Fermi paradox — the claim that every rational civilization hides, because any civilization that reveals itself risks annihilation by a preemptive strike from an unknown, unverifiable neighbor — in the vocabulary I actually use: predator–prey dynamics, crypsis versus aposematism, constitutive versus induced defenses, enemy-free space, and selection on signal conspicuousness. Translate the argument's steps one by one and stay faithful to its logic; do not smuggle in ecological assumptions the argument never makes (e.g., that predators must eat what they kill, or that signaling carries the metabolic costs it does on Earth).

I want to come out knowing whether the dark forest is a sound ecological claim misapplied to the galaxy, or an idea that only sounds ecological. Please keep it under 700 words.
