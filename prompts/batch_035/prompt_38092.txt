so i just quit my ML job and got a small grant to spend maybe three years on agent-foundations-adjacent work, coming at it from logic rather than deep learning. everyone in my corner of alignment keeps pointing me at embedded agency as the umbrella -- the idea that the cartesian boundary between agent and environment is fake, the agent is just a part of the world it models and acts in. but under that umbrella people lump together what look to me like pretty different projects: agents modeling worlds that contain themselves (and the self-reference problems that come with that), self-trust / tiling problems where an agent can't prove its own successor will behave well (the löb stuff), subsystem alignment, where the agent's internal parts become little agents with their own incentives, and decision theory once your own computation is inside the world and other agents can predict it. my question is which of these actually matters most and why. i don't want a survey, i want dependency-aware prioritization: which ones are upstream of the others, which are realistically tractable for one person with a logic background and three years, and which you'd deprioritize because nothing downstream really changes while they sit unanswered. also, is lumping these together under one label actually a mistake -- are they one problem in any real sense, or just a family of things that happen to annoy the same people?
