I do product ops at a small company making a voice assistant aimed at older adults -- medication reminders, "call my daughter", weather, plus a general chat layer. Most requests are innocuous, but we keep hitting the same kind of case: a user who's clearly being scammed ("read me the code they texted so I can prove it's me"), a user with probable dementia asking for something they'd never ask for at 3pm rather than 3am, and lots of milder stuff where we can't tell if we're being protective or just condescending. Right now refusals get made ad hoc by whoever's on call and it's inconsistently applied to put it mildly.

I want a decision procedure my team can run per request, and I want it built around a distinction I've been kicking around but haven't seen stated cleanly anywhere: when an assistant doubts a request there are at least three different moves -- refuse (tell them no), substitute (do what you think they actually want rather than what they asked for), and comply-with-a-warning -- and I suspect these come apart in ways the usual "paternalism vs. autonomy" framing misses, because substitute isn't even visible to the user as a refusal, it just quietly happens. If philosophy has a better carving of this territory, use theirs over mine, but say what it is.

Format: give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion. The items should be the steps/questions in order, including where the procedure hands off to a human (we can escalate maybe 1 in 10,000 requests, so that had better be rare) and where it just says comply even when we feel icky about it. Weave in how the steps land on these as you go: the scam case (where the scammer has already told the user WE'RE the scam), and one I got yelled at for last week -- a user asked us to stop medication reminders because her daughter checks anyway. Within whichever item you're least sure of, flag that uncertainty in a parenthetical, and make one item or parenthetical say how confident you are in the procedure overall -- don't add a sixth item to do it.
