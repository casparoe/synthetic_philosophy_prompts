For my senior philosophy elective, I have to submit a short position paper on free will, and before I write it I'd like someone to audit the set of views I've drifted into, because I suspect at least some of them don't hang together, but I genuinely don't know which. My starting point is Galen Strawson's Basic Argument: since you can't be responsible for how you originally are, and you act as you do because of how you are, nobody is ultimately morally responsible in the fullest, ultimate sense. Here's what I currently believe: (1) the Basic Argument is sound, so ultimate moral responsibility doesn't exist; (2) ordinary moral responsibility is real — if you act deliberately, knowing full well it's wrong, you deserve blame in a genuine desert sense, strong enough that punishment doesn't need a justification in terms of deterrence or public safety; (3) my grandfather quit drinking after twenty years and became a much kinder man, and he really earned the credit he gets for that — I'd feel like I was insulting him if I said luck did the work; (4) gratitude, resentment, and forgiveness are rational responses to people and I'm not giving them up, since a life stripped of them would be badly impoverished; (5) once the Basic Argument is granted, the whole moral luck debate seems redundant to me — constitutive luck, circumstantial luck, it all falls out of the same argument, so there's nothing further to argue about. Are these five jointly tenable? Please tell me which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, rank the real tensions by severity, and say for each one what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost me. I honestly don't know which commitment I'd give up first.
