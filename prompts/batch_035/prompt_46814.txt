Retired condensed-matter physicist here, got into philosophy of mind in my retirement and something keeps nagging at me. In my old line of work we took it for granted that near a critical point the microscopic details of a system just stop mattering — magnets, fluids, binary mixtures all show the same scaling behavior, and the renormalization group story even explains how the microstructure gets forgotten, leaving only a handful of collective parameters. I gather philosophers say something similar when they claim the same mental state can be realized in wildly different physical stuff — neurons, silicon, whatever. But Im not sure these are actually the same phenomenon. In my case its the macro theory itself losing dependence on the micro level; in the philosophers' case it seems more like a claim about classification, that we lump heterogeneous physical set-ups under one psychological heading. So: is there an established term for the pattern where a higher-level theory simply stops caring about lower-level details, as distinct from the claim that a higher-level kind can have many physical realizations? If the distinction doesn't have a name, coin one and define it, and then tell me where the edge sits — does it cover the way the Navier-Stokes equations don't care whether the fluid is water or mercury, and does it break down in cases where the macro level feeds back and changes the micro dynamics?
