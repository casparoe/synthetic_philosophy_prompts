I work in ML but not on interpretability itself (more evals-adjacent), and over the past year ive drifted into a fairly strong realist position about the whole enterprise: that the features, directions and circuits people find in networks are genuinely there. When someone pulls a code out of a sparse autoencoder that fires on payments-fraud text, or finds a direction you can add to the activations to make a model refuse everything, I take it they've discovered part of how the network actually works — not merely imposed a convenient description that happens to fit for now. On this view interpretability is closer to anatomy than to literary criticism: the network has parts with real causal roles, and our descriptions of those parts can be more or less accurate, even if the current tools are crude and the early maps will look embarrassing later.

But I've started to worry I've insulated the view without noticing. Every time a feature looks polysemantic, the superposition story rescues it — the feature is really there, it's just sharing a direction with other features in an inconvenient basis. Every time two labs train sparse autoencifiers on the same model and come back with different decompositions, I say "fine, the decomposition isn't unique but the underlying feature space is real". Every time a steering vector works beautifully on one prompt distribution and does nothing (or something weird and off-target) on another, I chalk it up to context-dependence rather than to the direction not being a thing. That's a lot of epicycles for someone who thinks of himself as a realist.

So tell me what would actually count against the position. What observations about how features decompose or fail to, what results about the uniqueness or stability of decompositions across seeds, architectures, and training runs, and what conceptual arguments — including ones imported from older debates about realism regarding psychological kinds or biological species — should genuinely move me? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and say which of them a committed realist would concede as real evidence against the view versus which they're entitled to shrug off. And don't dodge the epicycles question: is the superposition-era, qualifications-intact version of realism still a substantive claim that could lose, or has it been softened into something unfalsifiable? If it has, I want you to take a definite stance on whether that's acceptable (because it's a research programme rather than a single thesis) or whether it's a sign I should retreat to a more instrumentalist reading of interpretability — dont just lay out the options and leave the choice to me.
