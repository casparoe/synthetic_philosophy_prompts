I've been in an online ethics reading group for about a year and a half now, and every couple of months we end up back on negative utilitarianism, and every time it plays out exactly the same way and nobody budges, and im starting to wonder if we're even having a real disagreement anymore.

For context: the NU people in the group hold that suffering counts negatively and nothing else counts positively — or at least that suffering has lexical priority over happiness, so no amount of happiness can outweigh any amount of agony. The critics bring out the classic move: a benevolent world-exploder that instantly and painlessly ended all sentient life would, by NU lights, be the best possible action, since it eliminates all future suffering at a stroke. The NU folks reply with some combination of: (a) that's an epistemic argument about what we could justifiably aim at, not an axiological claim about what would actually be best; (b) a world-destroying act isn't even guaranteed to reduce suffering, given failed attempts or whatever might come after; (c) the real view isn't "any suffering outweighs everything," it's that extreme suffering is lexically worse, so ordinary happy lives are fine and maybe even good; (d) the exploder argument proves too much, since classical utilitarians have their own monstrous conclusions (utility monsters, replacing everyone with wireheaded rats) and we don't treat those as instant refutations. Then the critics say (c) is just prioritarianism in a trenchcoat, not NU proper, and the NU people say that if "NU proper" only means the exploder version, then the label is doing more work than the view. Then we all go home. Every single time.

So my question: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal — do the two sides mean different things by suffering (raw pain vs. frustrated preferences vs. anything bad happening to you)? Are they answering different questions without noticing, like "what should we do under deep uncertainty" vs. "what is valuable in itself"? Is one side making a claim about aggregation and the other a claim about decision theory? Or is it a genuine bedrock clash over whether happiness is a positive good or merely the absence of something bad — the kind of thing no argument reaches? And is the fact that the same four moves recycle forever a sign that the question is ill-posed, or just that it's hard?

I want a real diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of every position on the map — try to actually figure out what's going on underneath the surface exchange. Then tell me honestly whether anything would count as progress here: a distinction nobody's drawn, a thought experiment that splits the two camps in a new way, reformulating the question entirely — or whether this is just one of those debates that runs on intuition pumps until everyone gets tired.
