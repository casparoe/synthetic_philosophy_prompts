My sister and I have been fighting for months about our mother's situation, and I want to see how the debate actually plays out between two people who both know what they're talking about. Write a dialogue between her (an ICU physician, thoroughgoing consequentialist, thinks the doing/allowing distinction is basically superstition) and me (philosophy lecturer, defends the distinction but is honest about its weak spots). Set it at the bedside over the case that started our fight: our mother has been on a ventilator for fourteen months that requires someone to manually squeeze a bellows every few seconds — if everyone stops squeezing, she dies within a minute. The hospital's ethics committee says further ventilation is futile. I keep saying that stopping the squeezing is a doing, not an allowing, since it's continuous positive action that keeps her alive — the moment the hands stop, they've killed her. My sister says that's exactly backwards: the squeezing is the doing, and stopping it just lets her disease take the course it was always going to take; also she's been squeezing the damn thing herself for most of those fourteen months, so she thinks she gets a say. Make them actually engage with each others arguments rather than talking past each other, and have neither come off as a strawman — ideally they should at some point notice they might be disagreeing not about whether the distinction applies but about whether it could ever carry any moral weight at all.
