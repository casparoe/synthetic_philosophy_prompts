I'm taking a sociology course on gender and work this semester, and last week we covered the gender-equality paradox — the finding that in countries that score higher on gender equality (think the Nordic ones), the occupational choices of men and women are actually MORE gendered, e.g. the share of women going into STEM is in some ways lower there than in less egalitarian countries.

Here's my question. It seems to me the logic goes like this: the egalitarian countries have already removed the discrimination, the legal barriers, all the oppressive stuff. So whatever difference is left over in those countries must be the innate part — there's literally nothing social left to cause it. Which would mean the residual gap in, say, engineering vs. nursing is just biology expressing itself now that it's allowed to. And then it seems to follow that any further policy aimed at closing the gap (quotas, outreach, mentoring programs for women in tech) is by definition fighting against nature, and forcing people away from what they'd freely choose can't be justice, it has to be the opposite of justice.

Assuming that chain of reasoning is right (and honestly it feels pretty airtight to me?), my actual question is a normative one: how do we decide how much equalizing is enough? Like is there a principled cutoff where pursuing equal representation stops being justice and starts becoming coercion? My prof keeps saying things like "the paradox doesn't mean what you think it means" but she won't really explain, she just says we'll get to it after the midterm. Would appriciate some help working it out.
