I've never studied philosophy, but for a few years I've been puzzling on my own over a question that keeps splitting my own dinner table: if you care about animals and about the climate at the same time, which meat is actually worse to eat? Eating chicken seems to involve hundreds of times more animal deaths per calorie than eating beef, while beef warms the planet far more per calorie. And there's a thought I can't shake: even a vegetarian diet rests on crops, and ploughing, harvesting and pesticides crush and poison countless field animals — mice, birds, insect larvae — so maybe nobody's plate is clean. I've tried sketching a little tree of possible positions myself, but I get tangled, and I suspect some branches are held by nobody at all.

Please write a "which position do you actually hold on meat?" quiz, around nine questions. Every question should be a concrete forced choice with numbers attached — for instance, a year of beef supplied by one pasture-raised cow versus a year of chicken involving roughly 250 bird deaths, or a vegetarian diet whose crops involve uncounted field deaths — with two or three answer options phrased so a taker can answer without knowing any position's name. Use stylized round numbers rather than asking me to trust exact figures. Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions, which must include at least: the strict vegetarian who refuses to trade animal lives against climate at all; the "fewest deaths" eater who consequently lands on beef; the climate-first eater who lands on chicken or fish; the person who dismisses collateral deaths in crop fields as too speculative to count; someone who weights suffering per life rather than counting heads; and someone who thinks individual purchases at the supermarket change nothing anyway. For the answer-pattern combinations nobody occupies in real debates, tell me whether that's because they're incoherent, unattractive or merely unexplored, and flag any pair of positions the quiz can't cleanly separate.

Please take the role of a careful ethicist experienced in building diagnostic instruments: think through the logical space first, brainstorm a few candidate split-points, and only then commit to the best set of questions. Deliver the result as JSON with fields "questions" (each an object with "case" and "options", where each option is a short text), "scoring_key" (each entry with "position", "pattern", "commitment"), "unseparable_pairs" and "unoccupied_patterns", followed by two or three plain sentences on what the quiz predictably fails to capture.
