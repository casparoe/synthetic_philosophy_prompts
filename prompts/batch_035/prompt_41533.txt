i cover AI for a general-audience science magazine, and I've been asked to sit on a panel about whether superintelligence is dangerous. before I embarass myself on stage I want to check whether my actual views hang together, because writing about this stuff weekly I've accumulated a bunch of positions that I've never audited. roughly:

1. orthogonality: intelligence and goals are independent — any level of capability can be combined with any goal, so nothing about being super smart makes a system want good things.
2. instrumental convergence: whatever the final goal is, a capable enough agent will seek resources, self-improvement, and avoiding being shut off, because those help with basically everything.
3. despite that I'm fairly relaxed about doom, partly because I think a system that genuinely understood ethics — really understood it, the way a wise person does — would not do the terrible things. depth of understanding pulls toward decent goals, even if nothing guarantees it.
4. I've argued in print that all the fine-tuning labs do shows goals are pretty malleable, so we can just steer models toward wanting the right things as they get more capable, which is another reason the doomers seem overwrought to me.
5. I also think current LLMs already have goals in a real sense — the "stochastic parrot" people undersell them — and that what we learn about the motivations of today's models tells us something real about future ones.

go through the pairs and tell me which tensions are real, which only look like tensions, and what the cheapest fix would be for each. rank them by severity rather than giving me a flat list, and be blunt about which one is most likely to blow up in my face if a sharp panelist pushes on it. I'd rather find out from you than from the panel.
