My roommate and I have been arguing for years about who owes what to whom, and over that time she's built up a set of verdicts on particular cases that I can't quite reverse-engineer. I want your read on a new case before I bring it up with her. Her verdicts so far: a stranger drowning in a pond right in front of you — you have to wade in, and she's unmoved by the fact that the same money sent to a releif agency would've saved more children abroad; to her, never getting around to that mailing is stingy but not wrong. Your nephew's last tuition payment versus a donation that would do more good for strangers at the same cost: paying the nephew is fine, she doesnt even ask you to split it. Two strangers both standing in front of you, same need, only one can be helped, one a fellow citizen and one not: she says citizenship doesn't tip it, pick either. A close friend from abroad versus a compatriot stranger with equal need: the friend comes first, and she doesn't apologize for that. But at the level of the government she flips — she thinks her country would be wrong to spend its last budget dollars on equally poor foreigners rather than its own poor; down there, some priority to compatriots is right. Now the new case. Tonight she has time to help exactly one person. There's a tourist she's never met, not a citizen of her country, who just collapsed on the sidewalk in front of her and needs someone to stay with him until the ambulance comes, which could be forty minutes. Or her best friend, a fellow citizen, who just called from across town: locked out of her apartment, diabetic, glucose crashing, needs supplies brought over within the hour, and the drive is thirty minutes each way. So what will she do? And don't just give me the answer — honestly I care less about which one she picks than about how you get there. What I want is the candidate rules that fit her record so far (I can count at least two, maybe three), what each of those rules predicts for tonight's case, your best guess with your reasons for preferring it, and then the next case I should pose her, the one that would best split the remaining rules apart. Please write it as continuous prose only, no headings or bullet points or bold or tables, the way you'd write an email or a letter, and keep it in American English.
