so, some backstory first. my older brother has epilepsy that stopped responding to meds a while back, and at his last appointment his neurologist brought up corpus callosotomy — split-brain surgery — as something they might consider if the seizures keep getting worse and nothing else works. nobody has decided anything, but I went and read a pile of stuff about what the surgery actually does, and I've noticed my gut reactions to it dont really fit together. I wrote down where I actually stand so someone can audit it, because I keep chasing my own tail. here's the list:

1. I'm a physicalist. everything about a mind is fixed by the physical state of the brain, no extra ingredients floating around.

2. I also think how many conscious subjects there are in a given head is fixed by the physical facts — two brains that are physically identical cant differ on whether there's one experiencer or two.

3. separate from that, I believe there's always a determinate answer to the how-many question. it can't come out vague or somehow undefined. in any skull there's a fact of the matter, even if no one knows it.

4. I take the classic split-brain results at face value: after the surgery the hemispheres can be primed separately, they can be made to want different things, and one hemisphere often has no idea what the other just did. cross-cuing happens but it strikes me as patchy workarounds, not real re-integration.

5. but I also hold that introspective report is the gold standard for finding consciousness. we identify conscious centers by what they can tell us, and I'm suspicious of experience claims that by construction could never be reported. this is honestly part of why the right hemisphere in most of those patients doesn't worry me much — its language is so poor that most report can't come from it anyway.

6. and for people born without a corpus callosum (callosal agenesis) I have zero inclination to say there are two of them. they're single people to me, full stop. same for anyone with an intact brain — I never doubt there's one of us per head in the ordinary case.

something in here feels off but I cant tell if it's one problem or five or zero. like, maybe 4 and 5 are flatly inconsistent, or maybe 6 is doing way more work than it should given 1 and 4 — the surgery is basically a late-life version of what agenesis people have from birth, so why would the timing change the count for me? and maybe 3 is totally safe and maybe it's secretly the one that has to go. I genuinely dont know.

go through the pairs, tell me which tensions are real and which only look real, rank them by severity, and for each real one say what giving it up would cost me elsewhere. dont re-tell me the history of the experiments — I've read enough of that. I want the audit. the reason I want it is not abstract: if this surgery ever goes from hypothetical to actual, my family is going to have to think about who walks out of that operating room, and I'd like to know which of these I actually believe before that.
