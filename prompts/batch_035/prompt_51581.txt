Yet it feels natural to say that the probability of heads on this one flip is around 0.6, since the bend plausibly makes heads somewhat likelier, and people do say such things with a straight face. My question is what that 0.6 could be a fact about. My own credence that there is a determinate, mind-independent fact of the matter concerning the probability of heads on that single, unrepeatable flip is roughly 0.45. My math teacher, who treats probability as essentially measure theory plus convention, would put it near 0.1, while my physics teacher, who is perfectly comfortable saying that a single uranium-238 atom has a fifty-fifty chance of decaying within about 4.5 billion years, would presumably put it much higher. Please give me your number, but more importantly decompose it: the probability that single-case probabilities are objective propensities of the physical setup, the probability that they reduce to the betting dispositions of an ideally informed agent, the probability that such claims are really disguised statements about hypothetical frequencies, and any further terms you think belong, with one line of justification for each and then the combination. Afterwards, tell me which term you are least confident in and what evidence or argument would move it most. And if you can, locate where my teachers' disagreement most likely lives: in the math teacher's formalism, in the physics teacher's quantum habits, or in something specific to my bent, once-flipped coin.
