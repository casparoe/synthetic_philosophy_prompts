I'm on the incident review committee at a mid-size hospital network, and we just spent four meetings on a case where an AI triage tool (the kind that scores incoming patients for escalation) delayed flagging a patient who then deteriorated, and nobody could say who, if anyone, dropped the ball. The vendor tuned the model, the hospital approved the deployment, the nurses used it as intended, the doctor had twenty other patients -- everyone did their jobs, more or less, and the patient still nearly died. This is what the philosophy people call a responsibility gap, and I've decided that before I read yet another paper claiming to "close" or "deny" the gap, I want the actual spec. Write me the desiderata: what any adequate account of responsibility for autonomous systems has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. Things I suspect belong on the list but want you to actually think through: it has to handle learning systems whose specific behaviors nobody could foresee (not just the thermostat case), it has to keep apart blame from mere answerability from liability (our lawyers care about the third, the nurses care about the first), it can't just close the gap by definition or by leaning on "collective responsibility" as a magic word, and it has to tell me what the nurses on my committee should feel, if anything. Add whatever else belongs there that I haven't thought of.

Two things I especially want. First, mark which requirements are non-negotiable for my purpose -- which is running a practical incident review that assigns or declines responsibility, not building a metaphysics of agency -- versus which are parochial demands of one camp (I suspect the "someone must ALWAYS be answerable" requirement is more of a legal-institutional preference than a philosophical truth, but argue with me). Second, flag any pair of requirements you think can't be jointly satisfied, because if that's right the list itself is the interesting result: e.g., can you really demand that responsibility track foresight AND that learning systems not produce responsibility gaps, given that foresight of their specific failures is exactly what nobody has?

Then, briefly, run the main contenders against the list: the meaningful human control people, the distributed/collaborative responsibility folks, and the gap-deniers who say our existing tools (design responsibility, strict liability, role obligations) were always enough and the "gap" is an artifact of assuming responsibility requires individual causal control. Keep the scorecard short -- it's the criteria I want the thinking on, since I'll be applying them myself to the next incident whether or not any published theory passes. And if one single requirement knocks out the most popular contenders, say which one, because that's the thing I'll be quoting in the committee's year-end report.
