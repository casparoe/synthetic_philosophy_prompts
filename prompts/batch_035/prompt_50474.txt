My roommate thinks she's demolished infinitism in five steps and I half beleive her. (1) When a belief is justified by other beliefs, the justification has to come from those beliefs — it's transmitted, like momentum in billiard balls, and nothing transmits what it doesn't itself have. (2) In an infinite justificatory chain, every belief gets whatever justification it has from another belief in the chain. (3) So no belief is ever the first to have any justification, and by (1) none of them can pass any on: the chain is an infinite series of IOUs where nobody ever pays. (4) So no belief in an infinite chain is justified, and infinitism is dead. (5) The only escape is beliefs that have justification without getting it from anywhere — self-justifying basic beliefs — i.e., foundationalism. Here's what bugs me though: by her own lights the basic beliefs in (5) never *received* justification, so by (1) they have nothing to transmit up the chain either, and foundationalism dies along with infinitism. When I pointed this out she said basic beliefs *generate* justification rather than receive it, so (1) doesn't apply to them — but I can't see why the infinitist isn't allowed to say the same thing about every link in the chain. So either her argument proves skepticism, which she definitely doesn't want, or something snuck in somewhere. Which step do you actually reject, and what does rejecting it cost? And tell me straight: after the fix, does infinitism survive or does it die for some independent reason? I want a definite verdict, not a tour of the live options — pick one and defend it.
