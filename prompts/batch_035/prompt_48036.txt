I've been thinking about free-floating rationales — reasons why things are the case that no reasoner has ever grasped or formulated — and I've decided to work out what I actually believe by tabulating my verdicts on cases. Here they are, with my honest reactions.

1. The woodpecker's thick skull: there is a reason it is thick — it protects the brain during drumming — and no woodpecker, breeder, or process that reasons ever worked that out. Verdict: free-floating rationale.
2. In my city, everyone stands on the right on escalators and walks on the left. No one designed it, and it was never chosen from competing conventions by anything like selection; it persists because each person's daily self-interest keeps re-creating it, though no rider aims at aggregate throughput. Verdict: rationale, and it seems to be of the same kind as in case 1.
3. My firm makes every new hire watch a safety video about a hazard that was engineered out of the plant a decade before I was hired. Actual reasoners made it for a reason they fully understood; the reason has lapsed, and only inertia and paperwork keep the ritual going. Verdict: no rationale anymore, though there used to be one.
4. My grandfather sank a post decades ago to hold a clothesline. The clothesline is long gone; the rusted post now happens to stop the gate from swinging into the rose bed. A reasoner placed it, for reasons of his own, and it now serves a function that nothing ever selected it for and nobody knows about. Verdict: no rationale, even though the function is real and current.
5. A chess engine trained purely by self-play comes to prefer central control. There is a reason it plays the moves it plays, but the training process was optimizing for winning the whole time. Verdict: rationale — but I confess this one makes me uneasy, and I can't fully say why, since natural selection optimizes for something too, and case 1 is my paradigm case.
6. Courts follow precedent, and the point — predictability, equal treatment — is real even though no judge ever has to have it in view for the practice to work. Verdict: rationale.
7. The lottery number drawn last night was 27. There is a complete causal story about why it was 27, but no reason it was 27 rather than 31. Verdict: no rationale, not even a defunct one.
8. English came to require do in questions ("Did she leave?" rather than "Left she?"). Part of me says the language drifted into a shape that is easier to parse, so there is a rationale there; but I know the actual route ran through patterns of prestige and imitation that had nothing to do with parsing, and I can't pretend efficiency did the selecting. Verdict: rationale, but a weak one.

My question for you: is there a single principle that captures these verdicts, or something close? It will need to be stated precisely enough to decide cases, and it will have to confront the pairs that bother me — 2 against 4, where an unintended function persists in both but I find a rationale only in the case nothing designed; and 5 against 1, where my paradigm of a free-floating rationale (natural selection) and my hesitation case (gradient descent) seem structurally alike as optimizing processes without reasoners. If a principle that saves all my verdicts needs baroque machinery, tell me which verdict to give up instead.

Please also tell me what your principle predicts on three cases I have deliberately not verdicted. (a) Ice is less dense than liquid water, so ponds freeze from the top and fish winter safely underneath — is there a free-floating rationale protecting the fish, or is it just chemistry? (b) A village norm survives because violators are fined by enforcers who have entirely forgotten what the norm was for; the original function has quietly lapsed, but enforcement keeps the behavior perfectly in place. (c) A text-prediction system develops an internal feature that reliably detects sarcasm, and the "reason" it does so is grounded in the communicative function sarcasm serves in the data it was trained on — a function of human practices, not of the system's own performance. I don't need citations or references for this; positions can be named informally if you want to compare the principle to something already on offer, but what I want is the principle itself, honestly assessed against my list.
