My ethics seminar has split into two camps that have been talking past each other for a month, and instead of another shouting match I want them to produce the document a real adversarial collaboration would end with.

Camp A (the comparativists): they think historical atrocities can be legitimately compared, even ranked, along dimensions like scale, intentionality, and novelty of the evil, and that refusing to compare is a way of dodging moral accounting — you can't say X was worse than nothing or nothing was worse than X forever.

Camp B (the incommensurabilists): they think ranking evils is obscene, that no common scale respects what actually happened to victims, and that comparison smuggles in a moral bookkeeping that flattens suffering into numbers.

Write the joint statement both camps could put their names on. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides accept — they actually agree on more than they admit, e.g. that factual questions about death tolls have answers, and that some comparisons serve purposes nobody objects to (legal ones, say); (2) a table of the disagreements, each row worded so that camp would accept it as a fair statement of its own view, not a caricature; (3) the cruxes — subquestions where settling one would move at least one side. My hunch is one is whether ranking-as-such, detatched from any purpose, expresses disrespect for the dead, and another is whether any ordering metric is non-arbitrary or whether every one just bakes in somebody's values; (4) for each crux, a concrete case both camps agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction written down in advance. For example: a memorial exhibit that presents three atrocities ordered by estimated death toll — does each camp predict visitors read the order as a verdict on the victims' worth, or as a neutral demographic fact? Feel free to come up with better cases than that one.

And keep it honest — if part of their dispute turns out to be about the word 'ranking' rather than the thing, say which part and why. End the whole document with a machine-readable line: \answer{...} containing, in the order the disagreements appear in your table, the single word genuine or verbal for each one.
