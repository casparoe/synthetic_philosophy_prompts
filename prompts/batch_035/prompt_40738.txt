so, bit of background: I fell into just war theory arguing about ww2 area bombing online, and it's been my rabbit hole for about a year. I've been asked to write up where I stand for a debate club thing, and I want it stress-tested first, because when I list my views out they definately feel both obvious and somehow off, and I can't tell which. here's the package:

(1) moral equality of combatants — as far as who may be killed goes, soldiers on the two sides of a war are on a par. a conscript in the aggressor's army isn't a more legitimate target than the volunteer defending her own city, and the justness of the cause doesn't move who is liable to be killed.

(2) fighting in a war of aggression is a serious moral wrong, and not just for the people who planned it. the ordinary soldier who fires his rifle for an unjust cause, even while keeping every rule of war to the letter, is doing something badly wrong. if the same act happened on a street instead of a battlefield we'd call it attempted murder.

(3) my grandfather was drafted into a war I consider unjust (not naming it, doesn't matter), and by every account he was a decent man and a good soldier. I can't make myself say he was a murderer, and I don't think I should have to.

(4) I think the rules of war applying the same to both sides isn't a grudging compromise we put up with, it's actually correct. like the rules of a sport: they're what make the whole activity intelligible, and they don't care who started it.

(5) soldiers are morally responsible for what they do in uniform, not just order-following machines, and there's such a thing as a duty to refuse to fight in a war that's manifestly unjust — though obviously a conscript under a nasty regime isn't blamed like a volunteer who signed up for the signing bonus.

what I want from you: go through these, tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like they are, rank them by severity, give me the cheapest fix for each, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. please end with a line that just says "Verdict: X" where X is the number of the one commitment that has to give (or "none" if you think it all hangs together as is) — I'm putting the responses through a little script, so keep that last line exactly in that form.
