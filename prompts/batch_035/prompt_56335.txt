Something I've been chewing on and cant quite sort out. Unemployment insurance, where I live: if you're laid off you collect, but if you quit you generally don't — unless you quit for what the rules call good cause, like harassment, unsafe conditions, a spouse relocating for work. So far so intuitive. But the rest of the safety net doesn't work like that at all. Disability payments are the same whether you were injured commuting or on a backcountry ski trip you obviously chose. Disaster relief goes out to people who built houses in a known floodplain. And even inside unemployment itself the line wobbles: my coworker quit a job that was making him genuinely miserable — no harassment, nothing legally actionable, he just hated it — and didn't get a cent, and it's not like he ever picked what he'd enjoy doing with his life.

So the unemployment rules seem to care a lot about whether your bad outcome flowed from your choice or just happend to you, and the rest of the system doesn't seem to care at all. My question is what view of choice, luck, and responsibility the whole bundle presupposes, if any. Does the quit-vs-laid-off distinction only make sense if there's a real difference between outcomes you're responsible for and outcomes you're not, with the good-cause carve-outs tracking when a choice stops being genuinely voluntary? Does the disability/flood-relief side presuppose the opposite, or is it just doing something else entirely — say, not compensating bad luck but just covering needs? And what about my coworker — if what you want is partly not up to you, does the implicit theory even survive its own hard cases? I'd also like to know where the practice is underdetermined, i.e. which genuinely different theories of the choice/luck line would all license basically these rules anyway, and what would actually have to change if the system instead presupposed that the only thing that matters is how badly off you end up, choice or no choice. (I'm less interested in the specific law here than the general shape of the practice — assume unemployment schemes everywhere do roughly this.)
