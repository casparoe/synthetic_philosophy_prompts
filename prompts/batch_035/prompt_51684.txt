I've been asked to circulate a short position statement on collective choice for our department's colloquium on democratic theory, and before I send it out I want a rigorous audit of whether my commitments actually fit together. Over years of teaching social choice theory I've settled on the following package, and I confess I no longer trust my own sense of where, if anywhere, it strains: (1) I regard Arrow's theorem as sound, and I take each of its conditions individually to be a genuine desideratum of democratic aggregation rather than a technical artifact. (2) I hold that interpersonal comparisons of utility are strictly meaningless — preference information is ordinal and non-comparative, full stop. (3) I believe democratic outcomes must issue in a coherent, transitive social ranking; a collective preference structure that cycles is a real defect, not a mere bookkeeping inconvenience, and I've argued in print that talk of "the will of the people" presupposes such coherence. (4) I take pairwise majority rule to be the correct default decision procedure, and I think its verdicts carry genuine democratic authority. (5) I insist that the domain of aggregation be unrestricted — we may not exclude preference profiles on the ground that they look irrational or contrived, since citizens' preferences are whatever they are. (6) Increasingly, I'm attracted to the view that constitutional rights should operate lexically prior to any aggregation: a right trumps any majority, however large, and the agenda for collective choice is fixed by rights before preference counting even begins.

My own attempts to map the pressure points have been inconclusive, and I'd rather not anchor anyone by reporting my half-formed diagnoses. What I want is this: go through the six commitments, identify which pairs or subsets are genuinely in tension and which merely appear to be, and rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damaging. For each genuine tension, tell me what would have to give, what that concession would cost me elsewhere in the package, and which single revision would clean up the most. Pay particular attention to whether commitment (6) is a fifth column here — I genuinely cannot tell whether it's an innocent refinement of the framework or a quiet betrayal of one of (1) through (5) — and don't assume I have a favorite sacrifice in mind, because I don't.

Since this will be circulated to colleagues who enjoy blood sport, please be explicit about your confidence in each verdict and single out the point in your analysis you are least sure of. If some portion of your ranking rests on a contestable reconstruction of what one of my commitments commits me to, say so rather than papering over it.
