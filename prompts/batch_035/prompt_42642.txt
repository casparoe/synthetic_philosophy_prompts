I'm a journalist who mostly covers science and ideas, but right now I'm profiling a judge about to retire after two decades on the felony bench, and I want to work out what theory of punishment she's actually been running. Her record so far, from my notes: attempted murder where the gun jammed — she came within a few months of her usual sentances for completed murder; the architect of a Ponzi scheme, elderly and remorseful in his interviews, but he never named his accomplices and most of the money stayed hidden — near the maximum anyway; a 17-year-old's joyride, car returned undamaged — the minimum, plus community service; and during a citywide shoplifting panic she refused a prosecutor's ask to go above the guidelines on a $200 grocery theft in order to make an example of him. The case I need to predict before her last month on the bench: a man who held up a pharmacy at gunpoint four years ago, fled, and has lived cleanly since — steady job, new baby, no police contact — until his prints finally hit. The guidelines work out to about a decade. What will she do with him? I care less about the number than about which rules actually fit her record so far, what each of those rules says about someone who's already reformed by the time of sentencing, and which further case would really split the rules apart.
