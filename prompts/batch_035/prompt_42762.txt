I'm putting together a dissertation prospectus on the problem of old evidence in Bayesian confirmation theory, and I'd like your help turning my half-formed idea into a proper proposal. The idea: the familiar responses — reconstructing a hypothetical prior that ignores the evidence, or saying that what the agent learns is the theory's entailing the evidence rather than the evidence itself — all treat the evidence's epistemic status as binary: either it is already in the background with probability 1, or it is not. But working science runs on what I want to call stale evidence: old measurements with known error bars, results trusted to 0.95 rather than 1, datasets half-relied-upon. My question for the prospectus: is the old-evidence problem just the limiting case of a general problem about grading confirmation by near-certain evidence, or is it something structurally distinct — and does the general treatment yield a better solution to the classic case? Please produce a prospectus with the question stated precisely, an account of why the existing positions leave it open, a chapter-by-chapter plan, a statement of what would count as the project succeeding and what would count as it failing, and an honest verdict on whether this is a dissertation-sized project or really just a paper. Refer to positions and arguments as needed, but no bibliography — I will supply the citations myself.
