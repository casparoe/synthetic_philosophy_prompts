I'm a third-year on a philosophy of mind module and my term essay is a defence of the extended mind thesis — the Clark and Chalmers Otto's-notebook line, which my tutor seems fine with in the abstract. But in seminar last week she went at it with an argument I can't shake, and I think it proves way too much. Roughly how she ran it:

1. Parity: if something outside my skull plays the same role in guiding my behaviour that an internal cognitive process would play, then counting it as part of my mind is exactly as good as counting the internal process — the skin/skull boundary is arbitrary.
2. In the canonical case, Otto's notebook plays the role that Inga's biological memory plays: on the way to the museum he consults it and acts on what he reads.
3. The conditions we discussed for when external stuff counts — constantly available, easily accessible, automatically endorsed when consulted, and only there because I endorsed it in the past — are all met by my phone: notes app, calendar, saved places, search history.
4. But they're also met, for a whole chunk of my social life, by my flatmate Priya. She remembers which of my friends have fallen out, what I promised to do saturday, who I said I'd text back. I consult her on demand, I automatically trust her answers, and (this was the tutor's nasty bit) the information got there through a process I endorsed — I told her these things knowing she'd remember them for me.
5. So Priya's memory states are part of my mind.
6. Priya's memory states are also part of Priya's mind — they're in her head and were formed by her. A single state can't belong to two minds unless the two minds overlap, i.e. unless the boundary of my mind literally runs through another person.
7. And the same goes for the shared google doc where my group project lives, the family whatsapp, etc. So most of my mind isn't just outside my skull but overlapping with other people's minds.
8. Therefore either minds routinely overlap, so there's no fact of the matter about whose mind is whose and when Priya dies part of my mind is destroyed — or the parity principle is false.

I'm not going to accept that losing Priya is like Otto losing the notebook. But I also can't find the difference between the notebook case and the Priya case that saves me, because every condition I try to add (it must be a passive store! it can't be an agent!) either excludes things the extended mind people clearly want in — I'm fairly sure they're happy with socially extended cognition, maybe even with couples as shared memory systems — or it's just skull-favouring prejudice with extra steps, which is what parity was supposed to block.

So: which step do I reject? My instinct is the sleight of hand is somewhere between (4) and (6), but I can't name exactly what slips — something about who did the endorsing, or about the notebook not generating new content on its own? And please be honest about what rejecting it costs: does it save the notebook but sink the phone, or does it sink the notebook too? Does biting the bullet on (6) actually leave me with a coherent position, or does overlapping-minds collapse into there just being one big group mind, which even I won't swallow? Also tell me whether the tutor is running some version of a standard objection so I know if there's a known reply shape my essay can borrow — but don't bother with citations or reading lists, I just need the shape of the moves. And if the honest answer is that (6) is true and minds do overlap, say so plainly, because I'd rather find out now than in my feedback.
