I'm taking a course on collective responsibility this term and we just did the inefficacy objection -- you know, the line that one person's flight or burger makes no difference to climate outcomes so there's no point in not taking it, the whole King/Lewis style debate about thresholds and drops in the bucket. Anyway I've got to write a short paper picking some real practice and asking what it implicitly presupposes, and I want to use the carbon offset boxes airlines make you click through when you book a flight. You pay a few quid extra and the airline promises to plant trees or fund a wind farm somewhere, and then you're told you can fly "guilt free" or "carbon neutral."

So the question I want to ask is basically: what view of individual contributions to collective harms does that little checkbox only make sense under? Because it looks like it presupposes something like a compensation model -- that my one flight causes a discrete chunk of harm, and that the chunk can be individually measured (that's why the price differs by route) and individually cancelled out by an equivalent chunk of good. But that seems to sit really awkwardly with the inefficacy stuff we've been reading. If my single flight's marginal effect on climate harms is basically zero (no threshold gets crossed, no particular person's crop fails because of me), then what exactly is being compensated? The practice seems to presuppose a difference-making view of my contribution at exactly the point where philosophers say the difference-making view is most implausible. But maybe I'm wrong -- maybe the practice is compatible with an expected utility story or with some "fair share of a collective harm" view where I owe a contribution proportional to my participation regardless of marginal effects.

There's also a second weirdness: the same airlines that sell me the offset will, in their lobbying and PR, insist that individual flights are negligible compared to systemic factors, and governments basically treat aviation emissions as something dealt with by policy not personal choice. So the airline seems to presuppose difference-making when it's charging me and deny it when it's defending itself. For my paper I'd love if you could reconstruct what theory of individual contribution to collective harm the offset checkbox actually presupposes, distinguish what it strictly requires from what it merely fits nicely with, say whether the compensation framing is internally coherent given the inefficacy objection, and tell me what the practice would have to look like instead if the airline took the expected-utility view or the fair-shares view seriously -- like, would the box even survive, or would it turn into something quite different? Please dont just give me the standard "drops in the ocean" survey, I want the presupposition analysis specifically.
