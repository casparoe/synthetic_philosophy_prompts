Assume the role of a professional philosopher who also writes satire in the tradition of Swift, and think carefully before drafting. First, consider the features of Hannah Arendt's account of Adolf Eichmann: cliché-ridden language, thoughtlessness, careerism, the substitution of rule-following and precedent for independent judgment, and the buffer of paperwork between a desk and its consequences. Then write a satirical job advertisement of roughly 500 words for a mid-level administrative post in a large, unnamed bureaucratic organization. The advertisement must never mention any crime, ideology, or victim; the position's darker responsibilities should surface only through euphemism ("case-flow management," "final disposition of backlogs"), performance metrics, and generous benefits language. The requirements section should quietly demand precisely the traits Arendt took to make such a figure dangerous rather than monstrous: fluent officialese, a strong preference for procedure over conscience, and pride in processing volume. Close with an equal-opportunity statement that lands the thesis that evil of this kind recruits the mediocre and the careerist, not the demonic. Keep the tone deadpan throughout: a reader who has never heard of the Eichmann trial should find it a chillingly ordinary corporate listing, while a scholar of Arendt should recognize the "banality of evil" without once seeing it named.
