I've been trying to figure out what I actually believe about animals by writing down my gut verdicts on cases and seeing if they add up to anything. Eating factory-farmed pork: fine, I do it without thinking. Eating a dog that was raised and slaughtered the same way the pig was: no, and honestly I'd judge someone who did. A new drug that was tested on 500 chimps and saves one child: fine, I'd take the drug. The same drug tested on 500 cognitively disabled humans whose capacities are no higher than the chimps': obviously monstrous, not even close. Not swerving for a squirrel when swerving is mildly inconvenient: fine. Not swerving for a dog when swerving is perfectly safe: not fine, I'd feel sick about it. Donating to a guide-dog charity (~$50k to help one blind person): warm glow. Being told the same money could restore sight to hundreds of people in a poor country: suddenly the guide dogs seem hard to defend, but I don't think the charity is actually doing wrong. Killing a mouse in my kitchen with a snap trap: no hesitation. Killing it slowly because the snap traps were out of stock: no, I'd drive to another store. I can't tell if this all just amounts to speciesism — maybe the chimp/human pair forces me to admit that bare species membership is doing all the work, and the dog-meat thing is just cultural? Or is there some defensible principle about capacities, relationships, or the roles these animals occupy that saves most of it? What's the principle (or best couple of candidates) that captures these verdicts, which ones does it force me to drop, and what does it predict for cases I haven't listed — like eating lab-grown pork versus meat from a pig that died of old age on a sanctuary?
