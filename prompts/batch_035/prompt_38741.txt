My friend Maya has, over like two years of arguments, built up a track record on the immoral-art question, and I've finally started writing her verdicts down because they feel systematic. So far: Triumph of the Will — she says the craft is undeniable, on pure technique terms it's brilliant, but she can't bring herself to call it a good film, because glorifying the thing it glorifies is the whole point, and that poisons it. The Cantos — great poem, the fascist stuff is a real stain but it isn't what the poem is *for*, so it stays great. Caravaggio — the man literally killed someone, and to her this has literally nothing to do with the paintings, masterpieces, end of story. A certain torture-happy cop thriller — a good film, but she insists that the torture always working and being framed as necessary is an actual artistic flaw, not just a moral one, just not big enough to sink the whole thing. And Lolita — great novel precisely because of how it handles its monster, the danger of it is part of the design, so it comes out clean for her.

Three new cases came up and I want you to predict her calls before I text her. (1) A standup comic who is a genuine virtuoso — timing, callbacks, crowd work, everything — but the material is basically all cruelty aimed at one group, and the cruelty isn't incidental, the wit IS the cruelty. There is no version of the act with the cruelty sanded off that would still be good. (2) A songwriter puts out an incredible album of love songs, nothing morally off in the songs themselves at all, but it comes out that for years he'd been grooming young fans and the songs were partly the instrument of it — does she call the album good art? (3) A gorgeous nature documentary that turns out to have fabricated its central sequence — the deception of the audience is the moral failure, but the craft of the fake sequence is honestly superb, better than anything in it.

What I want from you, sections named and in this exact order: "Predictions" (one sentence each for the three cases, stated plainly), "Candidate rules" (two to four rules that each fit her whole track record so far, a line each — and no, you don't get to count her ambiguity on Triumph of the Will as a fifth rule), "Best fit" (which rule you think she's actually running and why, two short paragraphs), and "The tiebreaker" (invent one further case — don't reuse mine — where the two best-fitting rules give opposite verdicts, and say which verdict would go with which rule, so I know what to throw at her next). Roughly 600 words total. Be honest if her record underdetermines the prediction on any of the three — I'd rather hear that than a fake confidence, she has burned me on this before. Also don't just call everything "it depends on your theory of art," I can do that myself for free, the whole point of the exercise is to figure out what rule a person is running from a finite set of rulings. If it helps, the thing that keeps me up at night is case 2 — she's absolutist about Caravaggio, biography is noise to her, but if the works themselves were part of the harm I genuinely can't predict what she'll say.
