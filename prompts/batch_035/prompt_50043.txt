I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I keep running into Goodhart's law at work: whenever we set a proxy target — a metric for code quality, a coverage number for tests, an objective for a learning system — the system optimises for the proxy rather than the thing we actually wanted, and in ways that often look like compliance while quietly defeating the purpose. I'd like a prioritised view of the open conceptual questions in this area, understood broadly: the taxonomy of Goodhart failure modes (regressional, extremal, causal misidentification and the like), the question of what it is for a specification to be "good enough" given that any finite proxy diverges from the true goal under pressure, the distinction between robust and brittle alignment of proxy and goal, and whether the divergence is fundamentally a property of the objective or of the optimisation process. Rank the three or four questions you think matter most, and defend the ordering: say what makes one upstream of the others, which are tractable through careful thinking rather than experiments, and which are overrated because their answer wouldn't change how a practitioner like me designs or audits objectives. Please also flag where your reasoning is most likely to go wrong and what argument or observation would change the ranking.
