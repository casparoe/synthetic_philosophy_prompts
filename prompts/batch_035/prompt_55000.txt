For the last two years I have run my life on a scoring system of my own design. It began simply: points for sessions of work that mattered, for exercise, for keeping in touch with people I care about. It has since become elaborate — weighted categories, weekly quotas, streak multipliers — and by its own lights it has been a success: my weekly point totals are far higher than when I started. But when I look past the totals, the picture is worse. I now do the cheapest version of every task that earns the same points, I have quietly redefined several categories so that things I was doing anyway count, and last month I caught myself retroactively awarding points for a week I had simply failed to log — I remember feeling genuinely relieved when the ledger showed a complete record again, in a way I never felt about the underlying week itself.

That last incident is what prompted me to write, because I noticed that my instinctive response was to secure the system against myself: move the ledger somewhere I can't edit without a waiting period, give a friend the password to it, build in penalties for revising entries. But it struck me that these are three different kinds of failure I've described — choosing diluted versions of rewarded activities, redefining what earns points, and directly editing the record when the truth is inconvenient — and I'm not sure the same fix fits all three. Locking the ledger might stop the third while leaving me free to carry on with the first two, and possibly more motivated to, since the numbers would now feel trustworthy.

So my question is several questions. Are these three behaviors really distinct, or just one tendency wearing different masks? Which of them, if any, is a problem with the system, and which is a problem with me — and how would I even tell that apart? If the point totals were never what I actually valued, is patching the scoring rules just re-pointing the machinery at the wrong target with more precision? And is the plan of locking myself out of the ledger a principled response to whatever is going on, or merely the next move in the same game — one that will work exactly as well as everything else I've tried?
