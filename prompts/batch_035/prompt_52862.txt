so at some point over christmas my brother in law and I got into it about referendums (he's very much a "the people are idiots, that's why we have parliament" type), and it made me want to actually write down what I believe, because I'm supposed to give a short position piece at my local politics society — we're debating whether our city should introduce swiss-style citizen initiatives and local ballot measures. before I submit the piece I want a consistency check on my actual views, since I've never held them all at once in front of me. here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) on any big constitutional question — leaving the EU, rewriting a constitution, transferring major powers — a referendum is more legitimate than letting parliament settle it, since mps are whipped party animals and at least a referendum is the people speaking directly; (2) the median voter is rationally ignorant on these questions, i.e. the chance your individual vote matters is so tiny that no one has a genuine incentive to learn what a customs union or a transfer pricing rule actually does; (3) referendum outcomes should be fully binding even at 51-49 — no supermajority requirement, no turning it into an "advisory" plebiscite after the fact like critics wanted with Brexit; (4) courts should nonetheless be able to strike down or refuse to implement referendum results that trample minority rights — I was basically fine with how the Swiss handled it when parliament effectively declined to fully implement that popular initiative on expelling criminal foreigners, and I'd want a constitutional court to have that power too; (5) referendum campaigns need strict spending caps, officially published information material, and cooling-off rules, because otherwise moneyed interests can simply buy the result; (6) political legitimacy is about the process being fair and inclusive, not about the outcome being correct or wise.

So which of these actually pull against each other? My instinct is that (2) is the awkward one — my brother in law kept hammering it — but I suspect there's a decent reply to that, namely that the comparison is with parliament and not with some idealised informed electorate. What worries me more is whether (3) and (4) can live together, or whether I'm telling two stories about what a referendum is. And I genuinely can't work out whether (5) and (6) are a problem. Anyway: go through the set, tell me which pairs are in real tension and which only look like it, rank the tensions by severity rather than just flagging everything, say what would have to give for each and what that would cost me, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I'd rather find out now than get picked apart at the debate — I don't know which one I'd drop if forced, so don't let me off with a "they're all reconcilable really" either.
