ok so I'm writing up my epistemological profile for a reading group and I want it stress-tested before the others get at it. Here's roughly the package: (1) I'm a foundationalist — justification chains have to terminate somewhere, and they terminate in beliefs that are justified noninferentially. (2) The noninferential justifiers are seemings: perceptual seemings for observational beliefs, intuitions for a priori ones. (3) Everything is defeasible — I'm not claiming indubitable foundations, any seeming can be discounted when there's undefeated reason to distrust it in that setting. (4) I'm an internalist, and pretty strongly: if you can't say what your grounds are, you don't have grounds. (5) Coherentism is a non-starter as far as im concerned, circular justificaiton isn't justification. (6) No epistemic principle can confer justification unless the subject has some justification for taking it to be truth-conducive — otherwise relying on it is just dogma. (7) Infinitism I don't take seriously, nobody can actually believe infinitely many premises. And (8) I take it as a datum that ordinary perceptual beliefs are justified — I'm not a skeptic. Does this hang together? Which pairs are in real tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix for each? Rank them by severity rather than flagging everything as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
