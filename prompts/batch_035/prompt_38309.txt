For the past twelve years I've helped run a peer support group for adults diagnosed with Asperger's syndrome, and next month we're holding a dinner to announce the membership's decision on renaming the group, now that the diagnosis has been folded into autism spectrum disorder. I've been asked to give the closing speech, and I'm quietly dreading it. Years ago a magazine article introduced me to a philosopher's idea that has stuck with me ever since: when doctors create a category for people, the people in it start to understand themselves through it, form communities, and change what the category even means, so it never stays a simple description of what was already there. That has been my life. The Asperger's label didn't just find something in me; it gave me a self-understanding and a room full of friends I wouldn't otherwise have. But some of our members want the old word gone entirely, given its ugly history, while others feel that abandoning it would erase what we built around it. The audience will be about eighty people: founding members, family members, younger people who have only ever identified as autistic, and a few clinicians who have referred folks to us for years. Please write the speech. It needs to let the renaming go through without suggesting we were wrong to build our lives around the old word, and also without suggesting that word was simply a neutral discovery of what we had been all along. I'm not a philosopher, and neither is most of the room, but the ones who are will notice if I get it wrong.
