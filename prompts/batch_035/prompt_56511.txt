In my philosophy of mind course we've just finished a unit on Dennett's stances, and to review I watched a video essay that summarized his position as follows: Dennett holds that there are three predictive strategies — the physical stance, which applies the laws of nature; the design stance, which assumes a thing was built for a purpose and predicts it will function as designed; and the intentional stance, which treats a system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires. According to the video, Dennett's key claim is that the intentional stance can always be applied — any system whatsoever, even a thermostat or a rock, counts as a believer from some vantage point — so beliefs and desires are not real things in the brain at all, merely useful fictions, on a par with a center of gravity. The video adds that Dennett's work on "real patterns" supports this, since a pattern just is whatever many observers agree they can discern in the data, and concludes that Dennett is ultimately an eliminativist: the mind, strictly speaking, does not exist. My professor emphasized that Dennett resists being called a simple instrumentalist, so I suspect the video is subtly off in places, but I can't pinpoint where. Could you go through this characterization and tell me which parts Dennett would accept, which parts misrepresent him, and whether the eliminativist conclusion actually follows from the parts that are accurate?
