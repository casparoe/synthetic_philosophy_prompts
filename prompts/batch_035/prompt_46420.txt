so i've gotten pretty deep into buddhist ethics lately and i've ended up holding a bunch of commitments that i suspect don't all fit together, but i can't see it from the inside. here's roughly where i've landed:

(1) the whole system bottoms out in reducing suffering — an act is right insofar as it lessens dukkha, where dukkha includes mental stuff like craving and agitation, not just physical pain
(2) given (1), buddhist ethics is basically a species of consequentalism, just with a much richer account of what counts as a harm than western utilitarians had
(3) but i also take the no-self doctrine seriously — there are no enduring selves, just streams of momentary processes — and i suspect this actually undercuts the whole idea of aggregating welfare person-by-person, since there's nothing solid there to aggregate over
(4) i think the karmic valence of an act is fixed entirely by the intention behind it — the same act with the same consequences is worse done from malice than from carelessness, full stop
(5) i hold the precepts as absolute: no intentional killing, ever, even in a case where killing one person is the only way to prevent five deaths
(6) and i think non-attachment is the heart of the path — you ultimately have to relinquish grasping at outcomes, including the good outcome of making the world better

people keep telling me buddhist ethics is really a virtue ethics and i've never found that persuasive, so maybe i'm missing something there too. anyway, does this package hang together? go through my commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what the cheapest fix would be for each. don't just flag everything as bad — give me exactly five numbered points, ordered worst to least bad, each one a sentence or two, no intro or conclusion. i'm trying to figure out what i actually believe before i write this up for my sangha's discussion night.
