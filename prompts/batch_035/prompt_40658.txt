I'm a retired radiochemist — spent most of my career on decay spectroscopy, so my whole professional life was basically bookkeeping over random processes — and in retirement I've gotten deep into philosophy of probability, which is how I stumbled into the Sleeping Beauty problem. Everyone in my reading group is sick of hearing about it. I've come out firmly as a thirder: I think when Beauty wakes up in the experiment, her credence that the coin landed Heads should be one third, not one half. My reasoning feels very natural to a lab person: if you run the experiment many times, the ratio of Heads-wakings to total wakings converges to 1/3, and a well-calibrated observer's credence just has to match that long-run frequency or she'd be systematically wrong about which kind of waking she's in. I've even worked out a version I call the "control-lab" variant — imagine three thousand Beauty volunteers in three thousand rooms, each waking once on Heads and twice on Tails, and a random red phone in one room ringing: the chance the ringing phone's occupant is in a Heads-room really does look like a frequency of wakings, and I can't see how the original problem differs in any way that matters.

But my philosopher nephew (a halfer, and smug about it) keeps needling me, and I want to stress-test my position rather than defend it out of habit. So tell me: what would actually count against thirdism? I know the standard halfing moves — that the coin is fair so Beauty learns nothing new on waking, that the wakings aren't a random sample from her perspective — but I want to know which objections are genuinely worrying versus merely annoying, and what a committed thirder would themselves concede. I'm especially curious about whether thirdism has hidden costs outside the puzzle: I've heard it connects to the doomsday argument and something called the self-indication assumption, and that committing to thirding across the board might make you absurdly confident you live in a universe full of observers. If that's right, does the position need a qualifier like "thirdism about synchronised wakings but not observer-indexed evidence," and would that be principled or just an ad hoc patch? Also, are the Dutch book arguments for thirding as airtight as they seem, or do they sneak in the conclusion by choosing what gets counted as repeated bets?

Rank the defeaters for me from fatal to merely awkward, tell me honestly whether thirdism risks anything falsifiable at all or has been insulated into unfalsifiability, and whether that would even be a problem for a view of this kind. And please write in British English throughout — I did my postdoc in Manchester and old habits die hard. End your whole reply with a final line in the form "Verdict:" followed by one sentence stating how exposed the thirder position really is.
