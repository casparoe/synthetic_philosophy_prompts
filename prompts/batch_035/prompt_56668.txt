My friend and I have been arguing about the franchise for the better part of a year — she read philosophy, I'm reading public policy — and I've started to notice that her positions across a series of cases form a pattern I can't quite pin down. Over that time she has come down firmly as follows: long-settled non-citizen residents (people who have lived, worked and paid taxes in this country for two decades without ever taking citizenship) should be allowed to vote both in local council elections and in national parliamentary ones; tourists and seasonal workers on visitor visas should not be allowed to vote in anything; citizens who emigrated thirty years ago, hold no property here and no longer pay taxes should lose their vote in our elections; convicted prisoners serving their sentences should keep the vote; and once someone is enfranchised, she thinks voting should be made compulsory, backed by a modest fine for abstention. Next month our city holds a referendum on whether to close several schools and reroute the bus network, and the council is considering a one-off extension of the franchise for that referendum to sixteen- and seventeen-year-olds, who would otherwise remain excluded.

I'm less interested in her eventual answer than in the underlying rule. As far as I can tell, more than one principle would rationalise everything she has said so far — perhaps something like "everyone whose interests are governed by a body for a sustained period gets a say in that body", perhaps something like "the vote attaches to full membership of the cooperative scheme, that is, to those who bear its duties", perhaps something else entirely. My suspicion is that these rules come apart precisely on the referendum question, since teenagers are plainly affected by school closures and bus routes but bear few of the duties of membership — yet I can't tell which way her commitments cut, and I worry that she could answer either way and still be consistent with herself.

Could you therefore predict her verdict on extending the vote to the sixteen- and seventeen-year-olds for this referendum? In doing so, please lay out the candidate rules that fit her track record, say which rule you think she is tacitly running and why, give your best guess with your reasons for preferring it, and propose the single further case — involving, if you like, expatriate property owners, newborn citizens, or a sixteen-year-old who has left school and works full time — that would best discriminate between the rules you identify. Please write your answer in British English.
