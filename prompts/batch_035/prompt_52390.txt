I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics, and the part of the Everettian literature I keep circling back to is the Born rule problem: how a theory in which every outcome of a measurement occurs, each with a successor who sees that outcome, can also say that some outcomes are more probable than others. My sense is that this debate has been running in place for a while now, and I want a diagnosis of the stall rather than another restatement of the positions. On one side there is the decision-theoretic program, descending from Deutsch and Wallace, which argues that a rational agent in a branching world should weight branch outcomes by squared amplitude, deriving that from axioms about rational preference — things like diachronic consistency (don't prefer a branch now that you'll regret once you're in it), branching indifference (don't care about branching per se, only about what happens in the branches), and continuity as amplitudes vary. On another side there is the self-locating uncertainty program, in the vein of Sebens and Carroll, which locates probability in the agent's ignorance, right before the measurement resolves, about which future copy she is — with the Born weights emerging from how the Hilbert space decomposes into equally weighted, qualitatively indistinguishable sectors. And arrayed against both are the skeptics: some say branch counting is arbitrary because the number of branches depends on how you carve the Hilbert space into a basis and how finely you resolve the apparatus, so there is no fact of the matter to hang probabilities on; some say the continuum limit reintroduces measure-theoretic conventionality at exactly the point where the derivation needs to be non-conventional; some follow a line like Greaves's and concede that Everettian agents have no credences to speak of but can still have a "caring measure" over branches, which they say quietly changes the subject from probability to something else; and some just give the whole project an incredulous stare and deny that probability talk makes sense at all when everything happens. It may also be that part of the fight is verbal, turning on whether "probability" is being used for credence, for frequency, or for the quantum amplitude measure itself, with each camp silently fixing a different one.
