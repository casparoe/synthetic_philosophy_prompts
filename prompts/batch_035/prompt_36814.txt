I am a retired computational physicist who has spent the last few years reading philosophy of mind and ethics in a more serious way, and there is a configuration I keep running into for which I cannot find an established term. In my working life, the question of which computation a physical system is performing was never settled by the physics alone. The same trajectory through state space could be read, at one temporal and spatial resolution and with one choice of tracked variables, as implementing a particular algorithm, and at another resolution as implementing a different one or nothing worth calling a computation at all. Nothing in the underlying equations privileged one reading; the choice of descriptive grain was ours, and it came from what we cared about predicting. I thought of this as a practical nuisance. Having now read arguments that moral status might attach to computations rather than to the matter implementing them, I realize it may be a philosophical problem: if what matters morally is the computation, and which computation a lump of matter performs is fixed only relative to our bookkeeping, then the boundary of moral concern appears to be fixed by our choice of description rather than by the world, and I can see nothing that keeps that choice from being made arbitrarily.

Let me sharpen this with something I noticed in simulation work and have not seen discussed. Some patterns in a dynamical system survive a wide family of coarsenings and variable choices — they show up as the same pattern however you squint — while others appear only under the single description that was reverse-engineered to produce them. I have a suspicion that the width of that family of admissible descriptions is doing the work of making a pattern real, rather than an artifact of our conventions, and that moral weight should perhaps track that width. But I am guessing at concepts here. The nearest thing I have actually met in my reading is multiple realizability, and that is pointed the wrong way: it concerns one computation on many substrates, whereas my difficulty is one substrate under many competing descriptions, none uniquely favored. I am told there is also literature on whether the description-dependence of computation can be pushed so far that almost anything can be counted as implementing almost anything, which sounds adjacent, but I do not know what that result is called or whether it addresses the attribution problem rather than the triviality one.

So, my questions, and I warn you that a bare label will not satisfy me. Does the configuration I have described — a property that is well-defined only relative to a choice of descriptive grain, where that choice is unconstrained by the underlying physics, and where moral or other important attributions are meant to depend on the property — have a name in the philosophical literature? If so, what exactly does the named concept cover, and does it include the attributional side or only the metaphysical side? If no single term covers it, tell me the nearest ones and precisely where each fails to fit; and if the right response is that nothing established covers it, then coin a term for it, give the definition, and show me the boundary cases — in particular, say something about whether my robustness-under-many-coarsenings idea is a genuine criterion or just a way of smuggling our interests back in under a mathematical disguise.
