i help organize a meetup for prediction-market people (manifold degens, a couple ex-Metaculus folks, some DAO governance types) and our year-end session is on futarchy — Robin Hanson's old proposal that we should, in his words, "vote on values, but bet on beliefs": elected representatives define and measure national welfare, and a policy becomes law when a betting market clearly estimates that it would raise expected welfare. i want to close the night with a poem, and since futarchy is literally a proposal to split politics in two, it feels like the form should do the splitting: write a villanelle where one refrain is the values half (something ballot-flavored, about voters saying what we want) and the other refrain is the beliefs half (something market-flavored, about prices saying how to get it). the villanelle seems perfect for this because the two refrains come back seperate through the whole poem and only end up side by side in the final couplet — use that to gesture at the objection our skeptics always raise, that the split isn't as clean as Hanson wants: what people "value" in a policy is tangled up with what they think it signals about them, and the welfare measure itself is a value judgment smuggled in early. take the role of a poet who actually understands prediction markets — the put-your-money-where-your-mouth-is thing, traders who don't know eventually go broke and stop talking, conditional bets that get called off if the policy never gets tried — accurate enough that a Hanson reader wouldn't wince, but still a good poem for the half of the meetup that just likes markets and doesn't know the theory. standard 19-line villanelle, aba rhyme. think through where each refrain lands before writing, that placement is doing most of the work here.
