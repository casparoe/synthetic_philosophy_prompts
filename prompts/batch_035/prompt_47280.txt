I'm taking an upper-level epistemology course, and for my presentation on the James–Clifford debate I'd like a one-page decision tree that sorts my classmates into positions on the ethics of belief. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: work out first what the branch questions are, then locate the actual positions in the resulting grid. The branching should turn on concrete questions a classmate can answer without knowing any label — for instance, whether it can be permissible to believe somewhat ahead of the evidence when the belief is self-fulfilling (confidence that improves one's performance), whether a comforting falsehood told to a dying friend can be believed rather than merely stated, whether, where evidence genuinely runs out on a choice that is both forced and momentous, a tie may be broken by passion, and whether believing is ever up to us in the way that duties of belief presuppose. The leaves should include strict Cliffordian evidentialism, a threshold evidentialism permitting belief when the evidence is merely balanced, James's restricted permission, an unrestricted pragmatism on which we may believe whatever benefits us, and the deflationist view that without doxastic voluntariness there are no duties concerning belief at all. For any combination the grid allows but no leaf occupies, say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; and flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot really separate — in particular, say whether it can distinguish James's permission from ordinary wishful thinking. Assume the audience has read both primary texts but none of the secondary literature.
