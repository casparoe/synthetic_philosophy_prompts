i write novels, and i've realized that over years of building tragic scenes i've settled into a set of beliefs about moral choice that i lean on every time i plot a character into a corner. i've never once checked whether they actually fit together. here they are:

1. i don't believe genuine moral dilemmas exist. for any choice a person faces, however horrifying, there is a fact of the matter about which option is better. the tragedy is our ignorance, not the world's indeterminacy.
2. i think guilt can be fitting even when you did the morally better thing. the mother who gives up one child so the other survives and never forgives herself — i want the reader to feel her guilt as an appropriate response, not as a mistake she's making about her own record.
3. i hold that ought implies can. you cannot be obligated to do the impossible.
4. i think tragic scenes land on readers precisely because the reader senses that whichever way the character goes, something morally required gets left undone. that's where the dread comes from — not from bad outcomes but from the sense of a debt that will now never be paid.
5. i judge my characters by what they could reasonably have known and done in the moment, not by facts only the narrator has — though i also think there's a real fact about what the better choice was, one that even the character couldn't have accessed.

some of these feel obviously in tension to me, some feel obviously fine, and honestly i'm not sure my instincts about which is which are worth anything — i keep writing scenes that commit me to all five at once. go through the set and tell me which pairs actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damning. for each real one, tell me what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs me as a writer — like, does fixing it in the obvious place flatten the tragedy i'm trying to create, or is that a fake cost? and if you think the whole set is actually consistent, don't be polite about it, make the case, because i suspect i'm missing something.
