I'm taking a course on Bayesian epistemology this semester and we just hit the part on updating when your evidence isn't just some proposition being true. Jeffrey conditioning is fine for when you get a new credence over a partition, but the prof then brought up this old problem (I think due to van Fraassen? it's called the Judy Benjamin problem after the person in the story) and told us it's still open in a sense, which honestly kind of blew my mind. The story: a soldier is dropped by parachute into enemy territory at night, she has no idea where she is, and figures it equally likely she's in Blue territory or Red territory, or in the no man's land in between. Then she recieves a radio message saying something to the effect of: if you're in no man's land, then odds are 3:1 you're closer to Blue company than Red company. That's all she gets. The message is silent about no man's land itself — it only reshuffles things conditionally on already being there. So her credence in no man's land itself should stay untouched, but conditional on being there, "closer to Blue" goes from a half to 3/4.

Here's what I can't get straight, and it's what our whole section argued about. What everyone seems to agree on is that the new credence in no man's land should equal her old credence in no man's land — that part seems forced by the setup, since the message is entirely conditional on that hypothesis. Then within the no man's land part, you redistribute proportionally to 3:1, so conditional on being in no man's land, the Blue-side gets 3/4, Red-side gets 1/4. Fine. But it turns out that's not enough: the constraint "P(new no man's land) = P(old no man's land)" is weaker than the constraint people actually use, and depending on how exactly you tighten it you get different final numbers for the ordinary unconditional probabilities. The prof mentioned one approach uses a rule about staying as close as possible to your old credences under some measure, and that different measures give different answers, none obviously canonical. One TA said the 2/3-1/4 answer is the one you'd get by making her new conditional credence in Blue-side-given-no-man's-land equal 3/4 through a particular distance-minimizing rule, but another TA insisted a different rule was more natural and gave different results, and neither of them would say who's right.

Also there's one thing about this that bothers me more than the choice of measure, and nobody in section could address it. Suppose the radio message had said 100:1 instead of 3:1 — near-certain, conditionally. It seems like almost any rule on the table then drags her unconditional credence in "closer to Blue" up toward something like a half *no matter where it started*, because the message can shift all the conditional weight but can't touch the no man's land probability itself. Is that actually right, or am I misdescribing how the update goes? So I guess my question is two-fold: does this problem of which constraint to impose / which distance to minimize have a name or a settled literature, or is it genuinely open? And is there a principled reason the answer should be unique at all, or is this a case where the formalism just doesn't pin anything down and the different rules are all "rational"? If the latter, I'd like to hear what the strongest candidate rules are and on what grounds one would even argue between them, since at the moment it looks to me like people just pick whichever measure they already like for other reasons.
