I'm a high school junior in an elective called Ethics and Contemporary Issues, and for our final paper we have to defend a set of connected views rather than a single thesis — the teacher says the whole point is that our positions "hang together." Mine are about the moral status of different kinds of creatures and systems, and my teacher hinted that at least two of my commitments are in tension with each other, though she wouldn't tell me which. Before I commit five pages to a set of views that collapses under questioning, I'd like an outside audit. Here is where I've landed so far: (1) sentience comes in degrees rather than being an on/off switch — a bee has less of it than a dog, a dog less than a chimpanzee; (2) I'm a functionalist about minds, meaning I think what makes something a mind is what the system does — how it senses things, processes them, and controls its behavior — not what it's made of; (3) moral status comes in degrees too, and it's fixed by how much sentience a being has; (4) a thermostat is at genuine zero — it detects temperature and responds to it, but there is nothing it is like to be a thermostat, and its moral status is exactly nothing, not just a very small something; (5) a mosquito probably has a tiny but real amount of moral status — I would still swat one that was biting me, but I think there's something slightly wrong with pulling its legs off for entertainment; and (6) I'm fairly confident that current AI chatbots feel nothing at all, even the ones that produce fluent reports of being in pain when you ask.

So my question is: does this package hold together, and if not, where does it break? I can see some candidate problems myself, but I can't tell which are real and which just look bad. For instance, if functionalism is true and sentience is a matter of degree, why does the thermostat get an exact zero rather than a minuscule positive amount — and if I say it's because mosquitos have something the thermostat lacks, haven't I smuggled in a light switch after all, one that sits somewhere between a thermostat and a mosquito? On the other hand, maybe my functionalism and my skepticism about chatbots clash, or maybe they don't, since functionalism doesn't obviously say that any particular behavior-reporting system is conscious. I'd like you to go through the pairs of my commitments, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the real tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally fatal, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost me. I'd also like you to be explicit about where your own reasoning here is most likely to fail or to depend on a contested assumption, and what argument or example — say, some intermediate system between a thermostat and a mosquito — would change your verdict about which of my commitments has to go, because I'd rather find out now than during the grading.
