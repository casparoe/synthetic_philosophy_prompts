Is there a name for this? I'm a student taking philosophy of science and a machine learning course at the same time, and I keep noticing what feels like the same underlying issue in both, but nobody in either class uses the other field's vocabulary for it. In phil of sci we talked about how general relativity gets credit for explaining the wobble in Mercury's orbit, even though astronomers had measured that anomaly decades before Einstein was even born -- the evidence wasn't new, only the explanation was. In my ML class we just covered overfitting: a model that scores well on data it was trained on is telling you basically nothing, and you only trust performance on held-out data the model never saw. When I brought up the analogy in section, my TA pushed back and said they're not the same, because Einstein didn't fit GR to the Mercury data the way a network with millions of free parameters gets fitted to a training set -- GR also predicted light bending, which was only checked later. But then someone else pointed out that plenty of other physicists at the time did try to explain the Mercury anomaly with ad hoc tweaks (extra hidden planets and so on) that never went anywhere, so it's not like no one was "training on" that data point. So here's what I want to know: does the thing I'm pointing at -- the intuition that evidence only counts in a theory's favor if the theory didn't already have access to it -- have an established name in either field? I've heard people say "novel prediction" but it sounds more like a slogan than a precise concept, and I have no idea whether philosophers of science and ML people have ever seriously noticed they're circling the same worry. If there's no single concept that covers both cases, then give me the best carving you can: what exactly is the common core, where do the two cases genuinely come apart (like, what is even supposed to count as a theorist having "seen" the data -- reading a journal article? growing up in a culture that knows the fact?), and coin a term for the unified thing with boundary cases. I won't be satisfied with just a label, I want to know what the phenomenon is and isn't. Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence and then give the reasoning -- no preamble and dont restate my question back at me.
