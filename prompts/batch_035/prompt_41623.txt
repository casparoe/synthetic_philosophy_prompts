I'm a backend engineer, never took a philosophy class, and the only philosophy I know comes from arguing in a group chat with two guys from work, so keep that in mind. We've been going around and around on updateless decision theory (UDT, or functional decision theory / FDT as the later variants are called — the idea that you pick policies/whole strategies as if you're deciding for every computation doing the same reasoning as you, rather than picking a single act given what you've actually observed). And it's been going on forever, like since the late 2000s in the corners of the internet where people argue about this, and from what I can tell nobody ever switches sides. The loop is always the same: someone brings up Newcomb or counterfactual mugging, one side says the updateless agent just wins and that's what rationality is for, the other side says no, you're exploiting squishiness in what counts as "the same algorithm" and anyway you've smuggled precommitments in and renamed them a theory, then someone points out the whole thing assumes you can reason about your own source code, and it dissolves into vibes.

So my actual question is a diagnosis, not a verdict. What kind of disagreement is this? My working hypothesis, take it or leave it: the two camps aren't disagreeing about any decision, they're disagreeing about what the word "decision" even denotes. One side thinks a decision is an event that happens at a time, inside an agent that has already learned things — so conditionalizing on your observations isn't optional, it's what deciding means, and updatelessness is a sneaky way of answering a different question. The other side thinks a decision is an evaluation of a whole agent-algorithm, so the question "what should I do now, given what I've seen?" is confused in the same way "what is north of the north pole" is confused. If I'm right, it's a partly verbal dispute and progress would look like a formalism that has both camps as special cases of one thing, parameterized somehow.

But I could be totally off, and there are real technical cruxes that might be doing the actual work, like logical uncertainty (UDT seems to require reasoning about the outputs of computations you can't run, and I genuinely don't think anyone knows how to make that precise), or the fact that updateless agents do things that look insane case-by-case (paying up in counterfactual mugging when the coin actually landed against you and the CDT agent keeps the money and is fine, thank you very much). Diagnose which it is — verbal, or a hidden technical problem, or bedrock intuitions about cases, or a fight about what counts as "solving" a decision theory at all — based on what the sides actually say, not a history lesson. And then be honest with me: given your diagnosis, is there anything (a theorem, a formalism, a killer thought experiment) that would actually end this, or should my group chat just agree to disagree and get back to arguing about tabs vs spaces?
