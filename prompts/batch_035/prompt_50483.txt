My credence that the procreation asymmetry is true — that creating a person with a good life is not, in itself, better for anyone than not creating that person, while creating a person with a bad life is worse for that person than not creating them — sits around 0.45. A friend of mine, who accepts total utilitarianism, puts it at 0.1. Please give me your own credence, but more importantly decompose it into the sub-questions it actually rests on: (a) the probability that some person-affecting restriction on axiology is coherent at all, given the non-identity problem and the problem of merely possible people; (b) conditional on such a restriction being coherent, the probability it can forbid creating miserable lives without also generating an obligation to create happy ones (the horn of the dilemma that pushes toward the repugnant conclusion if refused, and toward what critics call the sadistic conclusion if accepted); (c) the probability that the asymmetry is anything more than a status-quo or existence-bias framing effect, given that the intuition seems to weaken or invert when we compare creating a miserable life to adding more miserable lives to people who already exist, or when we consider a life that starts below zero and later rises far above it; and (d) any further terms you think belong — for instance, whether the asymmetry can survive the move from lives with known wellbeing to procreative decisions under uncertainty, where the expected value may be positive while the downside risk is severe. Give a number and one line of justification for each term, show how they combine into your final credence, and tell me which term you are least confident in and what argument or case would move it most. Finally, locate the disagreement between my friend and me: is it really about the asymmetry itself, or does it live in one of the upstream terms?
