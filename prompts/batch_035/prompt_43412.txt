write a satirical press release, maybe 500-600 words, from a fictional company called Lachrymosa Labs, which manufactures and sells artisanal sorrows — curated tragic experiences for the discerning consumer (a boxed set of elegies, a subscription to devastating operas, single-origin weeping). the occasion for the release: they are issuing a voluntary recall of one of their flagship products, the "Ophelia" (a lake-adjacent sadness, very popular), after a customer reported feeling actual, real-world grief. the spokesperson must insist throughout that all negative emotions are fully quarantined inside the fiction — that their proprietary safety barriers guarantee the distress never carries any belief with it, no action tendencies, nothing that could leak into the customer's real life — and the jokes should come from that very idea, since the safety/quarantine story is one of the standard replies to the paradox of tragedy (hume's puzzle of why we welcome in art the very thing we flee in life: the pain is free-floating, we get the texture of the emotion without any of its consequences, we can enjoy the feeling of pity because nothing is actually wrong). so the recall is funny because the company treats the paradox as a solved engineering problem and the incident as a breach of containment, and the deadpan should never let on that maybe the containment story was too optimistic. somewhere in the fine print at the end, slip in a clause acknowledging, without commentary, that customers report preferring their sorrows more intense than anything they would accept in real life — leave that sitting there. the release should be funny to someone who knows the tragedy literature (aristotle's catharsis, hume, the idea that we pity anna karenina while being glad no anna exists) but fully readable to someone who doesn't. no named real customers or reviewers, and dont quote any actual text — the quotes are all from invented employees. keep the corporate voice consistent, definately no winking at the reader, and let the closing line undercut the quarantine claim without saying so
