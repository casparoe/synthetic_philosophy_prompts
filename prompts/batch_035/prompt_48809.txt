I'm putting together a seminar unit on biological function, and before we read any of the literature I want the bar written down in advance. Here is the job description the theory needs to fill, and I want you to turn it into an explicit list of desiderata.

The account has to be good enough for three working uses. First, medicine: if a disorder is standardly glossed as a part's failure to perform its function, the account has to make sense of that gloss — including the normativity, the fact that a diseased heart is failing in a way a merely unused one is not. Second, contexts where selection history is absent, truncated, or artificial: enzymes evolved by directed evolution in the lab, pathways installed by genetic engineering in organisms like Golden Rice, and hybrid cases like Lenski's long-term lines, where a genuinely new function (citrate use) arose under selection that humans designed. Third, evolutionary argumentation generally, where a lot rides on not letting adaptationist storytelling convert every fortuitous benefit into a function.

So the desiderata should at least tell me what the account says about: Wright's contrast between the heart's pumping blood and its making thumping sounds; the selective-surplus case of sperm, where the disposition selected for is executed a vanishing fraction of the time; exapted traits such as feathers, where the selected effect and the current effect diverge — including the awkward transition period when both are in play; parts recruited wholesale from other jobs, like the enzymes that became lens crystallins; traits fixed by drift, which had better come out lacking functions or the account will contradict Gould and Lewontin's point; the engineered Golden Rice pathway, which has a function in every ordinary sense and no selection history at all; and sterile worker castes, whose functions benefit a colony rather than the bearer's own fitness.

The account must also respect some distinctions rather than collapse them: function versus mere use versus fortuitous benefit; the function of a trait for an organism versus for a lineage; and whether a trait can have a function it never once performs.

For each desideratum, give the case or consideration that motivates it, mark which are non-negotiable and which are just commitments of one camp, and identify any subset you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable — if the selected-effect account's grip on normativity and the need to cover novel and engineered functions cannot be met by one account, that itself is the finding I most want, so tell me which requirement you would drop. Also tell me which single requirement does the most to exclude candidates. Then a one-line scorecard against the list for the selected-effect, causal-role, and propensity accounts.

Finally, say where your reasoning about joint satisfiability is most likely to fail: which case or consideration, if it went the other way, would flip your verdict about which requirement to drop.
