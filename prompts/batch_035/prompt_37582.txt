Argue for this position: if you take wild animal suffering seriously, the ethically best diet might include grass-fed beef and exclude both chicken and most crops. The rough idea is that growing a calorie of wheat or beans involves tilling, pesticide, and harvesting a field that's habitat for mice, frogs, birds and insects, so each crop calorie comes with some pile of wild deaths attached, whereas a ruminant grazing permanent pasture that never gets ploughed might involve one death per several hundred thousand calories. Chicken is the worst of both worlds on this picture -- a bird dies for every meal or two, and the bird spent its whole life eating harvested grain, so you inherit the field deaths too. Take the argument seriously enough to lay out its strongest form, be explicit about which empirical numbers it is most sensitive to (whether pasture really hosts fewer animals per acre than cropland, how to weigh a cow's death against thousands of insects), and then tell me where you'd attack it first if you wanted to bring it down, since Im honestly not sure it survives contact with reality.
