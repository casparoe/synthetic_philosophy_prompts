I hold a version of the institutional critique of effective altruism: not the crude claim that effective altruists are indifferent to systemic change, but the more specific claim that EA's decision procedure is structurally biased toward the measurable. Because the movement's flagship method is to rank interventions by expected impact per dollar, using evidence of the kind that randomized trials and DALY-style metrics can produce, it will systematically over-fund interventions whose benefits are countable and under-fund those whose benefits are diffuse, slow, or political — institutional reform, state capacity, basic research, movement-building. On my view this is not a temporary gap that better measurement will close; it is a selection effect. The method determines which projects can compete at all, so the neglected interventions don't lose the competition — they never enter it.

Tell me what would count against this view. I'd like a ranked map, from fatal to merely awkward, covering: observations that would show the bias is real but self-correcting as measurement tools improve; cases where EA institutions have funded hard-to-measure work at scale, and what such cases would do to my claim that the invisible interventions never get to compete; and structural predictions my view seems to make (about the shape of grant portfolios) that could turn out false. Then press on the philosophical core: is my implicit assumption — that diffuse, political, or slow-moving benefits can't be given fair expected-value estimates — actually tenable? If expected-value reasoning can in principle handle deep uncertainty, does my critique collapse into the much weaker complaint that the current estimates are poor, and if so, is that weaker complaint enough to sustain an institutional critique at all? Finally, assess honestly whether the view as I've stated it has been insulated from disconfirmation — whether anything could in principle show it false — and whether such insulation is a defect in a claim of this kind. Please distinguish what a thoughtful effective altruist would concede as counting against the critique from what only its committed opponents would say, and conclude with your own judgment of how exposed the view really is.
