Write a piece of satire, around 650 words, in the form of a corporate press release followed by a short Q&A section. The press release is issued by a fictional management consultancy (you may invent the name) announcing the successful completion of its largest engagement to date: the 'subject consolidation' of a mid-sized client organisation — that is, the merging of a very large number of micro-experiences into a single unified macro-subject, the client being, of course, an ordinary human being. The consultants are thoroughgoing panpsychists, entirely sincere, and proud of their work.

The comedy must come from treating the combination problem as ordinary corporate housekeeping. Each of the standard difficulties should be aired as a line item in the announcement or a question in the Q&A, without ever naming them as difficulties. Concretely, I want you to cover:

- The subject-summing problem — how many subjects become one — handled as an ordinary post-merger restructuring question, with a reassuring note that no experiences were made redundant in the process, or, if they were, that they remain fully experienced at the micro level.
- The boundary problem — why the new subject ends where it does, rather than a little wider or narrower — handled as a matter of jurisdiction: which experiences fall inside the consolidated entity, which are spun off, and what severance looks like for an experience that finds itself outside the boundary.
- The palette problem — how a limited stock of very simple qualities yields the full range of macro-experience — handled as brand consolidation: the retirement of certain experiential product lines and the introduction of new colourways assembled from existing components, with management declining to explain where the new shades come from.
- The structural-mismatch problem — the fact that the structure we find at the micro level does not obviously resemble the structure of experience — handled as a routine discrepancy between the organisational chart and how the organisation actually feels from the inside.

Two further jokes should be available to you if you can land them, though do not force them: the fact that the consolidation proceeded without any mechanism, mechanism being exactly the thing management was at pains not to need; and the fact that the spokesperson delivering the announcement is herself a consolidation of much the same kind, which nobody in the room remarks upon.

Constraints. Keep the voice deadpan throughout: standard corporate-communications register, milliard-gratitude boilerplate, a quote from the 'chief integration officer', no winking at the reader. The satire's target is not panpsychism itself but the breeziness with which its hardest problem is sometimes waved away — a panpsychist reader should find the piece funny and, if anything, fair, recognising every line item as a real objection taken seriously and absurdly reassured. A reader who has never heard of the combination problem should still follow the whole thing: let the Q&A do whatever explaining is needed, in universe. Do not attribute the position to any living philosopher by name; the consultants speak for themselves. British English, please.
