I'm a PhD student in political philosophy (dissertation on distribution-sensitive views of justice, which is why its constantly on my mind). Our department runs a semi-satirical end-of-term newsletter and I volunteered to commission this year's flagship piece. I want you to write it.

The piece: a press release from the fictional Ministry of Equal Outcomes in the Republic of Veridia, announcing its "Leveling-Down Initiative" — a flagship policy which, following a comprehensive review, has made everyone in society worse off, but equally worse off. The ministry is very proud. The satire target is telic egalitarianism under the leveling-down objection, so the jokes have to come from the actual structure of the view: the supposed impersonal value of equality, the two claims (that it's bad that some are worse off than others, and that some being worse off than others is itself bad), and the temptation to count the mere disappearance of inequality as a gain even when it was achieved without anyone benefiting. At some point the minister should explain why a society where everyone has 4 units of welfare is to be preferred, in one respect, to one where the worst-off have 5 and the best-off have 6. Include a short FAQ section in which a citizen asks what they personally gained and is told, roughly, that they gained the disappearance of a comparative relationship — that's the telic egalitarian's predicament made flesh. And end with a dissenting annex from the Ministry's outsourced "priority consultant" (a prioritarian) who resigns over the whole thing: the annex should make the real prioritarian point, i.e., that what matters is benefits to people weighted by how badly off they are, not relative positions, so an across-the-board loss can't register as any kind of improvement at all.

Constraints: around 700 words, formatted like a genuine press release (headline, dateline, a boilerplate quote from the minister, press-office contact info at the bottom). Deadpan bureaucratic register throughout. The ministry shouldn't come off as evil or incompetent — the minister should sound like a sincere, thoughtful person who happens to accept the telic egalitarian's claims, and the comedy is that everything she says follows from them.

Two caveats. First, no strawmanning: a real telic egalitarian would say equality is one value among several, so the monomania is the exaggeration — but the core mechanism (counting an absolute loss to everyone as partly good because it removed inequality) has to be exactly the leveling-down case and not a caricature of it. Second, after the piece, append a note of about 150 words on where you took liberties: which bits lean on the telic/deontic egalitarianism distinction, whether the "one respect in which it's better" framing matches how the objection is usually stated, and whether the consultant's annex is actually how prioritarians present their escape route. I have a feeling there are complications involving the person-affecting claim that make this look easier than it is — tell me if so. And if you think I've misframed the dialectic — say the leveling-down objection doesn't bite the way this brief assumes, or the assignment is confused in some deeper way — just say that plainly instead of writing around it, I'd rather fix the commission than get a piece built on a bad premise. thx
