So my roommates and I run a tiny housing co-op (6 people, one shared kitchen, constant low-grade warfare about chores, groceries, and movie night), and after one of us took a public economics course we all became weirdly obsessed with Arrow's impossibility theorem. The obsession has gone too far: we've collectively decided that since no voting rule can satisfy unrestricted domain, Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives, and non-dictatorship all at once (given ordinal rankings and three or more alternatives), the only rational response is to stop aggregating preferences altogether. We're aware this might be an overreaction. We're doing it anyway.

I want you to write a satirical welcome packet for new members of this co-op, around 600-700 words, in the deadpan bureaucratic voice of our governance committee, who have fully internalized the theorem and rebuilt daily life around dodging its conditions. The humor has to come from getting the theorem right, not from garbling it. For example: the co-op only ever holds votes between exactly two options (since the theorem needs three or more alternatives to bite), but each ballot's results are sealed in separate envelopes, and combining results across votes is forbidden — the committee's theory being that transitivity across a sequence of pairwise outcomes is exactly where the trouble gets in. There should be a rotating "chair of the week" whose ranking automatically prevails during their term, with a clause insisting this is not a dictatorship because the rotation is fixed in advance and everyone gets a turn — maybe a line about how they aren't violating non-dictatorship, they're "distributing it." Somewhere include the rule that members may hold cardinal utilities privately but must never voice them aloud, since the theorem only bites on ordinal rankings and the committee lives in terror of the day someone expresses a preference intensity within earshot of a quorum. And a sidebar on the ex-member who proposed a Borda count and was asked to leave — the charge wasn't that Borda is a bad rule, it's that she violated IIA, and the packet should say something like "we take independence of irrelevant alternatives personally."

The actual target of the satire is the overreaction to impossibility results in general — the slide from "no rule satisfies all the desiderata simultaneously" to "therefore preference aggregation is a scam and any workaround is as good as any other." So the piece should quietly make it visible that several of the co-op's arrangements are strictly worse than an ordinary majority vote (all those envelope ceremonies, the chair whose whims decide everything for a week), without the committee ever noticing or admitting it. A reader who knows the theorem should laugh, and should also catch the real point underneath: impossibility results constrain institutional design, they don't abolish the obligation to choose. A few ground rules: don't misstate the theorem anywhere (no Condorcet-cycle confusion, no implying it says all voting systems are equally bad, no applying it to two-option votes), don't wink at the reader — the committee believes every single word they wrote — and keep it dry enough that it reads like an actual document you'd recieve at a co-op orientation, typos and all not required but honestly wouldnt hurt.
