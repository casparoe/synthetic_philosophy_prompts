I ended a long relationship a few months ago, and I'm now trying to work out whether the decision was right, because I have to decide whether to try to win her back. When I look inward for my reasons for leaving, what I find feels authoritative — the reasons simply present themselves as the truth about why I left. But two things have shaken my confidence in that feeling. First, I've noticed that the reasons I give shift depending on whom I'm talking to, and I seem to sincerely believe each version while I'm telling it. Second, I recently read about experiments in which people were shown a pair of faces, asked to pick the more attractive one, and then handed the one they had rejected — and many not only failed to notice the swap but confidently explained why they had chosen the face they had in fact turned down. I can't see how I would ever catch myself doing something like that, since from the inside a confabulated reason would feel exactly like a discovered one. My question is practical rather than academic: since my decision about the relationship will have to rest largely on what I find when I introspect, is there any principled way to sort out which of my introspective judgments deserve trust and which should be treated as plausible stories I'm constructing after the fact — and if not, what should a person in my position rely on instead?
