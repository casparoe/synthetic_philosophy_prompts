My younger sister, who is now in her late twenties, has spent roughly eight years building up a single island in a life-simulation game — an elaborate project of terraformed gardens, museums she curated item by item, and a collection of rare items, some obtained through events that will never be repeated. Last month, her husband, during an argument, deleted her save data from the console and, because the cloud backup had been disabled months earlier (by her, to free storage space), the world appears to be unrecoverable. He has since apologized, but in a way that clearly signals he regards the incident as roughly on par with losing a board game or erasing a DVR full of shows: unfortunate, a bit rude, not a serious matter. My sister, by contrast, has been genuinely devastated, and she is now pushing the family to treat what he did as morally comparable to, say, destroying eight years of her sketchbooks or her garden. Our parents think this is melodramatic — it was "just a game," nothing physical was lost, and she can always start a new island. My sister counters that the old island cannot be started again: the rare items were tied to one-time events, and the specific arrangements and history are gone, so what was destroyed was something that existed and cannot be re-created, even though it existed only as data.

There are two complications that make me less sure than I'd like to be about whose side to take. First, some of the rare items on the island were acquired in trades where my sister gave real money (a few hundred dollars in total, informally, via a marketplace of fellow players) — so at least part of the destroyed world had a fairly direct monetary value, though most of it did not. Second, my sister has half-jokingly said that if her husband had set fire to a shed containing physical collectibles of equal sentimental value, everyone would agree he owed her real restitution — new shelving, replacement items, a genuinely serious apology — and she wants to know why the digital case should be different. My instinct is that she's basically right, but I can't shake the feeling from my parents' side either: there does seem to be something that stops short of physical destruction, if only because no physical object was harmed and the "loss" depends entirely on how much my sister happened to care.

I'd appreciate your advice on how to think this through. Was something of real value destroyed here, or is that framing confused in some way I can't articulate? Should the husband's moral debt, and the restitution owed, be scaled to the monetary component alone, the sentimental component, or something else? And how should I respond to my parents' "it's just a game" stance without simply asserting that feelings are feelings? For context, my sister and her husband are attempting to repair the relationship, so I'm not looking for reasons to condemn him so much as a principled way of sizing what he did.
