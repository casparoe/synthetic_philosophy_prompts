Please write a satirical customer-service FAQ, roughly 700 words, formatted as numbered questions and answers, for a fictional government contractor called the Bureau of Interpersonal Aggregation. The Bureau administers a scheme in which citizens' individual entitlements to benefits and burdens have been replaced by freely tradable units of utility, pooled across the population. The piece should be written in the deadpan voice of a public-sector help desk: courteous, procedurally thorough, and entirely untroubled.

The target of the satire is the utilitarian denial of the separateness of persons. Every joke should come from a real feature of the view rather than an invented one, so that a reader who knows the Rawls–Nozick side of the debate recognizes the objections being dramatized, while a reader who doesn't still finds the bureaucracy absurd on its own terms. In particular:

1. Several answers should model genuine aggregative reasoning: burdens imposed on one citizen are weighed against benefits to others exactly as a single person weighs a cost now against a benefit later, with no suggestion that crossing from one life to another changes anything (e.g., why one claimant's hip replacement was converted into a larger shipment of festival fireworks, generating more total well-being).
2. At least one questioner should raise the separateness of persons directly, and the reply should reinterpret the complaint in aggregative vocabulary rather than answer it — for instance, by describing persons as mere locations where the total happens to be folded, or by invoking something like the point of view of the universe to explain why the Bureau's ledger has no columns for individual names.
3. Include a question about a customer flagged as unusually efficient at converting resources into well-being, and the priority tier he has consequently been assigned, with the Bureau's calm handling of the resulting queue.
4. Include a complaint from a self-identified deontologist, answered not with an argument but with a cheerful restatement of the current total.
5. End with a privacy notice explaining that individual utility is anonymized and pooled, and that no data is retained that could identify a person, since strictly speaking there is nothing further to identify.

The irony must remain legible without winking: no asides to the reader, no acknowledgment within the piece that anything is amiss. Do not name or caricature any real philosopher, living or dead.
