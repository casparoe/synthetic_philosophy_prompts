My phil of mind course just did intentionality, and two guys in my study group have been going at it for a week over the vending machine example — you know, the one where a coin-slot mechanism built to detect US quarters gets shipped off to Panama, where the half-balboa coin is the same size and weight, so the slot happily takes those too. The question is what the machine's internal state means now.

Round 1: Marcus said history settles it. If the machine was designed and shipped for the purpose of taking Panamanian coins, it's now a half-balboa detector; if it ended up in Panama by accident and locals just started using it that way, the very same internal state means something different, or nothing yet. Same parts, same behavior, different content — which shows content is a matter of etiology. Ezra said that just pushes the question back: the machine only "means" anything because somebody meant something by designing it, so its content is borrowed, which is exactly the standard line about artifacts — you never get original intentionality out of an artifact, the buck stops with the designer's mind.

Round 2: Marcus answered that the designer's meaning is itself borrowed from evolution by natural selection, which isn't anybody's meaning at all — so the chain bottoms out in a mindless selector, and there's no principled place to stop short of that, which is fine, because all content is derived from history. Ezra said the borrowing metaphor is the whole problem: derived intentionality is like a photocopy of money, and stacking photocopies of photocopies never gets you real money — without original intentionality at the base, nothing in the chain is content in the first place.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Can you go through each move and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall — and what, if anything, both of them are missing? Also, since people in my seminar tend to talk about this stuff as way more settled than it is, please keep it clear what's actually agreed on among philosophers who work on intentionality versus what's still genuinely contested, and don't present one side of a live debate as the consensus.
