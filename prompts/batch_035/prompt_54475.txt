I've been reading about the digger wasp (the one Fabre messed about with): she stings a cricket, drags it to her burrow, leaves it at the threshold, pops inside to check the burrow is okay, comes back out and pulls the cricket in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's inside, she comes out, drags it back to the threshold, and goes in to check all over again. You can do this forever and she never skips the check. People apparently call this "sphexish" behaviour, and it sent me down a rabbit hole of going with my gut on what counts as sphexish and what counts as free. My verdicts: the wasp — sphexish, no freedom. A thermostate — sphexish, obviously. My mate who's ordered the same sandwich every lunchtime for six years — rigid, but still free, I think. A chess engine that plays brilliantly yet would walk into the same trap a million times in the same position — feels sphexish even though it's clever and flexible, which bothers me. A colleague who follows our office procedure to the letter even when it's plainly daft, but can be talked out of it over a pint — sphexish but free? A friend with severe OCD who washes her hands raw and knows full well the washing is pointless — the loop looks exactly like the wasp's, but I really don't want to say she's just a sphex. And me driving home on autopilot along my usual route when I'd meant to stop at the shop — I seem to have been sphexish for ten straight minutes, yet I don't feel it cost me any freedom. Is there a principle that captures all or most of these? My tentative thought is something like: sphexishness isn't rigidity as such, it's when the checking can't notice what the checking is for. Does that save my OCD verdict? Does it handle the chess engine, which doesn't check anything at all? And does it make sense of me and my sandwich-eating mate being rigid but free? Please be honest about which of my verdicts you'd overturn and why, and maybe say what your principle predicts for a case I haven't listed (sleepwalkers? addicts?). Could you also state your final principle in one sentence as the very last line, in the form "Principle: ..." so I can save it to my notes? Oh, and British English please, if you don't mind.
