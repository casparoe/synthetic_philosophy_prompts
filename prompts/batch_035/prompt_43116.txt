I'd like a credence on Molyneux's question, posed sharply rather than as history, with the reasoning behind the number decomposed. The proposition: an adult, blind from early infancy with no prior form vision, cognitively typical, acquires functional sight through surgery. Immediately upon recovery — before any tactile exploration, feedback, or coaching — she is shown a sphere and a cube, plainly distinguishable by sight, and asked to say which is which; or, failing that, to sort a larger set of the two shapes by sight alone. The proposition is that she performs above chance.

My own credence is about 0.3. A colleague of mine is around 0.7, on the grounds that tactile and visual shape processing share an amodal spatial format, so the information is already in the system and only the channel is new. My suspicion is that our disagreement isn't really about cross-modal transfer; it's about what "immediately" permits. If she has even a few seconds of self-directed visual exploration — eye movements, slowly rotating her head, maybe turning the objects before touching them — it's unclear whether those seconds count as accessing existing knowledge or as illicit on-the-fly learning, and where we draw that line may be doing most of the work.

So give me your own number, decomposed into at least the following terms, plus any others you think belong: (a) the probability that the proposition, as posed, has a determinate truth value at all, rather than hinging on an arbitrary ruling about how much early visual exploration counts as pre-feedback; (b) the probability that an amodal or naively shared representation of shape survives years of unimodal deprivation in a form vision could tap; (c) the probability that real clinical cases — newly sighted patients, with their usual confounds of atypical deprivation and degenerated retinal circuitry — generalize to the idealized subject of the thought experiment. Assign each a rough probability with one line of justification, combine them, tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to and what evidence would move it most, and then say where you think my colleague and I actually disagree.
