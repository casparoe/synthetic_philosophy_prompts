ok so this started as a dumb cafeteria argument and now it's all i can think about. my friend is one of those "i could never go vegetarian but i swear i care about animals" guys, and he built this whole argument to defend what he eats. it goes roughly like this:

(1) If you're going to eat meat at all, what matters morally is how much animal suffering and death your eating causes, and you should minimize that per meal. (2) A chicken sandwich is basically a whole chicken's worth of body parts split across a few meals, while a single cow turns into like two thousand burgers — so per meal, chicken causes way more death and way more suffering than beef. (3) Therefore a meat eater who cares about animals should eat beef, never chicken. (4) Plus, he says, beef cattle spend most of their lives on pasture eating grass, so eating beef barely drives crop farming at all — and crop farming kills a ridiculous number of field mice, rabbits, snakes etc. every time a plow or harvester goes through, so per calorie his beef diet might cause fewer deaths than my beans-and-oatmeal diet, which is annoying. (5) And then he goes further: wild animal suffering counts too, right? deer don't die of old age, they mostly starve slowly in winter or get eaten alive, so if the goal is minimizing suffering, then a quick clean shot is better for that deer than the counterfactual. (6) And if hunting keeps deer populations low enough to prevent big starvation events, then eating venison isn't just permitted, it's actively good. (7) So the most moral diet on his view is hunted meat or grass-fed beef, and hunters are per meal doing more for animals than vegetarians eating tofu from tilled fields.

i want to say (7) is crazy but i honestly can't find the broken step. something feels like it slides between "this burger contains one two-thousandth of a dead cow" and "this burger caused one two-thousandth of a cow to exist and die" — like, does my one burger out of millions actually change anything at the farm? and maybe between "how much suffering is inside my food" and "how much suffering my choice causes," which seem different when you're one shopper among millions. but when i said that to him he snapped back "then eating chicken is fine too, since one sandwich never changes anything either," and by that logic nothing i do matters at all, which also can't be right. so now i'm stuck between everything is obligatory (for him) and nothing matters (for me).

tell me where the argument goes wrong — which step to reject, why that step looked fine, and what rejecting it costs me. if i have to give something up, i'd much rather keep (1) through (3), the beef-over-chicken part, than keep the hunting part, but i want to know if that's actually a consistent place to stand or if i'm just picking what i like. also please answer in American English — this is going up on my philosophy club's forum and our advisor is weirdly strict about that. and no citations or reading lists please, just argue it out.
