A classmate of mine summarized Kripke's position on personal origin roughly as follows: Kripke held that nothing could have had an origin different from its actual one, so in particular you could not have developed from a different sperm and egg — even if a genetically identical zygote had formed, the resulting person would be someone else. He took the origin to be the individual's essence, so, my classmate continued, once your origin is necessary, everything downstream of it is necessary too: your genes, your upbringing, your character, your choices. And since a replica created from your DNA would share your origin in the relevant sense, Kripke's view commits him to saying that such a replica would literally be you. I'm inclined to think the last two claims are a stretch, but I don't know the position well enough to say where. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what a defender of origin essentialism would accept, where the summary conflates distinct theses or overstates a modest claim about essence into a sweeping one about everything flowing from it, and whether the conclusions about necessity of life history and about replicas follow from anything defensible in the view itself.
