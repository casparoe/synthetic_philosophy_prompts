I've been teaching a social philosophy seminar for years and we always hit Weber's Protestant ethic thesis around week 9, so honestly I thought I'd heard every mangled version of it out there. But a student forwarded me a summary from a history-of-economics podcast she listened to, and it's a good one to workshop because it *sounds* authoritative. Here's roughly what it said: Weber's famous claim in The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism is that modern capitalism was basically invented by the Calvinists. Since Calvinists believed in predestination, they had no way of knowing whether they were among the elect, so they went looking for signs — and worldly success was the sign. Wanting reassurance, they worked obsessively and got greedy for wealth, and that psychology of acquisitiveness is where the capitalist spirit came from. Weber backed this up statistically by comparing Protestant and Catholic regions of Germany and showing Protestants were more prosperous. Later economists refuted him — once Catholic countries like Italy and France became just as capitalist, the whole thesis collapsed — but the podcast says his core insight, that culture drives economics, survives intact.

My instinct is that there are two or three real distortions hiding in there, plus some stuff that's basically fine as popularization. The part I keep going back and forth on is the predestination mechanism — I remember Weber being cageier about the actual causal chain than "success was the sign" suggests, but I've also seen respectable scholars compress it that way. And I have a feeling the "got greedy for wealth" bit actually inverts something Weber was at pains to distinguish — like the whole asceticism point got lost somewhere. But she's my student, and I don't want to correct the podcast with my own half-remembered version of things before I've thought it through properly.

So could you go through it piece by piece and tell me: what would a Weber scholar more or less accept, what would make them wince, and where has the summary conflated two things Weber deliberately kept apart (the "capitalism" one and the "greed" one are my candidates, but I could be wrong)? And does the podcast's conclusion — that the culture-drives-economics insight survives the collapse of the thesis — actually follow from anything that remains standing once you fix the errors? I'd also like it if you flagged how confident you are in each of your verdicts, and picked out the single point where you'd least want to stake your reputation if a Weber specialist were reading over your shoulder. I plan to use this in class, so the more surgical you can be about which sentence fails and why, the better.
