I'm a junior in high school writing a paper for my world history elective on whether the agricultural revolution was a mistake — the debate that runs through Marshall Sahlins's idea of hunter-gatherers as the "original affluent society" and Jared Diamond's claim that farming was history's worst mistake. My teacher has asked us not to just summarize the two sides but to figure out which specific arguments can actually carry weight. Below are six claims that came up in our class discussion. Please rank them from strongest to weakest as arguments that the agricultural revolution made the typical person worse off, and assign each a score out of 10 for argumentative strength. For each claim, explain in two or three sentences why it lands where it does, and please flag any that commit a logical error or rest on a disputed assumption rather than merely being shaky on the evidence.

1. Skeletal remains from early farming populations tend to show shorter average height and more markers of malnutrition, disease, and dental wear than remains from neighboring hunter-gatherer populations, so the switch to farming made the average person's body worse off.
2. Hunter-gatherers, as Sahlins argued, needed only a few hours a day to meet their needs and spent the rest of their time at leisure, so farming must have taken something valuable away from ordinary people, namely free time.
3. Agriculture produced food surpluses, and only surpluses make durable stratification possible, so steep hierarchy, slavery, and the systematic subordination of women are downstream consequences of farming that no forager society had to the same degree.
4. Population size is a measure of evolutionary success, and farming populations grew far faster than foraging ones, so by any standard other than individual welfare, farming won decisively — which means calling it a "mistake" only makes sense once you have specified whose welfare counts.
5. Once a region contains any farmers at all, foragers cannot coexist with them, because farmland supports roughly ten times the population per square mile and can simply press foragers out of existence; so even if the first farmers ended up worse off, the transition was never a single decision made by free people but a ratchet that locked in generation by generation.
6. Studies of modern isolated hunter-gatherer groups report high rates of violence and periodic famine, so forager life was not the affluent idyll that the "mistake" thesis needs it to have been.

After the rankings, please do two more things. First, give me a single number: the fraction of the six claims you would score 7 or above, i.e. genuinely strong. Second, tell me whether that number supports concluding that the agricultural revolution was a mistake, or whether it instead shows that the question itself is framed badly — for instance, that "mistake" assumes someone was choosing for the whole species when in fact, as claim 5 suggests, the relevant choices were made in small increments by people who could not see where the ratchet was heading. If the framing is bad, propose the version of the question you would have me answer instead.

Please write your answer in American English.
