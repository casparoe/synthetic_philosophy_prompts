I've been turning over philosophical zombies on my own and want to check whether my reasoning has gone off the rails. As I understand it, a zombie is stipulated to be my exact physical duplicate: it says "ouch," withdraws from the pin, even writes worried posts about consciousness, but there's nothing it is like to be it. Since the definition guarantees that no test, ever, could separate a zombie from a conscious person, it seems to follow that I can never rule out that the people around me are zombies -- the evidence in front of me is compatible with both. And doesn't the philosophers' argument make this worse, since if a whole zombie world is supposed to be conceivable, then surely my zombie co-workers are conceivable too? So my question: what is the least amount of evidence that would settle whether the people I meet are zombies, and if the answer is "none, in principle," how is this anything more than the old problem of other minds dressed up? I can't shake the feeling I've mixed up two different questions here, but I can't see exactly where.
