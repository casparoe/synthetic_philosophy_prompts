My housemate, who abandoned an epistemology MA for machine learning, has an argument he claims shows that no finite being ever holds a justified belief, and I haven't been able to land a punch on it for three weeks.

1. A belief is justified only if the believer holds it on the basis of a reason.
2. A reason justifies only if it is itself justified at the moment it does its justifying work.
3. Nothing whose own justification is still pending can transmit justification to anything else.
4. So each reason demands a further reason, without terminus, and every belief hangs from a chain with no top link.
5. Finite beings can possess, inspect, and act on only finitely many reasons.
6. Therefore no belief of a finite being is ever justified — including (6).

He is serene about the self-application; he says an argument can be like an optical illusion, surviving its own measurement. What actually bothers me is his corollary. He claims (6) levels everything: since both of us hold an infinite debt neither can pay, my perceptual beliefs and his uncle's moon-landing beliefs have the same standing, and the difference between the careful and the credulous is temperament, not epistemic achievement. That seems to me to prove too much, which is my only real evidence that something in the argument is loose — I just can't locate it. Something seems to shift between reasons there are and reasons I have, but he accuses me of helping myself to whichever reading is convenient at each step, and I can't prove him wrong.

He has answers staged for each exit. Basic beliefs, he says, are just beliefs whose justification we have politely declined to audit. Coherence, he says, is a constraint on truth, not a source of justification. And the move of saying I need available reasons rather than possessed ones he dismisses as borrowing against a bank that doesn't exist. I would most like to keep premise (2), since giving it up seems to license anyone to halt the demand for reasons wherever it suits them — and he points out that this is precisely what the foundationalist does, which I resent. Tell me which premise to reject, why it looked acceptable in the first place, and what rejecting it will cost me.
