**Examination question:**

'Many philosophers hold that hypocrites lack the standing to blame others for wrongdoing of a kind they themselves have committed. Explain what this loss of standing is supposed to amount to, and assess whether it shows that the hypocrite's blame is in some way defective.'

**Student's answer:**

Strawson argues that blame consists in the modification of one's intentions towards another person in the light of one's judgement of their blameworthiness: when I blame my flatmate for lying to me, I revise my intentions about relying on him, withdraw my readiness to share confidences, and alter my expectations of our future dealings. Scanlon, in 'Freedom and Resentment', holds that such reactions are the reactive attitudes, and that they belong to taking the participant stance towards another person.

Standing to blame must be distinguished from blameworthiness itself. Whether my flatmate is blameworthy depends on what he did; whether I am in a position to blame him depends on facts about me. The hypocrite lacks standing because he has done the very thing he condemns: his blame is unfair because he claims a moral superiority he does not possess, thereby violating the demand of equal moral standing that Wallace identifies. This does not make his blame false. It remains true that lying is wrong and that the flatmate acted wrongly. What the loss of standing shows is that the hypocrite's blame is impermissible: in blaming, he commits a further wrong, since one cannot permissibly demand of another a standard one flouts oneself ('do as I say, not as I do'). And since the blame is impermissible, the flatmate is entitled to conclude that she did nothing wrong and to treat the hypocrite's protest as though it were mere noise.

Finally, standing is lost only through hypocrisy. Where the blamer has done the same thing himself, his blame is unfair; where he has not, nothing about him undermines it, and the only remaining questions are whether the target is blameworthy and whether blaming would be useful.
