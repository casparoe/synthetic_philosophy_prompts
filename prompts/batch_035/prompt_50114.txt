Could you write me a parody FAQ page for the Central Election Commission of a fictional country — say, the Republic of Veldnia? The setting matters: Veldnia is about halfway through its democratic backslide. Elections still happen, the opposition still wins the occasional city hall, but the constitutional court, the media regulator, and the prosecutor's office have all been quietly brought under new management, and this years election is the first one where the international observers went home early.

Format: exactly five numbered entries, nothing before or after them — no intro, no thank-you-for-your-question footer. Each entry is one or two sentences total, question included. So the squeeze is real; treat the compression as part of the joke.

Here's the part I actually care about. Every answer has to be literally true. The Commission never lies in this FAQ — it just never quite says the true thing. The reader has to reconstruct the actual state of Veldnia from what the answers conspicuously fail to mention. And keep the spokesman plausible: nothing in the piece should be something a real government lawyer couldn't say on the record. The comedy should come from the genuine mechanics of backsliding rather than from making the regime cartoonish — the sovereignty move, where someone asks who gets to decide whats free and fair, and the answer is the Veldnian people, which means the government, which means nobody; the we-are-the-only-real-democracy-here move; the foreign-funded-opposition move. Someone who's followed the last fifteen years in Hungary or Turkey should wince with recognition at every entry, and a political scientist who works on backsliding should find it fair rather than a strawman.
