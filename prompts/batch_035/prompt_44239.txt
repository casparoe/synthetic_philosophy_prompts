I'm an evolutionary biologist — comparative biomechanics mostly — so I spend my days with homology vs analogy, convergence, exaptation, developmental constraint, that toolkit. I've gotten interested in functionalism in philosophy of mind lately and I keep having the feeling it's doing something I already do, but I want it done properly instead of me hand-waving the parallel.

Restate functionalism and the multiple realizability claim in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. My working analogy, which you should stress-test rather than flatter: mental kinds like pain or belief are functional kinds the way wings are — individuated by what they do (generate lift, in the right ballpark) rather than by what they're made of or who they descended from, and multiply realized the way powered flight is: insect wings, bird wings, bat wings, built from quite different structures along independant lineages. Psychological laws would then be like the convergence-level generalizations I teach students — glide ratios, Reynolds number effects — true across the realizations but silent about the underlying anatomy. And the functionalist who says implementation details are causally irrelevant sounds like me telling a student that bat wings being mammalian limbs is beside the point for the flight analysis.

Then give me the residue report, and be honest about it: what translates cleanly, what translates only if you bend it, and what has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. A few things I especially want placed. (a) In my world, functional ascriptions come with an etiological story — selection built wings for flight, and that history is part of why they count as the same kind. It's not obvious the philosophers' causal-role individication has any analogue to that, and if it doesn't, the two senses of "functional" may be coming apart under the translation. (b) The objection that realization details matter after all — the version where our pain turns out to depend on specific neurochemistry, so the Martian isn't feeling pain even with the right causal role. Is that just developmental constraint / deep homology in a costume, or something the convergence framing can't express? (c) The absent qualia and inverted qualia stuff, and Block's China-brain — I genuinely cannot begin to map those onto anything I know, and I want to know whether that's a gap in my vocabulary or a gap in what the functionalists ever managed to say clearly.

Keep the translation honest — don't smuggle in selection talk where the philosophers didn't have it and call that translating — and treat the residue as the interesting part. If the qualia worries simply have no expression in terms a biomechanic uses, tell me whether that means my framework is missing something or whether the original claims were less clear than they sounded.
