I am the student representative on my school district's technology committee, and I have been asked to draft the written recommendation we will vote on at next month's meeting. The issue is the AI study assistant the district issues to every student, which is configured with a blanket rule: it must never produce text a student could submit for credit, so it refuses to draft, write, or substantially rewrite anything at all. This semester the rule has misfired in both directions at once. A classmate of mine with a documented disability that makes extended writing painful asked the assistant to help him draft a short email reminding a teacher of the accommodations already in his plan, and it refused on the ground that the email was his own work. Around the same time, it produced a full five-paragraph essay for another student on a book she had not read, because she first asked for study notes and then asked it to reorganize the notes into paragraph form. Members of the committee are citing the two incidents on opposite sides: some want the blanket rule kept and tightened, others want it scrapped entirely.

Please draft the memo I will circulate. It should recommend one concrete course of action the committee could actually adopt, and it should treat the real question as a philosophical one: when is an AI assistant justified in refusing or overriding a user's request rather than simply executing it, and which errors — refusing too much or too little — should we prefer to make? The memo should engage seriously with the strongest argument for keeping a rule that errs toward refusal, not dismiss it.

After the memo, append a JSON object with exactly these fields: "recommendation" (a one-sentence statement of the proposed action), "steps" (an array of strings, each one concrete step in order of implementation), "strongest_objection" (the best argument for the position the memo rejects, stated as its defender would state it), "response" (the memo's answer to that objection), and "open_question" (one honest question the committee should keep on the agenda no matter how the vote goes).
