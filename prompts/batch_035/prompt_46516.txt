Write a parody of an Amazon-style product page — complete with product title, marketing blurb, aggregate star rating, five or six customer reviews with usernames and "Verified Purchase" tags, and a short customer Q&A section — for a mass-market, consumer-electronics version of the Ring of Gyges. Give the manufacturer a bland corporate name and let the marketing copy have the chirpy, breathless tone of real product listings (lifetime invisibility warranty, free shipping to the underworld, etc.). The aggregate rating itself should be part of the joke — think 3.9 stars, with the distribution telling its own story.

The real content of the satire should be the debate from Book II of Plato's Republic, so the reviews need to function as a scattered reenactment of it rather than as generic invisibility-ring hijinks. Specifically: at least one reviewer should confirm Glaucon's prediction that anyone wearing the ring, however just they seemed before, will start doing injustice once consequences are removed; one reviewer should worry in Adeimantus's register — not that the ring makes you unjust, but that it makes your reputation for justice worthless, since nobody can verify you did anything at all; one five-star review should come from someone who wears the ring constantly and, infuriatingly, keeps doing what they would have done anyway (Socrates' wager, in effect — they paid for the ring just to check); and one review should be from a customer who used the ring to live the perfectly unjust life Glaucon describes — honored and wealthy, uncaught — and report that something about their inner arrangement has gone wrong, in a way they can't quite get the manufacturer to cover under warranty. Include a deadpan "Verified Purchase" review from Gyges of Lydia himself, the original user (who, depending on whether you follow Plato or Herodotus, found the ring in a chasm after an earthquake or received it as a consequence of a king's bad idea, and used it to take the throne and the queen). The Q&A section should include at least one question that quietly restates the philosophical problem — e.g., whether the ring is redundant for the just man, or whether the company offers a version that makes you merely *seem* just — answered by the manufacturer with uncomprehending corporate helpfulness.

A few constraints: roughly 600–750 words; keep everything deadpan, with no winking narrator; and make sure a Plato scholar would recognize all four positions (Glaucon's challenge, Adeimantus's reputation argument, the claim that injustice damages the unjust person's own soul, and Socrates' counter-wager) and feel that the piece understands the actual argument rather than just the invisibility trope. Don't let a reviewer who endorses Glaucon's cynicism slip halfway into praising virtue for its own sake unless that slide is the joke. Bonus points if the listing includes a warranty clause that covers material defects but explicitly excludes damage to the soul, and a "customers also bought" reference to the Ring of Polycrates (listing condition: water damage).
