So I got roped into giving the closing talk at my local rationalist meetup's secular solstice (~40 people: old-timers who've read their Nozick, plus newer folks dragged along by partners). The theme the organizers handed my slot is basically "would you plug in?" -- the experience machine, and what it says about the stuff people here actually care about (the FAI paradise, uploads, whole brain ems, that whole cluster).

Here's my problem: around these parts the experience machine gets quoted as a knockdown argument against wireheading, and I don't buy it. Nozick's machine does two things at once -- it lies to you (you think you're writing the great american novel, you're actually a floating tank) and it swaps real events for fake ones. But those two come apart. You can have a virtual world that never deceives you about being virtual, and honestly I lean toward the view that virtual events are still real events of a kind. Write the talk so it does real work separating the deception objection from the virtuality objection, then says straight which one survives the separation and which one dies, and then draws the consequence for our actual obsession: whether a well-run paradise that's heavy on virtual worlds and uploads is a good life, or just a fancy wirehead.

Constraints: the newbies have to stay on board (no unexplained jargon, brief and plain when you introduce the machine), the old-timers will catch any fumbles about the philsoophy so get that part right, and it should end solstice-appropriate -- warm, something people can carry home, not a lecture. Write in American English, ~8 minutes of speaking.
