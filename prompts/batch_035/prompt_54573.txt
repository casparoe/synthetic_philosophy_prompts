ok so last night my friend and i got into a like 3 hour argument about the statue and the clay (you know, the material constitution thing — how can the statue and the lump of clay both be sitting right there, sharing all the same parts, if they're different things, and if they're the same thing how come one survives being squashed and the other doesn't). it ended with her saying "you just believe in magic objects" and me saying "you just believe in two invisible things where i see one" and nobody changed their mind, which got me thinking that people don't actually know what they believe here, they just know which label they like.

so i want to build a "which view of material constitution do you actually hold?" quiz for my blog. here's what i need:

ten questions, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options. the questions have to be phrased so a normal reader can answer them without knowing any of the terminology — so no question can just ask "do you think the statue is identical to the clay?" or "do you accept sortal-relative identity?". instead each question should be a case where the views genuinely come apart. you must include at least these cases somewhere, phrased however you like:

1. the clay sitting in the studio before the sculptor starts — was there a statue in the room then? (and the mirror version: after she smashes it flat, is there still a statue in the room)
2. a case where the lump and the statue come into existence at the exact same moment and are destroyed at the exact same moment, so you can't split them by history — i want to see if any question can still separate them
3. gradual replacement: a garden statue that has every original bit replaced over centuries (she said this one was a trap but wouldn't say for what)
4. one case about whether the same pattern generalizes beyond artifacts, like a tree and the biological matter making it up, or a person and their body — i don't want a quiz that only works for statues
5. a case that tests the "too many things" reaction directly — like, if you buy the two-objects view, how many things are on my desk right now? (she hammered me on this one and honestly i folded a bit)

then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions: the plain identity view (there's just one thing there, the statue IS the clay), sortalism, the constitution-without-identity view where the statue has the clay as its maker or basis or whatever instead of being it, the four-dimensionalist dissolution via temporal parts, relative identity, and the deflationary "there are no statues, strictly speaking" option. the key has to work off answer combos, not off which label the reader likes — that's the whole point.

two more things. first, please flag honestly where the quiz can't separate two positions — i suspect sortalism and relative identity might get dragged to the same leaf by almost any case i can think of, and if so say so instead of pretending the quiz is sharper than it is. second, if some combination of answers is one that basically nobody holds, note it and tell me whether that's because the combo is incoherent, unappealing, or just a gap nobody's bothered to fill. i'd rather have an honest messy quiz than a fake clean one.

also keep the language plain, my readers are not philosophers, they're people who argue at parties. a table for the scoring key is fine. no reading lists please, half of them are paywalled anyway lol
