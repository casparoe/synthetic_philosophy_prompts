So I've spent maybe two years on and off in the presentism vs eternalism corner of metaphysics and I've come to suspect the whole thing is frozen in a weird way. Relativity was supposed to be the big external input: no privileged frame, no global present, so Putnam's old argument goes through and eternalism wins. And a decent number of eternalists definitely talk like relativity closed the case. But the presentists never converted, they just relocated — one says relativity is about clocks and rods, not ontology, another retreats to the here-and-now being the only objectively real point, another holds out for quantum gravity restoring a preferred foliation. Meanwhile the internal arguments also never go anywhere: the eternalist keeps asking what makes it true that their were dinosaurs if nothing past exists, the presentist keeps saying that's just a demand for a certain style of truthmaker they're allowed to refuse, and around we go. Growing block people occasionally wander through and everyone gangs up on them, then it's back to normal.

My working hypothesis is that this is mostly a verbal dispute about the word 'exists' — eternalists mean exists-at-some-time tenselessly, presentists mean exists-now — and once you disambiguate, the relativity argument doesn't even touch the presentist. All it shows is that 'now' isn't a global physical notion, which a presentist can happily grant, since they never claimed tense was a term of physics. Is that diagnosis right? Or is there a substantive disagreement hiding under the verbal one — about truthmakers for past-tensed claims, about whether tensed facts are part of the world's furniture (Prior's point that relief, when something bad is finally over, seems to be directed at the tensed fact itself and not at any tenseless description of it), about whether time genuinely passes? And what would even count as progress here — a preferred foliation actually turning up in some quantum gravity program, a semantic cleanup where everyone fixes the words and then discovers they still disagree, a knockout truthmaker argument? I'd also honestly accept 'no progress is possible and here's why', but I want the reasoning, not just the verdict. Bonus question if you have thoughts on it: does Gödel's rotating-universe argument that time is in some sense ideal still do real work in this fight, or is it mostly cited out of habit at this point?
