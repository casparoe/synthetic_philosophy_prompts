I've been puzzling over the Boltzmann brain problem on my own, without any philosophy or physics training: as I understand it, the worry is that in a universe that lasts a very long time, random thermal fluctuations will every so often assemble a lone brain with false memories of a past, and that there could be vastly more such momentary brains than ordinary ones like ours, so our own memories and observations are unreliable. It strikes me that Descartes already faced something like this with his evil demon, and I'm curious how he would react if someone explained the modern version to him. If Descartes could be brought up to speed on modern cosmology, what do you think he would say about the Boltzmann brain scenario? I'd like you to focus on two things in particular. First, would he hold that his cogito argument still secures anything for the fluctuation-brain, given that the thinking thing in this case has no past and only an instant of future — is existing for a moment enough for the conclusion he wanted? Second, would his move from the demon to a non-deceiving God, which supposedly guarantees the external world, have any chance of working here, where the deceiver is not an agent with intentions but just impersonal chance? Finally, tell me your own view on whether the Boltzmann brain case is genuinely a harder problem for him than the demon was, or whether it's just the demon scenario with the physics filled in, with nothing really new at stake.
