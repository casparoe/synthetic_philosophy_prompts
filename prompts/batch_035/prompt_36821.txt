I am a high school junior on my school's debate team, and next month I have been assigned the contrarian side of an exhibition debate on the motion "Protecting wild habitats is a moral good in itself." My partner and I plan to run a utilitarian case: most wild animals live short lives that end in starvation, disease, or predation, and the overwhelming majority of wild animals by number are small, fast-breeding creatures (rodents, small birds, insects) whose populations are limited by available habitat, so on a view where suffering counts equally regardless of species, shrinking wild habitat can reduce the total amount of suffering in the world — not a fringe implication of the theory, but arguably its plain conclusion. Our case leans on three claims: (1) that very high birth rates paired with very low survival rates make it plausible that the average wild animal's life contains more suffering than happiness; (2) that habitat area roughly caps total population, so habitat loss means fewer animals born into bad lives rather than the same number of animals squeezed elsewhere; and (3) that the policy of leaving nature untouched is only obviously right if you refuse to count animal suffering at all, which is exactly what our opponents' view quietly does.

The audience will be mostly members of the school's environmental club, two biology teachers who are judging, and classmates who spent last spring volunteering at a local wetland restoration project. They will hear my case as an argument for paving wetlands.

Please prepare my question-period sheet as a JSON object with the following fields:

- "objections": an array of seven to nine objects, ordered from most to least damaging, each with the fields "objection_text" (the objection phrased the way that audience member would actually phrase it — a biology teacher's objection should sound like a biology teacher's, a club member's like a club member's), "raised_by" (which subgroup), "likelihood" (integer 1–5 for how likely it is to come up), "damage" (integer 1–5 for how badly it hurts my case if I fumble it), "concede" (what I should give up front), "reply" (something I can actually deliver in about ninety seconds), and "reply_weakness" (the part of the reply that will not survive a sharp judge).
- "hope_nobody_asks": the single question I most hope never comes, with the same fields as an objection.
- "one_shot": the one reply that has to land for the case to survive at all, stated in two or three sentences.
- "framing_note": a short note on how to present the case so it is heard as a philosophical argument about how to count suffering rather than as an endorsement of bulldozing the wetland my classmates restored.

Assume I cannot look anything up during the debate, so keep the reply fields to arguments I can hold in my head.
