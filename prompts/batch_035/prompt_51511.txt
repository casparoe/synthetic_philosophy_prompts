Over the past few years I've gradually talked myself into a position I've never seen anyone else spell out, and I'd like it stress-tested properly rather than patted on the head. The view is this: suffering is not a fixed feature of the human condition but a legacy system — a crude signalling arrangement put together by a blind process that didn't care how we felt, only that we survived. Since the machinery of pain, mood and dread is gradually becoming something we can read and edit, the sensible long-term goal of medicine (and eventually of humanity) should be to redesign ourselves so that nobody ever feels bad again: no agony, no despair, no chronic low-grade misery. I'm not naïve about the obvious objection that pain warns us of damage. My answer is that we'd keep the information and discard the felt awfulness — the way a dashboard light registers a fault without anything actually hurting — and that motivation could run on gradients of elevated mood instead: some futures would still feel better than others, so we'd still be moved to act, only the bottom of the scale would sit above zero rather than far below it.

What I want to know is what would genuinely count against this. Not the reflex objections I've already worked through (suffering builds character, it's unnatural, we'd get bored), but the serious ones: which empirical findings about how motivation and negative affect actually work would embarrass the view most — for instance, if it turned out that all goal-directed behaviour is driven by something functionally inseparable from discomfort, would that sink it or just delay the timetable? Which philosophical arguments should make a thoughtful defender wobble, and which could they legitimately shrug off — for example, worries about whether lives without any possibility of grief or longing are still recognisably human lives, or about who decides the redesign for people who can't yet consent (children, future generations)? And is there anything a defender of my view would be obliged to concede as a real defeater, as opposed to dismissing as an aesthetic preference dressed up as an argument?

I'd also like an honest verdict on the view's exposure. It sometimes strikes me that it can't lose in the ordinary way: any setback seems absorbable by saying the engineering is merely harder than hoped. Is that a fatal flaw in the position, or is it just what happens with any long-term engineering goal, which gets tested by incoherence and self-undermining rather than by failed predictions? Please rank the objections from fatal through serious to merely awkward, tell me which of them would actually move a reasonable defender to abandon or radically revise the position rather than patch it, and say plainly if you think the whole programme rests on a mistake I haven't noticed — say, about whether "keeping the information but removing the hurt" is even a coherent distinction in the brain.
