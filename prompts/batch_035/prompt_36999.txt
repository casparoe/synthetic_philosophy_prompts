I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, I just got into this stuff through podcasts and arguments at parties. One fight I keep chewing on is the Humean vs anti-Humean thing about laws of nature. As I understand it, Humeans say the laws are basically the best compressed summary of everything that happens — like the best lossless compression of the universe's I/O log, scored on simplicity and coverage. Anti-Humeans say no, the laws are more like the running code that generates the events: things happen *because* of the laws, not the other way around. As a programmer the code picture feels obviously right to me, which is exactly why I distrust it — the analogy might be doing all the work.

Anyway, from everything I can tell this debate has been going for decades and it's totally stalled. Both sides agree on every actual event that ever happens, so no observation can settle it, and the arguments just go in circles — the anti-Humean says the Humean can't really explain why the universe is regular, the Humean says "explain" is a rigged word. So I want a diagnosis, not a scoreboard. Is this a substantive disagreement or a verbal one about the word "law"? Are they secretly answering different questions, like "how do we compress the data" vs "what makes the data what it is"? Or is there a hidden real question underneath, like what's actually true in a counterfactual — that's my half-baked theory, since the code-vs-summary thing only seems to matter when you ask what would've happened if things were different, and the two pictures seem to give different answers there. And if you buy your own diagnosis, tell me honestly: is there anything that would count as progress here, a distinction or an argument or whatever, or is this one just permanently stuck, and if so why do smart people keep at it?
