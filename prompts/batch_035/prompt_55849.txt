I'm teaching a section of my philosophy of language seminar on Grice and want to pair the Williamson-era debate over "Meaning" and "Logic and Conversation" with Jane Austen's Emma. Please read the Emma–Mr. Elton matchmaking subplot through the Gricean apparatus. Emma believes she is commending Harriet to Mr. Elton without ever saying so — all hint, circumlocution, and the business with the portrait Elton insists on escorting to London for framing — and she wants it that way: plausible deniability is the whole method. But on Grice's account of speaker meaning, the recognition of communicative intention is constitutive: I mean p by an utterance only if I intend you to recognize that I intend you to believe p. Emma succeeds in influencing Elton while partly wanting her intentions to stay off the record, and Elton accordingly reads the wrong woman. So: is what Emma does speaker meaning at all, or some manipulative cousin of it? You might also press on cancellability — implicatures are cancellable, but only by avowal, whereas Emma's hints are engineered to be retractable without ever being owned. Be faithful in both directions: to the actual scenes (the portrait, the carriage ride home from the Randalls party on Christmas Eve) and to the theory, saying where Grice illuminates Austen, where Austen resists, and whether the novel dramatizes a genuine gap — perhaps a category between meaning something and merely causing a belief.
