so I'm teaching an intro applied ethics unit on what makes consent valid, and the discussion allways stalls at "deception and coercion are bad" without anyone actually taking a stand on what consent IS or which flaws kill it. I want a decision tree that sorts students by their actual theory, not by which label they like. Could you write it cases-first: each node a concrete scenario the student rules on before they know any position names, each leaf a theory of invalid consent.

Here are the cases I want somewhere in the tree — add more if you need to, but these have to show up:

- a doctor says an examination is standard medical procedure while doing something else entirely (deception about the nature of the act itself)
- an identical twin impersonates his brother so the partner thinks she's with the person she chose (deception about identity)
- a guy secretly takes the condom off mid-act when the partner consented to sex-with-condom
- a man lies about being rich and recently divorced to get someone into bed (a collateral lie — it changed the circumstances, not which act happened)
- he lies about wanting a future together — and as a SEPARATE node don't merge these, the same lie told to someone who says afterwards "honestly, that wouldn't have changed my decision" (deception that made no difference to the choice)
- a boss hints a promotion is on the table if she "keeps him happy" (offer with leverage)
- a straight-up gunman version of the same request, for contrast
- someone who's had a few drinks but is clearly tracking what's going on

The branch questions should be answerable from the case alone, without knowing where they lead — e.g. "did the flaw change WHICH act was authorized, or only the circumstances around it?" or "when all of someone's decent options involve agreeing, is the yes real?" Questions like "was she really consenting?" are cheating since they presuppose the answer.

And the leaves should be genuine rivals, located by content: the view that invalid consent means no permission was ever granted, so the act is exactly as wrong as if there'd been no consent at all; the act-description view, where only lies about the act or the person's identity invalidate (collateral lies just make you a jerk); the broad view that any lie the person materially relied on invalidates; the will-bypass view, where deception and coercion invalidate for the same underlying reason — something got inserted between the person and the choice; the view that leveraged offers produce valid consent but the leveraged person is still wronged; and a deflationary "consent is just a signal, and signals can't be invalid, only dishonest" leaf if you can make it fit.

Please build the logical space first — work out the combinations of answers the case questions allow, THEN show me where each leaf sits in it — and afterwards flag two things: pairs of cases your tree can't actually separate, and answer combinations that end up unoccupied, saying for each whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Maybe 10 internal nodes max. Keep it usable as a handout: I want to be able to follow it live in class and land on a leaf.
