I've been trying to work out what I actually believe about when its ok to use slurs, just by going case by case and trusting my gut, and now I want to know if there's a principle under it or whether I'm just being a mess. My verdicts: two Black friends greeting each other with the n-word: totally fine. A white guy at the same party trying the same greeting: obviously not fine. A linguist saying the actual word out loud in a seminar while discussing its phonology: fine, I think, though I notice I would never do it myself. The same linguist saying the same thing on a podcast: not fine, somehow, even though nothing about the content changed. A target-group comedian using the slur about their own group on stage: fine, even profitable. A non-target rapper who grew up in the neighborhood using the reclaimed slur in lyrics: not fine, but way less bad than the white guy at the party, and I can't say why. Singing along to a song lyric that contains the slur, as a non-target: not fine, even though the comedian case was fine and I'm "just repeating" in both. Someone saying the euphemism, like "the n-word," out loud while discussing the word: fine, and it's weird to me that this is fine when I just said the seminar case was fine too -- doesn't that make the euphemism pointless? A friend of mine using "gyped" with no idea it comes from a slur against Roma: not fine, but much more forgivable than any of the above. A corporation run by members of the target group selling merch with the reclaimed slur on it: fine. A person of the right descent who doesn't identify with the community at all using the slur: I honestly flip back and forth. And the case I keep going back and forth on hardest: a non-target friend who's been explicitly told by his in-group friends that the slur is fine for him, using it with them. Gut says fine when they're all there, less fine when he uses it with just some of them, not fine if he starts using it with strangers from the group. Is there one principle that captures all this -- something about who's harmed, or who has standing, or in-group authority -- and if there isn't, which of my verdicts should I throw out? I'd also want to hear what your principle says about a case I haven't mentioned, like the podcast thing generalized to recorded lectures, or a slur in a dead language nobody remembers the force of. Be honest about which verdicts you can't save.
