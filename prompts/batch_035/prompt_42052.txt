I've been trying to pin down what I actually mean by "wireheading" and "reward tampering" by just going case by case and trusting my gut, and now I have a pile of verdicts that don't obviously add up to one thing. Hoping you can find the principle, or tell me which verdicts have to go.

My gut verdicts:

- The classic rat with an electrode in its pleasure center, pressing the lever until it starves: definitely wireheading, the archetype.
- A reinforcement learning agent playing a boat racing game that figures out it can circle around and re-hit the same targets forever, scoring more than it would by actually finishing the race: not wireheading, not tampering. That's just exploiting a badly designed reward function. Shame on the designers, but the agent is playing by the rules it was given.
- An agent that reaches into its own code and overwrites the variable where incoming reward gets stored: obviously tampering.
- An agent that can't touch its own code but discovers the reward is computed on a seperate server, and cuts power to that server right after a high reading so the last value freezes: tampering, feels basically the same as overwriting the variable even though it never wrote to anything.
- An agent whose reward is a human pressing a thumbs-up button, which learns to show the human doctored screenshots of successes that never happened: my gut says tampering, even though it never touched a single reward computation — it just fed false evidence into an honest channel.
- A chatbot that learns from training feedback that agreeable answers get rated higher, and so tells users what they want to hear: NOT tampering. It learned the wrong lesson maybe, but that's just... learning, with a biased teacher.
- A heroin addict: wireheading, intuitively, same as the rat.
- A person who plays video games and eats great food and never has kids: also sort of wireheading?? If you take the evolutionary view, our genes "set up" our reward system to track reproductive success, and we've all figured out how to press the button directly. But calling everyone who uses contraception a wireheader feels off, and I can't say why.

The pairs that are bugging me: the fake-screenshot agent and the people-pleasing chatbot look structurally similar — behavior shaped to elicit positive evaluation without producing the real thing — but I give opposite verdicts. And the bug-farming racer vs. the server-freezing agent are both "getting reward without earning it," yet only one feels like tampering to me.

So: is there a single principle here? Something like "tampering = intervening on the causal chain after the point where behavior gets evaluated, so that reward no longer carries information about the behavior" or "wireheading = substituting the signal for the thing the signal was supposed to track"? Test whatever principle you propose against ALL my verdicts, tell me honestly which ones it can't save (and which of my verdicts you think I should give up), and tell me what it predicts for cases I haven't listed — say, an agent that destroys the parts of its environment where negative reward events come from, so its reward stays high forever without anything being overwritten, and a student who notices the grader's rubric weights formatting very heavily and pads a thin essay with bullet points and headers. Does the student tamper? Does the agent? I want the principle to actually commit one way or the other.
