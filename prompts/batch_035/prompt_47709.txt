Robert Nozick died in 2002, before generative AI became commercially significant. Suppose we could consult the author of Anarchy, State, and Utopia about the following scenario and ask how his entitlement theory would handle it:

A singer-songwriter's recordings circulate online. A technology company trains a generative model on a corpus that includes those recordings without the artist's knowledge or consent; the resulting system can produce convincing imitations of her voice. The company's product becomes wildly popular, and its founders' holdings eventually reach billions of dollars, built on millions of small voluntary subscriptions.

Please reconstruct, as faithfully as the published record allows, how Nozick would most likely analyze this case. In particular:

1. Would he regard the initial training as an injustice? Consider whether the recordings fall under justice in acquisition or justice in transfer, whether the Lockean proviso has any purchase on cultural works and personal likeness, and whether self-ownership extends far enough to cover one's voice as reproduced by a model. Distinguish carefully between an ordinary copyright dispute — a matter of conventional property rules that Nozick took to be legitimate background institutions — and a genuine violation of the theory's core principles.

2. Would the founders' resulting wealth be just under the entitlement theory? Someone might run a version of the Wilt Chamberlain argument: millions of people voluntarily paid small amounts for a product they wanted, and no one is entitled to complain about the patterned outcome. Assess whether this adaptation succeeds, or whether the tainted provenance of the training data means the wealth inherits an injustice requiring rectification.

3. How, if at all, would Nozick's later reconsiderations — his admission in The Examined Life that his earlier framework underestimated the significance of social cooperation, and his acknowledgment that taxation to redress past injustices might not be the only legitimate rationale — change the verdict?

Where you must extrapolate beyond anything Nozick actually wrote, flag it as such and explain the basis for the extrapolation. Conclude with an overall judgment: is the AI company's position defensible on Nozickian grounds, and which part of the case is doing the most work in your answer?
