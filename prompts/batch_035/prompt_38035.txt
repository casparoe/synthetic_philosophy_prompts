At a philosophy discussion group I attend, a self-described libertarian defended the slogan 'taxation is theft' against the charge that it is mere rhetoric, but when someone raised land value taxation he immediately exempted it: no one created the land, he said, so no labor or voluntary transfer can ground a title to it, and taxing its rental value takes nothing anyone is entitled to. I want to know whether that pair of positions is coherent or ad hoc, and the only way I can see to find out is to get clear on what the slogan itself asserts.

Please lay out the distinct precise claims the slogan could be making, for instance: (1) the strictly legal reading, on which theft is a category of positive law and taxation fails to be theft simply in virtue of being legal — presumably not what anyone repeating the slogan means; (2) a natural-rights reading, on which forced transfers of justly held property are wrong-making independently of what the law permits; (3) an authority reading, on which the slogan is not about rights between individuals at all but about the state's lacking the standing to coerce contributions that a private extractor would lack; (4) the self-ownership and labor-based appropriation reading that the land-value exemption seems to presuppose, under which titles to produced things have one moral basis and titles to land another; and any further readings you think are live.

For each reading, tell me whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and — this is the part I most need — whether it is consistent with exempting a tax on unimproved land value while condemning income and sales taxes. Then give me your verdict on whether the exemption strengthens the slogan, by revealing that it rests on a genuine theory of original appropriation rather than blanket anti-tax sentiment, or exposes it, by revealing that its key premise — that titles to produced things are untouchable while titles to land are worthless — begs at least as many questions as the slogan itself. I want a menu of readings with verdicts attached, not a defense of one position, since I'll be presenting the readings back to the group.
