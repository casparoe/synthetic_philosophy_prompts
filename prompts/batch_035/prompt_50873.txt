I've been trying to figure out what actually makes promises binding by going on gut feeling through a bunch of cases. Promising to help a friend move and then bailing for a concert: obviously binding. Promising to hand over my wallet at knifepoint: not binding at all. Promising a dying friend I'll look after her cat for the rest of the cat's life: binding, and it stays binding even though she's gone now and can't be hurt by my breaking it. But promising to keep paying dues to a club that's since dissolved: feels void, even though the club can't be hurt by my breaking it either. A promise to lie for a colleague on his resume: not binding, I'd almost say I'm bound in the opposite direction. But promising to keep a friend's affair secret from her husband: binding, even though that's also covering up a wrong. A promise my kids extract from me after an hour of whining: still binding, which honestly surprises me. A promise I make out loud to myself, like promising myself I'll never smoke again: barely binds at all, if it binds at all. So is there one principle that gets all of these, or close to it, and which of my verdicts would you tell me to give up? The cat vs. the club is the pair that really bugs me — surely the difference can't just be that I loved my friend and didn't love the club. And I'd want to know what the principle says about the case I keep flip-flopping on: a promise I made half-jokingly at a party, in front of witnesses, that I'd fund someone's documentary if they ever made one, and now years later they actually quit their job to make it.
