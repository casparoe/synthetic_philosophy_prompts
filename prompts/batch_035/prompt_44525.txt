I'm a retired particle physicist — did detector work mostly, then some phenomenology at the end, thirty-odd years — and since retiring I've gotten properly into philosophy of science for the first time. I've now hit the Humeanism vs. anti-Humeanism debate about laws of nature and I have this nagging feeling that both sides are talking past what I'd actually say if you put the question to me in lab language. So here's the job: restate both positions in *my* vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Assume I'm fluent — you don't need to explain what a Lagrangian or a Wilsonian RG flow or integrating out modes is. The Humean best-system view (Lewis's line that laws are just the axioms of whichever deductive system of the total history of events best balances simplicity and strength, and the souped-up version Loewer pushes where the package including the Past Hypothesis and the statistical-mechanical postulates is the best system — the Mentaculus, I think he calls it, after the Coen brothers movie): render that in terms of Lagrangians, symmetry groups, boundary conditions, effective theories, RG fixed points. Then do the same for the anti-Humean side — Armstrong, Dretske, Tooley, laws as relations among universals that *govern* rather than merely summarize the pattern of events.

Then I want the residue report, and I want it honest: what translates cleanly, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. A few specific things to address along the way. First: in my world "the laws" just meant the Lagrangian plus the initial state, and nothing about the Lagrangian ever felt like it legislated anything — it's the thing you write down that gets the numbers right. Is that a Humean intuition I should be proud of, or am I smuggling in an assumption (that the only thing a law could *be* is something fit to data) and calling it common sense? Second: an old collegue of mine insists effective field theory already settled this — at every scale you have different "laws" that are just low-energy bookkeeping for the level below, so the best-system competition is just what we literally do when we write down the Fermi theory instead of the full electroweak Lagrangian and everyone knows both are fine for their regime. But he *also* insists there's a real Lagrangian at the top of the tower, some exact theory waiting at the fixed point, so as far as I can tell he's half anti-Humean without noticing it. Tell me whether I'm reading him right, and whether the EFT picture actually helps the Humean or quietly stabs it in the back. Third, and this is the part I actually care about: is there anything in this philosophical fight that my vocabulary genuinely cannot say, and if so, is that because physics-talk is missing something real, or because the disagreement is less substantive than the philosophers make it look? Don't be diplomatic with me — I've read enough intro-level phil of science to be bored by hedging.
