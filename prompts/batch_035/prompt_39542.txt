I am a high school senior writing an extended essay on whether an intelligence explosion would be fast or slow, and I have come to suspect that the arguments I read talk past one another mainly because nobody states what a theory of "takeoff" is supposed to deliver before defending one. Rather than commit to a timeline, I would like the specification first. Please write out the desiderata that any adequate theory of AI takeoff speed must satisfy. I have drafted the beginnings of such a list myself, and I would like you to correct, refine, or extend it wherever my versions turn out to be naïve: (1) the theory must state what quantity is actually growing during takeoff — general cognitive capability, the capacity to automate AI research itself, or economic output — since these can come apart, and a theory about one of them cannot silently borrow evidence about another; (2) it must locate the feedback loop, saying whether improvement flows through the system writing better training code, through designing better chips, or through the economy as a whole, because a loop that passes through semiconductor fabrication is constrained by factories in a way a loop confined to software is not; (3) it must express its predictions in some unit other than calendar years alone — perhaps doublings of effective research progress — because if the system doing the research thinks at machine speed, a year is not a natural measure, and the choice of unit can make the very same scenario look fast or slow; (4) it must distinguish a genuine discontinuity in underlying capability from a threshold effect, in which an ability that accumulated gradually only becomes visible or deployable once it clears some economic or safety-testing bar; and (5) it must say in advance what observation would count against it, so that it is not compatible with every possible headline. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a genuine requirement rather than a prejudice of one camp of forecasters, and mark which you regard as non-negotiable and which as negotiable. The part I most want to see is whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, whether a theory can be about a measurable quantity, calibrated in a non-anthropocentric unit, and falsifiable, while still telling a policymaker anything usable within a decade. If some subset cannot be jointly met, please say so plainly, since that result would matter more to my essay than any particular speed. Please do not supply a bibliography or cite specific papers; I would like the reasoning to stand on its own.
