Write a dialogue between Maya, who's signed up for gradual-replacement mind uploading, and her brother Theo, who thinks the whole procedure is an elaborate way of killing her and replacing her with a copy. The clinic's protocol has a couple of quirks that I want them to actually fight about: (1) each artificial neuron runs in shadow mode alongside its biological twin for weeks before the biological one is switched off, so for a long stretch every relevant computation happens twice, in parallel, slightly out of sync; and (2) the artificial neurons are slower than the biological ones, so they do most of their catching up in batch while she sleeps. Theo should press on things like whether the shadow-mode redundancy means there are briefly two of her (or zero), and whether a mind that's partly running on "last night's reruns" can be continuous with the person who went to bed. Maya should respond with her best arguments, not just vibes — she genuinely thinks the procedure preserves her, and she has reasons. Give them real back-and-forth where they engage with each other's points, and let the dialogue end unresolved but with each of them having sharpened their view.
