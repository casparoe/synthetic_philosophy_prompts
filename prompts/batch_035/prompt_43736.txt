My book club spent this spring on Dennett's Consciousness Explained, and the final meeting ended with two members, Maya and Dev, arguing about the Cartesian theater for a solid fourty minutes while the rest of us did the dishes. Dev is a psych PhD student; Maya is the only one who actually finished the book. They've asked me to referee, and I honestly can't, so I'll relay the exchange as faithfully as I can.

Maya opened by saying the Cartesian theater -- the picture where all the brain's distributed processing eventually gets delivered to one place, or one privileged moment, where consciousness officially happens -- is exactly what Dennett thinks the science rules out. Her centerpiece was the experiment where two dots flash in sequence at different positions, the first red and the second green, and people report seeing a single moving dot that changes color midway -- they even confidently report an intermediate yellowish phase, though no yellow light was ever shown. For Maya the lesson is that the brain can't be screening a finished movie for anyone: if it were, the yellow would have to be rendered somewhere before it got watched, and there's no such rendering, no second showing. What exists instead are multiple parallel drafts of content, and being conscious of something just means that draft has won out -- gotten influence over memory, report, and control.

Dev didn't dispute the experiment. He said Maya was answering the wrong question: the fact remains that there is something it's like to watch that dot turn green, and Dennett's move -- explaining how the brain produces the report while treating the actual showing, the experience everyone means by consciousness, as nothing over and above those judgments -- is changing the subject, not explaining anything. At most, he said, the asynchrony results prove the theater is spread out in space and time, not that there's no theater. Maya answered that this begs the question: insisting there must be a further showing over and above the discriminations and reports is just re-seating yourself in the theater you were evicted from. Dev came back that if consciousness is just whatever content wins influence over memory and behavior, the deflation is too cheap -- by that standard a lot of obviously non-conscious processing counts, and it's never explained why any winning draft should be mine, why there's a subject at all rather than a brain grinding out descriptions. Maya's last word was that the subject is itself a useful fiction the brain maintains, a kind of "center of narrative gravity," so Dennett has an answer to that too -- Dev just doesn't like it.
