I've been having an argument with my brother about whether the world is less violent than it used to be, and instead of the usual hand-waving I figured I'd just list my gut verdicts on cases and see what principle, if any, is behind them. Per capita battle deaths falling steadily since WWII: less violent, clearly. Small-scale tribal warfare killing a comparable or higher share of its (much smaller) population than WWII killed of the world: I still say the tribal world was worse, though I can't articulate why that feels so obvious to me. The Rwandan genocide packing most of a million murders into a hundred days: that makes me doubt the whole "modern world is more peaceful" thing. Sanctions that kill kids through malnutrition and wrecked healthcare: I don't want to count that as violence, but the kids are just as dead. Factory farming killing tens of billions of animals a year: if animals count, and I really do think they count, this is the most violent period in history by orders of magnitude — and yet I still balk at saying the present is more violent than the Mongol conquests. Car crashes: not violence. Deaths from diseases that cheap policy could have prevented in poor countries: somewhere in between. And on measurement, I go by per capita rates, not absolute numbers — the world getting bigger shouldn't count against it — but when I imagine billions of absolute future deaths from some catastrophe, I don't discount that at all. So: is there a principle about what counts as violence and which measure is the right one that captures all or most of these, what does it say about cases I haven't listed, and which of my verdicts does it force me to drop? Also tell me honestly whether you think there's even a fact of the matter about whether the world is "less violent" versus it just being a definitional choice.
