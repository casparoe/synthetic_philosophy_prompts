ok so I had my first kid five months ago and it has completely scrambled how I think about population ethics, which I used to treat as a fun cocktail-party topic and now can't. my sister is loudly childfree, my mom keeps saying the planet already has enough people on it, and I keep finding myself defending half-formed positions at thanksgiving. so before I say something dumb again, I wrote down what I actually believe, and I want someone to tell me if it even hangs together:

(1) deep down I'm basically utilitarian about welfare — happiness is what matters, and a world with more total happiness is better, other things equal.

(2) relatedly, adding happy people seems good to me. like a future with lots and lots of people all living genuinely great lives is better than a smaller future with equally great people. I don't want to be one of those "quality not quantity" people if it means saying a huge happy population is somehow worse.

(3) but the repugnant conclusion is repugnant to me, full stop. a zillion people whose lives are just barely worth living is NOT better than a few billion very happy people. that seems like a reductio of whatever produced it, not something I should learn to live with.

(4) the procreation asymmetry feels obviously right: it would be wrong for someone to have a kid knowing that kid's life would be miserable, but there's nothing wrong with choosing not to have a kid who would have been happy. you don't owe existence to anyone.

(5) I also don't think it's wrong for a couple to conceive when they know the child will have some condition, as long as the kid's life will clearly be worth living. that particular kid couldn't have existed otherwise, and their life is good, so who's the victim? (I think this is parfit's non-identity thing but honestly I half-remember it)

(6) whatever else is true, any view that says adding a person whose life goes badly is somehow BETTER than adding a person whose life goes well — I gather some average-welfare moves imply something like this to dodge (3)? — that view is just broken, non-negotiable.

(7) and despite all of the above, I feel like having my daughter was a good thing simpliciter, not just good for me and my wife. like the world is impersonally better with her in it. and I don't think my sister is doing anything wrong by not having kids.

to me (4) and (7) look flatly contradictory, but maybe that dissolves somehow? and I suspect (1) and (3) are fighting but I honestly don't know which pairs are the real problems. go through it: which of these are genuinely in tension and how badly, which only look like it, and for each real one what the cheapest fix would be. rank the tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad. and I'm not asking you to talk me out of the whole package or convince me to bite a bullet on any particular item — I just want the audit. keep it readable, I don't have a phd, I have a five month old and a phone.
