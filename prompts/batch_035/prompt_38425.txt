I'm not a historian, just someone who's spent the last few years reading about the "why did Europe industrialize first and China didn't" thing on my own time, and I've gotten to the point where I mostly argue with myself about whether the question itself is broken before I can even try answering it. People call it the Great Divergence debate or the Needham question, and there's a whole pile of books arguing about it, but half the fight seems to actually be about which question is even being asked. So here's what I want from you.

Say I've got three or four years of serious part-time attention to give this -- evenings and weekends, no grad school, no archive access, just reading and thinking and maybe writing some long essays. Which of the open questions in this area actually deserve that attention, and in what order? I care way more about the reasoning behind the list than the list itself.

Some candidates I keep going back and forth on, feel free to add your own or throw mine out:

1. Whether the question is well-posed at all. "Why Britain and not China" smuggles in a bunch of choices. Why Britain and China as if those are the natural two finalists? Why not "why not the Netherlands" -- they had the highest wages, the biggest trade network, and still industrialized late. Why not "why not Italy in 1500," or "why didn't Song China pull it off around 1100," which would make the whole story China fumbling a lead it held for centuries instead of Europe winning from behind. Each reframing gives a different answer, and I cant tell if choosing the frame is the whole ballgame or just a distraction from doing real work on any single version of it.

2. When the divergence actually happened. Some folks put it way back (deep institutions, culture, stuff going back centuries), others say no, look at the numbers, China's best regions were comparable to Europe's core until around 1750-1800 and then it's a rocket, so it's all about coal and colonies and late-18th-century specifics. This one feels upstream of everything else to me? Like if the divergence is recent and local, then the deep-causes people are answering a question nobody asked. But I've seen the reply that "recent and local" just pushes the mystery back a step rather than solving it. Is this actually settleable, and does it deserve top billing either way?

3. The Netherlands thing specifically, which I almost never see treated as it's own question. If the Dutch had high wages, markets, tolerance, a huge empire, and still didn't kick it off, doesn't that quietly kill every "Europe had X" explanation where X is something the Dutch also had? Or is there a decent answer out there that I just haven't found?

4. Whether the counterfactual is even coherent. "Why not China" implies China-with-a-twist could have industrialized, but nobody agrees how close it ever was, so are we doing history or building made-up worlds with British accents?

For each item in your ranking, tell me why it's worth a non-academic's finite attention specifically (not why it's interesting in general), what makes it tractable for someone with no languages besides English -- that matters a lot, since I've noticed some of these questions basically require material that only exists in French or Chinese, and I need to know which ones those are -- and what would have to happen for it to move up or down your list. Also be straight with me about which of my four are overrated or aren't really open questions at all, and if there's a question in this space that's both important and neglected that I didn't list, I want to hear it.

And don't give me a reading list. I know the books exist. I want the prioritization and the defense of it.
