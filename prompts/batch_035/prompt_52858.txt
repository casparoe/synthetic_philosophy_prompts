I've been asked to write a short statement of my position on the axiom of choice for a set theory reading group, and before I do I want to know whether what I believe actually coheres. Here it is. First, I'm a realist about sets: there is a determinate universe of sets, and CH and AC each have a determinate truth value. Second, AC is true; moreover, I think people who happily use Zorn's lemma but balk at the choice axiom are confused, since the two are equivalent over ZF. Third, the iterative conception of the set-theoretic universe is the correct conception of it, and an axiom is acceptable only if it is intrinsic to that conception, never merely because it is fruitful or convenient. Fourth, the effects of full choice on the reals strike me as pathological: a non-measurable set of reals, or a well-ordering of the continuum, is an artifact of an overstrong formalism rather than a feature of mathematical reality, and Banach–Tarski, whatever else it shows, shows that full choice licenses treating the reals in ways no well-behaved collection should be treated. Fifth, I accept that the reals can be well-ordered, but I also think it means something that no one can define a particular well-ordering of them. Sixth, the determinacy axioms describe how sets of reals genuinely behave, with their regularity consequences, even though I know full determinacy contradicts choice, so at most I would want determinacy for the definable sets. Go through these and tell me which of the commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what the cheapest consistent revision would be and what it would cost me; please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. And if you think the whole framing is confused somewhere I haven't noticed, or that one of the tensions I would worry about is actually harmless, say so plainly instead of hedging. Please answer in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
