Write a short story (roughly 800-1500 words, but I'm flexible) about a character attempting exactly what Wittgenstein's imaginary diarist attempts in the private language argument: they notice some recurring private sensation with no ordinary name — say, a wierd blend of déjà vu and low-grade dread, or whatever you think is evocative — and they decide to keep a diary where they write down the symbol "S" each time it shows up again.

The story should make it vivid why keeping the diary going never settles whether they're using "S" correctly. At some point the character has to face the possibility that today's sensation isn't actually the same one as before, that they just remember it as the same, or that they've drifted into marking anything vaguely similar. Ideally the story shouldn't just narrate this confusion from the outside but let the reader feel the exact moment the project starts to wobble — the point where there's no longer any difference between "I applied S correctly" and "it seems to me I applied S correctly." Bonus points if you work in an outsider (a friend, a therapist, whoever) who obviously can't check the diary against the sensation, which is sort of the whole problem.

Some constraints: no character should lecture about Wittgenstein in dialogue, and the narrator shouldn't explain the philosophy either — let it emerge entirely from the situation, the diary entries, the character's escalating doubts. Please don't end with a tidy resolution or a comforting "and then she realized language is public after all!" moment. Leave the reader with the queasy sense that the word "correct" has quietly gone missing somewhere in the middle of the story, and nobody can say where. Also, don't cheat by giving the sensation a hidden physical cause that the character eventually discovers — that would let everyone off the hook too easily.
