For about a year I have been chewing on an argument of my own about changing preferences, and I have now convinced myself that it proves something I am unwilling to accept. I have no philosophical training — I plan freight routes for a living and think about these things alone in the evenings — so forgive me if I have misdescribed something standard. The argument, numbered:

(1) A rational agent cannot be money-pumped: no sequence of offers, each accepted by the agent's own lights, can leave it holding what it values less than it started with, while someone collects a fee at every step. If a trader can walk you from A to B and back to A, charging you for each leg, you were irrational somewhere.

(2) What makes each offer acceptable is the agent's preferences at the moment of deciding it. Preferences I have already left behind, or have not yet acquired, cannot be the ones doing the accepting.

(3) Preferences change, and some changes are foreseeable by other people. For years I preferred city breaks to hiking; after every city break I would swear off cities for a season and prefer hiking; and the cycle repeats. A travel agent who had my number could sell me the city trip when I am in city-favour, buy it back cheaply when I have sworn off cities, and take a margin on each leg, indefinitely.

(4) By (1) and (2), any agent whose preferences move in ways another party can anticipate is exploitable, and therefore irrational.

(5) But everyone's preferences move in foreseeable ways: anyone who learns from experience, matures, is persuadable, or simply ages. A preference that changed only unpredictably could not be learning anything.

(6) So everyone who rationally revises their preferences is irrational — and that I cannot accept, since revising one's values in the light of experience seems to me close to the paradigm of rationality, not a failure of it.

Something must give, and I cannot see which. I would most like to keep (1): the thought that rationality is at least invulnerability to exploitation is the most solid thing I have. But (2) seems right too. Is the trouble perhaps in "foreseeable" — am I pumpable only because the trader predicts my changes better than I do, and is that ignorance rather than irrationality? Or in (1)'s phrase "holding what it values less", which never says whose valuing, and when? I cannot get a grip on it myself.

Please write in British English.
