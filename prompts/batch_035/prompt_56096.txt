I've been turning this over in my head for a while and want to make a fun little quiz for my group chat. The topic is basically: when we say someone did a good deed "for the right reasons," what actually earns a person moral credit? Like, is a person better if they help out of a sense of duty even though they don't feel like it, or if they help because they genuinely feel for the other person? Or what about someone who does the right thing even while believing it's wrong (I keep thinking of Huck Finn deciding to "go to hell" rather than turn in his friend, he follows his heart against his conscience)? Does HE get moral credit, and more or less than the person whose beliefs and feelings all line up with doing right? I've read a bit here and there online but I don't have any philosophy training, so I'll just describe the positions as I understand them and you can fix me if needed:

1. The strict duty view -- only acts done BECAUSE they're right have moral worth, so the person who helps from warm feelings gets no (or less) credit.
2. The character view -- moral worth belongs to people with stable good character, and reflecting at all (duty or calculation) is kind of beside the point, a truly good person just sees what needs doing and does it without an inner battle.
3. The "right reasons" view -- what matters is responding to the actual moral features of the situation (this person needs help), doesn't matter whether that comes through feeling, duty, or even mistaken beliefs, so Huck Finn is fully credit-worthy.
4. The effort view -- moral credit requires overcoming temptation, someone who never even wants to cheat isn't as good as someone who wants to and resists.
5. The "who cares" view -- all this agent stuff is confused, only outcomes matter, praising people's inner lives is like praising the rain for being wet.

Can you write me the quiz? Maybe 8-10 questions, each one a little scenario with 2-3 answer options, and then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto those five positions. The scenarios should be ones where the positions really disagree -- like cases of helping with no warm feeling, helping against your conscience, doing right effortlessly vs with a struggle, doing right by accident or by miscalculation that happens to land well, that kind of thing. Also please tell me honestly if there are two positions my quiz can't really pull apart no matter what questions I ask, or if there's some combination of answers that none of the five positions can explain. And if there's a real position I've totally missed, mention it, but keep the quiz to the five unless one is genuinely needed. Don't make any question just ask "which view do you hold" -- the whole point is that people answer the scenarios and find out where they land. Keep the language plain, my friends are smart but none of us are philosophy people lol.
