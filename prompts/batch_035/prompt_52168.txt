so some context first: my brother relapsed a few months back and said some genuinely awful things to me while he was in it, and ever since ive been weirdly unable to stop thinking about whether he — or anyone honestly — is really to blame for being the way they are. a girl in my philosophy class lent me her copy of strawson's paper on the impossibility of moral responsibility and it kind of broke my brain. the way i understand the basic argument is: you do what you do because of the way you are, and to be truely responsible for what you do you'd have to be responsible for how you are, which would mean you somehow made yourself that way — but any self that did the making would itself need to have been made by an earlier self, and so on forever, so you never get down to a self that started it all. and apparently this is supposed to work even if determinism is false, which is the part my roommate flat out refuses to accept, she keeps insisting that if choices are undetermined then responsibility is saved.

anyway, i want to end our argument with a villanelle, of all things. the two refrains should be the two load-bearing thoughts: one about doing what you do because of how you are, and one about how being truly responsible would require having made yourself. and the whole point of using this form is that the refrains come back whether you want them to or not — they way the regress reappears every time you try to dig down to the self that started it. each time you find a self that shaped the one you were looking at, the refrain returns and there's an earlier one behind it. and the final couplet, where the two refrains finally sit next to each other, should be the impossibility of being your own cause — the meeting itself, not some tidy resolution.

two things i want you to get right. first, don't smuggle determinism in as the hidden premise — as i understand strawson his point is that even if some choices are undetermined, randomness doesn't help, a random self is even less something you're responsible for. second, don't let it tip into preachy nihilism or "nothing matters" despair. the argument is supposed to feel cold and remorseless, almost mathematical, not tragic. someone who actually knows the basic argument should be able to tell which refrain is which and see exactly why they end up side by side at the end, and someone who doesn't should just get a good poem. and maybe write it in my voice — the person who keeps trying to find somebody to blame and keeps finding only more person.
