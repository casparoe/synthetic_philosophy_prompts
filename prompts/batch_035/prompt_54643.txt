The halfer–thirder debate over the Sleeping Beauty problem has run for over two decades with hardly anyone changing sides: the same variant cases, the same Dutch books, the same charges of begging the question, ever since Elga's original paper and Lewis's reply. I have a hypothesis about the stalemate, and I want it tested rather than applauded: much of it is verbal, with the two camps meaning different things by credence — one effectively answering what betting odds would be fair to Beauty across the whole experiment, the other what she should say given that this awakening is one among the awakenings there are — and the residue is a hidden formal question, namely whether self-locating evidence such as 'it is Monday' is the kind of thing one can condition on at all. Two facts I would like weighed as evidence. First, the question of what Beauty should say when told it is Monday splits both camps internally: halfers who start at 1/2 and simply condition end up at 2/3, while the so-called double-halfers refuse to move; thirders divide in mirror image, some following Elga in holding that the news leaves her at 1/3, others updating to 1/2. Second, the betting arguments never convert anyone, because each side claims the other's bets exploit Beauty across awakenings rather than within a single one. Diagnose the disagreement: how much of it is verbal, how much a substantive formal dispute about conditioning on indexicals, and how much bedrock intuition about whether a word like 'today' can be the subject of a probability at all? Then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress — a decision-theoretic reformulation, a new variant case, a distinction no one has yet drawn — or argue that no progress should be expected and why. Please answer in British English.
