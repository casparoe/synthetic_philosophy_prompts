I've been arguing with friends about the question raised in Hofstadter's "Ant Fugue" — the dialogue where Aunt Hillary, an ant colony, is treated as an intelligent being while her individual ants are treated as, roughly, her neurons. I have no academic background in philosophy or biology; I've read the dialogue and argued about it with friends, and the argument always runs the same course and then stops. One side says: a colony does things that look intelligent — it allocates workers, defends the nest, some species farm fungus and build mounds with something like ventilation — and if mind is a matter of organization rather than material, then a colony with the right organization is a mind to whatever degree it has that organization; the ants don't need to understand anything, just as no single neuron understands anything. The other side says: that proves too much. A neuron is dumb, fine, but the colony's "thinking," if any, is spread out in time and space in a way that never comes together anywhere; there is no location where the colony's point of view could sit, and everything the colony does can be explained as ants following simple local rules with nothing over and above the ants themselves. Nobody moves an inch. My own hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than confirm, is that the two sides aren't really disagreeing about the colony: one is asking whether the colony has the functional profile of a mind, the other is asking whether there is anything it is like to be the colony, and the shared word "mind" keeps them from noticing they have split the question. Is that right? Or is there a genuine substantive disagreement in there — about how much behavior can ever show, or about whether "just the sum of simple rules" and "a mind" are even incompatible? Diagnose the disagreement from what each side actually says, and tell me what would count as progress: a sharper distinction, a finding about how ants interact, a thought experiment, or a reason to think the question is ill-posed and no progress should be expected.
