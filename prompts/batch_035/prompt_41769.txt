Is there a name for the thing I'm trying to point at? It's easiest to say what it's NOT. Last year I bought a car, and the salesman never lied to me once — every single thing he said was literally true — and he didn't pressure me or threaten to end the deal or anything like that. What he did was get me to hold the keys for a while, walk me over to "my" car, ask me what my buddies would say when I pulled up in it, mention that two other people had looked at it that morning. By the time I signed I felt like I had decided completely on my own, and in a way I had, it's just that the whole room was set up so that my deciding would go his way. I walked out happy and the car turned out fine, which is honestly part of what bugs me. I saw the same shape recently with a friend who wanted her sister out of a bad relationship: she never once said "leave him," she just kept the sister over at her place on the worst nights, kept bringing up the small stuff in a casual way, untill the sister connected the dots herself and announced the decision like it came out of nowhere.

So: no lies, no threats, no taking away of options. The person arranges the scene — what you notice, what comes to mind, what feels like your own idea — so that your own thinking lands where they wanted it. It's not ordinary persuasion because no reasons were really offered, and it's not lying because nothing was false. The nearest words I know don't fit: "nudging" seems to be about governments setting default rules, like the organ donor checkbox thing, and that feels different, more impersonal, and also more out in the open. And it's not gaslighting, since gaslighters make you doubt your own perception with stuff that isn't true, and this guy did the opposite — everything he fed me was accurate, it was just curated. Is there an established concept for this exact move, and if there is, what do the people who study it say the edges are?

What I really want isn't just a word, it's the boundary. Because my friend steering her sister feels fine to me and the car guy feels slimy, but I can't explain the difference except that I like one outcome better — and that can't be the whole story, or the salesman would be innocent whenever he happens to sell a good car. Also not sure whether it's still the same thing when the target would approve if they could see what was done ("you'd thank them if you knew"), and there's the slippery end where EVERY conversation involves someone arranging something — your tone, your timing, what you bring up — so I don't want a definition so broad that just being socially competent counts. Basically: is there a name for it, how is it different from the nearby stuff, and where exactly does it stop?
