At Thanksgiving my cousin Dev and my aunt's partner Kat spent like an hour going at it about whether the chatbot on my phone "believes" anything. It started because we asked the bot about an old local diner that closed years ago and it gave us this really confident, totally made-up founding story, fake founder name and all. I mostly stayed out of it and honestly couldn't follow the last few rounds, so here's the exchange as best I can reconstruct it, numbered:

(1) Dev said that on Dennett's intentional stance, whether something has beliefs isn't a deep hidden fact — it's just about whether treating it as a believer helps you predict it. And it clearly does: saying the bot "believes" the fake founder story because it absorbed stuff from the internet predicts what it'll say next way better than talking about tokens and weights. If the attribution predicts, it's as real as belief needs to be.

(2) Kat said that's too fast. With a thermostat, "it thinks it's too cold" is harmless shorthand for a mechanism we already fully understand. For the bot, belief-talk might genuinely help predict outputs, fine — but the second you use the attribution for anything else (deciding it's trustworthy, that it can deceive, that it deserves moral consideration), you're spending money the stance never deposited. Her phrase was that predictive belief is "on loan" and can't be used to buy normative conclusions.

(3) Dev said fine, but her alternative is worse: if the bot believes nothing, then "believes" only applies to whatever we happen to understand mechanistically, and by that standard our own beliefs were unreal for most of human history, since we only learned how brains work recently. The whole point of the stance was to predict believers without peeking inside them.

(4) Kat said she's not demanding mechanism, she's demanding counterfactual sensitivity: the bot would have told the same confident founder story even if the diner's actual history had gone completely differently, because nothing about that history ever reached it. It's not a believer with false beliefs, it's more like a dreamer — confident because confidence is cheap for it, not because it's tracking anything.

(5) Dev said that proves too much. Someone locked in a room with nothing but forged newspapers for a year believes a mountain of false things, and nobody hesitates to say so. Bad inputs plus no correction channel gives you false beliefs, not zero beliefs.

(6) Kat answered that the locked-in person at least keeps one coherent web across contexts and updates when a correction finally gets through, whereas ask the bot the same question in two fresh chats and you can get opposite answers, both delivered with total confidence. So there's no fact of the matter about what it believes at the grain Dev wants. And the stance's predictive success at coarse grain ("it believes Paris is in France") is partly manufactured — you pick the grain where the attributions survive and quietly drop the ones that don't. She called this grain-shopping.

(7) Dev's last word was that all stance attribution is like that: we say our dad "believes" the news even though he'd flip on half his views if his favorite pundit flipped, so grain-shopping afflicts humans too, and Dennett never promised ungrainy beliefs — just ones that pay rent at whatever grain happens to predict.
