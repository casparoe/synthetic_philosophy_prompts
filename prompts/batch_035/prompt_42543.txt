i teach a grad seminar on social epistemology, and this term i want to open it with a diagnostic flowchart instead of the usual intro lecture. the students have all read fricker, most know medina, but dotson and anderson are spottier, so the sorting has to work off cases and not off whose name you can attach to a view. i also dont want it to double as a reading list — no citations, no bibliography, just the tree.

the question the tree should sort people on is: what makes a credibility deficit or a conceptual gap an *epistemic injustice* rather than just a bad epistemic outcome, and what would fix it.

here are the axes i care about (the tree should be built from these, in roughly this order, but adjust if you see a cleaner logical structure):

1. agent vs. structure. does the wrong require a prejudiced hearer (a doctor who half-believes the woman describing her pain because of some "hysterical" association), or can it live entirely in patterned outcomes no individual intends — a jury that just finds the heavily accented witness harder to follow, a recommendation feed that systematically downranks posts written in African-American English?
2. the remedy. is the fix cultivating virtue in individual hearers, or designing institutions and procedures that keep testimonial distortions out regardless of how any particular hearer is wired?
3. hermeneutical gaps that don't track identity. before "sexual harassment" existed as a concept, neither victims nor bystanders could name the wrong — everyone agrees that's the paradigm case. but before "sleep apnea" existed, neither could snorers name *their* condition. same structural gap, no identity tracking. is the second one an epistemic injustice too, or just a hermeneutical disadvantage?
4. credibility excess. the smooth-talking elite speaker gets believed too easily, the deferential listener overcredits him. is overcrediting an epistemic injustice in its own right, or only bad indirectly (because of what it does downstream to the disbelieved)?

the cases that must show up as branch questions (feel free to add more): the doctor/pain stereotype case, the accented-witness jury, the algorithm downranking dialect, the pre-conceptual sexual harassment case, the sleep apnea case, and the gullible listener who believes everyone equally and therefore can't be relied on to track who actually knows.

every node should be a case with two or three answer options phrased so a student can pick one without knowing which position it leads to — something like "this is an epistemic injustice / a bad epistemic outcome but not an injustice / not a wrong at all", never "are you a frickerian?".

the leaves must include: the virtue-theoretic view (the corrective is a virtue in hearers), the active/insurrectionary version of it (the hearer's job isn't neutrality but actively amplifying the discounted), the procedural-institutional view (fix the pipeline, not the people), the position centered on speakers adapting to hostile audiences rather than hearers being fixed, and a deflationary leaf (this is just ordinary injustice with an epistemic costume on). if a plausible combination has no occupant, include it anyway marked "unnamed" with one sentence on why nobody's there — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. and flag any pair of leaves the tree can't actually seperate (i suspect the virtue and active-virtue leaves collapse if you push on case 1, but i want to see if you agree).

format: one JSON object, nothing else around it, with these fields —
- "root_question": string, the starting question every branch hangs off
- "nodes": array of objects, each with "id", "question" (the case), and "answers" (an object mapping each answer option's text to another node id or a leaf id)
- "leaves": array of objects, each with "id", "position_name", "thinkers" (names or "unnamed"), "one_line_commitment", "awkward_commitment" (the consequence a defender has to swallow), and "path" (the list of node answers that get you there)
- "unoccupied": array of objects with "path" and "why_empty" ("incoherent" | "unattractive" | "unexplored" plus one sentence)
- "cannot_separate": array of objects with "leaf_ids" and "reason"

aim for roughly five or six branch nodes and eight to ten leaves. and please double check that every leaf is actually reachable from the root and no node is orphaned — i'll be feeding this into a quiz tool.
