so I've been an MWI guy since reading the sequences back in the day and I've recently been trying to actually write down my views on the Born rule thing, and I want someone to check whether the package hangs together before I post it and get torn apart. here's roughly where I stand:

(1) Many-worlds is just obviously correct. Collapse is an extra postulate whose only job is to hide the other branches, and hiding branches is not parsimony. Occam goes to Everett, this was one of the things that got me into this whole rationality thing in the first place.

(2) The probability problem is solved by self-locating uncertainty. Before I look at the detector, every outcome will happen — but I don't know which future observer I'll turn out to be, and my uncertainty about that is ordinary indexical uncertainty, like not knowing what time it is. Rational credence over those possible future me's is what "the probability of the outcome" means in Everett.

(3) I'm also on board with the decision-theoretic derivations, the Deutsch/Wallace style ones: accept some pretty weak axioms about how a rational agent weights the payoffs of their successors, and Born weights fall right out. This is a big part of why I tell people the Born rule problem is basically solved and everettians can stop being defensive about it.

(4) Typicality: my credences should be calibrated as if I'm a random member of my reference class. I'm not a specially selected observer, I should expect to see what's typical for someone in my epistemic situation to see. This seems like just rationality 101 + anthropics done right.

(5) Branch counting is a non-starter, and for a deeper reason than just that it gets the wrong answers: there is no fact of the matter about how many branches a measurement has. The decomposition of the wavefunction into branches is conventional, coarse-graining is arbitrary, "number of worlds" isn't even well-defined.

(6) Quantum suicide / quantum immortality is obviously insane, and I maintain the community was right to mostly drop it. No rational agent plays Russian roulette on the grounds that there's always a surviving branch where they walked away rich.

I have a feeling there's something rotten in here — like (2) and (3) aren't quite the same story about where probability comes from even though everyone quotes them together, and I suspect typicality is quietly doing work it can't do once you take (5) seriously — but honestly I can't tell whether that's a real tension or me pattern-matching to "measure problem" as a vague worry. Go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, rank the real ones by severity, and say what the cheapest fix is for each and which single revision would clean up the most. Don't go easy on me, this is going in a post.
