I am a retired epidemiologist. For thirty-five years I worked in drug development and pharmacoepidemiology, and since retiring I have been reading the AI-safety literature, where I keep encountering Goodhart's law and its cousin, specification gaming. The formulations I have met are roughly these: Goodhart's original observation that a statistical regularity tends to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for purposes of control; Campbell's related claim that a quantitative social indicator becomes increasingly corruptible the more it is used in decision-making; and, in the AI context, the claim that an optimizer targeting a proxy for what we actually care about will find ways to maximize the proxy while destroying the thing it proxied. My suspicion is that these are several distinct phenomena wearing one name, and that my field has understood most of them for decades under different labels. I would like you to test that suspicion.

Restate this family of claims in my working vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. That vocabulary includes: latent variables and their noisy or confounded measurements; regression to the mean, meaning that selecting on an extreme measured value buys regression toward the population mean on the underlying quantity; selection and collider bias, including the healthy-user effect that arises when those who receive an intervention differ systematically from those who do not; overfitting to a validation set, including the leakage that follows once the validation criterion becomes a target of optimization; surrogate endpoints, and the history of pharmacologically raising HDL cholesterol, a biomarker that predicted cardiovascular outcomes admirably in observational data until drugs were built to raise it, at which point the raised HDL stopped predicting much of anything; and the observational–interventional distinction, i.e., that conditioning on X is not the same as setting X.

As I read it, the literature distinguishes at least four regimes, and I want each one translated. (1) Regressional: the proxy is a noisy correlate of the target, so optimizing it selects for the noise. (2) Extremal: the proxy–target relationship holds in the bulk of the distribution but fails in the tails, which is precisely where the optimizer operates. (3) Causal: intervening to move the proxy fails to move the target, because the correlation was carried by confounding all along. (4) Adversarial: the measured agents model the measurer and game the metric — what we would recognize as the Hawthorne effect, or teaching to the test.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Specifically:

- Which of the four regimes reduce cleanly to familiar machinery? My working hypothesis is that regimes (1) through (3) are literally textbook material — regression to the mean, tail support, and the confounded surrogate respectively — and that (4) alone is structurally novel, because it introduces an agent who models the optimizer. Do you share that reading? Or is the adversarial case merely regime (2) or (3) with a cleverer confounder attached, rather than something different in kind?
- What does not survive the translation? I have two candidates. First, the normative question: in my world the true target is a well-defined endpoint, such as overall survival, whereas the AI-safety discussion presupposes a target that no one can fully write down. Second, reflexivity: the claim that in social measurement the true quantity is partly constituted by the measurement practice itself, so that there is no fixed latent variable to regress toward. Does my vocabulary simply lack machinery for these, or are they less well-defined than they sound?
- A point where I suspect the literature of imprecision: writers seem to run together selecting on the proxy and intervening to move the proxy, which in my field have different failure signatures — collider bias versus unmeasured confounding — and call for different remedies. Am I right that this distinction is doing real work in the Goodhart discussion, or have I imported a distinction that does not matter once the optimizer is strong enough?

I want a systematic translation rather than an essay of analogies: for each claim, say what translates cleanly, what must be distorted to fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Close with your own verdict on the residue — whether it points to something my vocabulary is missing, or to something in the original discussion that was never as precise as its proponents took it to be.
