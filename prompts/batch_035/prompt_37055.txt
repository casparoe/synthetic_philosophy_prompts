My thesis, which I did NOT come up with last night but which last night convinced me to actually defend rather than hedge on, is the intellectualist one: that knowledge-how is just knowledge-that — roughly, if you know how to ride a bike, you know that a certain way of riding is a way of riding, where the "way" is presented to you practically rather than by description, which is supposed to explain why it feels different from knowing facts.

My argument sketch, so you know what the objections are landing on: (1) Ryle's regress — the intellectualist says you can't get smart action by adding a thought in front of action, but I want to flip it and argue the regress only bites if you assume the knowledge-that requires a separate act of contemplating the proposition, and I deny that; (2) the standard linguistic moves — "Hannah knows how to swim" behaves grammatically like "Hannah knows that swimming this way is a way of swimming," embedded questions and all; (3) the pianist with arthritis / the ski instructor who can't ski anymore cases, which I use to argue knowledge-how and ability are separable, so skill itself can't be the knowledge.
