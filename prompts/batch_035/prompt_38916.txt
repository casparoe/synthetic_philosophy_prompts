I keep going back and forth on whether I'm an endurantist or a perdurantist, so I'm just going to lay out my gut verdicts on persistence cases and see if they add up to anything. Here they are. My bike, where over ten years every single part has been replaced one at a time: same bike, obviously, not even a hard call. A ship whose planks are removed one by one, stored, and eventually reassembled into a second ship, while the original hull keeps sailing with replacement planks: the reassembled one is the original ship, and the one that kept sailing is the copy, even though that feels sort of backwards. A caterpillar into a chrysalis and out as a butterfly: same animal, no question. A person destroyed on Earth and rebuilt from a scan on Mars: that's a new person, they died, full stop. Same setup but the Earth body isn't destroyed and now there's one on Earth and one on Mars walking around: I want to say neither is strictly the original, they're both descendants, which bugs me because it's the same process as the last case with one deletion of a step. A club where the entire membership turns over every five years but it keeps meeting under the same name and constitution: same club. A heap I subtract grains from one by one until it's clearly not a heap anymore, even though no single grain removal made the difference: fine with that, heaps are just vague, not everything needs to be exact. A lump of clay shaped into a statue: one thing there, I really don't believe in the statue and the lump being two objects in the same place, right up until you squash the statue and the clay obviously survives -- at which point I guess they were two things all along?? And a wire I bend from straight to curved: it's just the wire, one thing, that now has the property curved instead of straight, and I don't feel any pressure to say it has a straight temporal part and a curved temporal part, though I gather that's supposed to be the perdurantist's clean solution.

So: is there a single account of persistence that gets all or nearly all of these, or am I just entertaining myself with contradictions? In particular I want to know (a) whether "reassembled ship is original" and "Mars copy is not the person" can be made consistent, or whether one of them has to go and which, (b) what my statue/clay flip-flop says about whether I believe in coincident objects at all, and (c) whether the perdurantist story about the wire is really buying me anything given how flat my intuition is there. Please actually state a principle (or two competing candidates) precisely, tell me which of my verdicts it can't save, and give me its verdict on at least one case I didn't list -- say, a hemispherectomy patient who loses half a brain and keeps living, or two rivers, one dammed and rerouted and one just slowly changing course, where people say "same river." Don't just survey the options, tell me what I should think.
