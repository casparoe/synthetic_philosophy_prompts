My friend D. and I have been arguing about compatriot partiality for something like six years now, mostly in the comment threads of each other's blog posts, and after yet another thread where we talked past each other for 200 comments, we've decided to actually try the adversarial-collaboration thing (the setup the replication-crisis people popularized, where two parties who disagree commit to writing one joint statement together instead of parallel polemics). D. is a liberal nationalist, roughly in the David Miller mold: co-nationals have special claims on each other, grounded in shared institutions and a shared political culture, and it's fine — maybe required — that a state weight its own poor over the global poor. I'm closer to a cosmopolitan prioritarian: what matters about a national boundary is only which side of it the worst-off person is on, and special obligations to compatriots are either instrumentally justified or they're nepotism at scale. We've both had to concede the debate is stuck partly because we each use "impartiality" to mean different things, and neither of us is proud of how the last thread went.

Write the joint statement we should be able to sign. Structure it as: a numbered section of claims we can both accept (about what boundaries are, about what the institutional-grounding view and the relationship view each still owe an explanation of, and about what the extreme versions of both camps get wrong); then a disagreements table where each row is one residual disagreement with two columns, one phrasing it so D.'s side would sign the nationalist wording and one so I'd sign the cosmopolitan wording — no slanted phrasings like "arbitrary preference for co-nationals" that only one of us could accept; then the cruxes, meaning sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us, and for each crux a concrete case or argument we both agree bears on it, with both of our predictions written down in advance. We already suspect two cruxes. (1) Whether special claims flow through institutions (cooperation, reciprocity, being in the same scheme) or through the relationship of shared nationality itself — D. leans institutional, but then he owes us an answer for people who don't participate; the test cases we've agreed on are citizenship-based taxation (basically only the US and Eritrea do it) and the citizenship-by-investment passport holder who has never set foot in the country: does she acquire claims on her new compatriots, and do they acquire claims on her? (2) Whether "impartiality" means equal concern for persons or equal concern for claimants within a scheme — we're fairly sure a chunk of our six-year argument was verbal, and we want the statement to say exactly which chunk and why. If you can see an obvious third crux we're missing (I have a feeling it's about the distributive metric — prioritarianism vs. some sufficiency floor for outsiders — but neither of us has nailed it down), add it, but flag it as your addition so we know we didn't agree to it.

Keep the statement to roughly 800-1200 words; the JSON doesn't count towards that. And please dont soften the residual disagreement into mush — the whole point is that the document makes it exact.
