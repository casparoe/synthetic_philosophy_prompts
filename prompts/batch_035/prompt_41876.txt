A friend of mine, Dana, likes to run a party game: given someone's behavior alongside their statements about it, she rules on what they "really" want. Her verdicts so far: my uncle, who says he wants to get fit but never trains, though he did buy a rowing machine that now gathers dust — she ruled he doesn't want it, the machine purchase being cheap talk; my coworker, who complains about her commute daily but has moved apartments twice, each time further from the office and into a prettier district — she ruled the neighborhood outranks the commute and the complaints are noise; my cousin, who says he hates scrolling but does it nightly into the small hours — she ruled he wants the scrolling; and my aunt, who says she loves our weekly calls but has never once initiated one — she ruled she doesn't, and called it her closest call yet. Next month's cases: my sister, who has auto-renewed a subscription for three years to a service she never uses and keeps saying she'll cancel; my father, who drives four hours each way every month to visit my grandmother, complains the whole time, and says he'd rather stay home; and my roommate, who says he can't stand small talk but initiates it with the neighbors constantly and afterward calls it a chore. Dana would describe her game as inverse reinforcement learning in everyday clothes, so candidate rules can draw on that literature, but plain English is fine. I'm less interested in her answers than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her record just as well, which of the three new cases would split those rules most cleanly, and what further case would settle the question. Please give your best prediction for each verdict, say how confident you are overall, and single out the prediction you're least sure of.
