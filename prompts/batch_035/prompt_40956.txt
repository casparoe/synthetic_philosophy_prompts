A friend from my political-theory seminar has a hobby of filing every movement, party and cause into one of two boxes, left or right, and over the years she has issued verdicts on quite a few. Her record so far: a social-democratic party campaigning for steeply progressive taxation, nationalised utilities and strong unions, and otherwise secular, feminist and relaxed about immigration — left; a single-issue campaign against income tax, which says nothing whatever about cultural questions — right; a monarchist society devoted to hereditary succession and an established church, and largely silent on economics — right; an anarchist squat that recognises no property and no leaders — left; and a green movement that wants equal per-capita carbon rationing and a wealth tax, and is in other respects culturally permissive — left.

A new movement has now appeared in our town, calling itself Family and Labour. Its programme: nationalise the railways, energy and water; index-link pensions and entrench union rights in law; set a statutory family wage high enough that a single earner can keep a household, with a homemaker's allowance on top; halt most immigration; restore religious assembly in schools, make divorce harder and restrict abortion. Its rhetoric denounces the money power and the rootless professional class, and celebrates the household, the chapel and the pit village.

She has not ruled yet, and I would rather have your analysis than a bare answer. Which rules genuinely fit her record — I suspect more than one does, since almost every verdict so far either has the two natural axes pointing the same way or is silent on one of them. State the candidate rules, say what each of them predicts for Family and Labour, give your best guess at her verdict with your reasons, and describe the further case, of the same home-made kind, that would most cleanly separate the rules that currently fit. If you think her record already commits her to one verdict, tell me exactly where the commitment comes from.
