I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas for a general-audience outlet, and I run a newsletter where a couple times a year I do an interactive "what do you actually believe?" quiz — did one on free will last year that did pretty well numbers-wise, though the comment section turned into a war about the scoring key. The next one I want to do is on the internal vs external reasons debate: when we say someone "has a reason" to do something, does that have to connect up with what they'd want after thinking it through properly from where they actually are, or can there be reasons that hold of you even if no amount of deliberation, however ideal, would ever bring you to care? I want to sort readers by how they react to cases, not by which -ism they already call themselves, so no questions like "are you a Humean about reasons" — the labels should only show up in the scoring key at the end.

Please give me around eight questions, each a short concrete scenario with two or three answer options. I'd like these cases in there (or close variants): the person who wants a few drinks before the career-defining party tomorrow and just hasn't thought about how they'll feel; someone who is fully informed, perfectly intelligent, completely unmoved by other people's suffering, and doesn't care that they don't care — is that a mistake about reasons or just how they're wired; the arrogant man who would be devastated to find out his wife is deceiving him but whose arrogance is exactly what makes it impossible to get him to honestly reflect his way to caring; and someone who fully grants that a consideration counts in favor of something and still shrugs "so what." You can invent one or two more cases if a branch of the logical space needs its own diagnostic. Then the scoring key: map answer patterns onto the main positions — the strict internalist view where reasons must trace back to soundly deliberated motivation, looser versions that let very latent or abstract motivations count, the externalist realist view that reasons can hold independent of any motivational route to them, views on which seeing a reason just is being moved by it (so the shrugging person is in some sense defective rather than merely indifferent), and constitutivist-flavored views that ground reasons in being an agent at all. And please flag honestly what the quiz can't separate — e.g. can it really tell apart "there are no external reasons" from "external reasons exist but only license criticism, not motivation"?

One more thing: for any answer pattern that doesn't correspond to any actual position in the literature, say so and tell me whether that's because the combination is incoherent or just unattractive/unexplored — I find those "empty corners" fascinating and might write a little sidebar about them. Keep the quiz itself in plain American English since my readers are not philosophers, and don't water down the scoring key's accuracy just to make it friendlier; I'm fine with it being a bit spiky, readers seem to like that. Typos in my questions are my own fault but please fix any you'd introduce!
