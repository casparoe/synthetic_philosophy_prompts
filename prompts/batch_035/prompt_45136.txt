I've been arguing with my sister for weeks about whether happiness and suffering are symmetrical, and I finally just sat down and listed my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, and now I'd like someone to tell me what principle, if any, actually captures them. Case one: if a charity can either prevent ten kids from getting lifelong chronic pain or create fifty kids who will each have wonderful lives, I'm confident the charity should prevent the pain -- the suffering just seems to matter more, maybe a lot more. Case two: a world containing exactly one person who stubs a toe once and then lives a hundred years of bliss is better than an empty universe, which I'm also pretty confident about. Case three: between a future with a huge amount of happiness and a moderate amount of suffering and a future with much less of both, I lean toward cutting the suffering hard even at a big cost in happiness. Case four, and this is where I flip: someone offers me a button that would painlessly and instantly end all life on earth, and I would not press it, even though I also believe there's basically no chance the future's happiness outweighs its suffering -- and I notice I can't explain why I feel that way given everything above. Case five: adding a very happy person to a world where nobody is made worse off seems like a good thing, not a neutral thing.

So my question: is there a coherent principle that delivers all of these, or close? My sister thinks I'm just being squeamish about the button and that if I take the other cases seriously I should either press it or drop the "suffering counts way more" stuff. I'd like to know whether there's a way to carve this up -- maybe suffering gets some kind of priority over happiness without it being absolute, or maybe some of my verdicts apply to lives that already exist and others to potential people and that's doing more work than I realized. And I'd want the principle to actually predict things on new cases, like whether I should fund a project that's almost certain to create tons of happiness but carries a tiny risk of a future with astronomical amounts of torture in it. If you have to overturn one of my verdicts to get a clean principle, tell me which one and why, but don't just pick the dramatic button case by default -- I want the reasoning, not the pathos. Also worth noting I'm fine with the answer being that I hold two principles that each cover part of the terrain, as long as you're explicit about where one hands off to the other.
