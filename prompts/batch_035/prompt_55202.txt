i've been in a logic reading group for about two years now and the dialetheism thing never goes anywhere and i want to understand why. Setup as we've hashed it out: the liar paradox has premises everyone likes and an inference form everyone likes, and it concludes that some sentence is both true and not true. Priest's move is to say that when every individual step of an argument is fine and the conclusion is a contradiction, the honest response is to accept that a few contradictions are true and reason non-explosively from there (paraconsistent logic so a contradiction doesnt entail literally everything). The classicists in the group say no way: accepting even one true contradiction means nothing can ever be ruled out, and anyway the 'not' in the paraconsistent systems allegedly isn't real negation, just some weaker subcontrary-ish connective, so maybe the dialetheists are solving a different problem than they think. Then somebody always brings up revenge: whatever the dialetheist says about the liar, you can construct a sentence that says of itself that it is not both true and false, or that it is only false, and the whole fight restarts. nobody in this group has changed their mind in two years and i'm starting to suspect that's informative.

my own hypothesis, offered only as a hypothesis: the disagreement isn't really about the paradoxes, it's about what counts as a solution. priest demands a uniform treatment that grants the paradoxes their premises and validity and accepts the conclusion; the classicist demands a diagnosis that identifies some premise or rule as the culprit. those are different standards, so the same argument functions as data for one side and as a bug for the other. but i might be wrong — maybe it's actually a verbal dispute about the words 'true' or 'not', or the two sides answering different questions under one name, or a genuine substantive disagreement that just happens to be extremely hard.

diagnose the structure of this thing. what kind of disagreement is it, and what does each side's actual behavior tell you — the fact that revenge cuts in every direction, the recurring worry about whether paraconsistent negation is 'really' negation, the fact that everyone agrees on all the formal facts and still nothing moves? and given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: a new formal result, a conceptual distinction, a reformulated question, or nothing at all? give me your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each one or two sentences, no introduction or conclusion, and make the fifth item your honest take on whether this debate can ever end or whether the impasse itself is telling us something about the question.
