I've gotten pretty convinced that big discoveries are basically inevitable — the ideas are just "in the air" and whoever happens to grab them first is basically luck. Like, calculus shows up from Newton and Leibniz at basically the same time, Darwin gets scooped (or nearly) by some collector writing him a letter out of nowhere, and it seems like whenever you dig into the history of any discovery there's always someone else who was close. So I've started telling my friends that the credit we give famous discoverers is kind of like winning a raffle, and if Darwin had died young we'd just have Wallace on our tshirts instead, no big difference. They say I'm being to dismissive of individual genius but can't really articulate why. What would actually count against my view here? I don't want the lazy answer that some discoveries took humans a suspiciously long time (though if that's the real objection, explain why). I want to know what evidence or cases would genuinely shake it — like, are there known near-misses where the idea WAS in the air and nobody got it for decades, or cases where one person's specific weirdness mattered more than the times being ripe? And second, does the credit-is-a-lottery thing even follow from the inevitability thing, or am I smooshing two seperate claims together — maybe discoveries being inevitable AND individuals mattering could both be true somehow? Be honest about which parts of my view are actually exposed and which parts I could keep no matter what comes back.
