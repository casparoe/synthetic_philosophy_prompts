The question is whether propositional attitudes — beliefs and desires as classically individuated, i.e., discrete, sentence-like, truth-evaluable states whose interactions explain behavior — will turn out to belong to the ontology of mature cognitive science, or whether they will be eliminated in roughly the way phlogiston was. His credence in elimination, say by 2100, is about 0.6; mine is about 0.15. Please take on the role of a professional philosopher of mind with strong familiarity with the cognitive sciences, think through the considerations on both sides before committing to any numbers, and then give me your own credence together with the reasoning that produces it.

2. p2: that in such a framework, the states that do the causal work of beliefs will fail to map onto propositional-attitude vocabulary even many-to-one — that is, that there will be no principled answer to which neural/computational states count as the belief that p;

Please be explicit about the criterion of elimination you apply, since on a loose criterion — analogous to the way temperature survived the transition to statistical mechanics by being redefined — my advisor's 0.6 becomes far more plausible than on a strict criterion on which elimination means the folk term refers to nothing real.
