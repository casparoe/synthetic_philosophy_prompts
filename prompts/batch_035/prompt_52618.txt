I'm a software engineer, not a philosopher, but I've been trying to work out my actual position on population size by recording my gut verdicts on cases instead of reading up on the standard debates first. Here is my list. A planet with ten billion people whose lives are clearly worth living is better than the same planet with five billion such people, other things equal. A planet with a trillion people whose lives are just barely worth living is worse than either. Yet if you told me a future civilisation ran trillions of person-equivalents as long-lived digital minds, each moderately flourishing, I'd call that a wonderful outcome. A tiny population of a hundred thousand people with excellent lives strikes me as better than ten billion with mediocre ones, but I'd also say the ten billion world is better than the ten billion world minus a billion people who have decent, unremarkable lives -- which seems to pull in opposite directions. And I have an asymmetry I can't shake: creating a person whose life will be bad is a serious wrong, but failing to create a person whose life would be good seems at most a missed opportunity, never a wrong to that person -- yet I also think a policy that deliberately kept the population near zero forever, with every vacant planet left barren, would be a catastrophic moral loss of a kind, and not merely inefficient.

Can you find a principle, or a small set of candidates, that captures these verdicts? Please state each candidate precisely enough that I could implement its verdict function in code, tell me which clause of it is motivated by which of my cases, give its verdict on at least two cases I haven't listed, and be honest about which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate -- I'd rather overturn a verdict than accept a principle with ad-hoc patchwork. Please also tell me what would change your own recommendation: which argument or consideration, if it held up, would make you favour a different candidate, and where your reasoning from my case list is most likely to go wrong. Please answer in British English.
