You are advising a small foundation that funds research on animal consciousness and can support roughly five projects over two years on the question of insect sentience. The foundation is moved by the scale argument: on the order of 10^18 insects exist at any time, with trillions killed annually in agriculture and pest control, so even a small probability of morally relevant sentience multiplied by those numbers could, on standard expected-value reasoning, dominate the moral stakes of nearly any other animal-welfare question. Some of the foundation's trustees accept this reasoning straightforwardly; others worry that multiplying minute credences by astronomical counts is a way of laundering one's uncertainty into false precision, and that the same arithmetic structure has been used to justify actions driven more by the size of the number than by the quality of the evidence. Settle what the foundation should actually fund: which open questions in the science and philosophy of insect sentience are most worth attacking first, and in what order? Candidates you might weigh include whether nociception and self-administration of analgesics in insects are evidence of valenced experience or merely of complex nociceptive processing without phenomenal consciousness; how much the decentralized nervous systems of most insects count against sentience compared with the more centralized brains of bees; whether research should concentrate on a few tractable model species or spread across orders; and whether work on measurement — e.g., how to define and compare "degrees" of sentience across radically different architectures — is upstream of everything else and therefore the right first investment. Defend your ordering explicitly: say what makes a question upstream of the others, what a philosopher or cognitive scientist could realistically deliver in two years, and what empirical findings would change the ranking. Also say what you would deliberately not fund, and whether you side with the trustees who trust the scale arithmetic or those who don't.
