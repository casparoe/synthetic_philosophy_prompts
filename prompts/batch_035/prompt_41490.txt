As part of a graduate seminar on consciousness I've been asked to write up my own position paper, and before I do I want a consistency audit of the package I find myself holding. Here it is: (1) I am an illusionist: there are no phenomenal properties in the intrinsic, ineffable what-it's-like sense, and the real explanatory task is to say why creatures like us are so strongly inclined to judge that there are. (2) I hold that introspection, while demonstrably fallible about the timing, causes, and fine character of our mental states, remains incorrigible on one point: it cannot mislead me about whether I am undergoing an experience at all. (3) I hold that the badness of pain for the sufferer is constituted by its felt quality — which is why pain asymbolia, where the patient reports the pain but is entirely unconcerned by it, seems to me morally different from ordinary pain — and that our duties to animals and infants track their capacity to feel rather than their conceptual sophistication. (4) I hold that a silicon system that functionally matched a pain sufferer down to the pain reports might still fail to matter morally, because there might be nothing it is like to be it. (5) I accept the illusionist's treatment of the knowledge argument: when Mary leaves the room she gains new discriminatory capacities and a new concept, not acquaintance with a new property.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and which single revision would resolve the most trouble at the least cost — I would rather not give up more than one. Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad, and for each, say precisely what the tension is and what would have to give. Then step back and tell me where your own audit is most likely to fail: what argument or consideration would lead you to a different verdict about which commitment I should abandon, and why.
