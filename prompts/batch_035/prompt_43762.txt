My friend Daphne is an actuary with strong opinions about self-locating probability but no patience for the anthropics literature, so I only have her verdicts and one slogan to go on. The slogan: "Your credence should track how many indistinguishable tickets you hold." Her verdicts, all on Sleeping Beauty–style cases, are as follows. In the standard experiment (fair coin; heads → one Monday interview; tails → Monday and Tuesday interviews separated by an amnesia drug), she says the awakened subject should give 1/3 to heads. If the coin is biased 3:1 toward heads, same setup, she says 3/5. If tails would bring a thousand interviews rather than two, she says 1/1001. If the awakened subject is told it is Monday, she says 2/3 to heads. And if the whole experiment is run in a city of a million bystanders who never enter the sleep lab, she says the answer is still 1/3 — the bystanders are noise. She also insists that the subject's Sunday-night credence of 1/2 needs no correction on waking, because "the coin didn't change; only which ticket I am did."

Next week our reading group discusses a version of Bostrom's presumptuous philosopher. Two cosmological theories, T1 and T2, fit all available physics and astronomy equally well and are, let's stipulate, equally simple, so they start at even odds. T1 entails that the observable universe will contain about 10^10 observers over its entire history; T2, because it makes galaxy formation far more efficient, entails about 10^100. The presumptuous philosopher announces that the expensive experiment planned to distinguish them is a waste of money: his own existence, before any data is gathered, is evidence at roughly 10^90 to 1 in favor of T2, since there are vastly more chances to be someone in T2's world.

I want to predict Daphne's verdict before she gives it. Please do the following. First, extract the candidate rules that fit her track record — at minimum consider the self-indication assumption, which weights hypotheses by how many observers they posit; a self-sampling rule with a very broad reference class, on which you reason as if drawn from all observer-moments anywhere and anywhen; and any more minimal "indifference over indistinguishable experiences" rule that also reproduces her numbers — and check explicitly that each really does predict 1/3, 3/5, 1/1001, 2/3, and "bystanders are noise." Second, say what each surviving rule predicts about the presumptuous philosopher's claim, and whether her slogan discriminates among them. Third, give your best guess as to her verdict, with reasons. Fourth, tell me which further case would best separate the rules that still fit — for instance, the "God's coin toss" setup where a fair coin either creates you as the only observer ever or creates you alongside a million others (what would each rule have her say about the coin after she finds herself created?), or her likely attitude toward the doomsday argument — and what her answer to that case would reveal. Please end your reply with a single machine-readable line of the form \answer{LABEL, odds}, where LABEL is one of STRONG_UPDATE_TO_T2, MILD_UPDATE_TO_T2, NO_UPDATE, or UPDATE_TO_T1, and odds is your best guess at the probability Daphne will assign to T2 after her own existence is taken into account (e.g., \answer{NO_UPDATE, 1:1}).
