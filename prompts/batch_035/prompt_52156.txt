i'm a novelist and my current book is narrated by a mathematician who is, let's say, a little too invested in the MIU puzzle from Hofstadter's Gödel Escher Bach. you know the one: you start with MI, and there are four rules for making new strings (stick a U on the end of anything ending in I, double everything after the M, turn any III into a U, drop any UU), and your job is to derive MU. you can't. and the reason you can't — the number of I's never becomes divisible by three, no matter which rules you apply — is something you can only see from outside the system. which is convenient for me, because the whole novel is about my narrator being unable to see the shape of her own life from inside it. she just keeps applying her little local rules and getting nowhere.

i want a poem in her voice about the derivation attempt, and i want the form to do the actual work, not just allude to the idea. specifically: every stanza should contain a number of I's (the letter — as the word 'I' or buried inside other words, your call how to count it, just be consistent) that is not divisible by three, and the poem should never arrive at MU. no last line that's just 'MU', no clever resolution, no wrapping it up with wisdom. the speaker keeps doubling, appending, cancelling, and the count never gets to zero — and ideally a reader who steps outside the poem and actually counts can see why. someone who knows GEB should clock the invariant right away; someone who doesn't should still get a decent poem about being stuck inside a life whose rules you cant inspect from where your standing.

also: no name-dropping. she never says 'Gödel Escher Bach' or 'jumping out of the system' — she'd find that mortifying, and honestly so would I. 14-20 lines, no fixed meter, and end it on another failed derivation, maybe the one that gets closest.
