My friend Bea has pretty firm views on when you're allowed to take someone's word about aesthetic stuff. Her track record so far:

A critic she really respects trashed a novel she was about to read — she read the whole thing anyway and said she couldn't have an opinion on it until she'd finished it herself. A conservator tells her the pigment in a painting dates to the 1600s — she believes that instantly. A sommelier friend tells her which of two wines is the better wine — she says she can't just accept that, she'd have to taste them side by side. When her friend said the concert moved him to tears, she took his word for the crying, but pointedly not for whether the concert was actually good. And then her sister told her an exhibition was mostly filler, skip it — and she skipped it, no guilt, no urge to go see for herself.

Three new situations coming up:

1. There's a legendary bootleg that only like three people ever heard, one of whom says it was the most beautiful thing the band ever played. The tape is lost. Bea will never, ever hear it.
2. A friend texts her an hour before she sees a movie tonight: the last ten minutes are devastating.
3. A stranger she passes on the trail says the viewpoint she's hiking to is overrated.

What's she going to say about each of these? I'm less interested in the answers than in the rule she seems to be running — full-on aesthetic testimonial skepticism, some acquaintance/autonomy thing where she only insists on first-hand belief when she's actually going to experience the thing, a fact/value line, or something else that fits her verdicts. Also note the bootleg and the movie-night text pull in opposite directions for at least two of those rules, so tell me which of the three cases would best separate them, and whether her skipping the exhibition on her sister's word is actually consistent with whatever rule you land on or a wrinkle that breaks it.
