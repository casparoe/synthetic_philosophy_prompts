I run a little forecasting pool out of the philosophy department — me, a couple of historians, and Dana from economics, who is annoyingly good at it and knows it. We put numbers on things and keep score. I teach philosophy of science, so reference classes are supposed to be my department, and after two years of keeping a log of Dana's calls I still can't reverse-engineer the rule she's running. Her record so far: asked whether a colleague's new restaurant survives five years, she gave the national failure rate and wouldn't even hear about the location, which she called an inside view and a waste of everyone's afternoon. For tenure candidates she uses our department's raw rate over the last twenty years and treats the letters as noise. When someone asked how long American primacy would last, she read it straight off the durations of past maritime hegemons — Spanish, Dutch, British — gave a number on the order of a couple of centuries from the mid-1940s, and refused to cut finer than that. But asked whether the EU federalizes by 2100, she declined to give a number at all, said there's no reference class for it that isn't a joke, and that anyone who quotes you a probability there is dressing up a vibe. And when the Ukraine war started she called its duration off the distribution of interstate wars since Napoleon, fat tail and all, and beat the entire pool with it.

Next month's question is the one I actually care about: will the CCP still be ruling China in 2075? Predict her verdict, number included. Then tell me what rule she's implicitly running — I can see at least a most-specific-class-with-enough-members rule, a durations-for-entities-but-abstain-on-unprecedented-events rule, and some coarsest-pooling version, and they do not agree here, because the candidate classes pull in different directions: Leninist one-party states (several of which died somewhere in the forty-to-seventy-year range), Chinese dynasties if you take the dynastic cycle framing seriously (which puts 2075 comfortably inside the historical range), autocratic regimes generally, and so on. So: which rule fits her track record best, which class does it send her to on this question, and then design me the follow-up question for the pool that would actually split the remaining candidate rules — I want the case where two of them give different numbers. Keep in mind her track record isn't cleanly decisive, so I want your best guess plus an honest account of which of her past calls you're leaning on and which ones strain your story.
