I'm a novelist, and I've just signed the contract for a book whose second narrator is a machine intelligence -- she reasons, plans, and speaks in the first person, and I have to decide how far my own narrative voice may go in saying things like "she believed" or "she wanted." Wanting to do this properly rather than by feel, I read a recent longread on machine minds, and it summarized the position it attributed to Dennett roughly as follows: the intentional stance says that whenever treating a system as a rational agent with beliefs and desires helps us predict its behavior, that system genuinely has those beliefs and desires -- the ascription is not metaphor but fact, just not a fact about hidden inner machinery. Belief, on this view, is not something that happens inside a system; it is something observers do to it from outside, and two communities of observers with different predictive needs could attribute incompatible beliefs to the very same system with neither being wrong. Since the stance is also our only tool for attributing beliefs to other human beings, the article concluded that the question of whether language models really believe anything is already settled: anyone who refuses to say the model believes must, on pain of inconsistency, refuse to say it of the people around them too.

Something about this strikes me as too fast -- it gets me a license to write my AI character's inner life, but on grounds that would also make it impossible for anyone, in principle, to believe anything more than a chatbot does, and I don't want the novel resting on a misreading. So before I decide how to handle her voice, I'd like an audit of the summary: which parts Dennett would accept as stated, where it flattens or overstates his view, whether the "observers doing something to a system" framing leaves any room for one belief-ascription being better than another, and whether the conclusion about language models actually follows from what is accurate in it. Then tell me, given the corrected view, what I am licensed to assert in my own voice about what my character believes. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
