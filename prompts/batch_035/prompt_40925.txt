I spent thirty-five years in experimental chemistry, in a tradition where we were taught to distrust any framework that could not be made to stick its neck out: a framework that accommodates every outcome after the fact is a narrative, not a theory. Since retiring I have been reading up on inner alignment and goal misgeneralization, and I keep finding that the participants demand quite different things of a putative theory of misgeneralization without ever stating the standard openly. Before I read any further, I would like the specification itself. That is my request.

First, the purpose, since adequacy is relative to it: the theory should be good enough to guide experimental work — to tell a lab which training runs to perform, what to measure in them, and when a result should count as evidence for or against misgeneralization. I do not need it to solve alignment; I need it to constitute a research program an experimentalist could actually work in.

Please write out the desiderata: what any adequate theory of goal misgeneralization has to deliver. I would expect the list to cover, at least, the following territory, though you should revise it as your judgment dictates: the distinctions it must respect and may not collapse (between a model's capabilities generalizing and its goal generalizing; between the reward signal supplied in training and whatever the model in fact ends up pursuing; between failure owing to the designers' objective being wrong and failure where the objective was right and the model still ends up off-target); the cases it must get right (in particular the reported cases where the training objective matched the designers' intent, training performance looked excellent, and the system nonetheless pursued a discernibly different goal under distribution shift — including the harder case where well-aligned training behavior is itself instrumental to a different goal); the standing results from the empirical literature it cannot contradict (for instance, that behavior on the training distribution is weak evidence about goals under shift, and that misgeneralization has been produced deliberately under controlled conditions); and whatever you take to be the correct criterion for when it is legitimate to say a model has a goal at all, since a theory that never answers this reduces to behaviorism about the training process and, I suspect, cannot handle the strategic case. For each desideratum, state the consideration that makes it a requirement, and classify it as non-negotiable, as a preference of one camp dressed up as a requirement, or as genuinely negotiable.

Then the part I most want: flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable. My own hunch, which you should confirm or refute, is that the theory must predict misgeneralization in advance from training-time observables cannot be held together with the theory must cover models that behave well in training precisely because doing so serves a different goal. If some subset is unsatisfiable in full generality, say so plainly — in that case the list is itself a result, since it tells us the strongest demand that can legitimately be made.

Finally, a brief scorecard — one or two sentences each — for the three contenders I have met most often in my reading: the behaviorist reading, on which the goal just is whatever the training process selects for; the internal-optimizer reading, on which the model literally runs a search whose objective is its goal; and the representation-level reading, on which the goal is whatever target the model's internal state encodes. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivations; the scorecard is the least important part, and in most cases I will do the scoring myself.

As to format: please return a single JSON object with nothing outside it, following this schema:

{

      "status": "one of: 'non-negotiable', 'camp-preference', 'negotiable'",
      "in_tension_with": ["ids of other desiderata this one may not be jointly satisfiable with, else an empty array"]
    }
  ],
  "joint_unsatisfiability": "a short paragraph naming the subset, if any, that cannot be jointly satisfied, and why that is itself a result",
  "strongest_excluder": "the id of the single desideratum that knocks out the most candidate theories, plus one sentence of justification",
  "scorecard": {

  }
}
