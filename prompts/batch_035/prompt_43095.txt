I run a student rationality meetup — about 14 people, mostly CS and econ undergrads, 90 minutes, we've got a whiteboard and a big bag of candy. Next session is on races to the bottom / multipolar traps, the Moloch-ish stuff (we read the Meditations on Moloch essay ages ago so the name is already out there, no need to hide it). What I want is a game where they build the trap themselves: nobody does anything they'd call wrong, every single choice is locally sensible, and by the end everyone is worse off than at the start and can trace exactly which of their own choices got them there. Give me the full design — rules, materials, a payoff scheme in candy or points (no real money), round structure with a rough timeline.

One thing I specifically want: at least one phase where they're allowed to talk freely and make agreements *before* things go bad, so they discover for themselves that a handshake isn't enforcement. Then the debrief: map what actually happened in each phase onto the concepts — the everyone's-locally-rational point, why the equilibrium holds even when every player can see it perfectly, what exit or enforcement or mergers actually require. Use our actual scoreboard, not the hypothetical one, as much as you can given you won't know how it plays out.

Also two honest sections please. First, what will predictibly go wrong when I run it: my group is friendly, they all know each other, and I fully expect them to try to cooperate their way around the entire exercise from round one — tell me how to structure things (teams? anonymity? stranger matching? entry fees?) so that goodwill doesn't just dissolve the point, or whether I should let it dissolve and use that as the lesson instead.
