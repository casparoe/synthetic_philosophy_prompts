ok so have you heard the sphex wasp thing? The wasp paralyzes a cricket, drags it to her burrow, leaves it lying at the entrance, goes in alone to inspect the nest, comes back out, and only then pulls the cricket inside. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's in there, she comes out, finds it misplaced, drags it back to the entrance — and runs the whole inspection again, from the top. You can apparently do this to her over and over and she never catches on, its the same loop every time. From a distance the behavior looks intelligent, up close it's a script. There's a name floating around for this, sphexishness, and people use it as a gotcha about free will — either the wasp is just a script and we're only fancier scripts, or look how mechanical animals are, humans are obviously cut from different cloth. I'd rather think it through than pick a side by vibe, so here's what I want: the requirements list for a theory of free will that can actually handle this case. Like, it should say where rigid-but-smart-looking behavior belongs, distinguish genuine flexibility from mere randomness (a wasp that sometimes did something random would be worse off, not freer), place the sphex somewhere between a thermostat and a person and explain what exactly she's missing that a dog or a crow has, and not quietly collapse into either we're different in kind or nothing is free, both of which feel like cheating to me. For each requirement say why it's load-bearing rather than a nice-to-have, and tell me which ones are real desiderata and which are just doctrines one camp pushes. The part I care about most: point out where two requirements pull against each other, because I have a feeling the full list isn't jointly satisfiable, and I want to know if that's right or if I'm just being defeatist about it.
