My cousin and her husband have spent the spring hunting for an apartment, and at dinner last weekend their hunt turned into an argument neither could win. The building they've settled on is one where each unit is identical except that rent goes down three dollars a month for every flight of stairs you climb. Marcus's claim was that this building proves "better than" isn't transitive. For any single flight, he said, the three-dollar saving and the extra climb cancel out — between the second floor at one rent and the third floor at three dollars less, you're indifferent, you'd flip a coin. But he's not remotely indifferent between the ground floor and the twelfth: at that point the climbing clearly outweighs the savings, which are now under forty dollars a month. So the coin-flip relation chains from floor to floor while refusing to connect the endpoints, and since indifference just means "neither one is better," better-than can't be behaving the way everyone assumes. Priya answered that "better than" is transitive by its nature, like "heavier than": if A beats B and B beats C, A beats C, full stop. The coin-flip isn't indifference but coarseness of perception — each three-dollar step makes one unit a hair better, and you just can't feel a hair. Marcus replied that a value difference too small to register in any single choice he could ever face is a difference doing no work, and that his judgment is the only scale there is — there's no instrument for value the way there's a scale for weight — so the "true" order she keeps invoking is one nobody can read. Priya's last word was that a ruler marked in inches makes millimeter differences look like ties without making lengths equal, and that the cure is simply not to decide one flight at a time: compare the endpoints when the stakes are big, use the coin-flip judgment when they're small. Marcus said that concedes his point — now the choice-guiding relation and the true value order have come apart, which is all he claimed. Please go through their exchange step by step and tell me who is right about what, where each of them overreaches, and what, if anything, both are missing. I'd especially like to know whether the staircase shows anything about "better than" itself, or only about our ability to notice it.
