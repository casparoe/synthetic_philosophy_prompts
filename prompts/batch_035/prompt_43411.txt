I've read enough arguments about whether a huge number of dust specks in eyes can add up to something worse than a single instance of torture, and I've noticed the two sides mostly talk past each other by appealing to different unstated requirements. So before I take a side, I want the specification rather than another argument. Write the desiderata: what any adequate rule for aggregating small harms across individuals has to deliver, with the motivating case behind each requirement.

In particular, I'd want you to cover: which cases such a rule must get right — the same total quantity of trivial harm concentrated in one person's life versus spread across billions of lives; trivial harms repeated within a single life versus the same harms distributed so that each person gets only one; certain harms versus small probabilities of severe ones; and cases where each individual harm is so small that the person cannot notice or remember it, yet the population as a whole is measurably worse off than a counterpart population. Which distinctions must the rule respect rather than collapse — between the severity of an instance and the number of instances, between persons and person-moments, between aggregation within a life and aggregation across lives, between harms and mere unfulfilled improvements (no speck versus speck is not the same gap as no torture versus torture). And which structural constraints are non-negotiable — transitivity of the better-than relation, dominance reasoning when one population's situation is uniformly at least as good and sometimes better, impartiality between persons — as opposed to which of these are actually camp-specific prejudices dressed up as neutral requirements. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied: for instance, whether full sensitivity to arbitrarily small harms is compatible with any threshold below which we refuse to let numbers accumulate, and whether that incompatibility should be treated as a result about the desiderata themselves rather than about any particular rule. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate rules — a strict lexically ordered threshold, a power-law or otherwise convex weighting of severity, or plain additive aggregation — and then run those three candidates briefly against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on the scorecard; I'll do the final judging myself.
