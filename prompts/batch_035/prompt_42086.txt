My best friend and I spent most of Saturday evening arguing about the meta-problem of consciousness, and the conversation left me dissatisfied in a particular way: we kept evaluating proposed explanations by whether they fit our own pre-existing allegiances in the first-order debate, rather than by any shared standard of what such an explanation is supposed to accomplish. She thinks a full account of why we judge that there is an explanatory gap would simply dissolve the hard problem of consciousness (her sympathies are illusionist, roughly along the lines of the account Frankish has developed); I suspect that a successful meta-problem theory might be neutral on the first-order question, compatible with dualism as much as with physicalism, though I can't yet say precisely what such a theory would have to deliver to count as success. Rather than continue the argument from our trenches, I want to step back and get the specification right first.

So please write out the desiderata for a theory of the meta-problem: what any adequate account of why we have the problem intuitions about consciousness has to deliver. To be clear about the target, the meta-problem is the problem of explaining why we claim and judge that there is a hard problem at all — why the existence of phenomenal experience strikes us as leaving an explanatory gap that no amount of physical detail seems able to close. Some requirements I can imagine, which you should develop, refine, correct, or supplement: it must explain the *force* of the intuitions (the sense of obviousness), not merely their occurrence, since a story that makes the intuition look like a peripheral quirk rather than a deeply seated feature of our cognition will have explained the reports away rather than explaining them; it must not quietly presuppose an answer to the first-order question, if the two of us with opposite metaphysical commitments are both to accept it; it must explain why consciousness in particular attracts this treatment when other once-mysterious phenomena — life, heat, lightning — stopped seeming to resist physical explanation; it must cover the whole family of problem intuitions (the explanatory gap, the conceivability of zombies, the something-more-that-Mary-learns), not just one of them, and it must account for the awkward fact that philosophical training seems to intensify rather than dispel the puzzlement; and it must stay consistent with what is known from developmental and comparative psychology about the attribution of experience in children and animals. For each requirement, give the case behind it; mark which you take to be non-negotiable and which are really the prejudices of one camp wearing the costume of a demand; and flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — I am particularly interested in whether the neutrality requirement is genuinely compatible with the requirement that the theory show the intuition to be a predictable output of our cognitive machinery. Then, briefly, assess how a deflationary or illusionist meta-problem theory and a phenomenal-concepts-style account fare against the list, keeping the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard.

Please write your reply in British English.
