I'd like your credence that at least one species of insect is sentient, in the strong sense that there is something it is like to be that animal and that some of what it is like carries a negative valence — not mere nociception, not just flexible avoidance behavior, but phenomenally conscious states that are bad for the creature having them. My credence sits around 0.35; a colleague of mine puts hers at roughly 0.02. I'm less interested in your headline number than in where you think the two of us actually disagree, so please decompose the question rather than answering it as a lump.

The terms I have in mind, roughly, are these — restructure them if you think something is missing or misframed:

1. The probability that phenomenal consciousness requires some organizational or structural feature that insects lack — for instance, a certain kind of large-scale recurrent integration, or even just a centralized brain rather than a nervous system whose processing is spread across distributed ganglia along the body.
2. Conditional on insects being conscious of anything, the probability that valenced states specifically are among the conscious ones, as opposed to consciousness being restricted to perceptual or navigational content while affect stays subpersonal.
3. The probability that the standard evidential strands are genuinely independent of one another rather than all downstream of a single capacity. To be concrete: the experiments in which honeybees tolerate electric shock to reach sucrose only when the sucrose is concentrated enough to be worth it, the finding that agitated honeybees show pessimistic judgment biases of the kind used to index negative affect in mammals, and self-medication by infected bees. If these are three readings of one thermometer — an ancient nociceptive system that can trade off costs and benefits entirely without feeling — they should be discounted together, not added.
4. Whether moral patienthood requires phenomenal valence at all, or whether something weaker (a welfare function the creature has a stake in) could suffice — though note this term matters for what follows from the credence, not for the credence itself.
5. A structural term I suspect is doing more work than either of us admits: whether evidence for sentience in one species is evidence for all of them, because arthropods share a conserved neural architecture, or whether it must be assessed species by species. If sentience comes as a package across arthropods, then one positive result is positive for perhaps a quintillion organisms; if it is parochial, it is positive for one lineage and the vast majority of individual insects (ants, beetles, midges) remain near-zero on that evidence alone.

Give me a number and a one-line justification for each term, then combine them into your final credence for the headline proposition. Then tell me which single term the number is most sensitive to, and what empirical finding or philosophical argument would move that term the most.

Finally, the scale argument itself. Conditional on any per-insect probability above roughly one in a thousand, expected-value reasoning makes aggregate insect welfare dominate every other animal-welfare consideration, given somewhere on the order of ten to the eighteenth power of them. My colleague accepts that arithmetic but refuses to act on it, on the grounds that aggregating over metaphysical uncertainty at that ratio is structurally like accepting Pascal's mugging — that there is some point at which you stop multiplying and instead say the small probability should be attributed to a defect in your theory of consciousness rather than to the world. So also give me your credence that the refusal to multiply is rational — that is, that the scale argument can legitimately be blocked — and decompose that too: it seems to turn on whether degrees of belief over metaphysical questions are well-behaved enough to act on at small values, on whether the per-organism probability is independent across individuals or collapses into one shared bet about arthropod architecture, and on whether low-intensity welfare states, multiplied across a trillion-fold numerical advantage, really function like tiny probabilities of astronomical stakes or whether that analogy is exactly backwards. Close by telling me, given your full decomposition, whether you think my 0.35 and her 0.02 most likely diverge on the neurobiology, on the interpretation of the behavioral evidence, or on how much of a deflationary story about consciousness itself is available to a determined skeptic.
