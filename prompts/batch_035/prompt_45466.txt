"Bradley's regress comes from Appearance and Reality in 1893. The basic idea is that whenever you have two things standing in a relation, the relation can't actually connect them by itself -- it's just a third thing sitting alongside the other two. To connect it to them you need another relation, and then another relation to connect THAT one, and you're off on an infinite regress. So Bradley concluded that relations are self-contradictory and therefore don't exist at all. This is basically the same problem as the unity of the proposition, which is why Russell and Frege both saw Bradley as the guy who had exposed a fatal flaw in their whole logic. Russell just changed the subject and never gave a real answer. Bradley also used the regress to prove his idealism, since if no relation can ever do its work, everything has to be internally related inside one big indivisible Absolute. The regress is also the standard objection to the bundle theory of objects, and it's generally agreed nowadays that it showed relations just can't be taken as primitive, which is why no serious metaphysician treats them as basic building blocks anymore."
