I am a novelist preparing my next book, whose narrator is an entity I have deliberately placed in the borderlands of the living: a self-repairing chemical economy that has taken root in the flooded ruins of a botanical garden, which metabolizes, responds to its surroundings, and occasionally produces imperfect copies of itself, yet has no cell membrane, no genetic molecule, and no evolutionary history. In shaping this narrator's voice I have provisionally committed myself to a position about life itself: that "life" names a cluster concept rather than a natural kind, held together by family resemblance among several capacities — metabolism, self-maintenance, reproduction, responsiveness, the capacity to evolve — such that any one of these can be missing in something we would still call alive, and the genuine borderline cases are not gaps awaiting future science but places where things simply are alive to a degree. On this view my narrator is, let us say, seventy percent alive, and the novel's central question — whether it can be wronged, whether a reader may grieve it — inherits that partiality.

Tell me what would count against this position. I want a map of its exposure rather than a survey of familiar definitions. Which discoveries or cases would show that life does have a joint in nature at which to carve — a single criterion my cluster analysis somehow misses — and how would we recognize such a joint rather than merely stipulate one? Which entities, real or coherently imaginable, would strain the degrees-of-life picture most: something with the full cluster that we would firmly refuse to call alive, or something with almost none of it that we would firmly embrace as alive? Please order the defeaters from the genuinely damaging to the merely awkward, note which of them committed defenders of the cluster view would themselves concede versus explain away as trading on neighboring concepts like agency, individuality, or organismality, and include whatever the current treatment of viruses, prions, and other marginalia already tells us. Finally, tell me honestly whether the position risks unfalsifiability — whether "alive to a degree" is an insight or an evasion — and whether that risk should matter to someone in my situation, who must nonetheless decide whether the book ends in a death.
