(1) I'm a moral realist: there are stance-independent truths about what's right and wrong, not just widespread agreements.

(2) I think the WEIRD-critique is basically right: people from Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic societies are outliers on moral cognition — heavy on harm-and-autonomy reasoning, individualist, impartialist — so the "intuitions" that get pumped in philosophy seminars are the intuitions of one weird corner of humanity, and a lot of moral psychology generalizes from that corner.

(3) I think the cross-cultural differences are deep in a specific sense: they're not mostly downstream of factual error or missing information. The disagreement survives idealization.

(4) I think moral progress is real. Abolition wasn't just our preferences shifting, it was getting closer to the truth about slavery. Same for, say, torture.

(5) I take reflective equilibrium to be the right method in ethics: start from the judgments we actually make, including the intuitions, and adjust principles and judgments against each other until they cohere.
