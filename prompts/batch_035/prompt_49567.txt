Retired chemist here (35 years in materials testing, now I read philosophy of decision theory for fun, which my wife finds hilarious). I've written down my views on weakness of will and I want a consistency check, because I keep going back and forth myself:

(1) Weakness of will is a real phenomenon. Last night I judged, all things considered, that a third beer wasn't worth it, and then I drank it anyway -- no new information, no change of judgment, no alcohol impairing me yet. I just watched myself do it. That's something that needs explaining, not explaining away as me secretly wanting the beer all along.

(2) The mechanism is hyperbolic discounting: we discount near rewards steeply, so "feeling decent tomorrow" beats "beer now" when both are distant, but the ranking flips as the beer gets close. Preference reversal, no mystery.

(3) On what someone "really wants," I'm a revealed-preference guy. There's behavior and dispositions to behave, and talk of a true self whose preferences got overridden is homunculus talk. I'm also a determinist, no spooky agent causation, for what that's worth.

(4) Still, the akratic act is a genuine failure -- the kind it makes sense to be annoyed at yourself about. A friend of mine says the annoyance is just another signal, like pain, and I don't buy that as the whole story.

(5) Precommitment devices (don't keep snacks in the house, put the alarm across the room) are paradgm cases of rational agency, not an admission that something's broken.

I can feel friction between (3) and (1), and maybe between (2) and (4) -- if my discount curve just is hyperbolic, then the "reversal" is exactly what that curve predicts, and calling it irrational looks like faulting an agent for having the agent's own valuation function. But honestly I might be muddling several things at once. Go through the pairs, tell me which tensions are real and which dissolve on inspection, rank them by severity, give me the cheapest fix for each, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
