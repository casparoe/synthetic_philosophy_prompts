Something I keep chewing on: interpretability labs now have a whole research program around figuring out whether a language model "really believes" what it says, as opposed to just saying it because it pleases the user or matches the training distribution. Concretely, they train linear probes on the model's internal activations to find a direction that tracks whether a statement is true, and when they find one they call it the model's honesty or truthfulness direction; then they steer along it and check whether the model's answers change. Separately, lots of people treat a model's chain of thought as admissable evidence about what it knows -- if the reasoning appeals to the right fact, the model knew the answer, even when the final output came out wrong.

Here's what puzzles me. With humans we mostly do the opposite. If a person sincerely asserts P, and they're not lying or confused, we say they believe P, full stop -- we don't wait around for a brain scan. For a model, though, sincere output apparently isn't enough: the real belief is supposed to live somewhere in the weights, and finding it is a scientific project. And when the model itself is asked what it believes, we treat its self-report as weaker evidence than the probe results, which is a weird reversal of how we treat first-person authority in humans.

So what does this research program actually presuppose? I can think of a few candidates and I honestly can't tell which are load-bearing. Maybe it presupposes that belief is a real, localizable thing inside a network rather than a pattern smeared across contexts and prompts. Maybe it presupposes that our folk categories -- believing, knowing, being honest -- carve the model's internals at their joints even though nothing in the training setup was built to those categories. Maybe it presupposes that there's a fact of the matter about what a model believes that's independent of what it does with tokens. Or maybe none of that, and the practice is actually compatible with a deflationary view on which there's no deep fact of the matter and the probes are just useful compression of behavior.

Can you reconstruct the implicit theory for me? Tell me which commitments the practice strictly needs, which are just baggage riding along, and where things are underdetermined enough that several different views of what belief is would license the exact same experiments. And I'd like your take on the human/model asymmetry too -- is there a principled reason to demand internalist evidence of the model while accepting sincere testimony from a person, or is that just carbon chauvanism?
