Quick question before our reading group falls apart: we've spent a month on presentism vs eternalism and everyone is just picking labels they like and backfilling reasons afterwards. I want to force people to actually locate themselves. So I need a decision tree that starts from something basically nobody disputes (say, that dinosaurs existed, or that it was raining here last Tuesday) and walks down to a named position about time.

The tree has to be built from the logical space, not from a list of isms. What I mean: figure out the questions first — the commitments that genuinely split the field — and only then say which real positions live at which leaves. The questions should be things a person can answer without knowing which position the answer leads them toward. No node should be anything like "are you a presentist?" Obvious candidate questions (use these or better ones you come up with): whether there's a real ontological difference between past, present and future at all; whether past things like Caesar or the dinosaur exist in any sense; whether future things do; what makes it true that dinosaurs existed if they don't exist — is it something about the world now (footprints, presently-existing "Lucretian" properties like being-such-that-there-were-dinosaurs) or nothing, or do the dinosaurs themselves make it true; whether they're willing to accept a privileged reference frame once they've taken special relativity on board; whether the felt whoosh of time passing is a genuine feature of reality or a cognitive artifact; and whether the relief you feel when the dentist is done can be fully restated in tenseless language without losing anything.

Requirements for the leaves: standard presentism, the Lucretian/ersatz presentism variant where past-tensed truths are made true by present facts, growing block, moving spotlight, plain eternalism with a B-theoretic tenseless account, and an open-future view with eternalist ontology but an unsettled future. If some of those end up at the same leaf or can't be separated by any question you can honestly phrase neutrally, say so instead of inventing a fake distinction — I'd rather know the tree can't split them. Also: whenever a combination of answers has no occupant, note it and say whether you think that's because the combination is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored (there's a suspicious number of those in this debate, especially once you combine openness about the future with different attitudes to the privileged frame).

Audience is ten philosophy grad students who know the relativity but not much phil of time jargon, so keep the node questions in plain language. Maybe 6-8 internal nodes at most, one page total, and I want a worked example at the end: trace one person (say, someone who says yes to a privileged frame, no to Caesar existing, and can't accept tenseless paraphrase of the dentist relief) down to their leaf and tell me what they're stuck with. Typos in your output are fine since ill be retyping it anyway, but please keep the structure clean.
