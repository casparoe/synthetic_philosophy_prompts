so here's my situation. im a novelist, a few chapters into a book about a woman who worked in a mill town in the 1890s — someone ordinary, illiterate probably, no letters, no diary, nothing. the further i get the more i realize that almost everything that survives about people like her was written *about* them, not by them: court records, charity ledgers, asylum admission registers, the census taker's line. and the ones who never crossed an official's desk are just gone, completely. before i write another word i want the actual spec, not vibes: write me the desiderata — what any honest reconstruction of a life like this has to get right before it can count as more than costume drama.

the things I want the list to handle, at minimum: the difference between silence-as-absence (nobody thought to record it), silence-as-destruction (records that existed and were burned, pulped, or never indexed), and recorded-but-marginal (one line in a property inventory that's technically about her because she's listed as an asset) — it has to respect all three and not treat them as the same kind of gap. it has to say what a reconstruction may do with a single attested fact vs a rich trove, and where "plausible" stops being an excuse for "invented." and it has to deal with what i keep stumbling into, which is that the poor who got documented were disproportionately the policed, hospitalized, and prosecuted ones — so the surviving "voices" of people like my character skew toward crisis, and if I write her from those sources alone she's a tragic case study, not a person. whether that's a bias a reconstruction is required to correct, or just a fact i have to sit with, i genuinely don't know.

also tell me which requirements are non-negotiable vs which are basically taste (like whether an author's note flagging invention is required or just a convention), and flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied — e.g. make her life typical of women of her station AND grounded only in what documentation exists, since documentation mostly exists for the atypical. if some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, say so plainly, that's the part i most want to see, because it means i have to choose which to break and i'd rather choose knowingly. then a quick scorecard, a sentence each, against three strategies: the novelist who invents freely and drops an author's note, the microhistorian type who stays tiny and close to the documents, and the one who refuses to write the undocumented at all. don't go longer on the scorecard than that, the criteria are the point.

one more thing: i can't check any of this myself, that's why im asking. so as you go, mark how confident you are in each requirement, and call out the one you'd be least willing to defend if a working historian pushed back on it — i suspect it'll be one of the ones about the destroyed records, since we mostly can't know what was in them, but you tell me.
