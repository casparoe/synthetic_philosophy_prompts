Write an essay on whether objectivity is attainable in historical inquiry, taking as your focus the opposition between Ranke's ambition to reconstruct the past as it actually happened and the sceptical line running through E. H. Carr's *What Is History?* and Hayden White's *Metahistory*. Rather than surveying the debate in its usual form, build the essay around the following asymmetry claim: even if grand narrative interpretations are ineliminably perspectival, historical inquiry can still attain something close to Rankean objectivity about negative facts — that a document is a forgery, that a massacre did not occur at a given place, that a particular diplomatic overture was never made. Assess whether the sceptics' arguments, from the inevitability of selection to the shaping power of narrative form and the tropes White catalogues, really undermine the negative case or only the positive narrative one; consider whether the asymmetry is genuine or merely an artifact of negative facts being thinner and less interesting. Weigh both sides fairly, but reach and defend a definite conclusion about what, if anything, remains objective in history.
