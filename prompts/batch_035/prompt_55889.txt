I'm a serious amateur birder with no academic training, and over thirty years of birding I've become convinced of something that I gather philosophers argue about: that what one knows genuinely changes what one sees and hears, not just how one interprets it. When I finally learn a thrush's song after years of missing it, the song doesn't become easier to identify — it seems to pop out of the dawn chorus, as though my ears themselves had been retuned. When I know which perch a particular hawk favors, the hawk seems to present itself to me against the skyline rather than being something I infer from a speck. My position, stated as strongly as I can: expectation and belief penetrate all the way down into perceptual experience, and there is no layer of "pure seeing" that belief leaves untouched.

I've run into two things that complicate this and I don't know how much weight they deserve. One is that certain illusions never yield to knowledge — I can measure the lines and they still look unequal. The other is that learning a new visual discrimination seems to involve a lag: knowing what to look for doesn't immediately translate into seeing it, which suggests some stage that knowledge reaches only slowly, or not at will. And I have a home-made test: if penetration goes all the way down, then an expert and a novice looking at the same image should differ even in split-second, low-level discrimination tasks, not merely in slower identification.

Tell me what would count against my view — which observations, experimental results, everyday cases, or arguments from adjacent areas, sorted by how decisive they would be, from fatal to merely awkward. Distinguish what a committed defender of my view would concede as counting against it from what they would explain away, and be honest about whether the view can be qualified into unfalsifiability — and if it can, whether that's a real problem for a claim of this kind. I'd especially like to know what my position stands or falls with beyond birding: does it commit me to something about whether observation can ever be an independent check on belief, and if so, what would have to be true for that darker implication to be avoided?
