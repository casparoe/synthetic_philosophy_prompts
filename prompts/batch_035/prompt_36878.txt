i've been in a slow-motion argument about the Meno problem (why is knowledge better than, worth more than, mere true belief?) for about a year now, and I'm starting to suspect the debate isn't really about knowledge at all. some context so the diagnosis can actually engage it: the thing goes back to Meno 97a–98a, where Socrates says true opinions are like Daedalus' statues — they run away if you don't tether them, and knowledge is the tethering, working out the reason (the aitia). notice, though, that plato's own answer only buys knowledge a kind of stability: the true belief that stays put is more useful downstream. that's an instrumental story, and arguably it's the only one the dialogue gives you. when the modern debate picked the problem back up, it quietly upgraded the demand. kvanvig's primary value problem wants knowledge to be more valuable than true belief, full stop, not just more useful later. and the swamping worry — zagzebski's coffee version — says that if knowledge is true belief plus an extra ingredient, and true belief is the valuable part, the extra ingredient's value gets swamped the way a great espresso machine adds nothing to the coffee once the coffee's in your cup. virtue epistemologists (greco, sosa) respond by denying the additive picture: knowledge is an acheivement, and an achievement isn't valued as the sum of its parts — the arrow hitting the target because of the archer's skill isn't valued as hit-plus-skill. so my reading is that the two camps talk past each other: one side is doing value theory under an assumption about aggregation that the other side rejects for reasons that have nothing to do with knowledge specifically. the epistemology is, on this reading, window dressing.

that's my hypothesis anyway — that the impasse is really a disagreement about the structure of value. I might be wrong. what I want is a diagnosis, not a survey; I know the positions, and the last thing I need is another tour of everyone's take. some things I'd want weighed:

(a) the sides seem to disagree about what even counts as an answer. the instrumentalist says the stability story already answers the Meno problem; the intrinsic-value crowd says it just changes the subject — it explains why you'd want knowledge for future beliefs, not why this state is better than that one here and now. is that a substantive difference, or a verbal one about the phrase 'better than'? and if verbal, is it verbal in the way that dissolves the debate, or verbal in the way that reveals there are two seperate real questions traveling under one heading?

(b) kvanvig eventually concluded the problem is unsolvable for knowledge and moved the prize to understanding instead. was that progress, or a change of question, and what exactly is the difference between those two things in this case?

(c) there's also a genealogy angle people keep pushing on me: tell a story about why creatures like us would come to prize a state that reliably regiments belief, and you've explained the valuing without ever explaining the value. does that touch the normative question at all, or only a descriptive one?

and then the part I care about most: given your diagnosis, is there anything that could actually end this — a distinction, a result about how values aggregate, a cleverly constructed case, or just waiting for a new generation — or should we expect the impasse to hold, and why? an honest answer on that front counts for more with me than picking a side. don't give me a verdict on who's right about knowledge; give me the anatomy of the fight.
