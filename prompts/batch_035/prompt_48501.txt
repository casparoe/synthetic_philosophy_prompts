I write a little philosophy blog and I'm doing one of those "which view do you actually hold?" quizzes, this time on the special composition question — when do some things add up to one *further* thing, rather than just being some things? I want it to sort readers by cases, not by whether they know the jargon, so no question can use words like "mereology", "simple", or "fusion" (fine in the scoring key, banned in the questions). Build the logical space first: the first split is presumably between "composition never happens" and "composition sometimes happens", and the second branch has to say what does the restricting — contact, life, activity or working-together, fitting into a familiar shape, or nothing at all (in which case weird objects like the thing made of a trout and a turkey exist), or else "it's just brute which fusions there are". Then write 8-10 questions, each a concrete case with 2-3 options: the trout swimming past the turkey; whether "everything currently on my desk" picks out one item or just a way of counting; whether I exist over and above my cells; whether there's an object made of my left shoe and the northern half of the Moon; whether a chair is one thing or many things arranged chairwise; and at least one question about arbitrary undetached parts, like the top half of my coffee mug — is that a real object or just a way of talking? Also sneak in one question about taking a wheel off my bike, and say in the key what that question does and doesn't sort for — I suspect it catches readers who are conflating the composition question with the persistence question, and I want the key to flag that instead of silently scoring it. The key should map answer patterns onto the usual names where positions have them, mark the genuinely unnamed ones as unnamed, and for each answer combo nobody can coherently give, say wether that's because the combo is incoherent or just because nobody's bothered. End with a one-sentence note on which two positions the quiz does the worst job of separating.
