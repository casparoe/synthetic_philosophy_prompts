My clinic's ethics committee likes to think of itself as consistant, and I want to keep it that way. Our past rulings: we allowed PGD to screen out embryos with the Huntington's gene; we allowed picking an embryo that was a tissue match for a sick older sibling, even though the selected child himself would have been healthy either way; we refused a deaf couple who wanted to deliberately select a deaf embryo; we refused sex selection for family balancing; we allowed screening out a BRCA1 variant even though the cancer it raises risk for is partly treatable and might never develop. If we want to stay consistent with ourselves, what's the call? Which of our old cases is the closest analog, and if our record genuinely underdetermines it, what principle would we be adopting with each choice — plus, what's the next case that would best split those candidate principles apart?
