I keep going back and forth on the fink/mask stuff about dispositions and the whole thing seems genuinely stuck. Rough map of the terrain: the simple conditional analysis says something is fragile iff it would break if struck. Martin's electro-fink killed it — the live wire hooked up to a device that would make the wire dead the instant anyone tried to test it is disposed to conduct, but it wouldn't conduct if tested. Lewis tried to patch things with test conditions, but then Bird's antidote cases (glass packed in styrofoam, or next to a wizard who'd catch it — fragile but wouldn't break if struck) and the reverse fink cases (the dead wire that a fink would make live the moment you touch it — not disposed to conduct, but would conduct if touched) attack the conditional analysis from opposite directions, one breaking the disposition-to-conditional direction and one the conditional-to-disposition direction. And then Choi spends the 2000s arguing the conditional analyst can absorb the mask cases, and that the real trouble is the reverse finks. That exchange is pushing 20 years old and as far as I can tell nobody has moved an inch since.

My working hypothesis: this was never really a fight about the counterexamples, it's a fight nobody admits to having about what the word 'disposition' is doing. If you read disposition talk Ryle-style — as licensing inferences about behavior rather than naming an inner state — then something like the conditional analysis is close to trivially right and every counterexample is just a fact about which test conditions are the faithful ones. If you think dispositions are real intrinsic powers, the counterexamples aren't glitches, they're the discovery that powers outrun their manifestation conditions altogether. So maybe the two camps are answering different questions under one word and the stalled debate is mostly verbal, with a substantive residue about intrinsicness doing the real work.

So: is that diagnosis right, or am I letting both sides off too easy? And given whatever diagnosis you land on, what would even count as progress here — a case that forces one side to bite a bullet they can't bite, or just everyone conceding that 'disposition' and 'power' should be used as terms of art and the literature splitting in two? Also, if my map of the debate is off somewhere, tell me — I care more about getting the structure right than about defending my sketch.
