I am fourteen weeks pregnant with a very much wanted first child, and for most of my academic career I have worked sympathetically on antinatalist arguments, so I want an audit of whether the position I have defended on paper survives contact with my own decision. Here is the package I still, as far as I can tell, believe:

(1) Benatar's asymmetry: the absence of pain is good even when there is nobody for whom that absence is good, whereas the absence of pleasure is not bad unless somebody exists to be deprived of it. Consequently, coming into existence is always a net harm to the person created.

(2) A strict person-affecting restriction on betterness: one outcome can be better or worse than another only by being better or worse for particular people. There is no such thing as an outcome being better or worse "impersonally," by containing more good or less bad that belongs to no one.

(3) The deprivation account of the badness of death: death is bad for the one who dies because, and only because, it deprives her of goods she would otherwise have gone on to enjoy.

(4) My own life has been, and remains, very good for me, and I am glad to have been born; my parents did not wrong me by conceiving me.

(5) It would be seriously wrong to conceive in circumstances where one knows the resulting child's life would be barely worth living — constant low-grade suffering that never quite tips the life below the threshold — even though that child, once she exists, would honestly affirm her own life and prefer it to never having been.

(6) I am having this child, and I do not believe I am doing anything wrong in doing so.

Please tell me which pairs among these are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, and rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damaging. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give, and what repairing it would cost me elsewhere in the set. Conclude by identifying the single revision that would clean up the most. Lead with your overall verdict in the first sentence — whether the package is salvageable, and where its main fracture lies — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my commitments.
