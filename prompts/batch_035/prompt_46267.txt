My friend Elena teaches typography, and she plays this game with her students: flash a glyph on the screen, everyone votes "a" or "not an a," and she gives the official ruling. I've been keeping track of her verdicts and now we have a fight brewing about one from last week. Her record so far: a normal double-story Times 'a' — a, obviously. The single-story 'a' you'd write by hand or see in Futura — a. That same Times 'a' rotated 90 degrees — not an a, she said it's "just a shape now." An alphabet where every letter is built out of tiny chairs arranged along the letter's skeleton, so the 'a' looks like a chair-sculpture of an 'a' — a. A one-off font where the 'o' is a square and a round blob with a stub is what plays the 'a' role — she ruled the round blob is an a. A ransom-note 'a' cut from one font and pasted into a word made of five different fonts — a, barely. And some rune-like glyph from a font none of us had ever seen, flashed with zero context — a, instantly (that one confuses me, honestly).

The new case, from last week: a designer made an alphabet where 'a' and 'o' are both plain circles, exactly the same shape, and the only difference is that the 'a' circle is slightly bigger. "cat" written in this font reads fine once you know the trick. Elena refused to rule on it, called it a genuinely hard case, and tabled it for next class. Half our group thinks she's secretly running a skeleton rule — the shape's abstract structure decides, and a bare circle's skeleton says 'o' or nothing, so she'll eventually say not-an-a. The other half think she's running a style rule, where a letter is whatever plays that letter's role in a coherent system, in which case it's obviously an a and the hesitation was theater. Predict what she'll rule, lay out the candidate rules that fit her record (I can see two but I bet there are at least three), and tell me what glyph I could bring to her game that would actually settle which rule she's on. She's also a huge Hofstadter person and brings up Letter Spirit constantly, if that matters.
