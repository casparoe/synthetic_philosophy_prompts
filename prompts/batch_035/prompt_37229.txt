I'm a software engineer — twenty years in distributed systems and backend work, comfortable with the probability and information theory my job has required (enough to know what a measure-zero set is, roughly), and with no formal philosophy training. I read Nietzsche's The Gay Science some years ago and keep returning to the eternal recurrence, and I've realized that whenever I try to explain it to myself, it comes out entirely in the vocabulary of my day job. I'd like you to do that translation deliberately and then audit it.

Restate the eternal recurrence in the terms I actually use: deterministic replay and event sourcing, finite versus countable state spaces, cycles in state machines, pseudorandom number generators and fixed seeds, Poincaré recurrence, Kolmogorov complexity of a history, idempotency, and the difference between spawning a new process with an identical memory image and resuming the same one. Cover both of the things Nietzsche seems to have meant by the doctrine: the cosmological claim that the exact sequence of events recurs, and the practical test — the famous thought experiment in which a demon announces that you must live this very life, with every pain and joy in the same order, over and over — which is supposed to change how you live now. Do the translation as faithfully as you can without sneaking in my vocabulary's own assumptions and calling them his view.

Then give me the residue report. Specifically:

1. In my terms, living the same life again splits into at least two things I usually keep apart: a replay of the same execution versus a fresh process running the same program with the same seed. I genuinely cannot tell which of these Nietzsche needs, or whether he would care about the difference. Where does this distinction do real work for him, and where does my insisting on it distort him?

2. The cosmological version looks to me like a claim about recurrence in a physical state space — either thermodynamics or armchair speculation, and probably wrong either way. But I've gathered that Nietzsche had independent reasons for the doctrine that were not just bad 1880s physics. What, if anything, in those reasons survives the translation into my terms?

3. The practical test is supposed to function as a filter: a life is good enough if you could affirm it on pain of infinite repetition. Nothing in my vocabulary carries that normative force — a replay is just a replay, and a repeating state machine is just inefficient. Is that a defect of my vocabulary, or a sign that Nietzsche is packaging an evaluative claim inside a cosmological one and the packaging matters?

Finally, tell me whether my working instinct — that the thought experiment is the real doctrine and the physics is dispensable framing — is close to a standard reading or is my own caricature. I'd rather know that I've been flattening him than keep a tidy translation that happens to agree with me.
