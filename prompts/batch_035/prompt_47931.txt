quick context: i do grantmaking for a small funder that can support maybe 3-4 researchers for two years on the conceptual side of AI safety — philosophy and theory, no experiments, no compute. lately we keep recieving proposals about "impact measures": ways to make an agent avoid side effects without giving it a full specification of what humans value. the pitch is always some version of "specifying all human values is hopeless, so instead just penalize the agent for changing the world beyond its task."

i don't want a survey of the field. i want a prioritized agenda — which open questions in this space deserve the top slots and why. some candidates I keep seeing, though tell me if the real priorities are somewhere else entirely:

1. the baseline problem. impact is presumably a difference between what happened and what would have happened anyway — but which counterfactual is "anyway"? the state at the start of the episode? the agent sitting still from now on? the world where the agent never existed? proposals disagree, and i can't tell whether this is a minor technical choice or the whole ballgame.
2. one grantee's claim that every "impact" measure secretly encodes a value function, so low-impact AI is really just "optimize a weird utility function and hope the weirdness is benign" — i.e., there is no such thing as value-free side-effect avoidance. if that's right it seems to sink the whole program, so shouldn't settling it come before everything else?
3. someone wants to define side effects via how many different futures remain reachable — the agent shouldn't lock things in or foreclose options. elegant, but is that a definition of impact or a proxy that happens to look good on toy gridworlds?
4. the long-tail worry: an agent that looks low-impact on the training distribution can still be catastrophic if impact is being measured over the wrong variables. is there a principled account of which variables to measure, or is that exactly where the full value specification sneaks back in through the window?

rank these (or your better set) by what should get funded first. the criteria i care about: how much downstream work depends on the answer, whether it's tractable with theory/philosophy tools alone, and how neglected it actually is as opposed to just hard. and be honest about which of these are downstream of others so we're not paying twice for the same insight.

Format: first a table with columns — Question (one line), What it turns on (the crux, one line), Tractability (theory-only, 1–5), Neglectedness (1–5), Upstream of (which other questions), Verdict (fund first / fund later / pass). Then, after the table, a compact JSON summary with fields "top_two" (array of strings), "deprioritized" (array of strings), and "single_biggest_crux" (one sentence). the JSON goes into our internal tracking doc, so keep the values short — no essays inside the JSON.
