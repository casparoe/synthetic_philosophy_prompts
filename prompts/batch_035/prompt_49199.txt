i'm writing up where i stand on peer disagreement for a reading group and want a consistency check before anyone else sees it. here's my package:

(1) equal-weight conciliationism: once my friend and i have pooled all our evidence and i still judge her to be my epistemic peer on the question, i should move my credence most of the way to the midpoint between us.

(2) but in the restaurant-bill kind of case — we each did the arithmetic, i recheck mine and get the same number, and she still gets a different one — i think i'm entitled to stay closer to my answer than to hers. checking twice should count for something.

(3) i think my own judgments about who counts as a peer are pretty trustworthy even from inside a disagreement. when someone disagrees with me and i assess that i've thought about the question longer and harder than they have, i discount their view rather than splitting the difference; i don't see why being a party to the dispute should make me pretend i have no basis for that assessment.

(4) i think most persistent moral and political disagreement between informed, sincere people isn't peer disagreement in the relevant sense anyway, because it's driven by different weightings of values rather than evidence, so conciliationism just doesn't kick in there.

(5) and on the meta-question — whether conciliationism is true — i don't budge when my steadfast friends push back. i've actually read their arguments and i think they're bad, and i don't see how splitting the difference with someone who's made a bad argument is supposed to get me closer to the truth.

tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest repair is and what it costs me. my hunch is that (2) is where i've cheated but i honestly can't tell. if you think the package is fine as it stands, say that plainly instead of inventing tension to have something to report. and at the very end, put the single commitment you'd revise or drop in the form \answer{...} — its number plus a few words on the fix.
