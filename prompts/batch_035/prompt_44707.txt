A psychiatrist I sit with on the panel that decides which conditions go into the next edition of our diagnostic manual has built up a voting record I can't quite read. Her rulings so far: severe premenstrual mood disturbance, in; a depressive episode that set in after a parent's death, indistinguishable symptom-for-symptom from ordinary depression, out — grief, she insisted, is not a disorder however disabling; pathological gambling, in; sluggish cognitive tempo, the childhood pattern of spaciness and daydreaming where teachers and parents complain but the child does not, out; extreme shyness amounting to social anxiety, in.

Three proposals are coming to a vote, and I have to predict her. First, caregiver burnout: exhaustion, withdrawal, and dread in people nursing dying relatives at home, who are plainly suffering and asking for help. Second, lifelong callous-unemotional traits: adults who feel no guilt, harm those around them, and are not themselves distressed. Third, a syndrome whose symptoms spread by publicity — after each media cycle referrals multiply, patients present with exactly the advertised symptoms, and once diagnosed they build an identity around the label that outlasts the symptoms, as though the category were creating the kind of person it names.

Predict her vote on each case, and lay out the rules that could fit her record so far — harmful dysfunction requiring a failed internal mechanism, distress-plus-impairment with an exception for proportionate responses to circumstances, something else entirely — saying where the candidate rules agree and where they come apart on these three. Say which further proposal would best separate the surviving rules, and be candid about what would change your mind: which single additional vote in her record, or which feature of these proposals, would most shift your predictions, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail.
