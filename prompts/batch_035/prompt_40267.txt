I'm a philosophy master's student applying for a one-year departmental research fellowship, and I need a two-page proposal built around a topic I've only half-worked out. The topic concerns the importance–tractability–neglectedness (ITN) framework used in effective altruist cause prioritization, which ranks causes by scoring them on three factors and multiplying the scores together. My suspicion is that the multiplicative structure quietly treats the three factors as independent quantities, and that this assumption fails in at least two ways that matter to the arithmetic rather than just in the details. First, tractability and neglectedness look entangled by definition rather than merely correlated as a matter of fact: whether a problem is tractable *for a given actor* already depends on how crowded the field is, since diminishing returns to additional resources are precisely what neglectedness is supposed to measure, so multiplying by a separate neglectedness score risks double-counting. Second, "importance" as raw scope is a non-marginal quantity, while the framework is supposedly a proxy for the marginal value of the next unit of resources — and I don't think you can fix that by just relabeling the factor, because the way returns diminish is itself part of what makes a problem important or not. Please turn this into a proposal with: a precise statement of the research question (is the multiplicative structure a harmless simplification, a distortion that actually changes cause rankings, or a category error that no patching can fix?); an explanation of why the standard defense — that ITN is only a heuristic scaffold for ordinary expected-value reasoning, so the interactions wash out in practice — does not settle the question; a concrete method for the year, broken into stages (e.g., separating which entanglements are definitional and which merely empirical, reconstructing the scoring so the definitional ones are handled inside it, and stress-testing whether the reconstruction reorders the familiar example causes); a statement of what a successful deliverable looks like for people who actually use such frameworks to allocate funding; and an honest account of what would count as failure — including the possibility that practitioners' use of the framework is so loose that no reconstruction could be principled rather than ad hoc. Do not include citations, a bibliography, or a literature review organized around named authors; I will supply those myself, and the proposal should stand on the argument.
