I write about science and ideas for a magazine, and somehow thats gotten me invited to give a talk at a big effective altruism gathering. My thesis: Williams's integrity objection to consequentialism is still live, even against the sophisticated consequentialism everyone there endorses — the kind where you maximize the good at the level of the criterion of rightness, not the decision procedure, so you're allowed to just live your life. My sketch of the argument: the sophisticated reply leaves your projects answerable to an impartial ranking in a way Williams said integrity can't survive, and when the EAs reach for their favorite move — recasting the whole thing as moral uncertainty, where you weight your Projects-side intuitions against impartial ones like any other credence — I think they've changed the subject, since integrity was never supposed to be one more consideration to be weighed.

The audience: a couple hundred mostly consequentialists (philsophy grad students, people at ethics institutes, a lot of math types), plus the organizers, who think Williams was basically confused. Prepare me for the question period: the objections in roughly the order they'll arrive, phrased the way this crowd would phrase them (expect criterion vs. decision procedure, the one thought too many point turned back on me, and someone asking about integrity for terrible people — bigots and their bigoted projects). For each: a two-minute reply, what to concede up front, and where the reply is soft. And include the one I'm hoping nobody asks — you know the one, about whether integrity as Williams uses it does any actual work or is just a dignified name for not wanting to bother.
