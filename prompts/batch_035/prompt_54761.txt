So I finally sat down with the statue and the clay puzzle and I think I get where its supposed to bite: the statue and the clay share all their parts, but the clay existed yesterday as a lump while the statue didn't, and you can squash the clay without destroying it while squashing ends the statue. Fine. But every solution I read just says they're "constituted but not identical" and moves on like that's not insane. Two physical objects, same place, same time, exact same atoms, and the only thing that allegedly distinguishes them is stuff that happens in other possible worlds or happened last week. That's not a real difference, that's just modal bookkeeping, right? It's like saying my coffee mug and the mug-shaped region of space it occupies are two objects.

So anyway I came up with my own fix and want to know if it holds up: the clay is the only concrete object in the place. The statue is more like a pattern the clay instantiates — basically an abstract artefact, form imposed on matter, Aristotelian vibes. Then there's no spooky co-location because there's only ever one concrete thing there. The problem I keep hitting is that if the statue is abstract, it seems weird that you can smash it with a hammer, and that everyone (me included tbh) treats statues as things that have a location and can be heavy. "I broke the statue" should be a category mistake like "I broke the number seven," but it obviously isn't. Am I missing an easier way out? Or is two-things-one-place actually fine once they differ in when they came into existence and what they can survive — and if so why did I have the intuition that nothing can be co-located with anything in the first place?
