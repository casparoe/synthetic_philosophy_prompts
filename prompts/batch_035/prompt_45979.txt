Here's my opening move, the thing he kept dodging last night: what makes Goodhart interesting isn't that proxies break, it's that they break *because* you optimized on them. Selecting on a noisy proxy doesn't just reveal the noise, it actively concentrates it — the more pressure you put on the measure, the faster the gap between measure and target grows. Regression to the mean is what you get when you merely observe a proxy; the Goodhart version is what you get when you lean on it. And that difference between observing and optimizing is a real mechanism that shows up in Soviet factories hitting tonnage quotas, citation metrics, and RL agents pushing the same reward hack from the same starting point — which is exactly why the standard advice of "just use a better metric" keeps failing, since any metric you optimize hard enough degrades the same way. That's not Campbell's law in a costume, that's a claim about what optimization does to any proxy you're fool enough to push on. Your move, Jonas.
