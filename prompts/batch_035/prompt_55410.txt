Game theory question I've been chewing on and can't find a clean answer to. I'm not a student or anything, just working through textbook games where you "solve" them by crossing out strategies that are dominated — worse than some other strategy (or some mix of strategies) no matter what the other players do — then crossing out more in the reduced game, and repeating until you're left with a little box. Fine. Except I keep noticing stuff that makes me think the procedure is way less innocent than the textbooks make it look:

1. If I cross things out in a diffrent order, I sometimes end up with a different final box. Not always, just sometimes, and I can't tell in advance which games are going to do this.

2. Some of the rows I cross out early only look bad because of a strategy I cross out later. Like, row 3 is dominated by row 2, I delete row 3, and then it turns out row 2 itself is garbage and gets deleted too. So row 3 was eliminated by an argument that eventually eliminates itself? Did row 3 actually deserve to go?

3. When the only thing dominating a row is a mix of two other rows, one friend says you're allowed to delete it, the other says eliminating a strategy on the strength of a randomization you'd never actually play is dodgy. Who's right, and does the answer matter for 1 and 2?

4. A lecturer I watched used a slightly different procedure once — delete anything that's never a best reply, repeat — and on one game that gave a different final box than the dominance version. That one really bugged me. Shouldn't all reasonable versions of "crossing out until nothing's left to cross out" agree, at least on games that have a unique solution?

So: is there a name for this whole cluster of problems with the crossing-out procedure? Somebody must have noticed it by now. And if there is an established term, I want to know where the edges are — which versions of the procedure are actually safe (one friend insists that whenever the domination is strict, all of this trouble goes away — is that a real theorem, and does it also cover the best-reply version from 4?) and which aren't. If there's no single name, please coin one, because I need something to call this besides "the crossing-out mess."
