So here's my situation. I'm 34, single, no kids, and I just got about $100k out of a small acquisition of the company I worked for. I have a decent stable job lined up, rent is cheap, and I have ~$25k in an emergency fund separate from this money, so honestly if the $100k evaporated it would sting but it wouldn't wreck me.

My friend from college is founding a startup and offered me a chance to put the $100k in. His pitch, which I believe is honest, is roughly: 1-in-8 chance it eventually returns about 15x my money, otherwise it goes to zero. So expected value is around $185k vs. my $100k in — clearly positive. My brother-in-law, who did an econ PhD, says that given I can absorb the loss, turning this down would be flat-out irrational, like refusing free money, and that anyone who refuses positive expected value bets when they can afford them is just being cowardly dressed up as "caution."

I pushed back and he went further: he said if I refuse the startup, then to be consistent I should also have to refuse a coin flip where I win $120k on heads and lose $5k on tails, since that's positive EV too — and I'd take that coin flip in a heartbeat, which he says proves my "risk tolerance" talk is incoherent, just vibes.

But here's the thing he said that I actually want to dig into. When I told him I know people who would turn down a sure $40k in favor of a 50/50 shot at $100k, and that this doesn't seem crazy to me, he waved it off, but I vaguely remember reading about a philosopher — Buchak, Lara Buchak maybe? — who argues something like: it can be rational to weight the bad outcomes of a gamble more heavily than their probability alone would suggest, and that refusing a positive expected value gamble isn't automatically a reasoning failure. Something about separating your beliefs about how likely outcomes are from how much you dread the worst ones, with a "risk attitude" in between. I might be garbling it.

So my actual question, and please don't just give me life advice: is there a serious, worked-out philosophical position under which I can decline my friend's startup offer (or accept it!) without being irrational, and if so what does it say, exactly? Does it let me treat the startup differently from the coin flip without being accused of inconsistency — or does it just concede my brother-in-law's point in fancier language? And if the position is real, I want to know where it's vulnerable, because he will absolutely come back at me with the strongest counterargument he can find, probably something about how if I refuse enough positive EV bets over a lifetime I'm guaranteed to end up worse off.

(One more wrinkle if it helps: I noticed that whether I want to take the startup bet depends on how I frame it. If I think of it as "keep my $100k," refusing feels safe. If I think of it as "spend $100k to buy a lottery ticket that pays $1.5M," refusing feels like throwing away the ticket. That framing sensitivity is also bugging me — is that a sign something's wrong with my preferences, or is it just noise everyone has?)
