I run a small philosophy blog and want a quiz titled "What Do You Actually Think About Moral Responsibility?" for readers with no background in the free will literature. It should be a sorting instrument with ten questions, each a short concrete case told from the inside, plus a scoring key at the end. The questions must be cases, not labels: nobody should be asked directly whether they accept the principle of alternative possibilities or whether they're a compatibilist, and no answer option should reveal which theory it feeds. The cases I want included: (1) a classic Frankfurt scenario where a neuroscientist stands ready to ensure you do what you do, but never intervenes because you decide that way on your own; (2) a version where the neuroscientist's trigger is a prior sign in your brain that you could not have avoided having, whether or not you acted on that basis; (3) a case where the intervener skips monitoring entirely and instead implanted your desires and values in advance, after which you act with no further interference; (4) an ordinary case of negligence or forgetfulness in which things would have gone better only if you had been luckier; (5) a plainly deterministic world containing no intervener at all; and further cases in this spirit, each asking for the reader's blame or responsibility judgment about the protagonist. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these leaves: a strict reading of the principle of alternative possibilities; Frankfurt-style compatibilism that drops the requirement of alternatives; sourcehood views on which manipulated agents are not responsible even though they had no alternatives either; responsibility-by-tracing views; and hard incompatibilism. After the key, flag any two leaves your instrument cannot genuinely separate — for instance, whether Frankfurt-style and sourcehood views come apart only on the implantation-style questions — and note any answer combination your key treats as empty, diagnosing whether that combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
