I'm a high school senior writing my extended essay on the sorites paradox, and my supervisor keeps pushing me to state my criteria before I defend any particular solution, which I have come to think is right: every article I read seems to be answering a different question. Before I commit to one view, I want the full specification set out. Please write the list of requirements that any adequate treatment of the paradox has to meet. I have some candidates in mind, but I don't trust myself to separate the genuine ones from the things particular camps treat as non-negotiable. A theory presumably has to explain why removing one grain from a heap seems obviously unable to make it a non-heap while a thousand removals obviously can; it has to say something about whether the trouble is in the world, in our words, or in our knowledge, and I suspect it also has to deal with the version where you are asked to endorse each premise one at a time rather than confronted with a thousand premises at once, because those feel very different even though they are logically the same. It should also probably say what happens when a second sorites is run in the opposite direction, starting from a clearly bald or heapless case and adding, since a theory that only explains tolerance in one direction looks broken.

For each requirement, give the consideration behind it, then sort them: which are genuinely non-negotiable, which are merely the house rules of, say, epistemicists or supervaluationists dressed up as demands, and whether any subset turns out to be jointly unsatisfiable, which I gather is where the real action is and is the part my supervisor most wants to see. I would also like your view on which single requirement eliminates the most candidates, and whether demanding that a theory "solve" the paradox at all is itself a mistake, since some people seem to think the right attitude is to explain our tolerance judgments away rather than vindicate them. Please keep the emphasis on the criteria rather than on grading the existing theories against them; I have to do that part myself, and I would rather not have it done for me.
