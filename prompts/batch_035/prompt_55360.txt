I'm a high school senior who got interested in Bayesian reasoning after reading a popular book about probability and belief, and I've run into a question I can't find a standard name for.

Every presentation of Bayes' theorem I've come across treats evidence as something you learn with certainty: the test comes back positive, the drawn ball is red, and then you condition on that fact. But almost nothing in my own life works that way. Last month I heard a scraping sound at around two in the morning and became, say, seventy percent confident that something had knocked over the bird feeder. I never learned a proposition outright; I just became more confident in one. Is there an established way to update on evidence you only partially believe, or does standard Bayesian practice just assume this away?

Two things make me suspect the answer, whatever it is, is subtler than applying Bayes' theorem with a fuzzier input:

1. It seems to matter how I arrived at my partial confidence, not just how much of it I have. If my seventy percent confidence that the feeder was knocked over comes from hearing a distinct thump, that ought to raise my credence that a raccoon was the culprit by some amount. If the same seventy percent comes from noticing that I can't actually remember whether I righted the feeder myself the previous evening, the raccoon hypothesis arguably shouldn't move at all, even though my confidence in the very same proposition is identical. So if there is a rule for updating on uncertain evidence, does the rule depend only on the final probability I assign to the evidence, or also on the route that got me there? A rule of the first kind looks too weak to distinguish these cases, and I don't know what a rule of the second kind would even look like formally.

2. A move I keep wanting to make is to say that the real evidence isn't the feeder being knocked over but my hearing the thump — that is, to condition on the experience itself, which I do know with certainty, rather than on the worldly fact it points toward. But this doesn't always seem available: my confidence about what my experience even was often shifts too (was that a thump, or the house settling?), and conditioning on every gradation of every sensation seems to make the whole apparatus unmanageable.

Does the problem of updating on less-than-certain evidence have an accepted name and an accepted solution? If it does, I'd like to know what the rule is, what it assumes, and where people think it breaks down — in particular, whether it can distinguish the thump case from the faulty-memory case above. And if there is no settled account, I'd like to know that too, and what the main contenders look like. I'm not after a bare term; I want to understand how the field carves up the problem — what counts as the evidence when the evidence is graded, whether my confidence in the evidence is even a well-defined input to an update rule, and exactly where the ordinary conditioning picture has to be amended.
