My housemate and I keep arguing about split-brain patients and whether they're "one person or two," and instead of just trading intuitions I want to lay out my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and see if there's actually a principle behind them. So here goes: classic commissurotomy patient in Sperry's lab, word flashed to one hemisphere so the patient can't say what they saw but the left hand fishes the matching object out of a pile — during the experiment that feels like two minds to me, or at least two consciousnesses that aren't sharing. Same patient driving home and making dinner afterward: one mind, full stop, I cant take seriously the idea that some second consciousness was just strapped in for the ride to the lab and back. Alien hand syndrome after a stroke, where one hand keeps unbuttoning what the other is buttoning: still one mind — a weird body, not a hidden person. Someone born without a corpus callosum: one mind, and I half remember that they test way less dramatically than the surgery patients, though I couldn't tell you why off the top of my head. Two strangers coordinating over text so smoothly they play like a single chess player and beat a grandmaster: two minds, obviously, even though their information-sharing is faster and richer than a split-brain patient's hemispheres ever manage — this is the one I can't square with my lab intuition at all. Drunk me: one mind, the same one, just degraded. And the case I keep flip-flopping on: the split-brain patient asked whether he likes some object, whose left hemisphere says no while the left hand gives a thumbs up — is that one mind with a broken action system, or two minds disagreeing? My tentative principle is something like "one mind = one shared perspective on the world," but I genuinely dont know whether that saves the driving-home verdict along with the lab one, since in the lab the hemispheres aren't sharing a perspective either — they're just not being asked anything. Is there a principle that gets all or most of these? Please say what it predicts for cases I haven't listed (like reconnecting the hemispheres gradually over months, or a future implant letting two people share a visual field), and be honest about which of my verdicts it forces me to give up.
