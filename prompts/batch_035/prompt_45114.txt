I'm a novelist -- literary fiction mostly, long books that reviewers politely call "ideas-driven". I'm about two hundred pages into a new one about a translator in present-day Fez who becomes obsessed with a medieval manuscript she's working on, and whilst researching her I fell down a rabbit hole reading about al-Ghazālī, so now of course the novel has to be partly about that too. The thing that keeps snagging me is his occasionalism: the claim that fire doesn't actually burn the cotton, that God creates the burning fresh in each moment, and that what we treat as causal necessity is really just Him going about His usual habit. As a craft matter I know something he apparently didn't: I have actualy built worlds, and I've started to suspect the whole doctrine is a theory of authorship with the serial numbers filed off.

So here's what I want from you. Take the role of a philosopher of religion who has done time as a novelist and ghostwriter, and think carefully -- maybe do some brainstorming on paper before you commit to anything. Restate al-Ghazālī's position on causation in my working vocabulary, and then tell me what doesn't survive the translation. By "my working vocabulary" I mean things like: the worldbuilding rules that hold a setting together, what counts as canon, the difference between what an author decrees scene by scene and what follows "from the setting", Chekhov's gun (is the causal power in the gun or in the author's promise to the reader?), genre convention as customary order, a deus ex machina as a suspension of that order, and character agency as something readers insist on even though I know perfectly well I invent every move my people make. Map his claims onto those notions as faithfully as you can, one at a time, and don't smuggle theology in and call it translation -- I'll notice.

Then give me the residue report, because that's the part I actually care about: which of his claims translate cleanly, which come across bent, and which have no expression at all in craft terms -- and for each piece of residue, whether it shows my vocabulary is missing something or whether the original claim was less clear than it looked. I have a suspicion, for instance, that his problem of why the world stays regular is not my problem of keeping a fictional physics consistent, because in my case the consistency exists entirely for the reader's benefit, and I genuinely can't tell whether that has an analogue in his God. Be honest about whether the translation flatters him or flatters me. Structure it however you see fit, but end with a single line of the form "Translation verdict: ..." giving your one-sentence overall judgement.
