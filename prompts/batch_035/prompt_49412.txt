im writing the final for my upper-level medieval philosophy seminar and I want one essay question on Avicenna's flying man. The problem is that most students can recite the setup in their sleep: the person created fully-formed but floating in a void, limbs apart, eyelids closed, no sound, no sensory input at all, and yet — so Avicenna says — he would still affirm his own existence. So the question has to force them past that. The crux I want: does the thought experiment actually show that self-awareness is independent of sense experience (which is how Avicenna uses it, against anyone who identifies the soul with the body or with a faculty that needs objects), or does it only establish something weaker, like "a mind with no *current* sensory input can still attend to itself," which would be compatible with self-awareness having been acquired earlier through the senses? And there's a sneaky issue students should notice: the flying man is presumably a mature adult, so hasn't he already had a lifetime of experience — and if so, is the experiment even tracking what a newborn's mind could do, or just what a mind that already has the concept "I" can do when its senses go quiet?

So please give me: the essay question itself (suitable for ~90 minutes), a model answer of around 500 words, and a marking scheme as a numbered list of exactly five must-hit points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion — with one of the five being about whether the flying man's grasp of "I exist" begs the question.
