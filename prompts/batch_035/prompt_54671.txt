so I write long-form science and ideas pieces for a magazine, and I've been slowly pulling together a big feature on luck and merit. covering behavioral genetics over the years has basically convinced me that talent — and even the capacity for discipline, the whole protestant-work-ethic temperament — is way more lottery than anyone wants to admit. anyway, I had drinks with a philosopher friend last week who said my political intuitions are "a mess" (his words), and before I write 6000 words on this I want to check whether my actual views hang together or whether I've been smuggling in contradictions. here's roughly where I stand: (1) the central injustice is brute luck — it's wrong for how well your life goes to depend on the natural and social lottery, the talents, temperaments and families you didn't choose; (2) but informed gambles are fair — if you freely and knowingly take a risk and it goes badly, the loss is yours to bear, not the state's; (3) I'm flatly for universal, choice-blind healthcare — no penalties or deprioritizing for smokers, rock climbers, whoever — I find the whole idea of sorting the deserving sick from the undeserving sick grotesque and I dont want the piece arguing for it; (4) at the same time I have zero problem with brilliant people getting fame and big money — I literally write admiring profiles of scientists and dont loose sleep that some of them earn ten times what a nurse does, if anything I think it's good that excellence gets visibly rewarded; (5) though, like I said, I'm pretty sure the drive and self-discipline that produce that excellence are themselves substantially inherited or at least unchosen; (6) and I'm fine with full workers' comp for people injured in dangerous jobs they knowingly took, even the ones who took the risk for the hazard pay.

go through these for me and tell me which pairs are genuinely inconsistent, which only look like it, and how bad the real tensions actually are. rank them by severity rather than listing everything as equally fatal, and for each real one tell me what the cheapest fix is and what it would cost me to adopt it — I'd especially like to know if there's some single revision that would clean up most of the mess, or whether I'm actually stuck choosing between things I care about. I have about a month before I have to file the piece, so I can restructure the argument around whatever I have to give up — I'd just rather find out now than in the fact-checking stage.
