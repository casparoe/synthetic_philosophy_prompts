so i run a tiny blog about free will stuff (mostly for my own sanity, like 200 readers, half of them bots) and for the last month i've been completely obsessed with sphexishness. hofstadter's word, right, for the digger wasp that hauls its paralyzed prey to the burrow entrance, pops inside to check the nest, and if you so much as nudge the cricket a few inches while she's down there she comes back up, finds it in the "wrong" spot, drags it back to the threshold, and checks the nest again. do it forty times and she does it forty times. nothing in the wasp is broken — each little subroutine is perfectly executed, the sensing is fine, the motor control is fine — it's just that the overall architecture has no way of noticing that it's in a loop. i find this way more unsettling than any abstract argument about determinism, becuase the wasp isn't responding to a lack of freedom, it IS a lack of a certain kind of freedom, the freedom to represent your own situation and update on it.

anyway, the piece i want for the blog is a satirical advice column written by the wasp herself. like a proper agony-aunt column, "Ask Aunt Sphex" or whatever you want to call it, deadpan, about 700 words. the letters should all be about free will and related stuff — one from a hard determinist who's convinced he's "no better than the wasp," one from a parent worried their kid's compulsive re-checking (light left on, stove off, door locked) is mechanical rather than chosen, maybe one from a compatibilist complaining that the column is strawmanning their position, one from someone asking whether the whole thing means the wasp can't be blamed for anything. and here's the structural bit i want: the wasp's replies should all follow the exact same template, re-run from the top every time. she reads a letter, dispenses the identical boilerplate advice, hits some new detail in the letter's second paragraph that "moves the cricket," and then starts her entire answer over from the first word. the reader should be able to literally see the loop in the column's typography — the same opening lines repeated, the answer never quite getting to the new information. so the piece doesn't just describe sphexishness, it demonstrates it, and the pathos/humor is that the advice she keeps re-dispensing is, like, perfectly reasonable advice, maybe even good advice about flexibility and not being ruled by habit.

the jokes need to come from actually getting the philosophy right, not from wasp puns. things like: whether reasons-responsiveness (rather than "uncaused choices") is what people actually want out of free will, whether a creature can be blamed for what it does with the information it never registers, whether the person nudging the cricket is "taking away the wasp's freedom" or just revealing something about it, the difference between rigidity in behavior and rigidity in architecture (a wasp and a very stubborn professor can behave identically for a week and differ enormously in principle). the last letter could be from someone gently asking whether Aunt Sphex is maybe, y'know, an automaton — and her printed reply is just the previous answer, re-run from the top, one final time. don't let her become self-aware halfway through unless the never-becoming-self-aware IS the ending. also please keep it fair — i don't want a compatibilist reader to feel like the column cheats them, their letter deserves the same non-reading everyone else gets, that's kind of the point. no winking at the reader, no "little did she know." she never knows. that's the joke and also kind of the horror of it. ideally i want to reread it in six months and not be able to tell if i wrote a comedy piece or a philosophy post.
