You are a scholar of Victorian literature with a serious grounding in nineteenth-century philosophy of science, and I am preparing a seminar session on Darwin and the English novel. Read Hardy's Tess of the d'Urbervilles through Darwin's own views on morality and religion as he developed them in The Descent of Man and reflected on them in his Autobiography — morality as an evolved social instinct whose content drifts with the needs and habits of a community, and religion as a doctrine that, for Darwin personally, gradually lost its evidential force once nature looked as much designed to produce cruelty as beauty.

The crux is the novel's notorious final sentence, which I want held in view throughout: "'Justice' was done, and the President of the Immortals, (in Æschylean phrase) had ended his sport with Tess." On its face this is an indictment of a personal agent — someone who toyed with Tess and is now blamed for it. But on Darwin's picture there is no agent to blame: no plan, no tribunal, only causes. So which is the novel? Is Hardy's narrator a consistent Darwinian, showing that the conventional moralism which condemns Tess as "fallen" and lets her hang is an inherited social instinct that has outlived any function it once served? Or does that final line smuggle providence back in as a shadow-judge — so that the novel's famous sympathy for Tess quietly re-theologizes the very moral vocabulary Darwin naturalized? Take the subtitle "A Pure Woman" seriously, and treat Angel Clare's revulsion after Tess's confession, and the run of moral luck that ruins her (Prince's death, the confession letter slipped under the carpet), as the places where the novel stages this tension. I want you to be honest about scenes or turns that resist the Darwinian reading rather than forcing them into it, and to say what the novel dramatizes that Darwin's own prose does not.

Think carefully, and sketch the competing readings before committing. Then close with a one-paragraph verdict, followed by a final line in the fixed form \answer{...}, where the enclosed phrase is exactly one of: darwinian-naturalist, crypto-providentialist, torn-between-both.
