I lead the moderation team for an online community of roughly 40,000 members organized around reading, discussing, and publishing fiction. Our conduct rules prohibit "posting slurs," but the rule was drafted in five minutes and never revised, and it fails to distinguish between the several quite different ways a slur can appear in a message. As a result my moderators handle identical posts inconsistently and members have started to notice: some remove any post containing the string whatever the context, others remove only posts they take to be aimed at insulting someone, and the rest escalate to me, which bottlenecks the whole queue.

Please write a decision procedure my team can run on each reported post in under two minutes: a fixed order of questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the points at which the procedure terminates in a removal, the points at which it terminates in leaving the post up, and the points at which it hands the case to a person because the question is one of values rather than of fact. The procedure should be grounded in the distinctions philosophy of language has actually established here — above all the difference between using a word and mentioning it, i.e. talking about the word as a word, and the difference between what a sentence literally says and what a speaker succeeds in communicating by uttering it in a particular setting — but expressed in terms a moderator with no philosophical training can apply. It must at minimum handle the following recurring situations, which the current rule cannot:

1. A member quotes verbatim a passage from a mid-twentieth-century novel that contains a slur, in a thread discussing that novel.
2. A member files a report that quotes verbatim the slur a second member sent them in a private message, the quote being the evidence for the report.
3. A member who belongs to the group a term targets uses that term, in the group's own spelling of it, affectionately toward another member of the same group, in a public thread.
4. A member writes that a certain word is so ugly they can only bring themselves to type it in order to say they despise it — the word occurs, embedded in an explicit condemnation.

Then run the procedure on cases 1 through 4, showing the answer each step yields. Include at least one case in which the procedure returns an answer most of my moderators will dislike, and explain why the procedure should give that answer anyway. Close by stating plainly what the procedure cannot do: where the philosophy of language runs out and the call is irreducibly a judgment about this community's values, and how you recommend we mark those cases in our logs so that they are visibly human decisions rather than procedure outputs.

A few constraints to design around: moderators have no background in linguistics or philosophy; decisions are appealable to me, so each removal needs a one-line justification the procedure supplies; and two upheld removals within ninety days triggers an automatic account review, which makes over-removal costly, not free.

Begin with the procedure itself, with no preface and no restatement of this request; the worked cases and the statement of limitations come after it.
