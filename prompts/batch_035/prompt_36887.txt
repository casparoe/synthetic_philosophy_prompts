I teach a seminar on the Holocaust and have watched the Browning–Goldhagen exchange get recycled for decades without either side moving an inch. What strikes me is that they worked from substantially the same evidence — the postwar judicial interrogations of the men of Reserve Police Battalion 101 — and agree on most of the behavioral facts: the men carried out the shootings and deportations, their commanders explicitly offered individuals a way out, a minority stepped forward to refuse, and refusing cost those men little. Yet Browning reads the very same men as ordinary conscripts conforming to situational pressure, while Goldhagen reads them as carriers of a specifically German eliminationist antisemitism that made the killing unremarkable to them. My working hypothesis is that this is not a competition between two hypotheses about a single explanandum: Browning is explaining this battalion's behavior within a case, Goldhagen is explaining the genocide as a whole across cases, and each tacitly treats his own explanandum as the one that matters. I suspect there is also a dispute buried in the word ordinary, and a further tacit disagreement about what an explanation of atrocity owes us — a mechanism that would have operated on anyone, or a cultural background without which the mechanism never comes into play at all.

I want a diagnosis of the disagreement, not a verdict on who was right, and not a survey of the literature. Please present it as exactly five numbered claims, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction, no conclusion, and no headings. Between them, the five claims should say what kind of disagreement this really is — substantive, verbal, two different questions under one heading, or a clash of standards about what counts as explaining perpetrators — identify what distinction or finding, if anything, would count as genuine progress, and be honest if your conclusion is that the debate is doing something other than resolving a disagreement, such as keeping two research programs in play, and why that might be so.
