I'm writing a novel where the main character, mid-divorce and broke, sells one of her kidneys, and I want the book to actually take the ethics seriously rather than using it as a cheap pity prop. To set up how the plot treats her, I'm making a list of transactions that get treated as normal in some places and scandalous in others, and I want you to rank them by how morally tainted they'd feel to a thoughtful reader, from basically fine to genuinely repugnant. Here's the list: (1) selling a kidney to a stranger through a regulated market, (2) selling a vote in a national election, (3) a hardware store tripling the price of generators the night before a hurricane, (4) becoming a paid surrogate for a wealthy couple, (5) selling your body for medical experiments, and (6) paying someone to be your designated mourner / professional friend, which is a thing I invented for the book and plan to have her take up afterward as her actual job.

Now the part I actually need help with: I keep going back and forth between two ways of ranking these and I can't tell which one should structure the book. One axis is basically about harm and coercion — desperation, exploitation of the poor, whether the deal makes anyone worse off than refusing it. The other is more about what the sale *means* — the thought that some things lose their value or their point when they're bought and sold, like the professional mourner's tears are worthless precisely because she's paid for them. My instinct is the kidney sale scores moderately on the harm axis but low on the meaning axis (it's just an organ, its value is medical, and money doesn't corrupt that), while the professional mourner is the reverse — almost no harm done, but something feels off about it in a way I can't articulate for the novel's narration. Am I even right about that split, or am I missing something?

So what I'd like: give each of the six a score from 1 to 10 on each of the two axes, with one sentence of justification per score, then tell me which single item has the biggest *gap* between its two scores and what that gap says about which axis is doing more work in our intuitions generally. Also flag whether you think any item I've listed is somewhere the two axes come apart in a way I havent noticed — probaly the vote selling, since it seems bad on both but for reasons that fit neither cleanly, but you tell me. Last thing: I want my narrator to end the book convinced that the meaning axis is sentimental nonsense and only harm matters, but I want a fair-minded reader to disagree with her. Is that a defensible place for her to land, or would it just read as the author stacking the deck? Rank the six, and be honest about the deck.
