i teach intro ethics and we just finished the moral luck unit (nagel's four kinds, the two reckless drivers who differ only in whether a child happened to step out). i want to close the last session with a poem instead of more of me talking, and i want it to be a villanelle in the voice of the driver who got lucky — he made the exact same overtake as the other one, identical in every respect he controled, except his road stayed empty.

the two refrains should be: one that states the control principle (something like "you can only be blamed for what you choose," but in your own words), and one that's just the fact the speaker can't answer (something like "but only one of us drove home with it"). the form has to do the actual philosophical work: the refrains come back verbatim every time, but the tercets around them shift, so the control-principle line sounds less like a principle each time it returns and more like a plea — early on the speaker uses it to defend his unlucky double ("he only did what i did"), and by the end it's obvious he's using it to absolve himself, which is the same slide the strict control principle makes when it implies the successful killer is no worse than the attempted murderer whose victim happened to survive. somewhere in the middle i'd love a tercet where he half-notices this and starts talking faster — self-aware enough to be unreliable, not enough to stop.

don't resolve anything. the final tercet should just put the two refrains side by side so they lean against each other without either one winning, wich is honestly where nagel leaves us too. proper 19-line villanelle shape please, and it should still scan as a decent poem for someone in the class who zoned out during the reading.
