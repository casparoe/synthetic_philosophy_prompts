My labmate has this argument he thinks kills off the whole "tool AI vs agent AI" debate, and honestly its bugging me because it seems to prove way too much. Goes like this:

(1) A system is an agent only if it acts in order to achieve goals — it selects actions *because* they advance some goal, not merely in ways that happen to promote one.

(2) Whether a system selects actions because they advance a goal is settled by whether goal-talk earns its keep in prediction: if "it wants X and does whatever gets X" predicts the system better or cheaper than the mechanical story does, then it selects actions because they promote the goal.

(3) Goal-talk earns its keep for pretty much anything that came out of an optimizing process. A spellchecker "wants" to fix typos, a recommender "wants" engagement, a chess engine "wants" to win — and his favorite line: by the same test, a honeybee wants nectar at least as much as the spellchecker wants anything.

(4) So either spellcheckers and chess engines are agents, or bees aren't — and if bees aren't, the concept has lost all contact with the thing we were trying to point at.

(5) But if spellcheckers are agents, the distinction is useless anyway: we've been surrounded by millions of agents for decades, and none of the safety people's "keep it a tool, not an agent" talk ever ruled anything out.

(6) Therefore the tool/agent line should be scrapped; there are only degrees of goal-directedness, and "agency" is just whatever sits high on that dial.

He's smug about it. My problem is that the conclusion isnt actually crazy — lots of people talk in terms of a spectrum these days — but the route to it smells off. Every step looks fine on its own, and then somehow I'm agreeing my autocomplete is an agent in the same sense a dog is. There has to be a slide in there, probably somewhere around (2), since (1) uses "because" like it's a fact about the system and (2) makes it a fact about what's convenient for an observer — but I can't put my finger on exactly what got swapped. For what it's worth, I'd rather keep (1) than keep (2): if (2) is what agency turns on, then whether something's an agent depends on which descriptions happen to be cheap for whoever's watching, which makes agency observer-relative, and that can't be the right story for the difference between a calculator and a cat.
