I'm a second-year PhD student in metaphysics, working on laws of nature, and I've reached the stage of the project where I want to know how much of my disagreement with my supervisor is substantive rather than temperamental. She is a fairly orthodox Lewisian: the laws of nature are just the axioms or theorems that appear in the best system — the deductive systematisation of the Humean mosaic that best balances simplicity against strength. My own hunch is that something in the neighbourhood of primitivism, or at any rate a governing rather than merely descriptive conception of lawhood, is correct, and that the best-system analysis either smuggles nomic content in through the choice of what counts as best, or else yields laws too anaemic to do the explanatory work physics demands of them. After our supervision meeting this week I put my credence that Humeanism about laws is true at roughly 0.35; hers, I would guess, sits somewhere around 0.85.

Please give me your own number, but, more importantly, decompose it. The terms that seem to me to belong are these. First, the probability that the best-system analysis can be specified without covertly building in nomic or modal facts — the charge that simplicity and strength are only well-defined relative to a language already shaped by our nomic and inductive prejudices, so that the Humean borrows what she claims to derive. Second, the probability that Humean supervenience survives physics at all, given that quantum entanglement and arguably even classical field theories strain the picture of a mosaic of local matters of particular fact, each located at a point of spacetime. Third, the probability that Humean laws can genuinely explain their instances, given the familiar circularity worry: the trajectory of the projectile is explained by the law, yet the law earns its place in the best system partly because that trajectory renders the system simple — so it looks as though the law is explained by the very behaviour it is meant to explain. Fourth, the probability that the governing intuition — the sense that laws do something rather than merely describe something — tracks a real feature of the world rather than a cognitive illusion, in which case primitivism gains an in-principle advantage. And finally, whether the Humean has a good story about chance, where the fit of a system to actual frequencies has to do a great deal of quiet work. Add any further terms you think belong, but for each one give a number and a one-line justification, then tell me how you combined them and why you chose that combination rule rather than another.

Two last requests. Tell me which of your terms you are least confident in, and what argument, development in physics, or possible discovery would move it most. And tell me whether you think my 0.35 and her 0.85 most likely disagree about the second, empirical-sounding term — whether physics leaves the door open to Humeanism at all — or about the more a priori question of what we meant by a law of nature in the first place. I want to know where the fault line in my field actually runs, and I would rather hear it from a fresh decomposition than from either of us retreating to our corners.
