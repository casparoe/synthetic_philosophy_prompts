Please explain the conceptual question of whether generational labels — "Millennials," "Gen Z," and so on — pick out real kinds, or are instead mere bookkeeping devices for conveniently binned birth years. I already understand the general distinction between natural kinds and nominal kinds, so focus on what is specific to the cohort case: what a "generational realist" would have to establish, such as whether shared formative experiences could constitute a homeostatic property cluster in anything like Richard Boyd's sense, and what the strongest skeptical response would be — for instance, that cohort boundaries are artifacts of marketers and journalists rather than joints in the social world. I'm especially interested in a third option: that generations are best understood not as kinds at all but as historical individuals — bounded, spatiotemporally continuous entities with vague edges, analogous to species on the species-as-individuals thesis associated with Ghiselin and Hull — so that asking whether someone "is a Millennial" is less like classifying them under a kind and more like asking about membership in a particular historical process. Please explain how this reframing would change the terms of the debate, and say which of the three positions you find best supported and why.
