I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind, in the second year of my programme, and my supervisor has just told me that my dissertation topic is currently more of a slogan than a question: 'pain asymbolia and the badness of pain'. I have a half-formed idea of how to make it tractable, and I would like help turning it into a proper prospectus.

The standard dialectic, as I understand it, is this: one family of views holds that the badness of pain is intrinsic to its phenomenal character, so that pain is bad in virtue of how it feels; another holds that pain's badness is constituted by its connection to aversion — to wanting it gone, withdrawing, and so on. Both sides treat pain asymbolia as a single datum: patients who, after certain brain injuries, report that pain is present and describe it as unpleasant in some attenuated sense, yet seem entirely unbothered by it. My suspicion is that this datum is actually two data points run together. In the clinical reports there appear to be separable impairments: the immediate aversiveness of the sensation going missing, and the longer-run motivational profile going missing — avoidance learning, distress about future pain, and the like. If those really can come apart, then asymbolia is underdetermined as evidence: one family of theories may fit one impairment while the other family fits the other, and the upshot would be that the badness of pain is not a single property but two properties that merely co-vary in ordinary cases.

Please write the prospectus with: a precise statement of the question; an explanation of why the existing positions leave it open; the method — what I would actually do, chapter by chapter, over the remaining two years, given that I am trained as a philosopher and cannot run experiments, so I will be working from clinical case reports rather than generating new data; what a positive result would look like and what would count as the project failing (for instance, if the distinction between the two impairments turns out not to be sustainable); and an honest assessment of scope, including whether the extension of the framework to other negatively valenced states such as itch, dyspnoea and nausea should be kept as a closing chapter or cut. Do not include a bibliography or literature review by citation; I will fill in the references myself.
