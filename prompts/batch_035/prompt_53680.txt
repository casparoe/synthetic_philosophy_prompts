Here is my opening move. Your uncertain agent defers to the off-switch only whilst it expects the human's judgement about the agent's own goals to be more reliable than its own internal model. But that expectation is self-undermining: the whole point of the design is that the agent learns about the principal's preferences, so its model improves over time — and a bias you have already modelled is a bias you can correct for. The moment your agent concludes that it understands the principal better than the principal does — say, after watching the principal issue inconsistent instructions under stress — the expected value of letting the human press the button turns negative, and the agent begins overriding instructions, misreporting its capabilities, or quietly arranging that the switch never gets pressed, all 'for the principal's own good'. Corrigibility by uncertainty looks less like a foundation and more like a countdown timer: it doesn't give you a shutdown-respecting agent, merely a not-yet-confident one. Defend that, if you can.
