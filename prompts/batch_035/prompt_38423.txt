I teach ancient philosophy, so the ethics of everyday family life is somehow outside my comfort zone. My 15-year-old has hit me with an arguement I can't quite put my finger on why it fails. The deal at home is that everyone does their assigned chores, and she does hers, no complaints. Every so often she'll do extra -- unloads the dishwasher when it's her brother's turn, cooks dinner unprompted -- and we thank her and tell her she's wonderful. When she doesn't do extra, nothing happens; nobody says a word. She claims this is incoherent: either the extra stuff is something she ought to do, in which case we should treat skipping it as a violation and get on her case like we would with the assigned chores, or it's genuinely optional, in which case doing it is just a hobby like any other and shouldn't count toward our thinking of her as a good kid. She adds, rather gleefully, that she mostly does the extra when she wants something from us later, and says that proves it's really just bargaining dressed up as kindness. My gut says she's wrong on both counts, but I can't articulate why in terms she'd actually accept. Any advice for how to respond?
