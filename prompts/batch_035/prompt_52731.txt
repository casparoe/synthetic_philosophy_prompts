Quick background: I'm doing a PhD in law, and you'd think all the contract stuff would've given me tools for this, but it turns out contract law just politely dodges the actual question. So here goes. When I was 17, my grandmother made me promise I'd never sell the small cottage by the lake that's been in our family for three generations. I said yes, obviously, it clearly meant the world to her. Now I'm a broke PhD student, and the cottage is worth enough to basically erase my student debt and give me a real shot at staying in academia instead of bailing for a firm job. My mom (her daughter) genuinely doesn't care what I do with it, my brother feels the same, and there are no other claimants. So as far as I can tell, no living person is affected one way or the other if I sell. And yet I can't shake the feeling that breaking the promise would be actually wrong — not just "I'd feel bad about it" wrong, but wrong wrong.

I brought this up with a friend in my program and she said what I'm feeling is just sentimentality dressed up as ethics. Her view is that the promise can't bind me anymore because the person it was made to doesn't exist anymore, and obligations are things you owe to people who exist. That sounded clean for about five minutes, until I tried to figure out why I'd feel totally released if grandma were alive and said "honey, sell it, I don't mind at all." So maybe it's the releasing that matters — her releasing me. Except she can't release me now, which is sort of the whole problem, and my friend has no answer for why I can't just decide I'm released.

This is keeping me up more than is probably healthy. Is there a principled account of where the "force" of a promise even comes from, such that it can survive the promisee dying, or my friend right that it's all just an emotional artifact? And if it does still bind somehow, is it the kind of thing that can be outweighed by stuff like my debt and career, or is it more like a hard constraint? Be brutal if you think I'm just being sentimental — I can take it, I just want to know whether to call the realtor.
