So I'm a novelist about two thirds of the way through a book set in a fictional mid-sized country that has just replaced one house of its parliament with a citizen assembly chosen by lottery -- two year terms, one term only, no re-selection. Or at least that's the law on page one; the plot is mostly about people trying to change it. The problem I've run into is that all my political characters secretly agree with each other (and with me), which makes for flat scenes. What I want is a decision tree that takes a character from a starting conviction to the position they'd actually end up holding on sortition, term limits, and career politicians, so I can build the cast by picking different entry points and letting the tree generate the disagreements for me.

The tree has to work from cases a character could argue about in a scene without knowing any of the position labels. Things like: is the problem with elections that voters pick badly, or that getting picked and staying picked changes what you want? If a randomly selected citizen is measurably better at judging policy in month eighteen than on day one, is that a reason to let them serve again, or is it precisely the thing the one-term rule exists to prevent? Is the right analogy for a legislator a doctor (a skill built over a career of practice) or a juror (ordinary judgment is the entire point, and a professional juror class would be a nightmare)? The council's staff stay forever while members rotate -- does the power quietly migrating to permanent staff bother you, and if so would term limits on elected politicians make that same problem worse everywhere? Should a citizen assembly member be allowed to run for the *other*, still-elected house the moment their term ends? I want each branch node phrased as something two characters could disagree over at a dinner without either of them ever saying the word "sortition." Aim for around six branch nodes and six to eight leaves, covering at least: full sortition with strict single terms; sortition with re-selection by lot; sortition as one chamber alongside an elected one; elections with hard term limits; elections defended as letting expertise accumulate; and any position where career politicians are treated as a feature rather than a bug. For each leaf, give me one line of dialogue a character holding it could plausibly say, so I can hear the voice. Also please flag any pair of leaves the tree can't genuinely separate, and note which branches of the logical space seem unoccupied -- and say for each whether thats because the view is incoherent or just because nobody's bothered to defend it.

Don't bother with authority name-drops -- I already know the Aristotle point about elections being aristocratic and the lot being democratic, and my political-science character will get to make it in one scene and be told its a cliche. Start your reply with the tree itself, no preamble, and put the notes on unoccupied branches and inseparable leaves after it.
