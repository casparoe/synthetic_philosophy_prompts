My sister's art school has this rule: anything made with grant-funded materials belongs to the school until she pays them back for the materials. She used grant money to buy a big lump of clay and sculpted it into a statue, and now a gallery wants to buy the statue. Her position: fine, the school owns the lump of clay, but the statue is a different thing — she made it, so it's hers to sell. The school's position: no, the statue just IS the lump of clay we paid for, so it belongs to us, and if she sells it we get the money. I honestly cant tell who's right. If the statue and the clay are one and the same object, the school seems to win outright, but that feels unfair — all the sculpting work was hers, and it would be strange for her to have created something she had no right to sell. But if they're two objects, then they're two objects sitting in the exact same place at the exact same time made of the exact same stuff, which sounds like a weird thing to believe. There's also a wrinkle I keep turning over: she could smash the statue back into a lump, hand that to the school, and say she's returning their property intact. Does that even make sense — did the clay survive the sculpting? Basically, is there any principled way to sort out who owns what here without first settling whether there's one thing in the studio or two? And would the people who've argued about this kind of case actually disagree about the verdict here, or is the ownership dispute somehow separable from the metaphysics?
