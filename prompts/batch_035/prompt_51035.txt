i've been trying to write up my actual views on the special composition question for a reading group and want a consistency check before I embarrass myself. here's the package: (1) I'm basically on board with van Inwagen about artifacts — there's no table, no chair, just simples arranged tablewise — because I can't find any non-arbitrary restriction on when artifacts compose, and arbitrary ones feel like cheating. (2) Unlike the full nihilists though, I think organisms compose: my cat is genuinely one object, a real thing over and above catwise-arranged simples, and so am I. (3) What marks the difference is life — shared metabolic fate, integrated biological activity — and I'd insist that mere functional arrangement (what a table has) is nowhere near enough. (4) I'm also pretty convinced by the vagueness argument against vague composition: there has to be a sharp fact about whether some things compose something, no fuzzy cutoffs, since vague existence would be incoherent. (5) And I hold to extensionality of parthood: no two distinct objects with exactly the same parts. Go through these and tell me which commitments actually pull against each other, which pairs only look like they conflict, and rank the tensions by severity — then commit: if I have to drop or amend one of the five to save the rest, tell me which and why, don't just lay out my options. for what it's worth (3) is the one that feels most solid to me, but check that too.
