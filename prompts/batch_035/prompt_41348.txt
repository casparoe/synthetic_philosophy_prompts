My friend D and I argued about Frankfurt cases for like four hours last night over coffee and I think she beat me, but I can't pin down where. Let me lay it out and you tell me where it goes wrong.

We started from the classic case, which we both accepted. Black wants Jones to shoot Smith. Black has a device in Jones's brain that will force Jones to shoot if it ever detects that Jones is about to decide not to. In the actual run of events Jones decides entirely on his own to shoot — he wants Smith dead for reasons of his own — and the device never activates. Since Black's device did literally nothing, Jones seems responsible for the killing, even though he couldn't have done otherwise: any move toward refraining would've been overridden. So we both conceeded that the principle of alternative possibilities is false, or at least that you can be responsible with no alternatives open to you.

Then D went for the kill, and this is where I got stuck:

1. What makes Jones responsible can't be anything Black did, since Black did nothing. So it has to be features of the actual story: the shooting flowed from Jones's own deliberation, his own reasons, and the guarantee stayed idle in the background.
2. But now take the woman who hands over her wallet at gunpoint. The gunman never fires either. The handing-over flows from her own deliberation and her own reasons — she wants to stay alive. There is a guaranteeing condition in the background, exactly like the device.
3. And the addict who uses again after swearing he won't: the guarantee isn't even external, it's his own craving, and the using flows from his own motives, his own weighing of things, in whatever sense.
4. So the only differences between Jones, the wallet woman, and the addict are where the guarantee sits (outside them vs. inside them) and whether they can see it.
5. Whether a guarantee is internal or external, visible or hidden, can't be what seperates the responsible from the excused — moral status can't turn on that kind of luck.
6. So if Jones is responsible because his action flowed from his own reasons while the guarantee stayed idle, then the wallet woman and the addict are responsible too, since in every one of these cases the action flowed from the person's own motives.
7. That proves way too much — we excuse the coerced, and at least often the addicted. So something upstream has to go, and D says what has to go is our original concession: Jones is NOT responsible, and the case shows nothing, because once you ask what responsibility could consist in, having your action guaranteed in advance is disqualifying wherever the guarantee happens to sit.

Here's my problem. I want to keep the original concession — I really do think Jones is responsible, and giving that up feels like giving up on the whole point of Frankfurt cases. But I also can't accept (6). My gut says the trouble is somewhere between (1) and (2), that the gun and the device aren't the same kind of thing at all, but every time I try to say why it comes out as "the gun gives her a reason," and D just points out that the addict's craving gives him a reason too, and it's his reason in the most literal sense. She also says that if I reject (4) or (5) I'm commited to hidden guarantees being morally different from visible ones, which sounds insane to her.

Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it actually cost me?
