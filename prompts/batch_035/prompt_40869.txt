I got into a argument at dinner last week about whether the "Middle Ages" is a real thing or a later invention, and I want to be better armed before it comes up again. My position was basically that it's a legacy naming convention: Renaissance humanists slapped a label on a thousand years of history they wanted to feel superior to, and since nobody living in 1200 knew they were "medieval," the period is fake, full stop. My friend (also a software engineer, if that matters) pushed back that period boundaries are like version tags in a repo: the exact cut is somewhat conventional, but it's tracking a real change in the codebase, and you'd say the tag is *wrong* if you put it where nothing meaningful changed. I honestly couldn't tell her which parts of my view survive that analogy, and I suspect I was partly arguing about labels and partly about something else without noticing.

So before I wade back in, I want the spec, not the scorecard. Write me the requirements list: what does a *good* periodization of history have to deliver to count as carving at a real joint rather than being a convenient filing convention?
