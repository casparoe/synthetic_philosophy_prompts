I've held, roughly since graduate school, a fairly classical version of the language of thought hypothesis: thinking is computation over representations that have a combinatorial syntax and semantics, and the systematic, productive, and compositional character of our thought is explained — perhaps only explained — by the medium being language-like in that sense. I've never had to defend this in print, so I've never been forced to ask the question I now want answered: what would actually count against the view? I'm not looking for a rehash of the connectionist wars; I want the current map of its exposure.

Two developments have recently unsettled me, and I'd like them taken seriously as candidate defeaters rather than waved off as engineering. First, the systems I use daily now compose meaning across text, images, and audio in a single continuous vector space, and they seem to get the right behavior for novel combinations — describe a sofa shaped like an avocado and they produce one — without anything that looks like discrete symbols or recursive structure over constituents. If systematic-looking behavior can be achieved without classical compositional structure, does that undercut the claim that systematicity demands such structure, or is it simply irrelevant to the psychological thesis? Second, work on how people assemble ad hoc, temporary concepts on the fly — the things one would grab in a fire, or the loose categories we use to plan a move — sits awkwardly with the picture of a fixed stock of primitive lexical items in a stable medium, and I don't know whether flexible concept construction is a problem for the view or just a phenomenon happening at a different level.

So: give me the defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Which empirical results would genuinely damage the view — for instance, evidence that human concept-combination is not compositional in the required sense, or that some capacity of ours is productive and systematic while failing to display constituent structure? Which theoretical developments, such as prototype or theory-theoretic accounts of concepts that deny a stable compositional core? Which intuitions about cases? For each, tell me what a committed but intellectually honest defender of the view would concede as counting against them, and what they would dismiss as not engaging the thesis. And assess the insulation problem frankly: successive versions of the view have retreated from a rich innate Mentalese to something like "any medium with compositional structure," and from innate primitives to learned ones. As the view has been qualified, what does it still exclude — and is its near-unfalsifiability a defect in a theory of cognition of this kind, or something we should expect of a claim pitched at this level of abstraction?
