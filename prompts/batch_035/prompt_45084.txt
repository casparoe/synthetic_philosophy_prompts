Longtime LessWrong reader, been hanging around the interpretability discourse for years, and there's this one move I keep seeing that I've never managed to pin down. Someone points at a sparse autoencoder feature or an activation-space direction that seems to track "deception" or "sycophancy" or whatever, and someone else replies with some version of "it's just a next-token predictor, it doesn't have concepts" as if that settles the whole question of what's going on inside. And the same one-liner gets used the other way too -- to say safety worries about deception are confused because "it's just predicting text." I have a feeling the sentence is doing like five different jobs depending on who says it, and nobody involved notices.

So here's the ask: take "it's just a next-token predictor" and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be expressing, from the trivial to the radical. Off the top of my head it might mean (a) a claim about the training objective, which is just obviously true and nobody disputes; (b) a claim about what happens at inference time, e.g. no lookahead, output is a conditional distribution and nothing else; (c) a claim about the internals, that whatever structures exist inside the network don't rise to the level of "representations" or "features" in any robust sense -- basically deflationism about interpretability claims; (d) a claim about semantics, that ungrounded token manipulation can't amount to having concepts at all; (e) a claim that having representations would require something extra like agency or intent, which a conditional distribution lacks. But maybe there are readings I'm missing, and I don't trust my sorting of which ones are actually plausible.

For each reading, tell me: is it trivially true, substantive but defensible, or just false? And -- this is the part I actually care about -- say which readings would support the *conclusion the slogan is usually deployed for*, which in my corner of the internet is something like "so the interpretability findings don't mean what you think they mean" or "so alignment concerns about mesa-behavior are confused." My own suspicion is that the readings that are true don't get you those conclusions and the readings that would get you there are contestable at best, but I'd like this laid out properly rather than my vibes. Bonus round if you want it: there's a rival slogan people use, something like "to predict text that well you have to model the world it came from," and I'd love to know whether that one, precisified the same way, actually collides with any of the defensible readings of the first one or whether they can both be true at once.

No need for citations or paper references, I know where the literature is if I want it. Just the disambiguation, with verdicts. A list I can paste into a forum comment. And be honest about which reading *you* think people mostly intend when they type it, because I genuinely can't tell anymore.
