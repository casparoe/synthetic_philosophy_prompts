I've been trying to work out my actual position on what I may eat, treating my gut verdicts on cases as the data, and I'd like help extracting the principle behind them — or being told there isn't one.

My verdicts, such as they are: Substituting beef for chicken is a moral improvement, because one cow supplies the meat of a great many chickens, and each of those chickens' lives on a factory farm is bad. Pastured beef is better than feedlot beef, because the animal lives better. Yet a friend argues that growing the crops I do eat is worse than grazing cattle on permanent pasture, since tillage and harvest kill enormous numbers of mice, snakes, and other field animals; I'm inclined to dismiss this argument, but my only reason for dismissing it is that the field animals' deaths are collateral rather than aimed at, and I can't see how that distinguishes them from the chickens in the first verdict, whose deaths are also not intended by anyone in particular and occur only because of my demand. I think eating venison from a managed deer cull is acceptable, perhaps good. I think eating roadkill is entirely fine. And I'm fairly sure that converting wild grassland to pasture is bad for the wild animals that lived there, even though I don't believe I'm obligated to restore habitat that my choices aren't touching.

My tentative principle is something like: the deaths of animals brought into existence because of my food choices count fully, while deaths that are mere side effects of food production count for less. But I don't know how to make "count for less" precise, and I don't know how the principle survives the case where the side-effect harms of pasture conversion seem very serious indeed.

Please state the principle, or a small set of candidates, as precisely as you can; say which verdict motivates which clause; tell me its verdicts on cases I haven't listed — lab-grown meat, eggs from a backyard flock, insect flour, and whether wild-caught fish from a fishery with heavy bycatch differ morally from farmed fish; and tell me honestly which of my verdicts the best principle cannot accommodate, and which of them I should give up.
