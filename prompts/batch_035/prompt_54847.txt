My friend Dana has had a chronic, fluctuating illness for two years — crushing fatigue, joint pain, brain fog — and most of it still has no tidy explanation. She keeps a detailed symptom diary, but a specialist she saw recently told her, kindly, that her reports "read like anxiety" and suggested she consider that possibility before pursuing further testing. Dana recounted this at dinner, and two of our friends, Priya and Marcus, went at it for an hour. I couldn't follow all of it and definitely couldn't referee, so I'd like your help.

Their exchange went roughly like this. (1) Marcus said the specialist is simply being rational: in his clinic, most patients presenting diffuse, hard-to-explain symptoms do turn out to have psychosomatic causes, so down-weighting Dana's self-report is just sensible use of base rates, and a doctor who ignored such priors would be worse at his job. (2) Priya replied that the base rates aren't innocent data — medicine's statistics about which women's symptoms count as "unexplained" have been shaped by generations of dismissive attitudes, in the same way that multiple sclerosis was once routinely written off as hysteria, so appealing to the numbers launders the prejudice through a formula. (3) Marcus said that if every dataset tainted by history is off-limits, medicine collapses into paralysis — all evidence has a history — and besides, the doctor has met Dana-type patients who genuinely over-report when anxious; he's not making it up. (4) Priya answered that this is precisely the problem: Dana is being assigned the reputation of a group rather than assessed as this particular reporter, and the deeper harm is that her credibility is deflated in advance, so the very evidence that might update the doctor's priors is never allowed to count as evidence. (5) Marcus said that by this standard "injustice" covers any heuristic that misfires — there's no intent, no wrongdoing, just an imperfect shortcut; honest mistakes are misfortunes, not injustices. (6) Priya closed with a different layer: because Dana's condition is barely studied, she doesn't even have the vocabulary to describe her symptoms in a form medicine registers — she can't make a claim that could then be unfairly downgraded — and while no individual doctor is at fault for that, it is still a wrong done to her as a knower. Marcus grumbled that a research failure in an entire field isn't an injustice against any particular patient, and there it ended.

Please adjudicate this in two parts. Second, after the table, a JSON object with exactly these fields: "overall" (which of the two comes out ahead, with a sentence of justification), "central_issue" (the underlying philosophical problem, named using standard terminology), "marcus_best_point", "priya_best_point", "both_missing" (something neither raised), and "for_dana" (one or two sentences of practical advice grounded in your analysis, not generic reassurance).
