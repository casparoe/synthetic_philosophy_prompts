I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, mostly neuroscience and free-will-adjacent stuff, and after every piece I write I get the same letters: if the brain caused it, how can we blame anyone? Rather than dodge that next time, I've been noticing I actually have pretty firm gut verdicts on individual cases, and I want to know if they add up to anything. The guy with the tumor pressing on his amygdala who kills his family and leaves a note asking doctors to examine his brain: no blame at all, just sadness. The drunk driver who kills a kid on the way home from the bar: full blame, no discount for the impairment. A mother whose untreated depression made her neglect her kids: sympathy, but I still blame her, some. A guy who joined a cult at nineteen after a childhood of abuse and did ugly things for it: honestly I keep flip-flopping. The surgeon on hour thirty of a shift who makes a fatal error: blame, but muted. The homeowner who never once thought about his smoke detector until the fire hurt the neighbor's kid: full blame for something he never once considered doing or not doing. And a friend with genuine, diagnosed kleptomania who steals my umbrella: zero blame, even though the act looks identical to an ordinary thief's. Is there one principle that captures all or most of these, and if there isn't, which of my verdicts should I give up? Then tell me what the principle says about the one case I cant settle -- the cult guy -- since I'd like to at least know what I'm deciding between before the next batch of angry letters arrives.
