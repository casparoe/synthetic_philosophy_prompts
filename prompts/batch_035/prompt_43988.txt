Something happened in my family this hurricane season and I want an outside view on whether my brother-in-law actually did something wrong, because half the family won't speak to him now and I can't tell who's got the real argument.

He owns a hardware store in a small coastal town. For years, whenever a big storm was coming, he kept his prices exactly the same, and what always happened is the generators sold out in about an hour, mostly to whoever got through the door first — more than once he watched guys buy two or three and then flip them in the parking lot for double. Last month, with a serious one bearing down, he raised the generator prices maybe 60% over normal. Everything else stayed the same, batteries and plywood at the usual price, just the generators.

Well. He sold out again but over two days instead of one hour, he says more generators ended up with people who actually used them instead of parking lot resellers, and because the margin made it worth it he paid for air freight and got a second batch in before landfall, which never would've happened before. My wife says he exploited people at the single most terrified moment of their lives, that a mom choosing between an overpriced generator and the money she needs to keep her kid's insulin cold isn't making a free choice, and that's the whole ballgame. Her dad says the town has now learned that the exact moment they're desperate is the moment this guy reaches deeper into their pocket, and that message sticks longer than any one storm — people will remember it, and it changes how they treat him and the store forever after. My brother-in-law just keeps repeating that the old system rationed by luck and line-standing, that nobody was forced to buy anything, and that every buyer was better off with the generator than without it, so where exactly is the harm.

Honestly they all seem to be talking past eachother and I can't sort out which of these arguements actually carries weight, or whether they're even disagreeing about the same thing. Is there a principled way to work through this? Like, if what he did was fine, would it still be fine at 200% over normal? At 1000%? If it was wrong, was it wrong because of the mom's circumstances, or because of the standing he lost in the town, or something else again? Help me figure out which of the three of them is actually disagreeing with which, and on what grounds. Also, please write your answer in American English.
