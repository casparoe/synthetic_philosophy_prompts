A popular science piece I read recently covered the "extended mind" thesis of Andy Clark and David Chalmers, and I came away both intrigued and uneasy about whether I'd been given the real position. Here is how the author put it (from my notes):

"Clark and Chalmers argued that the mind does not stop at the skin: whatever a person relies on to think becomes part of their mind. Their famous example involves Otto, a man with Alzheimer's who writes down what he wants to remember in a notebook. Because Otto consults his notebook as naturally and as automatically as his counterpart Inga consults her biological memory, the notebook is literally a part of Otto's mind — his belief that the Museum of Modern Art is on 53rd Street sits on the page rather than in his skull. This is essentially an update of the old semantic externalism of Putnam and Burge, who showed that the meanings of our words depend on the environment; Clark and Chalmers extend the same insight from meanings to beliefs. The practical upshot is that your mind already includes your smartphone and everything you keep in the cloud, since you depend on them constantly the way Otto depends on his notebook. Critics replied that this confuses causal dependence with constitution — by this logic, my mind extends into my calculator, the telephone network, and eventually the entire economy — and this objection has generally been taken to have sunk the thesis, which survives today mostly as a slogan about how intimately we are coupled to our devices."
