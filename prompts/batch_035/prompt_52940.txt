Every year my grad school friends and I do a "state of your views" exchange — each of us writes up where we currently stand on some topic and everybody else tears into it. This year it's my turn, and I picked convention, mostly following Lewis. Here's the set of things I actually believe, and I'd like an audit of whether they fit together before I send it out:

1. A convention is a way of solving a coordination problem: a regularity in behavior that (nearly) everyone conforms to, expects the others to conform to, and wants to conform to given the others do. Pretty much straight Lewis.

2. The common knowledge part is load-bearing, and I mean it strictly: everyone knows that everyone knows that everyone knows... all the way down. Not just "strongly correlated expectations" or a convenient shorthand. If the infinite nesting isn't there in some form, you don't have a convention — you have a habit, a regularity, something else.

3. Conventions can get set up and kept going by completely dumb mechanisms: imitation, reinforcement, plain natural selection. Nobody has to grasp the structure for it to be a convention. There were conventions long before there were creatures who could reason about anybody's expectations, and I'd even count some animal stuff.

4. Conventions are arbitrary: at least one alternative regularity would have coordinated everybody just as well. That's what's supposed to separate a convention from a law of nature or a collectively enforced rule. (Though I also think some conventions are just better than others — I wish we'd standardized on metric ages ago, and don't get me started on QWERTY. Not sure how that fits with the arbitrariness thing, if it matters at all.)

5. Punishment doesn't disqualify anything. If the punishing itself is conventional — people sanction violators because they expect others to sanction violators too — then norms held up by sanction (gossip, ostracism, the stink eye) are still conventions, just higher-order ones.

6. My case verdicts, which I want checked against the above: tipping, in a country where it's customary, is a convention — even though every diner would rather keep the money, if the server expects it and everybody else tips, you'd rather tip too, so I file it under convention inspite of the conflict of interest. The words of English are arbitrary conventions. And the rule against theft: I keep going back and forth, but my current line is that it's NOT a convention — people comply because of the sanction, not because they'd prefer to comply given that others comply, so it's more like a collectively imposed rule.

So: do these hang together? Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and for the real ones, how bad each is and what the cheapest fix costs. If something has to go, say which and why — don't just tell me there's some friction somewhere.
