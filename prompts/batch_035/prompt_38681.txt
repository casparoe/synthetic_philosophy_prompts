I'm on the rebuilding committee of my housing cooperative after a fire damaged two members' units: one fire came from old wiring the co-op had voted twice not to replace, the other from a member's deep-fryer, which he'd been formally warned about. Our debates have split into two camps that map uncomfortably well onto the literature I've been reading since: one camp talks like luck egalitarians (compensation should track what people could control), the other like relational egalitarians (full rebuilding either way, because the point is that we stand together as equals). Since I've been asked to write the report that both factions on the committee might actually sign, I want to see how the philosophers would do it. You are a professional political philosopher; think carefully about how each side would word things before drafting. Draft the joint statement that a committed luck egalitarian (in the Dworkin/Cohen/Roemer lineage) and a committed relational egalitarian (in the Anderson/Scheffler lineage) could both sign about how a cooperative like ours should handle misfortune that tracks choice. Include: a numbered section of claims both can sign, including about what each takes the point of equality to be; the residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether responsibility-sensitivity is a principle of distributive justice or at most a constraint on it, and another is whether withholding full compensation from the imprudent expresses contempt or merely respect; and for each crux a case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction about that case, applied to our two fires where possible. If any part of the dispute strikes you as merely verbal, say which and why. Keep the whole statement under 800 words, formatted as agreed claims, then a disagreements table, then the cruxes.
