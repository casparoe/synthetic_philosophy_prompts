I am a journalist who writes about science and ideas, and I am at work on a feature about why so many philosophers have taken music, of all the arts, to be the deepest clue to what reality is like. In my background reading for the piece I found a general-audience essay that explains Schopenhauer's aesthetics, and since a fair amount of my article will lean on his views, I want to be sure I am not building on sand. The essay's summary runs more or less as follows: Schopenhauer held that the world we experience is the manifestation of a blind, striving force he called the Will, and he ranked the fine arts according to how high they ascend from particular things toward the universal. Music stands at the top of this hierarchy because, whereas painting and poetry merely imitate the Ideas that intermediate between the Will and the world of appearances, music skips the Ideas entirely and is a direct copy of the Will itself. This, the essay continues, is why a melody can single out a particular feeling — grief, longing, triumph — and express it more precisely than any words could; music is essentially a language of the emotions, one whose vocabulary we all understand without training. The essay closes by attributing to Schopenhauer a practical upshot: since aesthetic experience temporarily stills the desires of the Will, listening to music is the highest available form of that stilling, on a par with the ascetic's renunciation of willing, and so Schopenhauer preferred program music and opera, where the emotional meaning is made explicit by a text, to purely instrumental music, which he found too abstract to communicate anything definite. I have my suspicions about a few of these points — for instance, I dimly recall that Schopenhauer had little patience for opera — but the essay was written by someone who plainly knows more philosophy than I do, and I do not want to substitute my own confusion for theirs. Please go through the summary claim by claim: tell me which parts a scholar of Schopenhauer would endorse as stated, which parts conflate things he kept distinct or turn a qualified thesis into an unqualified one, and whether his actual view of music is better or worse than the essay suggests as a hook for an article. I also want to know whether the essay's final claim — that on Schopenhauer's account a life organized around listening to music would be the best life available to most of us — follows from whichever parts of the summary survive scrutiny. One request about form: please write your reply as continuous prose only, the way one would write a long letter, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables of any kind.
