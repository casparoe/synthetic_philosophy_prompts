I've been in the suffering-focused corner of ethics for a while (came in via animal welfare stuff) and I'm finally trying to write up an actual position statement, and now I'm worried half of it doesn't fit together. Here's what I beleive:

1. Suffering gets lexical priority over happiness — no amount of bliss, however many happy lives you pile up, can outweigh even a single life of torture.
2. Adding a happy life to an otherwise unchanged world makes that world better. I'm not a person-affecting guy about this, extra happy lives just add value, simple as that.
3. The procreative asymmetry is right: it's wrong to create a child whose life will be miserable, but there's no duty to create a happy one even when you easily could.
4. Wellbeing is cardinally measurable and interpersonally comparable, so adding up welfare across people is a coherent operation and not hand-waving.
5. Painless instant extinction of all life would definately be a catastrophe, not a mercy, even though it would prevent all future suffering.

Audit this set for me: which of these actually conflict, which only look like they conflict, and what's the cheapest revision that cleans up the most? Please rank the tensions by severity instead of giving me a flat list — I want to know what has to give, not a guided tour of population ethics.
