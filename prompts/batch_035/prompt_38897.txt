I need someone to poke holes in a view I walked away from a 2am argument still holding. Here's the position: there's an objective fact of the matter about how close a false theory is to the truth, and the history of science is, roughly, a march toward greater truthlikeness -- Newton is literally closer to the truth than Kepler, GR is closer than Newton, even though all of them are strictly speaking false. That's my view, and I want to know what would actually count against it.

My friend attacked the formal side. She brought up the old Tichý/Miller counterexamples that killed Popper's original definition, and then the thing that's bugging me: Miller's language-dependence result, where which of two false theories counts as "closer to the truth" can flip depending on how you set up your vocabulary -- redescribe your quantities in some jumbled-but-still-invertible coordinate system and the ordering reverses. We literally drew it on a napkin: suppose reality says x=5 and y=5, theory A guesses (4,4) and theory B guesses (4,6); B looks closer on some measures, not others, and apparently with a weird enough reparameterization you can make the comparison come out however you like. Her line was: if "closeness to truth" is that hostage to notation, it was never a real relation, just an artifact of description.

Here's my problem: I get that this wrecks any particular formal definition, but my position was never "Popper's 1963 definition works." It's "there is SOME objective closeness relation and science lurches toward it." I figure the similarity-measure people (the possible-worlds, ordering-theorists, whatever) will eventually find a measure that isn't hostage to vocabulary. But I can hear how that sounds -- every dead definition gets followed by "okay but the intuitive idea is fine, someone will formalize it eventually." At some point that's just retreating.

So, seriously: what would count against MY version, not Popper's? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Things I specifically want covered: whether the language-dependence results generalize beyond Popper-style definitions to the whole similarity/distance approach (that's what would move me most), whether pessimistic-meta-induction stuff about past theories being discarded counts as evidence the "march toward truth" story is wrong or just unconfirmed, and whether a view like this even needs a working formalization to be respectable, or whether that demand is a category error.
