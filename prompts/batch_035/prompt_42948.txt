I am writing a novel set in Weimar Germany, narrated by a war widow who spends the 1920s reading everything published about the July Crisis — memoirs of chancellors and ambassadors, pamphlets from the war-guilt office in Berlin, the Allied collections of documents — because she wants to settle, before the novel ends, exactly whom she is entitled to hate. Her final chapter turns on one sentence, and I cannot decide whether she should write it. Roughly fifty years after her era, a German historian named Fischer reignited the whole question by arguing that Germany's leaders had not stumbled into the war but consciously risked it, in pursuit of continental hegemony, and had effectively handed Austria-Hungary a blank cheque knowing it would cash it against Serbia. So the claim I need to weigh is this: Germany's civilian and military leadership in July 1914 bore primary responsibility for the outbreak of the general European war, in the sense that they understood their actions made a general war likely and either welcomed that outcome or accepted it with indifference to its scale.

I don't want a historiographical survey — I want your credence, stated as a number, with the reasoning decomposed into the sub-questions that actually produce it. At minimum I would want terms for these: the probability that the German leadership specifically preferred a general war to a localized Austro-Serbian conflict; the probability that a general European war occurs anyway if Germany restrains Austria in late July; the probability that the counterfactual and the intentional senses of responsibility can be combined into a single coherent judgment at all; and whatever else you think belongs — for instance, whether the answer should differ when responsibility is attributed to a nation over decades rather than to a dozen men over three weeks.

Give me a rough probability and one line of justification for each term, then the combination. Then tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and what kind of evidence — documents from the archive, diaries of particular figures, disclosures from other capitals — would move it the most. My own credence is around 0.6, my editor's around 0.3; if you can say which of those two people most likely disagree about which sub-question, that would help me decide how my narrator's last sentence should fall.
