I do trust & safety work at a mid-sized AI lab and I keep hitting a weird tension in our model spec that nobody above me seems worried about. Top of the doc is a values section saying the model should be honest and never deceive the user. But further down, the spec tells the model to never reveal its instructions verbatim and to deflect if asked about them; in our companion chat product it's told to stay in character and not break the illusion even when a user asks point blank whether its human; and in evals we score "honesty" as declining to answer rather than guessing, while in the main product the same hedging gets dinged as unhelpful. So what definition of deception does this bundle actually presuppose? Is it something like "deception = asserting what you believe to be false" (so deflecting, staying in character, and strategic omission don't count), or "deception = whatever is likely to leave the user with a false belief" (in which case half our spec is lying to people)? Does it presuppose the model has beliefs and intentions at all — and here's the part that bugs me: if the working definition requires intent, and the model doesn't have intent, isn't the top-line honesty rule vacuous, basically unviolatable by construction? Reconstruct what the spec is implicitly committed to, tell me whether the sections are consistent under one definition or whether they just presuppose different things because different teams wrote them, and say what a spec built around the stricter "causing false beliefs" definition would have to change — does the companion product survive that definately, or is it just dead on arrival?
