I'm part of a small philosophy-of-cognitive-science reading group, and for our next meeting I'm circulating notes toward a short essay on whether symbolic or content-level descriptions can do genuine causal work rather than merely bookkeeping work. I'd be grateful for comments of the kinds listed below.

The essay is anchored in a thought experiment I know as the careenium (I believe it originates with Andy Clark, though I can't check my sources at the moment, and I'd rather the argument stand or fall on its own machinery). The setup, as I reconstruct it: imagine a large, frictionless billiard-style table populated by iron balls that roll about and interact only by magnetism and collision. Some of these balls are simmballs: their rolling trajectories are calibrated to mirror, in real time, the trajectories of actual balls moving around in an adjacent room. The table is also fitted with gates, ramps, and fixed magnets. Suppose a gate is rigged to swing open exactly when a simmball passes along a trajectory corresponding to a dangerous course of the real ball in the adjacent room, releasing another iron ball that blocks the path. Then an economical explanation of why that gate opened makes essential reference to what the simmball tracks — to what its trajectory is about — even though every physical transaction is strictly steel-on-steel. The question the essay pursues: do the symbols push the substrate around, or is all the pushing done at the level of iron and magnetism, so that speaking of the symbol as a cause is just a compressed way of keeping the books?

My draft thesis proceeds in three stages:
1. In the careenium, symbol-level explanations earn genuine causal keep: they support interventions, they figure in counterfactuals that hold fixed exactly the right things, and they cannot be replaced at the iron-and-magnet level without a catastrophic and unusable blow-up in complexity.
2. The moral is meant to generalize: any substrate organized so as to be sensitive to patterns rather than to specific micro-events is a system in which those patterns have causal powers, and content-tracking organization is one such arrangement.
3. Therefore the standard worry about mental causation — that beliefs and desires, being abstract pattern-level items, cannot do anything — rests on a confusion about levels of description. What counts as "doing" is relative to what the substrate is keyed to.

Please comment specifically on:
(a) the accuracy of my reconstruction of the thought experiment, as far as you can tell;
(b) whether the move from stage 1 to stage 2 is legitimate — in particular, whether the fact that the careenium's simmball-gate arrangement is deliberately rigged by a designer to track something marks a relevant disanalogy with cognitive systems whose content-tracking organization is evolved or learned;
(c) the level-confusion objection: one might insist that every transaction in the careenium is steel-on-steel, that the symbol "causing" the gate to open merely redescribes a counterfactual structure rather than adding a causal transaction, and that my essay owes a positive account of what it means for an item at one level to have causal power over items at another;
(d) whether there is a stronger objection I have failed to consider, and whether stage 3 generalizes too quickly from tracking of the simple, demonstrative sort the careenium involves to propositional attitudes like beliefs and desires, whose content is structured and inferentially integrated.

Finally, please tell me which parts of the argument, if any, are genuinely interesting, and flag anything that is unclear, overstated, or unfair to the opposing view.
