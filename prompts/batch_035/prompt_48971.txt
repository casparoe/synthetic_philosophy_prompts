I'm on the constitutional review committee for my university's student government, and we've got a genuinely awkward situation to sort out. Our student constitution gives a "judicial council" of five students the power to strike down legislation and referenda — they're appointed by the student body president and basically serve until they graduate. Last semester the student body voted 69-31 in a referendum to add a transit pass to the activity fee, and the council nullified it, saying fee increases have to originate in the student senate and that the referendum procedure wasn't followed (the election commission forgot to publish the ballot language 14 days ahead, as our rules require, which is true). Now half of our committee wants to amend the constitution to strip the council's strike-down power entirely and make their rulings only advisory, and the other half points out that two years ago the same council blocked a referendum that would have defunded several identity-based student orgs — which was sort of the council doing exactly what it exists for. Please draft the short memo I'll circulate before our next meeting recommending a concrete change (or no change), and make sure it grapples with the strongest point on the losing side, not just the side you pick. Also, we only have like two weeks before this goes on the ballot for the general student vote, so whatever you recommend needs to be something a student body would actually vote yes on.
