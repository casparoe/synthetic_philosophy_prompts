My friend Dara is doing a sociology master's and has uncompromising views about which kinds are socially constructed, which we spent an entire dinner cataloguing. Her verdicts, which she gives instantly and without hedging: money, gender, race, national borders, childhood as a life stage, and the chess title of grandmaster are all constructed; electrons, the chemical elements, species, biological sex, and mountains are not. Two complications she volunteered: she dismisses the deflationary line that everything is constructed because all categorizing is something we do — she calls that trivial and insists she's marking a real distinction — and when I asked about chairs she said constructed, but "in a much duller way than money," and refused to elaborate. She was equally unmoved when I pointed out that biologists themselves draw the boundaries of species, so I don't think mere classificatory involvement is enough for her.

We ran out of wine before we got to the cases I actually care about, so I want your prediction of what she'd say about four new ones: millennials as a generation; time zones; redheads as a hair-color kind; and invasive species, where the underlying biological facts are real enough but the category sorts organisms by origin and harm rather than by anything a botanist could read off a plant. Don't just give me her verdicts — I want to know what principle she's implicitly running, since I suspect more than one principle fits everything she's said so far.

Give me exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the rule that best explains her verdicts so far; (2) the strongest rival rule that fits those verdicts just as well; (3) her likely verdict on each of the four new cases, with your reasoning; (4) which of the four cases the two rules disagree about, and what each rule predicts there; (5) the single further case, not among the four, that would most cleanly separate the two rules, together with what each rule would say about it — ideally one she'd find hard to answer either way.
