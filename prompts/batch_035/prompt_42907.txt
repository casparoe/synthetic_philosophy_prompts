Here's where I've landed after way too much reading about Weimar: some kind of right-authoritarian regime in 1930s Germany was close to overdetermined — Versailles, the Depression, antisemitism was everywhere in the culture, the army and the old elites wanted revision anyway — so a world without Hitler just gets you a military dictatorship or a German Mussolini, whatever. But the specific thing we mean by the Holocaust, the industrialized extermination, seems to me genuinely contingent on Hitler and the small circle around him. A junta would've done discrimination and maybe mass expulsion, not Auschwitz. So: structural forces get you the regime, the Great Man gets you the genocide. What would count against this split? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward — which evidence, arguments, or comparisons should move me (other genocides that didn't need a single charismatic leader? where ordinary German antisemitism actually was in the 20s? the intentionalist vs functionalist fight and which side of it I've quietly taken), what a serious defender of my view would concede counts against it versus what they'd wave away, and an honest check on whether I've set it up so nothing could refute it — if every genocide gets attributed to its leader after the fact, is the contingency claim even exposed? Also, does my view predict anything we could actually look at, like big variation in outcomes across regimes with similar structural conditions but different leaders?
