I'm on the policy team of a company building large AI systems, and part of my job is to work out what, if anything, the science of consciousness implies about how we treat them — whether there's any basis for monitoring obligations, reporting requirements, or design constraints now rather than in twenty years. The problem is that the literature I keep running into turns on a cluster of disputes I can't rank, mostly concerning global workspace theory and the distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness. So I want a dependency-aware prioritization, not a survey.

The candidate questions, as I understand them, are roughly these: (1) whether the very distinction between access and phenomenal consciousness carves nature at a joint or is an artifact of sliding between first-person and third-person senses of "conscious"; (2) whether global availability is necessary, sufficient, or merely incidental to experience, i.e., whether the workspace is what makes experience or just what makes it reportable; (3) whether "overflow" findings of the Sperling sort — subjects apparently experiencing more than they can report — are genuine evidence of phenomenology exceeding access, or whether the no-report paradigms built to test this smuggle the conclusion in; (4) whether the alternative picture, on which local recurrent processing rather than global broadcast carries experience, changes anything downstream, or just relabels the same functional profile.

For each question, I'd like to know: which other questions depend on it; whether it could in principle be settled by anything we'll learn about AI systems or only by neuroscience of biological brains; and, most importantly for me, which of the practical implications people draw are overrated — for instance, does "if global workspace theory is true, current systems aren't conscious" actually follow, given that the theory was developed to explain brains? Please give the ranking and the reasoning behind it, and flag anything on my list you think is a pseudo-question whose answer wouldn't change what my team should do either way.
