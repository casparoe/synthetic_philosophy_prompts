here's the thing — I'm supposed to write up where I actually stand on path dependence for a history-of-tech reading group, and I want a consistency check before I circulate it, because I have a feeling I'm contradicting myself somewhere but I can't see where. the package is:

(1) QWERTY is a genuine case of an inferior standard getting locked in, and nobody involved was being irrational — that's just how network effects work. same goes for a bunch of file formats and standards we're probably stuck with forever.

(2) in general I'm a contingency person. rewind the tape of history and you get nothing like our world — there was no inevitability to fossil fuels winning, cars being shaped the way they are, the internet looking like it does.

(3) that said, I think multiple independent discovery is the norm, not the exception — calculus, natural selection, the telephone, and so on. once the prerequisites are floating around, an invention is basically "in the air," and it matters little who exactly gets there first.

(4) I also think calling a locked-in standard "inferior" is trickier than people admit, because a lot of what makes a technology better or worse is relative to what everyone else is already using.

(5) and I think historical counterfactuals — like whether we'd have gotten computing a century early if Babbage's engine had actually worked — have real truth values, we're just bad at finding them out.

so: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix? go through the pairs, rank the problems by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and don't hold back — I honestly don't know which one I'd drop.
