Slogan type, social construction of kinds.

At a party a while back I got into it with two people on the same night, and they both leaned on the line "X is a social construct" without ever saying what that's supposed to mean. First guy said money is a social construct, and then concluded we could basically just redesign the whole system from scratch whenever we get tired of it, since it's all made up anyway. Twenty minutes later someone else said race is a social construct, and used that to shut down a question about whether doctors should use race in screening guidelines, like construct = nothing biological could possibly be tracking anything, end of discussion. And I've also seen gender is a social construct used in what feel like opposite ways -- both to say gender is pure invention we could remake however we like, and to say that identities within it are just as real and valid as anything else. Same sentence, very different jobs.

So what are the actual distinct claims hiding in "X is a social construct"? I want them laid out as a list I can bring back to these people, from the boring version that's so weak it would make tables and traffic laws constructed too (and so proves nothing in any argument), up through whatever the strong versions are. I half suspect one reading is just causal -- our practices explain how the kind came about -- while another is something weirder, like the kind exists *because of* our attitudes in a way trees don't, and I don't know if those come apart or not. For each reading tell me whether it's a platitude, something substantive a reasonable person could defend, or just false. And then tell me which reading, if any, actually supports the moves my two party guys made -- the redesign-money move and the ignore-biology move -- or whether they were both just using the phrase as a mic drop. Sorry if this is basic, I never took philosophy, I just keep noticing the sentence doing suspiciously different work every time.
