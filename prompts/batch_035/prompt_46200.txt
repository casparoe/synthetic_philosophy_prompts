Quick recap so we're on the same page: for Tolstoy a work's merit as art comes from its infectiousness, how strongly it transmits the artist's feeling to the audience, which he says depends on three conditions — the individuality of the feeling, the clearness of the transmission, and the sincerity of the artist. But then he ALSO brings in subject matter: the best art transmits simple, universal feelings that unite people, which is why he went after Shakespeare and late Beethoven as incomprehensible elite art.

Apply HIS criteria, not your own tastes, and sort these five into three tiers (top / middle / bottom): Hamlet, Beethoven's Ninth, War and Peace, a well-known folk song sung round a campfire with everyone joining in on the chorus, and a massively popular superhero blockbuster.

Please structure the answer as: "Tier list" (roughly three sentences per item explaining the placing), "Where his criteria strain" (a paragraph or so on which placements feel wierd or unstable — e.g. does the blockbuster's mass infectiousness push it up while its individuality and sincerity drag it down, and is that a genuine contradiction in the theory or just a tension between his two seperate tests?), and "Bottom line" (2-3 sentences: do the infectiousness conditions and the subject-matter requirement actually fit together, and if you were forced to drop one, which should go?).
