Please argue that coherent extrapolated volition (CEV), as a proposal for aligning a powerful AI system, should be implemented as a unanimity procedure rather than an aggregation procedure: the system should act only where the extrapolated volitions of individual humans converge, and should otherwise refrain from intervening, rather than computing some aggregate or average of divergent extrapolated preferences and imposing it on everyone. In developing the argument, please engage with what seems to be the strongest objection to this unanimity interpretation: that abstention is not neutral, because a default of inaction is itself a choice, and it may frustrate nearly everyone's extrapolated volitions — for instance, by allowing ongoing harms or sustained stagnation that no morally and intellectually improved version of us would endorse. You may also address the question of whether the divergence problem is mitigated, though presumably not eliminated, by the fact that extrapolation is supposed to remove errors, inconsistencies, and passing moods rather than substantive differences in values.
