You are a professional philosopher specializing in applied ethics and political philosophy, advising me, a member of my city's commemoration review board. Please think carefully about the record below before recommending a decision: start by reconstructing the candidate principles that fit our precedents, then apply them, rather than deciding first and rationalizing after.

Our board's precedents, in brief:

(1) We removed the 1915 courthouse statue of a Confederate officer, erected by a revisionist veterans' group; the local Black community had petitioned for its removal for decades.
(2) We kept, with an added interpretive plaque, the centennial statue of our city's founder, a slave trader, reasoning that the evil, though real, is not what the statue honors, and that no one harmed by him is alive.
(3) We renamed a boulevard that had honored a mid-century mayor in recognition of the very urban-renewal program that razed a Black business district; descendants of the displaced families, still concentrated nearby, had petitioned us.
(4) We declined to rename a street named for a sixteenth-century explorer infamous for the enslavement and massacre of indigenous peoples, on the grounds that after four centuries the name has become a mere place-label and no affected community is local.

The new case: a bronze statue, erected in 1970 outside the hospital he founded, honors Dr. Corwin, a physician celebrated for pioneering the county's rural vaccination programs and for building the hospital itself. But Corwin also directed the state institution's mid-century sterilization program, which sterilized several hundred institutionalized patients, and he regarded that work as part of the same public-health career. The last surviving patients died only a decade ago; their descendants are numerous in town, and a few have written to the board informally, though there is no organized campaign and the local paper's editorial board opposes removal.

What should we decide if we want to be consistent with ourselves? Identify the closest analog among our precedents, spell out the two or three principles that fit our record at least equally well, say what each predicts here, and be honest about which principle we would be adopting with each available choice. Finally, describe the hypothetical future case that would best tell those principles apart.
