I need some help diagnosing a dispute that's stalled our school board, because at this point we're just going in circles. Context: my kids go to a small bilingual school and I recently got elected to its board (parents can stand, long story). For the last six months we've been deadlocked over a proposed "community charter": things like expecting families to attend a couple of shared festivals a year, a strong norm of volunteering for school events, and formally recognizing the founding families' language in school ceremonies. One faction, call them the liberals, says the school is a service provider: it should coordinate neutrally and let each family decide how much of the "shared life" they want. Making participation a matter of expectation, even soft expectation, is a kind of soft coercion and it's none of the school's business. The other faction says that's a fantasy: a school like ours only works because people actually show up and pass the language and the rituals on, so the shared life isn't an optional extra, it's the precondition, and a charter just makes the implicit explicit. Both sides agree on every fact we've looked at (who attends, what the volunteer numbers are, what similar schools did); nobody has moved an inch.

While digging into this I realized we're basically re-enacting the big communitarianism vs. liberalism fight from the 80s and 90s, at a tiny scale. I've been reading around it: Sandel's critique of the "unencumbered self", Walzer on how different goods belong to different spheres, MacIntyre on traditions carrying reasons, and the liberal replies, like Kymlicka's point that a liberal culture is itself a culture that has to be reproduced, plus his distinction between a community you can exit and a cultural structure you can't just leave behind without cost. My working hypothesis is that the two factions on our board aren't disagreeing about facts or even about values exactly, but are answering two different questions under one heading — one side is answering a question about legitimacy (what may an institution demand?), the other a question about flourishing (what does this institution need to survive?) — and our deadlock comes from each side treating its question as the only one on the table. But I might be wrong; maybe there's a real value conflict underneath, or maybe it's partly verbal, hinging on what people mean by "community" in the first place. Either way I'd like a diagnosis of the structure of the disagreement, using what each side actually says, rather than a verdict on who's right or a list of books to read.

Since I'll be pasting this into my notes, please give me the diagnosis in JSON with this schema: an object with fields "disagreement_type" (your classification — verbal, substantive, bedrock intuitions, different-questions-under-one-heading, or a mix, with a one-paragraph justification), "side_a_steelmanned" and "side_b_steelmanned" (the strongest version of each board faction's position, a few sentences each), "hidden_crux" (the single underlying question, if any, that's actually doing the work in our dispute), "hypothesis_assessment" (whether my different-questions hypothesis holds up, and what it gets wrong), "what_would_count_as_progress" (the most concrete thing — a distinction we could adopt, a reframing of the charter proposal, a specific question we should put to the board — that could actually unstick us), and "generalizes" (a short note on whether the same diagnosis applies to the academic debate or whether the school version differs in some way that matters). Put the JSON up front, then add a few paragraphs of prose after it walking me through your reasoning. Thanks — our next board meeting is in three weeks and I'd like to walk in with something better than "we clearly just disagree."
