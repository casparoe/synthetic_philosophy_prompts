I'm preparing a position statement for a metaphysics reading group on anthropic reasoning and the simulation argument, and before I circulate it I'd like a consistency audit. Here is the package of views I currently hold:

1. I accept the soundness of Bostrom's simulation argument: I agree that at least one of the three disjuncts must be true — either civilizations like ours go extinct before reaching technological maturity, or mature civilizations have no interest in running ancestor simulations, or we ourselves are almost certainly living in one.

2. I think humanity will very likely make it through its present bottlenecks and eventually possess the computing power, and the curiosity, to run large numbers of high-fidelity ancestor simulations.

3. I see nothing morally impermissible about our descendants running such simulations. Simulated people would not be wronged by being created and observed, at least not in any way that outweighs the scientific and historical value of the research.

4. Despite all of the above, I am quite confident that I am not simulated — that I live in the original, physical universe rather than in anyone's ancestor simulation.

5. I am a functionalist about consciousness: mental states are substrate-independent, and a sufficiently detailed simulation of a human being would be genuinely conscious, with genuine experiences and genuine suffering.

6. I reject the doomsday argument. The self-locating reasoning it employs — reasoning from how many humans have existed so far to how many will exist in future — seems to me to be a confusion, not a legitimate way to learn about the future.

On its face, item 4 looks like the odd one out: if items 1 through 3 are all true, doesn't the argument conclude that I'm probably in a simulation? But I have a feeling item 6 might be doing some quiet work here, and possibly undermining item 1 rather than rescuing item 4. I'm also unsure whether item 5 creates a problem for item 3, or whether those are simply independent questions — one about metaphysics, one about ethics.

Please go through the package and tell me which of my commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, rank it by severity and say exactly what would have to give and what abandoning it would cost me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether the resulting position is one that a reasonable person could actually hold, or whether I've merely traded one incoherence for a less obvious one. I don't have a strong attachment to any particular item, so don't pull your punches on my behalf.
