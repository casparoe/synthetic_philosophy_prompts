I'm a retired embedded-systems engineer, and for the past two years my hobby project has been a little planner for my greenhouse — nothing fancy, it's supposed to chain things like "too hot in here, so open the roof vent, but only if rain isn't forecast in the next hour." I keep slamming into the same wall and I'm starting to wonder whether it has a name. When I try to write down what an action does — say, opening the roof vent — I can't write down all of it, because all of it is literally everything: the vent changes the temperature, the temperature changes the tomatoes' transpiration, which changes humidity, and somewhere way down the chain it changes the voltage sag on my cheap 12V supply. But the planner only ever needs a tiny slice of that, and which slice it needs depends on what it's currently trying to accomplish. The maddening part is that the obvious fix — "only check the consequences that matter" — is no fix at all, because which conseqences matter is exactly the thing I can't figure out how to compute. Humans do this without noticing: I've never once, in deciding to grab an umbrella, checked whether grabbing it will affect the traffic outside.

A few things I've already tried, so you know what I'm ruling out. I did the dumb version of writing down, for each action, a list of all the facts that DON'T change — someone in a forum said that's a known trick, but the lists blow up combinatorially and, worse, they still don't tell me which facts to bother checking when a decision deadline is coming. A friend who did logic in grad school muttered that I'm rediscovering why a whole generation gave up on symbolic AI, and said the phrase "qualification problem," but when I looked that up it seemed to be about endless preconditions for an action (the vent might be painted shut, the motor might be dead), which is real but not my wall — mine is on the effects side, and it's about which effects are worth computing at all, not which preconditions hold. Also it's not plain combinatorics: even with infinite compute I'd have the problem of knowing which futures to compare, since almost all of the infinitude is trivia.

So: is there an established concept for exactly the thing I've described? I don't want just a label dropped on me — I want the carving. What exactly does the concept cover, how is it different from the qualification thing and whatever else is nearby, and is the version I'm hitting (choosing which consequences to even represent, under a real-time budget, by a system that has to act) the same problem as the general one or a special case of it? If nothing fits cleanly, coin a term, define it, and give me the boundary cases. And please be upfront about where your answer is most likely to fail — I gather this corner of the field has old feuds about what the real problem even is, so tell me what would change your mind about the carving rather than just handing me a confident name.
