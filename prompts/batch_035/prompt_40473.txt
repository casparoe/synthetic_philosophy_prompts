Last night my friend Dana and I got into it about her kid's school, and we left it kind of unresolved and it's bugging me. Context: Dana is a middle school teacher, her son goes to the school she teaches at, and she's just gone down a determinism rabbit hole after listening to some podcasts. She came out of it convinced that praise and blame are both basically incoherent in a classroom. Her plan is to stop ever telling a kid "good job" or "that was a bad choice" and instead only ever describe what happened and what caused it. Like the kid who forgot his homework isn't "responsible," he's just the output of his genes and his home situation and so on, and saying otherwise is just physics denial.

I tried to push back with the one case that's stuck in my head: two kids, same missed homework, but one genuinely forgot because his family just moved and his life is chaos, and the other one looked the teacher in the eye and said "I did it, I just left it at home" while it's sitting in his backpack, half-copied. Dana says there's no deep difference, they're just different causal histories, and drawing a line between them is us pretending to be little gods outside the causal order. And honestly when she puts it that way I don't have a great comeback, but I still think a teacher should treat those two kids differently and I don't think it's because I believe in magic souls.

The other thing she hit me with, which I found sneakier: she says if blame is off the table because the kid "couldn't have done otherwise," then praise is equally off the table, and doing the nice version of the same error doesn't make it less of an error. Which feels right but also feels like it proves too much, like it would also make me a jerk for thanking someone who held a door.

So what I want to know is: is there actually a principled way to defend treating the forgetful kid and the lying kid differently without smuggling in some ghostly free will thing? Is there a version of "he could have done otherwise" that doesn't require it to be literally true, or some other thing blame tracks that Dana's picture is missing? And does her point about praise cut against me or against her, or does it land somewhere weird for both of us? Please don't just tell me to agree to disagree, I want to know if one of us is actually more right here and which one.
