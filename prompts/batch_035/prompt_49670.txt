The trouble is I mostly know this stuff from secondhand sources, and I just came across a summary that struck me as too convenient. A reviewer of some popular book on twins (I don't remember which one, I read the review in an airport) put it roughly like this: since twin studies find heritability around 50-80% for intelligence and personality, that means most of who we are is fixed at conception — the family you grow up in, the schools, all of it, barely matter, which is why the brothers would have turned out similar no matter who adopted them; and since heritability is a property of the trait itself, this holds for any population, so if some group scores lower on average, high heritability within the group tells you the gap between groups is genetic too; and because it's genetic, interventions like better schools can't really move the needle much anyway.

Something about this felt off even to me — I vaguely remember that adoption studies show adopted siblings resemble each other more than strangers on some things, which seems hard to square with "the family barely matters," but honestly I can't tell whether I'm misremembering that or whether it's compatible with the review being right. If some parts are right but need qualification (e.g. heritability being specific to a population and environment rather than being a constant of nature), please say so, because my narrator is the kind of guy who'd know exactly where the loose joints are and I want his corrections to be the real ones, not the movie version.
