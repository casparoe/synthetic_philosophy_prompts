Retired geochemist here, been on a big philosophy kick since I stopped working. My wife and I have been argueing about freedom for weeks and I've been keeping score of her calls. The question is always just "is this person free or not":

- our nephew could quit at the only mill in his town, but the owner would blacklist him across three counties and everybody knows it. She says not free.
- a hermit who lives off a stream that might dry up any given summer, no person has any say over him whatsoever. Free, she says.
- my brother, ten years of trying and failing to quit drinking. Free — she says his chains are of his own making, sad but not unfreedom.
- a citizen in a country whose laws genuinely restrict what she can do, but she voted for the laws and can campaign to change them. Free.

Next week we've got three new cases and I want to walk in with a prediction instead of my usual flailing. A woman in a country where the law still says her husband controls her property, though he has never once used that power and never will, and divorce is cheap and easy to get. A guy who makes his whole living on an app that could deactivate him tomorrow for no stated reason with no appeal — he loves the work, and he could go flip burgers but at half the pay. And a monk who took a lifetime vow of obedience, could physically walk out the gate any day of his life, and has never once wanted to.

So: what will she say about each one, and what rule do you think she's actually running? I'm fairly sure her record so far fits more than one rule (she's been reading some australian philosopher and mutters about masters and slaves alot, but I can't tell how much of that is just her being stubborn). Tell me the other rules that fit her verdicts just as well, what each one predicts for the new cases, your best guess with reasons, and what further case would really split the candidates apart. Honestly the prediction is secondary — I mostly want to know if there's a clean experiment here, the kind you'd design for anything else.
