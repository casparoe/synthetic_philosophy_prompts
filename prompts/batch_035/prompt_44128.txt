I'm trying to get clear on what a theory of decision-making under moral uncertainty actually has to deliver before I evaluate the leading candidates, and I want the specification written first. Assume the setting: an agent is confident in ordinary decision theory, but uncertain which moral theory is true, with credences spread across theories that can disagree in kind (one forbids an act, another merely permits it) and not just in degree of choiceworthiness. Write the desiderata such a theory must meet, with the motivating case or consideration behind each. It should at least have to cover: (a) comparing choiceworthiness across theories whose scales are not obviously commensurable (a prohibition-theory versus a welfare-maximizing theory versus a rights-based theory that speaks only in side constraints); (b) handling theories that deny the significance of moral uncertainty itself, such as theories on which only the true morality's verdicts generate reasons, or on which moral beliefs are not the kind of thing one can have credences about; (c) saying something about the value of moral inquiry, i.e., whether and when one should spend resources reducing one's own moral uncertainty rather than acting on it, including the self-undermining case where no theory endorses spending on moral inquiry; (d) preserving continuity with ordinary decision theory as moral uncertainty shrinks to zero, so that a morally certain agent's prescriptions are untouched; and (e) a stance on cases with a self-locating or precommitment structure, where an option locks in conditions before moral uncertainty is resolved. For each desideratum, say whether it is non-negotiable or reflects the commitments of a particular camp, and flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable — that is the part I most want to see. Then, briefly, score maximize-expected-choiceworthiness, the my-favourite-theory view, and the moral-parliament family against the list; keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard. Conclude with a final line of the form "Verdict: [the single desideratum that does the most to eliminate candidate theories]."
