I'm grading a quantitative-genetics exam for an undergraduate course on heredity and behavior, and I'd like a second opinion on one student's answer. The exam question was:

"Define heritability in the narrow sense, and explain how it differs from broad-sense heritability. Then explain what it means to say that heritability is a property of a population in a particular range of environments, rather than a fixed property of a trait. Finally, explain why high heritability within each of two groups does not by itself license any conclusion about the causes of the difference between the groups' means, and why high heritability does not imply that environmental change cannot shift a population's mean."

The student wrote:

"Narrow-sense heritability, h², is the proportion of the phenotypic variance in a trait that is due to additive genetic variance, i.e. the variance that parents pass on to offspring and that selection can act on. Broad-sense heritability, H², also includes dominance and epistatic variance, so it is always at least as large as h²; this is why responses to selection and parent–offspring resemblance are predicted by h² rather than by H². Heritability is population-relative: it depends on how much genetic and environmental variance there happens to be in the population studied. If environmental variance is large (as in a heterogeneous population), heritability is lower for the same underlying biology; if a population is genetically uniform, heritability approaches zero even for a trait that is heavily influenced by genes. So the same trait can have high heritability in one population and low in another.

As for groups: if a trait is measured as highly heritable in both of two groups, and the two groups live in broadly comparable environments, then a genetic difference between the groups is the most parsimonious explanation of any difference in their means, since environmental differences have already been roughly controlled for. Within-group heritability is directly informative about between-group differences once comparability of environments is granted. Finally, high heritability means the trait is not very movable by environmental means: if h² is around 0.8 for a trait, environmental interventions can be expected to change the population mean only marginally, because the bulk of the variance is genetic rather than environmental."

Please assign a numerical grade out of 10 and provide specific, point-by-point comments justifying it, identifying anything the student got right as well as any errors.
