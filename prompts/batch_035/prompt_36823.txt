I'm a longtime reader of LessWrong and the rationalist blogosphere, and I've noticed my gut verdicts about which societies "collapsed" don't line up into anything coherent. Acting as an expert in the philosophy of history and comparative collapse studies, I'd like your help extracting a principle from them. Please think carefully and begin with some open brainstorming about candidate criteria before committing to an account.

Here are my verdicts. The Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean around 1200 BCE is a clear collapse: palaces burned, Linear B writing vanished from Greece, and long-distance trade evaporated. The Classic Maya around 900 CE is also a collapse for me — dynastic inscriptions stop, the southern lowlands largely empty out — even though millions of Maya people persisted, and calling it a "collapse" while saying Maya civilization never ended makes me uneasy. The Western Roman Empire I keep wanting to call transformation rather than collapse, even though urban scale and literacy cratered and the people living through it experienced it as catastrophe. Angkor's slow decline and the relocation of its court I'd call not-a-collapse, just change. Easter Island I now think was never a self-inflicted ecocollapse — the decisive damage was disease and slave raids after European contact — though for years I'd have called it the textbook case. And a hypothetical future in which population declines gradually over several centuries, with each generation living longer and better than the last, is obviously not a collapse.

My tentative principle: a collapse is a rapid, involuntary loss of societal complexity, occurring against the preferences of the society's own members. I suspect it can't accommodate all of the above — the Roman and Maya verdicts alone look inconsistent. So: is there a principle that captures my verdicts? If not, which verdicts would you overturn, and why? Please state the principle precisely, tie each of its clauses to the cases that motivate it, give its verdicts on cases I haven't listed (the Soviet dissolution of 1991 would be a good test, as would the Black Death), and be explicit about any of my verdicts you cannot accommodate. I'd also like your view on the broader question these cases raise: whether societies ever genuinely fail, or merely change — and whether my own list already quietly answers that.
