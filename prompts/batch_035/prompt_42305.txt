So I'm giving a talk at a small free will workshop in a few weeks and I want to be ready for the Q&A, because the audience is very much not my people. I'm defending a new-ish Frankfurt-style case and I need a prep sheet.

Quick sketch of the case so the objections make sense: a neuroscientist implants a device in Jones's brain that monitors a sub-personal neural configuration -- call it state S -- which is fully sufficient for Jones deciding to vote for the incumbent. Whenever S is present, the decision follows, no exceptions. The device stays idle unless Jones is about to decide NOT to vote incumbent, in which case it fires and produces the incumbent-voting decision anyway. In the actual sequence S is present, Jones decides on his own, device never fires. Standard Frankfurt setup so far. My twist is aimed at the dilemma defense: I argue the libertarian is stuck, because if she says S is sufficient for the decision, she's conceding that choices can be deterministically produced by sub-personal states that Jones doesn't control -- which is basically giving up the farm even without the counterfactual intervener. And if she says S isn't sufficient, the device guarantees Jones votes incumbent either way, so my case tests PAP fine.

The audience: mostly agent-causation libertarians, a couple of whom are really into the Kane/Widerker-style dilemma defense and will come armed. Two compatibilists who think the entire Frankfurt literature is a sideshow since compatibilists never needed PAP anyway. Plus one grad student who keeps bringing up Fischer's distinction between regulative and guidance control at every opportunity.

Draft my prep sheet: the seven or eight objections I'll actually get from these specific people, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way this audience would phrase it, with a two-minute reply I can actually deliver and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the objection I'm praying nobody asks. And be honest with me about whether the sub-personal state move is doing real philosophical work or whether I've just relabeled one horn of the dilemma and the libertarians will see through it immediately -- if it's the latter, better I find out now than during the talk.
