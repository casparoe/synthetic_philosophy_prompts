I recently read that the Planck length (roughly 1.6 × 10⁻³⁵ metres) is the smallest distance that has any physical meaning, and that below this scale our ordinary notion of space may simply break down. My understanding is that space is therefore not infinitely divisible but is instead built out of indivisible units of distance, somewhat like the pixels of a photograph. If that is correct, it seems to dispose of Zeno's dichotomy and Achilles paradoxes outright: there is only a finite number of gaps to cross between me and the door, so the infinite sequence of ever-smaller distances never gets going, and there was never anything for calculus to resolve. Given this, please could you explain why philosophers still treat Zeno's paradoxes as genuine puzzles rather than relics of the Greeks' ignorance of quantum gravity, and whether the Planck length should count as the definitive refutation Zeno was missing? I would also like to know whether this same argument defuses the arrow paradox, since if time comes in indivisible instants the arrow arguably never moves at all. Please answer in British English.
