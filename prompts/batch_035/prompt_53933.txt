For about a year now I've been slowly putting together my own position on when it's okay to say a computer system believes something, mostly from arguing with a programmer friend of mine who thinks the whole question is silly. Rather than presenting him with a scattered mess, I want to lay out my commitments and check that they form one coherent picture. Here they are. First, I've been won over by the idea that attributing beliefs to a system is basically a bookkeeping device for prediction: we say the thermostat believes it's too cold, and there's no further hidden fact about whether it really believes that — what matters is only whether treating it as a believer helps you anticipate what it does. Second, following from that, I'm comfortable saying the chatbot believes the capital of France is Paris, in exactly the same instrumental sense as the thermostat, and I don't think silicon versus neurons is relevant — if a system of any make shows the right pattern of behavior, and the right pattern of breakdown when things go wrong, the belief attribution is earned. Third, I resist saying the chatbot lies when it confidently tells me something false, because lying requires intent, but I have no problem saying it believed the false thing; that distinction feels obviously right to me. Fourth, I generally take the chatbot's own remarks about itself — things like it being unsure, or not having access to something — at something close to face value, as evidence about what's going on inside it, except when they're obviously canned disclaimers. Fifth, despite all of the above, I can't shake the conviction that there's a genuine difference between the chatbot's beliefs and my dog's beliefs, that the dog's are real in a way the chatbot's are merely useful fictions, and that this difference is a fact about the systems themselves and not just about which predictive shortcut is handier for me.

Please go through these five commitments and tell me which of them actually pull against each other, which only look like they conflict, and what the cheapest repair would be in each case — I'd like the tensions ranked by severity rather than presented as equally bad, and for each one a concrete statement of which commitment would have to bend or break. A couple of things I'd especially like your judgment on: whether my third point is doing any work at all or is just a restatement of the first, and whether my fifth point is a genuine inconsistency or whether there's a principled way to hold both the bookkeeping view and the real-versus-fake distinction. Since I know people who think about these questions for a living disagree sharply among themselves, please keep a clear line between what's more or less settled reasoning on my own set of commitments and where you're choosing between genuinely contested positions — and don't sell me any contested verdict as if it were the consensus view.
