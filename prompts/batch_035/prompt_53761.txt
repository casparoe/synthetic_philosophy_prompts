So I've basically gone all-in on eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires: the idea that "belief" and "desire" are theoretical posits of folk psychology, that folk psychology is a bad theory of what's causing behavior (roughly like phlogiston or caloric), and that mature neuroscience will carve things up so differently that everyday mental-state talk will eventually look like talk of demonic possession. I'm not asking you to talk me out of it, or to give me the history of the debate. What I want is a map of the view's exposure: what would actually have to be true for it to be wrong.

A few things I want to make sure get dealt with head-on:

First, the self-refutation objection people always wheel out -- if you assert eliminativism, aren't you asserting something you beleive, so the view refutes itself? I've always found this weak (asserting isn't believing, and people asserted possession claims for centuries without there being demons). But I want to know whether there's a sharper version of it that should actually worry me, one that doesn't just beg the question against the eliminativist, rather than the strawman version.

Second, and this is where I'm honestly less sure of myself: my own way of holding the view leans on an analogy between folk psychology and folk physics. Naive physics is fine for catching balls and terrible for explaining why the stars don't fall, and I picture folk psychology as similar -- a shallow predictive apparatus that a real science would replace. But here's the thing nagging at me: folk psychology seems way better at its job than folk physics is at its. It predicts, like, nearly everything people do, day in and day out, with staggering accuracy in ordinary contexts. If a posit-package is predictively indispensable in a domain, does that rescue the posits as real (maybe the way "gene" survived molecular biology even though the concept got revised to death), or does it just mean we've got useful instruments with no reference behind them? And if the answer is the second one, has my view quietly morphed into something unfalsifiable -- "neuroscience will eventually vindicate us" is a bet that never has to be paid?

Third, I want you to distinguish between defeaters that eliminativists themselves would concede count against the view and defeaters that only critics claim for it. E.g., if neuroscience somehow found states that map cleanly onto beliefs and desires one-to-one, or if cross-cultural work showed belief/desire attribution is way less universal than assumed, or if folk psychology turned out to be a special case of a theory-like learning mechanism versus just a language game for giving reasons after the fact. Where do those fall?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which commitments of the view each one actually touches, and be honest if the conclusion is that the view is well insulated and whether that insulation is a bug or just the nature of a big-picture metaphysics bet. Please write the answer in American English.
