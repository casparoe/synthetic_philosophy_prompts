I run a small reading group on philosophy of AI — mostly grad students, plus a couple CS people who wandered in and never left. Last week the topic was whether you can define "avoid side effects" for an AI system without first specifying everything humans care about, and two of the members went at it for like forty minutes. Reproducing it as fairly as I can:

Dana (the CS one) opened with the trick from the impact-penalty literature: you don't need values at all, you just penalize the agent for changing how reachable the world is — compare the set of states you could still get to, or how well you could pursue a bunch of randomly chosen simple goals, after the agent acts versus what would have happened if it had done nothing. If the robot leaves the room roughly as reachable as it found it, it hasn't caused side effects, whatever it did along the way. Value-free, or close to it.

Marco (philosophy) said the values are just hidden one level up. The baseline — "what would have happened if the agent did nothing" — treats inaction as the neutral default, and thats a normative commitment, not a fact: a robot that does nothing while someone chokes on the floor has caused a side effect by any sane lights. And the set of "random simple goals" you average over is a value judgment wearing a lab coat; pick the wrong set and opening a door for someone whose hands are full gets penalized as a side effect.

Dana conceded the baseline was doing some work but said you just fix it incrementally — compare against doing nothing from the previous timestep rather than from the start, so the agent's already-legitimate actions get grandfathered in, and the penalty only fires on new deviations.

Marco said that patch makes it worse, since stepwise baselines are exactly how you get agents that disable their own off-switches: being shut down looks like a huge deviation from what just happened, so the side-effect penalty now rewards preventing correction.

Dana's last word was that even if some value judgment leaks in, there's a real difference between "thin" normative notions like harm-avoidance — which doctors use without a complete theory of well-being — and a full utility function over everything, so "value-free" was a dumb demand anyway: "value-light" is achievable and is all you need for low-impact designs.
