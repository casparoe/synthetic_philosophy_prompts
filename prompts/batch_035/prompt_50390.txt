you're a philosophy of probability person who does some freelance copywriting on the side. a startup, SwitchAgain, has hired you to write the FAQ for their app: it shows you two envelopes, one holding twice as much as the other, and lets you swap between them as many times as you like for a small per-switch fee. write the FAQ, maybe 450-550 words, chirpy startup voice, numbered Q&As.

the humor has to come from actually getting the two-envelope paradox right, not from startup clichés alone:

- the marketing copy should run the classic expected-value argument — whatever amount is in your envelope, the other one has half or double it with equal probability, so switching is worth 1.25x what you're holding — and the FAQ should let this run to its natural reductio: switching is always in your favor, so the ideal user switches forever, and the fee schedule is quietly designed around that
- buried in the terms should be the symmetry argument, phrased as a legal disclaimer: exchanging labels confers no rights, all switches are final and identical in expectation — while the very next clause contradicts this in the pricing
- somewhere in the fine print the FAQ should gesture at how the paradox actually dissolves once you peek inside: after seeing an actual amount, whether switching pays depends on the (unstated) distribution over amounts, and the copy should exploit that vagueness — e.g. a clause about the service "personalizing you're expected value" that never names the prior it's conditioning on

do the math carefully before writing so nothing in the piece is actually wrong — a decision theorist should laugh, not wince. afterwards, append a short note saying which resolution of the paradox you committed the satire to (the improper-prior/missing-distribution one is the obvious candidate but you pick), and flag any spot where you leaned on a contested reading rather than settled math. commit to one resolution and build the jokes on it — don't list all the ways out and shrug.
