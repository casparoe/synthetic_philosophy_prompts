I've been around LessWrong and its diaspora long enough to have watched the Sleeping Beauty thread cycle through the comments section at least a dozen times, and the striking thing is how little the debate has moved since the exchange in Analysis around 2000 that fixed the modern terms: halfer versus thirder, with both camps still claiming the other side's credences make them exploitable. Everyone can reproduce everyone's arguments from memory. Rationalist spaces then pile on complications — the Presumptuous Philosopher, the split between the self-sampling and self-indication assumptions, worries about whether observer-count evidence can swamp ordinary evidence — so that halfers and thirders tend to arrive pre-aligned with larger anthropic tribes, which I suspect makes the impasse look more ideological than it is.

Here is my candidate diagnosis, offered as a hypothesis rather than a conclusion. Whenever the elicitation protocol is pinned down — one bet per experiment versus one bet per awakening — halfers and thirders agree on every action. The standard thirder Dutch book pays Beauty per awakening; the standard halfer book pays her once per experiment; each argument yields a different exploitable credence only because it silently adopts a different rule for which awakening counts as the bettor. So perhaps the dispute is not about probability at all, but about the individuation of centered worlds: whether Beauty's Monday-with-tails and Tuesday-with-tails awakenings are two possible situations she might be in, or two perspectives on a single one. Variants designed to break the symmetry, like Technicolor Beauty — an extra coin deciding whether she sees the red paper on Monday and the blue on Tuesday, or the reverse — seem to me to relocate the question rather than answer it: she ends up with a fresh self-locating puzzle whose resolution just is the original controversy.

But the full dissolution won't go through, and that's what I want diagnosed. If credences about self-location were meaningful only relative to an elicitation protocol, then the anthropic applications should go quiet too; yet in the Presumptuous Philosopher setting the two sampling assumptions really do seem to issue different advice about which theory to investigate, and the halfer/thirfer split appears to track them. So: is this a verbal dispute about the word "credence," a substantive disagreement about the right formalism for self-locating belief that no behavioral test can settle, or a proxy war for the anthropic assumptions fought on ground where they don't matter? Diagnose it using what each side actually argues, tell me whether my individuation hypothesis survives contact with the Presumptuous Philosopher, and say what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a variant, a formal result — or whether the impasse is structural and nothing should be expected to end it.
