I write a science-and-ideas column for a general readership and my next piece is on whether beliefs and desires are real things in the head or just a handy way of talking about complicated systems — the old chess computer vs thermostat vs falling rock stuff. To go with the piece I want a 'which stance do you actually take?' quiz, maybe 7 or 8 questions, each a concrete case where the positions genuinely come apart: predicting a chess program's next move by attributing it the desire to protect its queen; a thermostat that 'wants' to keep the room at 70; a spider that 'believes' the fly is in the web; a depressed friend whose behavior you predict via beliefs and via medication, and whether those two stories compete. Please build it from the logical space first — starting with whether folk psychology is a predictive instrument like any other (so the question is just whether its patterns are objectively there in the data or projected by us), and only then locating the named positions as leaves: the mild-realist view that beliefs are real the way a statistical pattern is real, the view that treats them as inner causes like neurons or code, the deflationary 'useful fiction, nothing more' view, and the outright eliminativist who says the pattern talk will eventually go the way of phlogiston. No question should just ask 'do you believe beliefs exist' — sort readers by what they say about cases. Include a scoring key, flag any two positions the quiz can't really seperate, and note the unoccupied combos and why nobody sits there. Answer in American English please.
