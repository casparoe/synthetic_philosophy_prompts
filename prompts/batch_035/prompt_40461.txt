read The Road through that lens for me. specifically: is the mother who gives up actually the most philosophically consistent character, because she's the one who grasps that once there's no collective afterlife the whole structure of values collapses, while the father is just refusing to update? and the fire they're carrying — what does it even mean if the boy is the last or second-to-last generation? is being one of the good guys still worth anything when there's no future to hand the goodness off to? and the ending, the trout passage — why does that devastate people when it's about a world that's already completely gone?
