I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for over a decade — LessWrong in the Sequences era, the Slate Star Codex archives, the forecasting-tournament discourse around Tetlock — and these days I think fluently in the vocabulary that community taught me: credences, proper scoring rules, calibration curves, log-odds updates, base rates, the difference between a forecast and a commitment. Restate the lottery paradox and the preface paradox in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. By the lottery paradox I mean the one where each of a large number of individually very probable claims about lottery tickets appears justified, while their conjunction is known in advance to be false; by the preface paradox I mean the one where an author believes each chapter of her carefully checked book is accurate, and simultaneously, on the strength of her track record, believes the book contains at least one error.

Then give me the residue report. My expectation is that a good deal translates: the lottery setup looks to me like thresholding a calibrated forecaster's credences into flat calls and then conjoining them, and the preface like knowing your own error rate well enough to hold high credence in each chapter and low credence in their conjunction. Tell me whether that expectation is right, which parts you had to distort to fit, and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. The residue I care about most is this: the paradoxes seem to turn on a notion of justified belief that is closed under conjunction and can be inconsistent with what else one justifiedly believes, whereas my vocabulary has graded credence and no binary belief, so the paradoxes appear to dissolve into mundane facts about thresholds and conjunction. Is my sense that they are artifacts of thresholding a probability distribution correct, or is my vocabulary missing something — perhaps the norms governing flat-out assertion, or the preface author's act of publishing itself? And if the vocabulary is missing something, tell me whether that is a defect of the vocabulary or a sign that the philosophers' notion of justified belief was less clear than it looked. I want an honest translation, not one that smuggles in Bayesian assumptions and calls them the paradoxes' own content.
