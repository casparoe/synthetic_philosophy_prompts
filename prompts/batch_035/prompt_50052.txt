You are a professional philosopher working in metaethics and the normativity of attitudes. Please think carefully before answering, and begin with a brief brainstorm of the moving parts before you evaluate anything. Here is roughly where I've landed, and I'd like an audit of whether it hangs together:

(1) I accept a fitting-attitude analysis of value: for something to be admirable is just for it to merit admiration, for the good to merit a pro-attitude, and so on. There is no more to being valuable than being a fitting object of a favorable response.

(2) I think fittingness itself is reducible: an attitude is fitting just in case the reasons to hold it are sufficient and undefeated. Fittingness talk, on my view, is bookkeeping over reasons.

(3) I accept that if a villain credibly threatens to destroy the world unless I admire him, I thereby have an excellent — indeed decisive — reason to admire him. Refusing to count such prudential considerations as reasons at all strikes me as wishful thinking.

(4) I am equally confident that admiring such a villain would be unfitting: threats are no part of what makes a person admirable.

(5) I am suspicious of the terminology of "right kind" versus "wrong kind" of reasons. As far as I can tell it labels the problem rather than solving it, and I don't believe reasons fundamentally divide into kinds: there are just reasons, weighed against each other in deliberation.

(6) I hold a buck-passing account of value: for something to be valuable is for it to have properties that provide reasons to respond to it in certain ways, not for it to possess some further value property over and above those.

Which of these commitments are in genuine tension, and how badly? For each genuine tension, explain what generates it, what the cheapest repair would be, and what that repair costs elsewhere in my package. Please also flag any pair that merely looks inconsistent but isn't, so I don't tear apart something that was fine. I confess I can't tell whether dropping (2) — treating fittingness as a primitive normative notion — would clean everything up, or whether the problem would just resurface somewhere else in what I believe. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating all of them as equally bad, and close by telling me which single revision would resolve the most.
