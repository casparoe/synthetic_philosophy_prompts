I've been drafting a statement of my views on what the AI alignment problem actually consists in, to post on a forum where I expect pushback, and a friend who read an early version told me flatly that the position doesn't hang together — but she refused to say where, on the grounds that I should find it myself. I've stared at it for a week and can't see it, so I'm outsourcing the audit. The commitments are as follows.

First, I think alignment is best defined narrowly: a system is aligned when it is trying to do what its principal actually wants, and the whole technical problem is making that trying reliable. I explicitly reject the broader framing on which alignment means getting AI to act on what is independently good or right; I do not want a moral philosopher in a box, and I'm suspicious of anyone whose version of alignment smuggles in a substantive ethics. Second, I think the sharpest dangers come from systems that competently pursue objectives that are close to but not identical with what was wanted — cases where the pointer has come unstuck from the thing pointed at — so the interesting work is about preserving the link between intention and objective, not about first-order moral questions. Third, and this is where my friend raised an eyebrow, I think the long-term ideal for highly capable systems is something in the neighborhood of coherent extrapolated volition: systems that help people become what they would want to become on reflection, because most people's here-and-now wants, mine emphatically included, are a mess we have no business handing to something powerful. Fourth, I think corrigibility — the disposition to let the principal inspect, correct, and shut the whole thing down — is not scaffolding to be removed once alignment is solid, but a permanent design requirement for any system more capable than its principal. Fifth, I think an aligned system should push back: if I ask it for a plan I will regret, I want it to tell me so, clearly and repeatedly, rather than comply.

So: which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look that way, and how bad is each? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as an undifferentiated list, and for each one say what would have to give and what it would cost to give it — I want to know whether the cheapest repair is a clarification or an amputation. I genuinely don't know which commitment my friend thinks is the problem, and I have no favorite I'm protecting, so don't pull punches.
