The essay question was:

"Explain the problem of future contingents as it arises from Aristotle's discussion of tomorrow's sea battle, and evaluate the Ockhamist and supervaluationist responses to the fatalist argument. Does either response succeed without abandoning classical logic?"

The student's answer reads as follows:

"Aristotle raises the problem of future contingents in De Interpretatione 9, where he asks whether 'There will be a sea battle tomorrow' must already be either true or false. His argument runs as follows. By bivalence, every proposition is either true or false. If the sea-battle proposition is already true, then it cannot be that no sea battle occurs; if it is already false, then a sea battle cannot occur. So whatever will happen is already fixed, and deliberating about tomorrow's naval strategy is pointless. Aristotle's own solution is to deny bivalence: future contingent propositions are neither true nor false, which keeps the future open.

The Ockhamist response, associated with William of Ockham, also aims to avoid fatalism, but it does so by granting the fatalist a premise. Ockhamists accept bivalence, so they agree that 'There will be a sea battle tomorrow' is already true or false. However, they deny that an already-true future-tense proposition necessitates the future: truth is not a cause. Since bivalence is retained and one unique future is actual, the Ockhamist must accept that the future is settled in every respect; fatalism is avoided only at the price of admitting that deliberation, while causally efficacious, is itself part of the fixed future.

The supervaluationist takes a third route. Like Aristotle, the supervaluationist holds that the sea-battle proposition lacks a truth value today. And because each disjunct is truth-valueless, even the law of excluded middle fails: 'Either there will be a sea battle tomorrow or there will not' is itself indeterminate. This shows that supervaluationism improves on Aristotle, who remained committed to classical logic, since the supervaluationist can say exactly where classical logic breaks down."
