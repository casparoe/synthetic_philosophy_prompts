I'm giving a job talk in a few weeks on evidential cooperation in large worlds, and I need a Q&A prep sheet before I fly out. My paper argues for a deliberately modest version of ECL: I claim that when two agents recognize that their decisions are evidentially correlated -- because they run similar decision procedures on similar inputs, whether that's literal copies of the same algorithm in different places or just structurally similar minds facing structurally similar choices -- they have a reason to cooperate with each other in a way that neither causal decision theory nor plain altruism captures. But I explicitly refuse the maximalist version: I don't argue that you should condition on the outputs of every simulation of you in some infinite multiverse, and I try to do everything without committing to a particular decision theory, because I actually think ECL gives us a reason to cooperate even if causal decision theory is true -- the correlation is evidential, but the practical upshot is just: pick policies you'd want your correlated counterparts to have. I know this middle position is going to annoy everyone.

The audience is a mid-sized department that has one very sharp causal decision theorist who thinks Newcomb's one-boxers are basically mystics, two Bayesians who will treat my evidential correlations as a conditionalizing puzzle, a Kantian who I suspect will say the whole thing is just the categorical imperative with worse notation, and a couple of metaphysicians who will ask whether "similar decision procedure" is even a coherent primitive (and honestly I don't have a fully worked out answer, the best I have is a rough ressemblance metric over causal histories of the agents' learning). Plus grad students who've read the LessWrong-adjacent stuff and will be disappointed I'm not defending the simulation-heavy version. Draft me the objections in rough order of how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that specific person would actually phrase it in a Q&A, with a reply I can give in about two minutes and a note on where the reply is soft.
