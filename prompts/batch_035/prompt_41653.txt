My department runs a small extracurricular reading group on SETI and the Fermi paradox, and after two years of arguing, the group has effectively split into two camps that keep talking past each other. One camp — call them the Quietists — takes the dark forest hypothesis seriously: given that civilizations cannot verify each other's intentions across interstellar distances, that technological development can be explosive and unpredictable, and that a first strike may be cheap relative to the risk of being struck, they conclude that broadcasting is potentially suicidal and that METI attempts are a civilizational-scale gamble that no one has the right to make on behalf of everyone else. The other camp — the Loudists — thinks this reasoning is a paranoid artifact of applying Cold War deterrence logic to a domain with entirely different physics and economics: interstellar strikes are ruinously expensive and slow, hiding is anyway impossible since biosignatures and atmospheric technosignatures (industrial pollutants, artificial greenhouse gases) betray a technological species regardless of whether it transmits deliberately, and a civilization advanced enough to kill us at range could already know we're here, so deliberate silence buys nothing while deliberate transmission might buy contact, coordination, or at least legibility to potential neighbors. Each side thinks the other's position is obviously reckless in a different direction: one risks annihilation by shouting, the other risks stagnation, isolation, or slow-motion extinction by cowering.

I've been tasked with drafting the document both camps could actually sign, in the spirit of an adversarial collaboration. Please write it for me. I want (a) the claims both camps genuinely accept — about the Fermi paradox, about detection physics, about the game theory, about the state of our evidence — stated precisely enough that neither side would wince; (b) the exact residual disagreements, each phrased in wording the *side holding that view* would endorse, rather than a strawman; (c) the cruxes — sub-questions where settling them would move at least one camp — which I suspect include at least whether remaining detectably silent is even possible, whether benevolent civilizations can afford to be visible and thus dominate the population of loud civilizations, and whether the chain-of-suspicion argument actually survives once strikes cost more than observation; and (d) for each crux, a concrete test, observation, or argument both camps would agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction written down in advance — for example, what each would expect if we detect an unmistakably technological exoplanet atmosphere, or if a long-term analysis shows interstellar strike costs scale prohibitively with distance. If part of the dispute strikes you as merely verbal — say, disagreement over what counts as a "signal" or over what risk level makes silence rational — please flag which part and say why. Both camps insist they are not arguing about whether aliens exist, so don't rehash generic Fermi paradox speculation; the disagreement is about transmission policy under uncertainty.

Structure the document as follows, with these sections in this order: a section titled "Points of Agreement" containing roughly eight to ten numbered claims (about 500–600 words total); a section titled "Disagreements" formatted as a table with columns for the point of contention, the Quietist formulation in their own terms, and the Loudist formulation in their own terms (about a table of five to seven rows); a section titled "Cruxes and Registered Predictions" with roughly 350 words per crux, covering three cruxes; a section titled "What Would Change Our Minds" of about 200 words summarizing each camp's pre-committed update conditions; and a short closing section titled "Verbal Disagreements" of about 150 words identifying any parts of the dispute that dissolve under more careful phrasing. Keep the tone diplomatic but not mushy: vague hedges that either side could sign because they say nothing are exactly what we're trying to avoid.
