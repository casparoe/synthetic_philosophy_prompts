I'm a PhD student in cognitive science writing a chapter on why the Cartesian theater intuition is so hard to shake, and Pixar's Inside Out has become my star exhibit. I'd like a careful reading of the film through Dennett's critique of the Cartesian theater and the multiple drafts model from Consciousness Explained. Headquarters is a single place where the contents of Riley's experience arrive together, projected on one screen for an audience of homunculi who are themselves fully conscious agents — Joy plans, remembers, gets bored, and grieves — so the film seems to explain Riley's consciousness by positing further consciousness inside her, precisely the regress Dennett attacks. But I don't want a one-sided prosecution. Where does the film quietly resist the theater picture — the shared console, Sadness's functional role, the ending's mixed memories dissolving the doctrine of core memories? Is the theater just a convenient user interface the filmmakers need, while the story told through it is anti-Cartesian, or does the film's emotional power depend on our taking the theater literally? And the question I most need help with: could any narrative film dramatize the multiple drafts model at all, given that cinema shows one thing at a time to a seated audience — making Inside Out evidence about our intuitions rather than about the mind? Please take Dennett's arguments as seriously as the film's craft, and don't let the film off the hook with the claim that it's only a metaphor.
