I'm a grad student giving a job talk next month at a department that's heavy on Kantian ethics — the kind of place where half the faculty think desert and responsibility presuposes strict numerical identity and get touchy about anything that smells like weakening it — plus one hardcore metaphysician who thinks personal identity is a deep further fact and openly hates reductionism. My thesis: what matters in survival isn't identity but degree of psychological connectedness, and moral practices that look like they need strict identity — punishment, promising, compensation for past wrongs — can get everything they actually need from connectedness, tracked in degrees. My paper runs a modified teletransporter case where the machine degrades your memories in known increments, so you can literally dial connectedness up or down, and I argue that at no point does a further fact about identity kick in and change what anyone may do to you or what you owe. I also use a fission case where the pre-fission person signs a contract divvying up their debts between the two successors, which I claim is coherent and shows promising doesn't need identity either.
