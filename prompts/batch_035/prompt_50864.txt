I volunteer with a rural search-and-rescue group, and since last month I have been building the group a small Bayesian tool that fuses radio reports into a probability map of where a lost hiker might be. In a tabletop drill I gave it a version of van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin case (1981): a hiker is known to be in one of three areas — Blue territory, or Red territory, which divides into an HQ area and a Second Company area — with prior credences 0.1 on Blue, 0.1 on Red-HQ, and 0.8 on Red-Second. A trusted operator radios: if she is in Red territory, she is three times as likely to be in the HQ area as in the Second Company area. My tool ingested the message and returned P(Red) = 0.5, down from 0.9 — as though the operator had said something about whether she is in Red territory at all.

A friend who studies formal epistemology defends the tool as follows. (1) All rational updating is conditionalization on the propositional content of the evidence received. (2) That content must be some combination of the cells in the agent's Boolean algebra. (3) The only such combination that even approximates the radio message is the material conditional: not-Red or HQ-area — the "three to one" cannot be expressed in the algebra, so the plain conditional is the best available surrogate. (4) Conditionalizing on not-Red-or-HQ turns the 0.1/0.1/0.8 prior into 0.5/0.5/0 — I checked the arithmetic. (5) By the same reasoning, the effect of a conditional message on the probability of its antecedent is fixed not by anything in the message but by how the prior happens to be distributed inside the antecedent: rerun the drill with prior 0.1/0.45/0.45 and the identical message moves P(Red) only from 0.9 to 0.82. (6) A message whose effect on a question is dictated by irrelevant prior bookkeeping is not evidence about that question; so conditional testimony is never evidence about its antecedent, and since that is what conditionals are for, my tool should simply refuse conditional reports — as should I, when a friend says "if you end up going in after dark, take the north trail."

I cannot accept that conclusion: half of what the tool exists to do is exactly ingest such reports. But I also cannot find the flaw in the chain. I would most like to keep (1), and (4) is just arithmetic. Tell me which step to reject, why that step looked fine, and what rejecting it costs — in particular, what my tool is supposed to do with a conditional report once that step is gone.
