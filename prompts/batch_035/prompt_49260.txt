My study group has spent like four weeks on panpsychism and I've realized we keep talking past each other because none of us can actually say what would count as SOLVING the combination problem. People just name-drop it like its a settled thing and then argue about whether panpsychism is weird, but the real question is what the bar even is. So before anyone brings up another theory, I want the spec: write the desiderata that any adequate solution to the combination problem has to meet — what it must explain, which cases and distinctions it has to respect, what it can't contradict — with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and mark which ones you think are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are just preferences of one camp.

Some candidates I want on the list but am not sure how to weigh. It has to explain how micro-experiences get unified into one perspective without smuggling in a new subject on top of the micro-subjects (my friend calls this the "no extra spectator" requirement — if you need a further experiencer to enjoy the combination, youve just moved the problem up a floor). It has to say why the combined experience is silent about its parts: I never catch any glimpse of the micro-experiences that are supposed to compose me, and a solution should tell us whether that silence is forced or just contingent. It has to handle the palette problem — if the micro-level only stocks some limited menu of primitive qualities, where does the specific feel of my particular green come from, and if instead every quality is present at the bottom, why is the palette so small? And it can't be strong emergence wearing a costume, since avoiding brute emergence was the whole reason for going panpsychist in the first place. Also, settle an argument for me: is the demand that the solution be intelligible or explain "how" combination works a real desideratum, or is it just a prejudice we inherited from demanding more of consciousness than we demand of physics, where we happily accept brute fundamental laws?

The part I most want to see: tell me whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable. I have a suspicion that "no new subjects" plus "genuine unity of perspective" plus "no strong emergence" can't all be had at once, and if that's right, the list itself is the interesting result — it would mean the combination problem isn't a puzzle awaiting a clever theory but a theorem about what any theory can do. Then briefly, how constitutive panpsychism, emergentist panpsychism (the version where combination is itself governed by fundamental but non-structural psychophysical laws), and cosmopsychism score against the list — I know the cosmopsychist claims to swap combination for a decomposition problem, and I want your read on whether that's a genuine escape or the same failures under a new name. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not the scorecard; that part I can do myself.
