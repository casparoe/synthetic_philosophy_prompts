I'm the grad student who takes minutes for our faculty's internal small-grants committee (three voting members: Agnes, the chair, Tomas, and Priya — I just record, I don't vote). Every proposal gets scored on two criteria, methodological soundness and team capability, and the written funding rule says a proposal is funded if and only if the committee judges it sound AND judges the team capable. That's in the bylaws, not just custom. We just finished this cycle's docket except for one last proposal, and while typing up the minutes I realized I genuinely can't predict what we'll do with it, because looking back at our decisions I can't figure out what rule we've actually been running.

Here's the track record. Tidal energy project: Agnes and Tomas voted yes on both criteria, Priya no on both — funded. Migration policy study: only Tomas found it sound, only Agnes liked the team — not funded. Soil carbon one: soundness passed 2-1 (Tomas and Priya yes, Agnes no) but the team vote failed 1-2, only Tomas — not funded. And the archives digitization grant, where Priya was recused for being a co-author on the proposal: Agnes and Tomas split one-one on both criteria, and we funded it anyway, with Agnes saying something like she didn't see a majority against it (she voted yes on both, so resolving the tie her way funded it). Also worth knowing: last year the dean's office flagged us for inconsistent reasoning across our decisions and made us write a memo describing our "aggregation procedure," which we basically invented on the spot, so there's some pressure to look principled now.

The last proposal on the docket is an aquaponics thing, and the votes line up like this: Agnes yes on soundness, yes on team; Tomas yes on soundness, no on team; Priya no on soundness, yes on team. So both criteria pass 2-1, and if you take the written rule literally we should fund it. But if you just asked everyone "should we fund this?", only Agnes says yes — it's 2-1 against. I can see at least two readings of what our precedents amount to (tally each criterion separately and follow the majorities, versus just taking an overall vote, with maybe the chair mattering more than the bylaws admit), and as far as I can tell they agree on everything we've decided so far but come apart exactly here. What should we do with the aquaponics grant if we want to be consistent with ourselves, which reading fits our record best and why, and is there a kind of case we haven't seen yet that would really settle which rule we're running? Also, if the answer is just "the written rule forces your hand," fair warning that this committee ignores the bylaws whenever it suits them, so I'd want to know what we'd be commiting ourselves to either way.
