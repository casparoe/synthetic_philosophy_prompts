This isn't my field at all, I just read a lot of pop science and argue with my brother-in-law, so apologies if I'm using words wrong. I've been keeping a list of my gut verdicts about which categories are "real" out there in the world versus which are just filing systems we invented, and I want to know if there's a single principle behind all of them. Here's the list:

- Lithium: real. Anything with 3 protons in the nucleus is lithium, full stop, whatever else is going on with it.
- Heavy water: I say it IS water, even though its a bit heavier and behaves slightly differently chemically. But this one makes me sweat, because if heavy water is water then I guess deuterium is hydrogen, and chemists treat hydrogen and deuterium differently enough to actually separate them and put them to different uses... so is "hydrogen" one kind or two? I honestly go back and fourth on this, its the case I keep chewing on.
- Jade: not real. Its two completely different minerals, jadeite and nephrite, that happen to look similar and get carved into the same stuff. Two kinds sharing one word, like two families with the same last name.
- Fish in the everyday sense (tuna, sharks, goldfish, but not whales): not real either. Apparently a coelacanth is more closely related to a cow than to a tuna, so the everyday word is lumping across a real division and cutting through a real group. Though I do think there's a real bigger group there that the everyday word fails to track — one that would include cows — so its not like there's no joint at all, we just drew the fence in a dumb place.
- Pluto: the demotion from planet status was pure bookkeeping. Nothing about Pluto changed and no new fact about Pluto got discovered in 2006, astronomers were just tidying the filing. Calling it a planet or not doesn't carve anything.
- Whales: but when it turned out whales are mammals and not fish, that felt like discovering something real. Moving the whale across the categories told us something about the whale.
- Cancer: real, cells dividing out of control because a regulatory mechanism broke. Hypertension: not real, its just a line drawn on a ruler — the threshold got moved from 140/90 to 130/80 by a committee in 2017 and that was that. But this pair bugs me, because heart attacks and strokes seem like real things that the threshold is indirectly tracking, and because the Pluto/whale pair above has the same shape — committee vote versus discovery — and I can't tell if my rule handles both pairs the same way or if I'm just being consistent by accident.

My own rough stab at a principle: a category is real when the things in it are like each other because of something about themselves — their structure, their history, what they're made of — and not because of what we happen to want the word for. That makes lithium and cancer real, and jade, "fish," and hypertension not. But I can't tell whether that's one principle or three principles wearing a trenchcoat. And I'm not sure what it says about whether species are real kinds: I want to say yes, that dogs and wolves are genuinely different even though they interbreed, but then what about the salamanders that interbreed all the way around the Central Valley in California until the two ends of the ring meet and can't breed with each other? Where does the kind stop, and did the kind stop existing halfway around?

So that's the question: is there one principle that gets all or most of my verdicts? If yes, state it precisely enough that it decides cases I haven't listed, tell me what it says about the deuterium one and the salamander one, and be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save — should I drop the heavy water one, the hypertension one, or what? If no single principle works, tell me which verdicts have to go and why those. Please don't just name-drop a philosopher and leave it there, I want to know where the edges of the thing are.
