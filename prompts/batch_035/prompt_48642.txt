I spent forty years as a field entomologist and now that I'm retired I've gotten deep into philosophy, mostly via a little reading group that meets at the library. Next month it's my turn to run a session, and I want to do sphexishness -- the digger wasp thing, where the wasp drags her paralyzed cricket to the burrow, goes in to inspect, comes out, drags the cricket in -- and if you move the cricket back a few inches while she's inside, she drags it to the threshold again and re-inspects the burrow, again and again, apparently forever. Hofstadter coined "sphexish" for this kind of mindless rigidity (I think it was in one of his Scientific American columns, but don't quote me), and people like Dennett have used it when talking about whether there's real flexibility anywhere, including in us.

The group is maybe eight retirees, no philosophy training, though a couple of them are ex-engineers like me and very stubborn about definitions. What I need from you is NOT the explanation itself but a plan for the session -- I get about an hour and a half and a whiteboard. Specifically: what has to be established first before the philosophical question is even on the table (I suspect something like the difference between "doesn't work" and "works every time in the world it evolved for," but you tell me if that's the right first move); what the one carrying example should be -- obviously the wasp, but I want your advice on whether to also arm myself with a counter-example that looks like the wasp but isn't, because I know at least one guy will bring up his dog learning a new route to the park; and the misconceptions in the order I should expect them. I'm guessing the big ones are "the wasp is just dumb," "actually the wasp story is debunked so the whole idea collapses" (is it debunked? I've seen people claim Fabre basically set the wasp up), and the engineer's inevitable "so my thermostat is sphexish too, so what." Where in the sequence do I head each one off?

Here's my own addition, and I'd like the plan to make room for it if you think it's worth it: I've been wondering whether sphexishness comes in degrees rather than being yes-or-no. A wasp that repeats the inspection loop 40 times but eventually gives up and digs elsewhere -- is she a little bit less sphexish, or is she fully sphexish with a different loop? And if flexibility is a matter of how long the loop is before some check breaks it, doesn't that question eventually reach us? That's the free will angle I actually care about, but I worry it's too big for one session with this crowd and I don't want to end with vague hand-waving about determinism.

So: the order of ideas, the example(s), where the misconceptions get defused, whether my degrees-of-sphexishness idea should be in the session or saved for a second one, and one concrete question I can put to the group at the end to find out whether the idea actually landed versus them just nodding along. If there's something I should openly refuse to cover in ninety minutes, say that too.
