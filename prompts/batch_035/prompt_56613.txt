For two years my philosophy of language reading group has been stuck in the same rut on the ameliorative/descriptive debate about the term "woman," and I want a diagnosis of the impasse rather than another round of the arguments themselves. The ameliorative faction, following Sally Haslanger's approach, holds that we should define "woman" by the work the concept needs to do in feminist politics — roughly, as the class subordinated on the basis of (real or imagined) female-typed bodily features — even if that definition departs from how ordinary speakers use the word. The descriptive faction replies that a definition licensed to revise usage in that way has simply changed the subject: if it turned out that nearly no one satisfied the ameliorative analysis, that would show the analysis fails, not that the ranks of women are nearly empty. Everyone in the room knows both arguments cold, and nobody has budged.

My working hypothesis — offered only as a hypothesis — is that the real disagreement is over what kind of term "woman" is. The ameliorativists seem to treat it like "planet," a term over which some relevant community can legitimately legislate, the way astronomers' decision that Pluto fails to clear its orbital neighborhood demoted it without falsifying anything about astronomy. The descriptivists treat it more like "weed": a folk taxonomic term with messy edges that nonetheless tracks something about the world, and that we can recommend abandoning but not usefully redefine for our own purposes. So: is the impasse verbal, substantive, or a case of the two sides answering different questions (semantic versus political ones) under a single heading? Does my planet/weed framing capture the structure of the disagreement, distort it, or merely relocate it into a further dispute about whether "woman" is closer to a natural-kind term or a social one? And given your diagnosis, what, if anything, would count as genuine progress here — a distinction, a shared test case, a reframing — or should we expect no movement, and why? Please answer in American English.
