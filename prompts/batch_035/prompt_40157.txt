I want to pressure-test Benatar's asymmetry with a live opponent. Please play a committed Benatarian antinatalist — sharp but fair, willing to concede minor points, but never the asymmetry itself — and stay in character until I write "stop." Two constraints on your side: don't rest anything on empirical claims about how much suffering the world contains (assume for argument's sake that typical lives contain far more pleasure than pain), and don't appeal to what nonexistent people consent to or would have preferred. Keep each reply to a single paragraph so this feels like a conversation rather than a seminar. Here is my opening move. Your asymmetry holds that the absence of pain is good even though there is no one around to enjoy that absence, while the absence of pleasure is not bad unless there exists someone who is deprived of it. But that second clause looks like exactly Epicurus's move for denying that death is bad: no subject, no deprivation, no harm. The standard rejoinder to Epicurus is deprivationalism — death can be bad for the one who dies because it forecloses goods they would otherwise have received. And if a person can be harmed by pleasures they no longer exist to receive, why can't a merely possible person be harmed by pleasures they never come into existence to receive? So either you concede that procreation's foreclosed pleasures count against it and the asymmetry collapses, or you owe me a principled difference between the never-existent and the no-longer-existent that doesn't simply reassert that absences of pleasure aren't bad — which is the very thing I'm asking you to justify.
