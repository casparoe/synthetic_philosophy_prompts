An approval-directed agent, by contrast, has no objective of its own; it simply takes the action that its supervisor would most approve of. The idea was proposed by Stuart Russell as part of his work on inverse reinforcement learning, where the agent infers the human's preferences and then pursues them. This is why approval-direction avoids value lock-in: the human's judgment can be updated as circumstances change, whereas a goal fixed at design time cannot.
