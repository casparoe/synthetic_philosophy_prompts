My friend Maya and I have this running bit where we rate the behavior of people in stories we hear (mostly gossip from her office, plus whatever's in the novels her book club picks) as either lying, misleading-but-not-lying, or neither. She's weirdly consistent about it, and after a while I realized I can't actually predict her on the edge cases, which made me curious what rule she's running under the hood. Her track record so far: a guy asked whether he finished his roommate's leftovers said "I've been at the office since 8" — true, but he ate them at 7:30 while getting ready — misleading, not lying. A coworker who told a date "I'm not seeing anyone right now" while being in a relationship (they just never literally see each other, it's all texting) — also misleading. A guy who said "I don't have a girlfriend" when he very much does — lying. A tour guide who gave confidently wrong dates that she sincerely believed — neither, "just wrong." And a guy at a bar telling an absurd fishing story that nobody in the room believed for a second — not lying, she said, because there was no one there trying to be deceived.

I'm seeing her Friday and I plan to pose four new ones, and I'd like to walk in with actual predictions instead of guessing. Case one: a guy on a date says "I'm so glad I finally quit gambling" — he has never gambled in his life, but he wants to come across as someone who's turned his life around. Case two: a woman planning a surprise party gets asked point-blank by the honoree "you're not throwing me a party, are you?", and she answers, totally straight-faced, "Yes, we're all hiding in your kitchen right now with a cake" — which is true, and she says it precisely because she expects the honoree to take it as an obvious joke and conclude there's no party. Case three: a supplement seller at a farmers market telling customers "this will noticeably improve your focus within a week" — he genuinely has no idea whether it's true, doesn't believe it's false either, he just says whatever moves product. Case four, just to be annoying: the guy who says "I'll be there in five minutes" when he knows perfectly well it's a twenty minute drive.

So what I want from you: call each of the four the way Maya would, and lay out the rule (or rules) that best explains her record so far. Then tell me which rival rules fit her past verdicts just as well but come out differently on some of my new cases, and which of the four is likeliest to make her hesitate or contradict herself. And finally, if her record so far genuinely can't settle one of the calls, sketch the one extra case I should hold in reserve for Friday that would split the competing rules most cleanly. I care more about nailing the underlying rule than about getting any single verdict right, since honestly I could flip a coin on case two. Wether she calls the party thing lying or not is the one I'm most curious about myself.
