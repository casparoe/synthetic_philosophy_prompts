I'm a novelist working on short fiction with a philosophical bent, sort of in the tradition of Borges and Kafa but more grounded in mundane modern settings. My next piece is going to be about the rule-following problem that Kripke drew out of Wittgenstein -- the skeptical paradox where it seems like nothing determines that by "plus" I meant addition rather than some deviant quus-like function that agrees with everything I've ever computed so far but diverges on some case I've never considered. I don't want a story that explains the paradox or has characters lecture about it, I want the paradox enacted.

Here's the setting I've been toying with, feel free to improve on it: a small city office where the same clerk has worked for forty years, administering a pension rule written decades ago by a committee that's now entirely dead. Nobody has ever had to apply the rule to the case that arrives in the story -- some genuinely novel combination of circumstances, maybe a claimant who's technically both employed and retired, or who paid in under two different names -- and the clerk, who's always known exactly what the rule means, discovers she can't say what makes her application of it correct rather than merely habitual. Her whole sense of having always meant one thing by the rule starts to feel like it has no ground under it. Maybe there's a younger colleague who keeps asking her how she knows, and the clerk's confidence curdles into something strange. I'd like the ending to turn on the community aspect of meaning -- that whatever settles what the rule demands isn't in her head but in something shared, in how her responses mesh with others' -- without ever saying anything that explicit.

Write the story, roughly 1200 to 1800 words, third person, restrained and slightly deadpan register -- the horror (if it's horror) should be quiet. No philosophers mentioned by name, no meta-commentary, just the office, the rule, the claimant, and the clerk's growing vertigo. You can invent the rule's exact wording but keep it mundane-sounding, like actual pension legislation.
