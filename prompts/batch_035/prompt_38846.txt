so a bit of background: I've meditated on and off for a few years, and somewhere along the way, plus some reading, I arrived at this view about value: the only thing that's intrinsically bad is suffering, and craving is basically a form of suffering, so the only thing that's intrinsically good is their absence. everything else — joy, beauty, achievement — is good only instrumentally, depending on how it sits with that underlying peace. a friend who's deep into meditation-adjacent ethics says this is basically "tranquilism", fine, whatever it's called. I've been trying to write up where I actually stand for a discussion group and I want a consistency check before I present, becuase I have a suspicion not all of this can be true at once, but I honestly can't tell which part is the problem. here's the list:

1. Intrinsic value: only the absence of suffering and craving matters in itself. a peak experience doesn't add anything on top of the peace it's made of.
2. When a contented person dies young, that's bad for them — they're deprived of decades of good things (love, travel, watching their kids grow up). deprivation is what makes death bad when it is bad.
3. If all life ended instantly and painlessly tomorrow, nothing intrinsically bad would have happend. no one suffers, nobody's left to grieve. I think this follows from my view and I'm ok with it, honestly it's one of the parts I'm most sure of.
4. A life of craving-and-satisfaction cycles — the obsessed artist, the athlete who needs the next win — can be a better life than one of flat, craving-free contentment. the struggle seems like part of what makes those lives good, not a cost to be subtracted.
5. The states deep meditators describe as the most peaceful also seem to be the ones with the least craving, and I basically trust those reports.
6. And then there's just... my life. I spend real money on good food, I plan trips, I chase good times with friends. feels like if I really believed (1) I'd have to be a monk, so maybe I don't believe it at all.

go through these and tell me which ones are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give. then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. be specific, and don't just flag everything — if some of these are fine as they stand, say so.
