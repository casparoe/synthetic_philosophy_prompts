His example: a vegetarian who's deeply opposed to factory farming pays a meat eater to go vegetarian, and if the meat eater only mildly prefers eating meat while the vegetarian is horrified by it, both of them end up with a world more to their liking than before. Fine so far. But then he made two further claims I wasn't so sure about.

Is that actually right? It sounded plausible but also kind of like he was smuggling in a whole theory of interpersonal comparisons of value that maybe the view doesn't need? And then he took this last step: so moral trade shows moral disagreement doesn't really matter at the end of the day, it's just like two people disagreeing about which restaurant to go to — everyone gets a better deal by trading, nobody is really wrong.
