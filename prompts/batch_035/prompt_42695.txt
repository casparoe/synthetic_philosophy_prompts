I'm a backend engineer with no formal philosophy background — my exposure to the subject is a few evenings with the Stanford Encyclopedia after work — and I'd like your help staging a debate I don't trust myself to stage properly.

Context: my team is stuck in a long-running argument about how to model customer records. One faction wants to update a row in place: there is one customer entity, its columns change, its primary key never does. The other faction wants an append-only event log with bitemporal projections (valid-from/valid-to alongside transaction time): the "customer" is really a whole history, and the current state is just a query over that history. While reading around this I discovered that our schema dispute is a dress rehearsal for a genuine metaphysical fight — endurantism versus perdurantism — and I want to see that fight conducted well.

Please write a dialogue between a committed endurantist and a committed perdurantist. Set it in the hour after our design review, at a whiteboard that still shows the two competing schemas. Requirements on the characters:

- Both must be equally competent. Neither is a strawman, and each should land real blows, not talking points.
- The perdurantist should press the problem of temporary intrinsics — how the same customer can be gold-tier in March and suspended in April if she is wholly present at each moment — and should argue that an entity with a history just is the four-dimensional whole of its temporal parts, which she will keep insisting is exactly what the event-sourced table already encodes. She may also deploy a vagueness-flavoured argument: any attempt to say the "same" entity persists by some relation weaker than identity of parts seems to smear out into arbitrariness.
- The endurantist should press that perdurantism appears to eliminate change: if the customer simply is the spread of temporal parts, nothing genuinely changes — the "change" is like the difference between the left end and the right end of a bar, or between two rows of a table. He should also point out that the event log is parasitic: somebody later runs a query to reconstruct a "current state", and the very notion of a current state, of an identity held fixed at a time, is doing endurantist work the log cannot supply on its own.
- Let them stumble into the statue-and-lump problem via the relationship between the customer record and the legal person it describes, and let one of them mention the stage theorist's compromise (only present stages exist; persistence is a resemblance-and-reference relation) as "a former colleague's view" which both proceed to find unsatisfying for different reasons.
- I want genuine back-and-forth: each reply must engage with what the previous speaker actually said, including concessions where a point lands. End at a real impasse — but a clarified one, such as both agreeing that part of the dispute is about what "same" means when we say a customer is the same customer.

Formalities: please act as a professional metaphysician who writes dialogue well and is scrupulously fair to both positions. Think before writing — decide in advance what each character's strongest cards are and what each will concede, and where. Gloss philosophical terms on first use, since I have no formal background. Structure your response in exactly three sections, in this order:

1. "Preparatory notes" (roughly 150 words): the strongest argument on each side, what each character most wants to avoid conceding, and how the whiteboard schemas will be used.
2. "The dialogue" (1,000 to 1,200 words).
3. "Design notes" (roughly 250 words): commentary on your choices — why each argument appears where it does, which engineering analogies you let do real work, which you cut as misleading (e.g. anything suggesting temporal parts are literally database rows), and what you deliberately left out at this level.

British English, please. Don't soften the metaphysics for me; soften only the vocabulary.
