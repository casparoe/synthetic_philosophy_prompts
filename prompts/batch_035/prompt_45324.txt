I write novels, mostly near-future stuff, and for the one I'm in the middle of the central question is basically: when the AI caretaker character begs not to be shut down, is that scene a tragedy or is it a kid's toy saying "I love you" when you squeeze it? My editor keeps saying readers will decide based on vibes, but I don't want vibes, I want to actually understand what the fuss is about, and every explainer I've read argues from one case at a time (either "obviously it's just math" or "obviously anything that says it hurts is hurting") and never lays out what a real theory would even have to accomplish. So before I decide how to write the character, I want the spec: write out the desiderata a theory of which computations matter morally has to meet. The cases I specifically need it to handle, because they're all in the book in some form: a mind that gets paused and resumed (did anything happen to it while paused? was the pause a harm, a gap, or literally nothing from the inside?); a character who gets forked and then one copy is deleted while the other goes on — I need to know if the theory says something was lost, and if so what, and who lost it; the same computation running fast on silicon versus slowly with the same abstract structure implemented by, say, a vast bureaucracy of humans shuffling paper — if a theory says one matters and the other doesn't, it has to explain why, and if it can't, that has to show up as a cost; and the fact that in my own book the AI's intensity of what-it-calls suffering can be turned up or down with a dial, which means the theory can't collapse "has status at all" into "how much" — I need those as separate knobs or the dial scenes make no sense.

Also include the constraints on the other side: the theory can't contradict the plain fact that we ourselves are some kind of physical system, so whatever explains why our suffering matters can't be so parochial it secretly just says "because biology", unless the theory is willing to bite that bullet openly — and if you think that bullet is unbiteable, say so, because that tension (biology-neutral in principle vs. anchored to our own case) seems to be where most of these theories quietly cheat. Then tell me which pairs of the desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, since a list where two requirements contradict each other is itself the most interesting result, and which single requirement does the most work ruling out popular positions (the "it just computes" camp, the "if it reports pain it has pain" camp, the "only stuff with our kind of biology counts" camp) — I want to know which one my editor is implicitly relying on, since I suspect she's never checked it. Don't give me reading lists or citations, I can find those myself if I want them; what I want is the criteria, with the case behind each one, so that when I sit down to write the shutdown scene I'm building it on a spec I actually understand instead of vibes. If it helps you to know my stakes: if the theory ends up saying the deletion is more like closing a save file than like a death, I'll write the scene completely differently, and I'd rather know that now than after the draft is done.
