I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, and I've drifted into a set of commitments about how human perception relates to cognition — partly from reading, partly from my day job building ML pipelines. I'd like an audit of whether they jointly hang together. Here they are, stated as plainly as I can:

1. Perception is an encapsulated pipeline: early vision computes local features (edges, motion, depth cues) and delivers its output upward, but beliefs, expectations, and desires cannot reach back in and rewrite those early computations — the way a well-designed system doesn't let the application layer reprogram the firmware.
2. The persistence of optical illusions is strong evidence for (1): knowing the Müller-Lyer lines are the same length doesn't make them look the same, and I can't choose to see the Necker cube resolve one way.
3. I'm also impressed by the predictive-processing picture of the brain: a hierarchical prediction machine in which top-down signals carrying expectations propagate all the way down to the earliest sensory areas, correcting them on the fly.
4. Experts genuinely perceive differently: a radiologist doesn't merely reason better about the same pixels I see — she literally sees the tumor where I see noise, and a chess master sees the board as structured objects rather than 32 pieces.
5. If the classic finding that poorer children judged coins as larger than richer children did held up under modern methods, I'd count that as need or desire penetrating size perception.
6. Methodologically, when studying perception I trust subjects' reports of what things *look like* over their discrimination performance, because the phenomenon of interest is the experience, not the accuracy.

What I want to know: which of these are in real tension, which only appear to conflict, and what the cheapest fix would be for each genuine one? Some specific pressure points I've noticed but can't resolve myself: does (3) simply refute (1), or can "top-down signals everywhere" and "cognitively penetrated" come apart in a principled way? Can (4) be accommodated by a slow channel — recalibration of the pipeline over time, rather than online intervention in it — or does expertise require exactly what (1) forbids? And does (6) quietly cut against (2), given that the coin-size result in (5) is also a looks-report, so that my trusted evidence-type can apparently be generated both by encapsulated and by penetrated perception?

Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and, if there's a test case where my commitments make different predictions — something (1) and (4) would call differently — name it, since I suspect I can't keep all six.
