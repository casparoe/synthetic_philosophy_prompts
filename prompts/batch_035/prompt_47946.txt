here's where I stand on free will after a lot of reading and I want to know if it's a stable package or a mess. (1) I'm a compatibilist: if you act from your own settled values, uncoerced and undeceived, you can be responsible even in a fully determined world. (2) I don't think responsibility requires being able to do otherwise — someone who would inevitably have done the same thing, but does it for reasons they endorse, is still responsible. (3) I find Pereboom's manipulation cases convincing: an agent whose values were deliberately engineered by neuroscientists isn't responsible, and I can't find a principled line between that and ordinary deterministic causation, so nobody deserves blame in the basic sense. (4) I also think Dennett had the right framing — the real question is which varieties of free will are worth wanting, the ones we need to keep practices like praise, blame, and promising working. (5) concrete case: I think the willing addict — the one who wants to want the drug and endorses his addiction — is responsible for relapsing, even though he could not have done otherwise.

I can't tell which of these fit together and which are quietly at war. For each pair, one or two sentences: if the tension is only apparent, say how it dissolves; if it's real, say what would have to give and roughly what it costs me. Order the five most serious first.
