I've been keeping a list of my gut reactions to identity statements to figure out what I even mean when I say one of them "told me something." So: "Hesperus is Hesperus" obviously told me nothing. "Hesperus is Phosphorus" was a genuine discovery back when astronomers first worked it out. "Cicero is Tully" teaches you something if you dont know it, and nothing if you're a classicist — and the sentence itself didn't change, so apparently the informativeness lives partly in me, not just in the words. "Water is H2O" was a huge discovery, yet now that we know it, it feels about as unshakeable as "water is water." And with "Superman is Clark Kent" — Lois believes Superman can fly but wouldn't say she believes Clark Kent can fly, which pushes me toward saying the two names have different meanings, but I also can't shake the feeling that they're just two tags for one guy and the identity is true no matter what. Is there one account of how names work that saves all of these, or close — senses, plain reference, descriptivism? If something has to go, tell me which verdict Id be giving up and why it's the cheapest one to lose. Then tell me what your account says about the case I keep flip-flopping on: my grandfather swears there are two Robert Millers in town, one on the school board and one who coaches little league, and they're actually the same person — so when he says the school board guy is honest but the coach isn't, is that a coherent belief for him to have, or is he confused about the world rather than about the words? And if you can, give me one more case where the candidate accounts split, so I can check my gut against it.
