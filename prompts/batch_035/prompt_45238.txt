So I've been going down a rabbit hole about sports doping rules and I can't figure out what they're actually based on. Like, an athlete can get busted and lose their medals for taking EPO, but they're totally allowed to sleep in one of those altitude tents that basically does the same thing to your blood, just slower. A shooter or archer can get laser eye surgery that gives them better than normal vision, and nobody blinks, but if there was a pill that improved eyesight the same amount it would probably be banned tomorrow. Runners got told for years that the springy carbon-plate shoes were fine (after a bit of arguing), and meanwhile a cyclist gets a two year ban for a blood transfusion. My brother says the rule is just "don't take drugs" but that can't be it, since plenty of allowed stuff is drugs -- creatine, caffeine up to a limit, painkillers so you can compete through an injury, which honestly seems way more dangerous than some of the banned stuff.

So here's my question. All these federations obviously have some idea in their heads about what makes competition fair, or what counts as "the athlete's own" performance versus something fake bolted on from outside. What is that idea exactly? Is it that there's a "natural" human the athlete is supposed to be, and anything artificial that changes the body counts against you? But then why is LASIK ok and altitude tents ok -- those are as artificial as it gets -- and why does nobody ban someone born with freakishly good genes? Or is the real idea something about effort, like the point is that results should come from training, and the tent counts as training while the injection counts as skipping the training? That almost works for me, except a transfusion is also "training" in a sense if you did the work to make your own blood better beforehand... ugh.

I'd love it if you could lay out what the rules seem to be assuming, whether it all adds up to one consistent view or whether they're just drawing lines based on gut feelings about what feels like cheating, and if there IS a consistent view hiding in there, whether it's one I should actually respect. Like if the whole thing rests on some idea of natural talent being sacred, is that idea even defensible, given that we let people fix injuries and train like maniacs? And flip it around for me too: if you took the rules seriously and rebuilt them from whatever the actual principle is, what would change? Would more stuff get banned, or would half the banned list get legalized? I keep going back and forth myself -- one day the "natural athlete" thing sounds profound and the next it sounds like nonsense from people who think vitamins from food are somehow different from vitamins in a pill.
