My dad's memory has been going the last few years, and he now basically runs his life out of a little green notebook — every appointment, every name, written down the second he learns it, and he consults it before answering even simple questions. Somewhere along the way I stopped thinking of it as a crutch and started thinking of it as, honestly, his memory: it's always on him, he uses it the way he used to use his head, and when he answers "from the notebook" it feels like he still knows the thing, the same as before. Is there a name in philosophy for the idea that a person's mind can partly live outside their skull like this — that stuff you constantly and reliably use counts as part of your thinking rather than a tool your thinking makes use of? And more importantly, where does the line get drawn? Because intuitively his notebook seems to count and my phone doesn't, even tho I lean on it for everything, and it's clearly not reliability — my phone beats my memory on accuracy hands down. The only difference I can point to is that he wrote the notebook himself and now trusts it more than his own recall, while I trust my memory over my notes even when my memory turns out to be wrong. Is that a real distinction or am I just being sentimental about a notebook? If there's an actual litrature on this, I'd like to know how it draws the boundary and whether it says anything about the trust thing — or whether my phone is in fact part of my mind and I just don't like it.
