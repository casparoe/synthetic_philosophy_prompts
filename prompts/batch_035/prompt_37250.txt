I've been keeping a list of cases where my gut says "that's just treatment" vs "now you're enhancing," and I'm starting to suspect my gut has no theory at all. Here's the list with my verdicts. Statins for someone with dangerously high cholesterol: obviously treatment. Statins for someone whose cholesterol is fine, to squeeze the risk down even further: I still can't get worked up about it, and it's technically an enhancement by any definition I've heard. Laser eye surgery to get you to 20/20: treatment. Laser eye surgery that also gets you to better-than-normal vision (I'm told this happens sometimes): enhancement, slightly weird, but fine honestly. Antidepressants for diagnosed depression: treatment. The same pill, same dose, taken by a healthy person who just wants to be a bit more resilient: enhancement, but again I don't really object. Beta blockers for a surgeon with shaky hands during operations: treatment, kind of? Beta blockers for a violinist with normal nerves who wants steadier hands for a concerto: enhancement, but fine? Ritalin for a kid with an ADHD diagnosis: treatment. Ritalin for a healthy college kid cramming for the LSAT: enhancement, and this one I actually do feel is a bit wrong, though I can't say why the surgeon gets a pass and the student doesn't. Growth hormone for a kid with a diagnosed deficiency: treatment. Growth hormone for a short but entirely healthy kid whose parents want him taller: enhancement, and I have a real squick reaction even though nobody's harmed. And then there are the ones that never even enter the enhancement bucket: piano lessons for a kid, weight training for an athlete, a tutor. Those literally enhance the kid, but calling them "enhancement" in the bioethics sense feels like a category error, and I can't explain the difference.

My tentative principle was something like: it's treatment if it restores the individual to their own normal functioning, enhancement if it pushes past that. But my own list kills it. The statin case is pushing past normal (or at least past necessary) and I don't care; the healthy-but-short kid is arguably being restored toward the population average, not past his own normal, and that's the one I feel worst about. Also normal-for-whom: aging wrecks everyone's knees and memory eventually, so is treating age-related memory loss restoring normal function (it's species-normal at 80) or exceeding it (it's below the person at 40)?

So: is there a principle that captures most of these, or a small set of candidates, stated precisely enough that I can see which clause is doing which verdict? Tell me what your favorite one says about the case I keep flip-flopping on, which is modafinil for a healthy air-traffic controller on a long shift. And be honest about which of my verdicts you'd have to throw out to get a clean theory, because I suspect the "healthy short kid is squicky but the LSAT cramming is only mildly wrong" pair, or maybe the piano lessons thing, is where my gut is just wrong. Also if the whole therapy/enhancement line should just be abandoned, say that, but then tell me what I was tracking all along, since the verdicts clearly aren't random.
