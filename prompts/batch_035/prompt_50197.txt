I'm an indie game dev — twelve years of Unity, a stint in Unreal, currently shipping a cozy colony sim — and I got into this argument with my friend who did a philosophy master's. She told me about the statue-and-clay puzzle: you sculpt a statue (the literature apparently calls it Goliath) out of a lump of clay (Lumpl), and now there's supposedly a real question about whether the statue and the lump are two things or one thing sitting exactly where your statue is. Same atoms, created together-ish, destroyed together-ish, and yet the statue can't survive being squashed into a ball while the lump obviously can. She says serious people believe there are literally two objects in that one spot. I told her that in my world this isn't even a hard problem: two GameObjects can reference the same Mesh asset, I can Destroy() one and the other is completely fine, nobody loses sleep over it. She said I was missing the point and then couldn't quite tell me what the point was.

So here's what I want. Restate the actual debate in the vocabulary I use — GameObjects, components, shared mesh and material assets, prefabs, Instantiate/Destroy, references, the distinction between what lives in the project folder and what lives in the scene — as faithfully as you can. I don't need every position on the menu, but at least the big ones: the view that they're really two distinct objects, the view that they're one object and the can-survive-squashing talk is just sloppy, the view that there's no statue at all, only atoms arranged statue-wise, and whatever the temporal-parts people say about it. For each, tell me what translates cleanly, what has to be bent to fit — I already suspect my naive mapping (lump and statue as two GameObjects sharing a mesh) is doing something wrong, because the shared mesh is the *shape*, and shape is exactly what the statue has and the lump lacks, so I might have translated the puzzle into its own mirror image — and what has no counterpart in the engine at all.

Then the residue report, which is what I actually care about. In the engine, "two objects sharing one resource" is mundane because the runtime hands out object identity for free — there's a clean level distinction between asset, scene object, and component, and nothing ever feels crowded. The philosophers seem genuinely upset that the physical world doesn't come with any such level distinction drawn. So is *that* the real content of the puzzle — the question whether reality has anything like the asset/scene/object distinction in the first place — or does my engine analogy smuggle in an ontology (handles are cheap because the engine guarantees them, matter is a resource nobody's holding a reference to) and thereby cheat? If it cheats, tell me exactly where it cheats, because I want to know whether my "this is trivial" intuition is a genuine insight or just me confusing the map for the territory.
