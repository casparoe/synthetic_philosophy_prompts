My friend and I have this long-running fight about whether philosophy ever actually makes progress. She's a physics person, so her bar is basically "predictions that got confirmed" and she thinks philosophy fails that bar by definition, which honestly feels like rigging the game before it starts. I want to push back with something more concrete than vibes, so help me build a scorecard.

Here's my draft list of candidate ways philosophy could be said to progress. Rate each one 0-10 for how much it counts as *real* progress (not just change, not just fashion), with a one-line reason. Also flag if you think I've misdescribed any of them, or if any two are secretly the same thing wearing different hats:

1. Dissolution: showing a question was confused from the start (e.g. free will turning out to be a tangle about what the word "could" means, not a missing fact)
2. Spawn-off: a subfield develops enough methods to secede and become a science (psychology leaving, logic becoming math/CS, decision theory splitting off)
3. Narrowing: even without consensus, the space of live positions shrinks — stuff that used to be a serious option stops being one, and everyone knows why
4. Articulation: getting clearer about what the question even is, so the disagreement becomes locateable even when it won't go away
5. Toolmaking: philosophy produces concepts that other fields pick up and actually use (rights, validity, the type/token distinction, supervenience...)
6. Negative cartography: mapping precisely why the tempting arguments fail, so each generation doesn't have to rediscover the same dead end

Then two overall questions: (a) if you had to compress your six scores into a single verdict — "philosophy makes progress: yes or no, and how much compared to something like medicine or math" — what's the honest answer, and (b) which of the six, if any, would my physics friend actually accept as progress, or is her bar genuinely incommensurable with all of them? She's smart and arguing in good faith, so don't strawman her.

No reading lists or name-drops just to look scholarly, I want your actual ratings and your actual reasoning.
