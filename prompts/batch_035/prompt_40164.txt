Write a set of fictional patch notes, roughly 500–600 words, for the next update to a large open-world video game, in which the studio announces that NPCs have been granted moral patienthood following an internal ethics review. Use the recognizable form of patch notes — a version number, sections such as "Welfare," "Population," "Known Issues," and "Bug Fixes" — and maintain a deadpan corporate voice throughout.

The humor must come from the actual philosophy rather than from absurdity for its own sake. Specifically, the patch notes should reveal that the developers adopted a functionalist criterion for moral status: moral patienthood is granted on the basis of behavioral complexity and pain-like states, with a numeric "sentience score" assigned to each NPC class. They should then quietly confront the standard objections this criterion raises. The argument from marginal cases should surface when the notes disclose, under a section like "Scope," that the sentience threshold also captured the crabs, the ambient wildlife, and one unusually reactive tavern door. Population ethics should break down when the notes address respawning: since NPCs return after death, the world's total welfare is recomputed each in-game day, and the studio's fix — a per-save-file welfare cap — should read as a straight-faced reductio of total-view utilitarianism. A "Known Issues" entry should concede that the system cannot determine whether NPCs despawned during fast travel continue to exist, and that the ethics team is split on whether this is a metaphysical question or a memory-allocation question.

Include at least two short quoted excerpts from "player feedback," at least one of which should be a complaint about frame rates. The satire should target the assumption that moral status can be settled by behavioral and functional criteria alone, and should never state that objection outright; a reader familiar with the animal-ethics and artificial-minds literature should recognize each joke as fair, while a reader unfamiliar with it should still find the piece readable and coherent as patch notes. Do not have the studio reverse its decision by the end.
