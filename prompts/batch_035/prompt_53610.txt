longtime lurker here (lesswrong since maybe 2013, forum.now and then, never posted much), and I've been trying to actually write down my position on wild animal suffering instead of just vibing about it in comment threads. here's the package I've converged on and I genuinely can't tell if it's coherent: (1) the aggregate welfare of most wild animal populations is negative — mostly because of r-selection, most offspring die before anything good happens to them, pain being a stronger evolutionary stick than pleasure is a carrot, all the standard stuff — so when I tally up a typical hectare of rainforest, insects included, I think suffering plausibly wins. (2) draining a wetland or bulldozing a forest for a parking lot is a serious moral wrong, and I mean a wrong in itself, not just via carbon or flood control or human aesthetics. (3) preventing an animal from existing isn't bad *for* that animal — the deer that's never conceived has no complaint, nonexistence isn't a harm to anyone. (4) our ecological predictions are unreliable at exactly the level that matters — trophic cascades, mesopredator release, the whole mess — so I'm pretty skeptical we could contracept deer herds or mess with predation without reliably making things worse. and (5) we still have a duty to reduce wild animal suffering if and when we get good enough at predicting outcomes.

Some of these feel like they're at war with each other but I honestly don't know which ones. like, (1) plus (3) seems to imply that converting rainforest to a palm-oil monoculture, which supports vastly fewer animals, might reduce total suffering — and I can't see how (2) survives that, since the same reasoning that makes the parking lot wrong should make the plantation wrong. but maybe I'm sneaking in something like biodiversity-as-terminal-value and pretending it's about welfare? and (5) feels like it dies by (4)'s hand: "intervene once you can predict the effects" but the whole point of (4) is that we can't tell when we can predict the effects. at the same time I have a suspicion some of these tensions are fake — e.g. people keep telling me (2) and (5) are "preservationist vs interventionist" and I don't think that's actually the clash, but I can't articulate why.

So: go through my five commitments, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and how much it would cost me to give it up.
