Retired biochemist here (protein crystallography, 32 years, dont ask about the beamtime). Since retiring I've been reading philosophy of mind and doing a bit of meditation, and I keep running into something in my own experience that I can't find a name for, so let me try to describe it plainly.

When I try to observe the thing that is doing the observing, I come up empty. Every content of experience — a sound, a thought, the noticing of the thought — shows up as just another thing I can look at. The looker itself never shows up on the screen. Fine so far; that could just be like the fact that you can't see your own eyes, i.e. a real thing that happens to be epistemically hidden. But here's the part I actually want a name for. The empty search doesn't feel like an empty search. When I go looking for the self and find nothing, the finding of nothing registers as "it must be just one step back", and that one step back gets filled in automatically — as if each attempt to catch the observer leaves behind a fresh set of footprints pointing at someone who was just there. And the moment I stop checking, the ordinary conviction that there's someone in here comes straight back, untouched by the failure. It's as if the checking itself generates the impression of a checker, rather than the checker being found. A needle that only exists while you hunt for it.

Concrete instances, in case that helps: (1) after coming out of deep absorption in a task — I was chopping vegetables and apparently there was nobody home for a good stretch — the sense of a continuous someone who was there all along arrives within a second or two, with no evidence, and it feels like a memory of having been present rather than a reconstruction. (2) Trying to catch the moment of falling asleep always fails in this same way: the catcher is never caught, and there's a felt "almost had it" that belongs to no one. (3) Standing between two mirrors: I can see the seeing apparatus reflected all the way down, but the position I seem to occupy from the inside is nowhere in the picture, and yet I'd swear it's the most certain thing in the room.

So my question: is there an established concept or literature for the view that the self is an artifact of the search for it — that the sense of a subject is generated by introspective checking, not revealed by it? And if the answer is that this is basically what Buddhists mean by the no-self doctrine, please tell me how it differs, because as I understand it that doctrine says there is no self to be found, whereas what I'm describing is more specific: the not-finding is what keeps producing the finding, over and over. If nothing established covers it exactly, I'd want you to coin a term and define it so it's distinguishable from (a) the trivial claim that the subject can't become an object, (b) the self-as-illusion claim, and (c) the old regress idea where explanations of seeing need an inner seer. A bare name wont help me much; I want to know where the edges of the thing are — does it apply to the sense of agency in action as well as observation, for instance, and is there a version of it that is compatible with there being some self, only one that never gets caught because it is the catching?
