My classmate Priya and I have a running argument in civics class about how votes should become seats. I keep inventing countries that elect their legislature by first-past-the-post in single-member districts and asking her whether the outcome was fair, and she has now built up enough of a record that I suspect there is a rule underneath it — but I cannot tell which, and I would like your help both working out the rule and predicting her next verdicts.

Her verdicts so far:

- In country A, the Red party won 40% of the national vote and 55% of the seats, while Blue, with 45% of the vote, took only 30%, because its voters were concentrated in districts it was winning anyway. Priya called this unfair: a party should not get a majority of the seats from two-fifths of the vote while a larger party, by votes, gets fewer seats.
- In country B, the Green party had 12% of the vote, spread evenly across the country, and finished first in no district, so it won no seats. Priya called that the correct outcome: a party that is nobody's local first choice should not be awarded seats for its national total.
- In country C, the Island party had 9% of the national vote, almost all of it in one region where it won nearly every district; it took 15% of the seats. Priya said that was fair: they speak for an actual place.
- In country D, Rivers won 46% of the vote and 58% of the seats, Uplands won 23% and 42%, and Plains, with 22% of the vote spread evenly, won no seats at all, the remaining votes going to minor parties that also won nothing. Priya said that was unfair: a fifth of the country voted for a party that got nothing.

The questions I am planning to put to her next:

- Country E: five parties. The leading one takes 34% of the vote and 48% of the seats, the second 30% and 32%, the third 21% and 14%, and two small ones 8% and 7% of the vote for 4% and 2% of the seats. Every party's seat rank matches its vote rank, and nobody holds a majority. Fair or not?
- Country F: the Coast party has 8% of the national vote, all of it in a region holding about a fifth of the country's districts, where it polls in the mid-forties but, its votes being spread thin across that region, it wins only a handful of districts there — roughly 1% of the national seats. Fair or not?
- And if the Greens of country B had 18% instead of 12%, still spread evenly and still first nowhere, would she still say that zero seats is the right outcome?

I am less interested in the answers themselves than in the rule she seems to be running. The simple answers all die on her record: pure proportional representation is refuted by country B, pure local majoritarianism by country A, and bare preservation of the parties' ranking by country D. So what are the surviving candidates, what does each of them predict for country E, country F, and the stronger Greens, and which further question would best prise the candidates apart?
