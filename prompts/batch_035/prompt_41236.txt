I'm in a reading group that has spent basically two semesters on the equality vs. priority thing — the one that traces back to Parfit's leveling-down objection — and we're stuck in a loop I'd like your help diagnosing rather than adjudicating. The setup, briefly: telic egalitarians hold that inequality is intrinsically bad, so in a two-person world where one person is worse off than the other, there's *something* to be said for leveling down, i.e. knocking the better-off person down a peg even though that helps nobody. Prioritarians say no: only people's wellbeing counts, but benefits to the worse-off count for more, so leveling down is pure loss with no redeeming feature. This has been going since the 90s, everybody knows the moves, and nobody budges an inch.

Here's what makes me suspect it's not a normal substantive disagreement. When pressed on leveling down, the egalitarians in our group retreat to the move Temkin made famous: inequality is bad, but not impersonally bad, and not bad *for* anyone — it's bad in a way that still gives you a reason, just not a reason that flows through anybody's claims. The prioritarians in the group say this is a dodge — a value that never shows up in anyone's life, never gets weighed in anyone's deliberation, and never tips any actual decision looks like a name for a intuition rather than a value. And then we're off to the races again. My working hypothesis is that the real fault line isn't about equality at all but about whether morality has to be person-affecting all the way down — the prioritarians are smuggling in "if it doesn't touch a life, it can't matter," the egalitarians simply refuse that axiom, and neither can argue the other out of it because it functions as a starting point rather than a conclusion. But I'm not confident. Maybe it's substantive and resolvable, e.g. through the trade-off cases where the two views genuinely diverge on how much weight to give health spending for the badly off, or maybe the two camps are answering different questions under one heading — one about the *source* of reasons, the other about the *weight* of benefits.

What I want from you: a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, bedrock axioms, different questions under a single label, a hidden fight about the person-affecting restriction, or something else — argued from what the sides actually say, not a survey of who published what when. Then an honest answer about whether anything could count as progress: a distinction, a class of cases that discriminates between the views, a change in the question, or an argument that no progress should be expected and why. If you think my person-affecting hypothesis is wrong, say so plainly and give me a better one. And if the honest answer is that our reading group should just stop meeting, I'd take that too, but you'd have to earn it.
