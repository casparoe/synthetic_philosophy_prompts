i'm writing a scene for my new novel where two characters argue over a restaurant menu, and i want the argument to actually hold up rather than being one of them dunking on the other. one character, a consequentialist, has quit chicken and fish in favor of beef — his line is that a burger represents a fraction of a death per serving, while a chicken breast is a whole bird killed for that one meal, so if you're going to eat meat at all, beef is the humane choice. the other is a skeptical vegan who thinks this is way too tidy. he wants to press on what the beef guy is sweeping under the rug: whether pasture and the crops grown for feed kill a lot of small animals anyway (which would muddy the per-serving death arithmetic), whether the quality of a cow's life and how it ends matters even if it's only one death, and whether any of this reasoning can be stable given how shaky the numbers on animals killed in farming actually are. write the dialogue with real back-and-forth — each of them should land genuine points and concede where the other has one, not talk past each other. they're smart people who are fond of each other, so keep it friendly, and they shouldn't resolve it neatly at the end — my editor wants the scene to close with them still holding their menus. aim for under 900 words, and please dont have either of them lecture like a textbook; they can get a detail slightly loose in the heat of the moment, but nothing flatly wrong that a careful reader would catch.
