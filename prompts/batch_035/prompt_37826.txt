I've been in this running argument with my friend Dana about vagueness for like two years, and we've finally agreed to settle it the only way I know how, which is bets — I spent way too much of my early twenties on rationalist blogs and forums so my instinct is always "if you can't tell me how you'd wager on it, do you actually believe anything." Before we actually do it I want to predict what she'll do, and I keep flip-flopping, so I want your read.

Her track record, from many conversations: she's fine saying zero hairs is bald and 3000 isn't. She insists there is no specific n where n hairs is bald and n+1 isn't — she thinks a sharp cutoff would be a bug in English, not a feature. About a specific guy, Jones, with 250 hairs, she says "Jones is bald" is not true and also not false, it's neither. But she's equally firm that "either Jones is bald or Jones is not bald" is true, and that "Jones is bald and Jones is not bald" is false. Her whole thing is that a sentence is true if it comes out true on every acceptable way of sharpening the words, and most of the sharpenings agree on the 0-hair guy and the 3000-hair guy but disagree about Jones. When I asked her the probability that Jones is bald she refused the question — said it's not like a coin flip, it's not that she's ignorant of a fact out there in the world, the sentence just lacks a truth value. When I pushed with "so 50/50, wanna bet?" she got visibly annoyed and said betting doesn't apply here, which as you can imagine did not endear the position to me.
