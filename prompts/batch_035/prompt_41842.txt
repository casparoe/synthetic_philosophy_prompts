Reading around, I found that bargaining theory has actual proposed answers, and the one I want to teach is the Kalai–Smorodinsky bargaining solution, because it's the one that does something other than split the difference. The catch, as far as I can tell, is that it only becomes interesting when the two parties' maximum conceivable gains differ: if the bargainers' ideal points are equal and the disagreement point is zero for both, it apparently just says "split evenly," which is what everyone in the room will guess anyway — so the whole lesson lives or dies on an example where that symmetry is broken. Please correct me if I've misunderstood that, because I'm working from half-digested sources.
