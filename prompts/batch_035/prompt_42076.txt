So, bit of background on me: I spent thirty-odd years as a geochemist at an oceanographic institute, mostly measuring trace metals in seawater, and now that I'm retired I've gotten properly hooked on philosophy. Currently reading my way through the identity literature and it's eating my brain. The Ship of Theseus thing especially, because it turns out I've been having versions of this argument with my old colleague Walt for decades without knowing it had a fancy Greek name.

Walt and I have fished and argued together since grad school. Over the years I've kept a mental list of his verdicts about whether something is "the same thing," and I want to figure out what rule he's actually running before I spring my new puzzle on him. Here's the record as best I can reconstruct it:

1. His 1968 Triumph Bonneville. He's been restoring it since about 1990, one or two parts at a time, always kept it running in between, and at this point literally every original part has been swapped out. He insists it is the same bike he bought in 1968. Gets actually annoyed if you suggest otherwise.
2. The river we used to fish. Obviously the same river, he says, even though every drop of water in it is different water now. Coming from a man who spent his career analyzing that water, this amuses me.
3. The diner near the lab. Original owners sold out, new owners gutted the place, changed the whole menu, kept the name and the neon sign. He will not call it the same diner. Says it's the name wearing a costume.
4. The coral reef we dove on for years, which bleached badly in the 90s and has since regrown — with, as he'd be the first to point out, almost entirely new coral. Same reef, he says, no hesitation.

Now the new case, which I haven't sprung on him yet. Last month at a swap meet a guy shows him a complete, running Bonneville assembled from original 1968 parts that match Walt's bike exactly — and it turns out this guy bought up all the parts Walt sold off over the years and rebuilt the thing. So there are now two physical candidates for "Walt's 1968 bike": the one in his garage (continuous chain of repairs, zero original material) and the one at the swap meet (all the original material, but a twenty-year gap where those parts sat in boxes in someone's shop).

What I want from you: predict what Walt will say — same bike = garage one, swap-meet one, both, or neither — and give me your reasons. More importantly, work out the candidate rules that fit his four verdicts so far. I can see at least a "continuity of process, no gaps" rule, a "best candidate wins" rule, and something kind-relative (maybe rivers and reefs just ARE things that flow, so flux doesn't count against them the way it does against a diner), but I want to know which rules genuinely fit the record and whether they diverge on the new case. And tell me what extra verdict I should try to get out of him — I'm thinking the shipwreck version, where the bike gets fully disassembled and boxed for 20 years and then rebuilt from the same parts, but you tell me which case would actually split the rules apart.

Please don't just quote me the standard textbook positions and call it a day — I've read those. I want the prediction, the rule-fitting, and the discriminating case, spelled out like you're settling a bet at the bar.
