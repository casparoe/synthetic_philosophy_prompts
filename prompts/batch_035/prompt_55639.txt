I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about hard choices, going by my gut on cases, and I want someone to tell me what principle (if any) is behind my verdicts. Take the role of a professional philosopher who works on value theory and rational choice, think carefully, maybe do some brainstorming before answering, and don't just flatter me -- if some of my verdicts have to go, say so.

The question is when a choice is hard, in the sense that neither option is just better. Here are my cases and gut verdicts:

1. Two job offers, identical except one pays 5% more. Not hard. The higher-paying one is just better, take it, done.
2. Two restaurants I like equally, similar price, same cuisine even. Not hard. It's a tie, flip a coin, nothing is lost either way.
3. Career: corporate lawyer vs. poet. The lawyer life is better on basically every dimension I can list -- money, security, repect -- except day-to-day enjoyment of the work, where the poet wins. I honestly can't say one life is better. But this one IS hard, and it's not a tie: flipping a coin would feel wrong, like a betrayal of something. It's not that either pick is fine; the choice is somehow up to me in a way the restaurant one isn't.
4. Two cities to move to: one has my friends, the other has better weather and I've always wanted to live there. I keep going back and forth on whether this is a #2-type case (tie, coin is fine) or a #3-type case (genuinly hard). This is my wavering one.
5. Whether to have a kid. Everyone says there's no right answer, but I half suspect that for any given person there IS a right answer, they just can't find it out. Not sure where this one falls.

My tentative principle was: a choice is hard exactly when the options are equal in value, and when they're equal it doesn't matter which you pick; any difference in value, however small, makes one better and the choice easy. But I can already see this doesn't fit #3 -- I don't think the lawyer is better, and I don't think it's a tie either. And the principle can't explain why a coin flip is fine for #2 but would feel wrong for #3.

So: is there a principle that captures all of these, or close to it? What does it say about #4 and #5? What does it predict on cases I haven't listed -- invent one or two, including at least one where you'd expect my gut to mislead me? And which of my verdicts, honestly, cannot be saved?
