my brother did a weekend philosophy course and came home full of it. On the train he gave me this summary of Strawson's Freedom and Resentment thing: "Strawson proved that moral responsibility doesn't depend on free will. He says blame and gratitude are just natural emotional reactions, like flinching at a loud noise — they happen to us, we don't choose them, and since they're natural they're also rational. The emotions track real desert, so even if determinism is true, people really do deserve blame in the basic sense, and hard determinism is refuted. And because the reactions are involuntary, someone who feels no resentment at all is just making a mistake about the facts. Strawson calls taking that detached stance the objective attitude, and he says it's basically a defect to be corrected by learning more."

something about this smells off to me. I've heard people say Strawson was actually pretty modest in that paper — that he never claims the reactive attitudes are justified in some deep sense, just that we can't help having them — but my brother's teacher was a professional so he's doubting me. can you go claim by claim? tell me which parts a Strawsonian would accept as roughly right, which are simplifications a defender would let pass, and which are straight misreadings. The two bits that smell most wrong to me are the "it's natural, therefore it's rational" move and the "objective attitude = a defect where you got the facts wrong" part — I thought Strawson explicitly says we can and often do take the objective attitude toward people (therapists, kids, certain kinds of offenders) and that life partly consists of doing that? Also want to know: even if everything in the summary that's accurate stays, does "hard determinism is refuted" actually follow from it, or is that conclusion being smuggled in off the back of the errors?
