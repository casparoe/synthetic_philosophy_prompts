My coworker just got a DUI — blew over the limit at 3am on a totally empty road, nobody hurt, nobody even around to see it. I told him he deserves what he gets and I don't feel that bad for him honestly. My buddy thinks I'm wrong and has an argument I can't crack, and it's been bugging me for a week.

(1) You only deserve blame for things that are under your control. (2) The only difference between my coworker and some guy who drives home equally drunk but happens to kill a kid who runs out into the street is pure luck — the kid happening to be there. (3) So those two are equally to blame, and we shouldn't come down any harder on the guy who killed the kid. Okay, fine, I can actually live with that part. But then my buddy keeps going. (4) Everything about a person — how cautious they are, whether they take risks, even the fact that they decided to drink that night — traces back to genes and upbringing and how their life happened to go, none of which they picked. (5) So by his own principle (1), even our choices are luck in the relevant sense, and nothing about us is under our control. (6) So nobody deserves blame for anything — not the guy who killed the kid, not my coworker, not a hired killer. And here's the part that gets me: (7) my buddy says that's NOT his conclusion. He insists the whole point is that the two drivers ARE blameworthy, just equally, and that this proves blame should track what you do rather than what happens after. He calls this "moral luck" and says luck is just baked into morality everywhere and theres nothing weird about that.

Something feels off but I can't pin it down. His argument for equal blame looks like it works just as well as an argument for no blame at all, and he wants the first without the second — he can't have both, or can he? Where exactly does the reasoning go wrong, and if it doesn't, is he actually allowed to just accept where it lands and keep blaming people anyway? And if I have to throw out one of the steps to save responsibility, which one is cheapest to lose? I'd rather keep (1) honestly — blaming someone for something they had zero control over feels wrong in a deep way — but I also flat out refuse to accept (6). Help me out here.
