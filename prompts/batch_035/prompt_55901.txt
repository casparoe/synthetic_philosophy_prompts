My metaphysics seminar has spent four weeks on persistence and I've come out more confused than when we started, mostly because the literature keeps arguing straight past itself. Endurantists demand that a theory preserve the plain claim that I was wholly present at my tenth birthday; perdurantists demand that a theory explain change without attributing contradictory properties to one and the same thing. But it strikes me that nobody ever writes down what a theory of persistence is actually supposed to do before declaring victory, and I suspect that when you do write it down, some of the things each side treats as obviously mandatory are really just camp shibboleths, while some genuine requirements quietly conflict. So: write the desiderata. What must any adequate account of how objects persist through time deliver -- which cases it must get right (fission, gradual replacement like the Ship of Theseus, artifacts that survive disassembly and reassembly, persons in irreversible coma), which puzzles it must dissolve or at least not worsen (temporary intrinsics, coincident entities like a statue and the clay, the indeterminacy of "how much change is too much"), and which constraints it must respect (it should not make persistence unknowable, should say something uniform enough to apply to persons, artifacts, and maybe events, and should not presuppose the answer to a question persistence was supposed to settle -- though you should tell me whether that last one, autonomy, is a real desideratum or special pleading).

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and be explicit about which are non-negotiable and which are negotiable, and why -- I care less about the labels than about the argument that, say, explaining rather than explaining away the datum of change is something every theory owes us. Then flag any pair or subset of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, since that's the part I most want to see; if, for instance, accommodating fission cases and preserving the commonsense verdict that I survive ordinary small changes pull in opposite directions, say so and say which you'd abandon. My professor is also fond of insisting that a theory of persistence must be neutral on the debate between three-dimensionalist and four-dimensionalist pictures of what objects are, or else it begs the question -- but I've never understood how a theory of persistence over time could possibly avoid taking a stand on what time-slices or stages are, so evaluate that demand honestly rather than charitably. If some desideratum does all the work of ruling out one of the major contenders on its own, identify it and explain why it's doing that much work, since a requirement that just restates one theory's ontology in disguise isn't a desideratum at all.

Finally, give a brief scorecard: how do presentism-agnostic endurantism, perdurantism (worm theory), and stage theory do against your list, with at most a sentence or two per contender per requirement where it's interesting, and noting the places where you think a contender fails a negotiable requirement but is within its rights to, versus where it fails a non-negotiable one. Don't try to be neutral about the final verdict -- if you think one picture satisfies the list outright, or that the list itself shows the whole framing is confused and the real question is about parochial criteria of identity rather than persistence, commit to that and defend it. I'd rather disagree with a definite position than be handed a tie.
