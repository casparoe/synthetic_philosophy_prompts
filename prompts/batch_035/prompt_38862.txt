I keep ending up in the same argument with a friend about whether reinforcement learning agents can suffer, and after two years we have literally never moved an inch. The setup: we both know how RL works, we can both write out the equations — a scalar reward signal, generated by whatever reward function the designer specified, and the agent updating its policy to get more of it. She says that in any system complex enough to integrate behavior over time, persistent negative reward just is negative valence — maybe a dim, insect-ish valence — so an RL agent stuck in a state where its reward is steadily negative is plausibly suffering, and the fact that it's "just a learning signal" doesn't distinguish it from pain, since for all we know pain in animals is also a learning signal that got felt. I say reward is the target of optimization, not something it is like to have: nothing in RL theory or in what we can observe about the agent says the signal is experienced by anything, so calling it suffering is a category error until someone points to a mechanism that converts signal into felt state — and she can't, and neither can I, but I say that's my point and she says it's hers.

Here's the frustrating part: we agree on every fact in the vicinity. We agree nobody knows what makes a physical system have valence at all. We agree current agents are pretty dumb. We agree that if suffering is on the table we should be careful. And yet we run this circle every couple months, and each of us thinks the other one is being stubborn in an obvious way. My working hypothesis is that we're answering two different questions under one word: she's answering "what must we count as a moral patient if we take functionalism about valence seriously" and I'm answering "what do we actually have grounds to assert today," and the phrase 'can it suffer' glues them together. But I honestly can't tell wether that's right, because she insists she's making a semantic claim too — that my refusal to call persistent negative reward 'bad for the agent' is just me protecting the word suffering for things with faces, which would make it a verbal dispute after all.

So diagnose this for me. Is it verbal, a hidden empirical disagreement about which architectures give rise to valence, a difference about who bears the burden of proof, or genuinely two questions smuggled under one heading? Build the diagnosis out of what we each actually say, not out of a generic tour of the AI welfare debate. Then tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a distinction we could adopt, a rule for splitting the question, an agreement about burden of proof — or argue that no progress should be expected and we'll be having this same argument in ten years. And please commit: pick one diagnosis and defend it. "It's a bit of all of the above" is the answer we always give each other, and it's exactly why nothing ever moves.
