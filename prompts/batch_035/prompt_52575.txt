Write a villanelle on Dennett's contrast between skyhooks and cranes, built so that the form carries the argument. The two refrains should be, in effect, the skyhook's standing offer and the crane's standing refusal: one voice promising that somewhere above the built world there is a lifter that never needed lifting, the other insisting that every actual lifting has been done by machinery assembled from below, by processes that are themselves crane-built. Because a villanelle returns its refrains verbatim, let each return gain new force from its new context: the same skyhook line should tempt differently each time it recurs, as the lure of a from-above explainer resurfaces at each frontier — design, purpose, meaning, the self. At least one tercet should give the skyhook its strongest case rather than a strawman, and at least one should show the crane doing genuine explanatory lifting rather than merely denying that anything needs lifting. The closing couplet, where the two refrains finally meet in consecutive lines, should perform the crucial discrimination: the crane's refusal must not read as greedy reductionism — nothing in the poem should suggest that the phenomena needing explanation are illusions or nothing but physics; only that no part of their explanation may be lowered from above. Begin your response with the poem itself, with no preface and no commentary of any kind. The poem should stand alone, so that a reader who knows Darwin's Dangerous Idea finds it exactly right about the distinction, and a reader who does not still finds a good poem.
