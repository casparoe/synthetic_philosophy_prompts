So our department's moral psychology reading group has been on blame this term — standing, hypocrisy, the usual suspects (Strawson, Wallace, etc.) — and next week it's my turn to circulate a one-page statement of my own position so the others can pick it apart. Before I send it round I want a sanity check on whether it actually hangs together, because I have a nagging feeling something in it doesn't. Here's the set: (1) blame is essentially address — when you blame someone you're lodging a protest against them for violating a moral demand; it's a two-place act that can be done appropriately or inappropriately, not just an emotion that happens to hit you when you witness bad conduct. (2) Hypocrisy removes standing: someone who is guilty of the same failing, and hasn't owned up to it, has no business blaming another person for it — their blame isn't false, they're just not entitled to deliver it. (3) The reason for (2) is fairness: it's unfair to hold someone to a demand you openly flout yourself; you're treating the norm as binding on them and optional for you. (4) The self-effacing critic — the one who says look, I do exactly this myself and it's bad when I do it, but you still shouldn't have done that — has full standing; their blame is completely appropriate, no asterisk. (5) Standing doesn't come from being the victim: bystanders with no personal stake can blame just as legitimately as the person who was wronged, and nobody's standing is borrowed from the victim. (6) Standing is all-or-nothing: you either have the entitlement or you don't, it doesn't come in degrees — the blame itself can be harsher or gentler, but the standing doesn't come in more or less. And one verdict on a case I keep running into: a friend of mine gave up gossiping years ago and now lectures people about it, and I have to admit something still feels slightly off in her blame, like she's entitled to say it but with a slightly thinner license than someone who never gossiped — even though she's long since reformed and, honestly, is one of the most reliable people I know.

Go through this set and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly, which ones only look like they're in tension, and for each genuine tension what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs to make it. Order them by severity rather than giving me a flat list where everything comes out equally problematic — I'd rather know which one is doing the real damage.
