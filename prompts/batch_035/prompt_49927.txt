I'm a second-year PhD student in evolutionary biology, and my upgrade report goes to my primary supervisor next week. She has a long track record, from our reading group and from her marking, of ruling on which level selection is acting at in particular cases, and I've got reasonably good at predicting her — but only up to a point, and I'd like to be surer before I hand the report over. Here is her track record as I've compiled it:

1. A segregation-distorter allele in a beetle that poisons sperm not carrying it, at some cost to the male's overall fertility: gene-level selection, full stop. She is emphatic that the organism here is a casualty of selection, not its unit.
2. Sickle-cell heterozygote advantage in humans, and male lions killing cubs sired by rivals: individual selection, no hesitation.
3. The honeybee worker's suicidal sting: colony-level selection.
4. Sentinel behaviour in meerkats — where the posted sentry turns out to be at lower risk, not higher: individual selection; she warns the class not to be taken in by apparent altruism.
5. Myxoma virus evolving reduced virulence in Australian rabbits: she calls this group selection without apology, treating the viral clone within a host as the relevant unit.
6. Wolbachia spreading through a fly population by sterilising the offspring of uninfected females: maternal-lineage selection, and she refuses to hear it called organism-level.
7. Post-reproductive female killer whales improving the survival of their grand-offspring: kin selection, which she insists is individual-level and emphatically not group selection.

My report discusses three cases, and I want to anticipate her verdicts before I sit down with her:
(a) worker policing in honeybees, where workers destroy eggs laid by other workers;
(b) bacteria in a mixed-clone biofilm that secrete the protective matrix at a cost to their own division rate;
(c) cytoplasmic male sterility in plants, where a mitochondrial gene sterilises the anthers and nuclear restorer alleles counteract it.

What I'd like from you: work out what rule or rules she is actually running. My own hunch is that at least two could fit her track record — one keyed to who benefits from the adaptation, another keyed to the level of the entities that interact and differentially reproduce, and perhaps a third on which every level with a between-component variance component counts as a locus of selection, with her verbal labels simply tracking whichever component dominates. But I don't trust my hunch, and you should feel free to propose better candidates or to drop mine. Then: do the candidate rules diverge on my three cases, and if so, what does each predict? Give your best guess at her verdict for each case, with your reasons for preferring one rule over the others as the best model of her. My report also has to propose at least one further example for discussion, so tell me which additional case would best discriminate between the rules — the one where they give different answers, so that her verdict on it would settle what she's really running.

I'd also like you to push back where it's warranted. If her verdicts look jointly consistent only because I've recorded them too charitably; if she seems to be switching criteria from case to case; or if my framing assumes a cleaner disagreement than this debate actually contains — say so directly rather than smoothing it over. A prediction I can't rely on is worse than a diagnosis that my question is badly posed. Please write your answer in British English.
