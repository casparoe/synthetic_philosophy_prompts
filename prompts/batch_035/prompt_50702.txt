I am preparing a dissertation chapter on explanations of perpetrator behavior in mass atrocities — the terrain opened by the Browning–Goldhagen dispute over Reserve Police Battalion 101, but which I want to treat as a general explanatory problem, since the same families of theory get applied to Rwandan génocidaires, Soviet shooting squads, Khmer Rouge cadres, and desk perpetrators in distant bureaucracies. Before I evaluate anyone's theory I want the specification written down first: what an adequate explanation of perpetrator behavior has to deliver, which of the usual demands on such a theory are genuine requirements and which are prejudices of one camp, and where the requirements pull against each other.

Fix the purpose as follows: the theory should be adequate for comparative-historical explanation — it must not merely fit the unit Browning studied but stretch to at least two structurally different cases — and secondarily it should be usable in forensic contexts, where courts must distinguish duress, conformity, and conviction.

Deliver this as a single JSON object with no text outside it, using the following schema:

- "desiderata": an array of objects, each with the fields "name" (a short label), "requirement" (one or two sentences stating what the theory must do), "motivating_case" (the concrete phenomenon that makes this a requirement — for instance, that men who refused to shoot at Józefów suffered no serious sanction while refusal elsewhere was lethal; or that the Holocaust's killing moved toward secrecy and industrialized methods while Rwanda's killings were public spectacle; or that killing frequently depended on the local knowledge of the victims' own neighbors), "status" (one of "non-negotiable", "camp-preference", "negotiable"), and "status_reason" (one sentence). Include at least one desideratum no camp normally thinks to demand — for example, that the theory explain not only that people participated but the form participation took (pulling the trigger versus requisitioning trains versus denouncing a neighbor), or that it be consistent with perpetrators' post-war reintegration into ordinary life without supposing they had been monsters all along.
- "joint_tensions": an array of objects, each with "subset" (a list of desideratum names) and "tension" (why those requirements strain against each other or cannot be jointly satisfied — e.g., fine-grained within-unit variation against cross-case uniformity, or ideological specificity against portability to cases with different ideologies). If you believe some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, say so plainly; that would itself be a result.
- "strongest_excluder": an object with "name" and "why", identifying the single desideratum that does the most to knock out existing contenders.
- "contender_check": an array of three objects — a situational/conformity account in Browning's family, a cultural-ideological account in Goldhagen's family, and a character/dispositional account — each with "passes" and "fails" (lists of desideratum names). Keep this lean; the thinking belongs in the criteria, not the scorecard.
- "out_of_scope": an array of strings naming what the theory should not be required to explain (for instance, the initiation of a killing program as opposed to participation in it), so the desiderata do not quietly become a theory of everything.

Do not cite literature or suggest readings; the specification should stand on the phenomena alone.
