Two people in my novel-writing group, Marta and Dan, got into a long argument after our meeting last week about whether readers ever actually feel anything for characters, and I couldn't referee it. Marta writes very spare, craft-obsessed fiction; Dan writes doorstop family sagas. It started because Dan said he cried for two days after finishing a novel where the protagonist dies, and he meant it as a compliment to the book. Marta said that's impossible on its face — he knows the woman doesn't exist, and you can't feel real grief for someone you know isn't there, so whatever he felt, it wasn't grief for her. Dan said the tears were real and the thoughts running through his head were about her — what she'll never get to do, how her sister will take it — so Marta is telling him he doesn't know what his own emotions are about. Marta came back with the kid-playing-cops-and-robbers comparison: a child shrieking during the game isn't afraid of a robber, she's caught up in a game of being afraid, and adults reading novels are doing the same thing, just with more vocabulary. Dan said kids' games don't wreck your week — if his reaction were mere make-believe, why does he steer clear of rereading books with sad endings, the same way he avoids topics that actually hurt? Real avoidance for a pretend feeling makes no sense. Marta's last move was that Dan's emotion is real but its object is wrong: he's responding to the execution — the sentences, the pacing, the structure — and the character is just the handle he hangs that admiration on, which is why he cries at well-written deaths and not badly written ones. Dan said that's exactly backwards, because the moments he's aware of craft are the moments the feeling evaporates — noticing technique and being moved are mutually exclusive states for him, so the feeling can't be about the technique. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right at each step, and whether there's something both of them are missing? For what its worth we're all novelists, so I'd actually like to know which of them I should believe when a reader tells me they sobbed over a person I invented.
