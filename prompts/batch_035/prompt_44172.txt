Quick thing I keep running into: people say "strategy-proof" like it's one single concept, but it seems to be doing pretty different work in the voting-theory literature versus the mechanism-design-with-money literature. Can you compare strategy-proofness as it lives in the Gibbard–Satterthwaite setting — social choice functions, ordinal preferences, at least three alternatives, unrestricted domain over rankings, no side payments — against dominant-strategy incentive compatibility in the VCG setting, where agents are quasilinear and the designer can charge them money?

In what ways is the voting version special? What I'd really like is your actual take on where the impossibility comes from: is it the missing transfers, the restriction to ordinal reports, the unrestricted domain, or some interaction between those? As a concrete anchor so we don't float off into abstraction: with money, I can allocate a single indivisible good among bidders using a second-price auction and truth-telling is a dominant strategy; without money, the same-looking problem with three friends ranking three restaurants seems condemed to either dictatorship or manipulability, as long as every ranking is possible. So which is it — does VCG somehow escape the Gibbard–Satterthwaite logic, or were the theorem's assumptions just never satisfied in that setting to begin with?
