Here's the question as it appeared on the exam:

"Special relativity is often said to force a 'block universe' on us. Present the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for this claim as carefully as you can, making explicit the role played by (i) the relativity of simultaneity and (ii) the transitivity of the 'real for' relation. Then say whether the argument, if its premises are granted, establishes eternalism; whether it establishes determinism; and whether anything of importance hinges on the relative speed of the observers involved."

And here's the student's answer, verbatim:

"In special relativity there is no fact of the matter about which spatially separated events are simultaneous — simultaneity is relative to a frame. Observers moving relative to each other carve spacetime into different 'nows': each inertial observer has a plane of simultaneity, and these planes tilt as the observers' relative velocity changes. So suppose Alice is standing still and Bob cycles past her, and somewhere in a galaxy two million light years away a star goes supernova. In Alice's frame the supernova is still in the future; in Bob's frame it has already occurred. Since no frame is privileged, there is no saying that the supernova 'hasn't happened yet' full stop. Now, what is real for an observer is whatever lies on or below her plane of simultaneity. On that understanding the supernova is real for Bob but not yet real for Alice. Add a third observer, Carol, at rest with respect to the distant galaxy, for whom the supernova is simultaneous with her own present. Alice's present is simultaneous with Carol's present, and Carol's present is simultaneous with the supernova, so by the transitivity of simultaneity the supernova is simultaneous with Alice's present — it is real for Alice after all, even though it lies in her future. Iterating this for every observer and every event, we get that all events, past, present and future, are real for everyone: the block universe, i.e. eternalism. Moreover, since all future events are already real, they are already fixed, so our future choices are determined — special relativity delivers determinism. Finally, on the observers' speed: the tilt of the plane of simultaneity scales with velocity, so the argument only really bites when the observers are moving at relativistic speeds. At everyday speeds the disagreement about what is real is so small as to be negligable, which perhaps explains why the block universe strikes us as counterintuitive."
