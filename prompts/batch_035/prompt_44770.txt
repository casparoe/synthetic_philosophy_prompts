I'm a PhD student in cognitive science, working on intertemporal choice (how people discount future rewards, present bias, that kind of thing), and lately I've been reading into money pump arguments. Here's my understanding: if your preferences are intransitive — you prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A — a trader can cycle you around, skimming a little off you at every step until you're bled dry. And the same style of argument gets extended to dynamic consistency: if you plan to choose X tomorrow but know that tomorrow-you will want Y, there's a gap between your plan and your predictable behavior, and a patient trader can make money in that gap — sell to you today based on what you say you'll do, buy it back cheap tomorrow based on what you'll actually do.

So it seems to me the general lesson is: rationality means locking in your preferences, because any revision over time opens a pump-entry point. Which would mean the rational agent treats preference change as such as a threat — never update, never re-rank, and if you've already chosen in some domain, refuse further trades there on principle.
