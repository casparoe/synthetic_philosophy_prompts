For context: I've been a longtime reader of the rationalist corner of the internet — the Sequences, the big blogs, the forums, years of comment threads — and I now co-organize a small monthly in-person meetup that grew out of it. Attendance skews autodidact: software engineers, a couple of grad students, and a lot of people who learned essentially all of their epistemology from blog posts and are rather proud of that. Next month I'm running a ninety-minute session on the dogmatism paradox — roughly, that you can't acquire new knowledge from a reason unless you already know enough to recognize it as a reason in the first place, so learning from scratch looks impossible. Plato's Meno is the ancestral statement, with the slave-boy geometry lesson as the classical response, and the modern literature mostly refines the problem rather than dissolving it.

My worry is that this audience will treat the paradox as a word game that Bayes solves on contact — "start with a prior, update on evidence, done" — and a few will have run into the idea of epistemic learned helplessness and think the paradox is just that familiar feeling of no longer being able to distinguish a crank's math proof from a correct one. It isn't that, and I need them to see why.

Please don't explain the paradox to me; plan how I should teach it. Specifically: what has to be established first, before the paradox will register as a real problem rather than sophistry; the one example that should carry the whole session — I'm torn between the subtlety-wrong-math-proof case and something like a newcomer deciding whether an unfamiliar field's literature deserves trust, so give me your reasoning about which to use and what that example must contain and must avoid; the misconceptions this particular audience will form, in the order they'll form them — the just-use-Bayes reflex, the slide into reflexive skepticism, and the move I most fear, reading the paradox as only a defeater for deferring to experts while leaving their own first-principles ideal untouched — and where in the sequence to head each one off; what I should explicitly decline to cover at this level and say so; and one closing question that will let me distinguish the people who actually got it from the people who are pattern-matching to something they've read.
