I'm taking a phil of mind course and my turn to run discussion section is coming up (14 students, 75 min). We just did the dream unit — Malcolm's old line that dream reports can't be reports of experiences had during sleep, because a sleeper can't observe anything about their own sleeping, so anything you could check a dream report against is only available after waking, which makes the whole idea of experience-while-asleep conceptually confused. Then Dennett's 70s reply that this is actually an empirical question, not a conceptual one, with his alternative model where the "dream" gets composed/selected at the moment of waking instead of being played out in real time — and the point that the ordinary dream report is compatible with both models, so the report itself can't settle it.

My idea for section: a game where the students feel the underdetermination instead of me just asserting it. Rough sketch — half the class are "sleepers" and half are "sleep researchers," but I want some of the sleepers to genuinely have an experience in real time while it's happening (they're awake in another room doing something, or given something to experience) and other sleepers to confabulate the whole thing only at the "waking" moment, with the researchers never knowing which group any report came from. The researchers get to question, compare, run little tests, whatever — and the goal for them is to figure out whether any given report was experienced-when-reported-as-happening or invented at the end. If I've understood the unit right, the punchline I want them to hit themselves is that it can't be done from the reports alone, until somebody in the room invents the move of a pre-agreed signal sent *during* the supposed experience — which is basically what the lucid dreamers did with the eye-movement thing decades later, but the students shouldn't know that till the debrief. Also nobody should hear the name Malcolm or Dennett before or during the game.

Can you design this for real? I need: rules and materials (no special equipment, we just have the seminar room, index cards, my laptop), how to split the 75 minutes, how the confabulating sleepers should be briefed so their reports are convincingly dream-like and not obviously fake, what the researchers are allowed to ask/know, and a debrief that maps what actually happened onto the distinction (conceptual-impossible vs empirical-and-settled) without turning into me lecturing for 20 minutes. Also please tell me honestly what will go wrong the first time I run it — my guess is the confabulators will be too easy to spot, or the researchers will get bored — and whether there's some part of the actual philosophical dispute that this setup just cannot reproduce no matter how I run it, so I know what I have to cover myself in the last five minutes. One more thing: I don't want it to feel like a psych experiment, the "sleepers" should have a decent time too, not just sit around waiting to be interrogated.

(ps my prof said our writeups of section can't just describe an activity, they have to say what conceptual work the activity does that a lecture couldn't — so if you could note where in the game the students are doing something a lecture listener definately wouldnt be, that'd help me out a lot.)
