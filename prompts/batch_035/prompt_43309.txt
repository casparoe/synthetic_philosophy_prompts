I've been trying to figure out why I actually like sad stuff, by going through cases where I do and don't enjoy it, and I want to know if there's a principle underneath my gut reactions. Straightforward one first: I can watch a tragedy, cry my eyes out, and immediately want to watch it again -- loving every minute. But when my friend spent two hours telling me about her divorce, I was miserable the whole time and never once felt the urge to "experience that again." Same with reading about a famine in the paper: nobody wants more of that feeling. So far so obvious, art vs life.

But now it gets messier. I cried at a documentary about a real plane crash and rewatched parts of it that night. Real people, real grief, and I still enjoyed it in a way I clearly didn't enjoy my friend's divorce story. Is it that the strangers are distant? That doesn't seem right either, because I once binged a long youtube video essay about a hiker who died alone, basically savoring it, and I feel weird about that one honestly.

Some more cases. I love melancholy music when I'm already sad -- wallowing feels good, almost nourishing. But when I was actually depressed a few years back, sad music didn't work at all, it just felt like nothing, like pressing on a bruise. And engineered sadness annoys me: those movies that are transparently just engineered to make you cry, with the dying kid and the dog, I roll my eyes even when they get me. But then an honestly-made sad film can be about the exact same stuff and I'm all in. Also, I enjoy bittersweet stuff more than purely devastating stuff, but a tragedy that ends with total loss still feels worth it in a way a merely unpleasant film doesn't -- so it's not just that the enjoyment comes from some counterweight of hope.

And one more wrinkle: I enjoy re-reading a sad novel more the second time, when I know exactly what's coming. Which seems weird if the pleasure has anything to do with suspense or being surprised into feeling something.

So: is there a principle that captures all of these? The candidates I can half-articulate are something like "distance" (fiction and distant strangers are safe to feel at) and something like "control" (I can put the book down but not my friend), but the documentary case strains distance, and the depression case strains... something else, I'm not sure what. And the engineered-tearjerker one strains neither, really, or maybe it does? Please tell me which principle, or set of principles, best fits my verdicts, state each part precisely and say which case motivates it, and be honest about which of my reactions it just can't accommodate -- I suspect the plane-crash-documentary enjoyment is going to have to be thrown under the bus, and I'd like to know if that's really necessary. Also, whatever account you land on, tell me what it predicts about a case I haven't mentioned: watching a sad play performed by community actors who are clearly amateurs, where you know nobody involved is good at their craft but somehow it still lands.
