Can you write me a dialogue between two philosophers stuck at the conference bar after the session on meaning got cancelled? One is a Quinean who thinks the indeterminacy of translation runs all the way down — for her, there's no fact of the matter about what a foreign word means beyond what the totality of speakers' dispositions to assent fixes, and even that leaves room for multiple manuals that fit all the behavioral evidence equally well. The other is a Chomskyan who thinks the poverty of the stimulus shows there's rich innate structure, so all languages are cut from the same cloth, and a decent field linguist converges on the right translation once the data gets rich enough — what Quine calls indeterminacy is just ordinary lexical ambiguity plus missing data, and it dissolves.

Don't let them argue in the abstract. Give them a concrete case to fight over: a field linguist's notes from a recently contacted people. The notes record that the natives say gavagai when a rabbit runs past, but never when a rabbit stands still eating; that they use a different word, bogoi, for rabbit pelts and also (the linguist thinks) for clouds shaped a bit like a rabbit; and that when shown a photograph of a rabbit they assent to gavagai only once someone makes the photo slide across the screen. The Quinean wants to say the notes prove her point: the evidence underdetermines whether gavagai tracks whole rabbits, running rabbit-events, rabbit-phases, or something like rabbit-motion, and any further test you propose — pointing, counting (one gavagai, two gavagai), holding up a rabbit at different speeds — can be accommodated by a manual that quietly reinterprets the pointing and counting behavior too. The Chomskyan thinks this is banal polysemy plus sloppy field method: cross-check with syntactic distribution and compounding, ask about counting, and the competing hypotheses will collapse to one.

Make them actually engage with each others specific points, not talk past each other. In particular the Chomskyan owes an answer to why the counting test isn't itself open to the defiant-reinterpretation move, and the Quinean owes an answer to why innate syntax doesn't already constrain the semantics enough to kill the radical underdetermination — she can't just assert that syntax is neutral. Neither should be a strawman; each should land at least one real hit that the other has to work to answer. Let it end without a clean winner — ideally they end up agreeing (maybe grudgingly) that the real fight is over what counts as a fact of the matter in the first place, and adjourn for another round. Somewhere between 600 and 900 words. Casual register is fine — they're at a bar, not giving the Locke Lectures.
