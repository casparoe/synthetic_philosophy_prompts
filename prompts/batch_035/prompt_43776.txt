I keep a blog about animal welfare with a mostly effective-altruism-adjacent readership plus a handful of skeptical animal scientists who read it to argue with me. My next post is on the scale argument for insect welfare: even if you put the probability that insects are sentient pretty low, there are so many of them — farmed cricket and black soldier fly operations are already in the billions per facility, and wild insect numbers are just absurd — that the expected amount of suffering at stake arguably swamps everything we currently spend on vertebrate welfare. I want a decision tree readers can click through that sorts them into the position they actually hold, and I want it built from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names: figure out the questions that genuinely split people, and only then say which known position (if any) lives at each leaf.

The distinctions I care about for this post, roughly: how you're allowed to combine a small probability of sentience with huge numbers (straight expected-value multiplication, some risk-weighted or discounting scheme, or a threshold below which you just refuse to count it — and I'd like a branch for the person who says the probability isn't small at all given the anesthesia-and-analgesia-response evidence), whether moral weight per individual scales with anything like neuron count assuming sentience, whether wild insect suffering is in scope or only farmed, and whether the practical upshot shifts with the answers (funding insect welfare research vs. reforming farms vs. wild-population stuff like mosquito abatement, which cuts both ways since some people think fewer insects is better and others think that's insecticide-brained thinking).

Branch questions should be concrete cases a reader can answer without knowing any of the literature — e.g. whether to keep spraying a mosquito-abatement program that kills billions of larvae a year, whether to fund a pilot that rearers fewer crickets per container even though it means fewer insects alive, whether you'd take a pill that makes the wasp writhing on your windowsill painless if you knew it cost the same as one hen's cage upgrade. Leaves get the position name if one exists, otherwise 'unnamed' plus a sentence on why nobody's there — is the combination incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored? Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate.

Keep it to seven or eight questions and I'll turn it into an actual click-through quiz. And since I'm basically going to publish whatever you write: at the end add a note on how confident you are in the leaf placements and which one you're least sure about — I'd rather hedge honestly in the post than get taken apart in the comments by the entomologist who emails me every time.
