been reading LessWrong and adjacent blogs since around 2014, and next month I'm giving a talk at an AI safety fellowship workshop. Audience is maybe 25 people: mostly ML grad students, a couple of econ/policy think-tank types, and one person who does evals work at a frontier lab. My thesis: the "alignment tax" is mostly not a per-run cost you pay at training time — it's a fixed R&D cost paid early, under uncertainty about whether the approach will even pan out, and then it gets amortized so that by deployment it looks like a rounding error (which is why "RLHF is like 1% of a training run, what tax?" arguments mislead — they're measuring the marginal cost on top of everything that already got paid). Second claim: the tax is regressive. It lands hardest on actors with the least capital and the least slack, so it doesn't just slow alignment down, it selects for whoever can eat the cost or ignore it entirely — which means the competitiveness constraint is really a claim about market structure, not about anyone's virtue. Corollary I want to land by the end: the lever is changing the payoff matrix (monitoring, liability, conditional compute access), not exhorting labs to do the right thing.
