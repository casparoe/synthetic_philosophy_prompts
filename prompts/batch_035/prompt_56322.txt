I'm in a grad reading group on the cognitive science of religion, and we just did a week on whether religion is an adaptation or a byproduct, and we basically split down the middle and argued till the restaurant closed. I want to see how this debate actually goes when both sides know the other's literature well, so here's what I'm asking for.

Write a dialogue between two researchers seated next to each other at a conference dinner right after their joint panel (they're friendly but a little territorial). One holds the byproduct view: religion as a spandrel that piggybacks on cognitive machinery that evolved for other jobs — agency detection, intuitive mind-body dualism, memory for minimally counterintuitive concepts — with no need for religion itself to have been selected for at any level. The other holds the adaptationist view, at least for the big organized religions: moralizing high gods, costly ritual, and hellfire beliefs spread because the groups running them outcooperated their neighbors, which is cultural group selection doing real work past the ceiling that kin selection and reciprocity hit.

Both should be competent and neither gets strawmanned. Specifically I want the byproduct person to press on whether cultural group selection is "selection" in a sense that explains anything or just a relabeling of "some institutions spread because people liked them," and the adaptationist to press on whether "byproduct" quietly smuggles in a baseline where selection conveniently stops at the individual skull. And they should genuinely struggle with the awkward middle cases rather than each just claiming them — small-scale shamanic traditions with costly ritual but no moralizing high gods, or the fact that the byproduct machinery seems to produce ghosts and forest spirits everywhere but full-blown doctrinal religion only in some places. If they can, let them figure out how much of their disagreement is empirical (what would settle it?) versus just disagreement about what counts as an explanation. No citations or reading lists, just the argument. Also its fine to have them get sidetracked once over wine, but bring them back.
