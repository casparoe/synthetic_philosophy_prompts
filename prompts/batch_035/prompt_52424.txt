I'm an ML engineer on the team that monitors a deployed LLM (mid-size assistant, tens of millions of queries a day). We run the usual anomaly detection stack, but the question that keeps killing us in triage is this: when a detector fires, is the model doing the same thing it did in training, just on weird inputs, or is it genuinely doing something else — some mechanism that wasn't exercised (or didn't even exist) during training? Our escalation policy is built on that distinction — "mechanistic anomaly detection" is only as good as the notion of same mechanism underneath it — and right now we settle it by vibes.

My background is engineering but I got nerd-sniped by the philosophy side of this, and my read is that "same mechanism" is basically an identity question, like personal identity: you can individuate a mechanism by what it does (causal role, the analogue of psychological continuity) or by where it came from (training history, the analogue of bodily/origin continuity), and cases like circuit reuse, grokking, or continued-training reorganization are exactly where the two come apart, the way fission cases come apart for persons. I want a procedure that makes that identity choice explicit instead of pretending it's obvious.

Write me a decision procedure my triage teammates (smart, no philosophy background, about an hour per alert) can run: five numbered questions to ask in order — no intro, no conclusion, just the list — where each item is a sentence or two giving the question, what counts as a pass/fail/escalate, and where it punts to a human because it's a values-ish call about individuation rather than an empirical one. Somewhere in the five, deal with the hard cases: gradual circuit reorganization after continued training (ship-of-Theseus for mechanisms), one mechanism getting reused for a new off-distribution behavior (the fission case), and a benign-seeming flag that still has to count as a genuine mechanism change. Also include a case where the procedure gives an answer I won't like — a jailbreak-looking behavior that reads as same-mechanism under the procedure, so it says dont escalate — and be honest about what the procedure can't settle at all. Keep it casual, I'm pasting this into our runbook basically as-is.
