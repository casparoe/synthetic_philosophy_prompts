Write a dialogue between an epistemicist and a supervaluationist who both serve on a regulatory committee that must define "small business" for the purposes of a new tax exemption. A sorites-style worry hangs over their deliberations: any headcount threshold seems vulnerable to the argument that hiring or firing a single employee never makes the difference between small and not-small. The epistemicist holds that there is a fact of the matter about exactly which firms are small, even if no one knows it; the supervaluationist holds that many firms are neither determinately small nor determinately not. Their philosophical disagreement should have practical consequences in the dialogue: they should disagree over whether the final text should stipulate an arbitrary sharp cutoff, leave a discretionary zone to administrators for borderline cases, or be drafted so that it comes out true under every admissible precisification of "small." Both characters should be equally competent, and each should engage directly with the other's arguments rather than talk past them. Near the end, have them take up the question of what a court reviewing a borderline firm would be doing: discovering a boundary that was already there, or creating one.
