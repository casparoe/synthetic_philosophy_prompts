quick one before I let this stuff influence how I actually spend my money and time. I've been reading about evidential cooperation in large worlds (ECL) -- the idea being roughly: agents who can't causally interact at all, maybe in other galaxies or whatever, but whose decision procedures are similar to yours, should act as if they're in a coalition, because your choices are evidence about their choices, so you can effectively trade influence over each others' values without ever communicating.

before I decide whether to take this seriously as a guide to real decisions like donations or career, I want the spec first. write me the desiderata: what an adequate account of this kind of cooperation has to deliver, and the failure modes it has to rule out. some things I keep worrying about: the cutoff problem (people slide from "cooperate with agents similar to you" to "cooperate with everything remotely mind-like" which seems to burn all your local influence, and I can't find a principled line), whether the account has to reduce back to ordinary decision theory when the world is small or correlations are weak, how it handles uncertainty about who even correlates with you, and whether there's a double-counting worry when everyone in the coalition is doing the same reasoning at once. for each requirement, say what motivates it, and flag any pair of them you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- that's the part I care about most. no need to score existing proposals against the list, just the requirements.
