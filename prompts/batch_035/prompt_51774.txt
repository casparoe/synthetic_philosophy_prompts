My spouse and I have spent the past month deciding whether to try for a child, and after a colleague brought up Benatar's asymmetry I've found it harder to dismiss than I expected. As I understand it, the view rests on four claims: the presence of pain is bad; the presence of pleasure is good; the absence of pain is good even when no one exists to enjoy that absence; and the absence of pleasure is not bad unless there is someone who is deprived by that absence. Together these make the situation of someone who never exists look better, at least pro tanto, than the situation of someone who does. Please rate each of the four claims from 0 to 10 for plausibility and rank them from most to least secure, saying in a sentence or two what each score turns on. My spouse also proposes a fifth, symmetrizing claim of her own: that absent pleasure is bad in the same impersonal way that absent pain is good, so that the two absences simply cancel out. Rate that claim too, and tell me whether, if it were accepted, the asymmetry would collapse entirely or merely weaken. Finally, given your scores, state plainly how much weight you think the asymmetry deserves in a decision like ours relative to the more ordinary considerations—finances, health, our own wishes for parenthood—that are also on the table.
