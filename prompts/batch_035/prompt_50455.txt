I've been going through my gut verdicts on side-effect cases to figure out whether my word "intentionally" tracks anything coherent, and I want a principle that fits them, or to know which verdicts have to go. A CEO who launches a program to boost profits knowing it'll wreck a nearby wetland, and doesn't care: he harmed the wetland intentionally. Same CEO, same program, except the side effect is that it happens to clean up the wetland, which he also doesn't care about: he didn't help it intentionally. So far so Knobe. But now: a middle manager who knows a reorg will gut a colleague's pet project as a side effect, personally hates that it will, says so in the meeting, votes no — and the reorg passes anyway. I say she did NOT harm the project intentionally, even though she foresaw it and it happened. Then the weird ones. My roommate reads my diary without permission, and what she learns is that I'm about to make a terrible financial decision, which she quietly blocks with the bank; I'm grateful but I still feel she intentionally violated my privacy — even though the privacy violation was in service of something good. And a pharmacist who quietly undercharges struggling customers at small cost to the chain: I'd say she's intentionally helping them, even though helping wasn't what she was assigned to do.

Is there one principle — maybe something about who violated a norm, or what the agent is on the hook for versus deserves credit for — that gets all of these, including the manager case, which keeps bugging me? And whatever principle you land on, tell me its verdict on a case I'm stuck on: a landlord who never bothers raising rents to market because paperwork bores him, and tenants save hundreds a month as a result — did he intentionally help them? I'd also like to know which of my verdicts your favorite principle can't save, and whether the honest conclusion is that "intentionally" just isn't one thing.
