Two friends of mine got into it at a party after watching some documentary about the nature of time, and they eventually drafted me as referee, but honestly I lost the thread halfway through. Maybe you can sort it out.

So the setup: the documentary ended on the block universe idea, and Maya said relativity basically settles it. Her move (1) was the Andromeda thing: two people walking past each other on the street, moving at different velocities, will disagree about which events on a distant galaxy count as happening "now" — for one of them the alien council meeting is happening right now, for the other it's still days away, and if what's real is what's happening now, then what's real is relative to who you are. Since reality can't be relative like that, she said, the sane move is to say no event is privileged — past, present, and future events are all equally real. Eternalism. Done.

Dev wasn't having it. His move (2) was that relativity is a theory about coordinates, not about what exists. Simultaneity, he said, is like lines of longitude: the equator being "zero" is a convention we imposed, and the fact that different map projections distort Greenland differently doesn't mean Greenland's actual shape is up for grabs. The two pedestrians aren't discovering different realities on Andromeda, they're just using different labeling schemes for one and the same world.

Move (3): Maya said the analogy fails, because longitude conventions are obviously human inventions, but the spacetime geometry of relativity is objective — the simultaneity slices of different observers genuinely carve up the manifold in different, equally well-defined ways, and each carving is built into the actual geometry. So the presentist is stuck: either pick one slicing as the real one, which relativity says you have no right to do, or admit all the slicings' events are on a par, which is just eternalism wearing a hat.

Move (4): Dev said she's sneaking in an assumption — that "present" means "simultaneous with me in some frame." Why should the presentist accept that? Maybe the now isn't a slice of spacetime at all but something more like the word "I": a purely indexical fact about where in the world you happen to be standing. Physics textbooks don't contain the referent of my use of "I" either, but that doesn't make it unreal. The present could be like that — real, located, and simply not the kind of thing that shows up in a spacetime diagram.

Move (5): Maya said that makes presentism empirically idle — a "now" that no experiment could in principle detect, hovering outside physics like a ghost in the machine. At that point, she said, she can't even tell what distinguishes Dev's view from eternalism plus a decorative arrow pointing at one event and saying "this one!" Dev replied in move (6) that this cuts both ways: if eternalism's only selling point is that it plays nice with relativity, then presentism's support was never going to come from physics in the first place — it comes from how time feels. We dread tomorrow's dentist appointment in a completely different way than we dread yesterday's, and we can't make sense of that if all events are eternally on a par. Maya shot back that the brain-in-a-block will of course have all its memories and anticipations baked into it, so the felt difference proves nothing.
