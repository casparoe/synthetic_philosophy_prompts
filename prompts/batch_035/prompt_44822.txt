I write long-form features on science and ideas, and I'm at work on a piece asking a question that sounds simple and turns out not to be: which country deserves to be called the more liberal, Sweden or Norway? My sparring partner for years has been a friend who teaches political theory in Bergen — a Norwegian, which makes her answer more interesting — and she has issued a series of verdicts I now know by heart.

Asked flat out, she says Sweden, and not as a close call. On the economy, Sweden again: the reforms of the 1990s — school choice, the pension overhaul, the abolition of inheritance and wealth taxes — make it, in her view, the more liberal state, whereas Norway still taxes wealth and runs much of its economy through the state and the oil fund. On asylum and immigration, above all around 2015, Sweden: cosmopolitan openness is part of what she means by liberal. On church and state, Sweden, which separated from its Lutheran church in 2000, while Norway only loosened its church's official status in 2012 and still names it in its constitution. On speech, Sweden, whose free-press statute goes back to 1766.

Twice, though, she has gone the other way in ways I can't fully square with the rest. On gender equality she credits Norway, whose 2003 corporate-board quota she calls a liberal law, not an infringement — Norway got there first, and she counts that as liberalism. And when Norway criminalized the purchase of sex in 2008, following Sweden's 1999 law, she said the ban was the liberal side of that debate too, which surprised me.

I want to close the piece by putting three test questions to her, and I'd like your predictions before I do. First, homeschooling: Sweden's 2010 education law made it essentially illegal outside rare exceptions, while Norway permits it with notification and municipal oversight. Which country will she call more liberal there? Second, gambling: Sweden broke up its monopoly and licensed commercial operators in 2019, while Norway still channels everything through state monopolies. Which will she call more liberal? Third, holy-book desecration: if Sweden, after the Quran-burning turmoil of 2023, ends up banning public desecration while Norway leaves it legal, will she say Sweden has surrendered the title, or that protecting religious minorities is the liberal position?

So tell me: what rule, or weighted bundle of rules, is she actually running? What other rules fit her track record just as well? What does each of them predict on my three questions, and which of the three would best tell them apart — or, if none does, what further case would? Give me your best guess on each answer with your reasons, and, since my editor wants the piece to have a conclusion, tell me what evidence could genuinely settle which country is the more liberal, or whether the question only has an answer relative to a definition.
