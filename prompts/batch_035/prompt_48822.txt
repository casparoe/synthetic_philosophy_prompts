So here's my situation: I recently got asked onto the advisory board of a small family foundation that funds both SETI-style listening campaigns and origins-of-life research, and the board is split into two camps that have been arguing past each other for years. One side is basically rare-Earth: the chain from abiogenesis to a technological civilization is so contingent that we're probably alone or nearly alone, and the Fermi silence isn't a paradox, it's just what you'd expect. The other side says the Copernican point still stands, life is probably common where conditions allow, and the silence is explained by detectability limits, timing, or distances rather than by our rarity. Before we announce the next funding round, the chair wants a joint position statement we can all actually sign, and I've been tasked with drafting it. I want to do it properly, not produce both-sides mush. Can you write that document? Specifically: (1) the claims both camps can sign -- about the number of potentially habitable planets, what we genuinely do and don't know about how life starts, and what the null results so far do and don't show; (2) the exact disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording of its own view -- I suspect one dispute is whether "are we alone?" means any life vs. technological civilizations, and I'd want you to say whether that part is just verbal; (3) the cruxes, the sub-questions where settling them would actually move at least one side -- e.g. whether transitions like the one from simple cells to complex ones are inherently rare or just happened to be rare here, and whether decades of hearing nothing carries real Bayesian weight given we don't know how long civilizations broadcast; and (4) for each crux, a concrete finding or observation both camps would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance so we can check later. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a table for the disagreements. And keep it something a real person from each camp would put their name on.
