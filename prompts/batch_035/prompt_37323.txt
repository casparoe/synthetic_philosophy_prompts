As a novelist, I've grown tired of fiction that uses its characters as mouthpieces for a worldview, so I'm commissioning a piece and want it to do the opposite. Write a short story, at most 1,200 words, about three adult siblings who learn that a moderately popular podcast has devoted an episode to mocking their recently deceased father — a minor public figure the host accuses of having plagiarized part of his one remembered work. Each sibling responds according to a different logic: the eldest treats it as a stain on the family's honor that must be answered directly, with reputation restored or lost in the confrontation itself; the middle sibling treats the accusation as either true or false, a matter of record that calls for evidence and, if warranted, a formal correction, with the insult itself beneath notice; the youngest treats the episode primarily as a harm done to real people, to be named publicly so that third parties — listeners, the platform, other descendants of similar slights — can acknowledge it. Render each stance through concrete choices, dialogue, and what each sibling counts as the matter being settled, without the narrator weighing in on which is wiser or braver, and without any sibling conveniently failing. Ideally the story should leave a reader unsure which, if any, of the three would be at peace afterward, and why.
