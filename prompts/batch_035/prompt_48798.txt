Two members of my philosophy-of-science reading group — Tomas, who works in machine learning and joined us from the statistics department, and Aditi, a philosopher of science — have been circling a disagreement about Goodman's new riddle of induction all term, and it finally came to a head over coffee after our session on projectibility. They went through about six moves before deadlocking, and they asked me to referee, which I honestly could not do. Here is the exchange as accurately as I can reconstruct it.

(1) Tomas opened by saying the riddle is an artifact of neglecting compression. The hypothesis that emeralds are green is a shorter description than the hypothesis that they are green if observed before 2050 and blue thereafter, so any learner with a simplicity prior — minimum description length, or Solomonoff induction — always prefers green to grue; the riddle is just Occam's razor restated, and Occam's razor is not a puzzle but a working principle.

(2) Aditi replied that description length is relative to the language of description. In a language with grue and bleen as primitives, green comes out as the disjunctive, time-indexed gerrymander, and MDL now favors grue. So compression doesn't solve the riddle, it relocates it into the choice of primitives — which, she noted, is close to Goodman's own diagnosis.

(3) Tomas said the primitives aren't arbitrary: our perceptual categories are the product of a very long evolutionary history of inductions that had to pay off in survival, so relative to the equipment we actually have, green really is simpler, and there is no standpoint outside that equipment from which to lodge the complaint. An alien with grue-primitives is welcome to its projections; it will simply be surprised.

(4) Aditi said this either concedes the point or begs it: if simplicity is relative to our contingent machinery, then "project the simpler hypothesis" is no answer to the skeptic, just a description of what we cannot help doing; and if the appeal is to the past success of that machinery, it is induction vindicating induction. She added a counterexample to the idea that time-indexing and disjunctiveness are themselves the poison: she examines every coin in her pocket today and finds each was minted before 2020; the hypothesis that every coin that will ever be in her pocket was minted either before 2020 or after 2030 is time-indexed and disjunctive, yet inspecting today's coins seems to confirm it. So some grue-shaped hypotheses are confirmable, and the mark of the bad ones must be subtler than their logical form.

(5) Tomas answered from the engineering side: the invariance theorem makes the choice of universal machine a mere additive constant in description length, so grue-projections lose eventually no matter the encoding; and learners that keep losing get edited out by the world.

(6) Aditi closed by saying the constant is the whole game for finite agents: an overhead of even a few hundred bits can swamp every bit of savings available in a human lifetime of emerald observations, and "eventually" is a theorem about infinite data that nobody who dies gets to use.

Please adjudicate move by move: rate each of the six from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with a brief justification of each rating; then say who is more right overall; and identify anything substantial that both of them missed. Lead with your bottom-line verdict in the first sentence, then give the reasoning — no preamble and no restating of the exchange.
