So I'm an ML engineer at a mid-size lab, no philosophy background at all, but somehow I ended up on the team that reviews proposed training runs before they get compute. And lately people keep asking me, half-joking and half-not, whether our RL agents can suffer. Last month a run had the policy taking big negative rewards every time it explored into a "bad" part of the state space, someone said "rip buddy" in slack, and it turned into a whole multi-day thread that went nowhere. My manager has now asked me to make something real out of it: a decision procedure for flagging runs that might involve something suffering, that any engineer on the review team can run in about an hour as part of normal pre-run review. Not a philosophy paper — a checklist with actual stopping points.

Here's what I think I've picked up from reading around (correct me where I'm wrong): the reward signal itself is just a number, and whether it *feels like anything* presumably depends on other stuff — whether there's persistent internal state that tracks rewards across time, whether negative and positive reward get processed the way pain and pleasure are in animals, whether the agent has homeostatic variables it keeps in range versus just maximizing a scalar, whether it models itself, sheer model size, things like that. I have no idea how to weigh any of this against the rest, and honestly some of it might be junk reasoning.

So write me the procedure: the questions to ask in order about a proposed run, what counts as a concerning answer to each, where the procedure terminates with "no flag, ship it," and where it has to punt to the ethics people because the question is a values question and not something an engineer can settle. Be upfront about which steps are basically vibes dressed up as a checklist — I'd rather know that than pretend it's all rigorous. Also say what would need to be true about a run for the procedure to misfire badly in each direction (flagging harmless stuff, or waving through something bad).

Then run it on three cases: (1) a standard game-playing agent with reward shaping that takes large negative rewards during exploration; (2) an RLHF'd LLM being trained on a big batch of rejected responses; and (3) an actual proposal someone on my team floated — an agent with a persistent "deprivation" variable that decays over time unless the agent earns reward, so that early in training it sits in a chronically negative internal state until it figures the task out. The idea is this speeds up learning alot. That third one is the case I actually care about, and I have a feeling the procedure is going to say flag it, which will be annoying because it genuinely does speed things up — so if that's the outcome, tell me whether the flag means "don't do it" or "do it with changes," and what the changes would even be.

Last thing: tell me what the procedure can't do. Like if the whole framing of "reward as valence" is just wrong — reward is a training signal and valence is something else entirely — where does that leave the checklist? I want to know how much of this is real structure and how much is me needing to feel like I did due diligence.
