I'm taking a sociology of religion course, and we've just finished a unit on Weber's Protestant ethic thesis. I have an objection to it that I'm quite attached to, but I've only ever tested it against people who already agree with me, so I'd like to try it against a serious opponent. Please play a committed Weberian: historically literate, willing to concede that Weber overstated particular claims or got particular regions wrong, but absolutely unwilling to give up the core thesis that values and meanings can be independent causes of economic behavior — not just reflections of it. Stay in character until I say "stop," and keep each reply to a single paragraph so it feels like a real conversation rather than a lecture.

Here is my opening move; I call it the orphaned-habit problem. Weber's story needs the inner logic to do the motivational work: salvation anxiety, a doctrine of predestination, the search for proof of election in disciplined worldly success. The work ethic gets its grip from the theology. But consider a community — think of a fishing town founded two centuries ago by an ascetic sect — where the doctrine has been dead for two generations: nobody believes in predestination, the church is essentially a heritage museum, the ministers are功能aries of a secular bureaucracy. And yet the behavior persists: long hours, low consumption, a vague guilt around idleness, children raised to treat leisure as suspect. The materialist says this is exactly what we should expect: the habit now reproduces itself through upbringing, local expectations, and plain network effects — ordinary social transmission, with no values doing any causal work. The "culture" was just the seed, and once the belief died, the meaning stopped mattering. If values were genuinely the cause, the behavior should have decayed when the belief did. So you have two options, and both look bad for you. Either you can tell me what causal work a value nobody holds is still doing in this town — which sounds like admitting that "culture" is doing no work at all, just an inherited mechanism wearing the culture's clothes — or you accept that in your own paradigm case, culture was only ever a one-off historical spark, a cause that fires once and then hands everything over to mechanisms that need no concept of meaning. Which is it?
