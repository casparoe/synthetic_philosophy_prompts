Our reading group has spent the better part of a year on whether Malthus was refuted or merely reprieved, and the argument has deadlocked in a way I want diagnosed rather than adjudicated. The refutation camp points to the demographic transition, the collapse of fertility in rich countries, and the Green Revolution: every unconditional forecast of population pressing against the margin of subsistence failed, and that, they say, is what falsification means. The vindication camp answers that Malthus described a mechanism, not a timetable — economic historians find real wages pinned near subsistence for centuries before industrialisation, populations did expand to the margin, and the escape through innovation and fertility control is a contingent reprieve, so he was right about the mechanism and wrong only about timing. One member, trying to break the deadlock, proposed formalising the theory as a conditional: population tends to press against subsistence whenever technology is roughly static and fertility is uncontrolled. On that reading, industrialisation falsified nothing; it merely changed the antecedent. That struck me as suspiciously easy — any theory can be rescued by retreating to conditionals whose antecedents never obtain.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? My working hypothesis is that it is not about the demographic facts at all — both sides accept the history — but about how to individuate a theory: whether 'Malthus's theory' names the bare mechanism, the mechanism plus typical background conditions, or the unconditional forecast, and hence about what 'falsified' and 'right but early' mean. Is that right, or is a substantive empirical disagreement hiding in there, perhaps about whether the mechanism still operates and would reassert itself if technological growth stalled? Diagnose the impasse using what each side actually claims, then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a canonical formalisation, a case in which the rival individuations of the theory yield different predictions, a distinction between retrodiction and prediction, or an argument that the question is ill-posed and no progress should be expected. Please state how confident you are in each part of the diagnosis, and single out the point about which you are least sure.
