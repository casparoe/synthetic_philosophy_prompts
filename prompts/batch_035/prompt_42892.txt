I teach a bioethics seminar and every year we do a unit on consent, and every year I realize my own gut verdicts don't line up into anything like a theory, so this time I'm just going to lay them out and see if you can find the principle underneath, if there is one. Surgeon doesn't mention that a resident will do half the operation under her supervision: consent invalid, the patient needs to know who's cutting. Someone says I love you purely to get someone into bed: sleazy, but I find myself saying the consent is still valid, which my students tell me I can't have both of. Signing a consent form while in wracking pain that only the procedure can relieve: valid, I think — the pain is pushing you but it isn't anyone coercing you. An employee sleeps with her boss knowing he decides the promotions, and both of them know she'd say no if he didn't: I used to call that coercive and now I honestly waver. Tipsy yes after two glasses of wine: valid. A yes given while blackout drunk: invalid. But a yes given by someone who is sober but in the grip of a manic episode, who at baseline would never: invalid too, and I can't say why that one's clear to me when the wine one is clear and the middle cases aren't. Threat to end the relationship unless she sleeps with him: valid, I want to say, it's a rotten thing to do but you're allowed to leave for any reason. Same ultimatum but the threat is to reveal her immigration status: invalid. So the coercion cases split by what's threatened, not by the pressure itself, is that right?
