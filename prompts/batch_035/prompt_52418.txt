I've been reading about how psychiatric diagnoses change over the decades, and I've noticed a pattern I can't find a name for. When doctors settle on a diagnostic category, the category doesn't just passively sort people — it changes them. Patients read about their diagnosis, join communities organized around it, start interpreting their moods and habits through it, and over time the population carrying the label looks different from the population the label was originally written for. Doctors then notice the mismatch and revise the criteria, which changes who gets diagnosed, which changes the patients again, and so on. It's a loop: the classification molds the people, and the people force the classification to move. I know about self-fulfilling prophecies, but those seem to be one-directional — a prediction makes itself true and the story ends there. What I'm describing goes around and around, and it isn't even a prediction, just a category that keeps reacting to its own effects. Is there an established concept for this in the philosophy or history of psychiatry? If there is, I'd like to know exactly what it covers and where it stops — for instance, does it also apply to non-medical labels like criminal profiles or personality types, which are meant to describe rather than treat? And if no single term exists, what are the nearest ones, and what does each of them leave out?
