ok so this is kind of a heavy one but I'd rather ask someone who can actually think it through instead of just my group chat.

I'm Dutch. My grandfather was a young conscript in the colonial war in Indonesia in the late 40s — the one we usually call the police actions, even though that's not really the right name for the whole thing. He basically never talked about it and now he's dead. Recently my cousin got contacted by a community group of Dutch-Indonesian descendants. They're organizing a memorial event and they'd like family members of veterans to say a few words there, and my cousin wants me to be the one who speaks because I'm supposedly "the articulate one" in the family.

My first draft was something like "we are sorry for what we did." My dad hit the roof when he heard that. He says it's insane: I wasn't born, he wasn't born, and even his own father was a 19 year old shipped over there half against his will, and you can't inherit guilt like a debt. Nobody is guilty of something they didn't do, period, becuase that would make the word "guilty" meaningless. And honestly I can't find the flaw in his argument. But then when I imagine standing up there and saying only "the Netherlands is sorry" — pushing it all onto some abstract entity like nobody in particular ever did anything — that also feels like a dodge? Like there's something I'd be sliding out of and I can't put my finger on what it is. Whether that something is guilt, or shame, or duty, or just awkwardness, I genuinely don't know.

So my actual questions: when I'm standing up there, who is the "we" that should be apoligizing, if any? Is there anything I personally owe these people — and if so, owe in what sense, since it can't be guilt, right? And is there a real difference between "my country did this" and "we did this," or is one just more honest and the other more false comfort?

One more thing, and this matters to me. I've gathered that people who've actually studied this kind of question disagree about some pretty basic stuff — like whether a nation can even be the kind of thing that bears guilt, versus it just being individuals all the way down. So when you answer, can you clearly separate the parts that are more or less settled among people who know this area from the parts that are a genuine live debate? Don't pick a side in some ongoing argument and present it like it's just the consensus view. If the honest answer to "should I say we" is that people who know this stuff genuinely split on it, I'd rather hear that than get a confident line to read out.
