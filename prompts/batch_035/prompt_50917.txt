I hold that the therapy/enhancement distinction does no genuine moral work. Wherever it is invoked — against stimulants for the healthy, against germline edits aimed at raising children above baseline, in worries about lifespan extension — the real work is, I have come to believe, done by something else: risk, distributive fairness, pressure on people to intervene in order to stay competitive, doubts about the voluntariness of enhancements performed on children. Hold all of that fixed, and it makes no moral difference whether an intervention restores a lost function, maintains typical function, or lifts a person above the species-typical baseline. Tell me what would count against this. I don't want the standard wheelchairs-and-mood-brighteners exchanges; I want the view's exposure mapped. Specifically: are there cases in which risk, fairness, and coercion really are held fixed and intuitions still pull apart across the therapy/enhansion line — parents refusing growth hormone for a hormone-deficient child versus parents refusing it for a merely short one, or Deaf parents declining a cochlear implant for a deaf child where they would not dream of declining, say, cataract surgery — and if the intuitions do pull apart there, what exactly is doing the pulling? Relatedly: if one anchors "therapy" in species-typical functioning — Boorse's statistical account, or Daniels's use of normal functioning to explain why healthcare has a special claim on institutions — does that give the distinction non-circular moral content that my view is committed to denying, or does the anchor inherit its plausibility from the same fairness and opportunity considerations I claim are doing all the work? I'd also like to know which of the apparent counterexamples people on my side of this would concede as genuine costs rather than redescribe, and whether the position has been quietly qualified into unfalsifiability — every divergence in intuitions dismissed as a hidden difference in risk or justice. If it has, is that a defect, or just what one should expect of a reductionist claim?
