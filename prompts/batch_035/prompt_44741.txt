I'm drawn to David Pearce's abolitionist project as set out in The Hedonistic Imperative (1995): that involuntary suffering in all sentient life is a remediable genetic glitch, that biotechnology can rewrite the hedonic treadmill so that no one's well-being ever dips below zero, and — the part I find most distinctive — that motivation and information-signal value can be preserved entirely through gradients of super-well-being, with the raw negative valence of pain and dysphoria dropped out while its signaling function is retained. I've come to hold this view, and I want you to tell me what would count against it.

Give me the defeaters, ranked from fatal through serious to merely awkward. In sorting them, please attend to the three separable commitments the view actually makes: (1) an empirical-architectural claim that negatively valenced states are not functionally indispensable — that aversive phenomenology, as distinct from nociception and error-correction, can be genetically decoupled from the systems that do the work of steering an organism; (2) a normative claim that abolishing suffering outweighs whatever is lost in the redesign, including changes to the texture of human ambition, the meaning of achievement, and our attitudes toward mortality; and (3) a feasibility claim that this can be rolled out across all sentient life, not just willing humans, including wild animals and any descendants we engineer. Say for each defeater whether a committed abolitionist would concede it as counting against the view or absorb it with a qualification — for instance, if the evidence that suffering is constitutive of certain forms of learning or of self-transcendence came out a certain way, would Pearce's own defenders treat that as a cost to be weighed, or would they redescribe it as a cost of the old architecture we're well rid of? And be honest about whether the view, as its defenders currently run it, has been insulated into unfalsifiability: is there any conceivable finding in affective neuroscience, behavioral genetics, or moral psychology that its proponents would accept as refuting it, and if not, is that a defect in the view or just what it means to hold a fairly comprehensive ethical-technical program?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — your overall verdict on where the view is most exposed — then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restating of my question.
