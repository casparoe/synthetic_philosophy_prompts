ok so I'm writing up my philosophical profile on slur semantics for a language & ethics reading group — the idea is everyone brings their package of views and the rest of us poke at it. I've got mine below, and honestly I have a feeling something in it doesn't hang together, but I can't for the life of me see what, so I want a proper audit rather than encouragement. Here's the package:

(1) The derogatory content of a slur is semantic, not pragmatic — it's conventionally encoded in the word itself. What sold me on this: you can't cancel it (tacking "but no contempt intended" onto a slur doesn't work the way cancelling an implicature does), it still derogates under negation and inside conditionals, and a speaker who sincerely belives the word is just a neutral group term still counts as slurring.

(2) At the same time the derogation never touches the extension. A slur is true of exactly the same people as its neutral counterpart — you can ask whether someone is one, that's a genuine question about group membership, and you can answer yes while rejecting the contempt entirely.

(3) All slurs carry one and the same derogatory component. The only semantic difference between any two slurs is which group the neutral part picks out.

(4) Reclaimed uses are different at the level of the word, not just in context. In the in-group dialect the reclaimed term is genuinely non-derogative, and it's got a built-in warmth to it — you could not get the same effect by saying the neutral term in a warm tone of voice. Thats actually why I think it's the word that changed and not just the usage.

(5) Which dialect a token belongs to is fixed by the speaker's own linguistic community, never by the audience. So an in-group speaker's friendly use isn't slurring, while a well-meaning outsider who picked the word up from in-group music still slurs however friendly his intent — his community hasn't given him the dialect.

(6) And I think the fact that some slurs are much harsher than others is a fact about the words themselves — stable across speakers, contexts and tones — not a fact about who happens to be talking.

My main worry is that (1) and (2) flatly contradict each other (how can content be semantic and yet leave the extension completely untouched?), though I have a nagging feeling that one might dissolve on inspection, so don't just pattern-match to "semantic = truth-conditional".
