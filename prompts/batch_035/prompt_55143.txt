I help run a reading group that's half math majors, half philosophy majors, and we just finished a semester on the axiom of choice. For the last session I want a 'which stance on AC do you actually hold?' quiz -- not which axioms you *use*, but what you think is *true*. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with 2-3 answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto named positions.

Cases I want in there (pick and choose, add your own if they're better): Banach-Tarski; whether a non-measurable set existing at all is a cost; every vector space having a basis, including over horrible fields; the reals being well-orderable; picking one sock from each of infinitely many pairs of genuinely indistinguishable socks (the shoes version is trivial since you can say 'take the left one', the socks version is where it bites); and this dart thing, which I want as one of the questions: throw countably many darts at random at the unit interval -- for any single dart, the chance it lands exactly on one of the countably many earlier darts is zero, so a natural symmetry argument says every dart misses all the earlier ones, and that claim contradicts the reals being well-orderable, hence contradicts choice. (I don't care whether the argument is any good; I care whether people side with 'the symmetry intuition wins' or 'probability intuitions about uncountable sets are junk'.)

A few structural demands:

- Every question has to be answerable without knowing what any position is called. It sorts by what people say about cases, not by self-labels. No 'do you believe in the cumulative hierarchy?' style questions.
- Build the tree out of the logical space first, then locate the positions in it. The axes I care about are: (a) whether statements independent of ZF have a determinate truth value at all, (b) whether consequences -- nice ones like Zorn's lemma, ugly ones like Banach-Tarski -- can count as evidence for or against an axiom's *truth*, as opposed to merely its usefulness, and (c) how much choice someone will trade away: full AC, dependent choice, countable choice, none. Leaves should include: the garden-variety ZFC Platonist, the 'choice is baked into the iterative conception' crowd (if that's even a distinct leaf -- flag it if you can't seperate it from the first one), Hamkins-style multiverse views, the formalist/pragmatist 'axioms are tools' stance, constructivism, someone who takes determinacy (AD) seriously enough to give up choice, and the Solovay-model 'all sets of reals measurable' sympathizer. If some combination of answers maps onto no real position, say so and guess whether that's because the combo is incoherent or just unexplored.
- Flag at least one pair of leaves your quiz can't actually seperate. My suspicion is the pragmatist and the multiverse person answer most of these questions the same way, but I'm honestly not sure, and I'd rather you told me straight than fudged it.

After the scoring key, add a short closing note on where you're confident and where you aren't -- in particular, single out whichever mapping from answer-pattern to leaf you're least sure actually matches what defenders of that position would say, since I plan to test the quiz on our one actual constructivist, and if it sorts him wrong he will never let me hear the end of it.
