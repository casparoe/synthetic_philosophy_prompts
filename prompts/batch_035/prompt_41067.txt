I'm in a graduate reading group on higher-order theories of consciousness. Last week, discussing Rosenthal, the question came up of what the first-order state contributes: on Rosenthal's picture, the qualitative character of an experience is fixed by its first-order content, while the higher-order thought is what makes the state conscious. One member cut the discussion short by invoking the transparency of experience, sometimes called its diaphanousness. His line was that when you try to attend to the character of your experience you slide straight through to the world — look at a blue sky and try to attend to your experience of it, and you find only the blue, nothing at the second order to inspect — and that this shows the higher-order machinery does no work and answers a question introspection has already closed.

I'm confident transparency rules something out, but I doubt it rules out everything he wanted, and I suspect different readings of the one-liner do very different amounts of work. Could you lay out, as a numbered list I can bring back to the reading group, the distinct precise claims the transparency slogan might be expressing — for instance as a claim about what attention or introspection reveals, as a claim about what exists in an experience over and above its worldly content, and as a claim about what fixes an experience's character or what makes a state conscious at all? For each reading, say whether it is plausible, whether it is substantive or close to a platitude, and — this is the part I most need — whether it actually supports the use my groupmate put it to: does it show higher-order theories false, or show only that they owe an account of something, or show that they must agree with the first-order representationalists on one point while still disagreeing on another? Please flag which reading his dismissal genuinely requires, and indicate where in the space of higher-order views — thought theories versus perception theories versus same-order theories — that reading bites hardest and where it does not bite at all.
