Since retiring after a long career in physical chemistry, I have been working through the philosophy of physics with more diligence than training, and I have joined a science-and-philosophy reading circle. We recently read Eddington's The Nature of the Physical World (1928), the book in which he coined the phrase 'time's arrow'; he there instructs us to draw an arrow arbitrarily, and says that if, as we follow it, "we find more and more of the random element in the state of the world," the arrow points toward the future — adding that this "is the only distinction known to physics." Our discussion leader has since adopted the slogan 'entropy is time's arrow' as a kind of master key. Last week a member asked whether, in the far future of the universe — near heat death, when local downward fluctuations of entropy would be nearly all the structure left — there could be stretches in which time literally runs backward. The leader waved this off with the slogan: where entropy decreases, that just is the direction we call 'the past', so no epoch can run backward and the question is confused. I was not satisfied, for two reasons. First, I spent decades watching entropy decrease locally — in crystal growth, in refrigerators, in every system that dumps heat elsewhere — without ever suspecting that time was running backward inside my sample. Second, I understand that the weak interactions violate time-reversal symmetry outright, which sits oddly with Eddington's claim to have identified the only distinction physics knows.

So rather than another aphorism, I would like a careful disambiguation. Please lay out as a numbered menu the distinct precise claims that 'entropy is time's arrow' could be expressing — for instance, though you need not limit yourself to these: (i) the entropy gradient merely correlates with, and thereby tracks, an independently existing direction of time; (ii) entropy increase conventionally fixes which direction we label 'past' and 'future', the convention being anchored to a de facto cosmic gradient; (iii) every time-asymmetry we encounter — memory only of the past, causation running one way, radiation spreading outward rather than converging, omelets that do not unscramble — is explained by, or reducible to, the entropy gradient; (iv) the direction of time is constituted by the gradient, so that in a region where entropy genuinely decreases, time itself would run the other way; (v) some weaker epistemic claim to the effect that the gradient is simply our most reliable indicator of which way time goes. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, substantive but false, or outright confused; whether it survives the time-asymmetry of the weak interactions; and, most importantly, whether it would actually support our leader's dismissal of the backward-time question. If some readings would vindicate his ruling while others would not, say which reading he needs in order to rule as he did — and whether, equipped with that reading, he can still tell the far-future fluctuation apart from an ordinary crystal growing on a cold window, since both are local decreases of entropy sustained by increases elsewhere. I would like the list in a form I can circulate to the circle before our next meeting.
