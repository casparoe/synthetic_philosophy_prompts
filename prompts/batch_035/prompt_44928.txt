A friend and I argued late into the night about whether our individual choices matter, and he left me with an argument I can neither refute nor accept.

(1) An act of mine is wrong — at least insofar as harm to others is what makes it wrong — only if my doing it makes a difference to whether some harm occurs. (2) A single purchase of chicken makes no difference to whether chickens are raised and slaughtered for food: producers set volumes well in advance and adjust output in batches, so there is almost never a threshold waiting for my purchase to tip it, and when a tip does occur, it is as likely to be triggered by a stranger's abstinence as by my purchase. (3) The same holds, he says, for one person's carbon emissions, one vote in a national election, and one signature on a mass petition. (4) Therefore none of these acts is wrong, and no one who refrains from them has done anything morally creditable either.

Then came the coda that gives me vertigo: the reasoning is unchanged as we scale it up. Even if a hundred million people are each emitting, eating meat, and staying home on election day, each of their individual acts is exactly as non-decisive as mine, so (4) is true of every one of them. Hence, as he cheerfully put it, the resulting collective harms — a destabilized climate, billions of animals, an electorate that stops showing up — are nobody's doing: a harm produced by a hundred million acts is a harm produced by no one's act.

I would like to keep (1), or at least keep something in the neighborhood of the thought that morality tracks what you do to the world rather than what happens in nearby worlds you cannot distinguish from this one. But I suspect the cheapest-looking escape is denying (2), and I suspect that escape is a trap. Tell me which step I should reject, why that step looked innocent, and what rejecting it costs — including what I must then say about small-probability cases: the vote that happens to create an exact tie, or the donation that happens to complete a funding threshold. And tell me whether his scaling coda follows from the rest or smuggles something in, since if it follows, the argument licenses not just my dinner but the entire pattern of which my dinner is one instance.
