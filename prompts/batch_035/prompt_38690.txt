"The economist Kenneth Arrow proved that when voters are choosing among three or more options, there is no way to combine their ranked preferences into a single ranking for the group that satisfies a short list of conditions any fair system would obviously meet. Those conditions include unanimity — if every voter ranks A above B, the group ranking must rank A above B — and independence of irrelevant alternatives, which says the group's ranking of A versus B must depend only on how voters rank A versus B, not on their views about some other option C. The theorem's punchline is that every ranked voting system, including pairwise majority rule, must break at least one of these conditions, so the pathologies we usually blame on poor system design are actually baked into the mathematics.
