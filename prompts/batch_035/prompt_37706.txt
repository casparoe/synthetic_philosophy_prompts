For an upcoming workshop on machine minds, participants have been asked to circulate a short statement of their position in advance so the others can challenge it. The topic assigned to me is whether AI systems can literally have beliefs. Below is the position I intend to send, stated as a list of commitments. I would like an audit of it before I do.

1. Predictive indispensability. For large language models, the intentional stance is not merely convenient; it is the only predictive instrument we have that works at scale. I cannot see how anyone could forecast a system's outputs across novel contexts without modeling it as an agent with beliefs and goals, and I expect this to remain true as systems grow more capable.

2. The instrumental gloss. Following Dennett, I understand what the stance licenses as instrumental attribution rather than discovery of inner facts: to say the system believes that p is to say that it is reliably predictable as a rational agent whose belief set includes p. Attributing belief this way neither presupposes nor reports an inner state.

3. Realism about animals. I am nonetheless confident that my cat has beliefs, and that she had them before anyone took any stance toward her. Whatever makes her beliefs real, it is not our predictive practices.

4. Determinacy. Whether a given AI system believes a given proposition is a factual question with a correct answer — the same kind of question as whether my cat believes the door is shut. "Belief" does not shift its meaning between the animal case and the machine case.

5. The relevance of interpretability. If probes were to isolate, in a network's activations, a stable, causally efficacious structure corresponding to "Paris is the capital of France" — one whose disruption selectively degrades Paris-related outputs — that would settle that the system really believes that proposition. This is the kind of evidence I take the question to turn on.

6. Unity as a constraint on the concept. On any adequate account, believing that p requires more than producing p-congruent outputs in the context where p came up: a believer should be disposed, across contexts, to infer and act in ways that would be sensible if p were true. Current models seem to me to meet this bar increasingly well, and I expect mature systems to meet it fully.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look to be, ranking the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. For each, say what would have to give and what that revision would cost elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single revision, if any, would clean up the most. Finally, consider whether my real problem is a commitment I have failed to list — something the six of them jointly presuppose. Do not assume I am attached to any particular one of these; I genuinely do not know where the trouble lies, and I would rather find out now than during the workshop.
