I am preparing a position statement on the concept of disease for a seminar, and before circulating it I would like a consistency audit of the commitments I plan to defend. They are as follows.

(1) Disease is an objective, value-free matter: a disease is a subnormal functioning of some biological part or process, judged relative to what is statistically typical for the species, adjusted for age and sex, and this judgment is as factual as a measurement of kidney filtration.

(2) The statistical standard is anchored to the species' design rather than to bare frequency. If degeneration of the joints somehow became universal in the human population by age sixty, it would still be a disease; and, by the same token, aging as such is not a disease — only its discrete failures, such as cataracts or osteoarthritis, are.

(3) The concept of disease is in no way dependent on social evaluation. The nineteenth-century view that enslaved people who repeatedly escaped suffered from a mental illness, and the former psychiatric classification of homosexuality, were straightforward factual errors about biological function, not value judgments gone wrong; and no shift in a society's values can make a disease of something that is not one, or unmake one that is.

(4) No condition is a disease unless it is bad for the person who has it. A benign anatomical variant — a harmless extra renal artery, say, or a mirror-image arrangement of the organs discovered incidentally — is not a disease but mere variation, however statistically rare.

(5) Unexplained infertility in an otherwise healthy thirty-year-old woman is a disease, even when no examination identifies any mechanism performing below par.

(6) While disease facts are value-free, classificatory practice legitimately shifts with practical circumstances — for instance, severe short stature in an otherwise healthy child becomes an officially treated condition once growth hormone therapy exists — and this does not strike me as the corruption of medicine by values.

Please tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and, for each genuine tension, what would have to give and what giving it up would cost, ordered by severity rather than presented as equally serious. If any commitment is confused, or if the audit cannot be carried out as posed, tell me so directly rather than smoothing things over. Please keep your response under 700 words.
