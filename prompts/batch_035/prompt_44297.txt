Freud died in 1939, well before cognitive science of religion was a thing. Suppose you could sit him down and brief him on the main findings that have come out since: that humans seem to have a hair-trigger for detecting agents and attributing minds and purposes everywhere, that many researchers now treat religious belief as a byproduct of ordinary cognition rather than a wish-fulfillment illusion, and that religion also didn't fade away in rich secular countries the way he'd predicted. What do you reckon he'd keep from The Future of an Illusion and what would he actually concede? Would he dig in and say the cognitive stuff only explains the *form* the illusion takes, while wish-fulfillment explains why we cling to it — or would he treat the byproduct story as a genuine blow to his account? And seperately, what would he make of the fact that therapy culture basically won the culture war while religion just kept going anyway?
