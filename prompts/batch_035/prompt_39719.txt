Long story short, I have until Friday to submit a dissertation fellowship application and only the germ of an idea. The germ: everyone treats the paradox of phenomenal judgment as either a knockdown argument against epiphenominalism or just a bullet to bite. The argument, as I've had it put to me — if phenomenal properties are causally inert, then my sincere utterance that something looks red to me was produced entirely by my brain, with no help at all from the redness itself, so in what sense is the utterance about the redness? I keep thinking the whole thing turns on treating a judgment about an experience as one unanalyzed notion, and that if you split it into the token event in my head, the content that event expresses, and possibly a third thing like the epistemic standing of the subject, then some ways of stating the paradox turn out harmless and the ones that don't get sharp enough to actually evaluate.

I need a two-page research proposal out of this. It should have: the question stated precisely enough that it could in principle come out either way, a section on why the standard responses (bite the bullet, go interactionist, deflate the qualia altogether) don't settle it, a month-by-month method for what is purely an analytic philosophy project — no lab work, just conceptual analysis and thought experiments about inverted spectra and the like — an account of what would count as the project failing, and an honest note on whether twelve months is realistic. Skip the bibliography, I'll fill in citations myself, and frankly I don't trust anyone not to invent them.
