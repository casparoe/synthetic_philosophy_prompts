I have been reading around Moore's paradox — the puzzle Wittgenstein heard Moore present to the Cambridge Moral Sciences Club in 1944 and later discussed in Part II of the Investigations — and I have come to suspect that the literature on it has stopped making progress, and I'd like a diagnosis of why. As I understand the terrain, one camp treats the phenomenon as living entirely at the level of assertion: a token of "It's raining, but I don't believe it" is perfectly consistent and can be plainly true, and its absurdity is a pragmatic inconsistency between what is said and what assertively saying it implies about the speaker's beliefs. The other camp holds that the sentence reveals something about the mental: on something like the transparency or self-intimation picture of Moorean belief, first-person present belief is constitutively bound up with what is true, so the conjunction is genuinely unbelievable by the subject, not merely unsayable. Both camps have stable counterexamples to the other and neither seems to have moved position in a long time.

My working hypothesis is that much of the impasse is a disagreement about the explanandum. The pragmatics side takes the datum to be the felt absurdity of the assertoric utterance, while the philosophy-of-mind side takes it to be something about the impossibility of coherently believing the conjunction, and if that is right, the two camps may not be disagreeing at all — just explaining different data under one name. But I am not confident of this, because they do contest shared ground: whether the omissive and commissive forms ("It's raining but I don't believe it" versus "It's raining but I believe it isn't") are absurd in the same way, whether the conditionalized form ("If it's raining, I don't believe it") is as bad as the conjunctive one, and how to accommodate the fact that the third-person past-tense analogue ("It was raining, but he didn't believe it") is perfectly fine. There is also the blindspot literature treating Moore sentences as one species of a wider genus of sentences that are true but not assertable by a given speaker, which makes me wonder whether the Moore-specific debate tracks a natural kind at all.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, really — verbal, a clash over which phenomena count as data, two projects under a heading, or a genuine substantive dispute that is merely hard? What would count as progress given your diagnosis, and is there anything in the disputed cases that could decide between the camps, or at least force them onto common ground? And please push back where warranted: if my two-camp framing misconstrues the debate, if the explananda I distinguish are not really distinct, or if the question cannot be answered without first settling something I have not mentioned, say that plainly rather than working within my frame.
