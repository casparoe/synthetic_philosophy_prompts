I'm a novelist working on a time-slip story about a character who can revisit the past and preview the future, and I've been rereading A Christmas Carol as my model — only to find it metaphysically confusing. Dickens seems to give Scrooge three different relations to time: the past is fixed and fully real (Marley's death, the lonely boy at boarding school), the present is real, and the future is somehow merely potential — the visions of what is yet to come are revisable, and Scrooge's reformed life cancels them. Please read the story as a position in the debate between presentism and eternalism. Is the coherent reconstruction the growing block universe, on which past and present exist and the future does not yet — or does something in the story resist that reading? I am particularly troubled by Marley's ghost, who is dead yet walking: on which theory of time does the dead man exist to be visited, and does his appearance commit the story to the reality of the past? And does the story's demand that we feel urgency about the future — that Tiny Tim's death is a live possibility to be averted rather than a settled region of reality — quietly presuppose presentism even while the ghosts presuppose an existing past? I'd like a reading that takes both the story and the metaphysics seriously, that says where the story resists the reading, and that ends by saying what it adds to the debate that argument alone doesn't. Please also tell me what would change your verdict — which detail in the story or consideration about the metaphysics would push you towards a different theory — and where your reading is most likely to fail. Keep the whole thing under 500 words.
