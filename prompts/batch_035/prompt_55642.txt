A few months ago I inherited a small collection of mid-century vending machines from my grandfather, who ran a route of them outside barbershops and bowling alleys for decades. I have been restoring one of them, a candy machine from the early sixties, and everything about it is original, including the coin mechanism, which still just checks a coin's diameter, weight, and (with a magnet) its composition. Last weekend my cousin, who collects coins, fed it a foreign coin — I want to say it was some Latin American coin from the eighties, the same size and metal as a quarter — and the machine took it without complaint and gave him his candy bar.

This has started a family argument that I, foolishly, keep fanning. My cousin insists the machine believes it has been paid a quarter and has simply been deceived. My wife thinks that's absurd: the machine doesn't believe anything, it only looks like something that believes, because the engineers who designed it intended quarters and built it accordingly — whatever wanting is going on belongs to the designers and to us users, not to the machine. My son, who is seventeen and has strong opinions about our smart speaker, takes a third line: the machine wants a disk of a certain size and weight, full stop, and calling that "wanting a quarter" was our mistake all along, so there was never anything there to deceive.

I realize I have to adjudicate this, because it matters for what I do next: I can keep the original mechanisms and effectively sell candy for whatever foreign slugs circulate among collectors, or I can fit modern rejectors that verify coins by other means. And the same argument has begun creeping into my day job, where I have just been put in charge of our company's automated phone line and am expected to answer for whether it understands our callers or merely produces the sounds of understanding. So I would like a serious answer: is one of my three family factions right, or is the whole disagreement confused in some way I can't yet see? What exactly, if anything, does a thing like my machine take a coin to be, and where — in the machine itself, in its designers, in its users, or in the whole practice of commerce surrounding it — does that live?
