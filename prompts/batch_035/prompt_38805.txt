Some people conclude that ambiguity aversion is a bias that should wash out under repetition, since repeated play lets the agent effectively learn the urn's composition. Others reply that before any evidence comes in, the agent still has no basis for assigning a single precise probability to the next draw, and that aversion to that residual unknown remains defensible even in long-run play. Please explain the conceptual issues at stake in this dispute, making clear what turns on whether the agent is modeled as having a precise but unknown prior, or as lacking any unique prior at all.
