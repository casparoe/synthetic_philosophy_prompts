Two people in my history reading group got into it after we discussed a chapter of Carr's What is History?, the one where he goes after Ranke's dream of showing the past as it actually happened. Maya said that dream isn't just unachievable, it's incoherent: the past doesn't come pre-sorted into important and unimportant facts, so deciding what counts as the story is a value judgment, not a discovery. She gave a concrete case: whether a famine is the central fact of its decade or a sideshow next to industrialization is not something an archive could ever settle — archives just sit there until someone decides what to ask them.

Dan said this proves too much. No map shows every feature of the territory and no doctor records every fact about your body, yet we dont call medicine subjective. For him, objectivity is a property of methods: when historians with different politics work the same sources and converge — on the sequence of events, the death toll, who issued which order — that convergence is what objectivity can amount to for finite creatures. Nobody ever needed the God's-eye view, and he thinks the whole Ranke caricature is a strawman.

Maya replied that convergence is cheap: astrologers trained in the same tradition converge on horoscopes, and historians share a training and a culture too, so convergence shows shared priors at best, not truth. Dan said if truth-tracking cant be checked even in principle, then Maya's own claims about what historians do are in the same boat — she's doing history of historiography in order to argue that history can't be objective. She said thats fine, hers is an argument from within, and two can play at the self-undermining game anyway.
