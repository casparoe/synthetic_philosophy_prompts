I co-organize a grad reading group on decision theory, and two of our members have been having the same argument for over a year now: one is fully sold on risk-weighted expected utility theory in the Buchak mold, the other is an orthodox expected utility maximizer who thinks that once you've got your utilities and probabilities straight, weighting outcomes by anything other than their probabilities is just irrationality with a fancy name. Everyone else is sick of watching it, so we've guilted them into an adversarial collaboration, and somehow I got deputized to draft the joint statement they'll both sign. Please write that document.

It should include: the claims both sides can sign off on (about the vNM framwork, about what Allais-style preferences show or don't show, about what each theory owes us an explanation of); the exact disagreements, worded so each side would accept how its own view is described; and the cruxes. I think one crux has to be the "it's just beliefs in disguise" objection — the EU person keeps saying that risk-weighting can be redescribed as distorting your probabilities instead, and I've never understood whether that's a real objection or a purely verbal dispute, so I want the document to commit one way or the other. There's also a specific case they keep tangling over, which should be in there: a retiree who's offered two bets, each with positive expected value, turns each one down when offered alone, but accepts the package of both, because together they hedge her downside. The EU person says this is either incoherent or capturable by EU once you model her wealth and the bets' interaction properly; the REU person says it's exactly what a convex risk function looks like and perfectly rational. For each crux, say what argument or test both sides would agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance.

Format: a numbered section of agreed claims, then a disagreements table. One thing I insist on — no document where every disagreement ends in "both sides agree more work is needed." Where the drafting requires a judgment call, like how to word a claim so the other side can sign it, or whether a disagreement is substantive rather than verbal, make the call and say why.
