My friend and I keep a running game where we invent little choice agents and ask whether each one is rational, and she is remarkably consistent in her rulings. Her verdicts so far: (1) An agent who prefers the beach to the mountains, the mountains to the desert, and the desert back to the beach; a travel agent sells him each switch for ten dollars until he is back at the beach and broke — irrational, obviously. (2) An agent with the same circular preferences whom no travel agent ever approaches, and who keeps his beach reservation forever — still irrational; she said the flaw lives in the preferences themselves, and that escaping the con artist is luck rather than rationality. (3) An agent whose rankings at any given moment are perfectly ordered but whose tastes reverse on a schedule he knows in advance — in January he will pay to swap his mountain trip for the beach, and by June he always wants the mountains back — and a travel agent charges a fee for every switch, so each year he ends where he started minus two fees — irrational; the defect, she said, is not in any single day's ranking but in the pattern across time. (4) An agent with the same scheduled reversal who in January buys a nonrefundable, nontransferable package and sits tight — fully rational; arranging your life around your own foreseeable shifts is exactly what sense requires. Next week's case: an agent with the circular preferences from (1) and (2), fully aware of his condition, who pays a service up front to make his reservation nontransferable and to screen out all offers — he knows he would take the bad trades, so he has himself walled in before anyone arrives. Predict her verdict. I cannot tell whether the rule she is running concerns the structure of the preferences themselves, the possibility of exploitation, or the management of one's own incoherence, and I suspect those rules come apart here. Set out the candidate rules that fit her track record equally well, say what each predicts for the walled-in agent, give your best guess with your reasons, and tell me which further case would best tell the rules apart.
