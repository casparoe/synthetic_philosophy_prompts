Been trying to figure out what I actually think about getting emotional over made-up stuff, going by my gut on a bunch of cases, and I've realised my gut is not consistent, so maybe someone can find the principle for me.

Crying at the end of a sad movie: fine, that's kind of the point of the thing. Jumping in my seat at a horror movie even though I know nothing is actually there: fine. Genuinely caring what happens to a character in a show over multiple seasons: fine, I'd honestly worry about someone who didn't. Getting tense on a rewatch of a thriller, at the ending I've already seen five times: also fine?? which is weird because it feels a lot like being scared of something I know for a fact isn't in the room.

Now the ones that don't feel fine. A while back I woke up actually angry at my wife because of something she did in my dream. The anger was real, I was still a bit short with her at breakfast, and I felt like that was dumb of me — I knew full well she didn't do it, yet the feeling hung around anyway. When people send abuse to actors because of what their character did on a show, I think that's dumb too, and when people rage at an author for killing off a character, I'd say out loud it's the same thing as the actor case... but privately I get the author one, and I can't see what the difference is.

One more pair that bugs me. I cried at a "based on a true story" film, then read afterwards that the true story was basically invented by the screenwriter, and my memory of crying at it now feels cheapened somehow — retroactively, like the emotion was on false pretenses. But crying at something openly fictional doesn't feel cheap at all. And a friend of mine refuses to watch anything sad on principle, "it's not real, why would I be sad," and I think she's missing something real. Except my mom did the exact same thing after my dad died and I thought it was completely healthy for her.

So: is there one principle that sorts the fine from the not fine here? It has to say something about the dream case and the rewatch case and why hating the actor differs from being mad at the author, if it even does. And please be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save — the dream one and the movie one keep feeling like the same case to me and I keep flip-flopping on which verdict should give. What does the principle say about a case I haven't mentioned, like a kid who's scared of the monster under the bed but still wants you to check for it every night?
