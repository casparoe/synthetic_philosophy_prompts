I'm a software engineer who's been in way too many late-night arguments about Newcomb's paradox, and now I want to turn that into a blog post: a "which decision theory do you actually hold?" quiz for smart readers with zero philosophy background. The thing I keep noticing is that people (me included) will say "oh I'm a one-boxer obviously" and then their answers to weirder cases are all over the place, so I want the quiz to sort people by what they'd actually *do*, not which label they like the sound of. The core cases I want it built around are counterfactual mugging (someone flipped a coin before you existed, and only simulated you in the branch where they'd need your answer; heads you never hear from them, tails they show up asking for $1000 with the promise of a big payoff next time if you pay) and XOR blackmail (the letter arrives if and only if either you'll pay up or you actually have termites, and you can't tell which), plus a few variations I came up with that I suspect pull people apart: a mugging with a transparent coin so you can plainly see it landed tails, a mugging where the predictor never simulated a conscious copy of you at all and just extrapolated from your high school permanent record, and a blackmailer who is a dumb script that fires randomly and never modeled you, so the causal arrow from your future choice to the letter is definitively not there.

Here's what I want you to produce: a decision tree (or quiz with a scoring key, your call) with eight to ten nodes, where every node is a concrete case phrased neutrally — no answer option should say "as an evidential decision theorist I would..." — and each leaf should be a named position like causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, updateless or functionalist views, or whatever else genuinely lives in the logical space, including any combo that's coherent but that nobody seems to actually hold (tell me if a leaf is unoccupied and whether that's because the view is broken or just unpopular). I especially care that the tree uses my variations to split people who'd pay in the mugging because they identify with the simulation from people who'd pay because they're willing to precommit, so please make sure at least a couple of nodes do that work rather than all roads leading to the same leaf by different fonts.

Two practical things: readers are engineers, so if you use a term like "updateless" you need to gloss it in plain words at the leaf, not assume anyone's read anything. And if there are pairs of positions your tree genuinely can't separate with cases alone — like maybe the functionalist and updateless leaves collapse into each other once you push on them — flag that explicitly instead of papering over it with a fake distinction, I'd rather the quiz have an honest asterisk than a lying one. Also if you think one of my variations is doing no work (like, the dumb-script blackmailer just turns it back into vanilla causal reasoning), tell me that too rather than politely including it. Length-wise, aim for something I could actually put on a blog, so cases can be two sentences each, not paragraphs.
