I'm taking a third-year course on philosophy of mind and cognitive technology, and our seminar has got stuck on the question of whether large language models are agents, simulators, or something else. I'd like a diagnosis of the impasse rather than a verdict on which side is right.

The two positions, as concretely as I can state them. The simulator side holds that an LLM is, in the first instance, a device for predicting plausible continuations of text, and that whatever goal-directed behaviour we observe is a role the model plays while a certain "frame" is active — the same weights, they point out, can be conditioned into a cautious assistant, an unhelpful one, or a character with explicitly stated vices, and nothing in the model itself fixes which of these it "really" is. Agency, on their view, belongs to the character being simulated, not to the simulator. The agent side replies that this mislocates the distinction: plenty of systems deserve the term "agent" without having context-invariant goals, and what matters is the presence of internal structure that behaves like planning — the model's behaviour across a long chain of tool calls, they claim, is better explained by something inside it doing instrumental reasoning than by the outer scaffold, and they add that the simulator side never says at what point along a gradient of increasingly coherent cross-context behaviour the "mere prediction" story stops applying. The seminar has stalled: each side concedes the other's observations and repeats its own line.

My working hypothesis, which I'd like you to take seriously but test rather than confirm, is that the disagreement is substantially verbal. The two sides may mean different things by "agent": one using it for a thing that has its own persistent goals whose loss would be a loss to it, and the other using it for any system whose behaviour is well modelled at the intentional level, as we model a thermostat or a chess engine. If that's right, the two camps could both be correct, and the appearance of conflict comes from treating one word as though it marked one property.

So the questions I want answered are these. First, is my hypothesis right — is the disagreement verbal, or is there a substantive issue underneath about whether LLMs have the kind of internal organisation that grounds genuine goal-directedness, such that one side must be wrong? In answering, please distinguish carefully between claims about the model in isolation and claims about the model-plus-scaffold composite, since I suspect a lot of the talking past each other happens there. Second, if there is a substantive core, what would count as progress on it — a conceptual distinction, some pattern in the model's behaviour across contexts that would decide between the two pictures, a criterion for when intentional-level explanation earns its keep? Third, if it turns out the question is ill-posed, say so plainly and explain what better question our seminar should be asking instead. Please write your answer in British English, and don't just survey the positions — I want the diagnosis, with reasons, and an honest assessment of whether anything could move this debate.
