I'm writing a chapter on how popular Fermi-paradox arguments borrow their persuasive force from analogies that almost nobody checks. The case I want to work through is the jungle analogy behind the dark forest hypothesis, and I'd like a proper audit of it rather than the usual is-it-plausible back-and-forth.

The setup, as you likely know: in Liu Cixin's trilogy, the universe is pictured as a dark forest in which every civilization is an armed hunter stalking among the trees, and the sound of another hunter — any reveal — invites a shot. Two premises do serious work alongside the image: the chain of suspicion, on which I can't verify your benevolence, you can't verify mine, neither of us can verify that the other has verified anything, and the regress never bottoms out; and the technology explosion, on which a civilization that is harmless today can leap past you within centuries. In current debates about METI — whether we should deliberately beam high-powered, targeted messages at nearby stars — this picture is the load-bearing structure. It's invoked to conclude that quiet is safety and deliberate broadcast is, at cosmic scale, a snapped twig. I don't want a verdict on whether the dark forest hypothesis is true; I want to know what the jungle comparison can and cannot carry.

Please do the following. First, give me an explicit correspondence table: what plays the role of the hunter, the prey, the trees, the weapon, the snapped twig, the hunter's field of view, and the night that hides everyone. Second, say where the mapping breaks — and here is the part I care about most, separate the disanalogies that matter for the METI conclusion from the ones that don't. Some candidates I've been chewing on, which you should refine, extend, or reject: in a jungle, detection is local, instantaneous, and roughly symmetric, whereas interstellar detection is bounded by light-lag, so you always observe what a civilization was, decades or millennia ago, never what it is; a jungle hunter can at least see that the clearing is empty, whereas no civilization can verify the emptiness of its forest, which cuts against the silence-is-safety conclusion as much as for it; jungle predators are mortal, sated, and bounded in growth, while the technology-explosion premise makes every player a potential apex predator indefinitely; and a first strike across light-years of lag is aimed at where the target used to be, not where it is, which has no jungle counterpart at all. Third, there's the objection that the forest was never dark in the relevant sense: a biosphere like Earth's has been leaking waste heat and atmospheric chemistry for billions of years, and if such signatures are detectable at interstellar distances, silence buys nothing for the already-visible. Assess whether that point dissolves the analogy's practical conclusion or merely narrows it to the difference between passive leakage and deliberate, power-dense transmission. Finally, tell me what a better analogy would be, if there is one — whether the right source domain is a forest at all, or something structurally different, like a game with delayed, noisy, asymmetric observation. I'd like the audit to end with a clear statement of which conclusions, if any, the jungle picture can still support once its broken mappings are on the table, and which need to be rebuilt from non-analogical grounds — decision theory, detection physics, or whatever else you think actually does the work.
