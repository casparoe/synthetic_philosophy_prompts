So, quick context: I recently got voted onto my city's public safety commission (small city, extremely unglamorous, mostly arguing about parking) and I've somehow ended up drafting the section of our annual report about trends in violence. And this has turned out to be way harder than I expected. One faction on the commission wants a big "violence is at historic lows" headline based on per-capita assault and homicide rates. Another faction thinks that framing is basically propaganda: absolute numbers for some categories are up, and anyway the violence people actually experience in this city never shows up in those stats -- evictions, overdose deaths from the fentanyl in the drug supply, kids who hear gunshots from their bedroom and won't sleep, elderly folks who've stopped walking at night. My brother-in-law, meanwhile, keeps bringing up Pinker at every thanksgiving and telling everyone who'll listen that the long arc of history bends away from violence, so I can't even escape this debate at dinner.

So heres what I want: an essay on whether the world is actually becoming less violent. Not the standard textbook summary -- I want you to actually wade into the messy parts. What should even count as "violence"? Just interpersonal assault and organized warfare, or also structural stuff (poverty, incarceration, preventable deaths from pollution or a poisoned drug supply)? Does treating the latter as violence dilute the concept to uselessness, or is excluding it a way of rigging the trend line in advance? Then the metric question: per capita versus absolute numbers -- why do reasonable people disagree about which is the right one, and when does each choice quietly do moral work? And underneath all of it, the question I keep circling back to: does "is violence declining?" even have a fact of the matter, or is it mostly a definitional choice dressed up as an empirical finding -- in which case my commission is arguing about the definition while pretending we're arguing about the data?

Weigh the main positions honestly, but don't give me a wishy-washy "it depends on your definition" ending -- I need a verdict I can actually defend at the next commission meeting, even if it's a qualified or conditional one.
