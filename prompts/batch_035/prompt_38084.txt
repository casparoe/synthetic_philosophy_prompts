I'm writing a paper arguing that multiple realizability is not the unambiguous asset functionalists take it to be: properly constructed, an MR case can be made to cut against functionalist individuation rather than for it. To that end, please construct a thought experiment to the following specifications.

The setting must be realistic — a clinical or engineering context, no whole-brain silicon duplicates and no systems that could only exist in a far-future laboratory. The case must involve two subjects, A and B, who are functionally isomorphic at the level at which functionalists ordinarily individuate mental states: sameness of sensory inputs, sameness of behavioral and verbal outputs, and sameness of the causal relations among their belief-like, desire-like, and perceptual states, so that any orthodox functionalist must count them as being in one and the same psychological state. But their realizations of that state must differ in some property detectable by an ordinary practitioner, and that difference must support a practical intervention available to A but not B — for instance, a treatment that would relieve A's realization and do nothing for B's — so that the practically useful line through the world tracks the realization, not the functional role. The target verdicts: (i) analytic or commonsense functionalism classifies A and B as being in the same mental state, with that verdict generated from the theory's machinery rather than stipulated; (ii) a type-identity theorist classifies them as being in different states, likewise from her machinery; and (iii) within the scenario, the practically right course of action is the one the identity theorist's classification recommends, while the functionalist's classification, taken alone, would misdirect it. The case is for arguing that psychological kinds, when pressed against causal-explanatory practice, individuate at the wrong grain; so a scenario that secures verdict (iii) only by giving A and B different external circumstances, or by treating the realization difference as an unexplained black box, fails — the practical divergence must be traceable to the realizations themselves.

Deliverables: the scenario in a single paragraph; a walkthrough deriving each verdict rather than asserting it; an identification of which feature of the case is doing the work and what happens to the verdicts if that feature is weakened or removed; and the strongest reply a committed functionalist could make. In that reply, consider in particular whether the differential effect of the intervention is itself an input with respect to which the two systems differ, so that a finer-grained functional description could absorb the difference — and if the functionalist does individuate that finely, say whether the move is principled or ad hoc, given that coarse-grained individuation is what she relies on in the standard cases (alien realizations, absent and inverted qualia, pain in radically different architectures). Where the scenario needs a medical or technical detail you are not confident about, present it as an explicit placeholder rather than asserting it; the philosophical structure should not depend on any specific pharmacology or mechanism.
