So a friend and I are splitting up the small company we cofounded, and rather than haggling we agreed to let a formula decide: the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution. Normalize each of our utilities so the disagreement point is 0 and our personal best possible outcome is 1, then pick the point on the Pareto frontier where our normalized gains are equal — same fraction of the way to utopia for each of us. We went with it over Nash's solution because Nash famously violates individual monotonicity: if my best case gets better while everything else stays fixed, my share can actually shrink, which struck both of us as obviously crazy. And somewhere along the way I talked myself into the stronger view that KS isn't just a tidy formula for our situation, it's the right fairness standard for two-person bargaining generally.

What would count against that? I want defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward. The one I half-know already: KS gives up independence of irrelevant alternatives — add some third option neither of us would ever choose and the deal between us can shift. Is that just the mirror image of the vice I held against Nash, or is one vice genuinely worse? Then there's the utopia-point machinery itself: my entitlement gets pegged to my best possible outcome, so does dreaming bigger entitle me to more, or does the normalization neutralize that? Does "equal fraction of your ideal" secretly smuggle in interpersonal utility comparisons, or is it the one solution that avoids them? And whatever happened to consistency across bargaining problems, the axiom Nash has that KS doesn't?

Also tell me which of these a committed KS defender would actually concede versus wave off with "cardinal utility is just a modeling choice", and whether the view has quietly been insulated from any possible counterexample. And be honest at the end: for something like our split — two people, cash now versus deferred payments — should I genuinely be worried, or is this the philosophical equivalent of checking the load rating on a chair I'm already sitting in?
