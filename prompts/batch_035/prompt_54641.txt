Plan the explanation for me, don't just explain the thing to me. So what has to be in place before the grounding question even registers as a question for them? My instinct is a case where physics seems to stay silent -- a match being won, a promise being broken -- and yet nobody wants to say these things are nothing over and above particle configurations... but tell me if that's the wrong wedge. Which single example should carry the whole talk, and what does it have to do that a standard reduction example (water = H2O) wouldn't? The misconceptions I should expect -- grounding as causation is the obvious one, plus them quietly deciding fundamentality just means "smallest length scale" -- and exactly where in the sequence to head each one off. What I should openly refuse to get into at this level (the whole debate about whether grounding itself is grounded? priority monism vs pluralism?) given ninety minutes and this crowd, and how to say so without sounding like I'm dodging. And one question I can ask at the end that would actually show me whether the idea landed, versus them just nodding along politely.
