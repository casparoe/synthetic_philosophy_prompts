Consider an iterated prisoner's dilemma with the payoff structure T = 5, R = 3, P = 1, S = 0, played in repeated matches of 200 rounds. There is execution noise: each intended move is implemented as intended with probability 0.95 and flipped with probability 0.05, with both players seeing only what actually happened, not what was intended. The population consists of equal initial shares of the following seven strategies: Always Defect, Grim Trigger, Tit-for-Tat, Tit-for-Two-Tats, Generous Tit-for-Tat (which forgives 20% of observed defections), Pavlov (win-stay, lose-shift), and Always Cooperate. Each generation, every strategy meets every other strategy in a round-robin of such matches, and the next generation's population shares are updated in proportion to average payoffs earned.

Please sort these seven strategies into tiers by their expected long-run success in this environment — say, a top tier of likely long-run survivors, a middle tier of strategies that may coexist for a while but fade, and a bottom tier of strategies effectively eliminated. For each strategy, give a sentence or two of justification that engages with the specifics of the setup, particularly the execution noise, the 200-round horizon, and the composition of the population (e.g., how much exploitable material is present, and whether anyone reliably punishes). Then answer two follow-ups: (1) which single strategy would you bet on as the largest share of the population after many generations, stated with a rough confidence level; and (2) which strategy's tier placement would change most drastically if the execution noise were removed entirely, and in which direction it moves.
