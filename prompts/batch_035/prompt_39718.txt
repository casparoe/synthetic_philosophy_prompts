I just got made lead mod of a 90k-person Discord server for vintage synthesizer repair, and it's gotten weird lately. A long-standing core of members has developed a strong house line about which restoration methods and which aftermarket parts are "correct," and newer folks who cite outside sources (official service manuals, techs on other forums) get piled on. I've read a bit about the distinction between epistemic bubbles (you just never hear the other side) and echo chambers (you hear them, but you've been primed to think they're untrustworthy or stupid), and the remedies seem totally different -- a bubble might just need exposure, while forcing exposure on an echo chamber can backfire and make it worse. I can't tell which we have, or whether we're just a healthy community with a real expertise-based consensus, which I gather is a third thing.

Write me a diagnostic + intervention procedure I can actually run with two other mods in an afternoon of reading logs and threads. It should say: what evidence to look at in order (how would we even tell "never encounters outside views" apart from "encounters and dismisses them"), what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, and where it stops and says "this is a values call for the mods, the procedure can't decide." Then it should map diagnoses to interventions, being honest about which interventions a mod team with no real power except bans and channel structure can actually pull off, and which are likely to blow up in our faces. Run it on three cases: (1) a subcommunity around one particular synth family that treats a certain repair guy on YouTube as a fraud, (2) a general chat that's just never once linked anything outside the server, and (3) the case I'm dreading, where the house line is probably right, the outsiders citing manuals are mostly wrong, and my instinct to "introduce outside voices" would just be epistemic theater. Say plainly what the procedure can't handle.
