I'm a retired physical chemist -- spent about 35 years chasing reaction kinetics in a lab, and since retiring I've gotten properly hooked on philosophy, especially the places where it touches science and religion. My late-career reading has been all over the place: some Hume, some James, a lot of the "New Atheist" stuff from the 2000s which I'll admit I rather enjoyed at the time, and lately, out of a book club, George Eliot. I knew her as a novelist before but I've just learned the biographical bits about her: that she translated Strauss's Life of Jesus as a young woman, did the first English translation of Feuerbach's Essence of Christianity, broke with her father over churchgoing, and then spent her novels treating religious believers (Mr. Casaubon, Dinah, Mr. Tryan, Savonarola of all people) with this strange mixture of sympathy and complete intellectual detachment from the truth of their beliefs.

So here's what I keep chewing on while walking the dog: suppose you could sit her down circa 2007 with The God Delusion on the table and Hitchens holding forth on the telly. Where would she actually land? My book club has split into two camps. One side says she'd obviously be on the atheist side -- she did the hard, scandalous version of unbelief decades before it was respectable, and Feuerbach basically gave her the "religion as projection of human ideals" line that Dawkins sells in friendlier packaging. The other side (me, wavering) suspects she'd find the whole tone of it shallow -- that her whole outlook treats religion as the sediment of human need and history, something you dont get rid of by winning an argument, and that treating ordinary chapel-going folk as dupes would strike her as a failure of imagination, maybe even of moral seriousness. And I have a further hunch she'd be even less patient with today's vibe -- the "spiritual but not religious" crowd, mindfulness apps, people who want the consolations without the creed -- because that seems like keeping Feuerbach's projection while pretending you've seen through it.

Anyway: what do you think she'd say? I'd love it if you'd actually take a position rather than just laying out both sides -- and if you can ground your guess in things like her handling of specific characters, her essays, or the Feuerbach connection, that'd be great. Bonus if you tell me where my framing is off, or whether the New Atheist / secular-spiritual split is even the right axis to place her on.
