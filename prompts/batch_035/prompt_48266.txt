My friend Diya (second-year med student, zero philosophy, answers before I finish the sentence) refuses to engage with any theory when we argue about politics — she just answers specific cases. My justice course just finished the "equality of what?" unit, so as an experiment I've started feeding her cases and asking her one question each time: who is worse off? I write her rulings down right after. So far I have: my uncle in the village, tiny income, no clinic, the school is too far for the kids, says he's happy and wants nothing more — vs. my cousin in the city with a decent job who's miserable and on antidepressants: she said the uncle, "not even close." Two coursemates on the same stipend, one of whom needs nice food and a gym to feel okay and is miserable without them, the other fine with cheap hobbies: she said they're exactly equal, "being hard to please isn't a disadvantage and being easy to please isn't an achievement." My aunt with a chronic condition that's fully controlled by meds she can easily afford — she swims, works, travels — and who has more money than her neighbour: aunt is not worse off, "once it's handled it doesn't count." And two people with the same income, one living somewhere he can't publish, organise, or leave the country, who says he never cared about politics anyway: she said he's worse off than the free one, which honestly surprised me.
