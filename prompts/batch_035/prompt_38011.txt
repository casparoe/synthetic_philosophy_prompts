So our lab has this running bit: my advisor (cognition/complex systems, very much a Hofstadter person) likes to hand down snap verdicts about whether some system "has a mind" or is "just doing computation," and we've accumulated a track record over two years of lab lunches. I'm a 3rd year PhD student and I keep the list because I want to write something about the Ant Fugue chapter and she's the obvious person to annoy about it. Her verdicts so far: individual ant, no; whole ant colony, yes -- she basically buys the Aunt Hillary picture, the ants are like neurons, they don't understand anything individually but the colony-level organization does; single neuron, no; brain, yes; a starling murmuration, no, on the grounds that it's too ephemeral, nothing persists overnight, no colony-level memory; a slime mold solving mazes, yes, and her reasoning was explicitly "it's one cell, but who cares, it's the dynamics that matter"; Wikipedia, no, because the editors have minds and the artifact doesn't (nothing is going on in the artifact itself, it's a structure the minds leave behind); a beehive, yes; current LLMs as deployed, no, but she was careful to say it's because they're intermittently instantiated -- her phrase was something like "there's no continuing subject there between sessions" -- and that a continuously running system with persistent memory would be a live question she refused to answer on the spot.

Now there's a lab tour next month with three demos, and people are already placing bets on her verdicts. Demo 1: a fully virtual ant colony -- simulated ants with pheromone stigmergy, running 24/7 for two years in a changing environment, with genuinely colony-level memory living in the pheromone maps. Demo 2: a warehouse system of a few hundred extremely dumb robots plus persistent markings on the floor that function like pheromone trails, where individual robots get swapped out constantly but the trail system persists for months. Demo 3: the mycorrhizal network under a forest -- living, distributed, chemical signaling between trees, with some habituation-like dynamics reported in the literature.

Can you predict her verdict on each of the three? I can see at least two, maybe three, rules she could be running that all fit her track record equally well: one where the medium doesn't matter at all and it's purely the emergent dynamics plus persistence (which would be the honest Hofstadter reading), one where the system has to be alive / metabolizing as a single integrated body, and one where what matters is that the system itself is the beneficiary of what it does -- that the competence is for the system's own sake, like foraging is for the colony, not for anybody else. Do the three demos separate those rules, and if not quite, what fourth case would? Give me your best guess per demo with the reasoning, but also tell me how confident you are in each prediction and which single prediction you're least sure of -- the whole point of the betting pool is calibration, so don't hedge everything uniformly.
