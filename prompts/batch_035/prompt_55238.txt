So I've been going down a rabbit hole on the Vienna Circle's politics, and here's what's bugging me: everyone knows Carnap as the guy who wanted to clean up language, explicate concepts, build formal systems etc. But apparently he was also pretty politically engaged his whole life -- a democratic socialist who thought the tools of rational reconstruction weren't just for science, that they could extend to social life too. Like, the same move he makes with vague scientific concepts (replace them with precise, constructed ones) was supposed to somehow apply to political questions, and he seems to have been genuinely into things like planning, world government, democracy as a procedure for resolving disputes without force. His circle colleague Neurath was even more explicit about connecting a planned economy to the philosophy of science.

Meanwhile, the obvious counterpoint that came up in my reading is Hayek, whose famous point in "The Use of Knowledge in Society" is basically that planning fails on epistemic grounds: the relevant knowledge is dispersed across millions of people, much of it tacit, and it can't be collected and centralized by any planning body, markets just process it better.

Here's what I want you to do: compare Carnap's rationalist case for social planning with Hayek's epistemic case against it. A few things I'd love you to actually address rather than dodge:

1. Are these two even arguing about the same thing? Part of me suspects Carnap is talking about the justification of ends (making values explicit, showing socialism/democracy can be argued for the way scientific claims are) while Hayek is talking about the feasibility of means. If so the disagreement might be shallower than it looks. But maybe not -- you tell me.

2. Could Carnap's explication project actually be deployed against Hayek? Like, if we took a hopelessly vague concept like welfare or social utility and explicated it into something precise, does that help gather dispersed knowledge, or does the precision just hide where the knowledge went? I keep flip-flopping on this.

3. Whose epistemology is stronger here, honestly? Both of them have a kind of engineering optimism about their methods -- Carnap about formal reconstruction, Hayek about the price mechanism -- and I'd like to know if you think either one is basically coasting.

4. And a side question: do you think Neurath would've been a better opponent for Hayek than Carnap was? Neurath's whole calculation-in-kind thing (planning without money prices) seems to collide with Hayek more directly, but I'm not sure if it's a fairer fight or a more doomed one.

Don't hold back on the verdicts, I'm not looking for a both-sides-have-a-point essay. And feel free to bring in related stuff like Popper if it actually helps make the comparsion sharper, not just to name-drop.
