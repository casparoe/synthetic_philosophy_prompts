I'm a psych grad student and something about how the DSM actually builds its diagnostic criteria has been bugging me for a while, and nobody in my program seems to want to talk about it. For most disorders the criteria sets are polythetic: for major depression you need any 5 of 9 symptoms over a two week period (with the one restriction that depressed mood or anhedonia has to be among them), and for borderline personality disorder you need any 5 of 9 criteria with no single one being required at all. Which means two people can walk out with the same BPD diagnosis while sharing as few as one criterion, and the diagnosis doesn't tell you which one. The obvious analogy is Wittgenstein on games -- no common essence, just a network of overlapping similarities that fade out at the edges -- and some people in my cohort talk like the DSM quietly adopted family resemblance as its official theory of what a mental disorder is, without ever admitting that's a philosophical position it took.

But here's where I get confused, because I can read the exact same 5-of-9 structure the opposite way. You could also say it presupposes a hidden essence that the criteria merely indicate -- the symptoms are fallible, interchangeable probes of one underlying condition, which is why any 5 will do, and which is more or less how people who do latent variable modeling seem to talk about these diagnoses. Both readings fit the rule! And then the depression carve-out, where you specifically need depressed mood or anhedonia, seems to say some symptoms are more central than others -- which is neither pure family resemblance nor a hidden essence, more like a graded, prototype kind of structure. Meanwhile the DSM itself insists it's "atheoretical," purely descriptive, just reporting observed patterns and not committing to anything about what's underneath. I'm honestly not sure how it can dodge the question that way when it has to have *some* view of what makes a bunch of very different patients count as having the same condition.

So what theory of concepts or kinds does this practice actually presuppose, if any? I'd like the implicit view reconstructed -- what does the polythetic structure strictly require, what is it merely consistent with, and do the parts even cohere? Is the self-described descriptive, theory-neutral stance compatible with any of the candidate theories of concepts, or is it more like borrowing family resemblance for the categories while quietly assuming essences for the measurement models the criteria feed into? And when people push to replace categories with dimensions -- severity scales, spectra, that whole direction -- which of these presuppositions are they actually rejecting? Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my question.
