my two housemates got into it last night after jess squashed a little clay gnome she'd made, to reuse the clay, and dan made some joke like "murderer." then it got weirdly serious. i'll try to reproduce it because they each asked me to referee and i genuinely couldnt.

(1) dan said: look, the statue and the lump of clay are obviously the same thing. same location, same weight, same color, every atom in common. two objects cant be in exactly the same place at the same time, thats just physics. so one thing.

(2) jess said: then youve got a contradiction, because the lump can survive being squashed -- its still a lump, just a lumpy ball now -- but the gnome couldnt. squashing killed the gnome. if theyre really one object, then that one object both can and cannot survive squashing, which is impossible. so theyre two.

(3) dan said: nice try but those "survival" facts arent real properties of the object, theyre facts about what happens in other possible scenarios. the actual object has a mass, a shape, a color. those are the properties that matter for identity, and it shares all of them with itself. you cant derive a contradiction from what an object WOULD do, only from what its actually like.

(4) jess said: fine, forget other worlds. the clay is three weeks old -- i bought it at the art store, it sat in a bag, it existed last tuesday sitting on the shelf. the gnome came into existence this morning. so the clay existed last tuesday and the gnome didnt. thats an actual property, right here in this world, and they differ on it. your own rule gives you two objects.

(5) dan paused, then said: ok, suppose i just grant it, statue and lump are two things. then the shelf is packed -- the clay-constituting-the-gnome is another one, and the upper half of the gnome is another, and the gnome-minus-one-ear, and on and on, billions of overlapping things all in one spot. and now "statue" and "lump" are just two random members of this giant pile, nothing privileged about them. also, when you asked me to hand you "that thing" off the shelf, which of the billion did i hand you? and if i ask how many things are on the shelf, is the answer "one" or "like a billion"? if your view makes counting impossible maybe your views about what exists are the problem, not mine.

jess's comeback was that she only ever needed two, not a billion, but she couldnt really say why just those two, and dan looked pretty smug about that.
