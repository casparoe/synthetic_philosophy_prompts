I work as a human judge in experiments testing the debate model of scalable oversight: two AI systems argue opposite sides of a question that is beyond my ability to answer directly, and my job is to reach the right verdict by interrogating them. In each round I have roughly thirty minutes, a limited budget of interventions, and no independent way to check most factual claims — I can ask for a claim to be decomposed into subclaims the debaters must separately defend, ask each side to critique the other's strongest passage, demand sources for specific numbers, or end the round and guess. The stakes are real: these rounds inform decisions about whether debate is safe to rely on for evaluating AI systems on tasks where no human can check the work. Write me a decision procedure I could actually follow round after round, and that other judges could follow with reasonably similar results: what to ask for in what order, when to spend the intervention budget on decomposing a claim versus cross-examining it, how to treat a claim that is unverifiable within the debate but load-bearing for one side, how to penalize evasion without simply punishing the debater who got caught being honest, and when to stop and guess rather than keep spending time. Be explicit about which steps follow from the theory — the idea that making honest play optimal in a zero-sum game is what makes debate an oversight method at all — and which are merely heuristics dressed up as procedure, and mark the points where judgment cannot be eliminated. Then run the procedure on two cases: a debate that turns on a factual subclaim that decomposes cleanly into checkable pieces, and one where the disagreement is over how to weigh competing considerations, so there is no hidden fact for the debaters to converge on. For the second case especially, I want to know what the procedure tells me to do when I would much rather it delivered a verdict.
