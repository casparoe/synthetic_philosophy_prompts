I am a retired geophysicist who, since leaving the laboratory, has spent the better part of two years reading ethics and formal decision theory in earnest. I have gradually assembled the following positions, drawn mostly from the literature on idealized preference and extrapolated volition, and I would like an audit of whether they hang together:

(1) I am a moral anti-realist: there are no stance-independent moral facts; what makes a normative claim correct is its relation to what agents care about.

(2) I accept an ideal-advisor account of wellbeing: what is good for a person is what she would want her life to be like after ideal reflection — fully informed, coherent, deliberating well.

(3) I believe that properly idealized human preferences converge on a substantial common core, and that the appropriate target for choices affecting humanity's long-term future is the coherent extrapolated volition of humanity collectively — in the idiom of my former trade, that the deliberative dynamics have a single attractor rather than many basins.

(4) I hold a firm anti-paternalism: it is impermissible to override a competent adult's current, settled preferences for her own good.

(5) I hold that our preferences, including our preference for being informed and coherent in the first place, are artifacts of contingent evolutionary and cultural history, with no epistemically privileged status.

(6) In the concrete case of a contented member of a cloistered religious community whose faith would not survive ideal reflection, my considered judgment is that we harm her by entrenching the conditions of her faith, despite her settled and happy attachment to it.

Please examine the set pair by pair. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection and precisely why, and, for each genuine tension, what would have to give and what that revision would cost elsewhere in the set. I do not have a favored exit, so please do not attribute one to me. Order the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally grave, and distinguish carefully between a tension that is local to a pair of commitments and one that infects the whole set. Conclude with a final line beginning "Verdict:" that names, in a short phrase, the single commitment whose revision would clean up the most.
