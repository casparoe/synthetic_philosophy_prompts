Some years ago I came across an old idea — I believe it was Douglas Hofstadter's, from a piece he wrote back in the 1980s — that he called superrationality, and I've quietly organised a fair bit of my life around it since. As I hold it, the thought is this: when a group of people face the same choice structured the same way, and each is going to reason about it in much the same manner, it is a mistake for any of them to treat the others' behaviour as an independent fact about the world. If the reasoning is genuinely shared, my cooperation is evidence of theirs and theirs of mine, so I should choose as if we were effectively one agent deciding for the lot of us — which in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, or on whether to vote, recycle, keep an unobserved promise, or refrain from a bit of quiet freeloading, usually means taking the cooperative option even where defecting would pay. It has genuinely changed how I act, and I keep defending it to friends who tell me I'm just being sentimental about game theory.

What I want is to stress-test the position rather than have it flattered. Tell me what would actually count against it — not only the obvious point that real counterparties aren't copies of me, but which specific situations, arguments, or findings should move me: say, a counterpart who announces in advance that he will defect; a case where everyone reasoning "as one agent" lands everyone in a worse place than plain selfishness would have; a demonstration that my decision is somehow supposed to be doing causal work that only evidence can do. Rank these from fatal to merely awkward, say which of them a committed superrationalist should concede as genuine trouble rather than explain away, and be honest about whether the position has been insulated over time by quietly narrowing what counts as "reasoning like me" whenever a counterexample looms — and, if it has, whether that sort of insulation is a fatal defect in a principle meant for real life, where one never meets an exact twin of oneself. If you think the whole thing rests on a confusion — if I've smuggled a causal claim into what is at best evidence about what others will do — please say so plainly rather than going along with my setup; I would much rather hear the view is broken than have it handled gently.
