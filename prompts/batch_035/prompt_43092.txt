I'm applying for a 9-month postdoc fellowship and need a ~2-page research statement written up as a proposal. Here's my half-formed idea, do with it what you can.

Two-dimensional semantics gets sold as a tool for handling the Kripke cases -- water turning out to be H2O, Hesperus and Phosphorus being Venus, necessary a posteriori and all that. The epistemic dimension tracks how a competent but empirically ignorant speaker fixes reference via descriptions; the modal dimension then tells you what's necessary once the reference is fixed. Fine. But the more I sit with the literature the more I suspect the "epistemic" dimension is quietly doing two different jobs at once: (a) modeling the actual epistemic state of a speaker (what they'd accept given what they know), and (b) serving as an actuality-fixing device so that the diagonal proposition comes out with the right truth conditions -- which is what makes the contingent a priori and necessary a posteriori come out right. Everyone treats these as the same thing because in the classic Kripke cases they happen to coincide. But when you push the machinery onto harder cases -- epistemic modals, indexicals like "now" and "I", or attitudes about what other people know -- I think the two jobs come apart, and some of the celebrated results about 2D are artifacts of the coincidence rather than real features of the framework.

So the project: split the epistemic dimension into a speaker-state dimension and an actuality-fixing dimension, and work out which standard cases survive the split intact and which ones depended on conflating the two. My hunch (and I admit this is more of a hunch than I'd like at proposal-writing time) is that the necessary a posteriori survives fine but the contingent a priori mostly doesn't, which would be awkward for a lot of people.

Turn this into a proper proposal: state the research question precisely, say why the existing positions leave it open (I suspect it's because the framework is usually evaluated only against the classic cases where the coincidence holds, but argue that your way), the method -- what I'd actually do month by month over nine months, not vague "I will study the literature" filler -- what a result looks like, and what would count as the project failing honestly. No citations needed, I'll add those myself. And one thing: I don't want one of those "the project will explore both options" proposals that hedges on everything. You pick which half of my hunch you think is actually right -- that the split is deep and destabilizing, or that the two jobs can be shown to collapse back together with enough care -- and build the proposal around taking that side, with your reasons. If you think my framing is off in some more basic way, say so at the start and fix it, but still commit.
