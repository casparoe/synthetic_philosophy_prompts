I help run a philosophy-of-mind discussion group that grew out of a long-running rationalist meetup — about 18 regular attendees, mostly people who have read GEB, the Sequences, and a lot of Scott Alexander, and who will happily derail any discussion into meta-level disputes if you let them. We have one 90-minute session coming up on Hofstadter's "Prelude... Ant Fugue," specifically the Aunt Hillary material: the anteater who is a friend of the conscious ant colony he feeds on, the idea that intelligence might live at the colony level while the ants themselves are dumb, and the Tortoise's insistence that some questions about levels of description should be answered with "mu" rather than yes or no.

The problem is that everyone in the room has already had the abstract thought "the colony is like a brain and the ants are like neurons" — it's practically a cached thought in our subculture, with "superorganism," prediction markets, and "the market remains rational longer than you remain solvent" waiting right behind it. I want an in-class activity that makes them run into the hard part of the question with their own behavior, before any of that vocabulary gets deployed. My idea, which I'd like you to develop into something runnable: a pheromone-trail game modeled on the classic double-bridge ant experiments, where participants individually follow a fixed local rule and the group collectively has to converge on the shorter of two paths, with nobody being told there are two paths, or that one is shorter, or what the win condition is.

Please design the full activity: rules, materials (keep it to index cards, tape, and a stopwatch), the exact local update rule each participant follows, a payoff scheme that doesn't use real money but still makes them care, and how to run the rounds. Then I'd like you to extend it with a second phase that isolates the part I find hardest to stage: a "scout" variant in which one participant is shown the full layout and holds genuine propositional knowledge, but can only act on it through the same token-laying channel as everyone else — so the group has to discover whether procedural, colony-level signal-passing can carry something that started out as somebody's knowledge, and what gets lost in the translation.

Finally, the debrief. It should map specific moments of what actually happened onto specific moves in the Ant Fugue: the level-of-description question (did the colony "choose" the short path, given that no participant did?), the disanalogy Hofstadter builds in where ants are individually competent and mobile while neurons are neither, and the question the anteater's friendship with Aunt Hillary is really asking — whether a system can be a friend, an eater-of-your-parts, and a person to you all at once. I want the debrief to force the group to say, out loud, which parts of "the colony has a mind" the activity gave them evidence about and which parts it couldn't touch even in principle — my own suspicion is that a convergence result, however elegant, only shows you an optimizer, and that Aunt Hillary's consciousness has to be argued for on entirely different grounds than her competence, but I'd like the debrief to genuinely test that suspicion rather than confirm it. And be honest about the failure modes: what will go wrong the first time I run this, and which feature of the original thought experiment no staging with 18 humans in a rented room can ever reproduce, so I know what I have to supply myself.
