Is there a name for this? I've been following politics for years now and something keeps nagging at me that I can't put a word to. A politician (or a whole party, or a cable channel) lies constantly — not one big lie, but a steady drip, about big things and small, and lots of the lies get caught. But here's the part that puzzles me: his supporters don't seem to actually believe the lies. Push them and they say "well, all politicians lie" or "who really knows". They're not duped. They've just stopped treating true and false as different in kind. And after a while they'll shrug even at things that are trivially checkable, like "they'd lie about that too". Meanwhile the liar never needed to convince anyone — he only needs "nobody can really know" to become the ambient mood, and then he's free to do whatever.

I don't think it's gaslighting, since that's aimed at one person and their own memory of something specific. This is more like the audience's whole sense that checking would matter gets eroded. I have a vague memory of some philosopher saying that lying in politics works differently from lying to a friend, but I can't remember who or what the idea was called. Anyway: does this phenomenon have an established name? I won't be happy with just "propaganda" or "post-truth", those cover everything and carve nothing — what I want to know is whether the specific mechanism has been studied (why consistent lying doesn't produce belief in the lies, but instead produces this blanket cynical fog), and if there's no term for it, coin one and show me where its edges are — how its different from gaslighting, from crying wolf, and from ordinary spin. Also if that philosopher I half-remember exists, tell me what they actually said. Please answer in British English.
