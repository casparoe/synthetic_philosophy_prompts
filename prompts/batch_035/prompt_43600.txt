I'm on the scheduling committee for a mid-sized annual folk music festival, and every year we pick the 3-day lineup of headliners by a complicated hybrid vote that everyone hates. There are usually 4-6 serious candidate acts plus the "do a joint co-headliner night instead" option, about 30 voters (board members, stage crew leads, a few big donors with opinions), and each voter submits a full ranking of the candidates. This year the process fell apart in a kind of instructive way: after ballots were in, one act cancelled for family reasons and was removed from consideration, and when we just deleted her from everyone's rankings and recounted, a *different* headliner won than had won the night before -- which felt absurd to everyone even though nobody could say exactly what rule we'd violated. Separately, one board member later admitted she had ranked her true favorite's strongest rival dead last not because she disliked him but to drag his Borda count down, and I suspect she wasn't the only one. Also our bylaws say the donors' bloc can't just override everyone else, but they do get outvoted on things they all agree on sometimes, which they are furious about.

I've been reading about Arrow's theorem and I get that it says you can't have all of unrestricted domain, Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives, non-dictatorship, and a coherent collective ranking all at once (assuming more than two alternatives). But before we redesign the vote, I want the spec, not a specific voting method. Write me the desiderata: what an adequate decision procedure for *this* case actually has to deliver -- handle removal of a candidate after ballots are cast, not be trivially gameable by burying/ranking rivals last, give the donors' bloc its due without letting them dictate, produce a stable choice the crew won't relitigate, whatever else you think belongs -- with the concrete case or consideration behind each requirement. Mark which are genuinely non-negotiable for us versus which are just imported prejudices from the general social-choice literature, and tell me which single requirement does the most exclusionary work against the standard candidate methods (plurality, runoff, Borda, Condorcet, approval/score voting). The part I most want to see: given Arrow (and anything else like Gibbard-Satterthwaite on strategyproofness that applies), which subset of your desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable for our situation, and what's the least bad thing to give up -- the ordinal ballot itself, independence, unrestricted domain, or full collective coherence? If you think our specific constraints (30 voters, ~6 options, a status-quo default) let us dodge any of the impossibility results, say so.

Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence -- which requirement you'd sacrifice first and which method you'd run the festival vote with given all this -- then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my question back at me. A table for the desiderata is fine but the flagging of jointly unsatisfiable subsets is the part I'll actually read twice.
