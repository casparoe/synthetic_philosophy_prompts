I am a high school junior, and for the past semester I have been part of a small reading group on early Chinese philosophy that meets over lunch — four students and a retired teacher who once lectured in Taipei. We have now spent five sessions on the dispute between Mencius and Xunzi over whether human nature is good, and we are in exactly the state we started in. As I understand the positions: Mencius holds that we are born with incipient moral tendencies — he asks whether anyone could watch a child about to fall into a well without a flash of alarm — and he says the virtues grow out of these sprouts the way a crop grows from a seed, if nothing blights them. Xunzi answers that human nature is whatever is in us before training, that left to itself it runs toward conflict and disorder, and that goodness is therefore a human artifact; he seems to liken the sage to a potter who makes a vessel out of clay, rather than to a carpenter who merely uncovers a shape already hidden in the wood. What I cannot get past is that both prescribe nearly the same cure — education, ritual, exemplary teachers, lifelong self-cultivation — so I cannot tell whether their argument about human nature does any real work, or whether it is one program sold under opposite metaphysics. Our group has also split in a suspicious way: two of us think the dispute is verbal, that Mencius means "what humans are at their best" while Xunzi means "what humans are born as," and two think Xunzi has a genuine internal problem explaining, on his own premises, why anyone would want to become good if everything in us resists it. My hypothesis, which I would like tested rather than confirmed: the disagreement is neither verbal nor straightforwardly factual, but turns on whether moral goodness can be manufactured by culture from materials containing none of it, or must be the unfolding of something already there — and if that is right, then the two are disagreeing about what counts as an explanation of moral development, and no fact about infants could settle it. Please diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is, using what each side actually claims, and say what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a sharper question, a distinction, a test case — or else argue that we should expect none, and why. Answer as a numbered list of exactly five points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion.
