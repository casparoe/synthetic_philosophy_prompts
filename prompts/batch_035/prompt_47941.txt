In a discussion before the last election, my brother announced that he wouldn't be voting, because both major candidates were bad and, in his words, "the lesser evil is still evil." When I pointed out that one of them was clearly worse, he treated the line as a conversation-ender, as though it settled that staying home was the only clean option. Before I argue with him, I'd like to know what the slogan actually commits him to. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making — for example: that choosing among bad options is itself wrong regardless of which is worse; that voting for a candidate makes one complicit in, or shares responsibility for, what they later do; that there is a threshold of badness below which one may not lend support even to prevent something worse; that voting is an act of endorsement or expression rather than a causal intervention, so that a vote for the lesser evil says something one shouldn't say; or merely that both candidates are evil, a claim about them rather than about voters. For each reading, indicate whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false, and say which readings would actually support staying home rather than voting for the lesser evil, and which are compatible with voting anyway. Please present the readings as a numbered list I can take back to him.
