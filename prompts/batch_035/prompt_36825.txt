I am plotting a novel in two timelines, and I want a number before I commit to its structure. In the first timeline, my narrator's parents conceive a daughter on one particular night; in the second, the same parents conceive three days later, and a different child — different sperm, different egg, though the parents, the name, and the upbringing are the same — grows up in her place. The book turns on whether that second child is in any sense the same person as my narrator, and I need to know whether the metaphysics can support writing her that way. Please give me your credence that a person could have originated from a different sperm and egg and still have been that very person, decomposed into the sub-questions it rests on: the probability that origin essentialism is true outright, so that sameness of gametes is a necessary condition of personal identity; the probability that, if essentialism is false, some other relation — same parents, same developmental and social context, some form of psychological or narrative continuity — could instead ground identity across the difference; and whatever indeterminacy term you think belongs, since it may be that in a case like this there is simply no fact of the matter. Give each term a rough probability with one line of justification, show me how they combine into your final credence, and tell me which term the bottom line is most sensitive to and what would move you most on it. My stakes are practical: if your number is low, I am writing a book about the parents' grief for a child who was never anyone in particular; if it is high, I am writing about something like a soul indifferent to its body. I would like to know which book it is.
