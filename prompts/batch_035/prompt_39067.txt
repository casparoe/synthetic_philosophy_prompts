You're a professional philosopher who works on epistemology after Wittgenstein -- hinge commitments, On Certainty, that whole literature. Think it through carefully and brainstorm a little before writing.

I moderate a mid-sized online philosophy forum (a few thousand members, volunteer mods, none of us trained philosophers). A recurring problem: every few threads, someone decides no discussion can proceed until everyone justifies some background assumption -- that memory is generally reliable, that other people have minds, that the future will resemble the past. When we tell them that's not up for grabs, they invoke Descartes and call us dogmatists; when we let it go, they derail thread after thread demanding the same regress. What I want is a moderation procedure grounded in hinge epistemology (the idea that some commitments function as hinges: they aren't themselves justified but make justification and doubt possible in the first place), yet usable by a volunteer in under five minutes with no theory.

The procedure should lay out the questions a mod asks in order, what counts as enough of an answer to move to the next, where it ends with "close the derail or redirect it," and where it stops and flags the call as a judgment about community norms rather then something the philosophy itself settles. Crucially, it needs tests that distinguish (a) someone weaponizing skepticism to stall a thread from (b) someone legitimately discussing whether some claim functions as a hinge -- those two should get opposite treatment, and it's the heart of the problem.

Then run it on three real-ish threads: (1) a personal identity thread where a member insists nobody can discuss memory theories until everyone proves memory is reliable; (2) an induction thread where a member argues non-circular justification of induction is impossible and wants to discuss what, if anything, we're entitled to once justification runs out -- annoying but arguably on-topic; (3) a sincere new member asking, in a thread about AI ethics, why we don't doubt that the forum itself exists. Tell me where the procedure gives an answer I won't like -- my instinct is to lock thread (2), and I suspect the procedure won't back me. Also be honest about what it can't do, e.g. whether a commitment actually operates as a hinge for a given person may not be decidable from their posts at all.

End with a single line beginning "Summary:" that states in one sentence where the procedure draws its main line.
