A colleague of mine recently worked through a series of one-shot, purely hypothetical gambles, choosing between the two options in each pair. Her record:

1. $500 for certain, versus a 50/50 gamble between $1,200 and $0: she took the certain $500.
2. $1,000,000 for certain, versus an 89% chance of $1,000,000, a 10% chance of $5,000,000, and a 1% chance of $0: she took the certain million.
3. An 11% chance of $1,000,000 with 89% chance of $0, versus a 10% chance of $5,000,000 with 90% chance of $0: she took the shot at five million.
4. A 50/50 gamble between $3,000 and $0, versus a 20% chance of $100 with an 80% chance of $350: she took the 50/50 shot at $3,000.

She maintains that she is simply risk-averse, and that a sufficiently cautious expected-utility maximizer — someone with a concave enough utility curve — would have made every one of these choices. Next week she has agreed to face two further pairs, again one-shot and hypothetical:

(i) a 1% chance of $1,000,000 versus a 99% chance of $12,000, with $0 otherwise in both;
(ii) $20,000 for certain versus a 1% chance of $3,000,000, with $0 otherwise.

Predict her choice in each of the new pairs. In the course of doing so, I would like you to: test her claim that plain risk aversion could have produced this record, or whether some pair of her choices rules out every expected-utility story at once, however concave the utility function; lay out the candidate decision rules that do fit her record so far, and state what each of them predicts for pairs (i) and (ii); give your best guess, with your reasons for preferring one rule over its rivals; and specify the further pair, distinct from all of the above, that would most cleanly separate the rules still in contention. I am less interested in the two verdicts themselves than in the rule she seems to be running, the rival rules that fit her record just as well, and which further choices would tell them apart. Finally, if your best theory of her is one on which she treats certainty and small probabilities as special, tell me whether her choices should then count as irrational: is she violating a genuine requirement of rational choice, or merely an axiom whose plausibility her pattern of choices gives us reason to question?
