ok so this just got real for me: my partner was offered a posting in Singapore, we'd be there 3+ years, and our kid would start school there. before saying yes I tried to write down what I actually believe about political legitimacy, and I'd like someone to check whether it hangs together. the list:

(1) legitimacy has to come from the governed — some form of actual consent or authorization, not from outcomes. (2) consent can be tacit: living somewhere, voting, building a life there can count as consenting, and I don't buy the standard "you can't afford to leave, so it's not consent" objection, at least for wealthy states people can emigrate from pretty easily. (3) the Singapore government is genuinely legitimate — obeying it's laws there wouldn't be mere prudence, we'd owe it something like fairness. (4) I reject the comparative story: a regime can't gain legitimacy just by beating the alternatives, and "it works, and anything else would be worse" never by itself creates political obligations. (5) but results aren't nothing — a procedurally respectable government that ran the place into the ground would lose its legitimacy, so outcomes must be able to defeat consent somehow. (6) a vote only counts as consent if the menu isn't controlled — when the ruling party effectively decides what's on the table and goes after opposition, ballot majorities aren't authorization. (7) for all that, I'm confident China's government is not legitimate despite the poverty-alleviation record, while Singapore's is, and I don't think the difference is just scale or history.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like it, rank them by severity, give the cheapest fix for each, and say which single revision would clean up the most. I genuinely don't know where the problem is, so don't soft-pedal any of them on my behalf.
