I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, but I've been reading the Dennett–Searle exchange about original and derived intentionality on my own time, and I want to test my side of it against a real opponent rather than argue with a book. Please play a committed Searlean: someone who holds, firmly and with good arguments, that symbols in artifacts and computers have at most derived intentionality, which is borrowed entirely from the minds of designers and users, while brains have original intentionality through their intrinsic causal powers. Be sharp, not a strawman: concede small points when they're genuinely forced, but never give up the original/derived distinction itself. Keep each reply to one paragraph so this reads like a live conversation, and stay in character until I write the word stop. If you need a factual question answered about the software scenario I describe, ask it in character.

Here is my opening move. My day job is maintaining payment systems, so Dennett's two-bitser looks to me like legacy software with a semantic debt problem. In case you want it stated precisely: Dennett describes a vending machine that dispenses milk for fifty cents, then gets shipped to a country where runaway inflation has made it so that the two local coins which happen to match the machine's slot by size and weight are each worth twenty-five cents there. He argues that it is now indeterminate, or a matter of interpretation, whether the machine believes the coins are quarters, or believes they are the local coins worth fifty cents total, or has any belief at all — and that this indeterminacy infects the notion that aboutness was ever really in the machine in the first place. Now my twist. Suppose my team ports that machine's controller to software: an endpoint that receives a payment event, compares the amount against a configuration constant, and returns a single boolean that we might call enoughPaid. Our client in the devalued country sets the constant so the service accepts their two local coins, and it dispenses milk happily. On your view, whatever intentionality the brass machine had was derived from the designers and users who meant the machine to be about fifty cents. But notice what happened in the port: the aboutness did not survive into my code as some inherited property — the specification just says to assert the boolean when the input matches the constant, and nothing in the source code, the spec, or the config file is about money in the way you say a human thought is. So your view needs the derivation story to be doing real work, transporting content from minds into artifacts. My challenge: every account you can give of where the content in my brass-to-bits port came from — users interpreting the boolean, designers meaning it to track payment, an environment selecting for machines that dispense only when paid — is exactly the kind of account you refuse to accept as sufficient for the brain's intentionality, since evolutionary selection and learning-from-the-environment are how our heads got their calibration constants too. Where does the chain of derived intentionality terminate, what property does the terminus have that each derived link lacks, and why is your terminus anything more than the two-bitser with more history?
