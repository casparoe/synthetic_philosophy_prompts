For the past few years my sister and I have been arguing about what a person should do when she doubts her own moral convictions. My sister's position is that uncertainty between moral views works like uncertainty about ordinary facts: assign each view a rough degree of confidence, then choose whichever action comes out best when you weigh the views by those confidences. My position is that this is a confusion — different views don't share a common scale of "how good" an outcome is, so they can't be averaged into a single judgment, and the only coherent policy is to act on whichever single view you find most credible, even if it is only narrowly ahead. One case that keeps dividing us: suppose I am 55% confident in a view on which breaking a minor promise to a stranger would do a small amount of good overall, and 45% confident in a view on which breaking it is a grave wrong. Her method says keep the promise; mine says break it, and each of us thinks the other's verdict exposes a defect in the method. We have been talking past each other, and I would like to know exactly where we actually disagree. Please draft the joint statement we should have produced by now: the claims about acting under moral uncertainty that both of us could sign; the remaining disagreements, each phrased so that the person holding that view would accept the wording; and the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of us — where possible with a concrete case or argument both of us would accept as bearing on each crux, along with a record of what each of us would predict about it. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. If any part of our dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.
