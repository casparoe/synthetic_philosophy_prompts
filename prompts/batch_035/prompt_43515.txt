i've been going in circles on the hole argument for weeks now and i think the real problem is i keep evaluating positions one at a time (naive manifold substantivalism, sophisticated substantivalism, the structuralist escape routes) instead of figuring out what i actually need from an account in the first place. so before i pick a side, write me the requirments list: what any adequate view about the ontology of spacetime points has to deliver to count as an answer to the hole argument at all, where adequacy is relative to the purpose of telling me what GR commits me to ontologically.

some things i suspect belong on the list but am not sure about, so tell me if you agree:

- it has to say something non-circular about how points get their identity. if the answer is "via the metric and fields on them," that's fine, but then it has to own up to what that costs (points in diffeomorphic models being indistinguishable in principle) rather than sliding between treating points as primary and as derivative whenever it's convenient
- it has to deliver a verdict on whether GR is deterministic, and explain why determinism is even at stake here -- like, what exactly the hole construction shows about the failure of laplacian determinism when the initial data outside the hole doesn't fix what's inside it
- it has to respect that the diffeomorphism in the hole carries the metric and matter fields along, so whatever redundancy there is, it's in point identity, not in anything even remotely observable. a view that gets its answer by pretending the shifted model differs observably is disqualified
- it has to earn the right to say diffeomorphic models represent the same physical situation (or don't) -- some account of representation, not just a stipulation, since that stipulation is exactly what the argument is about
- it should generalize: does the story still work for spacetimes with symmetries, or the 'hole' being the whole manifold, or other gauge theories where the same problem shows up without the substantivalist baggage

for each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it. then do two more things: first, mark which of these are genuine desiderata and which are really just camp preferences dressed up (e.g. is "no haecceitistic differences" a demand or an anti-substantivalist prejudice? is determinism non-negotiable or is that just a metaphysical taste?). second, and this is the part i most want to see, flag whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable -- if you can't have bare point identity AND determinism AND diffeomorphism invariance all at once, say so plainly, because then the list itself is the result.

then, briefly, a scorecard of naive manifold substantivalism, sophisticated substantivalism (just accept leibniz equivalence), and a straightforwardly structuralist view against your list. keep the scoring short, the criteria are what i want.
