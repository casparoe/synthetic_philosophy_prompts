I'm an applied econometrician by training and temperament: I think in terms of treatment variables, confounders, directed acyclic graphs, identification strategies, instruments, and mediation analysis. Please restate Weber's Protestant ethic thesis — the claim that a cultural-religious package (Calvinist anxiety about one's election, worldly asceticism, the idea of a calling) helped cause the emergence of modern capitalism — in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Treat it as a causal claim I might try to estimate: what is the treatment, what is the outcome, where are the confounders, what would count as an instrument (missionaries saving the failed, perhaps?), and how do I handle the fact that nobody was randomly assigned a confession? Then give me the residue report: which parts of Weber's claim translate cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all. Two candidates for the residue interest me especially: Weber's insistence that the ethic worked by changing the meaning people attached to everyday economic activity — what he seems to have meant by an interpretive level of explanation — and his own hedging in terms of elective affinity rather than plain causation. I want your considered view on whether the untranslatable remainder reflects something missing from my toolkit or shows that Weber's claim was less precise than it looked; and if the latter, whether that vagueness is a defect of the thesis or is doing real explanatory work that precision would destroy.
