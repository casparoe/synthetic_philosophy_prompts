My dorm does a yearly weird betting thing and this time my friends Dana and Kris got into it over what to do. The game is a mini version of that Luring Lottery thing Hofstadter ran in a Scientific American column decades ago: six of us each put $100 into a pot, and by midnight we each privately text the organizer either IN or OUT. If exactly one person says IN, they take the whole $600. If two or more say IN, or if nobody does, the pot gets donated to a cause we all actively dislike (long story). So the only way anyone comes out ahead is if exactly one of us defects against everyone else staying out.

Kris went first: obviously you text IN. Staying out guarantees you nothing, entering gives you a shot at the whole pot, and it can never leave you personally worse off. It basically dominates.

Dana said that's exactly why it's a trap: we've all read the same stuff, we all know the dominance argument, so if it moves anyone it moves all of us, and we end up with the money donated. You should reason the way you'd want the whole group to reason.

Kris replied that "reasoning the way the group should" is just fancy talk for my choice being evidence about theirs, and evidence isn't causation — I can't un-text their entries, so at the moment I decide, entering is still whats best for me.

Dana conceded the bare correlation point wouldn't do it, but said its not correlation, it's that the six of us are one decision procedure running six times.

Kris: then your theory needs us to be literal copies, and we're not — Theo already announced he's going IN. One defector and the "reason together" outcome collapses into the donated pot anyway, at which point dominance is back for the rest of us.

Dana's last move: fine, but superrational reasoning still gives a recommendation — everyone should enter with probability 1/6, which maximizes the chance that exactly one of six enters, something like 40%, way better than zero. Kris said that smuggles in a shared randomizer plus everyone honoring a coin flip that dominance tells them to ignore the second it lands.

They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Who's right at each step, is the probability-1/6 move a genuine advance or a cheat, and does Theo's announcement actually settle anything?
