Retired physicist here (solid state, 35 years, never gave philosophy a thought until I had too much time on my hands). My reading group just did Wigner's unreasonable effectiveness essay and it turned into a shouting match, because it turns out there are like six different explanations floating around and none of us could keep them straight. So I want a decision tree we can actually use. Start from the shared starting point — math developed with no regard for physics keeps turning out to be exactly what physics needs (group theory for particle classifications, Riemann sitting around for 60 years before general relativity picks up his geometry) — and branch on questions you could answer without knowing where they lead: would aliens converge on our theorems; is the fit between math and physics something that even needs explaining, or is it only surprising if you expected it to fail; are we just noticing the mathematical structures that worked and forgetting the heaps of formalism nobody ever used; did math come out of distilling physical experience in the first place; and does the fact that math is strangely *ineffective* in biology and economics count as data, and against which branch? The leaves should include the platonist, the selection-effect view (Wigner kind of gestures at it himself), the empiricist story where math is just physics digested, the Kantian/constitutive view where math works because we literally could not do science without it, and the indispensability view where math gets confirmed along with the physics that uses it. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate, and note any branch that nobody actually holds — I'd like to know if thats because it's incoherent or just unpopular.
