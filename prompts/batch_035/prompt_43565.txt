So I just spent basically the whole evening fighting with my friend Dana about Dennett's multiple drafts thing and I'm still annoyed, so I want you to write an essay about it and settle it for me.

Background in case it helps: the Cartesian theater idea is that there's some central place in the mind/brain where all the info comes together and gets "shown" to consciousness, some inner audience watching the show. Dennett says this is a mistake, there's no single place, just lots of parallel drafts being edited and no privileged final version. His model is supposed to replace the theater with something more like a chaotic newsroom where different versions circulate and none is officially canonical.

Here's the actual fight. Dana's position is that the multiple drafts model is basically right and that the Cartesian theater is a strawman anyway — nobody serious thinks there's a little homunculus sitting in the head watching a screen, so what exactly did Dennett destroy? She says every scientific explanation of a system has something like a "presentation layer" where outputs get integrated (a thermostat, an autopilot, whatever) and nobody calls that a theater, so the theater is only scary if you smuggle in an audience, and Dennett's model rightly denies the audience but that's it.

My counter, and this is the part I actually want you to take seriously: doesn't Dennett need something theater-shaped anyway to explain the *sense* that there's a show? He talks about narrative fixation, and he's got this thing he calls the "center of narrative gravity," a kind of fictional self the brain constructs as the protagonist of the story. But the moment you say some drafts get fixed as THE story, for SOME perspective, haven't you just rebuilt the audience one level up and made it fictional? A fictional audience in a fictional seat is still a seat. I tried to sharpen this with a distinction: a *presentation* theater (just an integration bottleneck, which every complex system has, harmless) versus an *appreciation* theater (somewhere it's like something to be the audience). My claim was Dennett at best dissolves the second kind and then quietly keeps the first under a new name, which makes the "revolution" less revolutionary than advertised. Dana said that's cheating, because "fixed as canonical" doesn't mean "shown to anyone," it just means certain drafts win the competition for control of behavior and report, and you can get all the phenomenology-lite facts you need from that without any watching. Then we got into the color phi illusion and the whole question of whether the brain "revises history" (the rewrite happens before vs after the moment of awareness and whether that distinction even means anything without a theater) and honestly at that point we were mostly yelling about whether "it seems to the system as if" is doing illegitimate work in Dennett's account.

Write the essay on this controversy: does the multiple drafts model actually eliminate the Cartesian theater, or does it relocate and redescribe it? Weigh both sides fairly — give Dana's "it's just an integration bottleneck, stop crying about homunculi" version its strongest form, and my "narrative fixation is the audience in disguise" objection its strongest form. Don't end with some mushy "both perspectives have merit" thing — I need a verdict, and it can be a nuanced one (like "the model kills the appreciation theater but concedes a weaker presentation theater, and that concession matters / doesn't matter because..."), but commit to something.
