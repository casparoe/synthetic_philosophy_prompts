I'm in a bioethics reading group and we're doing a satirical zine issue on reproductive technology, each entry parodying a different corporate genre. My contribution is going to be a customer FAQ page for a fictional embryo-selection startup (call it something like "FirstChoice Genomics" or invent your own bland startup name). The conceit is that the company takes Julian Savulescu's procreative beneficence principle completely for granted — the idea that parents are morally obliged to select, among the children they could have, the one with the best expected life — and treats it as an unremarkable product feature, like free shipping.

Write the FAQ, around 600-700 words, in deadpan numbered Q&A format (Q7, Q8, etc.). The humor has to come from getting the philosophy right, not from generic designer-baby jokes. So for instance: one question should be from a parent worried about the non-identity problem — if they'd had a different child, that child would still have been happy, so whom exactly did the selection benefit? — and the company's answer should wave it away with a confidence that's funny precisely because it's philosophically shaky. Another question should come from a disability-rights angle (the expressivist worry: what does it say to existing disabled people that we screen against their condition?) and get a genuinely evasive corporate answer. Maybe include a question from an adult who was selected and is now annoyed at being optimized — "I'm fine, but I wanted to be a poet" — and a refund/warranty clause about what happens when the "best expected life" doesn't materialize. A sidebar or fine-print section about the fate of unselected embryos, handled with maximum corporate euphemism, would be great.

The voice throughout should be sincere, chipper, customer-service politeness — no winking at the reader, the satire should stay legible without the author ever breaking character. Aim it at someone who's read Parfit and Savulescu so the dodges are recognizable as the actual dodges, but it should still be readable to someone who just knows what IVF is. If you can work in one small original gag I haven't suggested — maybe about a "connoisseur tier" of selection, or a loyalty program — even better, I want at least one bit that isn't the obvious designer-baby joke everyone's heard before.
