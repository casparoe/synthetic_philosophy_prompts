My notes (rough):

1. Gongsun Long's famous thesis, that a white horse is not a horse, looks like trolling, but I think it's a fairly boring point dressed up provocatively. The boring point: white horse and horse are different terms that pick out different classes. Everything that satisfies white horse satisfies horse, but the terms still differ in what they demand — horse demands horse-shape, white horse demands horse-shape plus whiteness. So the terms aren't intersubstitutable: if you ask for a horse you can be fussy about colour afterwards, but if you ask for a white horse you have already committed on colour. Hence, in the sense relevant to what one can request or how one can name things, a white horse is not a horse — the claim is about sameness of terms, not about membership of classes, and the two terms are plainly not the same term.

2. This makes Gongsun Long basically a forerunner of Frege: sameness or inclusion of reference, but difference of sense. The dialogue is an early exercise in separating a term's extensional contribution from its intensional contribution, which is the same distinction Frege later draws between Bedeutung and Sinn.

3. So the School of Names people were doing semantics avant la lettre, not logic-chopping for its own sake. The reason the later tradition wrote them off as mere sophists is basically that the Confucian-influenced mainstream cared about ethical self-cultivation, not language. Their loss.

4. Dating: I've currently got Gongsun Long down as fifth century BCE but I'm honestly not sure, the sources I've seen are all over the place.
