Paul Grice down (he died in 1988, well before the contemporary debate about the meaning of slurs got going) and present him with the following puzzle.

Quick refresher so we're on the same page: Grice distinguished conversational implicatures — worked out on the fly from the assumption that the speaker is being cooperative — from conventional implicatures, which he tied to specific words like "therefore" and "but". Saying that someone is poor but honest, on his view, has the same truth conditions as saying they're poor and honest; the contrastive bit contributed by "but" is truth-conditionally inert, yet it doesn't seem cancellable the way a conversational implicature is. You can't really tack on "but I don't mean to imply any contrast" without sounding like you're denying what you just asserted.

Now here's the thing: one live position in the modern literature is that the derogatory content of a slur is exactly a conventional implicature. Its rivals say the derogation is (a) plain at-issue semantic content, (b) a presupposition the slur triggers, or (c) not really semantic at all — more like an expression of contempt, on analogy with showing a middle finger.

So my questions for you:

1. Knowing Grice's own machinery, would he plausibly have gone for the conventional implicature analysis of slurs? What would attract him to it, and where would he feel the pinch?
2. Slurs seem to project out of negation, questions, and conditionals ("if that guy is a [slur], then he shouldn't be hired" still sounds awful). Presuppositions famously project too — should that push Grice toward the presupposition camp instead, and how would he even keep the two apart for this case?
3. What about reclamation? When in-group members use a slur among themselves without any derogation, doesn't that suggest the content is cancellable after all — which is precisely what conventional implicatures resist?
4. Bonus: Grice also cared about the distinction between what a word means for everyone and what a particular speaker accomplishes on an occasion. Does reclamation fit that, or does it break it?
