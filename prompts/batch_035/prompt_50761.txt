Been going down a philosophy-of-truth rabbit hole lately after a LessWrong thread re-litigated whether the liar paradox is 'just a word game' (people love saying that and it's never once convinced me). What I want from you is a tier list of the main responses to the liar: Tarski's hierarchy of languages, Kripke-style fixed-point approaches where liar sentences come out ungrounded/gappy, dialetheism (Priest's route: some sentences are both true and false but logic is paraconsistent so nothing explodes), revision theory in the Gupta–Belnap tradition, contextualist approaches where the extension of the truth predicate shifts as the conversation moves, and — ranked even though it's barely a theory — the 'the liar just fails to express a proposition' dismissal. Sort them into tiers by how much you'd actually trust each one as an account of truth, with a sentence or two on why each lands where it does.

The catch is that the ranking has to be revenge-sensitive. What I mostly care about is how each approach copes with the strengthened liar — once you've declared the liar sentence gappy, or both-true-and-false, or failed-to-express, you get hit with the sentence that says of itself that it's not true in the richer sense you just helped yourself to. Two friends of mine have staked out views I'd like the ranking to adjudicate. One reckons Curry's paradox is the real load-bearing problem — you get trouble from a conditional alone, no negation needed — so any theory that handles the liar but chokes on Curry is treating symptoms rather than disease. The other says the whole job of a truth predicate is expressive economy: letting you say finitely what you'd otherwise need infinitely many sentences to say, doing semantic ascent and descent on demand, so a theory that's consistent but crippled (can't endorse its own T-biconditionals, or shatters truth into a heirarchy of predicates none of which applies to the language you're actually speaking) has lost the plot even if it's technically safe. For each tier, note where that approach stands on Curry and on whether the truth predicate still does its actual job.

And at the end, tell me straight: is any of these genuinely satisfying, or is the honest answer that the liar shows that truth in natural language is just broken, and we're picking which damage we'd rather live with? I'd also like one line on whether the 'just a word game' people deserve to be at the bottom of the list or whether they're onto something that all the fancy theories are elaborately missing.
