For about fifteen years I've had migraines preceded by weird auras, and somewhere along the way I built up a little notation system for tracking them. There are two distinct pre-aura feelings I get, maybe twenty minutes before an attack, that I've never managed to map onto anyone else's descriptions — I privately call them "the hum" and "the fizz" and have shorthand symbols for them in a spreadsheet. The system is genuinely useful: if I log a fizz I take my medication immediately and it usually staves off the attack, if it's a hum I can wait. So the notation makes predictions that either come true or don't, and I've refined it over hundreds of episodes.

Here's my problem. A friend who did a philosophy degree says this whole setup is philosophically impossible — something like "a language only one person can understand can't really be a language, there's a famous argument that your diary symbols can't actually mean anything." I looked into it enough to know she means the private language argument, but every version I find involves someone writing a sign in a diary for a recurring sensation, and honestly my case feels different: my symbols are anchored to public words ("pre-aura feeling", "migraine", "attack"), they're tested against public outcomes (did a migraine happen or not, did the medication work), and the whole thing is parasitic on English. Yet the core of what distinguishes hum from fizz — the feel of each — is something nobody but me has ever had direct access to, and I sometimes genuinely wonder whether I've been consistent in how I use the symbols over the years, since it's all down to my memory.

I want an expert take here, so please answer as a philosopher of language would, and think it through carefully rather than just giving me slogans. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble. Specifically: is my notation a genuine counterexample to the claim that a private language is impossible, does it simply not count as a private language for whatever makes the argument work (and if so, what exactly is the difference between my case and the case the argument targets), or is my friend right that something is wrong with my system but wrong about what? I'd like to actually understand what the argument is supposed to establish, so I can tell for myself whether my little spreadsheet is inside its scope or not.
