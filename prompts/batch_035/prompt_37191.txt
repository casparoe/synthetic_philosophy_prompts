Is there a name for this? I work in data science and keep bumping into a category I don't have words for. Some things are real in the boring way — electrons, this chair. Some things are real only because we all agree on them — which side of the road you drive on, the value of a particular baseball card. But there's a third thing I keep noticing that doesn't fit either bucket. Take a chess position. "White is up a knight" is just a fact — you can be flat-out wrong about it. But "knight" isn't a kind physics knows anything about; it's a pattern we impose on little carved pieces or on voltage patterns in silicon. Or take "depression" as a diagnostic category: literally invented by committees, but once invented it seems to carve the world at joints that show up independently in outcome data, heritability, treatment response. Or take my roomba: saying it "wants" to get back to its dock is in some sense the *shortest true description* of it I can give — the full causal story is thousands of lines of code, the want-story is one sentence and predicts where it goes. So the thing I'm after: stuff that is (a) not fundamental physics, (b) in some sense projected onto the world by us, but (c) not arbitrary — where there's a fact of the matter, being wrong is possible, and the pattern earns its keep by compressing and predicting. I know about "natural kinds" and I know about "socially constructed" and this feels like neither, or like it straddles them in a specific way I can't pin down. Is there an actual term or literature for patterns that are real *because* they're good compressions rather than despite it? And if so, is there any principled criterion for how much compression or predictive payoff a pattern needs before it counts as real rather than as a convenience? Edge cases I keep chewing on: constellations (patterns we picked for story reasons that don't predict anything), money during hyperinflation (a pattern that was real and then stopped being real), and chess engine evaluation numbers (a projected quantity that somehow turned into a measurement instrument). I'm not just fishing for a label — I want to know where the edges of this concept are, how it relates to the natural/social divide, and whether any of my edge cases actually fall outside it.
