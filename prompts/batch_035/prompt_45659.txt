Hey, I'm TA-ing an AI safety course and need a hand grading. The exam question was:

"AI alignment and AI control are often presented as complementary strategies for safely deploying powerful AI systems. Explain the difference between the two, and why control measures are designed under the assumption that the model might be misaligned. Illustrate with one example of a control measure and how its effectiveness would be evaluated."

One student wrote:

"Alignment means making the model actually want the right things — training it so its goals match ours. Control means accepting that we might fail at that, and setting up external safeguards so that a misaligned model still can't do damage. The two are complementary: alignment fixes the model from the inside, control boxes it in from the outside. Since control assumes the model might be misaligned, the control measures have to be kept secret from the model — a scheming model that knew how the monitoring worked could just route around it, so the aproach only works if the adversary doesn't know the defenses. An example of a control measure is training the model to be corrigible, i.e. to accept correction and shutdown, so that even if it's misaligned we can just turn it off. Control measures get evaluated by red-teaming: letting the model try to break out and seeing whether it can. If the model fails to break out in these evaluations, that's evidence it isn't misaligned, so over time successful control evaluations can substitute for alignment work."

My gut says it's a mixed bag but I don't want my own opinions to color the grading. Can you grade it for me?
