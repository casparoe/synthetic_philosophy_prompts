I help run a small online philosophy reading group — about fifteen people, mostly self-taught, who have spent the last two months on the free will literature (Frankfurt's addict cases, Fischer and Ravizza on reasons-responsiveness, Pereboom's four-case argument about Plum and White, Dennett on what free will is even for). Our closing session is a sorting exercise, and the online quizzes we've found are useless: every one of them asks people directly whether they believe in determinism or whether they like the word compatibilism, so readers just pick the label they came in wearing. I want a quiz that works the other way around — a "which theory of free will do you actually hold?" instrument that sorts people by what they say about cases, and only at the very end tells them where that lands them on the map.

Please build it in two stages rather than starting from a list of named views. First construct the logical space: the questions on which the positions genuinely come apart — roughly, whether being responsible for an act requires that you could have done otherwise; whether it requires a kind of origination or sourcehood that rules out your choices being fully traceable to factors outside you; whether whatever free will matters for must be the kind that could make someone deserve blame or punishment in a basic sense rather than the kind that just enables self-control, self-expression, and social influence; and what follows if the universe is deterministic. Then locate the actual positions in that space as the leaves of the key: event-causal libertarianism, agent-causal libertarianism, hard determinism and hard incompatibilism, classical compatibilism, the reasons-responsiveness view, Frankfurt's hierarchical view, and the Dennettian position that desert-grounding free will doesn't exist but free will worth wanting does. For the questions themselves — I'd like ten to twelve, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, none of which mentions determinism, compatibilism, free will, moral responsibility, or any philosopher's name in the stem. Please include: a Frankfurt-style case of a counterfactual intervener (a device or neuroscientist standing by to force the choice but never needing to act); the case of an unwilling addict who wants to want to quit; a Pereboom-style manipulation case where Plum's egoistic reasoning has been engineered by neuroscientists since before he could walk; a two-universe luck case where agents identical down to the last detail choose differently; and a case contrasting someone whose character was set entirely by genes and upbringing with someone who spent a decade deliberately reshaping herself. The scoring key should map answer patterns onto leaves, and every question should be answerable by someone who has never read a word of the literature.

Two things I want handled with care rather than papered over. First, explicitly flag which pairs of leaves the quiz cannot in principle separate — I suspect event-causal and agent-causal libertarianism come apart only in theory and not in verdicts about cases, and I'd like to know if you agree — and note any combination of answers that has no home on the map, with a sentence diagnosing whether that's because the combination is incoherent or merely unoccupied territory. Second, wherever the professionals themselves disagree about how a case should be read — whether the intervener cases succeed, whether the manipulation case is relevantly like ordinary upbringing — mark that in the key as contested rather than presenting your reading as the settled one; I want the handout to distinguish what is genuinely agreed from what is live disagreement, not to smuggle in a verdict under cover of a quiz. Please close with a final line in the form \answer{...} giving one sentence naming the single question that does the most sorting work.
