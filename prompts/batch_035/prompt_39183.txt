For my history of ethics seminar we have this kinda cheesy assignment where we write an imaginary interview with a philosopher, and I picked Bentham because we just read the essay on paederasty (written around 1785, later published as "Offences Against One's Self") that he never dared to publish during his lifetime. I'm supposed to come up with questions that actually put pressure on his argument instead of just letting him restate it, and each question needs a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give. Can you write me around eight of these? Things I especially want covered: the gap between showing an act produces no pain in anyone and showing it's positively fine; the awkward fact that his own taxonomy of "irregularities of the venereal appetite" lists same-sex acts right alongside necrophilia, bestiality and masturbation as cases of using an "improper object," which makes it look like his no-harm argument clears the whole catalog at once — does he accept that consequence?; whether his describing the relevant taste as "perverted" is just him adopting the enemy's language for rhetorical safety or a real concession that the preference is defective; and the tension between a philosopher whose whole political theory runs on publicity and transparency keeping this one manuscript in a drawer til death. Maybe also one question about whether the partners being "both willing" is actually doing any work in a system where the only real wrong is pain produced — like does consent matter in itself or only as evidence about pleasure and pain?
