i'm teaching an upper-level seminar on population axiology this term (mostly grad students, a couple of very keen undergrads) and i'm writing the take-home final. i want one long-answer question on the repugnant conclusion, 90 minutes to answer, worth 20 marks, and the thing i want to avoid is the standard "recite Parfit's ladder and then name-drop four views" performance that theyve all seen a dozen times. so i want the question built around a made-up view that i'll state in full in the question stem, so they actually have to reason with it rather than recall a label.

here's the view — call it the Logarithmic View. the value of a population of N people (N ≥ 2) is the total welfare across all lives, divided by ln(N). that's it. it's cousin to the number-dampening idea that keeps coming up in the literature, but stated in this exact form i'm confident they won't have seen it. the question should then ask them to: (i) work out whether the view escapes the repugnant conclusion as Parfit sets it up, or just postpones it — i want them doing the asymptotics, not vibes; (ii) find at least one implication of the view that is at least as ugly as what it was supposed to fix — i won't spoil it in the stem, but you should build the marking scheme around them finding the case where adding a life clearly worth living to a small happy population makes the population worse, and explain why that happens; (iii) say what a genuine escape from the repugnant conclusion would have to look like if damping won't do it (lexical thresholds, person-affecting constraints, or rejecting the whole axiological framing — their choice, but they have to argue for one). tell students the view is new to them and that showing their arithmetic earns marks even when the final verdict is wrong.

also — a sharp student is going to notice that ln(1) = 0 and that a single-person population is undefined under the formula as stated. handle that however you like in the stem, but i want half a bonus mark reserved for anyone who spots it unprompted, so put it in the scheme.
