You are an experienced instructor and grader for a graduate seminar in classical Chinese philosophy, with particular expertise in the Warring States period dialecticians and the later Mohist logic texts. You are known among colleagues for fairness: you neither reward fluent-sounding prose that quietly fudges the substance, nor punish students who take defensible but minority interpretive stances.

Below is the essay question from the midterm exam, followed by one student's answer. Your task is to grade it.

**Exam question:**
"Reconstruct, as charitably as you can, the central argument of the White Horse Dialogue (baima fei ma), attributed to Gongsun Long. Explain the main logical objection that critics have pressed against it, and situate the puzzle in its intellectual context: what was at stake for the School of Names, how the later Mohist canons responded, and how later thinkers such as Xunzi reacted to this style of disputation? Include any philological caution you think a responsible answer requires."

**Student's answer:**
"Gongsun Long, a philosopher of the Warring States period, is credited with the argument that a white horse is not a horse. The dialogue is preserved in the text that bears his name, and it gives us a reliable record of his actual words, which makes reconstruction straightforward. The argument has two main steps.

First, the shape-and-color step: the term horse picks out shape, while the term white picks out color. Since a white horse combines both shape and color, it is not the same as what horse alone picks out. Second, the substitution step: if you ask for a horse, a yellow or black one will do; if you ask for a white horse, a yellow or black one will not. So horse and white horse do not have the same extension. Since the two terms differ in intension and the second's extension is included only in one direction, the conclusion follows that a white horse is not a horse.

The standard objection is equivocation: the argument slides between saying the terms differ and saying that nothing falling under white horse falls under horse. If we read the copula as identity between terms, the conclusion is true but trivially so; if we read it as ordinary predication, it is false. The charitable reading takes it as term-level non-identity.

This was not an isolated puzzle. The same argument reappears almost word for word in the Hard and White chapter of the same text. And the wider School of Names program is captured in Gongsun Long's own slogan that heaven and earth are one pointing and the ten thousand things are one horse. The later Mohists were sympathetic to this project of analyzing compound terms, and their canon's treatment of naming helped develop it. Xunzi, though critical of sophistry in practice, borrowed the School of Names' technical apparatus for his own rectification of names, so the tradition's reaction was more absorption than rejection.

Overall, the dialogue anticipates the modern distinction between sense and reference, and shows that Chinese philosophy had logic long before it had contact with the West."

**Your task:**
Before writing anything, think carefully and work through the student's answer point by point against what you know about the texts, the historical figures, and the logical structure of the argument.
