Two friends in my cog sci seminar have been arguing all week about color phi -- the illusion where you flash a red dot in one spot, then a green dot a bit to the side, and people swear they saw a single dot moving across and changing color somewhere mid-path, even though nothing green existed at that midpoint. Dev says the obvious moral is that the brain rewrote the record after the fact: you first saw red, then green, but within a split second the memory got overwritten so you recall motion and a mid-course color change -- the Orwellian story, as our prof called it. Elena says that's crazy -- much cleaner that the brain holds back the final "presentation" of experience until it has already done the filling-in, like an editor who only releases the edited cut (the Stalinesque story). Dev shot back that this would make experience lag behind the world and we'd notice the delay. Elena said his story is worse: it needs a fully conscious experience that gets instantly and invisibly erased, and asked who's even checking the "original" before the rewrite supposedly happens. Dev said at least his keeps experience tracking the stimulus in real time. Elena answered that no probe could ever distinguish the two -- any report or button-press comes out the same either way, since the rewrite would be done long before you could report -- so maybe there just is no fact of the matter about when the green "got into" consciousness, which is (she claims) Dennett's actual point. They made me referee and I honestly couldn't follow who's winning. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step -- not just who wins overall -- and whether there's something both are missing?
