For our next philosophy club meeting I would like a decision tree sorting members into positions in the early Confucian debate on human nature, to print as a one-page handout. The tree should begin from a neutral starting commitment that everyone in the room would accept — that people do end up with moral commitments — and each branch node should be a single question about a concrete case, answerable without knowing any position's name. Suggested cases: whether a bystander's sudden alarm at seeing a child about to fall into a well reveals a moral response that precedes any teaching; whether an infant left entirely untaught would grow up virtuous, merely capable of virtue, or unruly; whether moral training works by developing something already present, or by imposing order on something disordered, the way a carpenter straightens warped wood; and whether the right response to wrongdoing is to transform the person or merely to control their behavior. The leaves should include Mencius's sprouts view (moral beginnings that require cultivation), Xunzi's view that human nature is disordered and made good through ritual and instruction, Gaozi's view that nature is morally neutral, like water that can be channeled east or west, and the view Mencius reports some of his contemporaries holding — that certain people are simply born good and others born bad. Please also mark any leaf in the logical space that no classical thinker occupied, with one sentence on what believing it would commit someone to, and flag honestly whether the tree can separate Xunzi from the Legalists, given that Xunzi taught Han Fei and Li Si. Build the tree from the questions first and then locate the historical positions within it, rather than starting from the names.
