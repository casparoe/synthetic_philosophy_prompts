You're a professional political theorist who's been asked to facilitate an adversarial collaboration, and I want you to draft the joint statement those two camps would produce. The camps: people who think Fukuyama's end of history thesis is basically right (liberal democracy as the last standing ideological competitor, no coherent systemic alternative), and people who think it's been refuted or was never coherent to begin with (authoritarian capitalism à la China, civilizational pluralism, democratic backsliding, nationalism as a live ideological force).

Do the actual drafting work, not a summary of the debate. Start with a numbered section of claims both sides could sign their names to — about what the thesis really claims (directional history vs a terminal state, the thymos/megalothymos story about why recognition drives the whole thing, the difference between "no alternative ideology exists" and "no backsliding will happen"). Be careful here, a lot of the disagreement is about what Fukuyama actually said versus what people assume he said, and both sides know it. Then a disagreements table where each formulation is worded so its own side would accept it as a fair statement of their view. Then the cruxes — I suspect one is whether authoritarian capitalism can keep delivering growth and legitimacy without liberalizing, and another is whether nationalism or religious revival counts as an actual ideology with a positive vision of the good society or just as resistance to liberalism — and for each crux, one concrete case or argument both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal (e.g. the two sides meaning different things by "end of history"), say which part and why.

Brainstorm a little before writing — jot down what each side would refuse to sign and why — then produce the document itself. Watch that neither side's framing leaks into the shared claims section; that's the part where most of these collaborations fail. Keep it tight, it's a working paper not a book.
