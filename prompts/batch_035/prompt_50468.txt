My bookclub has been stuck in the same fight for months and I want to figure out what it's actually about. The fight: can you rank historical atrocities, like is the Holocaust "worse" than transatlantic slavery or the Holodomor, or is asking that obscene in itself? Half the room says comparing is just what historians do and the only way to get general lessons about how these things happen; the other half says ranking suffering is spitting on graves, full stop. Nobody has moved an inch since March.

My own hunch is that the fight isn't really about history or even about moral facts — it's about what a comparison is FOR. When you compare in order to explain (why did a genocide happen here and not there?) comparison seems mandatory, almost respectful. When you compare in order to mourn, it feels like betrayal. Same act, two totally different purposes, and maybe each half of the room is doing one of them without noticing the other is doing something else. And here's the thing I keep getting stuck on: every reparations payout, every memorial budget, every government's "never again" priority list is already a quiet ranking of horrors. So the anti-ranking side might not really be against ranking — they might just be against it being said out loud. Which would make this partly a fight about shame, not truth.

So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Verbal? Two different questions hiding under one word? Bedrock intuitions about grief that can't be argued for or against? Diagnose it using what each side actually says, tell me what (if anything) would count as progress or move anyone, and then give me the diagnosis in one machine-readable line at the very end, like \answer{verbal} or \answer{two-different-questions} or whatever label fits.
