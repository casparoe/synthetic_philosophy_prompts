First, Platonia: an eccentric trillionaire picks twenty people, forbids all communication between them, and announces that if exactly one of them sends him a reverse-charges telegram by noon the next day, that person gets a billion dollars — if two or more send, or nobody sends, nobody gets anything. Second, the Luring Lottery, modeled on the one Scientific American actually ran in 1983: you enter by mailing a postcard with a number on it, the number counts as that many entries, and the prize is a million dollars divided by the total number of entries received, awarded to the submitter of one randomly drawn entry.
