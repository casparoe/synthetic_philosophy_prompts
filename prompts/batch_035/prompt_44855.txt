I write a column on science and ideas for a mid-sized paper, and I'm working on a longer piece (~2,500 words) about Kierkegaard's idea of the public and what it might tell us about social media, and about AI more specifically. Before I send the draft to my editor I want a serious second read, and since my usual go-to just nods along, I'm hoping you'll be the one who actually pushes back.

The rough idea: in The Present Age (1846) Kierkegaard argues that the press doesn't just serve the public, it manufactures it — the public being this phantom aggregate, a "we" that gets to have opinions without anyone in particular being responsible for holding them. The age of the press, he thought, was an age of reflection-on-reflection: everybody chattering about everybody else's chatter. My essay's move is that the 19th century version still needed actual human readers to fill the phantom, but we've now built the phantom its own body: recommender systems that infer what "the conversation" wants, bot accounts, and increasingly LLM-generated comments engaging other LLM-generated comments. The public was always an abstraction, but now the abstraction can generate its own members. That's my hook.
