my partner and i got into a genuinely multi-day argument after reading Darwin's Dangerous Idea for our little philosophy of biology reading group, and i want to commemorate it. she's a skyhook person, or at least she keeps insisting that natural selection can't possibly explain X (consciousness, meaning, morality, whatever we're on about that evening), so something from outside must be doing the real lifting. i keep saying the whole point of the crane picture is that the lifting gets done by things built up from the bottom, slowly, each bit of design resting on earlier design, no miracle required. but she also caught me once being greedy about it -- i waved my hands and basically said love is "just" oxytocin plus reinforcement learning and she said THAT is not a crane, that's you smashing the thing you're trying to explain and calling the rubble an explanation. which, annoyingly, is a fair hit. so the cranes-vs-skyhooks thing only works if you keep the greedy version out too, and that's the part i want the poem to hold onto.

so: write me a villanelle on cranes vs. skyhooks. the two refrains should be one skyhook line and one crane line -- i want the skyhook refrain to sound seductive and serene, the kind of line that promises to hoist everything up in one miraculous pull, and the crane refrain to be the plodding counter: build from what's already there, no faster than the ground allows. the form should do real work here -- the way a villanelle's refrains keep coming back is the skyhook's persistence, but also, crucially, the crane's repeated assembly, so by the middle the two lines should start to sound like they're arguing about different questions while using nearly the same words. and somewhere in the poem (i don't care where, maybe in one of the middle stanzas) i want the greedy-reductionist temptation to show up and get refused -- a moment where the crane voice almost cheats by collapsing something into "just" its parts, and then backs off, because that's not building, that's demolition with a bow on it. the closing couplet... i mean the final stanza where both refrains land together should show why the skyhook never actually explains anything, it just hangs there -- explanation by suspension instead of support.

someone who knows Dennett should be able to tell which refrain is which and feel that the poem is fair to both sides (my partner will read it, and if the skyhook is just a strawman she'll never let me hear the end of it -- it has to be genuinely tempting). someone who's never heard of any of this should still get a decent poem out of it. don't name-drop Darwin or Dennett in the poem itself, the machinery should do it without labels. and no i don't need it to rhyme perfectly in every place but the form should be recognizable -- if you flatten the villanelle into just "a poem with a repeating line" she WILL notice, that's basically what the whole argument was about, things that look like the structure but skip the load-bearing parts.
