our reading group just finished a term on parfit and the next meeting is the session where everyone's position gets torn apart by everyone else. I pulled the short straw and have to go first, so before I get wrecked in person I want to check whether my position even hangs together. here it is:

(1) I'm a totalist about comparing futures — the ranking runs on total wellbeing. same people, better lives: better. same lives, more people living well: better. I don't really see what else it could be sensitive to.

(2) mere addition: adding one more life worth living, where nobody else is affected, can't make the world worse. people who deny this always seem to be working backwards from some conclusion they hate, though I admit I don't know which conclusion that would be, ha.

(3) transitivity: if A beats B and B beats C, then A beats C. I treat this as basically non-negotiable for a better-than relation — a ranking that doesn't chain isn't ranking anything.

(4) the repugnant conclusion is false. a world of 10^50 people whose lives are each just barely worth living is clearly worse than a world of 8 billion people with wonderful lives. this one feels rock solid to me.

(5) no lexical cutoffs: there's no quality threshold above which lives count and below which they don't. every life worth living adds something positive, however faint, and enough faint additions can outweigh a smaller number of intense ones. thresholds strike me as ad hoc.

(6) I accept ordinary quantity-for-quality trades: a future of 40 billion living good-but-not-great lives seems better to me than 8 billion living our lives, and I'd say the same about each further step of that kind.

(7) the sadistic conclusion is also false — adding a few lives not worth living can't be better than adding a lot more lives that are worth living. I take this as extra ammunition against anything average-shaped.

(8) I reject the make-people-happy-rather-than-make-happy-people slogan as a blanket rule — creating happy lives can make a world better — but I also think that when potential happy people are never concieved, nobody is wronged. the unconceived can't be victims, and I don't feel any tension here, though the group might.

so: go through these pair by pair. which pairs are genuinely inconsistent, which only look inconsistant, and for the genuine ones rank them by severity and tell me what the cheapest fix to each costs. then, if you had to amend or delete exactly one entry to clean up the most, which one? I honestly can't tell where the problem is and I'd rather find out from you than mid-session on tuesday.
