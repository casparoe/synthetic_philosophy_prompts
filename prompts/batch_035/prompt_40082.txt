Write a parody product page — Amazon style — for the Ring of Gyges (invisibility ring, shepherd edition). Around 700-800 words, with the usual sections: item description, "about this item" bullets, specs, a short seller Q&A ("asked by customers"), and at least five customer reviews with star ratings.

The target is Glaucon's challenge in book II of the Republic, so the jokes have to come from actually understanding the argument, not just "invisibility ring = funny." A few things I want worked in:

The "about this item" should retell the origin story — the earthquake, the chasm, the bronze horse, the corpse wearing the ring, discovering that turning the collet inward makes you invisible — and at least one review should be a five-star rating that doubles as a horror story: the ring worked exactly as advertised, and what Gyges did with it (messenger to the king, the queen, the coup, the throne of Lydia) is presented as a satisfied-customer outcome. The comedy there is that a perfect review of the product is a perfect indictment of the species.

Glaucon's setup should show up in the marketing copy itself: his claim that people are only just unwillingly, that justice is a compromise between doing injustice without paying the penalty and suffering injustice without being able to get revenge. So the seller should pitch the ring as the thing that lets you exit the compromise — "get away with it" as a listed feature, in the same deadpan register as battery life. No winking.

For the reviews: at least one reviewer should claim, apparently sincerely, that they wore it for months and stayed perfectly just — and you decide whether the joke is that they're transparently lying, that they're sincere and thereby quietly answer Glaucon, or that the ambiguity makes the reader do the actual philosophical work. Another reviewer should reply in the comments that anyone claiming to have stayed just while invisible is exactly who the ring was made for.

One review should be from someone who points out the ring only solves getting caught, not the being-vs-seeming problem: they got a reputation for injustice before the ring ever arrived (mixup, mistaken identity, doesn't matter) and have lived as the just person with the unjust reputation, so they have notes. They can be bitter about it.

The Q&A needs the obvious question — "will this make me a better person?" — answered by the seller in a way that quietly concedes Glaucon's whole point while remaining on-message about the product.

And one in-the-weeds touch: a review should mention that an earlier historian tells the Gyges story completely differently, so who even knows if the origin story in the listing is the right one. Do what you want with that.

Deadpan throughout — no authorial winks, the piece should stay legible as satire of the argument rather than of the characters. Someone who has actually read book II should feel the piece is fair to Glaucon (he's stress-testing a view, not confessing to anything), and someone who hasn't should still just find it a funny, mildly ominous product page. Please write it in American English.
