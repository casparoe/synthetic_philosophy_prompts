You are a philosopher of science with working knowledge of mechanistic explanation and of how modern interpretability research talks about features, circuits, and components inside neural networks. I am a research engineer on a team building runtime monitors for deployed language models, and I want your help with a conceptual problem that keeps stalling us: we call our method "mechanistic anomaly detection," meaning that a monitor should flag inputs that the model processes differently from how it processed the training distribution — not merely inputs that are statistically unusual, but inputs handled by what we casually call a different mechanism. The trouble is that nobody on the team can say what "the same mechanism" means, and our disagreements about particular cases track incompatible tacit accounts. One colleague individuates mechanisms by similarity in activation space; a second, by which causal routes through the network carry the relevant computation; a third says it's whatever a set of carefully chosen ablations would change. Before we argue further, I want the specification written down: what any adequate account of mechanism-individuation, for this purpose, has to deliver.

To make the desiderata non-trivial, here are the kinds of cases our account must rule on, which I offer as seeds rather than as fixed requirements — part of your job is deciding which of them genuinely constrain the account and which are prejudices of one camp. When the model answers 7×8, does it use "the same mechanism" as when it answers 63×29, if the first is a memorized table lookup and the second a digit-wise algorithm — and if the second is instead done by pattern-matching on neighboring facts? When a safety refusal fires, is the refusal "one mechanism" even when it is triggered by quite different input features (explicit criminal vocabulary versus an innocently worded request that happens to fit a harmful template)? If a mechanism is recruited at half its usual strength, or runs in parallel with a competitor that loses, is that the same mechanism, a weakened one, or two? And must the account be invariant under paraphrase — the same reasoning carried by different tokens in a different language — or is token-level difference a legitimate reason to call the mechanisms different? Also, since a monitor must render verdicts on single inputs without ground-truth labels at deployment time, the account has to be usable under that constraint, whatever else it demands.

Please think carefully, and begin with a short brainstorm before committing to the list. Then write the desiderata: each requirement with the case or consideration behind it, a marking of which are non-negotiable for our monitoring purpose and which are preferences of a particular research culture, and — the part I most want — a flag on any pair or subset of the desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. Finally, say which single requirement excludes the most candidate accounts, and close with a brief scorecard, no more than a few lines each, of the three rough accounts I named above (activation-similarity, causal-route identity, interventionist) against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard. Please write in American English, and do not include any citations, bibliographies, or reading recommendations — I want the reasoning, not the literature.
