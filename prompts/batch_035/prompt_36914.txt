I've been in a reading group on laws of nature for about two years, and the Humeanism vs. anti-Humeanism arguement has completely frozen in place. The Humeans (following Lewis) say the laws are just whatever regularities show up in the best systematization of all the particular facts -- a mosaic of local property instances, no governing needed. The anti-Humeans say no, laws govern: they produce or constrain or necessitate what happens, and that's what separates a law from a mere accident that looks just like it. Everyone in the room knows both sides' moves by now. The Humeans keep saying that "governing" is a metaphor that was never cashed out; the anti-Humeans keep saying the best-system account makes the laws too dependent on our systematizing interests and, worse, can't explain the patterns in the mosaic, since on that view the laws are just a summary of the very thing they were supposed to explain. Even the newer statistical-mechanics versions of Humeanism (where the best system gets to include a postulate about the low-entropy past) just spawned sub-arguments that stalled the same way. Nobody has moved an inch.

My working theory is that the whole thing turns on a disagreement about explanation: the anti-Humeans think a genuine explanation of an event has to reach something deeper than the event's own pattern, while the Humeans think explanation just is fitting things under a regularity, and each side finds the other's demand (or refusal to make the demand) literally unintelligible rather than wrong. But I'm honestly not sure. It could also be a hidden quasi-empirical question -- are there counterfactual-supporting necessities in nature or aren't there -- or it could be partly verbal, with "governs" and "law" meaning different things to the two camps.

What I want is a diagnosis, not a survey of positions we all know. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, using what each side actually says as evidence for your reading, and tell me whether anything real would count as progress here -- a new distinction, a thought experiment, a formal treatment of laws, a change of question -- or whether we should expect this one to grind on indefinitely, and why.
