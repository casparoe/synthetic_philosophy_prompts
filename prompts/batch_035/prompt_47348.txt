Been reading rationalist blogs since the lesswrong sequences days, SSC back when it was still updating, the whole ecosytem — and I've noticed a genre: someone gets into meditation, reads a bit of early Buddhist stuff, and posts the "no-self is just obviously true and here's the clean derivation" writeup. Saw one of these on a forum last week and it was more confident than most. From memory, the poster's summary went roughly like this:

The Buddha proved there is no self by doing an exhaustive search over the five aggregates — everything you could possibly mean by "yourself" falls into one of five buckets (body, feeling, perception, mental formations, consciousness), and none of the buckets can be the self, because they're all impermanent and you can't actually control any of them. So the self is a cognitive illusion, basically a user interface the brain renders, and the reason you suffer is that the UI includes a fictional owner of experience. Modern neuroscience confirms this: the default mode network is where the self-sense gets constructed, experienced meditators show altered DMN activity, so the "illusion" claim is now empirically settled. And once you see through the illusion, suffering ends automatically, because there's literally no one left to suffer — that's all nirvana is. The poster also claimed this makes the Buddha's position basically Hume's bundle theory two thousand years early, and that the only reason Buddhists still talk about rebirth and karma is cultural inertia — a bundle theory obviously can't have anyone who gets reborn, so traditional Buddhists are just running on a contradiction they haven't noticed.

I'm inclined to buy this because it's tidy and it matches the meditation-neuroscience stuff I've read, but I've seen people get absolutely roasted in comments for less confident takes. So: is this a fair summary? Please go claim by claim. What would someone who actually knows early Buddhist doctrine accept, what would they wince at, and what would they flat out say is wrong? I'm especially unsure about two things: whether the "none of the aggregates is you" argument is meant as a metaphysical proof that no self exists, or as something narrower and more pedagogical; and whether the "no one left to suffer" line is a real Buddhist claim about cessation or a pop-meditation gloss that quietly turns nirvana into annihilation. And does the poster's punchline — that rebirth talk is just an incoherent leftover — actually follow from the parts of the summary that are true, or does it depend on one of the parts that's wrong?

Also, don't just audit the poster, audit yourself. Where is your own reading most likely to be off — e.g. if your verdict leans on a contested interpretation of the texts rather than a settled one, say so instead of hiding it — and what argument or piece of evidence would actually move you to the opposite verdict on the fair/unfair question? I'd rather get a calibrated "here I'm maybe 60/40 because the scholarship itself splits" than a confident landgrab.
