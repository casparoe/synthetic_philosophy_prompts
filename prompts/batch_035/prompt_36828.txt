I write a small newsletter about consciousness and philosophy of mind, aimed at readers who've heard of panpsychism and the combination problem but haven't gone much deeper than a wikipedia skim. I want to build one of those "which position do you actually hold?" quizzes, and I want it to actually work, i.e. sort people by what they say about cases and not by which label sounds cool to them.

Some constraints:

1. Ten questions max, each one a mini-scenario with two or three answer options. No question may contain any -ism word or the word panpsychism itself. The scenarios should be things like: a straight line of 10,000 atoms where adjacent ones touch vs. a pile of the same atoms where they also touch — does arrangement alone matter for whether there's one subject there? A thermostat, a nematode, and a honeybee swarm, and where (if anywhere) unified experience cuts off. A split-brain-ish case where you gradually pull a composite subject apart into two spatially separated halves. Whether a being could be made of micro-experiences and have a single unified one without there being any further fact over and above the parts.

2. The scoring key needs to map answer patterns onto at least: constitutive panpsychism where some intrinsic relation does the combining, panprotopsychism (the parts have proto- properties that aren't quite experiential until combined), emergent panpsychism or strong emergence with new subjects that aren't composed of the micro-ones, and the "this is all a mistake / consciousness gets deflated" option for readers who think the problem presupposes its own answer. Dualists and neutral monists who wander in should get routed somewhere sensible too even if they're not the target audience.

3. I want the epistemic version of the problem kept seperate from the metaphysical one — even someone who thinks subjects can in principle be composed might deny we could ever find out which composites are subjects. At least two questions should target that distinction specifically, and the scoring key should say which answer combos land you in "composition is fine, knowledge of it isn't."

4. There's a specific tension I keep hitting in comments: readers who say something like "consciousness is just the intrinsic nature of matter" and then, in the same breath, deny that a bee colony or a crowd has any unified mental life. At least one question should be built to catch that, i.e. it should be awkward to answer both ways.

5. Be honest about failure modes: if two of the target positions can't be seperated by any scenario you can write for a general audience, flag it instead of inventing a fake diagnostic. Same if one of the questions only works on people who already know the literature.

Deliverable is the questions plus the scoring key, written so I can drop it straight into a post. Keep it casual — my readers run on coffee, not JSTOR access.
