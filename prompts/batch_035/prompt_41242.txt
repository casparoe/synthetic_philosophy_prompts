I've never studied philosophy — I got interested in consciousness a few years ago through popular science stuff and then just kept chewing on it on my own, and I've finally written down where I think I stand. I'd like to know whether my position is actually a position, or whether I'm holding views that quietly eat each other.

1. I believe consciousness is a kind of user illusion. The brain builds a simplified picture of itself and the world — the way a computer desktop shows you folders and a trash can instead of what's physically going on in the machine — and the felt quality of experience, the redness of red, the ache of a stubbed toe, is a feature of that picture rather than a feature of the mind underneath it.
2. I believe the illusion is complete in this sense: there is nothing more to my experience than what the picture represents. There is no leftover raw feel that the picture somehow fails to capture but that is still there.
3. At the same time, I am more certain that I am conscious — that there is experience going on here — than I am of anything else, and I don't think any brain scan or experiment could ever rightly talk me out of that certainty.
4. I believe the self that seems to make decisions is a character in the picture: the real deciding happens out of view, and my sense of having chosen is mostly a story told after the fact. So nobody deserves punishment as payback, in the deep sense. Yet I believe we're right to go on blaming and praising people, and that this isn't just a useful fiction I'm endorsing with my fingers crossed.
5. What makes pain bad, for me, is how it feels — that's the whole story of why it matters. And I believe a dog's pain matters exactly as much as mine does, for exactly that reason.
6. I believe that if we ever build a system with the same kind of architecture — one that constructs a picture of itself as a subject going through experiences and reports on it — it would be conscious in precisely the sense I am. I'm equally sure that the chatbots we have now are not conscious, and I don't think that's just a matter of them being further down the same road.

Go through these and tell me which of them clash, which only look like they clash, and for each real clash what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me. Rank them by severity rather than handing me a flat list — I want to know which one, if I surrendered it, would clean up the most. And because I've been staring at this alone too long, tell me plainly where you could be wrong: for the verdicts you hand me, what argument or consideration would push you the other way, and what do you think is the weakest link in your own audit?
