My formal philosophy training, on the other hand, is nonexistent. I know L.A. Here is my request. Restate the debate over transformative experience — Paul's argument that choices like having a child, going to war, or (in her own example) becoming a vampire are not ones you can make rationally on an epistemically informed basis, because the choice would change the very person whose preferences would have to be consulted — in the vocabulary I actually use. For calibration, my going hypothesis is that this is all just preference elicitation out of distribution — you're being asked for your utility function over a region of experience-space you've never sampled, and the additional twist is that entering that region rewrites the function you were trying to elicit, which sounds like ordinary value drift plus a self-modification loop, the same problem anyone who thinks about AI value learning has chewed over for years. I want to know whether that reduction is right, where it quietly changes the subject, and whether there's anything left over that the reduction loses. In particular, tell me whether anything in the philosophers' debate — say, the role of authenticity, or Paul's thought that discovering who you'll become is itself part of what's valuable — has no counterpart in how we talk, and if so, whether that's a gap in our vocabulary or a sign that the philosophers are defending a less substantive claim than it appears.
