I've spent twenty years doing IT support and running small-office networks, and I have no academic background at all, but I've been chewing for a long time on a philosophy puzzle I came across: Daniel Dennett's "Where Am I?" thought experiment. Let me lay out the setup as I understand it, in case I have any of it wrong. Dennett imagines being recruited for a mission that requires his brain to be removed from his skull, kept alive in a vat in a laboratory, and connected to his now-empty head by a two-way radio link that carries every nerve signal back and forth. He then walks around, sees, hears, and feels exactly as before, while the brain doing all the work sits physically miles away. The story goes on to raise further complications: he is told the link could in principle be rerouted so that his body is driven by a different brain, or even by a computer that reproduces the input-output behavior of his brain, with him none the wiser. And he points out that he seemed to feel no different at all once the operation was done. The question he presses is where he himself is located -- with the brain in the vat, with the body walking around, or somehow spread across the link -- and what would even count as settling that.

Here is what I want from you: translate this whole puzzle into the vocabulary I actually use every day -- clients and servers, thin clients, remote desktop sessions, I/O forwarding, virtual machines, the distinction between hardware and instance, where a process "lives," failover to a mirrored server. Do it systematically and be honest about the fit rather than forcing it. Some parts, I suspect, map well: the vat-brain setup looks like a thin client driven by a remote server, the rerouting scenario looks like failover, and the question of where the person is looks like asking where the computer really is when everything the user touches is a terminal. But I want the residue report as much as the translation. Which of Dennett's questions survive as genuine questions about distributed systems, and which only look like they survive? One thing in particular bothers me: when I ask where a remote session lives, there is a precise technical answer and nothing feels at stake; when Dennett asks where he is, the structurally identical question feels momentous. Is that difference something my vocabulary can express at all, or does it fall out of the translation entirely? Also tell me whether the picture of the brain as a server and the body as a terminal is itself doing argumentative work -- whether calling a brain the server already smuggles in an assumption, about which part of me is really me, before the translation even starts. End with a verdict: does what fails to translate point to something my engineering vocabulary is missing, or does it suggest the original question was less substantive than it looked to me?
