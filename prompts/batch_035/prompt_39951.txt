I've never studied philosophy; what follows is just where I've landed after years of turning over hard cases in my own head, and I'd like to know whether it all fits together. My commitments: (1) some things are wrong no matter what anyone believes — moral truth is not a matter of opinion. (2) You can only be obligated to do what you're able to do; where the right thing is impossible, the obligation simply doesn't arise, which is why we don't count someone as having failed when they were trapped. (3) Real dilemmas exist: if I can reach only one of two drowning swimmers in time, then whichever one I don't reach, I let a person die who was counting on me, and that is a wrong — the fact that the alternative was also wrong does not cancel it — and the guilt carried afterward is appropriate to what was done, not a psychological malfunction. (4) In every situation there is a fact about what one ought to do, all things considered; a morality that goes silent and says "there is no answer here" has stopped doing its job. (5) Blame and self-reproach are deserved only where a person could have done better; "couldn't have done otherwise" is a complete defense. Does this set hang together? If not, tell me which of these pull against each other, which conflicts only look like conflicts, and what the least costly repair would be and what it would cost me — and please order the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally serious.
