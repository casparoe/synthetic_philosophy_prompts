I run a philosophy of physics reading group (~10 grad students, comfertable with special relativity but hazy on GR) and I want a decision tree that sorts them on substantivalism vs relationism, starting from Newton's bucket. Every node has to be a thought experiment they can answer before knowing any of the labels: the bucket itself, the two globes connected by a cord in an otherwise empty universe, whether a lone spinning body in an otherwise empty universe would show centrifugal effects at all (Mach's worry), whether a universe containing exactly one particle moving uniformly in a straight line makes any sense, and something GR-flavored like frame dragging or the water climbing differently if the bucket were surrounded by huge distant masses. Leaves should include Newtonian absolutism, Leibnizian relationism, Machian relationism, and the view where the metric field is a genuine dynamical entity but space isn't a container. Build the tree from the questions first and then locate the positions in that space — not the other way round — and flag any answer-combos nobody actually holds, plus pairs of leaves the tree genuinely can't separate (i suspect absolutism vs the metric-field view, but tell me if im wrong). Node questions should be answerable without jargon, no asking people straight up whether they believe in absolute space.
