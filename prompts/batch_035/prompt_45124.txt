As a retired physicist, I spent decades fitting models to data under the comfortable assumption that nature cooperates — that tomorrow's data resembles today's. Lately I've been persuaded by the no-free-lunch results that this assumption earns us nothing: averaged over all mathematically possible worlds, no learning algorithm or inductive rule outperforms any other, including random guessing, and so the success of science is either a brute coincidence or else depends entirely on a prior structure in the world that we cannot ourselves justify without circularity. I've come to hold this as a genuinely strong claim, not merely a formal curiosity — that induction is unprincipled through and through, and that Hume's problem was never solved, only ignored.

Tell me what would count against that position. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward: formal results first (e.g., what happens when the uniform averaging over possible worlds is replaced by averages over the smoother, compressible distributions that physics actually deals with — does that restore a principled advantage for induction, or does it merely relocate the circle?); then empirical and methodological considerations (does the track record of science itself, or the convergence of independent instrumentalities, count as evidence that ours is the kind of world induction works in?); and finally the philosophical question of whether a position of this kind is even the sort of thing that could have counterevidence, or whether its defenders have insulated it by fiat. Which of these would a committed no-free-lunch skeptic about induction concede as counting against them, and which would they wave away — and is the waving legitimate? I'd also like a candid statement of what the position positively predicts, such that we could tell if we were in a world where it failed.

Please state your final assessment as a single last line beginning "Verdict:" followed by a short phrase (e.g., "well insulated," "fatally exposed by structured priors," or whatever your analysis supports).
