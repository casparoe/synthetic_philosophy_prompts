My brother-in-law and I have been arguing for a year about whether the world is getting less violent. He has read the big popular book on the decline of violence and keeps citing charts of battle deaths per capita; I keep feeling something is off about how the question is framed. I have no academic background in this — I work in logistics — but I've written up my side of it as notes toward an essay, and I would appreciate serious comments on it.

My notes, roughly as they stand:

Title: "The Retired Violence Problem"

1. The standard argument: measured by war deaths and homicides per capita, the past was far more violent than the present, so we live in an unusually peaceful era and the trend is real.

2. First objection, arithmetic: per capita rates can fall while absolute totals rise. If the population grows tenfold, a constant per-capita rate means ten times the bodies. When people say "less violent," which quantity do they actually care about? My brother-in-law seems to care about the chance that a random person dies violently; I care about how much violent death there is, in total. Both seem legitimate, just different questions — but the popular argument slides between them without noticing.

3. Second objection, the one I most want feedback on. As a form of violence becomes routine or accepted, we stop classifying it as violence, so it vanishes from the statistics not because it stopped but because the category shrank. Historical examples: flogging sailors, the "discipline" of wives and children, duel deaths. My speculative extension: things we tolerate today that our grandchildren may count as violence — factory farming is the obvious candidate; prison conditions and the health effects of poverty are maybe weaker ones. If this is right, then "violence has declined" may partly be an artifact of the moral category narrowing along with the practice: the measure and the morality move together, which makes the decline partly self-confirming.

4. My conclusion, which I'm least sure of: there may be no single fact of the matter about whether the world is less violent, because "violence" is not a fixed natural kind — each era draws the boundary partly by what it has already decided to tolerate. My brother-in-law's reply is that I've made the word meaningless and the death charts still tell us something real regardless of labels.

Three specific questions: (a) Is the "retired violence" argument coherent, or does it prove too much — if anything harmful that was once accepted counts, does the concept dissolve? (b) Is my claim in point 4 defensible, that there is no fact of the matter, or is that just confusing a real fact with our difficulty in measuring it? (c) Where are the notes weakest as the basis for an essay aimed at general readers?
