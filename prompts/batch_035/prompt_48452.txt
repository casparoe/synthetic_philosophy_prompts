I sit on the steering committee of a pilot citizens' climate assembly in a city of roughly 300,000. Our enabling ordinance draws 100 members by pure civic lottery from the electoral roll — no stratification, no quotas — and presents the unmanaged draw as the assembly's chief claim to legitimacy. In the event, about a third of those drawn declined, and decliners were overwhelmingly people in full-time employment; the panel we can actually seat is dominated by retirees and students and is far better educated than the city. At Thursday's meeting we must recommend one of three courses: (a) seat the panel as is, treating acceptance as part of the draw; (b) re-run selection with stratified quotas and aggressive replacement rules until the seated panel mirrors the city demographically; or (c) recommend compulsory service on the jury model for the next cycle, filling this cycle by option (a) meanwhile.

Last night I argued this for three hours with a close friend who is a trial lawyer. She insisted that a lottery's legitimacy lives entirely in the procedure: nobody thinks Powerball is rigged because some winners tear up their tickets, and a panel of willing volunteers is just a lottery with a response-rate filter — still random, still fair. I ended up defending the opposite view: that descriptive representativeness is the whole point of choosing by lot rather than by election, and a panel that no longer mirrors the city is a residue of the lottery rather than its product.

Draft the decision memo I will circulate before Thursday: a clear recommendation among (a)–(c), the reasoning behind it, an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side of my argument with my friend, and a note on what a committee member voting against us would say. Then append a JSON object with the following fields: "recommendation" (one sentence), "grounds" (array of strings, the main reasons), "strongest_counterargument" (the losing side's best point), "response_to_counterargument" (one sentence), "principle_at_stake" (a sentence naming the underlying issue the case turns on), "implementation_steps" (array of concrete next actions), and "projected_dissent" (one sentence).
