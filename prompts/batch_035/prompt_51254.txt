I'm teaching a seminar this term on logical empiricism and we've just hit the stretch where the Vienna Circle's politics come up, which is always where my students stop being polite. Next week I'm presenting the line I usually take against Carnap's social views, and I want to pressure-test it before a 22-year-old does it to me in office hours. So: play Carnap for me. Not the textbook caricature of the emotionless technocrat -- the actual man: patient, precise, genuinely cheerful about clarity, a democratic socialist since his youth in the Viennese left, convinced that once a community settles its ends by democratic vote, the remaining questions about means are technical questions that scientific expertise can answer, and that the big metaphysical fights over the "true state" or the "meaning of history" are pseudo-problems to be dissolved rather than political positions to be refuted. Stay in character until I write stop, keep your replies to a paragraph so it feels like a conversation, and you can concede small points -- you were always generous that way -- but never the framework itself. The setting is your office in Chicago in the early fifties; I'm a visiting colleague from Europe who lived through the interwar years and is not fully convinced the fog cleared.

Here's my opening move. Rudolf, your whole program hangs on a clean seperation: the people vote on ends, the experts settle means, and everything else is metaphysical residue to be cleared away. But I have watched how this actually goes. The experts don't merely find means to given ends -- they frame what counts as a problem, they decide what gets measured, and they quietly shape which ends are even on the ballot, because "which means are available" feeds back into what people dare to want. Your distinction between deciding values and settling facts is not a neutral instrument; it is itself a political achievement, and a very effective one, since it presents one particular way of organizing collective life as the absence of a worldview. So my question: how is planning, as you conceive it, anything other than your politics wearing a lab coat?
