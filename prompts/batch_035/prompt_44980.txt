I'm on the people-ops steering comittee at a ~400-person software company (boilerplate mid-size, nothing exotic). Our CEO read something on a flight and came back convinced our "flexible, passion-driven" culture is quietly grinding people down: internal survey shows median 50+ hour weeks, half of engineering says they answer Slack on vacation, two people out on medical leave for burnout this year. So we have to actually pick a policy at next month's meeting, and there are three live proposals:

1) A hard 35-hour week -- hours logged, managers dinged for overage, basically the French thing, on the theory that if you don't measure it it doesn't exist.
2) Unlimited PTO plus sabbaticals -- the Silicon Valley favorite. We actually tried this three years ago and people took LESS vacation, but the execs love the signal.
3) The weird one, from our most senior engineer. She spent four years at a Tokyo firm where a colleague of hers literally died at his desk -- karoshi is not a hypothetical to her, it's a name she had to say at a funeral. She wants what she calls "protected leisure": no hour-counting at all, but contractually enforced off-time -- no email delivery between 8pm and 8am, vacation where HR takes your laptop, a company-wide closed day once a month. Her argument is roughly that the problem isn't how much we work but that our leisure has been colonized: recovery counts as work-prep, hobbies get rebranded as upskilling, the weekend is just a buffer so the machine can run again Monday. She keeps citing some line about the Greek word for leisure being the root of our word for school, which honestly I haven't checked.

And of course we have a VP who pushes back on all three with what he openly calls the Protestant work ethic: effort is intrinsically ennobling, people who love their craft SHOULD work sixty hours, and capping hours is paternalism that treats adults as children. He's not a cartoon -- he mentored half our best engineers and they'd walk into traffic for him, so I can't just dismiss him.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting. I want a concrete recommendation -- pick one of the three or a hybrid, whatever you actually think is right, with rollout details a committee could vote on -- and I want the VP's position taken seriously, steelmanned even, not just litigated against. The engineer's "colonized leisure" idea is the one I find most interesting and also most hand-wavy: if it's doing real work in your recommendation, say exactly what work. And include a section on what would change the recommendation -- what evidence from the rollout, or what argument someone could raise at the meeting, should make us flip. Also flag where you think your own reasoning is weakest.
