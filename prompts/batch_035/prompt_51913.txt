I've been asked to state my position in infinite ethics for a graduate seminar, and I'd like an audit of whether it hangs together before I submit. Here is the package: (1) I'm a total utilitarian — the value of a world is the sum of individual welfare levels. (2) I assign substantial credence to the universe being spatially infinite, and hence to there being infinitely many welfare subjects. (3) I take this to be a technical problem to be solved rather than a refutation: some infinite worlds are genuinely better than others, and what we do still matters. (4) I hold the mere addition intuition — adding a life worth living to a world, with everything else unchanged, makes the world better. (5) I maximize expected value over outcomes, and I refuse to discount minuscule probabilities of astronomical payoffs. (6) Whatever account we give for infinite worlds must be continuous with the finite case: restricted to finite worlds, it has to coincide with ordinary summing. Does this set stand jointly? Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what would each revision cost? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging.
