A friend of mine has gone pretty deep into Fomenko's New Chronology (the idea that ancient history is mostly phantom copies of medieval history, shifted back by centuries, no conspiracy needed — just everyone copying everyone else). I keep telling him its basically zero and he keeps saying I can't put a number on it since I haven't checked the manuscripts myself. Fine — put a number on it. What's your credence that Fomenko's core claim is true, meaning specifically that at least one major event conventionally dated before 500 AD is actually off by more than a thousand years? Decompose it: the probability that the main independent dating methods (radiocarbon, tree rings, eclipse retrocalculation, stratigraphy, cross-checks against Chinese records and so on) are all circular or contaminated enough to hide an error that big, the probability that recorded ancient eclipses like the ones in Thucydides were back-calculated and inserted into the text later rather than actually observed, the probability that a compression of history on that scale could happen without leaving traceable contradictions somewhere in the sources, and any other terms you think belong. Then tell me which term is doing the most work in your final number, and what evidence would actually move it.
