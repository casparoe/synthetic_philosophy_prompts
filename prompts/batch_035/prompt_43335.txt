so our reading group is doing a thing where everyone writes down their commitments on a topic and the rest of us try to find the cracks. mine's on the knowing-how / knowing-that debate and I want a proper audit before I send it round, because I have a nagging feeling something in here doesn't fit but I can't put my finger on it. here's the set:

(a) Ryle's regress argument is sound: knowing-how can't be analysed as knowing-that plus applying what you know, on pain of an infinite regress.
(b) Stanley and Williamson's 2001 paper got it wrong — when I say my mum knows how to make a roux, I'm not attributing to her propositional knowledge of some way of making roux under a practical mode of presentation or whatever the intellectualist gloss is.
(c) the deep story is ability: to know how to do something just is to possess the capacity to do it, reliably, when you try.
(d) a concert pianist who gets paralysed by a stroke still knows how to play the piano — she could coach students, spot a wrong fingering from across the room, hum the concertos.
(e) genuine know-how has to be learnt by doing — you can't acquire it by absorbing descriptions, which is why I don't think a text-only LLM, however fluent, knows how to do anything at all. all knowing-that and mimicry, zero know-how.
(f) playing chess at a high level is a paradigm of knowledge-how, and I'm completly comfortable saying AlphaZero knows how to play chess in exactly the sense a grandmaster does — better, actually.
(g) a first-time poker player who cleans up on a lucky night doesn't thereby know how to play poker.

I take (a) and (b) together to basically settle things in favour of (c), and I'd assumed (e) and (f) fit fine since AlphaZero never read a book in its life. go through the set and tell me which bits are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, how bad each one is, and what the cheapest fix would cost me. rank the problems rather than listing them all as equally fatal, and if there's a single revision that would clean up the most mess, tell me which one. don't pull your punches just because it's my position — that's literally what the group is for.
