Write a sestina in the voice of someone born three centuries into Parfit's Depletion scenario: a nation that chose easy, wasteful living, whose choices altered who would later be conceived, so that the depleted world and its inhabitants are made of the same decision. The six end-words should be *born*, *choice*, *harm*, *same*, *debt*, and *world*, and each return of a word should shift its sense — "born" as verb and burden, "debt" as ecological and moral, "same" doing the argumentative work that identity is the same fact as the wrong, allegedly done to no one. Let the form carry the paradox rather than stating it: the words cannot be subtracted from the poem any more than the policy can be subtracted from the speaker, and yet nothing in the poem should read as straightforwardly harmed. Do not resolve whether the policy wronged her; end the envoi on "same," so the poem closes on the identity claim rather than a verdict. Someone who knows the nonidentity problem should recognize the point that no cleaner possible world contains her; someone who doesn't should still find a good poem about inheriting a spoiled world one would never trade for a livable one without oneself in it.
