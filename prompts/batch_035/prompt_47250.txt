I run the foundations-of-mathematics program at a small independent research institute, and our next funding cycle allows us to support roughly four postdocs for four years on conceptual work surrounding the axiom of choice. The institute deliberately funds philosophy and logic rather than technical set theory — we're not looking to sponsor another relative-consistency result — so the portfolio has to be built around questions that a mathematically sophisticated philosopher or logician could genuinely make progress on. My board and I keep circling the same candidate topics without settling on an ordering, so I'd value a defended prioritization.

The candidates on the table: (1) a kind of accounting project — which theorems of mainstream mathematics actually depend on full choice versus weaker fragments like dependent choice or countable choice, versus which can be salvaged by more careful proofs, and whether such an inventory could be organized into something usable by working mathematicians; (2) the philosophical standing of pure existence proofs, i.e., whether an argument that a solution exists but yields no witness should count as settling a question, and whether the standard 'we can name the witness once we well-order everything' response carries real weight; (3) the question of how much of the widespread discomfort with Banach–Tarski and nonmeasurable sets is genuinely about choice rather than about the structure of the continuum, and hence whether that discomfort is a rational signal about which axioms to trust; (4) the seriousness of anti-choice frameworks — most prominently determinacy-based ones — as potential foundations for the mathematics non-set-theorists actually do, rather than as exotic alternatives; and (5) the sociology and epistemology of practice: what it does to a field when most proofs pass through Zorn's lemma as an unexamined black box, and whether anything like an 'AC-free' certification of results would be worth building.

Which of these deserves the four postdoc-years most, and in what order? I care more about the reasoning than the list: for each item, tell me whether it's upstream of the others, how tractable it is for philosophers rather than set theorists, how neglected it is relative to its importance, and what developments in the next few years — from the community, or from us — would move it up or down the list. Please also feel free to argue that something I haven't listed belongs on the agenda instead, or that one of my five is a dead end that has absorbed too much attention already.
