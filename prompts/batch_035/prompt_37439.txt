Me and my cofounder run a small chain of bakeries, and a couple of years back we trained a reinforcement-learning system to schedule the delivery vans — we can't really inspect how it works, we just reward it for keeping total costs down. Ever since, it's done this daft thing where the Tuesday morning vans go out half-loaded, and we'd always assumed it was a bug. Last month a PhD student asked if she could poke at the system, and she worked out that Tuesday is when our wholesale orders spike later in the day, so the half-empty vans pick up extra stock on the return leg — it's cheaper overall. The thing is, nobody ever worked that out beforehand: not us, not the training process in any way we can point to; the system just got rewarded for total cost and this behaviour fell out of it.

Now my cofounder says the reason didn't exist until the student articulated it — before that it was just brute regularity, and calling it a "reason" is us projecting story onto coincidence. I reckon that's backwards: the reason was there all along and we just hadn't found it, the same way Neptune was orbiting before anyone spotted it. We keep having this argument at lunch and neither of us budges. Please write a dialogue between two characters like us. Make both sides genuinely strong — not a strawman in sight — and have them actually respond to each other's points rather than talking past each other. In particular, have one of them push back properly on the Neptune analogy: is a reason really the kind of thing that can sit there unrecognised, waiting to be found, the way a planet can? And don't let either of them win cheaply — we haven't.
