Two friends of mine, one a rare-Earth pessimist (life or intelligence is a fluke, the hard transitions are rare) and one a common-life optimist (the silence is about detectability, timing, or filters ahead of us, not about life being rare), have argued about the Fermi paradox for years and finally agreed to a proper adversarial collaboration. Draft the joint statement they'd produce. I want: a numbered section of claims both can sign -- about what the Great Silence does and doesn't show, about exoplanet statistics, and especially about anthropic selection messing up any inference from our own existance; then a disagreements table where each residual disagreement is worded so its own side would accept the wording; then the cruxes, with for each one a concrete test or observation both agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance. Some things that should definitely make it in: abiogenesis, and whether finding a second independent genesis (Enceladus/Europa, or a shadow biosphere on Earth) would actually move anyone; the hard-steps picture of evolutionary transitions and whether the waiting times in Earth's history look like they scale with how much time was available or not; and the fact that abiotic oxygen is a known false positive for biosignatures around M-dwarfs, so both sides should say what an apparent biosignature detection there would and wouldn't show. If any part of what's left between them is just verbal (e.g. whether this even deserves the word 'paradox'), say which part and why.
