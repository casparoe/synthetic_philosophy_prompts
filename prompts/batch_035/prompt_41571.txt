A friend who reads a ton of pop neuroscience is now convinced that pain isn't actually bad in itself, and honestly the way he put it kind of unsettled me. He said that people with a condition called pain asymbolia feel pain at full strength — they can tell you where it is and how bad it is — but they simply don't mind it: no flinching, no distress, they'll hold their hand under a hot tap without caring. So the badness obviously can't be in the sensation itself; it's just an emotional reaction layered on top of a signal, basically a judgement we add. And he pointed to people born without the ability to feel pain as more proof: they never suffer at all, so the feeling was never doing the real work anyway. His takeaway is that if you could just switch off the aversion part, a life full of pain would be no worse than a life without pain, and everyone who talks about pain being intrinsically bad is just confused.

Part of me thinks he's onto something, part of me thinks its way too quick — especially the last step, which I cant quite pin down. Also I'm honestly not sure that feeling pain and not minding it is the same thing as not feeling it at all; those sound like different situations to me. Can you go through his little speech bit by bit?
