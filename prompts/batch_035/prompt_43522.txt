My friend and I got into it over engagement rings. She thinks buying a mined diamond is basically getting scammed, since lab-grown stones are chemically the same and a fraction of the price. My cousin shot back that the whole point is that it costs you -- if it didn't cost anything it wouldn't mean anything. Later I googled around and found people explaining peacock tails and something called the handicap principle: the tail is such a burden that only a genuinely fit male can lug it around, so the cost itself is what makes the display honest, and the same logic supposedly explains everything from expensive rings to unpaid internships to why peacocks and luxury brands keep doing it.

Here's my problem though: "it only means something because it costs you" could mean a bunch of different things and everyone in the argument kept sliding between them. Is it the claim that NO signal can be honest unless it's costly? That sounds too strong -- a password proves who you are and costs basically nothing. Or is it that costly signals are honest because fakers can't afford them specifically, i.e. the cost has to hurt the low-quality guy more than the high-quality one (is that actually what the peacock story says, or do the tails work some other way, like the female just liking big tails and the honesty being a side effect)? Or is the ring not about proving quality at all but about commitment -- you burn the money up front so walking away gets expensive, more like leaving a deposit than showing off, in which case it doesn't matter what the diamond is "worth" only that it was a sacrifice? Or is the cost the message itself, like the whole point of a flashy watch is proving you can afford to waste money (I saw the word Veblen thrown around)?

Can you lay out the different precise claims that one-liner could be making, as a list from more or less trivial to pretty radical, and for each one say whether its true, half-true, or flat out wrong, and -- this is the important part -- which readings, if any, actually get my cousin to his conclusion that buying the lab-grown stone defeats the purpose? I suspect some readings support him and some don't, and I want to know which fight we're actually having. Also, if lab diamonds really did kill the appeal of diamond rings, what would each reading predict happening instead -- another expensive thing taking over, or nothing?
