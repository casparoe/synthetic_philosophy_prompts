So this got weirdly personal last month: routine bloodwork came back and my doctor told me I'm "pre-diabetic", which to me sounds like something invented to sell glucose monitors. One of my sisters (a nurse) says it's a real condition, the other says it's big medicine inventing diseases as we speak, and I realized I couldn't say what actually makes something a disease in the first place. I write a small blog and want to settle this with a quiz: "what do you actually think a disease is?"

Please give me 8-10 questions, each one a concrete case, with 2-3 answer options per question. Some cases I'd like in there (pick and add your own): a guy whose kidneys run at 40% of the typical rate but feels nothing and needs no treatment; someone with chronic pain where every test comes back normal; a Deaf kid whose parents are weighing a cochlear implant; grief that's still crushing eight months after a spouse died; the old drapetomania diagnosis for enslaved people who ran away; whether aging itself counts; a healthy but short teenager who wants growth hormone; and my pre-diabetes thing. The options should be judgments about the case (yes disease / not a disease / sort of, but here's the real reason), never theory labels, and no question may just ask whether the reader thinks medicine is value-free — I want people sorted by what they say about cases, since anyone can nod along to any label.

Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto: Boorse-style biostatistical naturalism, Wakefield's harmful-dysfunction hybrid, plain normativism (a disease is just a condition we judge bad), pluralist/family-resemblance views, and the deflationist option that "disease" has no essence and is just a ragbag term. Also flag which pairs the quiz can't really separate — my hunch is the hybrid and the normativist collapse into the same bucket unless a question cleanly splits dysfunction from harm, but tell me if I'm wrong about that.
