Me and my brother-in-law have been arguing for months about what actually counts as corruption, and I've realised I don't even know what a good answer would look like. He's from Denmark and I've got family in a country where, honestly, you don't get anything done without a small gift or knowing someone who knows someone, and to him all of it is corruption, full stop, while to my uncle it's just how you treat people, and giving nothing is the rude/corrupt move. Then I watched a documentary about lobbying in Washington and it looked like the same stuff with better tailoring. So before I keep arguing about specific cases I want to figure out the spec: what does ANY adequate account of corruption have to get right? Like, which cases does it have to call corruption and which does it have to leave alone, what distinctions can't it collapse (a gift vs a bribe, a favour vs a sale, a campaign donation vs a kickback), and what's it even for -- is the point fairness, or trust in institutions, or something else entirely? And please tell me which requirements you'd bet are genuine and which are just one culture's preferences dressed up as universal, because that's basically my whole argument with my brother-in-law.

A few cases I want the spec to handle, since these keep coming up: a nurse who gets expensive wedding gifts from grateful families on her ward and never asks for them; a clerk who moves your file to the top of the pile because you're his cousin's neighbour and it would cost you nothing either way; a politician who steers a road contract to his brother-in-law's firm, which does the work properly and at a fair price; and an MP who takes constituents' problems seriously mostly in the run-up to elections because he needs their votes. I genuinely can't tell if a definition should treat the last one as corruption or just as democracy working as intended, and I suspect any rule that handles all four cleanly doesn't exist. If some of the requirements you list can't be satisfied together, say which ones and why -- that's the bit I most want to see. Also, if one requirement does most of the work of ruling out popular ways of defining corruption (like "anything that violates the formal rules" or "private gain at public expense"), point that out too.

You don't need to be academic about it, I haven't read any philosophy in my life, just make it clear and concrete. And please write it in British English, since this is going to my brother-in-law and he's a bit of a pedant about American spellings. Oh and I know definately nothing about how political scientists actually use the word, so if my framing is off somewhere just say so gently.
