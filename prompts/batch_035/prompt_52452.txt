I'm an ML engineer who's gotten somewhat sucked into AI-safety-adjacent reading lately, and one thing I keep noticing is that "we should just make the AI have low impact" gets said by people who mean really different things by it. For a blog post aimed at people like me (engineers, not philosophers), I want a decision tree that starts from a plain starting commitment — "I can't write down a full specification of what I care about, but I want my AI, while doing its task, to not wreck all the other stuff I care about" — and ends at a named position on whether "avoiding side effects" can even be defined without first solving the value specification problem.

The branch nodes should be diagnostic questions someone can answer from intuitions about cases, not from knowing the literature. Some cases I'd like worked in: a cleaning robot that can prevent a passerby from knocking over a vase, where depending on your baseline the "safe" choice is to let the vase break; an agent that locks the door or disables its off-switch so it can finish its task uninterrupted and argues this is precaution, not a side effect; an AI whose task genuinely requires huge changes to the wolrd, like building a factory — can "impact" be task-relative or is it a global quantity that such an agent is just stuck with?; and whether an agent should be penalized for effects its action merely enables rather than directly causes. As for leaves, I want at least these to show up: the generic-penalty family (penalizing change in attainable utility, or loss of reachability of states, relative to some baseline), the baseline-choice issue as its own axis (what would have happened if the agent had done nothing at all vs. doing nothing from this moment on — I gather these diverge in annoying ways), conservatism/irreversibility-flavored views where the core idea is preserving options or not doing things that can't be undone, the position that low impact is hopelessly value-laden and the real goal is corrigibility — keeping the agent correctable — and the deflationary view that "impact" does no real work and the right move is to not build open-ended agents at all.

Please build the tree from the logical space first (which combinations of answers to your diagnostic questions are even coherent), then locate the actual named positions inside that space, and tell me about the unoccupied corners — e.g., if nobody combines a stepwise baseline with a corrigibility-first stance, I want to know whether that's because the combo is incoherent or just because nobody bothered. Also flag any two positions your tree can't genuinely separate, since my readers will call me on it if they notice both routes end at the same leaf. And since I'm not an expert here: tell me how confident you are in each leaf placement, and single out the one assignment you're least sure of.
