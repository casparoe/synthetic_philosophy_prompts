My family has run a small orchard for three generations and I've been the one running it for the last nine years. It looses money most years and every year I dig into savings a little more to keep it going. My brother in law, who's an accountant, says the math is simple: the money and years I already put in are gone either way, so only what happens from here forward should count, and on that math I should sell. I get where he's coming from, but I've been turning it over in my head for months and I think he's missing two things.

First, my past decisions don't feel like they're just "gone" -- they feel like information. Past-me knew this orchard better than anyone alive, and he kept deciding, year after year, that it was worth staying in. That's like nine votes from somebody with inside knowledge. Quitting now means overriding all those votes based on how I feel during one bad season. Isn't there something weird about treating my own track record of choices as worth nothing?

Second, I worry about the habit of quitting itself. If I bail whenever things look bad, I'll get good at bailing, and that might cost me on the next thing even if the next thing would have worked out if I'd stuck with it. My brother in law says both of those are just fancy ways of avoiding the pain of admitting it's over, and honestly sometimes at 2am I think he's right.

So who's closer? Is there something real in what I'm saying, or am I rationalizing? And if there IS something real, how do I tell the difference between using my "past choices are evidence" idea honestly and just using it as a shield? Please answer in American English.
