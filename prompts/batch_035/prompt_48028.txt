Me and two of my friends have been going in circles on the same argument for like two years now, and I want someone to tell me what kind of argument it actually is, because I've stopped believing it's about the thing we all SAY it's about.

Some background so this makes sense. A while back I read about this wasp — the Sphex wasp, I think it was in a Hofstadter book but honestly I might be mixing up my sources, it was years ago. The mother wasp drags a paralyzed cricket to her burrow, leaves it at the entrance, goes inside to check the burrow, then comes out and pulls the cricket in. But if you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's inside, she comes out, finds it, drags it back to the entrance... and goes in to check the burrow again. You can apparently do this to her over and over, like a dozen times, and she never once just pulls the cricket in. She's stuck in a loop. There was a word for this that I loved when I read it — being "sphexish" — running a fixed routine that looks smart from outside but has no give in it.

So here's the fight. Dave says people are the same thing as the wasp, just with alot more branches. Every time I say "I could have done otherwise," he says: sure, in the sense that if the inputs had been different, the output would've been different — and the wasp satisfies that too, so it proves nothing. Same kind of thing, different amount of complexity, end of story. My brother Mike takes the total opposite lesson: the wasp shows that mechanical and mindful are opposites, and since we're obviously not wasps, there has to be something in us that isn't mechanical at all. Neither of them has budged and inch in two years, and honestly neither have I, and I'm not even sure what my own position is anymore.

Here's my tentative diagnosis, take it or trash it. I suspect a chunk of this is verbal: when I say "could have done otherwise" I sometimes mean something like "if I'd noticed what I was doing, I could have caught myself and changed course," Dave hears "same exact past, different outcome," and Mike hears something about souls. But I also suspect that once you clean up the words there's a real question left over — whether the difference between the wasp and me is only one of degree (more branches, routines stacked on routines) or one of kind (something like being able to step outside a routine and look at it, which the wasp seems flatly unable to do even a tiny bit). And I can't tell whether Mike is answering a different question than the rest of us under the same words, or whether he's the only one taking the real question seriously.

So what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. What kind of disagreement is this actually — verbal, a hidden factual question, different questions wearing one label, bedrock intuitions nobody can argue for? Go by what the three of us actually say (I've given it to you above), not what some textbook version of our positions would say. And then tell me what would count as progress here — a distinction we're missing, a thought experiment, whatever — or whether I should expect none, and why. If your answer is "it's just verbal," be specific about WHICH words are doing the damage, because I've already been told "you're using words differently" about ten times and it has never once helped.
