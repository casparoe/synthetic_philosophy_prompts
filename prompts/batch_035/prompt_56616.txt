Near the end she also said pain and pleasure 'aren't even on the same scale', which I think is a third claim, but honestly by then we were both just frustrated.

I'm pretty sure the core sentence is doing several different jobs and I couldn't pull them apart mid-argument. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims 'suffering matters more than happiness' could be standing for, roughly from weakest to strongest? My guess is some are near-truisms (for beings like us, suffering usually outweighs comparable pleasure), some are substantive but defensible (suffering gets extra weight per unit, maybe a lot extra), and some are radical: e.g. a lexical claim where no amount of pleasure compensates any amount of pain, full stop; a lexical *threshold* version where that only holds above some intensity of suffering and normal tradeoffs apply below it; or full negative utilitarianism where positive welfare is worth nothing at all — which I suspect is the only reading that actually supports her second conclusion, since everything weaker lets you aggregate at least some pleasure against the suffering. And separately, what would 'not on the same scale' even mean — an incommensurability claim about no common currency for trading them, vs just a claim about relative weights on a shared scale?

For each reading: is it plausible, and does it get her the conclusions she drew? A menu I can bring to round two would be perfect.
