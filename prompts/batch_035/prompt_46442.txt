I'm a cryptographer: interactive proof systems are my daily bread — completeness, soundness, knowledge extractors, zero-knowledge, common reference strings, trusted setups — but my knowledge of philosophy is that of a tourist. Translate the debate over G.E. Moore's proof of an external world into my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then report the residue. Moore claimed to prove that things outside the mind exist by holding up his hands during a lecture: each hand is an external thing, he said, and he was then presenting two of them, so external things exist. His critics typically grant that the argument is valid and its premises true while denying that it proves anything to anyone who doubted the conclusion; some later philosophers add that the warrant backing the premises fails to transmit through the inference to the conclusion, or that such premises function as hinges that every proof presupposes rather than as claims that could themselves be proved. Map this onto proof systems: what, if anything, plays the roles of completeness and soundness here; is the skeptic a verifier who refuses to accept a sound proof, and if so, what exactly does the verifier hold fixed in rejecting it; do hinge commitments have any counterpart in something like a trusted setup or common reference string, which all parties must already accept for the protocol to count as a proof at all? Then give me the residue report: which parts of the philosophical dispute translate cleanly, which had to be distorted to fit, and whether what is left over points to something my vocabulary cannot express or to a dispute that is less substantive than it looks.
