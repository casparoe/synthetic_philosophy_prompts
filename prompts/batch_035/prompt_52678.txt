And here's the student's answer as best as I can transcribe it:

"Gödel found an exact solution to Einstein's field equations describing a universe that rotates but doesn't expand. In this universe there exist closed timelike curves — paths a traveler could follow that bring them back to their own past. Since these curves occur in a solution of the field equations, and Gödel thought the observed rotation of galaxies suggested our universe is in fact like this, he concluded that our own universe contains closed timelike curves, and therefore that time travel to the past is physically possible for us. But time travel to the past is logically contradictory (you could kill your grandfather), so the laws of physics permit a contradiction, which shows time itself is not real. Gödel concluded that time is an illusion of consciousness, exactly as Kant had argued in the first Critique — time is a feature of our minds only, not of the external world at all. Einstein, in his reply in the same volume, accepted this conclusion and admitted that the rotating solution had forced him to give up his earlier views about time. What the argument does not show is that clocks are useless or that relativity is wrong — Gödel kept the physics and the philosophy carefully separated."
