I've been thinking through where I stand on doing vs. allowing and want a consistency check before I put it in writing. Here's my package:

(1) Killing is intrinsically worse than letting die — a real pro tanto difference, not just a useful rule of thumb.
(2) But in the bathtub-type cases (one person drowns their cousin for the inheritance; another stands by, fully able to save him, and lets him drown, same inheritance either way) I say the two are exactly equally culpable, since everything else is held fixed.
(3) I think there's no difference between withholding a ventilator and withdrawing one once it's running — refusing to start it in the morning and switching it off that night are morally on a par.
(4) Yet switching off the ventilator feels totally different from giving a lethal injection with the identical outcome, consent, and prognosis — I want to say the injection is ruled out where the switching-off is fine.
(5) I'm skeptical there's any deep metaphysical fact about which events count as doings vs allowings; the distinction has to earn its significance somehow, maybe through something about agency rather than causation.

Does this hang together? Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look that way, and what's the least costly revision? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as all equally bad, and tell me which single change would clean up the most.
