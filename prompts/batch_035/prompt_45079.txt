So I'm a novelist, three books in, and my next one has these two characters who've been arguing in my head for months and I can't make either of them lose, which is usually a sign the argument is actually good. Character A: a quantitative-historian type, the kind of person who builds comparative datasets of death tolls across atrocities and says things like "if we can't even say one atrocity was worse in magnitude than another, then 'worse' has been emptied of meaning." Character B: a woman whose grandmother survived a massacre that never makes anyone's lists, and who thinks the whole ranking business is obscene -- that suffering doesn't queue up politely to be weighed, and that every ranking is really a bid for whose grief gets to count.

I want them to end up doing an adversarial collaboration on paper. It's a plot device -- they get snowed into a archive together for a week or something, and out of stubbornness they produce this joint document. And I want that document to be real: actually what two smart, good-faith people on opposite sides of "can you rank historical evils, or is it obscene" would produce, not a fake where one of them secretly wins because I like them better.

Please draft it for me. The structure I'm imagining:

- A numbered section of claims both could genuinely sign (what the numbers do and don't measure, what rankings are even for, whatever else belongs there)
- Then the precise disagreements, each one phrased in wording its own side would accept -- this matters a lot, I refuse to have either character sound like a strawman of themself
- Then the cruxes: the sub-questions where settling one would actually move at least one of them, not just make them restate their position louder
- And for each crux, a concrete case or argument both would agree bears on it, with each character's prediction written down in advance

If some chunk of their fight turns out to be verbal -- like maybe "worse" means magnitude of harm to him and moral uniqueness or something to her -- I want that flagged explicitly, because that's exactly the kind of thing these two would figure out at 2am and it would quietly wreck them.

One more thread I keep circling: I want B to concede somewhere that she ranks things privately all the time -- which cause gets her donation, which news story ruins her whole week -- so she's not against comparison per se, she's against a specific public, ledger-flavored kind of comparison. Whether that concession belongs in the agreed-claims section or turns out to be one of the live disagreements, you decide, but it should show up somewhere because it's the crack in her position that makes her interesting.

Don't name real historians or cite real books -- I'm keeping the novel vaguely placeless, and my memory for who-said-what is not to be trusted. The arguments should stand on their own.
