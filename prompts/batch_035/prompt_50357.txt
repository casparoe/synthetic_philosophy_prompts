My department's little reading group has been stuck for months on the color phi thing and I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not a rerun of the debate itself.

Quick setup in case it helps: in the classic color phi experiments you show a red spot, then after a brief blank a green spot a little to the side, and people report seeing a single moving spot that changes color about halfway through -- even though there was never any spot in the middle. The puzzle everyone knows: how did the experience of the first flash already contain the color change before the second flash (the information that "turns it green") had arrived? Two stories from Dennett's Consciousness Explained: the Orwellian one, where you really did first experience red-then-blank, and the record gets rewritten after the fact, so what's falsified is memory; and the Stalinesque one, where consciousness runs on a tape delay like censored live TV, the brain holds the feed briefly, edits, and only then does the experience happen, so the experience always already included the green.

Dennett's own line, as I understand it, is that both stories are wrong because both presuppose a place where "it all comes together" -- the Orwellian says the show aired and the archive was doctored, the Stalinesque says the broadcast is delayed, but both assume there's a show with a definite airing time. His answer is that theres no fact of the matter about *when* the color change was experienced, just multiple drafts in parallel and a confused question.

Here's where we're stuck. Half the group thinks that's profound: the debate dissolves once you drop the Cartesian theater. The other half thinks it's a dodge: they say there obviously is a fact about when experience happens -- that's what the whole postdiction literature is about, the cutaneous rabbit, binding windows, integration times, psychophysics keeps measuring this stuff -- so Dennett is answering a different question (when the brain settles the content) and quietly refusing the one asked (when it is experienced). One guy in the group claims the real split isn't about the theater at all but about whether introspective timing reports are data about experience or data about memory, and honestly I can't tell if that's a substantive point or just a relabeling.

My working hypothesis: this is a verbal dispute dressed up as an empirical one, where the empirical part is basically settled and both sides agree on it (the brain does integrate over windows, content is fixed after the fact), and the residual fight is over what the word "experienced" is for -- a fact about a stream of events in the head, or just a disposition to report. But I'm not sure, because the anti-Dennett people insist their question is empirically open: they think some future experiment on temporal integration could in principle reveal a real "now" of consciousness, while the Dennett side says any such finding will just be one more draft settling and won't touch their view.

So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Verbal, substantive-but-ill-posed, or two groups answering different questions under one heading? If it's the last one, spell out exactly what the two questions are. And given your diagnosis, tell me whether anything could count as progress -- a conceptual distinction, some specific kind of experiment, whatever -- or whether we should just expect this one to stall forever, and why. I'd rather get an honest "no progress is possible, here's why" than a fake ray of hope.
