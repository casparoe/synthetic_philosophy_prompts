(a) Reconstruct the argument, stating clearly what it presupposes about how reference works.

Here is the student's answer:

---

(a) Putnam's argument starts from the causal theory of reference, which he developed alongside Kripke in the early 1970s in opposition to the description theory of names held by Frege and Russell. On this view, a term for a spatiotemporal object refers to whatever objects of the relevant kind stand in the right sort of causal relation to the speaker's use of the term. Now take the sentence "I am a brain in a vat." If I am an ordinary embodied person, the sentence is plainly false. If I am a brain envatted from birth, then I have never causally interacted with real brains or real vats — my only causal contact has been with the electrical impulses fed to me by the computer. So my words "brain" and "vat" cannot refer to real brains and real vats; they refer, if they refer at all, to features of the computer simulation. Either way the sentence comes out false: it is self-refuting, false in every possible circumstance.

(b) Since the sentence is false no matter what, and this is something we can establish by a priori reflection on how reference works, we can know that we are not brains in vats. The skeptical challenge is thereby dissolved: once the brain-in-a-vat hypothesis is ruled out, none of my beliefs about the external world is threatened by skeptical doubt. Indeed, the argument supports a broader anti-skeptical moral: since any adequate interpretation of the envatted brain's utterances must be charitable, most of the beliefs the brain expresses must come out true, so the skeptic's claim that a brain in a vat has mostly false beliefs is itself incoherent.

(c) The same reasoning applies to Descartes' evil demon: if I have always been deceived by a demon, then my word "demon" refers only to demon-images in my experience, so "a demon deceives me" is self-refuting in just the way "I am a brain in a vat" is. And the timing of envatment makes no difference: even if I was envatted five minutes ago, my word "vat" refers to vats in the simulation rather than real vats, so "I am a recently envatted brain in a vat" is equally self-refuting.
