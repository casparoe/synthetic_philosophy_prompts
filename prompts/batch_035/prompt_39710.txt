I'm taking a seminar on confirmation theory and we just covered the "problem of old evidence," and it got me thinking about Rudolf Carnap. Here's the thing: Carnap died in 1970, roughly a decade before Clark Glymour's book Theory and Evidence (1980) put the problem of old evidence on the map for Bayesians. The problem, roughly: if some evidence E is already known with certainty, then P(E) = 1 and P(E|H) = 1, so on the standard account E can't raise the probability of any hypothesis H — yet intuitively old evidence does confirm, the classic example being the advance of Mercury's perihelion confirming general relativity well after GR was on the table. Now, most of the literature treats this as a problem for subjective Bayesians, but Carnap's inductive logic seems to have the same issue baked in: his confirmation functions assign degrees of confirmation relative to a total evidence statement, and if the total evidence includes E with certainty, it's hard to see how E could still confirm anything.

So here's what I'd like you to do: imagine Carnap lived another fifteen years and encountered the old evidence problem as a challenge to his own system, not just to subjective Bayesianism. How do you think he would have reacted? Would he have treated it as a technical problem to be solved within the framework (maybe by some kind of time-slicing of confirmation, or by appeals to his requirement of total evidence in a subtler role), or would he have bitten the bullet and said that old evidence genuinely doesn't confirm, only newly discovered entailments or new theories do? A friend of mine swears Carnap would just have said the problem is an artifact of conflating his two senses of confirmation — incremental vs. absolute — and that the "confirmation" of GR by Mercury's perihelion was really just discovering that the old evidence entailed the new prediction, but I'm not sure that gets him out of the hole. Please reconstruct his most likely position as faithfully as you can to his actual philosophical temperament and commitments, and then tell me honestly where you think that position would be vulnerable. Don't just survey the literature at me — I want your reasoned guess at what the man himself would say, and why.
