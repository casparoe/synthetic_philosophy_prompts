software engineer here, no formal philosophy background, so bear with me. I just reread 1984 for the first time since school and got completely nerd-sniped by Newspeak. The premise, as Syme explains it, is that if you shrink the vocabulary and make heresy literally unsayable, you eventually make it unthinkable — thought corrupts language and language corrupts thought back. Then I fell down a wikipedia rabbit hole and hit Fodor's language of thought hypothesis, and it seems like it straight up wrecks the novel's premise: if thinking happens in an innate Mentalese that's prior to and independent of English or Newspeak, then the Party can control what people say but never what they think, and thoughtcrime should be permanently safe inside people's heads. Natural language would just be an output channel, and the Party is throttling the output, not the process.

But the novel keeps confusing me on this. Doublethink seems like the Party reaching into the thought level directly, not the speech level — Winston holds two contradictory beliefs at once, which feels more like a bug in Mentalese itself than a speech filter. And duckspeak cuts the other way entirely: quacking orthodoxies without engaging your brain at all, which is speech fully decoupled from thought.

So heres what I want: a reading of the novel through the language of thought hypothesis. Does Orwell's regime only make sense if thought is actually conducted in natural language (which Fodor would deny), or does the Party function like a compiler that got compromised between the thought layer and the speech layer? What do you do with the ending, where Winston genuinely loves Big Brother — is that just them retraining the outputs (he'd still have the old Mentalese somewhere) or is it proof that the thought-language itself is more plastic than Fodor allowed? And do scenes like Winston's dreams and his diary writing fit LOT better, since those are cases where inner stuff leaks out despite the vocabulary constraints?

I'd like you to take both sides seriously — the actual LOT debate stuff (innateness, systematicity, whether you can think what you can't say, natural language as input/output vs as the medium of thinking) and the actual novel, not a strawman version of either. Say where the reading holds, and be honest about where the novel resists it or where Fodor's framework has to bend to fit. I'm not writing a paper or anything, this is for my own sanity and maybe a blog post, but I want to be able to tell readers something the standard Orwell commentary misses.
