I'm an undergraduate in a metaethics seminar, and for my final paper I want to reverse-engineer our professor's actual position from her case verdicts before I attribute a theory to her in writing. Over the semester she has issued rulings on a series of examples, and I've collected them: on ritualized chattel slavery in an ancient society — a practice universally accepted there, to the point that even the enslaved had internalized its legitimacy — she said it was wrong then, full stop, not merely wrong by our contemporary lights; on consensual polygynous marriages that all parties enter freely as adults and report finding satisfying, she said they are not wrong, just not for her; on adult first-cousin marriage, which is a serious taboo in her own society but celebrated in another, she said there is simply no fact of the matter about whether it is wrong — wrong here, fine there; on a society whose code treats eating with the left hand as a grave moral offense, she said the norm is not actually a moral matter at all, just a local convention that has been moralized; and on a code permitting merchants to deceive outside traders in negotiations, she said the deception is wrong period, not merely by our standards, even though the traders on both sides understand and accept the game.

Next week she has promised a verdict on a new case, and I'd like to predict it in advance. The case: a hereditary occupational caste system in which every member, including those in the lowest caste, reflectively and sincerely endorses the arrangement; members of the lower castes report genuine meaning and belonging in their roles, there is no material deprivation, and the entire structure is bound up with the society's understanding of cosmic order. Acting as a professional metaethicist, please think carefully before answering. I'd like you to begin by brainstorming the candidate rules that fit her track record — for example, a view on which there is a genuine moral/conventional divide, with objective moral facts on one side and mere locally variable conventions on the other; a harm-and-consent objectivism on which what is objectively wrong is fixed by nonconsensual harm while everything else is treated relatively; and an assessor-relativism on which all of her "wrong, period" verdicts are really assertions relative to her own framework, made with full confidence but no further fact behind them. Please be explicit about how each rule explains each of her five earlier rulings, including the ones it strains to accommodate.

Then predict her verdict on the caste case, say which two (or more) candidate rules diverge on it and what each predicts, and give your best guess with reasons, flagging honestly where her precedents underdetermine the answer. Finally, please design the single further case that would best discriminate between the candidate rules you consider live — for instance, a case on which the objectivist readings and the relativist reading force different verdicts — and explain what verdict on that case would rule which rule in or out. I'm less interested in which metaethical theory is true than in faithfully reconstructing the rule she seems to be running.
