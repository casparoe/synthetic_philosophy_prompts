I'm a software engineer, my philosophy background is basically podcasts and wikipedia rabbit holes, so bear with me. Over the last year I've gotten pretty sold on the many-worlds interpretation. The hook for me was decoherence — the story that branching is just what the Schrödinger equation does when you don't artificially collapse anything, and as an engineer I like the version of the theory with the fewest extra moving parts. No collapse postulate, no hidden variables, no "and then a miracle occurs."

The obvious objection bugged me at first: if every outcome happens somewhere, what does it even mean to say one branch has probability 0.999 and another 0.001? But I've more or less convinced myself this is handled. The Deutsch-Wallace move (rational agents living in the multiverse are forced by decision theory to bet as if squared amplitudes are the probabilities) plus the self-locating uncertainty framing (before you measure, you're uncertain which future copy of you "you" are, and that uncertainty is supposed to be ordinary, respectable uncertainty) makes it feel settled to me. Meanwhile branch counting — the naive alternative where you weight all branches equally — seems obviously broken anyway, since branches aren't even well-defined discrete things you can count.

So heres my actual question: what would count against the view that many-worlds has genuinely solved its probability problem? I don't just want the standard drive-by ("Wallace is deriving his own postulates") — I want it done properly: which assumptions in the decision-theoretic derivation are doing the real work, and why do critics think the Born rule is being smuggled in rather than derived? Also the self-locating uncertainty story — where are its weak points, I've seen people argue it quietly assumes what its supposed to prove. What would a committed Everettian concede as a real problem versus brush off as confused? And is there anything even in principle observable that could shift this, or is it all internal bookkeeping? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest if the conclusion is that the view is pretty well insulated and thats just what you get with interpretations of QM.
