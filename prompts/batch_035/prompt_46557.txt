Is there an established term for the following explanatory configuration, or a literature I could locate if I only knew the keyword? I have in mind explanations that cite a rationale without any reasoner behind it: sentences of the form "the reason X is as it is, is that being that way achieves Y," where Y is an outcome that nobody ever planned, represented, or even noticed — and yet the sentence seems to do genuine explanatory work rather than functioning as a loose metaphor.

A few instances to fix the idea. A naturalist explains that the reason certain moths emit clicks when hunted by bats is to jam the bats' echolocation; no moth or bat grasps this, and no designer built it. A beekeeper explains that honeycomb is hexagonal because hexagons enclose the most honey for the least wax; the bees follow simple rules and know nothing of economies of wax. An economist writes that the reason prices move as they do is to pool information scattered across thousands of traders; no trader intends this, and most would be unable to state it.

I can find partial matches, but none exact. Teleology is the obvious neighbor, but teleological explanation has traditionally been understood to presuppose a mind — a designer, an agent, a cosmic purpose — and here the whole point is that no mind is available. Function talk in biology ("the function of the heart is to pump blood") is close, but functions look like selected effects, whereas what interests me is the reason-giving character of the explanation: these explanations purport to answer a why-question, not merely to record what a trait was selected for. And talk of "as if" intentionality seems to license the idiom without explaining why the explanations so often strike us as correct rather than merely convenient.

So: does the phenomenon have a name? If several candidate concepts partially fit, I would like to know which carves it best and where each leaves something out. If nothing in the literature matches, I would like a coined term with a definition precise enough to settle the boundary cases — for example, whether the phenomenon extends to social practices whose point has been forgotten by every current participant, and whether artifacts with conscious designers fall outside it by definition or only by degree. Finally, is the standard view that such explanations are shorthand for selection histories, or do some philosophers take the unowned rationale itself to be something real?
