my friend who did IR in grad school leans on the chicken picture to argue deterrence is basically stable: neither leader can rationally pick the crash, so the only real dangers are accidents, false alarms, and misread signals — spend on hotlines and deconfliction, not missile defense. but my other friend, more of a game theory guy, says chicken is exactly the wrong picture here. in chicken the crash is a choice both drivers make at the wheel, but in a crisis nobody selects war off a menu — the risk ratchets up through seperate steps that each look rational, and the crash can arrive without anyone choosing it. he kept describing brinkmanship as a competition in risk-taking rather than a contest of nerve, and talked about threats that leave something to chance — which sounds to me less like throwing the wheel out the window and more like swapping swerve() for a random number generator that only sometimes swerves.

audit the analogy for me: what maps onto what (who's the driver, what counts as swerving, what's the steering-wheel move in an actual crisis, what's the crash), where the mapping breaks, and then the part i actually care about — does my IR friend's stable-unless-someone-trips-over-the-cord conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was chicken carrying it the whole time? and if chicken is the wrong game, give me a better picture that a programmer could actually hold onto.
