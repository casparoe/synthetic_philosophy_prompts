I've been trying to work out what privacy actually is by going with my gut on a bunch of cases, and I want to know if there's a principle somewhere under the mess. Watching me dance badly in my living room through my window: violation. Watching the same dancing from the sidewalk because I left the blinds up: not a violation, my own fault. A data firm piecing together my purchases and inferring I'm pregnant before I've told my family: violation, obviously. My doctor knowing wildly intimate things about me: not a violation, it seems right that she does. My partner reading my journal without asking: violation, and I think it would still be one even if she only learned things she basically already knew. But now it gets weird. A stranger builds a detailed dossier on me purely from my public Instagram — everything I posted myself — and my gut says that's a violation, which seems flatly inconsistant with the blinds case. And a smart speaker that keeps a local log of everything said near it, which no human ever reads and which never leaves the device: I still feel like something's wrong there, even though nobody ends up knowing anything they didn't before. So the "who gets to know what about you" framing dies on the speaker case, and the consent framing dies on the doctor case (no real consent beyond a form, still fine) and on clicking agree to a 40-page policy (full consent, still a violation). My tentative principle was control: privacy is violated when information moves in ways I didn't direct. But that says the Instagram dossier is fine, since I directed every single post. Is there any principle that saves all or even most of these verdicts, and if not, which ones should I throw out? I suspect the blinds case and the sidewalk case can't both survive the dossier, but I can't tell which to give up. Also curious what your principle says about a case I didn't list: a painter who does a hyperrealistic portrait of a stranger crying on a park bench and exhibits it. And if my verdicts are just mush that no principled person should hold, say so straight up instead of stitching something together to please me.
