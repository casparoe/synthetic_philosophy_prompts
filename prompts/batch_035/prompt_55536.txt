Write a parody of the official terms and conditions for a magazine prize draw obviously modelled on Hofstadter's Luring Lottery: the prize is $1,000,000 divided equally amoung all entries received, and there is no limit on how many entries each person may send in. The gag is that the lawyer drafting the T&Cs has read Hofstadter on superrationality and is trying to legislate it into existence one clause at a time — an eligibility section defining the ideal entrant, a clause on what to do if you suspect the other entrants aren't reasoning the way you are, an anti-spam clause framed as an appeal to symmetry, a dispute-resolution procedure that quietly presupposes everyone will have cooperated. The deadpan legalese should slowly reveal that the whole contest only works if all entrants are clones of one identical reasoner, which they are not, and that the drafter knows it and is getting more anxious in the sub-clauses. The humour should come from the game theory being right — dominance reasoning, the tragedy of the commons, what actually happens to the prize when a million readers each submit a thousand entries — rather than from randomness, so a decision theorist nods along instead of wincing. Finish with a liability waiver covering emotional damages caused by other entrants failing to be superrational. About 600 words, numbered clauses, and please write in British English.
