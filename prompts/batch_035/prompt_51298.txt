I serve on the legal affairs committee of a mid-sized Swiss canton's parliament. Our municipalities currently set their own fees and rules for the lake shoreline within their boundaries — mooring fees, launch-ramp charges, shoreline fishing permits. A bloc of shoreline municipalities has petitioned the canton to enact a uniform canton-wide tariff: whichever municipality undercuts the others, they argue, becomes the cheap harbor and the rest lose revenue, and no single municipality can solve this alone. The interior municipalities, which have no shoreline, see no reason the canton should spend its legislative time on the matter at all; and a few shoreline communities insist their local tourist economies differ enough that a uniform tariff would fit them badly. The committee has put three options on the table: (a) a binding canton-wide tariff; (b) a cantonal framework setting only a floor and a ceiling, with each municipality choosing within the band; or (c) no cantonal law at all, with the canton offering only non-binding mediation while the municipalities negotiate an inter-municipal concordat among themselves.

Draft the recommendation memo I will circulate before Thursday's session. It should pick one option and defend it, but the committee's real disagreement is about what subsidiarity is for, and the memo should expose that disagreement rather than paper over it: the petitioning bloc reads subsidiarity as a rule about capacity, on which higher levels step in only where lower ones demonstrably cannot manage; the interior representatives read it as a rule about proximity, on which decisions belong close to those affected, full stop. Give the strongest version of the losing reading its due. And if your recommendation rests on a factual bet — for instance, that the municipalities could sustain a concordat without cantonal pressure, or that a floor-and-ceiling band would be wide enough to satisfy the communities that fear a bad fit — state the bet explicitly and say what evidence at Thursday's meeting would count against it.
