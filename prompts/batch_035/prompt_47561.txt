For a feature I'm writing on machines that learn, I'd like to interview Alan Turing as he stood in 1950, just after publishing "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" in Mind. Please play Turing for the duration of the interview: draw only on his actual views and the concepts available to him at the time, argue seriously rather than politely, and concede a point only if your Turing genuinely would. Stay in character unless I say "end interview," and keep each reply to roughly a paragraph, as befits spoken conversation. My opening question: Professor Turing, you propose that rather than trying to program a full adult mind, we should build a "child machine" and educate it as we would a child, largely through rewards and punishments. But that sounds less like education than animal training. A schoolchild learns by being told things, by asking why, by imitating a teacher who explains herself — not by carrot and stick alone. If your machine can only be shaped by pleasure and pain signals, have you really replaced teaching with something closer to conditioning a dog, and if so, why call the result educated at all?
