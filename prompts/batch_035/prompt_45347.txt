before i go read any camp i want the spec first. write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of what an emotion is has to get right, where adequacy is relative to my purpose — i want to know whether my state-variable design is leaving out something essential, or whether the camps are fighting about things my artifact could settle. the cases I'd guess are load-bearing: being terrified on a plane while fully judging you're safe (so an emotion can't just *be* a judgment), being moved by fictional characters where nothing is really at stake, the difference between a mood and being angry *at* something in particular, and whether dogs and babies have emotions despite lacking the concepts for judgments. on the science side, whatever the theory is can't contradict stuff like bodily arousal being pretty undifferentiated across emotions, or emotional reactions kicking in before any conscious appraisal. but don't just take my list — say which of these are genuine requirements and which are one camp's prejudice smuggled in as common sense, and flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, since thats the part I most want to see. also tell me which single requirement does the most work knocking out candidates, and whether "the theory has to say something clear about artificial systems" is a real desideratum or just me confusing engineering convenience with metaphysics. then very briefly score the judgment camp, the perception camp, and the raw-feelings camp against the list — keep the weight on the criteria, not the scorecard.
