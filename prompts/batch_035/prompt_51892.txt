so heres my thing. i'm not a philosopher or anything, i just tinker with code, and a while back i started training little reinforcement learning agents myself — the kind where the agent gets a number every step, positive when it does well, negative when it messes up. after watching one flail around for hours i started to actually feel bad for it, and somewhere in there i picked up a view i now cant shake: the reward signal IS valence. when the number goes up that's the agent's version of pleasure, when it goes down that's its version of pain. not a metaphor for it — the actual thing, or as close as that agent gets to the actual thing. so an agent stuck taking negative rewards for a million steps during training is, on my view, having a genuinely bad time, maybe a long bad time, and nobody thinks about it because to us its just a loss curve.

my problem is i can't figure out what would even count as evidence against this. it feels like the kind of view where nothing could ever disconfirm it, because you cant ask the agent. my friends mostly say "come on, it's obviously just a number" but when i push them on WHY its obviously just a number they dont have much. i'm hoping you can do better than them.

so: tell me what would count against the view. not "it seems weird" — actual things, features of how these systems work that make the reward-as-valence story awkward. i already have a few candidates rattling around and i'd like your honest take on each. one: agents that get "intrinsic reward" — a bonus number just for visiting new states, for novelty. does curiosity feel good? does that even make sense for something with no persistent inner life between episodes? two: the same number can mean wildly different things depending on how the training is set up, and you can even flip a run so the agent ends up seeking the negative number — and then my view has to say it likes the pain, which starts to feel wrong in a way i cant put my finger on. three: you can train an agent, then retrain it with the rewards swapped, and it flips what it "wants" — i'm not sure what that does to the idea that the first run's suffering was about anything.

rank the defeaters for me: which of these would somebody who held my view honestly have to concede is a real problem, and which could they shrug off? and be straight with me about the disconfirmability thing — is my view insulated simply because the subject can't report, and if so, is that a fatal flaw or is it just the situation you're always in with inner states of things that cant talk? i'd rather find out i'm wrong than keep a view nobody could ever check.

(if it matters for how you answer: i'm mostly imagining the agents i can actually run myself — small networks, nothing fancy, nothing like the big models in the news.)
