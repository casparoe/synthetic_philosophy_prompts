I run a monthly rationality meetup, usually 15-20 people, 90 minutes, and next month it's on hypocrisy and standing to blame. Design a game where they feel the problem instead of just nodding along to it. The wrinkle I actually care about isn't one-shot hypocrisy (person does X, then criticizes someone for X, everyone boos) — it's the graded, accumulating version: blame itself should be a move in the game, so players have to *choose* to spend something on blaming, and the record of their own past defections erodes how that move lands. Concretely something like: over several rounds players can take small profitable defections against the group, and can also publicly call out another player, which costs the caller a bit but shifts norms for everyone. By midgame nearly everyone's hands are dirty, the rational move is silence, and they should feel the room slide into a state where nobody can blame anybody without the whole table going "you? really?" — and where they notice it's the accumulated record doing that, not the last act. Give me rules, materials, a payoff scheme that doesn't use real money, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the difference between "you did it too" as a cheap tu quoque fallacy and as a genuine loss of standing to blame. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which part of the real concept — the bit where standing is about the blamer's character and relationship to the person, not just their factual rap sheet — the game flatly cannot reproduce, so I have to supply it myself afterwards. No lecture up front, and they shouldn't hear the phrase "standing to blame" until the debrief. Ideally the game also produces at least one player who stayed clean the whole time, so we can look at whether their blame landed differently — but don't rig it so that's guaranteed, I want to see whether it happens.
