My husband and I have a long-running thing about whether the city will actually finish the light rail extension to our end of town by the end of 2030 — it's been "two years out" since we moved here, and every year there's a new delay announcement. A while back we tried to settle it with a bet, and since we couldn't agree on odds right away, we ended up negotiating like a couple of bookmakers: I'd propose terms, he'd counter, and we kept shifting the numbers until we found a point where one of us would actually take the bet. We landed on 3-to-1 against it opening, so I pay him 30 if it doesn't happen and he pays me 90 if it does. We shook on it and forgot about it.

Here's the part that's bugging me. Since then some new rumors about the contractor came out, we each privately re-estimated how likely the line is, and when the bet came up again neither of us wants to touch it. Not at the old odds, not at better odds for either side — at any price, one of us says no. My brother-in-law, who's an economist, overheard us talking about this at dinner and said it's exactly what his theory predicts: the moment we stopped being able to bet with each other, we effectively came to agree, even though if you asked us separately we'd each still say the other is being naive about the constructon schedule. He wouldn't explain, he just smiled and said "no trade."

That last bit annoys me. How can refusing to bet at every possible price mean we agree, when we'd still argue about it over dinner? And if he's right, does that mean any time two people who've talked something to death are still both eager to bet on opposite sides, one of them must be irrational, or hiding something, or is there some other way out? It also feels backwards on its face — surely a market where nothing trades is just a market where nothing happened, not a market that has reached some deep consensus? Part of me thinks he's just being smug at dinner, but he seemed genuinely confident, so before I bring it up again: is there actually something to this, and what exactly is it claiming about the two of us?
