Longtime lurker here, been reading the rat-sphere since the OB days, and last weekend's meetup put me in an argument I couldn't cleanly win. Topic drifted to utilitarianism and someone tried to end it with revealed preference, and his summary of the theory has been bugging me since. Paraphrasing him: "Revealed preference is Samuelson's idea that if you pick A when B was affordable, you've revealed you prefer A over B. Utility is then just defined as whatever you turn out to maximize, so the whole thing is circular — by construction you always maximize your utility, since any action counts as maximizing it, which is why the theory can't be tested and can never be wrong. It's basically a tautology dressed up as science, and behavioral economics refuted it anyway once it showed people's choices are systematically inconsistent, so nobody takes it seriously anymore. Which means when economists say people act to maximize utility they're just saying people do what they do."

Some of that sounded right to me and some of it felt like a motte-and-bailey, but I couldn't say exactly where it slides. Like, I know the circularity complaint is a real one that people raise, and I know utility in econ isn't supposed to be happiness — it's more like an index of preferences? But is he right that utility gets defined as whatever you maximize, or is that smuggling in a step? And I vaguely recall that revealed preference was supposed to do the opposite of what he says — put demand theory on observable data instead of unobservable mental states — which would make it weird if it also made everything unfalsifiable by design.

So: how fair is this as a summary? Please go through it piece by piece. Which parts would an economist who works on this accept, which parts are conflations of two things the theory keeps apart (I suspect "utility" is doing double duty somewhere), which parts are real objections being oversold as refutations, and does his big conclusion — untestable tautology, refuted by behavioral econ, nobody takes it seriously — actually follow from the parts that survive? Also, if there's a version of the circularity worry that does land, I'd like to know what it is and what the standard response to it is, seperately from the version that doesn't land. I don't need a reading list, just the actual analysis.
