My friend and I argued late into the night about moral responsibility, and I want to know whether her position is even coherent before we go another round. Her verdicts so far: ordinary uncoerced wrongdoing deserves full blame; if physics turned out to be deterministic, nothing would change for responsibility (she's a compatibilist); the drunk driver who kills a pedestrian is responsible, because he drank voluntarily; the kleptomaniac who cannot respond to moral reasons is not. But she also holds that an agent whose deliberation is covertly steered by neuroscientists — including Pereboom's later manipulation cases, where the tampering leaves the agent's practical reasoning outwardly intact — is not responsible, even when the agent wholeheartedly identifies with the resulting desires and would endorse them on reflection. When I pressed her with Strawson's Basic Argument — that she can't be responsible for what she does unless she's responsible for how she is, and that the regress of character-formation never bottoms out — she waved it off: nobody needs to be causa sui, she said, and ordinary responsibility doesn't require ultimate self-creation.

Next week we fight about a new case: an adult whose values were deliberately engineered from infancy by parents executing a detailed design, whose reasoning is now entirely normal and reasons-responsive, and who wholeheartedly endorses those values. Can you work out what rule she is actually running — a reasons-responsiveness rule with an ownership condition on the mechanism's history, a mere-bypass rule on which covert steering matters only while it actually bypasses deliberation, a source-of-character rule on which any pro-attitude the agent didn't shape herself undermines responsibility — whether her manipulation verdicts can really coexist with her compatibilism or whether something has to give? Then predict her verdict on the engineered adult under each candidate rule and say which you think she'll give, and design the further case that would most sharply divide the rules — including one that tests whether her quick dismissal of the Basic Argument survives her own treatment of the manipulation cases.
