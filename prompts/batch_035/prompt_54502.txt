I've been going through Frankfurt-style cases trying to work out what my gut verdicts on moral responsibility actually add up to, and I can't find the thread. Here are my verdicts, honestly reported:

1. Jones decides on his own to shoot the ambassador. Black is in the next room monitoring, ready to force Jones's hand if Jones shows any sign of deciding otherwise — but the trigger never fires, Jones does it entirely on his own. Responsible. The backup intervener just doesn't seem to matter to me.

2. Black intervenes before Jones even starts deliberating — a device directly writes the decision into his brain. Not responsible, obviously.

3. Plain deterministic universe, no Black, no device, Jones just is the kind of person who'd do it and always was going to. Responsible.

4. Jones's values were engineered by decades of careful conditioning started when he was a kid. No intervener standing by now, the conditioning finished long ago, and today he acts smoothly from those values, the way anyone acts from their values. NOT responsible — and this verdict feels stronger to me than almost any of the others.

5. Jones votes Clinton because he wants to; in the nearest world where he starts to waver, an undetectable implant would make him vote Clinton anyway. Responsible. Basically same as case 1.

So here's my problem. Cases 1, 4, and 5 all involve "couldn't have done otherwise," but my verdicts diverge, so whatever my principle is, it's not the principle of alternative possibilities — fine, that's what Frankfurt cases were supposed to teach. But cases 3 and 4 are both fully deterministic histories that produced the agent's desires, and my verdicts diverge there too. Is my principle something about being the "source" of the action? Maybe. But then what exactly makes determination-by-laws (case 3) different from determination-by-a-conditioner (case 4)? If the answer is "a manipulator had intentions about Jones," then a conditioning program generated by a random coin-flipping machine would restore responsibility in case 4, and I honestly don't know if I believe that or not. Also someone will tell me that even in case 5 there's a "flicker" of an alternative left — Jones could start to waver — but that flicker does zero work for me, it can't be what my verdicts are tracking.

Can you give me the principle, or maybe a couple of candidate principles, that fits these verdicts? State your best one in a single sentence right at the top, then tell me: which of my verdicts, if any, it forces me to give up and how bad that loss is; and what it predicts for two cases I haven't been able to decide — (a) Jones was raised in a cult, so his deepest values are cult-made, but there's no individual manipulator, it was just the whole social system; (b) Jones voluntarily drank himself blackout at a party, and Black's device only operates on people who are blackout drunk, so in the actual world the device fires and moves his hand — do we hold him responsible, and if so, on what grounds, since it wasn't his hand in any normal sense? Keep the whole thing under 700 words please, I want to actually use this, not frame it.
