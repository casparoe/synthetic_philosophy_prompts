I'm putting together the closing session of an undergraduate seminar on confirmation and induction, and I'd like to end with a villanelle on Goodman's new riddle of induction. The two refrains should be, roughly, the two rival projections from the same evidence: one line to the effect that every emerald examined so far has been green, so the next one will be green too, and one to the effect that every emerald examined so far has been grue — green if examined before some future time t, blue otherwise — so the next one will be blue. The obsessively returning refrains should carry the philosophical burden: the same evidence keeps generating both projections with equal formality, and the reader should come to feel that no additional observation of emeralds made before t could ever adjudicate between them, since both hypotheses fit every such observation perfectly. Somewhere in the late development I'd like a gesture at the symmetry point — that if grue and bleen had been our primitive color terms, then green would be the one needing a time-indexed, disjunctive definition — but the poem should not resolve the rivalry in favor of either predicate. The final quatrain, where the two refrains finally share a stanza for the only time, should leave them resting on the identical evidence, neither defeated.

Please write it as a poet with graduate training in philosophy of science would. Before drafting, take a moment to settle on the formulation of grue you want the poem to presuppose — for instance, whether t is a fixed calendar date or whether the predicate is indexed to each object's examination — since this affects what the refrains can literally assert. Keep the technical machinery accurate enough that a philosopher who teaches the new riddle would nod rather than wince, but avoid name-dropping: Goodman's name should not appear in the poem, and a term like "entrenchment" should appear at most once, if at all. The vocabulary available to a reader who knows nothing of the riddle should still make the poem work as a poem about two predictions that no future Tuesday could separate. Please don't force rhymes with technical coinages.
