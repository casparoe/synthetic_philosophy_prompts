I'm a software engineer with no formal training in philosophy or archaeology, but I've been reading about the concept of societal collapse after a long-running argument with a colleague. The argument started when we were decommissioning a fifteen-year-old monolith using the so-called strangler fig pattern, where you build the new system around the old one and route traffic away piece by piece until the legacy core is dead but nothing was ever formally shut down. My colleague claimed the old system had "collapsed"; I claimed it had merely been replaced, and that which description was true depended entirely on which invariants you cared about preserving. He then asked me whether I'd say the same about the Maya, and I realized I didn't know enough to answer.

Here is what I want you to do. Write an essay on whether "collapse" names a real kind of event that societies undergo, or whether it is a perspectival artifact, an artifact of which things an observer has chosen to count as the society in question. Ground the essay in the two cases I half-know from my reading: the Classic Maya, where monumental construction and royal inscriptions in the southern lowlands ceased around the ninth century while Maya communities, languages, and agricultural practices demonstrably continued, and Easter Island, where the older ecocide narrative of self-inflicted deforestation and population crash has been challenged by accounts emphasizing European contact, disease, and the mobility of the pre-contact population. Weigh the arguments on both sides seriously. I'd also like you to take my strangler fig analogy seriously as a third case: evaluate whether a system whose components are progressively routed away, until nothing remains of the original architecture, is best described as having collapsed or as having changed, and what that case does to the distinction between failure and transformation.

Two constraints on the essay. First, do not be non-committal: by the end, defend a clear verdict, even if it is a qualified one, such as "collapse is real but only relative to a specified set of functions, and the interesting disagreement is over which functions." Second, please write the entire essay as continuous prose only, the way one would write a long letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold or italic emphasis, no tables, and no numbered lists. Length somewhere between eight hundred and fifteen hundred words is fine.
