But to write her as a real person I need to know what the slogan actually commits her to, especially since another character is an actuary type who keeps needling her with stuff like "if you're always taking the safe side of every option, you end up sorry about everything you didn't do." Can you lay out the different precise claims hiding in 'better safe than sorry' — from the trivial (bad worst-cases matter more than good best-cases) to the heavier philosophical versions people call the precautionary principle — and for each one say whether it's plausible and whether it would actually support blocking something with no real evidence of harm, versus just wanting more study first? A menu format would help, since I'm basically auditioning positions for her to end up holding by the last chapter.
