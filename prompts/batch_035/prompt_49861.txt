quick sanity check before I embarass myself in front of my metaphysics reading group. I'm writing up where I stand on when some things add up to a further thing, and here's the list:

(1) there's no object composed of my left shoe and the Eiffel Tower. stuff scattered across the world doesn't suddenly make a new thing.

(2) but when bricks are stacked into a wall, the wall is a real object. definately not a shorthand, it's an actual thing that exists.

(3) that said, the wall isn't some extra entity piled on top of the bricks. it just IS those bricks, arranged wall-wise. so counting walls in your ontology is innocent — you're not inflating your inventory of what there is.

(4) composition is never vague. for any given things there's a hard fact of the matter whether they compose something or not. no borderline cases of existence.

(5) statue and lump of clay are two objects sitting in the exact same place at the same time, and I'm fine with that. they're not identical because they come apart under squishing — the lump survives being squished, the statue doesn't.

my main worry going in is that (1) and (2) sound like special pleading, like I'm just picking favorite composites (walls yes, shoe-Eiffel no) without any principle behind it. people keep telling me that. but honestly I suspect theres stuff I'm not seeing, so don't limit yourself to that. go through the whole set, tell me which bits genuinely clash, which ones only look like they clash, and rank the clashes by how bad they are. and if something has to give, tell me what the cheapest revision is and what it costs me elsewhere.
