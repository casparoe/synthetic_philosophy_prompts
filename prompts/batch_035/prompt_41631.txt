I've basically signed on to the naturalist view of disease: a condition is a disease only if some part or mechanism fails to perform its biological function, and that's it -- you don't need any claim about welfare, suffering, or what society finds acceptable. Normativism, the view that disease is partly constituted by our values (disease = dysfunction PLUS harm, or whatever people happen to disapprove of), strikes me as a mistake about what medicine is tracking. But I want to stress-test my position rather than just enjoy it. What would actually count against it?

Here are the places I already feel some pressure, so you can go beyond them rather than just repeat them: aging (everything is running roughly as selected for, yet it sure looks like the paradigm disease); sickle-cell trait (dysfunction relative to one environment, adaptive in another, so which function do we use?); and myopia in modern environments (a mismatch case where the "dysfunction" is really in the environment). And here's one I've been chewing on that I haven't seen discussed much: suppose a vestigial-looking organ, like the appendix, gets shown to have a genuine selected function after centuries of everyone (including naturalists) calling its inflammation a disease. Did appendicitis only become a disease retroactively, once the etiology was filled in? That feels like it should embarrass my view, but I can't tell if it's a real problem or just the ordinary fallibility of medical fact-finding.

What I want from you: a ranked map of defeaters, from fatal to merely awkward. Distinguish the ones a committed naturalist like me should concede really bites (my own candidates above, or better ones you can construct) from the ones I'm entitled to shrug off, and say why the line falls where it does. I'd also want you to consider the collapse risk: if I keep tacking on epicycles -- evolutionary functions here, causal-role functions there, an environment-relative clause for mismatch cases -- is the view still risking anything, or have I quietly rebuilt normativism inside the machinery? And if it HAS been insulated into unfalsifiability, is that fatal for a definition of disease, or is it the kind of thing (like definitions of "species") that should just tolerate some gerrymandering? I'd find it most persuasive if you can give me a concrete case where my view and a harm-inclusive hybrid view give different verdicts, and defend one of the verdicts. Answer in American English pls.
