I'm also in our school's philosophy club and we just did a unit on philosophy of time, specifically presentism (only the present moment is real) vs eternalism (past, present, and future all exist equally, so time is like a 4D block where nothing ever really "goes away"). That stuff honestly breaks my brain a little and I'd love to build the story around it.

Here's what I was imagining: a teenage girl named Maya who works weekends at her late grandmother's clock repair shop, trying to finish restoring an old pocket watch her grandmother never got to fix. While she's working on it she starts slipping into this dreamlike state where she perceives time "from the outside" — her whole life, her grandmother's whole life, every tick of every clock in the shop, all as one single object that just IS, sort of like a painting she's inside of. From that outside view nothing is lost: her grandmother isn't "gone," she's just at another part of the block, exactly as real as Maya is. I want the story to genuinely wrestle with whether that idea comforts Maya about her grandmother's death or whether it feels cold and weird and like it cheapens her grief, and then how she snaps back into ordinary flowing, rushing time when the watch finally starts ticking again. Like maybe the ending can be ambiguous about which view of time she ends up believing, or which one she *needs* to believe.

Can you write it, something like 900-1200 words? Please make it an actual story with scenes and dialogue and not just characters standing around lecturing each other, but I do want the presentism vs eternalism stuff to actually matter to what happens and how Maya feels, not just be vibes or decoration.
