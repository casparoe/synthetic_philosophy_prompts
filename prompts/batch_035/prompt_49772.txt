A guy on my team and I have been going back and fourth for about a year on whether "intent alignment" — build the AI so it pursues what the operator intends for it to pursue — actually solves the alignment problem or just relabels it. To be clear about the terms as we use them: intent alignment is when the system does what the operator means rather than what they literally typed or specified; value alignment, the broader thing, would require the system's goals to track something like the operator's (or humanity's) actual values, including in situations the operator never thought about. His line: if the operator's intentions are greedy, careless, or just badly informed, a perfectly intent-aligned AI is a very efficient way to optimize for bad goals, so the narrow framing hides the hard part. My line: most near-term risk lives in the gap between what people mean and what machines do, and the value-level stuff can't even be specified yet, so narrow is the only workable target.

I'd put my credence at maybe 0.65 that intent alignment, if fully achieved, would avert most of the risk from misaligned AI specifically — set aside deliberate misuse and weaponization, or the question becomes hopeless. He'd say 0.15. Give me your own number, and more importantly the decomposition: how much rides on whether the operators of powerful systems will mostly have intentions that stay benign at scale, how much on whether the gap between what an operator intends and what's actually good shows up in individual decisions or only in the aggregate (thousands of operators each locally reasonable, collectively a mess), how much on whether "intent" is even determinate once a system's choices shape things the operator never formed any intention about, plus whatever other terms you think belong. A number and a line of justification for each, then the combination. Then tell me which term you're least confident in and what would shift it, and whether me and him most plausibly disagree about the facts of who will actually be running these systems, about what "solved" has to mean, or about something neither of us has managed to put into words yet.
