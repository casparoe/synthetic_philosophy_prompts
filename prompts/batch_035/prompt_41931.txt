I'm a retired agricultural geneticist -- thirty years of breeding barley, so heritability estimates were just daily tools for me: you'd compute them from your field trials and they'd roughly tell you the response to selection you could expect next generation. Since retiring I've gotten deep into philosophy of science, and I've been floored to learn that for human traits (IQ, height, personality) there's this whole fight about whether heritability numbers mean anything at all for malleability. My philosophy reading group has two members who have been going at it for over a year: one is gung-ho about behavioural genetics and insists that twin-study heritabilities around 0.5-0.8 for IQ genuinely constrain what interventions in the current environment can achieve, the other thinks these estimates are nearly information-free about malleability, citing gene-environment covariance, the equal environments assumption, and the point that high heritability can coexist with any amount of change given the right environmental shift. They finally agreed to try an adversarial collaboration, and I've been asked to draft the joint statement, which I am decidedly not qualified to do -- so you do it.

Structure it as follows: a section called "Agreed claims" (8-12 numbered claims, a sentence or two each) for everything both could sign; then a "Disagreements" table with each residual disagreement stated in wording its own side would accept; then a "Cruxes and tests" section (about the same length as the agreed claims) giving, for each crux, a concrete case or empirical finding both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. The Dutch height increase and the Flynn effect are the obvious test cases -- use them if they're the best ones, but I'd like at least one crux or test that isn't the standard fare, since as a breeder I know the animal and plant literature has cases the human folks never mention (selection experiments, the difference between narrow and broad sense heritability, what happens when you standardize the environment). And do tell me if part of their dispute is really about words -- I have a suspicion the phrase "tells you nothing" is doing a lot of work in it. Please write in British English, and keep it fair -- I genuinely don't know which of them is right.
