I'm a member of the five-person admissions committee of our housing cooperative. Our bylaws state that an applicant should be admitted only if they can comfortably afford the monthly fee and would be a good fit with the community. Last month we ran into a situation that still bothers me: three of us thought the applicant could afford it, and a different three of us thought they'd fit in well, yet a majority ended up voting against admission, because the two majorities overlapped in only one person. In other words, a majority of the committee accepted each of the two conditions, but a majority rejected the conclusion that the conditions were supposed to entail. I later learned that social choice theorists call something like this the discursive dilemma, and that it is related to an older legal puzzle about whether judges should vote on the premises or the verdict.

Our committee has since split into two camps. One side wants us to vote only on the final question of admission and never record our views on affordability or fit at all. The other side wants us to vote on the two conditions separately and then treat the committee's decision as whatever follows logically from the majority verdicts on those conditions, even if a majority of individual members would have voted the other way on the bottom line. I've been circulating a third proposal of my own: at the start of each year, every member secretly rates every other member's judgment on each of the two conditions, and during admissions our votes on each condition are weighted by those reliability ratings, so the more trusted members count for more. I like this because it seems to respect both the logical structure of our bylaws and the fact that we don't all have equally good judgment on money matters versus community matters, but I can't shake the feeling that I'm missing something.

Please argue that my weighted-voting scheme is the wrong way forward, and that the committee should instead adopt the premise-based approach: vote on the conditions, let the decision follow logically from the majority verdicts on them, and accept that this will sometimes override the majority's preference on the final question of admission. In making the case, please explain why the committee should tolerate outcomes that most of its members would have rejected when asked directly, and address what you see as the strongest objection to the position you are defending.
