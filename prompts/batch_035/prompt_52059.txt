For the annual parody issue of our department's graduate-student newsletter — we're a philosophy of science program, and last year's issue (a fake referee report that rejected Galileo for insufficient statistical power) went over well — I'd like a piece this year satirizing Kuhn's account of scientific revolutions. The form I have in mind is a set of software release notes: a changelog announcing a major version bump of a scientific theory, written by the new paradigm's maintainers in the earnest, self-congratulatory voice of actual release notes. Use standard changelog headings — Highlights, Breaking Changes, Deprecations, Migration Guide, Known Issues, FAQ, Contributors, Roadmap — and aim for roughly 650 words.

The humor has to come from the actual structure of Kuhn's picture, not from cheap shots at scientists. Normal science should read as routine maintenance: bug-fixing, patching, incremental work within accepted specs. Anomalies should appear in Known Issues as bugs that have been open for decades, repeatedly deferred with the note that they'll be addressed in a future release, until the maintainers give up and ship a whole new version instead. The central joke should be incommensurability, played straight: the Migration Guide cannot actually be written, because any attempt to say what changed is phrased in the new version's vocabulary, so users still on the old version cannot tell what changed; cross-version comparative claims ("this release is twice as accurate") should be marked undefined, on the grounds that there is no common measure across versions. Rollback should be unsupported — once you see the rabbit you cannot go back to seeing the duck, and users who attempt a downgrade report only that everything now looks like a duck that was secretly a rabbit all along. Include a Deprecations section for concepts the new version drops without any successor mapping, and a quietly sinister line in the changelog's history section noting that prior release notes have been amended to make the old version look like an early draft of the new one.

A few constraints on tone and fairness. Keep the paradigm generic — something like "Classical Theory v1.0 → v2.0" — so the satire targets the structure of paradigm shifts rather than Newton or Einstein partisans. Stay deadpan throughout: no winking at the reader, though the piece should remain legible to someone who has only heard of Kuhn in passing. The closing Roadmap section should promise steady progress in terms the maintainers can't cash out, which is where the piece lands its deepest cut: Kuhn denied that paradigms improve the way software does, and the joke is precisely that the maintainers don't know that. A historian of science should finish it feeling it was written by someone who has actually read The Structure of Scientific Revolutions — and a specialist should laugh at the incommensurability material rather than wince at it. If you include a FAQ entry on holdouts who never convert, let the maintainers' answer be a faithful one: conversion happens by persuasion rather than proof, and the holdouts mostly age out while continuing to publish in the old version's journals.
