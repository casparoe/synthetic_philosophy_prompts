For about a decade I've followed the wild-animal suffering and moral-weights discussions on the rationalist and EA blogs, and I've noticed my own views oscillating without a principled anchor, so I want to force the reasoning into the open.

The proposition: at least one currently existing insect species has phenomenally valenced experiences — states there is something it is like to be in, and that feel good or bad — as opposed to mere nociception or adaptive behavioral control implemented without any feel at all.

Please give a number: your credence in that proposition. Then decompose it into the sub-questions it rests on. The ones I can see are roughly: whether some broadly functionalist theory of consciousness is correct, versus one that ties valence to specific neural machinery of vertebrates; given functionalism, whether insect nervous systems satisfy its conditions, and whether those conditions are graded — so that "partially sentient" or a fractional welfare range is coherent — or threshold-like; and how diagnostic the experimental evidence on learning, self-medication, and trade-off behavior in bees and flies is of valence, rather than of sophisticated non-conscious control. Add, drop, or restructure terms as you see fit, assign each a probability with one sentence of justification, and show how they combine into your final number.

Then two further things. First, a friend of mine claims the decomposition barely matters morally: on his view, at even a 1% credence, expected insect welfare already dominates nearly any other cause, so the only question worth answering is whether the credence sits above or below some astronomically small threshold — meaning the sensible response is to invest in driving the credence toward zero or decisively away from it, not refining the middle. Say whether you agree, and if not, where his argument breaks. Second, tell me which term in your decomposition you are least confident in, and what realistic evidence — short of solving the hard problem outright — would move it the most.

Hard limit: at most 500 words. If you run out of space, cut the background exposition, not the numbers.
