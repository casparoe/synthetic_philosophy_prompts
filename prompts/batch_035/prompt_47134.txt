Taking a phil of science course this semester and still chewing on Kuhn. There's a pattern I keep noticing in real academic fields that I can't find a word for: a discipline gets known for a certain kind of success — predictions, explanations, interventions — then the successes dry up, and instead of either fixing the theory or going through a Kuhnian crisis/revolution, the field quietly redefines what it was trying to do all along, so the failures stop counting as failures. Like economists after 2008 saying economics was never supposed to predict crises, or chunks of psychology deciding they're an "exploratory" science after the replication mess. It's not the ordinary ad hoc patching of a theory, because the theory itself doesn't change — what changes is the field's story about its own goals. Is there an established concept for this move? The nearest thing my classmates keep throwing at me is a degenerating research programme, but that's about tacking on hypotheses to save a theory, and I'm talking about revising the mission statement. If nothing established fits, can you coin a term, define it carefully, and tell me where its edges are — e.g. is it different from just admitting your field had unrealistic ambitions, and is it always bad? I won't be happy with just a name, I want to know how it relates to degenerating programmes and to Kuhn's crisis stuff.
