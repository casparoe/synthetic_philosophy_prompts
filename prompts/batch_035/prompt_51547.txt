The question we set was:

"P.F. Strawson, in Freedom and Resentment (1962), tries to answer the worry that if determinism is true, holding people morally responsible makes no sense. Explain what the reactive attitudes are, how Strawson uses them to answer the determinism worry, what the objective attitude is, and what role excuses and exemptions play in his argument."

Here is the answer I have to grade, word for word:

"Strawson's reactive attitudes are the emotions we feel in response to how other people treat us and treat others — resentment when someone slights you, gratitude when they go out of their way for you, indignation when you watch someone mistreat a third party, guilt when you are the one who did wrong. These emotions belong to what Strawson calls the participant stance, our normal way of relating to people as agents rather than as things to be managed. The determinist says that if all our actions are fully caused by prior events, then blaming and praising people is a mistake. Strawson's answer is that this worry cannot really get a grip on us, because the reactive attitudes are too deep in human nature to be given up — a society whose members adopted the objective stance toward everyone is barely imaginable, and for a member of it to try to adopt it wholesale toward everyone would be a kind of practical impossibility. So his argument is basically that these practices of praise and blame are justified because they are socially useful — society needs sanctions to function, and that is what makes it rational to go on holding people responsible, whatever determinism says. The objective attitude is the stance we take toward a person when we stop holding them responsible: instead of resentment you get, say, treating their behaviour as a fact to be dealt with. Strawson says we take this attitude toward anyone whose conduct is explained by causes rather than chosen, which is exactly what determinism claims about everyone, so in the end his own argument concedes that if determinism is true the participant stance loses its footing. As for excuses and exemptions, Strawson points out that we already exempt children and the severely mentally ill from blame, and he takes this to show that the reactive attitudes are optional in principle — we can simply decide to switch them off, which is what the determinist is really asking us to do."
