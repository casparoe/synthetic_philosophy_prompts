Some background before my question. I grew up in a tight-knit religious community whose beliefs were, from the inside, simply not up for debate: not because anyone had arguments, but because the questions never arose. Leaving that community in my late twenties was less like losing an argument than like watching the floor I stood on quietly relocate. When I later worked through Wittgenstein's On Certainty in a reading group during my PhD, I recognized the phenomenon immediately in his remarks about hinges — the certainties, like the earth's having existed for a long time or my having two hands, that don't function as conclusions from evidence but as the untested background against which anything counts as evidence at all. What struck me hardest was that my deconversion didn't look like one of those dramatic overthrows of a belief; it looked more like a hinge being loosened over years, by accretion, without my ever being able to point to the proof that did it. Nobody in the reading group could tell me what, on the hinge picture, had actually happened to me.

I've now started a three-year postdoctoral fellowship in epistemology with no teaching duties in the first two years, and I have to decide what to work on. The open questions I can see from where I stand are these, roughly: whether hinge commitments have epistemic status at all, or are better understood as something non-epistemic (a stance, a way of acting) — and what turns on that choice; how hinges change, if they're not revised on the basis of evidence, since my own case suggests they do change somehow; whether hinge epistemology, built mostly as a response to radical skepticism about the external world, extends to actual deep disagreements between frameworks like the one I left and the one I now inhabit — or whether extending it just collapses into relativism, such that I can no longer say anything was wrong with the community that raised me except that I no longer share its hinges; and finally whether the whole reading of On Certainty as a positive epistemology is a projection onto a therapeutic work that would have resisted systematization.

What I want from you is not a survey of the field but a defended agenda. Rank the open questions above (or add one I've missed and say why it outranks mine), and justify the ordering explicitly: state the criteria you're using — importance, tractability for one philosopher working solo over three years, and, above all, which questions are upstream of which, since my suspicion is that whether hinges are epistemic quietly determines the answer to everything else. For each of your top three, tell me what working on it would actually consist of, what would count as making progress rather than rearranging terminology, and what development in the next few years would make you promote or demote it. Please also be candid about which of my candidates you think is overrated or badly posed. I don't want a reading list, and I don't need citations — positions and arguments are enough.
