I've been trying to figure out what I actually think about the nonidentity problem by going with my gut on cases, and now I have a pile of verdicts that I suspect don't all fit together. Here's what I've got.

1. A woman who could wait a month decides not to, takes a drug she knows will make her child born without arms. Clearly wrong, and I feel like I want to say it's wrong *to the child*, not just wrong in some abstract way.

2. Same scenario, but now I'm told: if she'd waited, a different child, with arms, would have been born instead. The armless child she actually has still has a life worth living and is glad to exist. My gut still says what she did was wrong, but I get queasy saying anyone was harmed, since the alternative for that particular kid was never existing.

3. The depletion thing: a society burns through resources so that people two centuries later have much worse but still decent lives, and because of all the economic changes, completely different people end up existing than would have under a conservation policy. I'm confident the depletion policy is wrong. Like, extremely confident.

4. But here's where I wobble: a 41-year-old woman who wants a kid now rather than waiting two years, knowing the risks of various conditions go up with her age and that waiting means a different child. This seems fine to me. Or at least way less wrong than case 1, even though the structure looks the same, just with probabilities instead of certainty.

5. Two deaf parents doing IVF who pick a deaf embryo over a hearing one, on purpose, because they want a deaf kid. Wrong, I think? But the deaf kid will presumably have a good life within Deaf culture, and the alternative for that embryo-situation is a different hearing child. I keep flip-flopping on this one, honestly.

6. Someone points out that basically any policy decision shifts the timing of conceptions downstream, so in fifty years it's different people who exist than otherwise. I refuse to accept that this means no policy can wrong future people, so add that verdict too.

So: is there a principle that gets all six of these, or close? I have a vague thought that maybe the difference between case 1 and case 4 is certainty vs. probability, but that feels like a dodge, and I can't tell whether it even handles case 5. Please give me the actual principle (or a couple of candidates), state it precisely enough to apply, tell me what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on, and be honest about which of my verdicts it just can't accommodate if any. If you think one of my verdicts is plain mistaken and should be abandoned rather than saved, say which and why. Also curious what the principle predicts for a case I haven't mentioned: someone in a war zone who conceives a child knowing it'll grow up in poverty and danger, with no realistic option to wait, where the life is still just barely worth living.
