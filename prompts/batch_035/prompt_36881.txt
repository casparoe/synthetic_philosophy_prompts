I keep hitting the same wall in the betterness literature: everyone insists 'better than' has to be transitive because anything else is irrational, and then the spectrum cases come along (trading duration of pain against intensity, or longer-but-duller lives against shorter-richer ones) and each local step seems fine while the global comparison seems obviously right. Before I pick a side I want the spec first, not another defense of one camp.

So write me the desiderata: the list of things any adequate verdict on whether 'better than' is transitive has to get right — the cases it has to handle, the intuitions it has to explain rather than just wave off, the distinctions it can't collapse (worse vs. merely not-better, betterness vs. choiceworthiness, all-things-considered vs. pro tanto), and what it owes to the theory of choice, since transitivity is usually defended via choice principles and the spectrum cases seem to let you choose sanely at every step while walking into disaster. For each requirement, say what puts it on the list. Mark which ones you'd treat as non-negotiable and which are basically camp prejudices, and if you think some subset of them can't be jointly satisfied, say so — that part is what I most want to see, and don't soften it.
