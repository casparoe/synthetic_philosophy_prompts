ok so for my philosphy elective we have to write a ~1200 word essay on this question my teacher gave us: is being as morally good as possible actually a good way to be? like picture the "moral saint" — someone who donates a kidney to a stranger, gives away basically all spare money, never lies even to spare someones feelings, always picks helping people over their own projects. the question isn't whether that person is admirable (everyone agrees they are), it's whether you'd want to BE them, or want your best friend to be them.

apparently there's a famous paper arguing no — that a perfect moral person would have to be kind of terrible to be around, because things like having a sarcastic sense of humor, or being obsessed with a dumb hobby, or being a great cook just because food is good, all take time/energy that a truly maximized moral person would have to give up. the paper also splits the saint into two types which I found interesting: the loving saint who helps everyone because they just genuinely love everyone (weirdly their life might actually be happy), vs the rational one who does the moral math every time and always follows through. and I guess the problem hits differently for each one?

anyway here's what I need the essay to do. weigh the arguments on both sides — the "you lose everything that makes a person interesting" side, but also the pushback my classmate raised that honestly messed me up: maybe hobbies and wit and whatever only SEEM valuable to us because we're already not very good people, so you can't use them against morality without cheating. also there's the lazy-student objection (which is tempting me lol) that the whole argument is just an excuse for us to keep being mediocre. don't end with a wishy-washy "it depends on your values" non-answer — my teacher explicitly banned that. you HAVE to defend a verdict, even a partial one like "the 100% saint is bad but the 90% saint is great," as long as you actually justify where you draw the line and why there and not 10% lower.

write it at the level of a decent high school senior — smart but not pretending to be a grad student, no name-dropping philosophers I havent mentioned, no made up citations (we get marked down for fake references so just don't use any). and if it helps, my teacher said the strongest essays will say something about which specific things a saint genuinely CAN'T have vs which they could keep, so try to actually do that breakdown instead of hand-waving it.
