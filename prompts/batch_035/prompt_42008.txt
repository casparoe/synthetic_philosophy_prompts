I've come around to a view about free will and determinism and I want to know what would actually count against it. The view: determinism and predictability come apart, and once you take chaos seriously, determinism stops being scary. Chaotic systems are fully deterministic yet unpredictable in principle for any predictor embedded inside the system -- you can't measure the fine-grained state to infinite precision, and whatever error survives your measurement grows exponentially until it ends up mattering. We're all embedded predictors, so the future is epistemcially open to us for good, and I think that's the only kind of openness the free will problem ever needed. The future isn't sitting there waiting to be read off, so my choices aren't 'already made' in any threatening sense. Play it straight: tell me what should move me, ranked from fatal to merely akward. A couple of worries I half anticipate -- the incompatibilist's argument was never about anyone actually predicting me, it was about the future being fixed by the past plus the laws, readable or not, so maybe I've confused 'no one can read it' with 'nothing has written it' -- and there's the question of whether my deliberation is even chaotic in the relevant sense, or whether choices could be coarse-grain predictable even when the underlying dynamics are chaotic, which would blunt my story. Say which of these a smart defender of my view would concede versus fight, and whether my fallback -- that epistemic openness is the only openness we ever cared about -- risks anything or is just an unfalsifiable hedge.
