For my population ethics seminar we've just finished the unit on the procreation asymmetry, and I'd like a dialogue for my notes in which the two positions are defended at full strength. The setting: two advisors on a committee drafting the founding charter of a long space voyage that will end with settling a new planet, and the committee must decide what the charter says about the settlers conceiving children en route. One advisor is a total utilitarian; the other accepts the procreation asymmetry, holding that creating a life below the neutrality line wrongs the person who must live it, while there is no one wronged by a happy life that is never created.

Rather than trading slogans, they should argue through concrete cases the committee actually faces: (1) a settler who wants to conceive, knowing the child will certainly inherit a painful but manageable condition and will have a life that is happy overall once it is counted up; (2) a choice between founding the colony with ten thousand settlers whose lives are predicted to be extremely good and a hundred thousand whose lives will be barely above neutrality; and (3) a screening rule that would prevent the conception of any child predicted to fall even marginally below neutrality, at the cost of also preventing some very happy conceptions.

Make sure the total utilitarian raises the continuity challenge — that as the predicted life improves headache by headache, there must be some point at which the reason to create it switches on, and the asymmetrist owes us an account of where and why — as well as the non-identity point that the child in case (1) would prefer her life to never existing, so the wrong the asymmetrist cites seems to wrong no one. Give the asymmetrist a real reply about why existence and nonexistence may not be comparable for the person themselves, and let the total utilitarian then push the harder question of whether that restriction leaves any sense in which a colony full of flourishing lives is better than the dead rock it replaced — a question the asymmetrist must either bite or explain away. Both advisors should be genuinely competent, each conceding exactly what their view can afford to concede, and each reply should engage the other's last actual point rather than restarting the lecture.
