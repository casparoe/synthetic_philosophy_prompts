So, hi — I'm a novelist. Twenty-odd years in, mostly literary fiction, and my whole career has basically consisted of making readers cry over people who don't exist. Which is why the debate about whether video-game characters matter morally keeps snagging on me: is it wrong to torture an NPC, is there some tiny cruelty in deleting your Animal Crossing villager, that kind of thing. I have zero philosophy training, so don't assume I know what "moral patienthood" means. What I do have is a working craft vocabulary, and I want you to do the whole translation into that.

Restate the philosophers' debate in the terms I actually use every day: flat versus round characters, whether a character is "alive on the page," what a character wants (the want that drives the plot), killing your darlings, how much work the reader or player does to fill a sketchy character in, consistency, wincing at second-hand embarassment on a character's behalf, the difference between a character the author controls and one that's started running on its own. Translate all the main positions — the ones that say what matters is the capacity to suffer, the ones that say it's really about corrupting the player or coarsening them, the ones that say the NPC itself has some claim — one by one, into my vocabulary.

And then I want the honest residue report, because that's the part I actually care about: which of their claims translate cleanly into craft terms, which ones you had to distort to squeeze in, and which can't be said in my vocabulary at all. Is there something they're arguing about that I just have no words for — and if so, is that because my vocabulary is missing something, or because their disagreement is less substantive than it looks? I'd especially like your read on whether my vocabulary smuggles in an assumption that characters are made rather than found, since that could quietly decide the whole question.

My own hunch, which I want tested against the translation rather than just nodded along with: that a character's moral pull is entirely borrowed — the character is a hook for OUR feelings, and anyone who says the NPC itself matters is confusing the puppet for the puppeteer. If that hunch survives the translation without distortion, say so plainly. If it only survives because my craft vocabulary can't express the alternative, I'd want to know that even more.
