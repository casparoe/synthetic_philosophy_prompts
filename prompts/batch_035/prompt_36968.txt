Background: I'm a novelist, 300 pages into a book with a first-person narrator whose voice is nothing like mine. Quick thing I've noticed over years of doing this, and it's gotten stronger with this book. When I'm deep in a drafting session, my own inner monologue basically shuts up. Decisions still get made — this sentence and not that one, this scene ends here — but there's no felt sense of anyone making them, and the sense of "me" only shows back up afterward, a few minutes or an hour later, kind of taking credit for the whole thing, like a press secretary who wasn't at the meeting. Related and maybe more interesting: when I switch between writing my narrator and being myself, the muscle feels like the same muscle. Dropping into her voice and dropping back into mine — "what would she notice here" vs "what do I want for lunch" — run on the same machinery, and neither one feels more discovered and less invented than the other. On a good day I get the unsettling sense that "me" is just a character I've been writing continuously for forty years and so happen to be very fluent in, and that there's no one behind the pen, so to speak, no author distinct from the prose. My editor thinks I'm describing burnout. I don't think I am — it's not unpleasant, and I'm more productive than ever.

So my question: is there an actual name for this configuration? Not the flow thing exactly — I know athletes and musicians talk about self-loss during performance, but what I care about is the part after, where the self comes back and narrates itself as having been in charge, and the part where selfhood and characterization feel like the same operation running at different update rates. And I want to be clear it's not that dissociated, floating-outside-yourself feeling — I've had that once during a panic attack and it's nothing like this; this is more intimate, not less. I've half-gathered that some of the Buddhist-adjacent philosophy people say stuff in this neighborhood (self as construction, something like that), but I've also seen enough meditation-culture hand-waving to be wary. I want the real carving, not vibes.

So — and I'd like you to actually put on the philosopher-of-mind hat for this, someone who knows both the analytic personal identity literature and the contemplative traditions and can keep them separate — tell me: does the thing I'm describing have an established concept attached to it, one or several? If yes, say precisely which parts of my description it covers and which parts it leaves out, and how it differs from the nearby things it isn't (flow, depersonalization, whatever the clinical framing would be). If it doesn't map onto anything clean, then coin a term for it and give me a definition plus a couple of boundary cases that show where it ends. Please brainstorm a few candidate framings before committing, and be honest if the answer is "this is three different things you're gluing together" — I'd rather know that than get a tidy label slapped on.
