For years I've held the view, familiar from the LessWrong "simulators" line of thought, that a base LLM is not an agent at all: it's a superposition of characters, an engine that simulates agents and processes, and whatever goals it appears to have are properties of the simulated persona, not of the model. On this view, "the model" has no more goals than a novel does. I've recently been pushed to ask what would actually falsify this, and I want a serious answer, not a defense.

So: what would count against it? I'm interested in observations that could in principle go the other way, not just restatements of the view. Some candidates I can think of, roughly: behavioral tendencies that survive arbitrary framing — if RLHF or other fine-tuning leaves the model with preferences that persist across wildly different personas and can't be steered away by prompt context, is that a "default character," or something more like the model's own agency? Consistent cross-context goals that don't track any plausible training distribution? Situational awareness — the model modeling itself as an entity persisting across the conversation, planning over that fact? Where exactly is the line between "a simulator that has collapsed onto one strong simulacrum" and "an agent," and does the distinction do any real work at that point, or is it verbal?

Please rank the candidate defeaters from fatal to merely awkward; say which of them a committed defender of the simulator view would concede as genuine counterevidence versus explain away (e.g., by saying any observed agency is just a particularly persistent simulation), and flag honestly where that explanatory move shades into making the view unfalsifiable. If you think the view has in fact been insulated by qualifications, say whether that's a defect for a claim of this kind or just what happens when a coarse framing meets a messy reality. I'd also like your own view on which defeater is most likely to actually materialize, and whether anything in the last few years of observed model behavior already constitutes weak evidence against the simulator framing rather than for it.
