retired physicist here (35 years in condensed matter, so quantum formalism is old hat to me, philosophy of physics is new). I've been arguing about Bell at the pub with my grandson, who's a philosophy grad student, and he says I'm contradicting myself somewhere in my list of positions but can't say where. honestly neither can I. here's the list:

(1) the experiments are done — loopholes closed, and the CHSH correlations really do exceed what any local theory could produce.
(2) so the lesson of Bell is that it's nature itself that's nonlocal: no theory of any kind, however unlike quantum mechanics, can reproduce those statistics with purely local goings-on. nonlocality is a fact about the world, not about a particular formalism.
(3) at the same time I've come round to Everett — no collapse, everything is in the wavefunction, and I like it precisely because it adds no extra machinery and its dynamics is just the local schrödinger equation.
(4) I still hold the relativistic picture is the fundamental one: nothing propagates faster than light, physics lives in block spacetime.
(5) and I don't buy superdeterminism — the detector settings are free in every sense that matters, and a universe that arranges the photon arrivals to fool us into thinking we choose is too ugly to take seriously.

go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look like it. my own suspicion is (2) and (4) are the problem, since nonlocal sounds like it should mean something faster-than-light happens, but I half suspect I'm using 'nonlocal' to mean two different things without noticing. and I can't shake the feeling that (3) is quietly at odds with (2) as well — if everett explains the correlations with purely local dynamics, in what sense did Bell prove nature is nonlocal? rank the tensions by severity, tell me which commitment has to give and what it costs me, and don't go easy — I gave a seminar on this in 1994 and got it wrong then too.
