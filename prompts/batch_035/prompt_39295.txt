ok so I've been going down the everett rabbit hole for a few months and finally wrote down where I stand, and I want someone to poke holes in it before I bring it to our phil-sci meetup. here's the package:

1. I'm an Everettian — no collapse, all branches of the wavefunction are equally real, nothing gets erased when the world splits.
2. I'm also a strict determinist about the fundamental dynamics: the wavefunction just rolls forward unitary step by unitary step, there is literally zero chance anywhere in the physics.
3. I think the Born rule problem is basically solved by the decision-theoretic derivations, Deutsch and Wallace style: if you grant some rationality axioms about caring about your branch-successors and their payoffs, weighting branches by amplitude-squared stops being a postulate and falls out of decision theory.
4. But I also think the deeper story about why there's probability in a deterministic theory at all is self-locating uncertainty — before the measurement I genuinely don't know which future branch-person is "me," and quantum probability is just that kind of ordinary ignorance about where I am in the multiverse.
5. I think branch counting is a total non-starter, because the number of branches isn't even well defined — branching is a fuzzy coarse-grained thing we impose on the wavefunction, so you can't count branches and expect to get anything like probabilities out.
6. And I think typicality reasoning is fine and we already need it elsewhere anyway (e.g. assuming we're not in some specially cursed location in cosmology), so everett isn't uniquely guilty of smuggling in "typicality" assumptions.

does this hang together? go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinly in tension and which only look like it, rank the real ones by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what that costs me. I don't know which one I'd drop and I'm slightly worried I've been leaning on two incompatible stories for why everett gets probabilities right at the same time.
