Before I try to decide between the competing accounts of why pain is bad for you — that it is bad in virtue of its distinctive phenomenology, that it is bad only in virtue of its downstream effects or its attitudinal component, that its badness is primitive and not further analyzable — I want the specification first. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of the badness of pain has to get right. At minimum I want you to cover which cases it must handle — pain asymbolia, where subjects report locating and intensity-rating the pain while being unbothered by it; congenital insensitivity to pain, where there is no bad sensation but plenty of badness happening to the body; pain that is welcome or instrumentally useful, as in exercise or a warning that prevents greater harm; and chronic pain that persists after any signaling function is exhausted — along with the distinctions the theory must not collapse, such as the difference between the badness of a pain and its intensity, and between pain's being non-instrumentally bad and its merely being unwanted. Include which empirical results from neuroscience and psychophysics the theory cannot contradict (for example, the apparent separability of the sensory-discriminative and affective-motivational components in lesion and imaging studies), and for each requirement say what motivates it and whether it is non-negotiable or reflects the bias of one camp. Most importantly, tell me whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect that the conjunction of first-person authority about pain's badness with the asymbolia cases is unstable, and I would like to know if the list itself is effectively a result. Then close with two things: which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate theories, and what would convince you that this requirement is a mistake rather than a constraint — that is, where your own reasoning in building the list is most likely to go wrong, and what argument or case would change your mind about the final shape of the list.
