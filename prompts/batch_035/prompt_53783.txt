I've basically talked myself into the view that the sunk cost "fallacy" isn't always a fallacy -- that honoring sunk costs can be rational in at least three ways. First, the evidential route: if a past decision to invest reflected information I no longer have full access to, my continued persistence is data, and abandoning the project just because the pain of remembering costs is salient can throw that data away. Second, the self-signalling route: someone who reliably finishes what they start is a different, more credible agent than someone who doesn't, and that trait is worth building even project-by-project, in the way akratics deliberately bind themselves. Third, the interpersonal route: being known as someone who doesn't cut and run changes what projects and partners you're offered in the first place -- it's a reputation asset that a clean-slate decision theorist trashes. My friends think I've just rationalized finishing a bad PhD. What would actually count against the view? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward -- including things defenders of the fallacy-as-irrationality would concede and things they wouldn't, and whether the evidential and self-binding routes quietly collapse into ordinary forward-looking reasons (in which case my view is just a relabelling, which I'd want to know).
