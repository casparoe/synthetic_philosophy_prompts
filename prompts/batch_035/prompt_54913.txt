quick background so you know where im coming from: I run a small nonprofit that publishes very short biographies — think Plutarch-sized, like 40-60 pages each — aimed at teenagers, and the whole premise is that you can learn about how to live from studying individual lives, not from abstract ethics. We're now planning our next five years and I want to think about which of the *unresolved questions* behind this project actually matter most for us, because right now we're making editorial decisions (which lives to publish, how much of a person's ugliness to include, whether to pair lives the way Plutarch did with his comparisons) that implicitly take positions on all of them, and I'd rather do it deliberately.

The questions I keep circling, roughly: whether admiring someone's courage or integrity commits you to a stance on the whole person (our subjects are basically never clean — most were monsters about *something*), and whether the standard fixes (local admiration, separating the achievement from the life) are actually coherent or just rhetorical; whether hagiography is simply corrupted moral learning or whether selective emphasis is doing necessary work that honest biographies can't do, i.e. whether the saint-making instinct has a function we shouldn't fully abandon; whether imitating exemplars can be distinguished from unthinkingly copying them, or whether "emulation" just collapses into rule-following once you make it rigorous; whether the narrative form itself is a problem, since a biography presents a life as coherent and chosen when most lives are neither; and whether pairing or contrasting lives (the Plutarch move) teaches comparative judgment in a way single lives can't.

Which of these (or something I'm not listing) should a small outfit like ours actually spend its limited thinking budget on? Rank them and defend the ordering — I care about things like which questions are upstream of others, which we could actually resolve with our own editorial experiments vs. which are just philosophy we'd be ventriloquizing, and which are overrated because no answer would change what we publish anyway. Also tell me what would make you reshuffle the ranking — e.g. if we decided to include living people as subjects, or shifted to a much younger audience. I'm less interested in a survey than in a defended agenda.
