Next month I'm giving a job talk to a department that's strong in general philosophy of science but has nobody who works on philosophy of history, and I could use a prep sheet for the Q&A. The thesis I'm defending: narrative explanation in history is a genuine form of explanation in its own right, not a defective or elliptical version of covering-law explanation. My argument, roughly: narrative explanations work by making an outcome intelligible in hindsight — they present the event as the thing a trajectory was heading toward — and they do this using what Danto called narrative sentences, descriptions of past events that only became available later (nobody in 1789 could have described something as an early episode in the French Revolution, because that description only exists once the Revolution exists). That later vantage point is doing real explanatory work, and a Hempelian covering-law model can't reconstruct it, because on that model the explanation of an event can't essentially involve information about what came after it. So the covering-law theorist who treats historical narratives as explanation sketches — tacit laws waiting to be made explicit — is, I want to say, misdescribing what historians are actually doing when their explanations are any good.

The audience: about half the department is phil of science, mostly people who think Hempel was basically on the right track and will say the sketch view handles narratives fine once the laws are filled in; a couple of them work on causal modeling and will come at this with interventionist tools, wanting to know what my narratives do that a causal graph doesn't; there's one metaphysician who asks about backwards causation in literally every Q&A; and two affiliated historians who show up to these things and, in my experience anyway, think the whole covering-law debate is sixty years of philosphers arguing about a discipline none of them read. The historians are honestly the ones I can't predict — I don't know if they'll attack me for selling narrative short or for dressing up their craft in theory it doesn't need.

Include the one question I'm quietly hoping nobody asks.
