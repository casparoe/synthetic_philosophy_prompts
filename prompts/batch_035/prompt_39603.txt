My next door neighbor and the guy across the street both did the exact same dumb thing before leaving town for thanksgiving week: left an old space heater plugged in on autopilot, each in a bedroom nobody would be in. The guy across the street came home to a perfectly fine house. My neighbor's house burned down, her dog died, and the fire nearly jumped to my side of the duplex too. Here's what's eating me. Everyone on the street, myself included tbh, now treats these two like they're morally different people. The unlucky one keeps apologizing like she killed someone (the dog, basically, which she loved), and the lucky one jokes about how he "got away with it." Intellectually I know they did the identical thing and the wind that day is the only real difference. But I can't get myself to actually feel that way, and part of me even thinks the lucky one is worse now, for joking. Is there any actual difference between what they did, or only in what happened? And is the guilt my neighbor is drowning in appropriate — does she owe it to the dog somehow — or is she just unlucky in a way that shouldn't include feeling like a bad person? I keep flip flopping on whether its fair that she's now "the one whose dog died in the fire she caused" and he's just some guy. Who's right, me-the-feeler or me-the-thinker?
