So I'm a novelist, and the book I'm working on is told in close third person from inside a woman's head. At one point in it she catches herself noticing that shes noticing her own jealousy — like a camera panning back to reveal the cameraman. My editor thinks this is just a stylistic trick, but I've been reading about higher-order theories of consciousness lately (roughly: a mental state only becomes conscious when there's some further mental state directed at it), and I suspect theres an actual philosophical fight under my scene that I should understand properly before I write it.

Write me a dialogue between two characters who take opposite sides in that fight. One is a higher-order theorist: for her, consciousness is essentially a kind of self-awareness, so a state is conscious only insofar as something else — a thought or representation — is about it. The other is a first-order theorist: for him, a state can be conscious all by itself, just by representing the world, no inner monitoring required. Make them equally sharp — neither should cave in or talk past the other, and no strawmen. Specifically I'd like them to actually press each other on a couple of points my chapter keeps circling around:

1. Misrepresentation. If the higher-order state gets the first-order state wrong (say it represents me as seeing red when the underlying state is really of orange), what am I actually conscious of? The higher-order person should have an answer ready, and the first-order person should push back on it.

2. Absorption. When I'm lost in a book I don't seem to be conscious of the words on the page, and when I snap out of it, suddenly I am. Does that mean the absorbed state was unconscious? That seems wrong. The first-order person should use this to argue that attention, not a second-order state, is doing the real work — and the higher-order person should have a comeback.

The dialogue doesn't need to mention me or my novel at all, just two people arguing over coffee. Keep it to maybe 600-900 words, with real back-and-forth rather than alternating speeches. And please write it in American English.
