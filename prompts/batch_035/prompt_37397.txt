I'm a retired geologist (35 years, mostly mineral exploration, then a stretch teaching undergrads) and since I stopped working I've gotten properly hooked on philosophy. The thing I keep chewing on lately is population ethics — whether a bigger population is better, all else equal. Every time I think I've pinned down what I believe, the next thought experiment contradicts the last one, and the reactions I've recieved from friends I've quizzed are even messier. So instead of another essay, I want a sorting instrument.

Please build me a decision tree that takes a person from their starting commitments to a named position on whether a larger population is better. Build it from the logical space, not from a list of famous names: each branch node should be one concrete case a person can answer without knowing which position it leads to, and no leaf's name should leak into the questions. Roughly 6 to 8 nodes, and at minimum I want these leaves to appear: a total view, an average view, some kind of critical-level view, a person-affecting view, the procreation asymmetry, and one leaf for people whose answers just don't fit any coherent theory. Somewhere in the tree please use cases along these lines: (a) 10 billion people all living wonderful lives vs. 40 billion people whose lives are good but noticeably less rich; (b) a very large population whose lives are barely worth living, with the number growing without bound; (c) adding one new person whose life would be quite bad, no one else affected, versus adding one whose life would be quite good, again no one else affected; (d) whether there's some welfare level above zero at which adding extra lives stops making things better and starts making them worse. After the tree, flag any branch combinations that end up unoccupied (say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored), and flag any pair of leaves your tree genuinely can't seperate.

Then run me through it, because the whole point is to find out where I actually stand. My honest answers, for what they're worth: in (a) I say the 40 billion world is better; in (b) I'm horrified by it and say past some point adding barely-worth-living lives makes the world worse, not better; in (c) I say adding the bad life clearly makes things worse, but adding the good life does NOT make things better — it just doesn't make them worse; on (d) I wobble, sometimes I'm sure there's a level like that, other times it strikes me as totally ad hoc. Apply your key honestly and don't sand off my rough edges to land me somewhere flattering.

Format request: I keep my notes in a plain text file that I parse with a little script, so I'd like the very end of your answer to state my result in machine-readable form — \answer{...} enclosing just the short label of the leaf my answers put me on, even if it's the "incoherent" one.
