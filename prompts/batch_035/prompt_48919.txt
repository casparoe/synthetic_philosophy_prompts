I grew up in a Chinese family in Canada, and in a few months I finish my training as a nurse. My parents have arranged — really, decided — that I will work in the clinic my uncle runs in their neighbourhood, so that I can help look after them as they age, and eventually take it over. To them this is simply what a daughter does: a person, they say, is not a sealed-off unit but something like a knot in a web, made of what she owes and to whom, and a life that ignores that is not freer but thinner. My closest friends, all raised here, are appalled. They tell me it's my life, that my parents' sacrifice doesn't give them a claim on my future, and that an adult who lets others write her script has never really become herself. I've noticed something odd while turning this over: when I imagine refusing the clinic, I feel like I'd be protecting something real in me; when I imagine accepting it, I feel like I'm protecting something else that is just as real — not the same self, maybe, but something like continuity with people who made me. My friends aren't purely selfish people either; they bend their lives around friendships and causes all the time. So my question is this: are my parents and my friends actually disagreeing about what a person is, or just using different words for the same mix of attachments? If they genuinely disagree, is there any way to work out which picture gets the relation between me and my family right — or at least which one I should live by — or is the honest answer that both are self-consistent and the choice is just temperament? I want more than reassurance; I've been chewing on this alone for months and can't get past the feeling that I'm only flipping between two vocabularies rather than reasoning.
