Over thanksgiving my cousin and his wife got into it about their son going vegetarian, and I watched the whole thing play out without being able to referee it myself. Quick background: their son (16) announced he'd stop eating meat, and the argument wasn't about whether factory farming is bad — everyone agreed it is — but about whether his abstaining matters at all. My cousin's position was that it basically doesn't: the meat counter at the grocery store is stocked weeks in advance based on aggregate forecasts, no pig is saved by one person's skipped ham, and thinking otherwise is vanity dressed up as ethics. His wife countered with what she called the expected-value point: sure, your individual purchase almost never tips anything, but production does adjust to demand over time, and probabilistically spread out over a lifetime of purchases your expected effect on the number of animals raised and slaughtered isn't zero — it might even be several animals' worth, so "no pig is saved" is just refusing to average.

Then it got more interesting. My cousin said the expected-value move proves too much: it would apply equally to voting (one vote essentially never decides a national election, but multiply a tiny probability by a huge payoff and you get "you must vote"), and he takes that as showing the argument is broken, not that he should vote — if a calculation tells you a ham sandwich and a presidential election have comparable stakes-per-action, the calculation has a bug. His wife answered that he was dodging: the harm exists either way, millions of people each doing a negligible bit add up to something enormous, and if every individual can truly say "my part does nothing" then nobody is responsible for a harm everyone participates in producing — which she claimed is flatly irrational, like being the tenth person to pile onto a victim and saying the tenth blow made no difference to the outcome. My cousin's last word was that meat-buying isn't like the beating at all, because in the beating scenario the harm is over-determined only if the others act, whereas meat demand would crush the same number of animals even if he personally had never existed — his purchases are redundant, not just small, and redundant contributions can't wrong anyone. Then dessert was served and it never got resolved.

Also please say whether there's anything both of them are missing that would reframe the whole thing — my cousin's wife hinted at some "complicity" idea she couldn't fully spell out, and I couldn't tell if that was a real move or just changing the subject from causation to guilt.
