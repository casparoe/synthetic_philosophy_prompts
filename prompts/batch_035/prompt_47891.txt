My housemate does philosophy and she's got me halfway converted, but she's also landed me with an argument I can't find the exit to, and I'm pretty sure it proves too much. Some background so this makes sense: we'd both agreed the old Epicurean move — that death can't be bad for you because when it arrives you aren't around for anything to be bad for — doesn't settle things by itself, and her preferred fix is the deprivation story: death is bad for you not because of anything it does to you while it's happening, but because it leaves you worse off than you would have been had you kept living. Her argument, her numbering:

(1) For an event to be bad for you just is for it to leave you worse off than an alternative — worse off than you would've been in some other way things could have gone.
(2) Dying when I did leaves me worse off than surviving longer, since there was good stuff ahead of me.
(3) So death is bad for me.

So far I'm honestly fine with this. The trouble is what she does next, because she leans on the "an alternative" in (1) and refuses to let me narrow it:

(4) On (1) as stated, any alternative counts — it doesn't have to be the closest one, and it doesn't have to be a world where the event simply fails to happen.
(5) Being born a week earlier would've given me a week more life. Not winning last month's lottery would've left me much better off. Having slightly better knees would've let me keep running.
(6) So being born when I was, losing the lottery, and having my actual knees are all bad for me.
(7) And if every non-optimal feature of my life is bad for me, then "death is bad for you" was bought at the price of "basically everything is bad for you," which feels like alot to pay.

For what its worth, I want to keep that death is bad for you, and I want to keep that ordinary small harms (stubbing my toe) are bad for me, without "the lottery result harmed me" being on all fours with those. My instinct is to force her to compare death only to the closest world where I survive, but I can already hear her asking whether the closest world where I don't die is one where I'm kept alive on machines for four decades, and whether the closest world where I don't stub my toe is one where I wore different shoes, and at that point I've lost the thread. Which premise or step do I reject, why did it look fine, and what does rejecting it cost her account?
