Heres the thing I cant stop chewing on. Look at how we actually use random selection in real institutions:

- Juries in the US are picked by lottery, but then both sides' lawyers spend enormous effort un-randomizing the result -- voir dire, strikes for cause, and a limited number of peremptory challenges to knock out jurors who seem undesirable. The lottery gives you a pool, and the adversarial process then corrects it.
- Citizens' assemblies like the Irish one that dealt with abortion back around 2016 do something almost opposite: they use a lottery but then curate it with stratified sampling so the group "mirrors" the population on age, gender, region, education, etc. And crucially the assembly's output was advisory -- parliament and eventually a referendum got the final say.
- Jury service is compulsory (you can be fined for dodging it), while voting is voluntary and running for office requires you to volunteer, campaign, and want it.

So one institution treats the lottery as a neutral starting point to be corrected away by interested parties, another treats it as something to be refined until the sample is representative, and the two differ on whether the random people's verdict binds. Meanwhile the compulsory/voluntary split seems to run the other direction from what you'd expect: we force ordinary people to decide guilt but make all the lawmaking voluntary and self-selected.

What theory of representation and democratic legitimacy does this whole family of practices presuppose, if any? Is it something like "random citizens are good enough to find facts but not to make rules"? Does compelling service presuppose a view of consent that the voluntary election system quietly contradicts, or are those two consistent somehow? Reconstruct the implicit theory, tell me where the practices are underdetermined -- which rival theories would also license them -- and say what would actually have to change if we took the lottocratic view (that random selection itself confers authority) seriously instead of treating it as raw material to be fixed up.
