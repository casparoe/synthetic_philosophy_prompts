My reading group has been stuck on the Judy Benjamin problem for two terms now and I want a diagnosis of the stall rather than another proposed solution. Quick recap of how I see the state of play: Judy is told that if she's landed in Blue territory, the odds are 3:1 that she's in the Red-Blue area rather than Blue HQ, and the puzzle is how her probabilities should shift given she was told nothing about Red territory. Everyone agrees conditioning on the material conditional is a non-starter, since it perversely raises her credence in Red territory. Everyone also agrees the obvious replacements disagree with each other — imaging in Lewis's version, the Gärdenfors generalisation, van Fraassen's minimise-the-divergence fix, and the crude intuition that the Blue mass should just be redistributed 3:1 with everything else untouched, which as far as I can tell no formal rule quite captures. And then, nothing. The literature splinters into different distance measures and nothing is declared the winner.

My working theory is that the desiderata people wanted (leave Red alone, preserve the conditional's probability, minimise change, etc.) were shown at some point to be jointly unsatisfiable, and instead of drawing a lesson the field just dispersed — each camp keeping the desideratum it liked best and calling the rest intuition-mongering. My other suspicion is that the deeper issue is that nobody ever fixed what kind of thing being *told* a conditional is: a single proposition you can condition on, a constraint on the posterior, or something that's only well-defined relative to a similarity ordering between worlds, in which case the puzzle as posed is underdetermined and no rule could be the right one. It might even be that the story withholds exactly the info about the informant's evidence and reliability that any determinate answer would need, which would make the stall a symptom of the question being ill-posed rather than hard.

So: is this a substantive formal disagreement, a verbal one about 'updating on a conditional', or an ill-posed question wearing the costume of one? And given whichever diagnosis you think is right, what would count as actual progress here — a theorem, a taxonomy of kinds of conditional testimony, or giving up on uniqueness — or should we expect none? Please engage with the specific proposals rather than surveying them, and tell me if you think my 'dispersal' story about the desiderata is even accurate.
