I'm in a social epistemology seminar this semester and we just talked about the difference between epistemic bubbles and echo chambers. The way it was explained to us: in a bubble you simply never encounter the other side, whereas in an echo chamber you do hear opposing voices but you've been pre-loaded with distrust toward them, so exposure doesn't help and can even backfire. For my term paper I want to add my own twist though. I think there's a mirror-image failure mode that nobody names: a community that is so terrified of being an echo chamber that it treats every outside voice as automatically credible, endlessly "updates" toward whoever disagrees with it, and ends up unable to hold any stable position at all. I've been calling this an "openness collapse" in my notes (name is a work in progress lol).

So my question: could you compare epistemic bubbles and echo chambers as diagnostic categories, and then fit my openness collapse into the comparison? Specifically I'd want to know whether it's best understood as a species of echo chamber (just with reversed polarity of the trust-discount), as the inverse of both, or as a genuinely third kind of structure. And which of the three do you think is hardest for an individual to fix on their own, without restructuring their whole social environment? I have a suspicion its the echo chamber because the distrust mechanism is self-insulating, but maybe the openness collapse is worse since the person can't even tell something is wrong. Would love your take since atleast my TA didn't seem convinced the third thing exists at all.
