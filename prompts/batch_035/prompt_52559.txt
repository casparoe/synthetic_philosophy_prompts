I'm a backend dev at a mid-sized company, and there's this guy on my team — let's call him D — who is constantly calling out other people's screwups, in standups and especially in code review. He'll write these long comments about how someone's error handling is sloppy, how they should have caught something in testing, etc. Thing is, D is honestly not a great engineer himself. Last quarter he shipped a bug that took down our billing service for like 6 hours, and his own PRs regularly get flagged for the exact stuff he complains about (copy-pasted tests, no doc updates, you name it). When I brought this up to my tech lead — basically, why does anyone listen to D's criticism at all — the tech lead said something that stuck with me: the blame is cheap talk, but the technical points are often correct, and a correct point doesn't become incorrect just because the person making it is being a hypocrite about it.

So now I'm genuinly torn. On one hand I feel like D has sort of forfeited his right to blame people. If someone else on the team made the same review comment I'd take it fine, but from him it feels like it carries no weight — like he's using review as a status game rather than to improve the code. On the other hand, the tech lead's view also seems right: if I dismiss a valid point just because of who said it, I'm shooting the messenger.

Is there a principled way to sort out when a hypocrite's blame should carry weight and when it shouldn't? Does it matter whether D genuinely doesn't apply the standards to himself, versus someone who's harsh on everyone including himself? And here's the part that really bugs me: if I call D out on his hypocrisy, aren't I sort of doing the same thing he does — I'm not blameless either, I've cut corners on code review plenty of times. Does that undermine my standing to call him out, or is that a different situation somehow? Would love to hear how you'd think through this, because right now my gut and my tech lead are pointing in opposite directions.
