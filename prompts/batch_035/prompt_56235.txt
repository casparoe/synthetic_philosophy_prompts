I write novels (literary stuff under my own name, one long-running mystery series under a pen name) and I've been staring at a Ship of Theseus problem in my own files for months now. Ten years and eleven drafts into one book, and at this point literally nothing survives from draft one: not the plot, not the protagonist (he became a she, then a different she), not the setting, not a single sentence. My agent insists it's still the same book, just grown up; my writing group says it's a different novel wearing the first one's file name; and I honestly can't tell anymore. And it's not just the manuscript — the same thing keeps coming up with characters (I have one who survived four books but got a new backstory, new voice, and new goals in each one), and now with the series itself, since my publisher brought in a co-writer for the lastest installment while my name stays on the cover.

So what I'm after is not another wikipedia-tier retelling of the Greek ship. I want a procedure I can actually run — a checklist of tests, in order, with clear stop conditions — that takes the real philosophical positions on identity over time (continuity of matter, continuity of form or structure, continuity of function, whatever the narrative-identity folks would add) and translates them into questions a working novelist can answer about her own files. For each test, tell me: what answer counts as same, what counts as different, and which tests actually settle anything versus which just restate the question in fancier words. And be honest about the points where the procedure has to give up and hand it back to my judgment. Then run it on three cases: (1) the eleven-draft novel where nothing original survives but I made every change myself, one at a time, over a decade; (2) the character who kept only her name across four books; (3) the series installment that's mostly someone else's prose but follows my outline and my characters. I'd actually like it if the procedure gives me an answer I hate on at least one of these, because my gut says all three are "the same," and I have a suspicion that's just nostalgia talking.
