Write a dialogue between a defender of the Nash bargaining solution and a defender of the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution, set during an actual negotiation: two co-founders, Ana and Ben, dissolving their startup and haggling over how to split the sale price plus who does the (tedious) transition consulting work. That asymmetry is exactly where the two solutions part ways: Nash just maximizes the product of gains on the actual frontier, which in this setup can hand Ben essentially his whole potential gain while Ana gets well under half of hers, whereas Kalai–Smorodinsky insists each person walk away with the same fraction of their respective utopias. Have the Nashian lean hard on independence of irrelevant alternatives — why should how wonderful things *could have been* change what's fair now? — and have the KS supporter counter with individual monotonicty — it can't be right that Ana's payoff shrinks just because her ceiling happened to be higher. At some point have one of them invent a new variant on the spot — say a third founder with a veto, or a version where one side can secretly inflate their own utopia — and use it to stress-test the other's axioms. They should actually answer each other's best points, not talk past each other. End with them agreeing on a single thought experiment that would settle the matter, and disagreeing about which way it would come out. No citations needed, just the argument.
