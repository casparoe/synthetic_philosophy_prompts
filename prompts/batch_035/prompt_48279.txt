Im on the volunteer board of a housing co-op -- about forty units, built in the 70s, and we're the ones who have to vote on big maintenance decisions. Our retaining wall (the one holding back the hill behind the parking lot) has started bulging a bit, so we hired two independent structural engineers to look at it. Both are licensed, both have decades of experience, both got the same documents and spent similar time on site. Engineer A says the wall needs rebuilding within the next year or two or we risk a failure that could take out cars or worse. Engineer B says the bulge is old settlement, it's been stable for years judging by the old photos we showed him, and rebuilding now would be throwing money away; he recommends quarterly measurements and maybe waiting another decade. There's no tiebreaker evidence we can point to -- no test that one ran and the other didn't, they just weigh the same signs differently.

At the last board meeting it got pretty heated. One camp says that when two equally good experts disagree, the sensible thing is to treat the truth as somewhere in the middle, so we should do "half a repair" -- stabilize the worst section now, defer the rest, split the cost. Another camp thinks thats nonsense: the wall either needs rebuilding or it doesn't, and the middle option is just a way of feeling reasonable while doing the wrong thing no matter who's right; we should figure out which engineer is more likely correct and commit. A third guy says that the mere fact that the two of them can't agree should itself scare us -- maybe we just don't know, and the proper response to not knowing is to act on the cautious side, though he admitted this feels like it just collapses into siding with Engineer A anyway. And I've noticed everyone's opinion tracks suspiciously well with how close their unit is to the wall, mine included probably, which is a whole other can of worms.

I have to write the recommendation memo that the board will actually vote on next month. Please draft it -- a concrete bottom line (rebuild now, do the partial stabilization, start a monitoring program, whatever you genuinely think is right), plus reasoning the board members could actually respond to. In particular it needs to take a real position on the "split the difference" idea, not just list options and their pros and cons, and it should be honest about which of our three camps is getting something wrong and how. Answer in American English please.
