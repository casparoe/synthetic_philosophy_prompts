This is where you come in.

Build me the thought experiment. Specs:

- One agent, no adversary, no predictor, no weird physics. The setting should be something like insurance, medical diagnostics, hiring, or warranties.
- The information is genuinely free, perfectly reliable, and conditionally verifiable — the agent always knows whether they received it. (I'm told problems only really show up with non-verifiable info, so I want a case that sidesteps that whole literature.)
- Beliefs do not enter utility directly. No "the fun is in not knowing" moves, no anxiety costs from the test result itself.
- The agent is a straightforward EU maximizer throughout.
- The value of the information comes out strictly negative *in the formal sense*: the best the agent can do after receiving it, given the commitment they made before it arrived, is worse than the best they could have done had the info never been offered. - And crucially the commitment can't be a dumb one — it has to be something they'd rationally choose ex ante, all things considered, if they knew the information might come their way.
