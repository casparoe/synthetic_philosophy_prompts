i'm on the "culture council" at my office (sounds fancier than it is, its basically 6 people from different teams who meet once a month) and we have to make a recommendation to the whole company about a new "no white lies" policy. our CEO read about dutch directness somewhere and now he wants us to go the same way -- no more "great idea!" when you dont mean it, no "i might be busy friday" when you just dont want to go, no softening criticism in reviews, everyone just says what they actually think. he says the fake politeness is basically lying to peoples faces and honesty is a form of respect.

half the council is all in on this. the other half thinks its a trainwreck waiting to happen, and one of them (she's japanese) says its not that indirect communication is dishonest -- the consideration IS the honesty, like saying a hard thing gently is telling someone "i care about how this lands," and stripping that out isnt truth-telling, its just bluntness for its own sake. she also pointed out we have people from all over and the ones being asked to change how they talk are never the dutch-sounding ones, if you know what i mean.

i've been chewing on this by myself for weeks and i cant actually settle whether a white lie is a small act of kindness or a small act of disrespect. my half-baked theory so far: a white lie is like a little promise about what kind of relationship you two have ("we are the kind of people who smooth things over"), and maybe the real question is whether both people signed up for that. no idea if thats right though.

can you write the memo we'd circulate? like an actual recommendation the council can vote on -- adopt it, reject it, or something in between with concrete rules employees can actually follow (like, what do you say instead of "great idea!"?). it needs to take the directness side seriously as a real position and not just rudeness with a philosophy attached, but also give the indirect side its due. and keep it to about a page, nobody here reads long documents, definately not the CEO.
