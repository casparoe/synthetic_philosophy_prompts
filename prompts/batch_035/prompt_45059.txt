Two of my friends from work, both software engineers like me, spent a long drive back from a conference arguing about something one of them read, and I want a referee. The topic was what they ended up calling tranquilism: the claim that, ultimately, the only thing that matters for how well a life goes is the absence of craving and suffering — nothing positive is needed on top of that, and nothing else counts in its own right. (1) Dan defended it: all of our unhappiness traces back to wanting things to be other than they are, so a life free of craving is simply the best possible inner state, and talk of "positive" goods like achievement or love just describes ways of quieting or distracting from the wanting. (2) Maya objected that this makes well-being purely negative: on that view the best life is indistinguishable, from the inside, from being deeply sedated, and it can't distinguish a person with projects, curiosity, and people she loves from someone with none of those, so long as both are equally craving-free. (3) Dan answered that the things Maya lists only look like goods because they satisfy cravings — a person who "loves" someone in a way that would be lost without the attachment is, by that fact, attached, and the same goes for anyone who needs achievement to feel whole. (4) Maya said that move makes the view unfalsifiable: any counterexample gets reclassified as covert craving, so tranquilism is no longer a claim that could turn out false. (5) Dan's last word was that this isn't a defect but an insight — the pull Maya feels toward "something more" is itself the craving the view is about, so the fact that his view sounds impoverished to a craving-mind is exactly what it predicts. I genuinely couldn't tell who was right where. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: for each of the five numbered moves, say whether it's right, wrong, or right about something smaller than it claims, with your reasons. Then say, briefly, who comes out ahead overall and what (if anything) both of them are missing. Keep it under 500 words.
