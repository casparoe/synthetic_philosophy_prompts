I do ML for a living — applied side mostly, but I've read enough learning theory to be dangerous (VC bounds, Rademacher stuff, Wolpert's no-free-lunch theorems). Lately I've been reading the old philosophy of induction literature, because people keep telling me Hume basicly anticipated no-free-lunch, and I want the actual mapping, not the hand-wave.

Here's the job. Take the classical skeptical apparatus — Hume's argument that expecting the future to resemble the past can't be justified without circularity, and Goodman's grue/bleen thing where the exact same data supports incompatible extrapolations — and restate it step by step in the vocabulary I use every day: training distribution, hypothesis class, inductive bias, uniform convergence, regret, distribution shift, out-of-distribution generalization. Be honest about the seams: where my vocabulary has to distort a step to make it fit, flag it, and where there's no way to say the thing at all, say that too.

Format-wise, I'd like a table with these columns:

- claim: the philosophical step, stated in plain words
- translation: the same step in learning-theory terms, or the literal word "none"
- status: one of clean / distorted / inexpressible
- nearest_neighbor: the SLT concept people usually gesture at for this step, plus a note on why it isn't quite the same thing

And after the table, a JSON object along these lines:

{
  "survives": [steps that translated cleanly],
  "distorted": [{"claim": ..., "how": ...}],
  "inexpressible": [{"claim": ..., "missing_concept": ...}],
  "verdict": one of "residue_is_my_vocabularys_fault" / "residue_is_the_philosophys_fault" / "mixed", plus a one-sentence reason
}

The thing I actually want answered at the bottom: is Hume's point just the no-free-lunch theorem in a powdered wig, or is there a residue — something normative about which inductive bias your entitled to hold, not just which one happens to be baked into you — that learning theory is structurally unable to express? And if there is, is that a gap in my vocabulary, or was the philosophical claim less clear than it looked all along?
