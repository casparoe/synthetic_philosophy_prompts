ok so last night turned into a four hour argument with my friend dana (strawsonian compatibilist, very smug about it) and I ended up defending a package of views I'm not actually sure hangs together. before I embarrass myself at a workshop I'd like an audit. here's roughly what I committed to over the course of the night:

(1) Strawson was basically right in Freedom and Resentment that resentment, gratitude, hurt feelings etc. aren't going anywhere — no argument is going to talk humanity out of them wholesale, and they don't need a metaphysical license to be legitimate parts of human life.
(2) I also think Pereboom-style: nobody is ever morally responsible in the basic desert sense. nobody, at bottom, deserves to suffer for what they've done — determinism or indeterminism, either way the buck never stops with you.
(3) but I want to say reactive attitudes can still be fitting. resentment as an apt response to ill will directed at you, gratitude as an apt response to someone regarding you well. fittingness without desert.
(4) I'm all for keeping the practice of holding people responsible in the moral address sense — protesting, demanding justification, asking for regard. that's just what being in relationships with people IS, and I don't think my view of (2) gives me any reason to want a world of pure treatment and management.
(5) and when I imagine someone wronging me and then going fully objective on me — handling me like a case, sentencing me to therapy — I think they'd be failing me somehow, treating me like a broken thing instead of a person, and that reaction feels right to me even on reflection.

dana says (2) and (3) are flatly contradictory and that I've just renamed desert "fittingness," since if nobody ever has the property that would make resentment deserved, it can't be apt either. she also says (1) and (2) can't both be true, because if the attitudes never have the warrant they purport to have, calling them "legitimate" is a sleight of hand — ineradicable isn't the same as legitimate. my gut is that she's right about one of those two objections and wrong about the other, but I honestly can't tell which. also can't work out whether (5) is a real problem for (2) or just (1) redescribed as a feeling about my own case (which would be a bit pathetic as an argument, I know).

so go through the whole set: which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and rank them by severity rather than giving me the everything-contradicts-everything treatment. for each real tension tell me what has to give and what it costs me to give it, and if there's one cheap revision that cleans up most of the mess, say that too. don't be shy about telling me if the whole thing is just strawson minus the parts that make strawson work.
