I am a retired physicist who has spent the past two years reading ethics seriously, and I have been asked to give a short position statement on whether an advanced AI system should be built to reason as a utilitarian or as a deontologist. Before I do, I would like someone to audit my views for consistency. Please take the role of a professional moral philosopher and think carefully before answering: begin by restating my commitments in your own words to make sure you have read them as I intend, then go through the possible tensions between them, rank the genuine ones by severity, and for each tell me what the cheapest revision is and what it would cost me.

Here are the commitments:

(1) Where lives can be treated statistically, arithmetic wins. An autonomous vehicle facing certain deaths should swerve toward the one rather than the five, and the complete reason is that one is fewer than five. I do not think the doing/allowing distinction carries any weight in that case.

(2) The same system must never use an identifiable individual as a means — the organ-donor scenario, one traveler and five transplant patients — even in a fully stipulated counterfactual in which no one ever learns of it, no precedent is set, and no trust is eroded. It would still be wrong, and I want that robustness built in.

(3) My justification for building in constraints of kind (2) is nonetheless consequentialist at the level of design: a world containing many AI systems that do not calculate about identifiable lives is better than one in which they do, for reasons of trust, predictability, and precedent — exactly the way my old lab's safety rules were justified by the accidents they prevented, never by invoking rights.

(4) An AI system ought always to be able to state, in one plain sentence, the true operative reason for what it did, and that statement must be checkable by an inspector — not a rationalization assembled afterward.

(5) I am a thoroughgoing determinist and physicalist about such systems: they are designed artifacts and nothing more, and I suspect this means the whole question of what an AI should reason as is confused in some way I cannot pin down.

My own guess is that the trouble lies in (5), or perhaps in holding (3) alongside (2) as a mere matter of taste. I am not sure where the real problem is. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what I should give up — and, if one single revision would clean up more than the others, which one it is.
