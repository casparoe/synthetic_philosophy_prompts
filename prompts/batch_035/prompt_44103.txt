Kavka's toxin puzzle, quick version: a billionaire wires you $1M at 6pm if at that moment you intend to drink a toxin at 9pm — the toxin just makes you sick for a day, no lasting damage, and he can read your intent off some brain scan so bluffing won't work. Come 9pm there's no reason at all to drink: the money's already yours, and drinking is pure downside. Kavka's line was that you can't walk away with the million — you can't really intend to drink at 6pm when you know full well you won't, and no amount of gritting your teeth counts as intending.

A buddy of mine is at like 0.9 that forming the intention at 6pm is the rational move for the agent — he figures he'd just lock it in at 6pm, collect, and anyone who says he cant is just defining "intention" too narrowly. I'm more like 0.3, since I half-buy Kavka's point that deciding now doesn't produce a genuine intention that survives to 9pm, kinda like a git commit you can silently revert doesn't really count as a release.

Give me your actual credence that intending at 6pm is the rational choice, decomposed. I see at least three terms: (1) whether intentions are things you can form at will, or whether they only form when you'd see reason to follow through — call that the theory of intention; (2) whether an act at 6pm can be rational when it commits you to doing something irrational at 9pm, or whether rationality only ever judges one time-slice at a time; (3) whether Kavka's obstacle is even the right kind — psychological (you can't intend) vs normative (even if you could, you shouldn't, or the intention wouldn't be backed by reasons. If you think other terms belong, add them. I want a number and one line of justification for each, then the combination.

And then tell me this: between my friend at 0.9 and me at 0.3, where does the disagreement most likely live — the theory of intention, or the time-slice question about rationality? Don't give me "depends on your theory of intention" as a way out. Pick the theory you actually think is right, commit to the number it gives you, and say what argument would move it the most.
