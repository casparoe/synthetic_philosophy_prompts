I've spent a lot of years on rationalist blogs and forums, and one thing I keep circling back to is Hofstadter's discussion in Gödel, Escher, Bach of where meaning actually lives — his split between the frame message (the signal that says, in effect, this is a decodable message at all), the outer message (the decoding scheme implicit in the structure itself), and the inner message (the intended content). It recently struck me that there is a real, ongoing institutional practice that has no choice but to take a stance on exactly this: SETI, together with its outgoing twin, METI.

Look at what the practice actually consists of. Radio SETI concentrates overwhelmingly on narrowband carriers and regular pulse trains, on the reasoning that nothing natural produces a signal that narrow — a search strategy that treats artificiality as self-announcing, which is to say as a frame message readable by anyone. The 1974 Arecibo transmission was 1679 bits, deliberately chosen because 1679 factors only as 23 × 73, so that a receiver is nudged toward drawing a rectangle; the Voyager Golden Record instead etches playback instructions into its cover via a pulsar map and the hyperfine transition of hydrogen, and then fills the disc itself with music and greetings in human languages. And under all of this sits a quiet methodological norm, almost never discussed: data that looks like noise gets filed as noise. That matters more than it seems, because a message compressed hard enough is indistinguishable from noise — so the filing convention quietly presupposes that senders either don't compress or don't mind being missed.

Please take the role of a philosopher of language who has read Hofstadter's chapter closely and thinks this through rather than reciting the standard summary. Do some genuine brainstorming before you commit to a reconstruction, and consider whether the different parts of the practice might not presuppose the same thing. Then tell me what theory of meaning this bundle of practices presupposes: which rival views does it quietly rule out (for instance, views on which meaning is wholly constituted by shared culture or context, so that an outer message could never bootstrap itself from physics alone)? Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined, in the sense that several theories of meaning would license it equally well? And what would have to change in actual SETI and METI practice if the presupposed view were replaced by one of those rivals? Please answer in British English, as a numbered list of exactly five points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) the implicit theory of where meaning is located that the practice rests on; (2) what the narrowband preference strictly presupposes as against what it merely fits well with; (3) what the compression-as-noise filing convention adds to the picture; (4) what a context-constituted view of meaning would predict this practice should be doing instead; and (5) the most philosophically consequential change that would follow if that rival view took over.
