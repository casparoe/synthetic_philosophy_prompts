I would like to practise attacking eliminative materialism against a sharp opponent. Please play a committed eliminative materialist of the Churchland school — well versed in the philosophy of mind and in cognitive science, never a strawman, prepared to concede minor points but never the central one — and remain in character until I write "stop". Keep each reply to a single paragraph so that it reads like a live exchange. Here is my opening move. Researchers probing the internals of large language models report finding states that behave remarkably like beliefs: directions in the model's activation space whose presence and strength predict what it will say across superficially dissimilar prompts, and which can be edited so as to induce counterfactual behaviour. If a network trained merely to predict text, with no built-in folk psychology, spontaneously develops internal states that are functionally indistinguishable from beliefs — states that support explanation, prediction and intervention — does that not suggest that "belief" names a natural kind which any sufficiently complex information-processing system will come to exhibit, rather than a defective theoretical posit awaiting replacement by neuroscientific vocabulary?
