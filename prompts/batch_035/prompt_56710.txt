I've been going down a rabbit hole on the idea of making two AIs argue with each other in front of a human judge as a way of keeping them honest — reward whichever one wins the debate, and the hope is the winning strategy becomes telling the truth, even for questions way past what the judge could ever check on their own. The problem everyone points at is "obfuscated arguments": a debater can produce something that looks airtight to the judge but is actually built on a subtle lie or a rigged step, and if that kind of thing is cheaper to make than honest arguments, the debate trains a con artist rather than an expert. People argue about whether this is a solvable engineering problem or a fatal flaw, and I've stopped caring about that question and started wondering what the whole setup quietly assumes about truth. Because it seems like it needs something like "in a long enough fair fight, honesty beats deception" — that truth has a home advantage in persuasion. Is that an empirical claim that could just be false, or is it closer to a defining hope? It also seems to assume a very particular judge: someone modestly competent who can still referee fights about things they don't understand. And here's the tension that bugs me: the reason we need the debate in the first place is that we can't verify what the AI tells us, but the debate only works if the judge can eventually tell who's right — which sounds a lot like saying we CAN verify it, just indirectly. So what I want is the implicit epistemology spelled out: what view of argument and truth does debate-as-a-safety-method actually rest on, which rival views of truth-seeking would it collapse under, and is the obfuscated-argument problem a technical bug or the symptom of a presupposition that's just wrong? Please write it as a proper discussion, and then afterwards give me a JSON object with these fields: "presuppositions" (list, each with a one-line explanation), "compatible_rival_views" (views of inquiry that the practice could ALSO live with), "core_tension" (the strongest internal strain, stated in one sentence), "coherence_verdict" (coherent / strained / incoherent, plus a phrase saying why), and "what_would_change" (what a redesigned practice would have to do differently if the weakest presupposition were false).
