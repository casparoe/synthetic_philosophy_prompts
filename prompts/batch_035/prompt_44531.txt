longtime reader of the rationalist corner of the internet here (lesswrong, the slatestarcodex comment sections back in the day, etc). been chewing on free will for years, mostly settled into the "free will is an incoherent question, dissolve it and move on" camp, but then i picked up the Dennett/Caruso debate book and now im genuinely unsure whether the two sides are disagreeing about anything real or just talking past each other.

the specific thing im stuck on: Caruso (following Pereboom) says what compatibilists can't rescue is "basic desert" — deserving punishment just intrinsically because you did the bad thing, not for any forward-looking reason. Dennett's move, as best I can tell, is that basic desert was never a sensible thing to want in the first place, so who cares — we keep the "good enough" desert that does all the real work: moral education, protection, shaping behavior, expressing condemnation of choices as made by reasons-responsive agents. Caruso's counter is that this is quietly moving the goalposts, because ordinary people and the whole criminal justice system really do believe in the basic-desert version — that's why we call it the "just deserts" of punishment at all — and compatibilism, by telling everyone their belief is "mostly fine," gives cover to it. Dennett would say Caruso's model (treat dangerous criminals like carriers of a disease, quarantine with no blame) can't even explain why we apologize to people, or why we resent the guy who wronged us and not the rock that fell on us.

so: write a dialogue between a Dennettian compatibilist and a Caruso-style free will skeptic who are both advising a state sentencing commission. the concrete question on the table: the commission has to decide whether to strip retributive language ("the defendant deserves punishment proportionate to his offense") out of the sentencing guidelines and replace it with purely forward-looking language (deterrence, incapacitation, rehabilitation), and there's one case in front of them as a test — a repeat violent offender with a documented frontal lobe lesion that plausibly impaired impulse control.

requirements:

- both characters should be genuinely sharp. no strawmanning. the skeptic is not a smug "neuroscience disproves free will" guy and the compatibilist is not doing handwaving about how it's all semantics
- they should actually respond to each other's points. specifically i want them to hit (a) whether basic desert is a strawman or the actual thing people believe, (b) whether the compatibilist's "good enough" desert gives cover to retributivism in practice, (c) whether the resentment/apology point actually does work for the compatibilist, and (d) whether the brain lesion case is genuinely different in kind or just different in degree from every other criminal's causal history (this is the part I keep flip-flopping on — if the lesion guy gets the quarantine model, why doesn't everyone, and if he doesn't, what exactly did the lesion change?)
- ideally the dialogue ends with the two of them locating the precise premise they disagree about, rather than one of them winning. I want to walk away knowing what the crux is, because right now I can't tell if I became a compatibilist at some point without noticing or if the whole dispute is verbal

moderate length is fine, like 800-1200 words of dialogue. doesn't need scene setting beyond the commission room, don't waste words on that.
