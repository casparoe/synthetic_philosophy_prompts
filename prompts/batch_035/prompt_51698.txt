I keep flip-flopping on who should actually get to vote in municipal elections, and I want to know if there's a principle under my gut verdicts or whether I'm just making it up case by case. Sixteen-year-old with a job paying income tax: yes. Sixteen-year-old with no job: no. Tourist in town for two weeks: obviously no. Foreign grad student on a student visa, been here five years, plans to leave after graduating: I lean yes. Same person on a work visa who intends to stay permanently: yes, clearly. Someone who moved abroad three years ago but still owns a house here and pays property tax: I'm genuinely stuck. Permanent resident, 30 years in the country, never naturalized because the fees and paperwork were a pain: yes. Someone who never naturalized out of a kind of ideological refusal to swear the oath: also yes, but that one bugs me, because someone who could easily naturalize and just never bothered for 20 years: no. Incarcerated felons: yes. People with advanced dementia: I honestly don't know, and I can't tell if that uncertainty is telling me something. Is there one principle here — affected interests, being subjected to the laws, stake in the community's future, whatever — that captures most of this, and if so what does it say about the property-owner-abroad and the ideological refuser, the two I keep circling? If nothing captures all of it, tell me which of my verdicts has to go and why that one rather than the others.
