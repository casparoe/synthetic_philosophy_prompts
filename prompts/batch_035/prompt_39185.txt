I'm on the appeals panel of our national advertising self-regulatory body, and we have a case that's split us down the middle. It's basically about Grice, though nobody on the panel has put it that way yet.

A bank sent out a mailer pushing its new Growth Account, an investment product where principal is at risk. The mailer's headline reads: Since 1904, no customer of ours has ever lost a penny. Every word of that is true — deposits at this bank have always been safe, and federally insured besides. But the Growth Account is not a deposit, it can lose money, and that fact is disclosed only in 8-point type on the reverse side. The complainant argues that readers walk away thinking money at this bank is safe, including in the very product being advertised. The bank's counsel replies that every statement in the mailer is true, the risk disclosure exists, and our code prohibits misleading advertising — you can't hang "misleading" on a sentence that is true.

Please draft the decision memo I'll circulate before the hearing. It should recommend upholding the complaint, dismissing it, or something conditional, with reasoning the bank's counsel could actually respond to. I want the memo to take seriously the idea that what a speaker means is not just what is literally said — the bank chose that sentence because of what readers would infer from it — but it should also give an honest shake to the other side's strongest worry: if we start policing impressions rather than claims, where does that end, and doesn't the fine print on the back count as cancelling the false inference anyway? A concrete recommendation on what the bank must do going forward would help too.
