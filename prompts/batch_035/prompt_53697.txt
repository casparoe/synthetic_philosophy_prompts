Two historians have spent the past year in a formal adversarial collaboration, and you have been asked to draft the joint statement they should produce.

The first — call her the singularity historian — holds that the Holocaust is categorically distinct from every other genocide: a program of total annihilation pursued as an ideological end in itself, directed at a people defined solely by descent, carried out through modern bureaucratic and industrial means, and continued even when it demonstrably harmed the perpetrators' own war effort, so that no utilitarian residue explains it. The second — call him the comparability historian — holds that the Holocaust is one member of a class of modern genocides: continuous with earlier colonial exterminatory practice (he stresses the Herero and Nama war), enabled by the same state capacities, racial science, and administrative rationality that other mass killings drew on, and best understood, in the spirit of Bauman's *Modernity and the Holocaust*, as a product of ordinary modern processes rather than a rupture in Western civilization. Both are committed to repudiating the misuse of comparison seen in the Historikerstreit, where the demand for causal context slid into exculpation, and both want the final document to say exactly where comparison ends and excuse begins.

Write the statement. It should contain:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims — including what both accept about the evidential record, about what comparison can and cannot establish, and about the difference between explanatory comparison and moral equivalence.
2. A disagreements table with four columns: Disagreement | Formulation the singularity historian accepts for her own view | Formulation the comparability historian accepts for his own view | Underlying crux. Each formulation must be worded so that its own side would actually sign it — neither vacuous nor slanted.
3. A cruxes section: for each crux, one concrete argument, case, or comparison both sides agree would bear on it (candidates they have floated include: the continued deportations of 1944, when transport and labor were militarily scarce, as a test of the "end in itself" claim; the Herero precedent as a test of whether the relevant capacities and rationales predate the Holocaust; and the counterfactual of the same ideological goals without modern administrative machinery, as a test of Bauman's modernity thesis), with each side's prediction written down in advance.
4. A closing section identifying which of their disputes turned out to be verbal — for instance, whether "unique" and "unprecedented" are being used to make the same claim or different ones — and which are genuine.

After the statement, provide a brief structured summary as a JSON object with the following schema: "agreed_claims_count" (integer), "genuine_cruxes" (array of objects, each with "crux", "singularity_prediction", "comparability_prediction"), "verbal_disputes" (array of strings, each explaining what the verbal residue is), and "residual_disagreement_in_one_sentence" (string).
