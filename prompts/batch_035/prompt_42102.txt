I'm a high school junior who has spent the last two years deep in the replica sneaker community, and I'm fluent in its vocabulary: reps, UA ("unauthorized authentic"), 1:1 copies, batches and batch ratings, QC pics, legit checks (LC), "retail" versus "resale," grails, factory flaws, and the endless arguments about whether a given pair comes from the same production line as retail pairs. My philosophy background is much weaker — one elective semester and some half-remembered fragments — but I've gathered that there's a long-running debate about forgeries and originals in the art world, and I'd like that debate translated into the vocabulary I actually use. Assume I know every piece of sneaker and rep slang but only fragments of philosophy; paraphrase the philosophers freely rather than quoting anyone, since I wouldn't recognize a quote anyway.

Here's the debate as I understand it, roughly. One side holds that if a fake is indistinguishable from the real thing under inspection, then everything you can see or touch or experience is the same, so any remaining difference in value must come from somewhere else — scarcity, history, or plain snobbery — and not from the object itself. The other side, which I associate with a philosopher named Dutton (I could be wrong), says an artwork is like an athletic feat: the object is a record of how it was made, the way a race time is a record of the runner's training, so even a molecule-for-molecule duplicate is a different performance, and it makes sense to value it differently for that reason. Please restate both sides in the terms of my world. What is the counterpart of "the hand of the artist" when the artist is a production line of anonymous workers? Is the UA "same factory, same materials" claim the sneaker version of the perfect forgery? Does authentication by a third-party tag (the way StockX or a legit checker certifies a pair) play the role that provenance plays for paintings? Where does "the story" — the creases, the receipt kept in the box, the memory of paying resale for a grail — fit, if anywhere?

Then give me the honest residue report, which is the part I actually care about. What translates cleanly, what gets distorted to fit, and what can't be said in my vocabulary at all? Two spots I already suspect are trouble: first, a painting is one-of-one while a retail sneaker is one of several hundred thousand "originals," so it's not obvious my community even has a concept that maps onto "an original" — maybe "authentic" just means "blessed by the brand," which is a legal and market fact, not an artistic one. Second, it's tempting to translate "authenticity" as "whatever passes a legit check," but that smuggles in our community's own assumptions and calls them the philosophers' view, which I gather is exactly what a translation isn't allowed to do. At the end, tell me whether the leftover material means my framework is missing something — for instance, that what philosophers call the value of the original is, in sneakers, mostly status economics we've dressed up as reverence — or whether it means the philosophers were theorizing about unique handmade objects all along, and their claims were never supposed to stretch to mass-produced multiples in the first place. I want to know which of the two frameworks blinks.
