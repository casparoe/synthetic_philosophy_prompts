my dad died in march and my friend sam, who did a metaphysics degree, has been trying (i think) to console me about it. the consolation is that dad still exists -- past things are just as real as present things, they're just not *here-now*. eternalism, he calls it. honestly it worked on me for about a week. then he wrote the argument down and now i cant tell if im comforted or on the hook for believing in sherlock holmes.

the argument:

(1) Dad doesn't exist. that's what dying is.
(2) I miss dad. missing someone is a two-place relation -- me and him.
(3) relations need relata. you cant stand in a relation to nothing; if im related to him, there's a him to be related to.
(4) so dad exists -- and sam is careful here, no tensed verbs smuggled in, not "existed," plain exists.
(5) so (1) is false as i stated it. i was wrong to say he doesnt exist, full stop.

and here's the part that spooked me: the argument doesnt stop at dad. i can think about napoleon, so by the same steps napoleon exists. i can dread my dentist appointment next thursday, so it exists. i can think about sherlock holmes, and about the largest prime, and about the leak in my kitchen ceiling, and apparently all of those exist too. when i pushed him on this sam just shrugged and said fine, the thinkable and the existent were never supposed to come apart. i think he said something about a jungle at some point, meinong's jungle maybe, and seemed ok living there.

i want out but i honestly can't see which step is the bad one. (1) sounds like just the brute fact of it, that's the whole thing i'm grieving. (2) is about as undeniable as anything gets. (3) sounds like boring logic-class stuff, relations need relata, the kind of thing you'd have to keep to do any work at all. and yet chain them up and you get every thinkable thing existing, which is supposed to be the reductio of reductios.

so which step do i reject, and what am i paying for it? if the fix is something like "exists" quietly changing its job between (1) and (4), i want to know why that move is allowed for whoever gets to make it and not for every crank who wants their pet objects to exist -- sam will say exactly that if i bring it up. also, be straight with me: does the comfort survive at all, or was the comfort only ever the conclusion and never anything the argument could actually get you to?
