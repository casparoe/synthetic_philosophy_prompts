I took up ant-keeping a few months ago (long story, it started as a birthday present for my nephew and now the colony lives with me), and it's got me weirdly preoccupied with a question I can't shake. At first I just watched the ants as individuals, but after a while I started feeling like the interesting thing isn't any single ant — it's the colony. When I moved their food source, the old trail evaporated and a new one appeared within hours, like the whole thing got re-planned. When I gave them two nest options, scouts checked out both and then the entire population gradually funneled into one of them — and it wasn't the queen deciding, as far as I can tell she just sits there laying eggs, nobody gives orders, each worker just follows simple rules and pheromone signals.

My brother says I'm basically seeing shapes in clouds: the "colony" is just a label I put on a bunch of individually dumb bugs, and the pattern is in my head, not in the world. But that doesn't feel right either. When I look at any one ant, it genuinely seems clueless — but something is solving problems, and it doesn't seem to be located in any ant. So my question is: is there a principled way to decide whether the thing doing the deciding is the colony and not the ants? Is this the same kind of question as asking whether it's my brain that thinks rather than my neurons, or is that analogy doing more work than it should?

Related thing I keep turning over: I ran across an old book excerpt where an anteater has a conversation with an ant colony as if it were a person — I think the colony was called something like Aunt Hillary, a pun on something. I assumed it was pure whimsy at first, but now I wonder if it's actually making a serious point about levels of description. And a nastier thought that follows: if the colony-level story is legitimate, is it just descriptive shorthand, or could there be a real sense in which the colony knows things no ant knows — and if shorthand can carry information around like that, where's the line between a metaphor and a mind? My brother also asked whether it matters morally — like if the colony is the real organism, should I be more careful about disrupting it than I would be about, say, relocating a single bug? I honestly don't know where to start with that one.
