Hey! I'm TA-ing a philosophy of language course, and for our unit on Austin and performative utterances I want to give the students something more fun than another essay prompt. My idea: a deadpan corporate FAQ / terms-of-service page for a fictional startup (call it something like "Felicity Solutions" or pick a funnier name yourself) that sells third-party certification of felicity conditions. You pay a subscription, and their compliance officers show up to verify that the officiant actually has the authority to pronounce you married, that the christening procedure is executed both correctly and completely, that the beaker actually touches the ship's hull before the champagne count runs out, that the person saying "I bet you five bucks" is not under duress, etc.

You're a technical writer with a PhD in ordinary language philosophy who has gone into industry -- write the piece so the humor comes from getting the theory exactly right, not from tech-startup clichés. A few things I'd love to see worked in, though you should feel free to ignore me: a warranty clause making very clear that the company guarantees the illocutionary act only, and perlocutionary effects (persuading, deterring, scaring the burglar off) are the customer's own responsibility; a disputes section about wether an insincere apology or promise counts as void or merely "procedurally fine but abused"; some tiered pricing scheme that quietly tracks the difference between misfires and abuses; and at least one customer testimonial from a very confused sports umpire whose calls keep getting voided for lack of authority. Maybe a line about how they can't help you if your performative is embedded in a poem or a soliloquy, since stage utterances are explicitly exempt.

It should be readable to someone who has never read a word of Austin, but someone who knows the speech act literature should be nodding along instead of wincing. About 700 words, formatted like an actual FAQ with numbered sections. Before you write the final thing, please do a quick brainstorm of other felicity-condition jokes you could include (say, 4-6 candidates) and then pick the best ones -- I want to see that you're choosing jokes deliberately rather than just riffing. Thanks!
