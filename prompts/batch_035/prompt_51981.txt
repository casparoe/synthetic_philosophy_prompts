I'm a longtime Dennett guy — Darwin's Dangerous Idea is basically my sacred text, or anti-sacred text I guess lol — and I've read enough LessWrong over the years that unexplained explainers and mysterious answers to mysterious questions now bother me the way a flickering light bothers other people. Right now I'm doing a reread of His Dark Materials (finally got my partner into it after we finished the HBO show), and I cant stop thinking the whole trilogy is secretly a Dennett book. The Authority turns out to be, not the creator, but the first angel to condense out of Dust — so God himself is a crane pretending to be a skyhook, a contingent product of the very process he claimed to author. And the whole arc is about toppling the skyhook and replacing it with the Republic of Heaven, which as I understand Pullman's interviews is his way of saying meaning has to be built from the bottom up, right where you live, no celestial hierarchy holding it in place.

But the deeper into the reread I get, the more slippery it feels, and thats what I want an actual reading of, not the fan wiki version. Is Dust itself a residual skyhook? It's self-aware particles that existed before the world but concentrate around conscious beings, and as far as I can tell Pullman never gives you a crane account of where it comes from — which is exactly the move we'd dunk on in any other context, a magical-sounding explainer that just relocates the mystery one level up. And then prophecy seems to cut the other way entirely: the witches foretell everything, Lyra is a new Eve, and I half remember something about her being destined to bring an end to destiny, which if true is at least Pullman winking at the problem. So the dramaturgy runs on skyhooks (fate, design, a plot that was foretold) while the theme is busy demolishing them. Is that a deliberate tension, or did the story simply not notice?

So, read the trilogy through the cranes vs. skyhooks lens, with greedy reductionism in the toolkit as well. Where does Pullman genuinely earn a crane account — the Authority's death, which is just an old being coming apart in the wind once nobody's holding him together; the mulefa with their wheel-pod trees and their view of Dust as something that flows and can leak; the whole anti-clerical insistence that everything, angels included, is material and contingent — and where does he quietly reinstall a skyhook (Dust itself, the alethiometer functioning as an oracle that is always right)? Does greedy reductionism even apply here — is there a spot where the books explain something too fast by skipping levels, or is that concept about theories and just doesn't transfer to novels? Please be honest about where the books resist the Dennett reading rather than contorting them to fit, and tell me whether the trilogy ends up adding anything to the cranes/skyhooks debate that a philosophy essay couldn't.
