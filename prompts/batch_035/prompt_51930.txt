I teach a bit of political philosophy on the side, and writing up my own views for a seminar next term I've realised I can't tell whether they form a stable set or just a pile. I'm somewhere in the luck egalitarian camp. The package, as best I can state it:

(1) Inequalities that trace back to things nobody chose — the birth lottery, native talent, getting ill — are injustices, and the state ought to correct them.

(2) If you take a risk with your eyes open and lose — the gambler who's broke, the climber who needs a rescue — it's not unjust that you're worse off, and you've got no claim on the rest of us as a matter of justice.

(3) But I dont accept responsibility-sensitive healthcare. The lifelong smoker with lung cancer gets treated on need exactly like anyone else, and I find schemes that deprioritise or surcharge patients for lifestyle definately wrong, not just inefficient.

(4) I'm committed to real equality of opportunity for children, with the state doing a lot of the work — schools, early intervention, the lot.

(5) Yet parents going all out for their own kids — reading to them, paying for music lessons, leaving them money — strikes me as not merely permitted but good, and I'd resist the inheritance taxation or anything else that tried to neutralise it.

(6) And, relatedly, I'm comfortable saying a brilliant surgeon deserves her income in the everyday sense, even though talent itself is brute luck.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the literature: which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest fix in each case — then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad. And since I'll be defending this in front of people who will push back: for each verdict, say what would change your mind — which argument or consideration would flip it — and point out where your own reasoning here is most likely to go wrong. I'd much rather be told a tension is shallow and be shown why than have everything flagged as suspicious.
