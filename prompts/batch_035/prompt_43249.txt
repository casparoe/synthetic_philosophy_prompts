Is there a name for this? I studied physics, then spent thirty years in an engineering job, and now that I'm retired I'm working my way through philosophy of science for fun — Popper, Kuhn, and lately Lakatos on research programmes. Here's a pattern I keep bumping into and can't find a term for. A theory gets in trouble, and someone adds a patch: a hypothesis whose only job is to absorb the anomaly, which everyone at the time dismisses as ad hoc. Then the theory dies anyway, but the patch survives, and in the succesor theory it's no longer a patch at all — it's the load-bearing part. The two cases I keep thinking about are Lorentz and Fitzgerald's contraction, invented to save the ether from the Michelson-Morley result and widely treated as the textbook ad hoc rescue, only for the Lorentz transformations to end up sitting at the very center of special relativity; and Einstein's cosmological constant, thrown in to keep the universe static, later called his biggest blunder, which then came back as the standard home for dark energy — from fudge factor to central mystery. Put in Lakatos's own vocabulary, it's as if a protective-belt hypothesis gets promoted to hard core when the programme is replaced: the thing that looked like a symptom of degeneration turns out to have been the seed of the next programme.

Does anyone in the literature discuss this promotion explicitly, and does it have a name? I can find Lakatos on degenerating problemshifts and on one programme superseding another, but nothing on the fate of individual belt hypotheses across the succession. It seems to mess with the whole progressive/degenerating judgment, because it suggests that wether a patch was merely ad hoc can't be fully assessed at the time — the same hypothesis can look degenerating inside one programme and like the beginning of the next one. Or is that just Kuhn's point about hindsight dressed up in new clothes, with nothing genuinely new in it? If there's no established concept, then please coin one: define it precisely, say what it's not (an ordinary auxiliary hypothesis that survives a minor revision, or a simple case of one theory absorbing another's content), and give me a couple of boundary cases where it's genuinely unclear wether the promotion happened. Don't just hand me a label and stop — I want to know where the edges of the phenomenon are. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my question back at me.
