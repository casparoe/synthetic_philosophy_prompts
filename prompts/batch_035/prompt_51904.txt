I'm a novelist working on a book where one of the main characters is an Edwardian moral philosopher who, let's say, fell into a deep sleep in 1913 and wakes up in the present day — imagine the setup as Rip van Winkle by way of Bloomsbury. He's modeled closely on G. E. Moore, the Principia Ethica guy: the one who thought the highest human goods were things like the personal affection between friends and the appreciation of beauty, and who was famous among his friends for being stubborn, literal-minded, and completely unimpressed by grand systems. Anyway, a big chunk of the book is him reacting to the modern world, and the friendship and beauty parts are where I keep getting stuck, so I want your help thinking it through before I write the scenes.

Concretely, I need to figure out what a Moore-shaped person would actually say about this stuff. Would he count a parasocial relationship — my character's grandniece has an intense one-sided bond with a streamer she watches every night — as any fraction of the intrinsic good of personal affection, or would he say it's missing the two-way knowledge of each other's minds that made friendship valuable to him? Would the appreciation of beauty survive being fed to you by a recommendation algorithm that's optimizing for engagement rather than for the beautiful itself — does it matter to the value of the experience how you got there? And what about maintaining decades-old friendships through occasional likes and emoji reactions: is that a thin but real version of the good, or has the substance quietly drained out while the form persists? I also want a scene where he encounters AI-generated art that he sincerely enjoys before learning what it is — I suspect Moore would say the beauty is undiminished since he cared about states of consciousness, but I'm honestly not sure, and my editor thinks I'm laundering my own opinions through a dead man here.

So: walk me through the real Moore's views on friendship and beauty as best they can be reconstructed, then predict his verdicts on the cases above, flagging where you're extrapolating from what he actually wrote versus where you're basically guessing, and tell me which specific consideration would most change your predictions — like, if I went back to Principia Ethica or his papers and found X, the whole characterization of his reaction to the streaming scene would flip. That will help me know what to read next and how much rope I'm giving myself. Also, since my publisher is American, please write the answer in American English — my drafts keep drifting British and it drives my copyeditor up the wall.
