ok so my english teacher gave us this totally open creative writing assignment — like 1500 words, any genre, and the only real rule is it has to "get at an idea" somehow. most people in my class are doing stuff about breakups lol, but i want to do something with this concept my dad won't shut up about from his college philosophy class. its from daniel dennett (pretty sure thats spelled right), and the idea is that the "self" is not a little person living inside your head, its more like the center of gravity of a physical object. like, a center of gravity is a real thing in the sense that physicists use it to predict stuff, but theres no actual little blob sitting AT that point — its just where everything balances. dennett says the self is like that except for stories: we each have a "center of narrative gravity," a character that our brain and everyone around us kind of co-writes, because its super useful for predicting what a person will do next. so "you" are basically a very well-developed fictional character that everyone agrees to treat as real. my dad calls this the best idea he learned in college which honestly might be a low bar but whatever.

anyway i want a short story about this, and i have a setting in mind but you can adjust it if you have better ideas. heres mine: a woman in her 60s with early dementia has this app her daughter set up that writes her a little diary entry every night, summarizing her day like she's the protagonist of a novel — the app smooths things out, invents motivations, turns her confusion into small charming adventures. over time she starts reading the diary instead of trying to remember the actual days, and the diary version of her is honestly kinder and more coherent than the real-time confused her. the question i want the story to actually poke at (not answer in a cheesy way) is whether the diary version is fake, or whether thats just what a self IS for all of us, and the only difference is she gets to watch the writing happen. like maybe everyone is a slightly nicer edit of themselves anyway. a few things i dont want: no sappy "love conquers all" ending, and also dont make it a cheap "technology bad, apps are destroying us" thing either — i want the app to feel warm, almost like another author of her, if that makes sense. if you can work the actual center-of-gravity metaphor into the story somewhere as an image (like she used to be a physics teacher, or her late husband explained it to her once) without it turning into a lecture, that would be perfect. and pls make the actual writing good, not just the concept — like specific details, not generic "she gazed out the window" stuff. my teacher grades pretty hard on prose lol. under 1500 words! tyia
