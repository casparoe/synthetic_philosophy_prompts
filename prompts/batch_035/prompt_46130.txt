I've been really into Dennett's real patterns stuff since reading about the intentional stance a while back, and somewhere along the way I converted to a pretty strong version of it. My view now is roughly: the question "what really exists" bottoms out in whatever patterns are robust enough to support prediction and intervention at some level of description, and no level gets to be the official one where the real stuff lives. So beliefs and desires are real the way genes are real, centers of gravity are real, the thing my thermostat doesn't have that my cat does is real -- and the microphysics is just one more pattern bookkeeping, not the ground floor. Real patterns all the way up and all the way down, with the design and intentional stances as just higher shelves in the same store. My flatmate thinks this is logical positivism wearing a fun hat and keeps saying "useful for prediction isn't the same as existing," which honestly I find the least interesting objection, but it does make me want to actually stress test my view rather than just enjoy it.

So: what would count against it? I want a map of the view's exposure, not a defense of it. Which observations, arguments, or cases would genuinely embarrass it -- like, is there a possible discovery about how prediction works (say, if it turned out intentional-level predictions were always convertible into mechanical-level ones without loss, so the intentional pattern buys you literally nothing once you have the full microstate) that would make the "real" in real patterns do no work? Are there structural objections that it can't answer -- e.g. that it has to either collapse into straight instrumentalism (patterns are tools, not inhabitants of the world) or quietly re-import a mind-independent notion of pattern that does all the real work, with nothing stable in between? Is the view even risky, or has it been qualified into unfalsifiability -- and if it's unfalsifiable, is that a defect for a view of this kind, since it's arguably supposed to be metaphysics-with-teeth rather than just a slogan about tolerance for abstraction? Please distinguish what committed defenders of the view would themselves concede as counting against it from what only critics claim, and rank the defeaters from fatal through merely awkward. Also, if some of the exposure is shared by basically every deflationary ontology and not specific to real patterns, say so -- I want to know which bullets I personally have to bite, not just which bullets exist.
