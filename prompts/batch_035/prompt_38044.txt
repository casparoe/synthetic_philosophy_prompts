I got into a huge argument with my brother-in-law over thanksgiving (he's a personal trainer, 43). he takes prescribed testosterone for "low T" and feels totally fine about it, but he's furious that Olympic sprinters might use the same hormone, and he also thinks my cousin's kid shouldn't get growth hormone just because the kid's healthy but projected to be 5'3". meanwhile my mom's on statins, i drink coffee like water during exam season, my friend takes beta-blockers before violin auditions because otherwise her hands shake (she says stage fright is technically a diagnosis?), and my sister got laser eye surgery not because she was going blind but because -8 diopters was annoying and she was sick of contacts. every person I ask where the actual "treatment" line is gives me a different answer, and I'm starting to suspect they're all just describing whatever their insurance already pays for, backwards.

so before I argue about any of the policy stuff (what insurance should cover, what sports leagues should ban, whether parents get to pick their kid's height), I want the real spec first. write me the list of requirements any principled therapy/enhancement line has to meet: which cases it has to sort the way everyone reflexively sorts them (statins for a sick 65-year-old; vaccines, which fix nothing you have and add nothing you lack, so where do they even go; daily aspirin; the altitude tents distance runners sleep in versus injecting EPO, when both just raise your red blood cell count; cochlear implants, given that a big part of the Deaf community says deafness isn't a disease at all so an implant can't be a "cure"; hormone treatment for menopause, which is about as natural as it gets and which I definately want available); which distinctions it has to respect (between the state of your body and the act of changing it; between "back to average" and "good for you" — the average person used to have no fillings and died around 45; between what your body is doing and what your job demands, which is where the beta-blocker case really bites); and which moves it can't be allowed to make (can't just be "whatever a doctor prescribes," that's circular; can't let the verdict flip when the population average drifts — kids today are taller than their grandparents were, did grandpa's vitamins become enhancement retroactively?; can't assume natural equals good while also wanting menopause and appendicitis treated).

for each requirement, say what case or worry forces it, and be honest about which ones probably can't be satisfied at the same time — I have a feeling the "don't move with the averages" requirement is already in a fight with the "normal species functioning is the baseline" one, which I gather is roughly what that bioethics guy Daniels goes with. if some subset of the list is jointly unsatisfiable, say that outright, that's the part I most want to see. then at the very end, one short paragraph each on how three contenders do against your list: the normal-functioning people, the transhumanists who think the whole distinction is fake, and the "it's just subnormal part-function, period" crowd. but keep the list the main event — the scorecard is for arguing with my brother-in-law, not the other way around.
