Last week I actually signed the paperwork to get cryopreserved when I die (yes, really — Alcor membership, funded by a life insurance policy, my family thinks I've lost it), and I know for a fact that Friday dinner with my best friend Elena is going to turn into round fifteen of our ongoing personal identity argument. She's read Parfit's Reasons and Persons twice, so I can't win on charisma alone. I want to walk in knowing what she'll say. Her track record so far, from years of arguments: the classic teletransporter (scanned on Earth, destroyed, replica wakes up on Mars) is death, full stop, the Mars guy is just a copy; but replacing your neurons with functionally identical silicon chips one at a time over a decade is survival, "it's still you the whole way through"; anesthesia every night is obviously survival; her grandfather had open heart surgery where they cooled him down until his brain was flatlined for like 45 minutes and then restarted it, and she has zero hesitation that that's still him; total permanent amnesia is survival too, "same brain, just wiped"; and when I asked about a machine that scans you and makes two copies while destroying the original she said neither copy is you — "that's dying twice."

So, acting as a professional philosopher of personal identity, please predict her verdicts on my two new cases before I have to hear them live. Case one is the actual cryonics scenario: legally dead, perfused, vitrified in liquid nitrogen, stored for 80 years, then the same brain gets rewarmed, repaired, and rebooted (grant that the tech works). Case two is the neuron replacement she already approved of, except the silicon replicas of my neurons sit in a server across the country and talk to my skull over a wireless link, so by the end there's nothing left in my head and I'm entirely running in the server. Before you predict anything, brainstorm the candidate rules she could be running that each fit her whole track record — I want the actual list, because I suspect more than one rule fits — and say what each one predicts for both cases. Also flag any rule that seems natural but is secretly excluded by one of her verdicts (like, is there a reading of "same brain" she is definately NOT using?).

Then give me your best guess with actual reasons, and tell me what further case I could spring on her that would best split the remaining candidate rules apart. Think carefully and be concrete — she will smell handwaving from across the table.
