Take the division of Korea in 1945 and analyze it as a natural experiment on whether institutions, geography, or culture explain why some nations are rich. The standard story is seductive: one peninsula, one language, centuries of shared political history, so geography and culture were held fixed while institutions diverged — and the result is the gap you can see from orbit in nighttime satellite imagery. I want the careful version, and then an honest account of where the model breaks.

Start with the mapping, done properly. What exactly is the treatment: the line itself, drawn in about half an hour in August 1945 by two American colonels with a National Geographic map and no expertise on Korea; the regimes that consolidated on either side of it; or both at once? What is the baseline population — keeping in mind that the border did not seal immediately, and roughly a million northerners, many of them Christians, landowners, and others with reasons to fear the northern regime, had moved south before the war began in 1950, so the two populations were being sorted by the very regime difference the case is supposed to test? And how fixed were the fixed variables, given that Japanese colonial development had concentrated heavy industry, mining, and hydroelectric capacity in the north while the south remained largely agricultural — so that an endowment-based read actually predicts the north wins, which cuts in the institutions theorist's favor? Tell me whether that last point is fair or merely seductive.

Then the breakdown: the war's asymmetric destruction, the asymmetric cold-war patrons, the fact that we have one peninsula rather than many, and the deeper worry that history's natural experiments are not sampled at random but collected by researchers who already find institutions interesting. Finally, what does the episode establish — the institutions-first thesis as a general account, the weaker claim that institutions matter somewhere, or something more unsettling about what single cases, even excellent ones, can show?
