My band's saddest song is by far the most popular thing we've ever written and its been bugging me for weeks. I've been reading about the paradox of tragedy, and my credence is around 0.75 that the right story is some version of "we enjoy being moved, and being moved by tragedy is itself a positive experience" -- i.e. the pleasure is a distinct thing that co-occurs with the sadness, not the sadness converted into something good. My songwriting partner thinks that's cheating: on his view, any account where the enjoyment comes from something other than the sad content concedes the paradox instead of solving it. He'd put maybe 0.3 on it.

Give me your own number, but decomposed: (a) the probability that audiences even feel genuine negative affect at tragic art, as opposed to some flattened quasi-emotion or imaginings that don't hurt the way real loss does; (b) the probability that the enjoyable part is a distinct co-occurrent state -- feeling moved, catharsis, pleasure at our own responsiveness, appreciation of the craft -- rather than the sadness itself being somehow valuable in its own right; (c) whether "we enjoy X" even picks out a single attitude here, so that the question is well-formed in the first place; plus any other terms you think belong. Combine them however you think is right and tell me which term you're least confident in and what would move it. And separately: is my partner right that co-occurrence stories dodge the question rather than answer it, or is insisting the pleasure come from the sadness itself just refusing to take the empirical answer seriously?
