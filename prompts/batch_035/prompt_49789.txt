I help run a legal-philosophy reading group, and we've just finished several weeks on the Hart–Devlin debate over legal moralism: Devlin's claim that a society needs a shared public morality to hold together and may use the law to defend it, and Hart's rejoinders that nothing shows society actually disintegrates from private immorality, that the "reasonable man" standard he leans on is really just the majority's discomfort wearing a costume, and that the Wolfenden-style line between public enforcement and private life was worth keeping. For our closing session I want to assign a piece of satire instead of another discussion paper — something that rewards the members who did the reading rather than those who only know "moralism" sounds stuffy.

Please write a welcome packet, roughly 650 words, from the "Consensus Commons" homeowners' association — a residential community run on rigorously Devlinian lines. It should have two parts: a welcoming letter from the Board Chair, a retired judge who is perfectly sincere and never winks, and a selection of bylaws clauses in deadpan officialese. The comedy must come from getting Devlin right rather than caricaturing him: the neighborhood as a community held together by an invisible bond of shared moral sentiment, such that any single deviation — however private, however harmless to anyone's property values — is treated as a crack in a load-bearing wall; a "reasonable resident" standard that purports to channel what any decent composite of the community would feel (disgust, indignation, intolerance — you may seed those words into a clause defining violations) while actually laundering whatever the current majority happens to feel; and a treatment of privacy as a bounded amenity which, like the tennis courts, must itself be kept in good moral repair to remain open. Include at least one clause establishing an enforcement body (a Conscience Committee, say), one clause permitting moral eccentricity in homeopathic doses as a kind of seasoning that proves the community's robustness, and one moment where the packet almost draws a distinction between public conduct and private life and then quietly declines to honor it.

End the packet with an appendix: a letter from a homeowner — Ms. H. of Number 42 — raising exactly the objections Hart raised, namely that no one has ever shown a community actually disintegrating from private nonconformity, that the "reasonable resident" is a composite of nobody real, and that majority comfort is doing all the argumentative work while being dressed up as social necessity. Her letter must be answered with warm appreciation, formally thanked for its valuable contribution to our shared conversation, and then definitively outvoted, filed, and forgotten. She should be granted full procedural rights throughout — that's part of the joke. A reader who knows the debate should be able to reconstruct Hart's entire side of it purely from what the Board so politely brushes aside; a reader who doesn't should still find the packet funny as bureaucratic overreach, with the horror of the disintegration logic only landing at the end, when the welcome packet's cheerful tone makes clear that the next letter from Ms. H. will be handled exactly the same way, forever.
