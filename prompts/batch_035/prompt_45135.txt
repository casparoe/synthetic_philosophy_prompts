I'm a PhD student in bioethics, and my funding package has me serving as graduate observer on the coverage committee of a large teaching hospital. Part of the committee's remit is deciding which drug requests count as treatment for the purposes of our internal medication subsidy fund — in effect, it rules on the therapy/enhancement distinction case by case, and after two years of minutes I can mostly predict its verdicts. Two cases come up next week, and I want your read before I place a friendly bet with the other observers.

The track record so far:

1. Growth hormone for an eight-year-old with confirmed pituitary growth hormone deficiency — ruled therapy.
2. Growth hormone for a healthy ten-year-old at the 15th percentile for height, with parents of average height — ruled enhancement.
3. Modafinil for a 29-year-old with laboratory-confirmed narcolepsy — ruled therapy.
4. Sildenafil for a healthy 72-year-old with age-related erectile dysfunction and no other identified pathology — ruled therapy.
5. Methylphenidate for a healthy graduate student who wanted steadier focus for qualifying exams — ruled enhancement.
6. Propranolol for a classically trained violinist with normal cardiovascular function who wanted steadier hands at auditions — ruled enhancement.

The two new cases: (a) a 34-year-old with subsyndromal depression — several weeks of low mood, disrupted sleep, and marked impairment at work, but formally below the diagnostic threshold — whose psychiatrist requests a subsidized antidepressant trial; (b) a 41-year-old actuary with a formal adult ADHD diagnosis, currently performing within the normal range but well below his own level at 28, requesting subsidized stimulant medication to recover his earlier performance.

What I want from you: identify the candidate rules that each fit the six past rulings (at minimum, I can see a diagnosis-gate rule, a normal-functioning rule, and a medical-need rule in play — and I suspect the normal-functioning rule itself forks between statistical typicality for one's age and restoration of species-typical functioning, a fork the sildenafil ruling may or may not settle), say what each rule predicts for the two new cases, and then commit. Don't hand me a menu: give your single best guess at the committee's ruling in each case, name the rule you think actually drives it and why you prefer that reading of the precedents, and identify the closest analog for each. Then tell me what further case, if it came before the committee, would best discriminate between the rules you take seriously — and whether you'd bet with me or against me.
