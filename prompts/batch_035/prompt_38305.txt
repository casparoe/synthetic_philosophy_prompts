For some years now my friend Amy has been handing down verdicts about which works are genuinely beautiful, and she has quite a track record. A hugely popular, technically accomplished blockbuster film she calls skillful but not beautiful, whatever the crowds think. A small watercolor by a forgotten amateur, probably seen by fewer than a hundred people in her lifetime, she declares genuinely beautiful, though she cannot point to a single other person who has noticed. The pop song she has loved since childhood she judges not actually beautiful — her affection, she insists, is doing all the work, since being moved is not the measure. On one occasion she ranked a piece she finds frankly boring above one that entrances her, calling her boredom a defect in her rather than in the work. And when a composition dismissed for a century was finally championed by a famous performer and the world came around, she said it had been beautiful all along — the century of neglect was simply collective error. Her justifications, every single time, run in terms of formal features: proportion, economy, the resolution of tension.

Next week she and I are meeting to discuss a newly authenticated orchestral score. It was written for an instrument that produces only overtones above the range of human hearing, so no human ear could ever hear it as anything at all; but the score and the spectrographic analyses show that it instantiates, to an unusual degree, every formal property she has ever cited in praising a work. Predict her verdict. I am less interested in the yes or the no than in the rule she seems to be running. Beauty as an intrinsic formal property of works fits her record — but so does something like the view that beauty is whatever ideal, practiced, unprejudiced judges would settle on, and possibly other rules besides. Do those rules agree on the inaudible symphony? If not, which rule should I expect her to be running when she gets there, and on what grounds? And which further case — one her record so far leaves untouched — would best tell the candidate rules apart?
