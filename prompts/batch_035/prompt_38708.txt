Our Chinese philosophy reading group has spent basically a whole year on the Warring States Confucians and we've split into a Mencius faction and a Xunzi faction who argue at every single meeting. We've decided the mature thing to do is write it up like an adversarial collaboration instead of re-litigating the same points over tea, and I got volunteered to draft it. So: write the joint statement both factions could actually put their names to.

Here's where we are, so you know what needs to be in it. Both sides agree self-cultivation matters and that becoming a decent person is slow, hard work; the fights are about what's there at the start. The Mencius people read the four sprouts passages and the child-near-the-well case as showing everyone has incipient moral responses that just need nourishing and extending, and they love the Ox Mountain parable -- the bare hill tells you nothing about what the hill would grow if it were left alone. The Xunzi people say you can't get from weak incipient tendencies to actual virtue without the scaffolding -- ritual, teachers, the standards -- that Mencius downplays, and that desires left to themselves won't order themselves, hence the crooked wood needing the marking line and the steamer to bend it. We've also started to suspect we talk past each other on what xing even refers to: whatever is inborn and spontaneous, versus the direction development takes when things go well plus whatever doesn't have to be forced. One member of the group claims that if we fix the wording, the substantive leftover is partly an empirical claim about early proto-moral responses, which we can't settle in the reading group, but we can at least write down in advance what each side expects the evidence to show.

So the format I need: a numbered section of claims both factions could sign (cultivation, the insufficiency of appetite left alone, the role of family and ritual, whatever else is genuinely shared), then a disagreements table with each row phrased so its own side would accept the wording, then the cruxes -- we suspect one is whether the sprouts are genuine motivational tendencies or mere capacities that need something from outside to become active, another is whether ritual works by reshaping desire or only by policing it, and maybe one about whether our visceral aversion at seeing suffering is evidence for the sprouts or is fully consistent with Xunzi. For each crux, name the kind of case or evidence both sides would agree bears on it (toddler helping and sharing studies, what happens to moral development when social scaffolding is thin or absent, whether ritual practice changes what people enjoy vs merely what they do, that sort of thing) and record each side's prediction. And since the whole point of the exercise is honesty: tell us plainly which parts of the fight you think would dissolve once the wording of xing is pinned down, and which parts are genuinely about the world. Don't try to adjudicate who's right -- we'll do that ourselves and probably keep arguing anyway, but at least we'll be arguing about something.
