Anaesthesia departments monitor for intraoperative awareness in three ways at once, and I want to know what theory of consciousness that practice, taken as a bundle, presupposes.

The first is definitional. Awareness during surgery is established after the fact: patients are asked structured questions in recovery and afterwards — whether they remember anything between going under and waking up, whether they heard voices, felt pain, or tried to move — and an episode counts as awareness with recall only if the patient can report remembering it. The incidence is estimated at one or two per thousand general anaesthetics, and the retrospective interview is treated as the gold standard: no report, no episode.

The second is machine monitoring. Processed-EEG devices (the bispectral index monitor is the standard example) reduce the frontal EEG to a single dimensionless number on a scale running roughly from 0 to 100, and the anaesthetist titrates drugs to hold the patient in the band indicating adequately deep anaesthesia. The operative assumption is that consciousness has a neural signature readable from the scalp, from the outside, with no role for report at all — indeed in patients who, being paralysed, could not report.

The third is the isolated forearm technique, used mostly in research. Before the muscle relaxant is injected, a cuff on the upper arm is inflated, so that when paralysis sets in everywhere else, that one arm remains mobile. The anaesthetist then periodically asks, during the operation, for the patient to squeeze their fingers if they can hear. Some patients squeeze appropriately — at that moment they are perceiving a spoken command, understanding it, and acting on it — yet report remembering nothing afterwards.

What I want is the implicit theory of this bundle. Work through each component. What does defining awareness by retrospective recall strictly require — a higher-order view on which a state is conscious only when available to thought or report, or merely something weaker, such as access plus a memory-forming capacity that the anaesthetic can abolish independently of consciousness? What does the EEG monitor presuppose — that consciousness is a first-order neural condition that can obtain in a patient who will never report anything, or only the much weaker claim that it correlates with a measurable signature well enough for clinical use? And the isolated forearm technique: does treating a command-following squeeze as evidence of consciousness commit the practice to anything about the relation between responsiveness and awareness, or is the squeeze precisely not consciousness by the practice's own lights, since the same departments do not count these episodes as awareness at all?

Then say whether the components presuppose consistent things or whether the bundle quietly takes a side. In particular, consider the patient who squeezes on command mid-operation and remembers nothing: was that awareness? Say what a first-order view implies (the patient was conscious, and the recall-based definition undercounts, possibly badly); what a higher-order view implies (either the patient's states were not conscious in the relevant sense, or consciousness without reportability is possible and the higher-order theorist owes an account of which memory-independent route to the higher-order state survived the anaesthetic); and what the practice itself implies by counting such episodes as neither awareness (no recall) nor plain absence of awareness (the squeeze happened). Distinguish what each practice strictly presupposes from what it merely sits comfortably with, and say where the bundle is underdetermined — is there a single theory of consciousness under which all three components come out right, or several? Finish with the counterfactual: what would actually have to change in monitoring practice if one view won over the other — would the EEG targets move, would the interview be supplemented or replaced, would departments have to start counting and following up the patients who respond under the cuff — or is there a reading on which the current bundle is an accidental truce between theories rather than an inconsistent one?
