Quick context: I work as a data analyst for a mid-sized city government, and over the last few years Ive collected a folder of cases that all smell the same, and I'm trying to figure out whether social scientists or philosophers of science already have a proper taxonomy for them, or whether I'll have to invent one myself. You seem well-read on that end, so please brainstorm broadly first — list the candidate concepts you can think of before committing to an answer — and don't just hand me the first label that comes to mind.

Here's the pattern. An institution starts measuring something as a stand-in for a thing it cares about but can't observe directly. Then the measure becomes a target and people optimize it. That headline version has a famous name, I know that one. But when I line up my cases, the ways it goes wrong look really different from each other, and I can't find words for the differences. Case 1: a police department quietly reclassifies burglaries as "lost property" so the serious-crime number stays down. The amount of crime out there doesnt change one bit — only the report is now fiction, and worse than useless, because even the historical trend line is lying now. Case 2: an airline is judged on on-time departures, where departure counts as the moment the plane leaves the gate, so planes push back right on schedule and then sit on the tarmac for forty minutes. Nobody is lying — the planes really do leave the gate on time — but once the pressure arrived, the whole system reorganized around the letter of the measure instead of its spirit. The number got better, the thing people actually cared about (passengers getting where they're going) got worse. Case 3, my favorite weird one: a school drills test prep so hard that scores shoot up, but a year later the kids genuinely know the material — the drilling, aimed only at the number, accidentally did the job the number was standing in for. So it was "gamed," and the goal got achieved anyway, and I honestly dont know whether that even counts as the same phenomenon.

So three questions. (a) Does the existing literature on indicators, evaluation, and measurement have names that cut my three cases apart — case 1 corrupting the report while reality stands still, case 2 reality reorganizing around the letter of the measure while the number stays honest, case 3 gaming that collides back with the goal? If the nearest concepts fit only imperfectly, tell me exactly where the fit fails; I'd rather get the edges of each concept than one confident label. (b) If nothing established carves it this way, coin terms — give definitions and say what boundary cases would test them. (c) One more thing I keep chewing on: in case 2, where exactly did the meaning of the measure go? It's like the referent slid from "passenger journey quality" over to "gate event" while the same words stayed printed on the dashboard. Is there a name for that quiet migration of a metric's meaning, as distinct from the gaming itself? Please think step by step, be willing to say the literature has no clean answer, and skip the generic lecture on perverse incentives — I'm past that.
