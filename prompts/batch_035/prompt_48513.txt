A friend and I spent last night arguing about voting reform, and I came away suspecting my own position doesn't hang together. Before I write it up, I'd like an audit of the package. Here is roughly what I maintain:

(1) The Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem shows that every voting system that offers voters a real choice among three or more candidates can be manipulated, so manipulability is not a fixable design flaw but a mathematical fact of life.
(2) Approval voting is my preferred reform, because marking every candidate you find acceptable is an honest act, unlike being forced to pretend a candidate you merely tolerate is your second choice rather than your third.
(3) Manipulability is nevertheless disqualifying in itself: a system that rewards voters for misrepresenting their views institutionalizes lying, and no other virtue of the system can compensate for that.
(4) Under our current plurality system, voters are sometimes obligated to vote strategically — abandoning their favorite for a lesser evil — since throwing the election to the greater evil just to keep one's hands clean is moral vanity.
(5) Condorcet methods, which elect the candidate who beats every other candidate head-to-head, are the gold standard for democratic legitimacy.

Tell me which pairs of these are in genuine tension and how severe each tension is, which ones only look like tensions, and what single revision would clean up the most at the least cost. Please take a definite position on where I've gone wrong rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose.
