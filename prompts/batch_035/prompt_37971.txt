I'm a novelist, about halfway through a book set in a mid-size industrial town where the council has been fighting for two years about a bronze statue of a Victorian mill owner out front of the library -- philanthropist, builder of the workers' cottages, and by modern standards a man who made his money off things we'd now call exploitation. I keep going to council meetings "for research" and honestly the debates are a mess: someone says it's about honour, someone else says nobody even notices the statue, someone compares removing it to the Soviets airbrushing people out of photos, and a woman from the local history society wants a plaque added instead and swears that solves it. I don't want my novel to just be people shouting past each other, I want the shouting to have actual joints in it, if that makes sense.

So before I write the big set-piece argument chapter, I want the theory-side spec: what does any adequate ethics of commemoration (statues, street names, building names, the lot) have to deliver? Like, the desiderata. What cases must it get right -- I'm thinking of the obvious ones (Confederate monuments, Columbus) but also the ones nobody agrees how to classify: a bridge named after an engineer whose technical achievement was genuinely great and whose morals were standard for his day, a street named after a man whose name was actually put there by a clerical error and no one remembers who he was, a hospital wing that exists because a robber baron's widow endowed it. Which distinctions can't it collapse -- I suspect "honouring someone" and "recording that they existed" are different, and also maybe "what the statue meant when it went up" versus "what it means now", and maybe "who chose it" matters too (a community erecting its own hero vs. a regime imposing one)? Does it have to give a verdict on all of those or can some be non-cases? And then the harder bit: are any of the requirements jointly unsatisfiable? My hunch is that "commemoration is about honour, not history" and "commemorative choices can't be rerun every generation without something bad happening" pull against each other, but I've never seen it stated properly.

Also please tell me which single requirement knocks out the most popular positions (the "keep it and add a plaque" camp, the "remove everything tainted" camp, the "context decides" camp) -- that part I want to get right because I'm giving one of my characters the best version of each position and I don't want any of them to be a strawman.

One practical thing: write it in British English please (my editor is in London and I'm not doing the spelling conversions myself again). And if you use tables or lists that's fine, whatever's clearest. No need for reading lists or citations, I just want the actual structure of the problem -- the plaque lady has been haunting me and I need to know if she's right.
