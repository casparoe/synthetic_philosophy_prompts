So I recently got elected to my condo board (rookie mistake, I know) and we're in the middle of this standoff with the developer who still owns six unsold units in our building. He wants us to sign off on a bunch of amenity changes that would basically let him market the building differently, and our only real leverage is that our bylaws give the board veto power he'd have to fight in court to overturn. A lawsuit would be brutal for everyone — it'd drain our reserve fund, tank sales prices while it drags on, and his units would sit unsold. Mutually assured destruction basically.

Here's what's eating me. One board member, Dana, says we should put it to a formal vote NOW, instruct the lawyer to file the moment he moves, and send him the minutes — that way he knows we can't back off even if we cool down later. She says a threat you can walk back isn't really a threat at all. Another member says that's insane, the whole thing is theater on both sides and we all know nobody's actually going to burn the building down over a gym reno, so we should just quietly negotiate and settle.

And then there's Max, who's been on the board forever and is, let's say, not a calm man. He genuinely seems to WANT the lawsuit. He's sent the developer unhinged emails at 1am. And weirdly, the developer's people seem way more nervous about Max than about anything the rest of us do. Dana now wants to basically hand Max the keys to the negotiation because his unpredictability is "our best asset," which honestly gives me the creeps.

I keep going back and forth on whether Dana's committed-threat plan is smart or self-destructive — if it works we never actually go to court, but the only way it works is if we'd really go to court, and would we? And whether parking Max out front is using a crazy person as a weapon or just good tactics, and whether there's even a difference. Is it rational to set up a situation where we'd have to do something irrational? Am I overthinking a condo dispute or is there something real here?
