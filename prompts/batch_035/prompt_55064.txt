Write a deadpan customer-support exchange (about 600 words, formatted as a ticket thread) between Swampman — the guy who popped into existence, memories and all, when lightning struck a swamp, with no evolutionary history and no causal past — and an agent at the Original Function Warranty Co., whose policies are written by a hard-line selected-effect teleosemanticist. Since content and function only come from selection history, the agent has to keep denying, in ever-more-polite corporate-ese, that Swampman's beliefs are *about* anything, that his eyes are "for" seeing, or that his heart "malfunctioned" when it stopped — a malfunction presupposes a function to fail at, and he never had one. But the agent can't verify he isn't just an ordinary customer with a gap in his paperwork, and that gap is where the comedy lives. Include at least one escalation to a supervisor that makes things worse, and one moment where Swampman lands a genuine objection the agent can only dodge — ideally something like him pointing out that the policy document itself only has a function because of its production history, and so does his head, so whats the relevant difference. The jokes have to come from the actual commitments of etiological teleosemantics — the swampman intuition, states that are indistinguishable from contentful ones but aren't about anything, the idea that misrepresentation requires a prior function to deviate from — not from generic corporate humor. And keep it deadpan throughout: the agent never realizes the policy is insane, that's the joke.
