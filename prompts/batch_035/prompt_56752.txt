I'm on the board of a small community foundation in the town where I grew up (pop. around 40,000). Some background, and I still find this hard to talk about even now: from the sixties through the late nineties we had a children's home on the north hill where a lot of the kids were badly abused by staff. It came out in 2004, a few people were prosecuted, two walked on technicalities, and one housefather took a plea deal that amounted to probation — he still lives on the same street as a couple of the men he hurt. Everybody knows, nobody talks about it. Now the town has gotten some state money for a memorial and a community archive, and our foundation has to recommend to the council whether to run a truth-and-reconciliation style process — victims and former staff invited to give public or private testimony, everything documented, an official report in about two years.

Here's what the board is actually split on, and its the part I can't stop chewing on. The retired detective who kept the files says there's one case from 1997 that could maybe still be prosecuted, involving two former staff members. The process designer says those two will only ever talk if their testimony can't be used against them, and without them the record has holes right in the middle of it. Some of the survivors have told me flat out they want to see one of these men in a courtroom before they die; others say a trial would just re-traumatize everyone and probably end in nothing anyway. I keep coming back to how South Africa did it — you got amnesty, but only if you showed up in person and told the whole truth, publicly, and it didn't cover things done for private gain. Something about that conditional part seems important to me but I can't put my finger on why exactly, and I've never studied any of this formally so I might be getting it wrong or missing better options entirely.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate before our next board meeting. It needs a clear bottom line — endorse the process, reject it, or endorse it with conditions, and if conditions, spell them out — and it has to treat the "they're owed a trial" side as more than just a feeling, even though honestly that's where I lean. It should also flag what the foundation can actually decide versus what belongs to the DA and the town council, because I suspect half our arguments lately are about things that aren't even in our hands.
