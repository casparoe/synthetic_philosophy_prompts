Last term our club captain, Dara, was telling us this and said obviously it's still the same boat, "Falcon" is "Falcon", end of story. My friend Priya went nope: at some point you've just built a new boat in the same slot, the way she sees it continuity of repairs is not identity, it's just slow construction. Dara said that's absurd — if a repair can't destroy identity then a thousand repairs can't either, there's no magic plank where it flips. Priya said that's the sorites fallacy or whatever, like "no single grain makes a heap" doesn't mean heaps don't exist.

Dara said fine, but then you'd have TWO boats and at most one can be Falcon, and it's clearly the racing one, because Falcon is the thing that never stopped being Falcon — the club, the results board, the oaths newbies take, all of that history attaches to the hull that kept racing, not to a pile of wood sitting in a shed. Priya said hold on, if you rebuild the old one, THAT one is made of literally the original matter in the original arrangement, and if matter and arrangement don't make it Falcon, then what is your "same boat" even made of besides sentiment? Priya rolled her eyes and said this proves the whole question is just about how we feel like using the word "Falcon," there's no fact hiding under the label, and Dara said that can't be right because the club genuinely could not race the shed-boat as Falcon without doing something wrong, which shows the word isn't free.
