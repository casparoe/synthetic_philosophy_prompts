I sit on the finance committee of a mid-sized city, and we must decide whether to accept a proposed local community currency as payment for certain municipal fees (library fines, recreation programs). The currency would be issued by a nonprofit residents' association as a digital token, nominally pegged to the national currency but not redeemable for it, spendable only at participating local businesses, and subject to a monthly demurrage charge that steadily shrinks holders' balances to force circulation. Proponents argue that money is nothing over and above what a community treats as money, so our acceptance would itself help constitute it as money, with local economic benefits. Two of my colleagues disagree in different ways: one holds that money is essentially a creature of state authority, so a city accepting a private token is a category error; the other grants that money is socially constructed but argues that construction has succeeded historically only when the token was insulated from the issuing body's discretion, which the demurrage policy blatantly violates. Please draft the short decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should recommend a concrete course of action (accept, reject, or pilot with specified conditions), engage the strongest point on the losing side rather than dismissing it, and be candid about the extent to which whatever we decide will itself be partly self-fulfilling.
