I run a value theory seminar for undergrads (14 students, 75 minutes, I don't want to lecture that day). Design an in-class activity that makes them feel the difference between being *indifferent* between two options and there being no common currency for comparing them at all — the thing people gesture at with "incommensurable values."

Rough shape I have in mind, but improve it if you have better ideas: a series of pairwise choices between options that pull on genuinely different values (career stuff works — money vs. autonomy vs. meaningfulness — but make the cases concrete and a little messy, not a noble option vs. a crass one), and then a forced "pricing" round where students have to state actual numbers for what it would take to move them off each option. The payoff I'm hoping for is that they discover two things at once: they can't produce a consistent exchange rate across their own choices, AND a small improvement on one side doesn't flip them the way it would if they'd really been indifferent all along. That gap — not a tie, but no answer — is the whole point, and I want them to run into it themselves before anyone names it.

So I need: the actual cases, mechanics for how they record choices and prices, some way their inconsistency becomes visible to *them* without me pointing it out (pairing them up? having them predict each other?), and a debrief that maps the moments onto the distinction. Hold back terms like "parity" or "small improvement argument" until the debrief at the earliest — they should hit the raw phenomenon first. Also be honest about what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it; I'd bet at least a few students will happily price everything down to the dollar and think they've refuted the premise, so tell me what to do with them.

Act as a veteran workshop facilitator who's run this kind of session a hundred times and knows the difference between an activity that produces the phenomenon and one that just produces compliance — think through how each round actually plays out before you write the rules. Keep it under 800 words, I want the design, not an essay about it.
