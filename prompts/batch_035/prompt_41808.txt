I spent most of my career in laser physics, retired a few years ago, and have now fallen properly down the Maxwell's demon rabbit hole, and I want a diagnosis of something that bugs me about the whole debate, not another physics lecture.

Here's the standoff as I see it. Szilard's one-molecule engine back in 1929 seemed to show you can extract work from a single heat bath if you have a device that measures which half of the box the molecule's in. For a while the standard answer was that the measurement itself must cost entropy (Brillouin's line, roughly). Then in the early 80s Bennett argued no, you can measure reversibly — the real bill comes due when you erase the demon's memory, which is Landauer's principle: erasing one bit costs at least kT ln 2 of dissipated heat. Case closed, everyone celebrates, "information is physical" gets printed on t-shirts.

Except some people never accepted the exorcism. Their objection, as I understand it, is that it's circular: Landauer's principle is itself derived from the second law, or at least from statistical-mechanical machinery that already encodes it, so invoking it to save the second law assumes the thing you set out to defend. The other camp says no, it's independently grounded — it falls out of the fluctuation theorems, and you can check it in the lab with colloidal particles in optical tweezers. My little reading group (three retirees, two years of coffee and arguing) has not moved an inch, and neither has the literature as far as I can tell.

My working hypothesis: the two camps aren't really disagreeing about the physics, they're disagreeing about what an exorcism owes you. One side wants the second law restored as a theorem from premises that don't presuppose any thermodynamics, the other side just wants a good argument that no demon can actually be built, and each thinks the other's standard either begs the question or quietly changes the subject. Is that right? Or is there a hidden substantive question doing the work — something about whether "erasure" is even well-defined for a memory that's not in equilibrium, or about what physically counts as the demon's memory in the first place, or about whether the demon was ever supposed to be a possible device rather than an argument about the second law's status? Diagnose the disagreement from what the sides actually claim, tell me whether it's substantive, verbal, or two questions under one name, and then be honest about what would count as settling it: a cleverer derivation, an experiment, or nothing — and if nothing, why nothing.
