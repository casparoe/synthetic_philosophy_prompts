Suppose Epictetus could observe the present-day revival of the philosophy associated with his name. He would encounter, among other things: psychotherapists openly acknowledging that cognitive behavioral therapy descends from Stoic doctrine, on the theory that emotional disturbance tracks our judgments about events rather than the events themselves; a mass-market self-help industry that compresses his teaching into the injunction to focus only on what one can control; corporate wellness and resilience programs that train employees in equanimity toward restructuring, layoffs, and stagnant wages; an online subculture that markets Stoicism as a hard, masculine discipline of self-mastery; academic philosophers arguing that his famous dichotomy of control is really a trichotomy, since much of life — one's health, reputation, even one's own actions — lies partly within our power and partly without; and critics, political and therapeutic alike, who charge the whole tradition with quietism: teaching the powerless to reclassify injustice as indifferent rather than fight it.

For each of these developments, say whether you think he would regard it as a faithful extension of his teaching, a corruption of it, or something genuinely ambiguous — and defend the verdict from his actual commitments rather than from the generic reputation of Stoicism. I am especially interested in the harder cases. Consider corporate resilience training: his doctrine that externals are indifferent to happiness appears to vindicate the training's content, while his insistence that philosophy exists to make people better, not more useful to an employer, appears to condemn its purpose. Which consideration would carry more weight with him, and why? On the quietism charge: how would a man who had been a slave, and who taught that we should perform well whatever social role falls to us while never treating its outward success as good, distinguish between interior equanimity within an unjust order and complicity with that order? Is there anything he could say to the modern critic that the critic would not expect to hear?

Where your extrapolation is speculative, flag it as such, and keep two kinds of worry distinct throughout: cases in which the modern development simply misreads him, and cases in which it follows him faithfully to a conclusion we now find troubling. Please do not treat him as either a saint or a straw man; where you believe his own position contains a real internal tension, name it.
