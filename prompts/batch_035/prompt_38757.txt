I'm on the board of a family foundation that gives out roughly $10M a year, and the board has calcified into two factions: the humanitarians, who want everything going to malaria nets and deworming, and the animal-welfare people, who want it all going to corporate cage-free campaigns — and each side privately thinks the other's grants are close to worthless. Every December we fight and land on an exhausted 50/50 split that nobody thinks is actually good. Last meeting, one of the animal-side members floated making an explicit "moral trade" instead: her side votes the full $10M to the humanitarians' top-ranked health program this cycle, and in exchange the humanitarians vote the full amount to a big multi-year cage-free commitment next cycle — plus, on the personal side, the animal folks each pledge a real chunk of their own money to malaria prevention and the humanitarians pledge to cut chicken from their diets. I have to write the recommendation memo the board will vote on. Draft it — accept the trade, reject it, or accept a modified version — and take seriously the objection that keeps coming up: that this isn't trade at all, just logrolling, because if you genuinely believe the other cause is worthless you're agreeing to burn half the foundation's money on nothing, so neither side should rationally sign. The memo should say what makes the deal rational or irrational for each side given their own values, and it has to deal with the fact that the personal pledges aren't enforceable the way the votes are.
