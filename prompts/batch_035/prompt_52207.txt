I'm in a reading group that spent this term on metaontology — Chalmers on verbal disputes, Hirsch on quantifier variance, that neighborhood — and I walked in last week with an argument that the whole ontology game, and possibly more, is just people talking past each other. Everyone hated it, nobody could say exactly where it breaks, and now I can't tell whether I've found something or just embarrassed myself, so I'm bringing it here.

(1) A dispute between A and B is merely verbal iff, when A's and B's words are properly interpreted, both of their claims can be true. (2) The proper interpretation of a speaker's words is whichever eligible interpretation makes her claim come out true — charity says we read people so they're right when we can. (3) The mereologist says "there are tables"; the nihilist says "there are no tables, just atoms arranged table-wise." Each claim is true under some eligible reading of "there are": one whose domain includes composite objects, one whose domain doesn't. (4) So the tables dispute is merely verbal. (5) By parity, every ontological dispute is merely verbal — numbers, God, moral facts, you name it — since for each side theres an eligible reading on which it's right. (6) The dispute about whether the tables dispute is verbal has the same shape: the deflationist's "it's verbal" is true under her terms, her opponent's "it's substantive" is true under his, so that one's verbal too. (7) So nobody ever really disagrees about ontology, including about whether they really disagree about ontology.

The tail end of (7) is what gives me the sweats, but honestly every step looked fine to me when I wrote them down. My hunch is that the trouble is around (2) — that "properly interpreted" is doing all the work, and charity shouldn't get to decide what people are fighting about — but I cant make that precise without it sounding like I'm just insisting that they ARE disagreeing, which is the very thing in question. So: which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? If the answer is that I have to bite the bullet on (7), I'd at least like to know what bullet-sized hole that leaves in the rest of my views.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning — no preamble, and don't restate my argument back at me.
