Like, my aversion to eating insects could be a considered stance or just disgust sensitivity misfiring, and the standard tools philosophers offer for seperating the two (what you'd endorse after reflection, what coheres with your other commitments) seem kinda circular — reflection might just be the bias talking more slowly. Now say someone builds an app that scans your moral reactions and flags which ones look "biased" — tracking them to framing effects, order effects, disgust sensitivity, whatever — and offers to quietly correct the flagged ones. What are the most important implications of the preference/bias distinction for whether I should trust and use a thing like that? I care about which implications would actually change what I do day-to-day versus which are overrated, and whether the distinction even survives the app scenario.
