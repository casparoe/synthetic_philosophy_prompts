The essay question was: Explain the symbol grounding problem — what it is, how it relates to the Chinese Room argument, and whether contemporary large language models face it. Then discuss one proposed solution.

The student wrote:

The symbol grounding problem, introduced by Stevan Harnad, asks how the symbols in a formal system can come to mean anything at all, rather than merely being manipulated according to rules. Harnad illustrated the problem with his Chinese Room thought experiment: a person in a room follows rules for matching Chinese characters to other characters, and the resulting input-output behavior is indistinguishable from that of a fluent speaker, yet nothing in the room understands Chinese. This shows that symbol-to-symbol connections alone never touch the world: looking a word up in a dictionary only yields more words, and chasing that chain forever still leaves all of it ungrounded. Harnad argued that any system that could pass the Turing test would thereby have grounded symbols, since verbal behavior indistinguishable from a competent speaker's guarantees genuine understanding. His proposed solution was a hybrid: ground an elementary core of symbols directly in sensorimotor categories through a robot's interactions with the world (bottom-up), and then build the rest of the vocabulary out of combinations of that grounded core (top-down).

Do large language models face the problem? No, and here is why: their training text was produced by humans whose words were grounded, so the grounding present in that data is inherited by the model's representations. A model's use of the word red traces back, through the corpus, to people who saw red. One might object that inherited grounding is not real grounding, but human children also acquire most of their vocabulary from language rather than from direct experience, so if that counts as grounding for us it should count for them. In the end, the symbol grounding problem is the hard problem of consciousness under another name: whether symbols mean something just is whether there is something it is like to use them.
