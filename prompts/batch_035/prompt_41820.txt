write the joint statement i need from them. so:

1. a numbered section of claims BOTH would sign — about what actually went wrong in the crisis (publication bias, tiny samples, the garden of forking paths, undisclosed analyst degrees of freedom), about what p-values were never meant to do, about what preregistration can and can't fix, that kind of thing. i want them to have to find real common ground, not platitudes.

2. a disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated so that its own side would accept the wording. e.g. on optional stopping (bayesian peeks at the data every 20 subjects and thinks it's fine, frequentist thinks it inflates type I error and that's cheating), on whether a successful replication is about the parameter or about the decision, on bayes factors vs error control, on what "the data support H1" even means. phrase each row so neither of them would balk at how their position is described.

3. the cruxes — the sub-questions where settling one would actually move someone — and for each crux, a concrete case, simulation, or analysis both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. like, for the optional stopping one: simulate a null world, have a researcher peek after every n=20 up to some cap, both sides say what they predict happens (to the type I error rate, to the distribution of bayes factors, to which inferences get made), and both agree the simulation counts as evidence about SOMETHING even if they'll fight about what it shows. do that move for each crux.

4. finally, tell me honestly if any part of their dispute is purely verbal — e.g. whether they just mean different things by "evidence," "reliable method," or "calibrated" — and if so which part and why.

keep it in the weeds (likelihood principle, jeffreys-lindley, sequential looks, severity, whatever is load-bearing) but remember the audience is grad students who know r but not measure theory. and no reading lists or citations, just the actual arguments — i'll get yelled at if i ask them to produce a bibliography they can't verify. also feel free to flag places where you suspect my two labmates would actually converge more than they realize. that'd be very satisfying for me personally lol
