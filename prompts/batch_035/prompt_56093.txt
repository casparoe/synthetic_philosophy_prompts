Here's the argument as I understand it. Mental events supervene on physical events: fix the physical state and the mental state is fixed too, there's no mental difference without a physical difference. Now suppose a mental event M causes some later physical event P*. The physical base of M, call it M*, is by itself causally sufficient for P*. So either M is doing no causal work (M* excludes it), or every case of mental causation is a case of systematic overdetermination, where two complete causes each independently produce the same effect. Since nobody accepts the overdetermination horn, mental causation looks doomed unless you identify the mental with the physical, which is just reductionism. And I gather there's a whole industry of responses -- some people bite bullets, some tinker with the laws, some say the causation is "different levels" somehow.
