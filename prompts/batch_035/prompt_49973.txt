so i have to write up my "philosophical profile" for a math reading group and before i embarass myself in front of everyone I want a sanity check on whether this package actually fits together:

1. I'm on board with the indispensability argument: quantification over numbers and functions is baked into our best physics, and I can't believe the physics while sneering at the math it's stated in.
2. I'm a Quinean naturalist — no first philosophy, we just believe whatever our best overall theory commits us to, and revisions are driven by how the whole package fares, not by armchair reflection.
3. Mathematical truths are necessary and knowable a priori. No experiment could ever force us to revise a theorem.
4. Abstract objects, if they exist, have no causal powers — nothing you do to the number 7 changes anything anywhere.
5. Given 4, I honestly don't see how anyone could know anything about numbers. Benacerraf's challenge seems right to me and I don't think anyone has really answered it.
6. When I prove something myself, the phenomenology is discovery, not invention — that prime was already there, finding it wasn't a choice. I take that seriously as evidence.
7. I'm a pluralist about set theory: CH has no determinate answer, there are just different universes satisfying different axioms, all equally good.

which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix for each? Rank the problems by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. For what its worth I don't have a strong sense of which one is the troublemaker, so don't go easy on any of them.
