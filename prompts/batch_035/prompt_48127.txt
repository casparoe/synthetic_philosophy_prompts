I write a science-and-ideas column for a Sunday magazine, and my editor greenlit a piece on the paradox of phenomenal judgment — the puzzle that if epiphenomenalism is true and qualia cause nothing physical, then my sincere report that I'm seeing red can't actually be caused by the redness, so how do the reports end up accurate anyway? Fine, but every explainer on the internet runs the same zombie-twin beat, and I refuse to do that again. So: construct a fresh thought experiment to carry the column, with a twist — the case should leave the epiphenomenalist in BETTER shape than the interactionist dualist, the reverse of the usual direction. The verdict has to fall out of the theories rather than be stipulated: no asserted perfect correlations, no demons, no brain implants, and the setting should be somewhere readers recognize (a sleep lab, an ICU, a cockpit) followable by someone who's never heard the word qualia. After the scenario — keep it to one paragraph — walk through what each theory predicts and why, tell me which feature of the case is doing the work, and how an interactionist would counterattack, since I'll have to give them their say in the column anyway.
