Consider the Judy Benjamin problem in formal epistemology, which I will state in full so that we share the same setup before I ask for your credence.

A soldier, Judy Benjamin, is dropped by parachute into a swamp divided into three territories — Red Army territory, Blue Army territory, and Yellow Army territory — and, knowing nothing further, she assigns each a prior of one-third. Within Blue territory, she initially considers it equally likely that her own forces or the Red Army holds the ground. She then receives a radio message, which she takes to be fully reliable, telling her exactly one thing: if she is in Blue territory, then the probability is 0.75 that it is occupied by the Red Army. She learns nothing directly about Red or Yellow territory. The question is what her posterior distribution should be over the three territories (and, within Blue, the two occupation states).

Two candidate answers have some pull. On one, the message is entirely about what is the case within Blue territory, so the probabilities of Red and Yellow territory should remain untouched at one-third each, with the Blue mass redistributed 3:1 between enemy-held and friendly-held ground. On another, the rational update is the one that departs minimally from her prior in the sense of minimizing relative entropy subject to the new constraint; this update, which Bas van Fraassen introduced in 1981 as a challenge for exactly this method, shifts the probability of Red territory off its prior — a consequence van Fraassen regarded as a defect, since the message says nothing about whether she is in Blue territory at all.

Here is my question: what is your credence that the correct normative answer leaves the probability of Red territory exactly at one-third? I do not want a survey of positions or an "it depends on your update rule" — I want a number, and the decomposition of the reasoning that produces it. At minimum, please assign a rough probability to each of the following sub-questions, with a one-line justification, and add any further terms you think belong:

1. That the content of the message is exhausted by the constraint P(enemy-held | Blue) = 0.75 — i.e., that the probability of an indicative conditional just is the corresponding conditional probability, so nothing is lost in representing what she learned as that single numerical constraint.
2. That, given the constraint, the posterior is underdetermined — the constraint by itself plainly does not fix the distribution — and that among the many distributions satisfying it, some single one is rationally required.
3. That if some one update is required, the privileged one is a minimal-change principle of the relative-entropy type, rather than one that preserves the marginal probabilities of the hypotheses the message did not mention.
4. That the shift in the probability of Red territory under the entropy-minimizing update is a genuine defect rather than a legitimate reflection of the message's evidential import.
5. That there is, in the end, a fact of the matter about the "correct" posterior here at all, as opposed to a wide range of permissible responses.

Also consider a limiting case I find diagnostic, and say where it fits in your decomposition: if the informant had instead said that, conditional on being in Blue territory, the Red Army holds it with certainty, the update would arguably reduce to Jeffrey conditioning on the Blue cells, which leaves Red and Yellow untouched at one-third. Does the correct treatment of the 0.75 case need to approach that limiting case continuously as the stated probability rises toward 1, and should that constrain your answer?

Finally, combine your terms into a single final credence, identify the one sub-question your number is most sensitive to, and tell me what argument or result would move that term — and hence your overall credence — the most.
