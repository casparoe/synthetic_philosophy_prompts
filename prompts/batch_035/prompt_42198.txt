I'm a journalist who covers science and medicine for a general readership, and the story I keep circling back to is the line our institutions draw between treating illness and improving the healthy. Consider the collection of rules and reimbursements that governs this. In elite sport, the anti-doping code bans a substance when it enhances performance, yet the same system grants therapeutic use exemptions: an athlete with diagnosed asthma may use a bronchodilator that would earn anyone else a suspension, on the ground that it merely restores what illness took away. But an athlete born with unusually low haemoglobin may not take EPO to lift himself to the field average, while a rival born with an unusually high haematocrit competes with it unimpeded — nobody proposes levelling him down. On the civilian side, health services generally fund growth hormone for a child with a certified deficiency but baulk at funding it for a short child with no diagnosable condition; they will pay for a cochlear implant but not for a drug that sharpens memory in a typical student; and antidepressants are prescribed for clinical depression while being increasingly sought, off-label, by people who are sad but functioning and want to be better than fine. What theory of health, and of the therapy/enhancement distinction, does this pattern presuppose? Does it presuppose that there is a fact of the matter about normal species functioning which treatment restores, and if so, is the relevant baseline statistical, evolutionary, or relative to the individual's own constitution? Could the practice be justified without any such baseline — say, by appeal to fairness between competitors in sport and to scarcity of resources in healthcare — and if so, do those two justifications track the same distinction, or merely coincidentally overlapping ones? I'd also like to know where the practice is philosophically underdetermined: which rival theories of the good would license it just as well, and what would actually have to change if a system dropped the therapy/enhancement line altogether and simply asked whether an intervention made a life go better. Please keep your answer to no more than 700 words.
