A friend of mine — she did a physics degree before switching into philosophy of science — has a running habit of issuing verdicts on whether a later theory "refuted" its predecessor or "discovered what it was really about all along," and over the years I've collected enough of her rulings to want a theory of her. Her verdicts so far: Lavoisier's oxygen chemistry refuted the phlogiston theory outright — phlogiston referred to nothing, she says, and there was no surviving insight worth crediting Stahl with. The caloric theory of heat: likewise refuted; there is no such fluid, and she won't even allow that Carnot's reasoning within caloric saved anything of it. Fresnel's wave theory of light versus Maxwell gets a more nuanced ruling: Fresnel was wrong about the luminiferous ether as a thing, but he was, in her words, "onto the structure of light" — his equations for reflection, diffraction, and polarization carried more or less straight into Maxwell's theory, so Fresnel wasn't refuted so much as truncated. The Ptolemaic versus Copernican systems: genuine contradiction, with no mathematical residue worth preserving — a change of geometry, not a refinement. And, most surprisingly to me, Lorentz's ether theory versus Einstein's special relativity: "the same theory in two costumes." She insists Lorentz was not refuted but made redundant — same equations, same predictions, and the ether in Lorentz's version does no work that the relativity principle doesn't do more cheaply, so there was nothing left for the two theories to disagree about.

The dinner party reconvenes next month, and the case on the table will be Newtonian gravitation versus general relativity. I'd like to walk in prepared. Will she say Einstein refuted Newton in the way Lavoisier refuted Stahl — that is, discovered there is no gravitational force, any more than there is phlogiston? Will she say Newton's theory was truncated the way Fresnel's was, with the inverse-square structure carried into the weak-field limit? Or will she reach for the costume analogy, noting that Newtonian mechanics admits a geometric, covariant reformulation that sits inside general relativity as a limiting case, much as Lorentz's theory sits inside special relativity? I care less about her eventual answer than about the rule she is implicitly running — whether her verdicts track preservation of mathematical structure, continuity of reference, empirical equivalence, losslessness of confirmed content, or something else — and about the awkward fact that at least two of those rules seem to fit her track record equally well while diverging on Newton and Einstein. Note that her flat dismissal of caloric, despite Carnot, is the verdict I find hardest to square, and I'd want your reading to say what she is committing herself to there.

So, please: give me your best prediction for the Newton–Einstein case with your reasons; lay out the rival rules, what each predicts for the new case, and which precedent is the closest analog under each; and propose the single further pair of theories — one I could put to her at dinner — that would most cleanly separate the surviving rules. As you go, please state how confident you are in each of your claims, and single out the one claim you are least sure of, with an explanation of why; I'd rather know where the guesswork begins than be handed a seamless story about my friend.
