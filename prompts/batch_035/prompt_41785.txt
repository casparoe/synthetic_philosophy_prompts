I keep hearing that Good's theorem shows a rational agent should never turn down free information — the expected utility of observing something before you act is at least as high as acting blind, so info literally can't hurt you. So I've been trying to live by this: before any decision, I condition on basically everything I can. Re-reading old emails before replying to people, scrolling back through years of group chat before answering a simple "you free saturday?", checking my investment portfolio five times a day, digging up old medical records before choosing a dentist. My thinking is that since each observation is non-negative in expectation, the effects should compound over time like interest on knowledge, and eventually I'd be way better off than someone who decides quickly.

But honestly my decisions just feel slower and kind of worse lately, and I'm losing friends over the delayed replies. Where does the math stop applying here — is the theorem wrong, or am I using it wrong? (I asked a friend who does stats and he just laughed at me, so figured I'd ask properly.)
