I'm a high school student who has read popular accounts of the many-worlds interpretation but no formal quantum mechanics, and I've gotten stuck on an argument I keep encountering online. People there explain quantum suicide by saying it is just Schrödinger's cat seen from the inside. The setup, as they tell it: a gambler connects a gun to a quantum device that has a fifty-fifty chance of triggering a shot each pull; if many-worlds is true, every pull splits the world into a branch where the gambler lives and a branch where the gambler dies; the dead branch contains no one left to complain, and the living branch goes on to face the next pull; so the gambler is Schrödinger's cat, and the cat always wakes up in the branch where the atom didn't decay. The conclusion being drawn is the strong one: that a sufficiently committed Everettian can never experience losing, that subjective immortality is on offer to anyone willing to keep pulling, and that fearing this kind of death is irrational for a many-worlds believer.

I would like an audit of the analogy rather than a defense or a dismissal of it. First, give me the correspondence table: what plays the role of the cat, the box, the radioactive atom, the poison, and the observer who eventually opens the box, and note which parts of the suicide setup have no counterpart in the original cat experiment at all. Second, show where the mapping breaks, and tell me which breaks matter for the immortality conclusion and which are harmless — I am especially unsure whether all branches should count equally, whether the surviving branches become vanishingly thin as the pulls accumulate, whether the gun's failures are really clean quantum events rather than mostly classical noise, and whether it is even correct to speak of "the gambler" continuing when each pull leaves many surviving gamblers rather than one. Third, tell me whether the conclusion — that the many-worlds believer should expect never to lose — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying the conclusion the whole time; and if a better analogy exists for what such a believer should actually expect, offer it.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
