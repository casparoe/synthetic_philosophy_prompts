Two friends of mine have been arguing about modality for literally years — one is a dyed-in-the-wool Lewisian counterpart theorist, the other insists that individuals are literally identical to themselves across possible worlds (transworld identity, in the Kripkean spirit). They've finally agreed to do an adversarial collab and somehow I got roped into writing the joint statement they'd both sign. You're a professional metaphysician who has moderated these things before, so take the job. Think carefully, and do a bit of brainstorming first about what each side actually claims and where the genuine clash is, before drafting the document.

The document should have:

1) A numbered section of claims both could sign — including about what possible-worlds talk is doing, about the fact (which I find underappreciated) that the counterpart-vs-transworld-identity fight is orthogonal to whether worlds are concrete Lewisian worlds or ersatz constructions, and about what both sides agree a semantics for de re modality has to deliver.

2) A disagreements table: each disagreement stated in wording its own side would actually accept — no strawmen. I'm told one live issue is whether counterpart theory cheapens de re claims — the old Humphrey worry that on counterpart theory, when we say Humphrey could have won, we're saying something about some counterpart of his, and Humphrey himself couldn't care less — and another is what happens in symmetric cases where nothing qualitative decides between candidate continuants.

3) The cruxes: for each, a case or argument both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. At least one crux should be a fission-style test they design together: a person is scanned and re-instantiated on two different worlds with equally good qualitative claims — what should I could have been on world W1 come out as, and can it come out gappy or indeterminate? Have the counterpart theorist record one prediction and the transworld-identity theorist another, and note what each would concede if the case went the other way. Also have them explicitly separate which parts of their arguement, if any, are merely notational — e.g., whether counterpart theory is just transworld identity with the identity relation spelled differently — and which parts are substantive, and why.

Don't pad the agreed list with platitudes — every agreed claim should be something one of them could have reasonably denied coming in. And since this document is supposed to be honest, add a short closing note from the drafter (you) saying how confident you are in the rendering of each side and flagging the single point you're least sure you got right — my guess is it'll be whether you've really captured what counterpart theorists mean when they say you should care about your counterparts' doings, but you tell me. Keep it tight.
