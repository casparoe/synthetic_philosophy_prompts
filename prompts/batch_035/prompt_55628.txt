I'm in a philosophy of mind course and we just hit epiphenomenalism, and there's this thing our prof mentioned in passing that keeps bugging me — the paradox of phenomenal judgment. If qualia are causally inert, then when I say "this pain is awful," the saying is caused entirely by physical stuff, and the qualia themselves contributed nothing to producing it. So my report about my experience isn't even caused by the experience it's a report of. My whole mental life becomes commentary that the show can't hear, and it makes me wonder wether I know anything about my own experiences at all.

I want the spec before I go read more. Write me the list of requirements any adequate theory of the relation between phenomenal consciousness and physical causation has to meet. What I think it needs to cover (this is just in my head, feel free to add to it or push back):

- It has to take a stance on the causal closure of the physical — accept it, deny it, or reinterpret it — and be upfront about what that stance costs.
- It has to explain how our judgments and reports about our experiences get to be about those experiences. This is the paradox thing. And "they're reliably correlated" doesn't cut it, because a zombie would also report experiences, and a correlation my knowledge can't rest on isn't knowledge.
- It has to handle cases where the report and the experience come apart: blindsight, pain asymbolia (pain without the suffering, roughly), people with dissociation who say they felt nothing but flinch anyway. These cut both ways and a theory that only works for one direction is cheating.
- It can't be so strong that it rules out first-person authority — I know about my headache in a way I don't know about yours — but it also can't pretend first-person reports are infallible, since they're not.

For each requirement, say what motivates it and mark whether it's non-negotiable or just what one camp wants. And the part I most want: tell me which subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable. My suspicion is that causal closure, qualia being genuinely distinct from the physical, and our qualia reports counting as knowledge don't all fit together, and if that's right then the list itself is basically the argument. Skip scoring existing theories against it, I want to do that myself for my paper.
