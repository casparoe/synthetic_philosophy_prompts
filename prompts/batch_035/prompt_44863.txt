My thesis advisor has an argument about standing to blame that I can't refute but also can't acccept. It goes like this. (1) Blaming someone isn't just privately disapproving of them; it's addressing them, demanding a justification from them, and you can only demand that from a position you actually occupy. (2) With respect to a given fault, you occupy that position only if you're not guilty of the same fault yourself — that's what hypocrisy is, and hypocritical blame is disqualified. (3) "The same fault" has to be read at the level of the vice, not the specific act. His example: an aunt who texts at red lights lecturing her nephew about distracted driving. She's not guilty of his act, or even of his kind of texting, but everyone agrees she has no standing — so sameness of act can't be the test; sameness of vice must be. (4) And nobody's record at the level of vice is clean: everyone has been dishonest somewhere, unfair somewhere, careless somewhere. (5) So almost every act of blame targets a vice the blamer has exhibited at some point in their life, which means nobody has standing to blame anybody for virtually anything. He takes this straight: standing almost never exists, and our feeling that the victim of a con may blame the con artist is sentiment, not principle.

The conclusion is mad, but each step looks decent on its own. Something feels off between the aunt case and the "nobody's record is clean" step, but I can't put my finger on it. Which step should go, and what does rejecting it cost? If you think more than one could go, tell me which is cheapest and why.
