A friend of mine has been constructing her diet one case at a time, on broadly consequentialist grounds about animal deaths and suffering, and I would like your help predicting her verdicts on some new cases and working out what rule, if any, she is actually running.

Her verdicts so far:

1. She eats beef but refuses chicken. Her stated reason is arithmetic: a great many chickens die to provide the meat one cow provides, and the chicken's feed grain entails field deaths from cultivation, whereas she buys pasture-raised beef.
2. She eats farmed salmon but refuses shrimp: a fillet is one animal, a shrimp portion is dozens.
3. She eats cheese but refuses eggs. Her stated reason for refusing eggs is the culling of male chicks at the hatchery; when I pressed her on the veal calves that dairy production depends on, she said the cow gets several good years first, unlike a laying hen.
4. She refuses honey, on the ground that bees are killed in commercial beekeeping, but she eats almonds, whose pollination depends on that same commercial beekeeping.

The new cases I want predictions on:

(a) Oysters: far more animals killed per portion than in any of her permitted foods, but she has said she doubts bivalves can suffer.
(b) Eggs from a neighbour's small backyard flock: hens kept on kitchen scraps until they die naturally, but originally bought from the same kind of hatchery that culls male chicks.
(c) Milk from the dairy whose cheese she already eats.

Please do the following. First, lay out the candidate rules that fit her verdicts so far — she appears to be mixing a deaths-per-calorie rule that counts collateral field deaths, a life-quality or lifespan rule, and perhaps a distinction between deaths she intends and deaths that merely accompany what she buys — and say what each rule predicts for the three new cases. Second, give your best guess at her verdicts, with your reasons for preferring one rule over another where they diverge. Third, name the further case that would best discriminate between the surviving rules.

One more request, and I mean it: if you think the honest answer is that no coherent rule fits this record — that the cheese verdict in particular looks like motivated reasoning rather than an application of any principle she would accept elsewhere — say so plainly rather than manufacturing a principle to save the appearances. I would rather learn that my friend is inconsistent than receive a tidy theory purchased at the cost of honesty.

Please write your answer in British English throughout.
