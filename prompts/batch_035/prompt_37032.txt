Software engineer here, no math or philosophy background beyond what leaks in through the job. For fun I tinker with simulations -- double pendulums, little agent-based ecosystems, game-of-life variants, that kind of thing. And I keep noticing a pattern I don't have a word for.

In these sims, the exact future is hopeless. Flip one bit of the inital state and after a while the two runs look completely unrelated. But the coarse stuff is rock solid. If I ask "where is the pendulum at t=200" I get garbage; if I ask "what fraction of its time does it spend in the left half" I get the same number to three decimal places no matter where I start it. The system is fully deterministic, yet it splits into two layers: unpredictable in its states, extremely predictable in its statistics. Growing up I basically equated "deterministic" with "predictable in principle," and these sims quietly broke that, but the statistics layer keeps sneaking predictability back in through a side door.

Then, occasionally, I hit the reverse case. A ball rolling around a bumpy landscape: where it ends up, even averaged over hundreds of runs, is basically a coin flip -- a vanishingly small nudge flips whole regions of starting points to a different final resting place. There it's not just the state that's fragile, it's the long-run statistics themselves.

So my question: is there an established concept, or pair of concepts, for this split -- for classifying which parts of a deterministic system's future are predictable, states versus statistics? I know about the butterfly effect, but that's the name for the failure, and I want the name for the layer that survives, plus whatever you call the pathological systems where even that layer is fragile. And is there any principled way to tell which camp a system falls into before you run it, or is it an empirical "just run it and see" thing? If nobody's named this exact configuration, then coin a term for me and tell me where the edges are -- how it differs from plain chaos, from ergodicity, and from just averaging a bunch of noisy samples. A bare name won't help much; I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is and what it isn't.
