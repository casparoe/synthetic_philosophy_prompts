I would like a document of the kind two long-standing opponents ought to have produced years ago. The parties: an uncompromising adaptationist in the Dawkins–Pinker tradition, who holds that good reverse engineering of traits is the engine of evolutionary biology and that the charge of "just-so storytelling" is mostly a slur; and a pluralist in the Gould–Lewontin mold, who holds that the adaptationist program systematically overstates what natural selection can build and neglects constraint, drift, and the byproducts of structure. They have been arguing past each other since the Spandrels of San Marco critique appeared, and I want to know exactly how much of their quarrel is real.

Draft the joint statement they could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Structure it as follows. Section A: a numbered list of claims both accept — including claims about the raw power of selection, the reality of developmental and architectural constraints, the legitimacy of adaptive thinking as a generator of hypotheses, and the existence of genuinely bad adaptationist practice in the literature. Section B: a disagreements table, with each row containing the precise formulation of one disagreement, worded so that each side would accept its own side's phrasing as fair — cover, at minimum, whether "adaptationism" names an ontological thesis about the prevalence of adaptations or a methodological commitment about research strategy, and whether the famous complaint about just-so stories is a point about falsifiability or about prior probabilities. Section C: the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one party — and for each crux, one concrete case or line of evidence both agree would bear on it, with each party's prediction written down in advance.

Use three specific test cases to discipline the cruxes: the human chin, treated by the spandrels side as an architectural byproduct of growth in the face and by the other side as possibly a functional, selected unit; the female orgasm, where the pluralist side aligns with Lloyd's byproduct hypothesis and the adaptationist side with the various proposed adaptive accounts; and one trait of your choosing where both camps already concede the other side's story is right. For each, state what would count as evidence for each party and where the predictions coincide, so the disagreement can be located exactly rather than gestured at. Finally, tell me which parts of their dispute you judge to be merely verbal — say which ones and why — and which parts could in principle be settled by evidence that does not yet exist. Do not let either side smuggle its background commitments into the agreed-claims section; a formulation the adaptationist would quietly read as "selection did it" and the pluralist would quietly read as "selection could not have done it" is a failure, not a compromise.
