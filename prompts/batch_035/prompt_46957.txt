The paradox of tragedy has bugged me for years and I've finally decided the only way to make progress is to write down my actual case-by-case gut verdicts and see if any principle survives contact with them. So, verdicts: crying at the end of a novel where the character I've spent 400 pages with dies, then rereading it two years later -- straightforwardly something I want, no puzzle. Sad breakup songs on repeat at 2am after a breakup -- actively sought, would be annoyed if someone confiscated them, and this is the sharpest case because the sadness and the desire coexist in real time, not just in retrospect. A documentary with real footage of a real flood that made me cry -- watched once, never want to again, it felt like homework, not dessert. But true crime podcasts about actual murders -- I binge those happily, which seems to flatly contradict the documentary verdict and I can't see how both can be true. A friend telling me over coffee about their actual divorce -- distress, zero appetite, I sit through it for them. Schadenfreude when my professional rival's project implodes -- pleasure at someone's misfortune, but it feels like a different phenomenon entirely, not even in the paradox's jurisdiction. And a tragicomedy where I'm laughing and crying in the same scene -- feels like it counts, but I genuinely can't tell if the sadness is doing any work or is just set dressing for the jokes.

My tentative principle is something like "I enjoy sadness when I control the dose" -- songs I can skip, novels I can close, versus the friend's divorce which arrives at its own pace. But this dies on the documentary case (I had full dose control there, could've paused anytime, and still never touched it again), and it's weirdly unclear about true crime too. So: is there a principle that captures all or most of these? Does the documentary/true-crime pair force a real-vs-narrativized distinction, or is one of those two verdicts just mine being wrong? What does your principle predict for cases I haven't listed -- an immersive VR drowning experience (maximal dose control, but I'm suspicious it should count differently), a sad play I can't pause once it starts, a retelling of a true tragedy that's obviously been embellished for effect? And please be honest about which of my verdicts has to go if a simpler principle gets everything else. I've seen LW-adjacent people wave the whole paradox off as you just like stories, reveal preferences, next -- which I find definately unsatisfying. The asymmetry between the breakup songs and the friend's divorce is real and needs an explanation, and the catharsis/conversion stories I vaguely remember from Aristotle and Hume never seemed to even address it.
