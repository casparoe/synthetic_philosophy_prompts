Software engineer here, no philosophy background at all, but I fell down the quantum rabbit hole a while back and something about how people argue over Bell's theorem keeps bugging me. The math and the experiments are basically settled (I read about the Delft experiment around 2015 that supposedly closed the remaining loopholes at once), so you'd think the argument would be over. But it never is. Instead everyone just picks which ingredient of the setup they refuse to believe in. One guy says, fine, the particles don't carry predetermined answers, only the wavefunction is real and it doesn't "do" anything anywhere. Another guy says the source and the detector settings must be correlated from the very beginning, so nothing ever outruns a light signal. Another says causes can run backwards in time. Another just shrugs and accepts that the world is nonlocal, who cares. And I've noticed you basically cannot argue anyone out of their choice with data, because every camp is consistent with every experiment that could ever be run.

Here's the part that feels important and that my friends keep missing when I try to explain it: it's not just "quantum is weird" and it's not just "philosophers can't agree on anything". The escape hatches are mutually exclusive. Exactly one picture of the world is right, the proof forces you to pick, and there is apparently no way for any experiment to tell you which pick is correct. It's like a test suite where all the tests pass but the argument is over what the tests actually test, and each engineer insists on a different spec for what the system "really" is.

So, questions. Does this overall situation have a name? Not "the interpretation problem of QM" — I mean the structural thing, where a rigorous result converts into a forced choice among unfalsifiable bullet-biting moves, and the choice is forever immune to new data. I half-remember the term "underdetermination" from somewhere (philosophy of science, maybe?) but I'm not sure that's it either — from what I can tell that usually means multiple theories fit the same evidence, whereas here the alternatives aren't just empirically equivalent, they flat-out contradict each other about how the world works, and we still can't choose. Is there a name for that extra wrinkle, or does it reduce to plain underdetermination once you look closely?

Second, seperate question: is there an actual technical name for the specific escape hatch where the detector settings and the particle source are correlated from the start? I'm pretty sure it has a name and I've forgotten it, and I'd like to know whether it's considered a serious option or the "you're basically assuming a conspiracy" one, and what the standard objection to it is.

And if there's no established term for the general pattern, honestly feel free to coin one — just be clear about what it covers and what it doesn't, because I've tried describing this to people twice now and both times they heard something much dumber than what I meant. I want to know whether I've noticed a real structure or just reinvented something that already has a literature attached to it, and if it does, how far off my description is from how the pros carve it up.
