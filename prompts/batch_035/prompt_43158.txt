Some terms you might want: your probability that small-animal mortality in crop farming is high enough per calorie for this to even be in the running; your probability that "grass-fed" cattle actually get almost all their calories from pasture and hay rather than from harvested feed (if they eat a lot of grain the whole argument collapses back onto the same field deaths); your probability that the right way to add up deaths weights a cow and a mouse roughly equally versus scaling somehow by sentience or lifespan or brain, since if you weight heavily toward cows the math flips the other way; and your probability that it even matters morally that harvest deaths are unintentional rather than deliberate — is accidentally shredding a mouse more like killing it or more like collateral damage you didn't aim at, or does the death count not care about intentions at all.

And as a quick side question, does anything change if instead of beef vs a vegan diet it's chicken vs a vegan diet? My brother-in-law eats plenty of supermarket chicken too and I've always suspected that part of his diet is doing way more damage than he admits, but I'd like to hear whether the same decomposition says that.
