The debate over why there is something rather than nothing appears to me to have stalled into three camps that have been talking past one another for decades, and I would like a diagnosis of the standoff rather than yet another answer. Camp one, following Adolf Grünbaum, holds that the question is ill-posed because it covertly treats absolute nothingness as the natural default that requires no explanation, while the existence of anything counts as a deviation that does — a presupposition Grünbaum regarded as a residue of creation theology rather than something earned by argument. Camp two accepts the question and answers it: Derek Parfit explored probabilistic explanations on which certain global features of a possibility make it more likely to be the one that is actual; John Leslie's axiarchism holds that the universe exists because it is ethically required to; and theists, along with necessitarians like Peter van Inwagen, answer that something exists necessarily, so the question has the same standing as asking why there is a necessary truth. Camp three replies that every answer merely re-poses the question: explain the existence of things by a selector, a necessary being, or a meta-law, and you can still ask why that exists or why it has the content it does — so the only honest options are brute fact or refusal.

Here is my hypothesis about the stall: the camps are not disagreeing about a single thing. They mean different things by nothing — no concrete objects, which leaves numbers and necessary truths untouched, versus no facts or truths whatsoever — and the standard answers only look like competitors because they are silently aimed at different targets. The necessitarians are answering why there are concrete things given that necessary truths hold anyway; Parfit and Leslie are answering why this world rather than another; and the brute-fact camp is answering a third question, why there is any fact at all, which it takes to be unanswerable in principle. Meanwhile the Grünbaum camp is neither agreeing nor disagreeing with any of them, since what it disputes is an unstated assumption about explanation itself. On this hypothesis the standoff is partly a verbal dispute under one heading and partly a substantive disagreement about the explanatory default — whether nothingness or something is the state that needs no accounting — that no one ever argues for directly.

Is this diagnosis right? If it is, lay out what each camp is really committed to and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a sharper taxonomy of readings of nothing, a formal account of when an explanatory regress may legitimately terminate, an argument that only one reading of nothing is even coherent, or something else entirely? If the hypothesis is wrong, tell me where it breaks — for instance, is there a genuine head-on collision between axiarchism and brute-fact views that my different-questions story papers over, or between Grünbaum and the necessitarians about what explanation owes us?
