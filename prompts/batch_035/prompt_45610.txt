A couple of friends from my reading group and I got into a long argument at the pub last week and I still can't sort out who was right. Some context: we've been reading stuff on the problem of induction, Hume and all that, and one of the group, Dan, works in ML and brought up the no-free-lunch theorems. The way the argument went was roughly this. (1) Dan said the theorems show that averaged over all possible problems, every learning algorithm performs exactly the same as every other, so all the hype about this or that method being "smarter" is empty, and more than that, the theorems are basically the mathematical proof of what Hume said: induction has no justification, since a world where the future resembles the past is, from a logically omniscient standpoint, no more likely than a world where it doesn't. (2) My friend Priya, who's doing a philosophy PhD, pushed back that the theorems only hold when you average over a uniform distribution of all objective functions, which is a kind of maximal-structurelessness assumption, and the actual world demonstrably has structure, so the theorems tell us nothing about learning in our world. (3) Dan answered that saying "the world has structure" is itself an inductive claim you can't justify without circularity, so all the theorems have done is make Hume's point precise, and the circularity is still there hiding in Priya's premises. (4) Priya replied that the circularity isn't vicious, that we're entitled to a simplicity-biased prior as a starting point rather than the uniform one, and anyway the no-free-lunch framework itself smuggles in a substantive assumption, because treating every objective function as equally probable is just as much a prior commitment as privileging simple, compressible functions, so if the uniform prior discredits learning, it discredits itself. (5) Dan's last word was that then every prior is equally arbitrary and Occam's razor is just our cognitive bias dressed up, and a universe tuned to be anti-inductive is exactly as coherent as ours, so nothing rational separates them. (6) Priya ended by saying that machine learning works in practice every day and that vindicates induction, and Dan kind of laughed and said that was the most question-begging thing anyone had said all night.
