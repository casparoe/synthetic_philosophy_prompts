I run a forecasting workshop for grad students from mixed departments -- about 18 people, one 3-hour session, and they've all had some exposure to Tetlock-style calibration stuff but nothing deeper. I want a structured activity that makes them feel the inside view vs outside view tension in their bones, not just nod along to it, and then feel the awkwardness of epistemic modesty once they've felt the first thing.

Here's roughly what I've imagined but feel free to redesign: split the room. Half the participants ("planners") get a rich dossier on a real historical project -- say, a big infrastructure build or a product launch that actually happened -- with timelines, budgets, design docs, press coverage, everything. The other half ("statisticians") get nothing but a one-paragraph description plus the outcome distribution across a broad reference class of comparable projects. Both groups forecast the same outcome variables (final cost overrun, delivery delay, whether it got cancelled). Neither group knows what the other has been given. Then we reveal how much the groups' forecasts diverged and what the actual outcomes were.

But I want two twists layered on top of the basic design, and this is where I need your help making it actually work:

Twist 1: I want the statisticians to be handed a gerrymandered reference class in round one -- one that's too narrow or cherry-picked so the outside view is actually misleading -- and a reasonable one in round two, so participants discover that "use the outside view" is not free advice, you have to make a judgment about which class is yours, and that judgment is itself an inside-view-ish move. How do I construct or stage this without it feeling like a rigged gotcha?

Twist 2: after the reveal, everyone sees everyone's forecasts on all rounds and gets a new set of cases to forecast individually, plus one final aggregate decision that pays off based on accuracy. Some participants should secretly get a small piece of genuine private information about the new cases, and some shouldn't. The question they have to face -- without me ever naming it -- is whether to collapse toward the group mean or trust themselves, and the payoff structure should make blanket modesty costly for the informed ones and blanket confidence costly for everyone else.

So: full rules, materials, timing across the three hours, how to source or construct the case dossiers (they can be anonymized/composite if that's easier to prepare), a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money, and a debrief that maps specific moments onto the inside/outside distinction and onto modesty vs its critics. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which part of the whole inside-vs-outside debate an activity like this just cannot reproduce and I'll have to supply in the debrief by talking at them.

I do NOT want the phrases "inside view," "outside view," or "epistemic modesty" said out loud until the debrief. If a participant uses them earlier that's fine, but I won't confirm anything.
