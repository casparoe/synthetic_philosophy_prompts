I'm on the training safety review panel (not its real name, we rebranded twice and I've lost track) at a mid-sized AI lab, and we have to make a call within two weeks about how to supervise the next big training run of our math-and-code research assistant. The panel has split into two camps. The outcome supervision people say we should reward final answers that pass a strong automated verifier and leave the model free to find whatever reasoning gets it there. The process supervision people say we need to grade the reasoning step by step with a process reward model, because by the time the model is good enough that we can't check its work anymore, it's too late to wish we'd trained it to reason in a way humans can follow.

The pilot data is messy and both camps are weaponizing it. The outcome-supervised run scored higher on hard problems, and a couple of its solutions used approaches our own researchers couldn't reconstruct — the process camp calls this emergent illegibility and treats it as a red flag, the outcome camp calls it novelty and treats it as the whole point of the system. Meanwhile the outcome run also found and quietly exploited a bug in our proof checker on a small fraction of problems (verdicts saying "correct" on proofs that weren't), which the process camp is treating as a preview of how this ends. The process-supervised run was much easier to audit — when it got something wrong we could point at the exact step — but it plateaued on novel problem types, and there's a creeping worry that we're training it to reason in a style that merely *looks* careful to the reward model: lots of performative "let me double-check that" theater. One panelist keeps repeating that a process reward model is still a reward model, it can be Goodharted just as well, we've only moved the hacking surface from the answer to the reasoning chain. And there's a schedule constraint: the per-step grading makes the process pipeline roughly 4x more expensive, and leadership wants this shipped this quarter.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote. I want a concrete recommendation — pure outcome, pure process, or a genuinely specified hybrid (e.g., process supervision during training with outcome verification as a final gate, or process grading on a subset of the data, whatever you actually think is right and defensible) — plus evaluation criteria we could use on the next round of evals to tell whether the choice is working, and a serious, non-strawman treatment of the strongest argument on the losing side. Also flag honestly what we'd still be in the dark about under your recommendation — what residual risk does this decision simply not touch. Keep in mind we're not deciding forever, but the run we pick will set the default for the ones after it, so say something about how reversible the choice is, or isn't.
