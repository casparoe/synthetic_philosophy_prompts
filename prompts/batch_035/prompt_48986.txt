I've been trying to sort out, from my gut reactions to concrete cases, when a person's epistemic situation is defective in the way people mean by "echo chamber" or "epistemic bubble," and I'd like to know whether some single principle captures the pattern. My verdicts: a climate science conference where everyone takes anthropogenic warming as settled and no skeptic is in the room — not defective in the least; the informational situation is insular, but nothing is wrong with it. A Flat Earth forum whose pinned post explains that NASA fabricates satellite images and that arguments from "globe-pushers" are deliberately designed to shake your faith — clearly defective. A breastfeeding support forum where nobody discredits outsiders, but where members who report supply problems quietly drift away until only enthusiasts remain — defective, though I'm not sure why, given that no discrediting goes on. A Marxist reading group that reads only Marxists but engages cheerfully with liberals, holding that the liberals simply haven't read enough — somehow fine. A village where nobody has ever encountered a doubter of the local religion, purely because no such person lives within a hundred miles — not defective, though the members' exposure to dissent is no wider than the Flat Earthers'. And the case I keep going back and forth on: a tightly organized rationalist-adjacent community that is, as far as I can tell, right about nearly everything, but that instructs members to discount mainstream coverage of its own doctrines as systematically distorted, so that outside counterarguments lose force at the source rather than on the merits. Is there a principle that accommodates all of these, or close to it — for instance, something about whether the community's practices would steer you back if you were wrong, rather than whether they expose you to dissent — and what does it predict about cases I haven't listed, such as an ordinary person whose news diet is curated by an algorithm they never notice? Please state the principle precisely, tie each clause to the case that motivates it, and tell me plainly which of my verdicts, if any, you would overturn rather than complicate the principle to save. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question. Please answer in American English.
