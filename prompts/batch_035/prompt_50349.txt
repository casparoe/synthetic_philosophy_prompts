I'm writing up my position for an aesthetics reading group that's doing Tolstoy's What Is Art?, and I want to check whether my own views actually hang together before the others get at them. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. Tolstoy is basically right about the core of it: art is the transmission of feeling from artist to audience, and a work's merit is a matter of how strongly it infects the audience with that feeling.
2. I also buy his universality point, at least in spirit — the best art can be felt by ordinary people across classes and eras, and if a work can only move people who've put in decades of training, that's a demerit, not a neutral fact about it.
3. That said, my own favorite works are dense, allusive, difficult stuff — late modernist novels and the like — and alot of what I genuinely think is the greatest art is not immediately infectious to anyone without that training.
4. I think Tolstoy wildly overrates sincerity. Plenty of great art is calculated, ironic, or just craftsmanlike rather than a sincere outpouring of felt emotion, and it infects just fine.
5. Kitsch is my sticking point. a tearjerker of a commercial can be maximally infectious, and arguably sincere, but it's bad art, and I can't see how infection alone explains that. I also refuse to take Tolstoy's escape hatch of grading the *content* of the transmitted feeling (his whole religious-perception-of-the-age business), since that smuggles a content standard back in and I don't want one.

So: does this set of commitments hang together? Which ones are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest revision for each? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and tell me which single fix would clean up the most. Also flag anywhere I'm just misdescribing Tolstoy, since that might be part of the problem. Answer in American English, please.
