I have no academic background, but for the past year I have been thinking on my own about whether one can genuinely come to know that a painting or a piece of music is beautiful merely by being told so by someone whose taste one trusts. My tentative view is that there are two different things one might want from a friend's verdict: being entitled to say "this is beautiful" in conversation, and actually seeing the beauty for oneself, and that testimony can deliver the first but never the second. My friend disagrees; she holds that once you accept the verdict of someone you trust and understand the reasons behind it, the difference between the two collapses. Please score each of the following considerations from 0 to 10 for how much weight it should carry in settling our dispute, and defend each score in a sentence or two: (a) the analogy with a colour-blind person trusting sighted friends' reports about colour; (b) the thought that beauty is supposed to reward first-hand acquaintance, so that second-hand knowledge of it is self-undermining; (c) the observation that we happily defer to others about the beauty of places we have never visited and feel no unease about it; and (d) my friend's point that combining a trusted verdict with the reasons behind it amounts to a kind of seeing with another's eyes. Finally, tell me, on the strength of those scores, who comes out ahead.
