I write crime novels for a living, but twice a month I run a tiny philosophy podcast from my spare room, and next week's guest is a logician who's spent her whole career on what she cheerfully calls taking the tortoise's side. Her position, as best I can reconstruct it from her papers: she thinks Lewis Carroll's old dialogue about Achilles and the tortoise shows something genuine, namely that no rule of inference, however you state it, ever compels a rational mind to accept a conclusion. If the tortoise accepts A, and accepts that if A then B, but won't accept B, you can write down a third thing — that anyone accepting the first two is entitled to B — and he can refuse that one too, and so on forever. Most people treat that regress as a puzzle to be solved. She thinks it's just the actual shape of things: what stops it in real reasoners is training and habit, we're schooled into making the moves, and she's fine biting the bullet that deductive justification is in the end a psychological fact about us rather than a logical fact about the inferences. She also reckons the same regress quietly infects induction, testimony and even perception, and she takes that as evidence she's onto something rather than as a reductio of her view.

Draft me eight to ten interview questions. At least one needs to press on whether she's conflating "can't be compelled" with "isn't justified" — nobody thinks a proof wrestles you to the ground, so what exactly is missing? One should pin down where her story leaves the tortoise himself: does he believe the premises and merely refuse to make the move, or does she think he can't even count as having the beliefs? One should push the parallel with rule-following worries (how does a rule determine its own future applications), and one should put to her the charge that she's swapped a logic question for a psychology question and answered the wrong one. For each question, give me the follow-up keyed to the reply she's most likely to give — she's fond of saying the regress is "structural, not psychological", and I've never managed to pin down what that's supposed to mean, so I'd like a question that makes her spell it out or admit it's a slogan. She's also said in print that a community of tortoises who never took the modus ponens step would be recognisably rational by their own lights, which sounds alot like relativism to me, so work in a question about that too if it fits.

Please, no generic stuff about how she got into the field or what she's reading — I did that when she was on the show before and it died on its arse.
