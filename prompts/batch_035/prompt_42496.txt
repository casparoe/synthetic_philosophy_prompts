next month I'm giving a talk at a small workshop on the philosophy of the scaling era, and my thesis is basically realism about interpretability findings — which is really the old question of whether these networks have representations in any serious sense, or are just a pile of matrix multiplications that happens to work. Concretely: when interpretability work finds a direction in activation space that tracks code vs. prose, or a feature that fires on French medical jargon, I want to say that's not just convenient bookkeeping imposed by the researchers. The network itself has a fact of the matter about what features it computes, and good interpretability work discovers those rather than inventing them.

My argument, in sketch, so you know what the objections should be aimed at: (a) interventions — ablating or patching a feature reliably toggles one specific capability while leaving neighboring ones intact, and that looks like finding real causal structure, not laying a grid over noise; (b) convergence — probes, sparse autoencoders, and causal tracing methods that share basically no training objective keep lighting up in the same neighborhoods; (c) portability — you can steer with a feature that was found on one corpus and the model's behavior changes coherently somewhere else entirely, which would be a strange coincidence if the feature were an artifact of the measuring method.

The room is maybe fifteen people. Six or so are mech interp researchers who are closet instrumentalists — they'll say "feature" all day long and then get squirrely the moment you ask whether anything in the network priviledges one decomposition over another. Two philosophers of science who know the realism/instrumentalism literature inside out but have never trained anything, so every analogy they reach for will be about thermometers or electron microscopes. One stats postdoc whose default explanation for any empirical result is overfitting, and who will want to know how many things I reran before I believed them. A couple of phil of mind grad students who will try to connect this to consciousness no matter how off-topic it is — I'd like a quick line for politely shutting that down, because they will try. And one senior person who worked on superposition back when, and will raise basis-dependence: if there are far more features than neurons and everything is smeared across the space, in what sense is any particular decomposition the network's rather than ours?

Write me the prep sheet. I want the six objections most likely to actually come up, each in the voice of the person who'd raise it, my strongest two-minute response to each, and an honest flag on which ones I'd be better off conceding on the spot instead of fighting.
