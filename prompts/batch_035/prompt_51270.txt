Quick sanity check I can't quite resolve on my own. In Descartes, the guy doubts everything he can, notices the doubting itself won't doubt, and lands on "I think, therefore I am" as his unshakeable first principle. But look at what actually got nailed down: something super narrow, like, thinking is going on right now. What everybody (Descartes included, honestly) walks away with is something way bigger — a persisting self, a thing that has these thoughts, maybe even a mind-stuff substance. The narrow claim is the indubitable one; the wide claim is just a normal claim that could be wrong. But because they're packed into a single sentence, the certainty of the small one seems to stain the big one. Nobody commits an obvious error — it's more like the certainty gets minted in one place and then spent somewhere else, and no one notices the transfer.

And I see this move everywhere once you look. A friend of mine says the one thing she can't doubt is that she's conscious — fine, read as experience is present — and two sentences later it means a unified inner observer that stays the same over time, riding on the same word. Or someone says you can't doubt you're in pain, and then uses that to prove pain is a private object only its owner can inspect.

It's not motte-and-bailey, I don't think, because there's no retreat under attack — the wide claim never gets defended at all, it just quietly inherits the certainty of the narrow one. Someone once told me Descartes' line should really read more like it thinks, the way we say it rains, which feels like the same itch, but that's about Descartes specifically. I'm asking about the general pattern: an argument establishes a narrow claim, and the wording quietly hands you a wider one for free. Does that pattern have an established name in the literature? If yes, tell me where its edges are — how it's different from plain equivocation, and whether it even counts as a fallacy or is more like a structural fact about how certainty attaches to sentences. If there's no established name, coin one and define it, and give me the boundary cases where the pattern is doing legitimate work instead of smuggling. Don't just hand me a bare label, I want the carving.
