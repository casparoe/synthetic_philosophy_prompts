I'm TA-ing a decision theory course and want a second opinion on a problem set answer before I hand it back. The question on the sheet was:

"Suppose an agent's preferences over options A, B, and C are cyclic: she prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A. (1) Starting with the agent holding C, explain how a trader could extract money from her through a sequence of trades. (2) Beyond the cyclic preferences themselves, what does the pump require? Name at least one assumption about the agent or the setup. (3) Now suppose the agent is sophisticated: she correctly predicts her own future choices and decides by backward induction. How does she respond to the pump, and does this show that cyclic preferences are harmless? Justify briefly."

The student wrote:

"Start with the agent holding C. The trader offers to swap C for B in exchange for a small fee; since the agent prefers B to C, she accepts and pays. The trader then offers B for A for another fee, and finally A for C for a third fee. After the last trade the agent holds C again, minus the three fees, so the trader has extracted money and the agent gained nothing — she is worse off then if no trades had happened, even though each individual trade looked good to her at the moment she made it.

For the pump to work the agent has to be naive: she takes each trade as it comes, with no though of the sequence as a whole, treating every choice as if it were her last. That is the key assumption — once the cycle is in place and the agent is naive, the pump goes through.

If the agent is sophisticated she reasons backwards. She sees that taking the first trade would lead, through the later trades she knows she would accept, back to C minus the fees, so at the first node she compares C with C-minus-fees and refuses to trade at all. In effect her preferences becomes transitive once she looks ahead, so no money changes hands. Since the sophisticated agent never loses anything, the money pump argument fails: cyclic preferences cannot be irrational, at least for agents capable of planning ahead."

Can you grade this and give me the specific comments that justify the grade, point by point — ideally one set per part of the question? Lead with the bottom line: put the grade in your very first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate the question back to me.
