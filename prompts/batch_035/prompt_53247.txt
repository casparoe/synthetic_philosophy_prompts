My acquaintance with AI alignment comes from blog posts and conference talks, and the field's recurring internal fight confuses me. As far as I can tell it splits like this. One camp holds that for sufficiently capable systems we should aim for a guarantee in the worst case: a demonstration that no circumstance the system itself can bring about — including circumstances that arise only once the system is deployed and acting — yields catastrophic behavior. The other camp holds that this is unattainable for learned systems of this kind, and that we should instead evaluate on realistic distributions, measure failure rates, and drive them down: risk management rather than proof. Members of the second camp sometimes add that this is simply how security engineering has always worked, and that the first camp is asking for something no deployed artifact has ever had.
