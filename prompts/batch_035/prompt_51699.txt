Two friends of mine have been arguing for years about the norm of assertion and finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration — one takes the knowledge norm (roughly the Williamson line: assert p only if you know p), the other holds a belief norm (assert what you believe, with belief itself answerable to truth). I'm supposed to draft the joint statement they'll both sign and I keep producing something slanted towards whichever of them I had coffee with last, so write it for me.

What I want in it: the claims both sides accept (that Moorean assertions like "p, but I don't believe p" are defective somehow, that you can properly assert something you believe but admit you don't know, that a norm's being violated shouldn't imply the assertion is untrue); the disagreements stated so each side would accept the wording for their own view — I especially want the pre-draw lottery assertion handled, the retraction cases ("hang on, I might be wrong about that" said after checking) that the knowledge-normer thinks only her view explains, and whether a norm of assertion is a rule you can break or just a standard critics appeal to; then the cruxes — I suspect one is whether the Moorean data really needs a norm of assertion at all rather than falling out of general facts about knowledge, and another is whether the belief-normer can say anything non-ad-hoc about why hearers reach for knowledge when challenging assertions. For each crux give a test case both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. They've also started using this term "retraction tax" for the cost you incur when you have to take back a claim after a challenge — work it in somewhere if it fits.

Format: numbered agreed claims first, then a disagreements table. And if you think part of their dispute is merely verbal — e.g. whether "norm" here means a constitutive rule of the practice or just an evaluative standard — say which part and why, since they'd both rather know that than keep going.
