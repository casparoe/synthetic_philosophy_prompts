I've bought into the geometric rationality picture, and I want to know where it's actually exposed. The position, roughly: whenever the outcomes of your decisions compound multiplicatively over time -- wealth, obviously, but also career capital, skills, reputation, even health -- the right thing to maximize is the geometric mean / time-average growth rate, not expected value. So you size bets the Kelly way, you avoid any chance of ruin no matter the payoff, and ordinary risk aversion isn't a bias to be explained away but the shadow of this norm. The blog sequence I read even extends it to non-monetary stuff: since almost everything in life compounds, expected-value maximization is a kind of category error for beings who live through time rather than across an ensemble of copies of themselves. I find this really compelling and slightly worried I've talked myself into something unfalsifiable.

So: what would count against it? Not just the obvious "Kelly is only log-utility in disguise" complaint -- I want the full map of exposure. Which observations, arguments, or cases should move me? Things I half-know about: the single-shot objection (why care about time-averages if you only get one shot at a compounding process?), the claim that a Kelly bettor is way too timid about losing half their wealth in ways that seem psychologically and maybe normatively wrong, the point that real agents have background risks (uninsurable ones) that the clean ruin-avoidance story ignores, and something about how a team of bettors pooling money should each bet *less* than Kelly, which if true is awkward for the view being about rationality per se rather than about a particular utility function. There's also an old famous rebuttal written entirely in one-syllable words whose existence annoys me. And on my end, the biggest worry: since I've extended the idea to everything compounding (habits, projects, relationships), almost any cautious-seeming behavior can be redescribed as geometric optimization, and almost any bold behavior as the right play for some multiplicative process I haven't identified. That smells like insulation by flexibility.

And be honest about the insulation question: is a view of this kind *supposed* to be hard to falsify (like a decision theory, where you bite bullets), or is that a defect here? Reach some verdict on how exposed the view actually is.
