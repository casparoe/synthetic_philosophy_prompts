Okay so I just got home from a coffee thing with my friend D that turned into a THREE HOUR argument about commitment races and I'm still kind of annoyed so I want someone to actually adjudicate it in essay form. Background in case it's not your thing: the commitment races idea (it comes out of the AI safety / decision theory world, think schellingian bargaining meets agents that can self-modify or otherwise bind themselves) is that when agents can make binding commitments, there's pressure to commit early, because the first mover sets the bargaining landscape for everyone else. If I credibly commit to never backing down, you're stuck bargaining against a fixed wall. So everyone races to commit, and the worry is the race rewards speed over judgment — you lock in a position before you've thought through what you'd even want from the counterfactual where you'd deliberated longer. A rational agent might deliberately make itself worse at revising its plans, which seems like it should be irrational, except it wins the race.

That's the standard framing and we both agreed on it. Where we blew up was this move D makes that I've decided to call commitment laundering, though he calls it "reversible commitment stacking" to make it sound respectable. His claim: agents facing a race can defuse it by making lots of small, cheap, individually revocable commitments instead of a few big irreversible ones. The pattern of which ones you revoke and which you keep becomes public information, so you get most of the first-mover signal (other agents learn what you're like) without paying the irreversibility cost — the revocability itself is doing the work deliberation would otherwise do. He thinks this basically dissolves the races problem. I think it's sleight of hand: the whole reason a commitment moves the bargaining landscape is that it's *costly to back out of*, so a stack of revocable commitments is just... posturing, and the race re-erects itself one level up, as a race to make the revocation itself costly (committing about when you'll commit). We literally could not agree on whether the race just reappears at the meta level or genuinely bottoms out.

Write an essay that takes a side on this. I don't want a survey — I want you to actually adjudicate between "reversible stacking dissolves the race" and "the race just recurs at the meta level," and defend your verdict. Along the way it would help if you said something about whether early commitment is ever genuinely rational versus only rational-relative-to-other-committers, because I think that distinction is doing a lot of hidden work in our fight. Feel free to bring in whatever's relevant from bargaining theory, credible commitment, time consistency, etc., but don't just name-drop — use it.
