I've been drafting an argument for my political theory seminar defending lottocracy, and it has a way of running past its intended conclusion that I can't diagnose. Here it is:

(1) A decision-making body is legitimate only if it answers to the interests of the governed.
(2) It answers to the interests of the governed only if its members are descriptively like them — sharing their circumstances, incomes, education, and outlooks — since elite chambers systematically miss the interests of those unlike them.
(3) Electoral selection is structurally biased toward the wealthy, credentialed, and ambitious, so elected chambers fail condition (2).
(4) A fair lottery gives every citizen an equal chance of selection, so a lottery-selected chamber is representative of the population in the sense (2) requires.
(5) Representativeness comes in degrees, and a stratified lottery — sampling within categories like income, region, gender, and age so the chamber is guaranteed to match the population on each — is more representative than an unstratified one, because it removes the chance of a lopsided draw.
(6) Whatever makes stratification an improvement at step (5) also makes deliberate quota-filling an improvement on stratification: if matching the population on each category is what confers legitimacy, then matching it exactly, by hand, confers the most.
(7) Therefore the most legitimate decision-making body is not a lottocracy at all but a hand-assembled panel, chosen by officials from a pool of volunteers to mirror the population category by category.

Step (7) strikes me as giving away the game — the whole appeal of the lottery was that nobody gets to pick. But I can't find the flaw: each step looks like a modest improvement on the last, and each of the premises is one that sortition advocates themselves lean on somewhere. If I had to choose, I'd rather keep (1) and (2) and give up something later. Which step should I reject, and what do I pay for rejecting it? And is there a way to cut the argument earlier — at (2) or (4) — that doesn't land me back with elections?
