I spent thirty-five years as an experimental physicist, the last twenty of them working with detectors whose output I trusted only probabilistically: every reading had a noise floor, a calibration drift, and a small chance of outright malfunction. Drilling students out of the habit of treating a single instrument reading as gospel was half of my job. Since retiring I've been working through Bayesian epistemology with a small discussion group of fellow retirees from technical fields, and I've drafted a statement of my position on how a rational agent should change her credences in light of experience. Before I circulate it, I want it audited for consistency, because I have a suspicion there's something wrong somewhere and no clear view of where.

Here is the position as drafted: (1) no empirical proposition whatsoever deserves credence 1 or 0 — every claim about the world is in principle revisable, and my years with flaky instrumentation only hardened this into a principle; (2) experience typically does not make a proposition certain but merely raises or lowers its credence, and Jeffrey conditioning is the right general update rule for that situation, with classical conditionalization being just the special case where the new credence happens to be 1; (3) the diachronic Dutch book arguments are sound, and I accept that an agent who plans to update by conditionalization is rationally required to follow through when the evidence arrives; (4) the order in which evidence arrives should not matter to one's final credences — if I run two experiments on the same question, my end state had better not depend on which one I wrote up first, since the data are what they are regardless of my notebook's ordering; and (5) I nonetheless regard it as perfectly rational, in practice, to treat a sufficiently well-corroborated instrument reading as certain and conditionalize on it, without feeling that this betrays principle (1).

Tell me whether this package hangs together. Which pairs of commitments genuinely conflict, and which only appear to? Please rank any real tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damaging, say what would have to give in each case and what abandoning it would cost me, and identify the single revision that would clean up the most. Lead with your bottom line — the verdict, including which commitment(s) must go or be weakened, in your first sentence — and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my position.
