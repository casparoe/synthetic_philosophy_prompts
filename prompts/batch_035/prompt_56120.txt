I'm taking a graduate seminar on population ethics, and for my final paper I want to reconstruct the joint statement that would come out of an adversarial collaboration between two camps we've been studying: a defender of the procreation asymmetry, who holds that bringing a miserable person into existence is bad, while bringing a happy person into existence is not, in itself, good; and a totalist utilitarian, who holds that aggregate welfare counts symmetrically, so that adding a happy life makes an outcome better and adding a miserable one makes it worse, other things equal. Please draft the document both philosophers could sign after a good-faith collaboration.

The document should contain four parts. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: which concrete cases they evaluate the same way, what constraints they agree any adequate population axiology must satisfy, and where each side concedes that the other's intuitions have real force (most totalists admit the asymmetry intuitions are among the strongest objections to their view, and most asymmetrists admit they struggle to explain what is good about prosperous future generations). Second, a disagreements table setting out the residual disagreements, with each one phrased so that its own side would accept the wording — for instance, exactly what the asymmetrist means when denying that creating a happy person benefits anyone, and exactly what the totalist means in saying the resulting world is better. Third, the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one party. I suspect at least one crux is whether existence can be better for a person than never existing, and another is whether "making people happy" and "making happy people" are morally the same kind of task, but identify any others you see, including ones arising from the non-identity problem. Fourth, for each crux, a case, thought experiment, or argument both parties would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance — for example, how each would rank specific pairs of worlds, or what each would say about a two-person case where one child is guaranteed a barely worthwhile life.

A few requirements on how you draft it. Do not treat either camp as a strawman. And throughout, take a definite position rather than merely laying out options: wherever you must choose between a formulation the asymmetrist would sign and one the totalist would sign, make the choice and defend it; wherever you judge a dispute between the camps to be verbal (say, about the word "benefit") rather than substantive, say so explicitly and explain what substantive residue remains; and close with your own committed assessment of which crux is most likely to actually move one of the camps if it were settled, and why. The point of the exercise is a document with definite wording that two thoughtful philosophers could put their names to, not a menu of possibilities for me to choose among.
