I run a small online reading group that grew out of the rationalist blogosphere — the sort of crowd that came to causation through Pearl's do-calculus and causal graphs first, and only later worked backward into the philosophy literature. Over the past few months we've gone slowly through the classic theories: Lewis's counterfactual analysis, Woodward's interventionism, the structural-equation tradition running through Pearl and Halpern–Pearl, the agential and manipulability views of Menzies and Price, and the Salmon–Dowe process camp, with a running sidebar on Russell-style eliminativism about causation. The problem I've noticed is that our self-adopted labels have almost no diagnostic value: two of us who both say we're interventionists disagree about nearly every concrete case, and one member who claims Lewis's view has quietly abandoned the closest-possible-world machinery that made the view distinctive in the first place. We're sorting ourselves by which theory we like the sound of, not by what we actually believe.

So I'd like you to design a decision tree for us, to be posted as an interactive quiz on the group's blog. The tree should start from a commitment everyone in the group shares — that causation is more than constant conjunction — and each branch node should be a concrete case or question that a member could answer without knowing which theory it funnels them toward. The cases I want to see on the tree (add others if you think they do better splitting work): the preempted-backup scenario, where one assassin shoots a moment before a second would have, so the victim's death doesn't counterfactually depend on the first shot — was the first assassin nonetheless the cause?; absences and omissions, like the gardener who fails to water the plants; whether backtracking counterfactuals ("if the effect hadn't occurred, the cause wouldn't have either either") are ever the correct way to evaluate a counterfactual, or whether the dependence must run forward only; whether one can say what an intervention is without smuggling in causal vocabulary, and if not, whether that circularity is fatal or benign; whether a causal claim must be one an agent could in principle act on; and whether a cause must be connected to its effect by a spatiotemporally continuous process. The leaves should include Lewis's counterfactual theory, Woodward-style interventionism, structural-equation accounts in the Pearl/Halpern–Pearl lineage, Menzies–Price agentialism, the Salmon–Dowe process theory, and the eliminativist leaf, plus any logically available combinations of your node-answers that no camp occupies — for each unoccupied leaf, say whether you think it's empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Please also flag honestly any pair of leaves the tree cannot really separate; I suspect Woodward and Halpern–Pearl may be candidates, but tell me if I'm wrong, and if there's a further question that would split them, add it.

Two constraints on presentation. First, since our interest in causation is ultimately decision-theoretic — we care about it as the thing that supports acting on the world — include at least one node about whether a theory's causal judgments should be invariant under, or sensitive to, the perspective of a deliberating agent. Second, since a tree forces everyone into exactly one leaf, I want the writeup to be candid about where the forced choice distorts: which thinkers are awkwardly straddling branches, which node hides a continuum, which leaf is really two views wearing one name. Finally, a request about your own epistemic state: at the end, give an overall assessment of how confident you are in the shape of the tree, and single out the single placement you are least sure of — some node that might belong higher or lower in the tree, or some thinker whose position you're assigning to a leaf on contested grounds — so we know exactly where to push back when we take it apart in the next session.
