I'm in a philosophy of mind course and we just read Dennett's Where Am I? piece -- the one where his brain (Yorick) gets pulled out and parked in a vat in Houston while he goes off in his body (Hamlet) to deal with some radioactive stuff in Tulsa. What struck me in seminar is that Dennett's own verdict is kind of anti-climactic: he decides "where he is" is wherever his body is, not the lab where his brain sits. Like, he's standing outside the vat looking at his brain, and it still feels right to say he's there with the body, out in the world, and just has this weird causal detour in his head. Fair enough. But it got me thinking -- is that verdict forced by the setup, or is it an artifact of how Dennett rigged the information flow? In his story the brain gets all its info about the world through the body's senses in real time, and basically zero info about its own situation in the lab. Of course the point of view lands on the body then.

So here's what I want you to build: a variant of the brain-in-a-vat setup where the felt location flips to the brain's side. The person's sense of "here I am" should point at the vat, not the body -- the opposite of Dennett's conclusion -- while the body stays awake, sentient-ish, and out doing things far away. Constraints:

- no exotic physics, no telepathy, nothing a neuroscientist would laugh out of the room
- you can't cheat by making the body unconscious, numb, or a zombie -- it has to keep sensing and acting roughly normally
- and you can't just have the person TOLD where their brain is and reason their way to it; the flip has to happen at the level of where their point of view phenomenologically sits, the way Dennett's sits with his body without him needing an argument

The only lever I can see for doing this legitimately is messing with the information asymmetry (maybe timing, maybe richness/detail of the signals), but maybe you see another route. If you use timing or bandwidth, say so explicitly.

After the scenario, please do the walkthrough like our TA would want: (1) explain why the point of view ends up at the vat rather than the body, (2) identify which single feature of the setup is doing the work, (3) say what happens to the verdict if you remove just that feature -- does it collapse back to Dennett's original answer, and (4) give the best objection a Dennett-style philosopher would raise to claim the case is rigged or doesn't generalize (e.g., that you've gerrymandered the senses rather than moved the self). If you think the constraint set is actually unsatisfiable and Dennett wins no matter what, argue that instead -- but then you still owe me a diagnosis of WHY the body always wins, not just a restatement of his conclusion.

Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph, realistic-ish (a lab, not a trolley), and don't worry about it being publishable -- it's for my term paper, I mainly need the structure to be airtight.
