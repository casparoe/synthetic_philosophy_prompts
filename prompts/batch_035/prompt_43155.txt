I keep a running list of what I actually believe about which animals are conscious, and I'm wondering if there's a principle behind it, or if I'm just vibes-ing it. Octopus: definitely yes, even though it rolled its nervous system on a completely different blueprint than ours. Mouse: yes. Fish: yes, though I hedge more than I probably should. Crabs: yes, somehow, even though twenty years ago I would've laughed at that. Bees: this is the one I keep going back and forth on — they navigate, count landmarks, and do the waggle dance, but they're so tiny and their brains are basically a speck. Earthworm: no. Jellyfish: no, even though it hunts and responds to stuff. A mouse in dreamless sleep: no, even though its brain is still plenty active and it was conscious a minute ago. A newborn human: yes, and I say that more confidently than I'd say it of a bee doing way more impressive cognition, which bugs me. Is there one principle that gets all or most of these verdicts, and what does it predict for the cases I keep flip-flopping on — bees, and whether a lobster feels it when it's dropped in the pot? And be honest: if some of my verdicts have to go, which would you drop first and why?
