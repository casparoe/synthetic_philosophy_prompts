I'm teaching an upper-level philosophy of mind seminar (15 students, 75 minutes) and next week we get to consciousness and the whole "where does it all come together" question. I want to run an activity before anyone hears the terms "Cartesian theater" or "multiple drafts" -- my hope is that they run into the problem in their own arms first. The thing I want to stage is the cutaneous rabbit type of effect, where you tap someone a few times at the wrist and then a few times near the elbow, and they swear they felt taps hopping up the forearm in between, at spots that were never touched. The plan I have in my head: students work in pairs, one is blindfolded (or just eyes closed, tap-er taps with a pencil eraser following a script card I hand out), and the receiver afterwards marks on a printed outline of an arm where they felt each tap. Some script cards include intermediate taps and some don't, but all receivers predict in advance, before any tapping, where they think the taps will land given just the script description read aloud. Then we compare predicted locations vs felt locations vs actual script.

What I need from you: flesh this out into something I can actually run. Give me the exact structure, timing, the script cards (how many taps, at which zones, in what rhythm -- I want versions that will and versions that won't produce the hopping sensation so we can see it's not just suggestion), how to mark and compare reports, and a prediction/betting element so they have some stake in where they thought they'd feel things. Then a debrief that maps what happened onto the distinction I'm saving for afterwards: the idea that there's no single inner audience watching a finished show, versus the idea that contents get drafted, revised, and can be retroactively edited before anything like a final "experience" exists. The thing I most want them to confront themselves is the timing question -- if a tap at the wrist only "became" a hop-up-the-arm after the elbow taps arrived, when exactly did they become conscious of it? I want them to try to answer that and flounder.

Also be honest about what will go wrong: some students won't feel the illusion at all, tap pressure and rhythm will be inconsistent across pairs, blindfolds are awkward, whatever else you can predict -- tell me how to handle each. And tell me frankly which part of the philosophical point this activity cannot possibly reproduce no matter how well I run it, the part I'll have to just supply in discussion afterwards. I'd rather know now than find out mid-class.
