I am a novelist, and somewhere in the course of writing my current book I have talked myself into a position about the Ship of Theseus that I now want stress-tested. The novel concerns a fishing village that rebuilds its harbor boat plank by plank over fifty years, and at some point the shipwright's daughter starts saving the old planks in a shed. The puzzle was supposed to be texture; it has become a conviction.

Here is the position. There is no fact of the matter about which ship is the Ship of Theseus, and there is no fact of the matter because "ship" is not a natural kind: artifacts get their identity conditions from our practices of use, repair, record, and storytelling, not from some physical relation that could simply be read off the world. On this view, the question "which ship is it?" is always shorthand for "which ship does our practice of tracking pick out here?" — and where the practice goes silent, as in Hobbes's version with the reassembled planks lying at the bottom of the harbor, the question is not merely hard but empty. I want to go further than this, though, and this is where I suspect I'm exposed. I want to say that fictional characters show the view is not mere conventionalism: Sherlock Holmes is as practice-dependent an object as the village boat, and yet there seem to be determinate facts about him — that he lived on Baker Street rather than, say, in Edinburgh — which no reader or author can simply decide otherwise. So practices don't invent their objects wholesale; they can be wrong about what they have made. I hold, roughly, that artifacts occupy this same position: made by our practices, but not ours to reshuffle at will once made.

Tell me what would count against this. Not the objections I've heard a hundred times — not just "so nothing survives replacement?" — but the ones with teeth: which cases, which arguments, and which neighboring results about persistence, kinds, or fiction the view stands or falls with. Where does the analogy between an artifact and a fictional character first crack? What would a committed defender of my view concede as genuinely counting against it, and what would they dismiss as missing the point? Could the view be true of the boat but false of a nation, a marriage, or a person — and if it cannot be extended that far, is that a bug or merely a scope limit? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, be honest about which ones I should actually be losing sleep over, and tell me whether you think the position has quietly been qualified into something unfalsifiable — and if it has, whether that is a problem for a view of this kind.
