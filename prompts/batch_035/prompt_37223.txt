I've been keeping a list of my gut verdicts on little everyday honesty cases, trying to figure out whether there's an actual principle behind them or whether I'm just making it up as I go. Here's the list.

My Dutch coworker looked over my slide deck and told me straight up that section three was confusing and the chart was unreadable. She didn't ask first, no softening. Verdict: fine, honestly kind of her.

My aunt asked if I liked the sweater she knitted me. I said I loved it. I don't. Fine.

The same aunt later asked whether I thought she should sell her house and move nearer my cousin. I told her I genuinely thought she should think hard about the winters there, which is true and not what she wanted to hear. Fine, and I'd have felt wrong smoothing that one over.

A friend asked if her business plan was any good. I said it had real potential. It doesn't, really. Not fine — this one bugs me.

A different friend asked if her new haircut suited her. I said yes. It... does not. And yet my gut says fine?? I can't explain why this feels different from the business plan one but it clearly does and it's embarassing.

At a dinner in Osaka my host asked if I'd enjoyed the long tea ceremony. I said it had been a wonderful experience. I was bored out of my skull. Fine.

A guy I'd just met at a party told me, completely unprompted, that my French accent was mediocre and I should drill my vowels. Not fine, even though it was true, and even though it was exactly the kind of thing my Dutch coworker would have said without blinking.

So that's my track record. My question: is there one principle that gets all seven? The obvious candidates each seem to die on one case — 'unsolicited bluntness is presumptuous' can't handle the coworker, 'white lies are fine when the stakes are low' gets tangled between the sweater and the haircut somehow (or does it?), 'bluntness is fine when it's useful' arguably makes the party guy fine since the advice was useful. So please tell me: what principle would you extract from these verdicts, which of my verdicts can't it accommodate and would have to go, and what does it predict for the case I keep flip-floping on — my sister asked me, ten minutes before walking out the door to a job interview, whether she seemed nervous. She did, very. I said she seemed fine. Was that the sweater case or the party guy case? And is there some further case, one I havent listed, that would tell me whether the real principle is about the speaker's standing or about what the listener can do with the truth?
