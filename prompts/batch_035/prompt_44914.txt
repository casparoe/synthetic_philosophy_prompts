I'm a PhD student in cognitive science, working on a dissertation chapter about self-models, and before my committee meeting I want to test whether the position I have drifted into over the past year is internally stable. My background sympathy is with the strange-loop picture of the self, roughly in the spirit of Hofstadter's I Am a Strange Loop, but the claims below are my own working formulation. Here is what I actually believe:

(1) A self is not a substance or an owner of experiences but a self-referential, self-updating pattern — a loop that takes itself as part of its own content — and such a pattern is substrate-independent in the way any structure is: it is realized in whatever implements it faithfully.

(2) These loops are not confined to one skull. My partner and my closest friends run generative models of me that partially constitute my "I": when they anticipate my reactions, complete my sentences, or act in my terms, part of me is running on their hardware. These are not merely representations of me; they are components of me.

(3) Survival is continuity of the loop, in degrees. What matters for whether I persist into next year, or through some intervention, is how much of the pattern continues, not which physical organ realizes it.

(4) Special prudential concern — the rational permission to weight my own future over everyone else's — is grounded in precisely that continuity, and its strength should track the degree of continuity.

(5) That said, I hold that prudential concern is a relation between me and my own future instances only: I have no self-interested stake, as opposed to an affectionate one, in whether my friends continue to model me well after my death. I also accept the standard view that posthumous wrongs — slander of the dead, exploitation of their likeness — are wrongs to the living or to norms, but never to the dead subject, since there is no subject left to be harmed.

(6) Desert and responsibility attach to the individual agent and its actions. If my friend, running part of my loop, does something out of character for her and in character for me, that is her act, and she answers for it, not I.

(7) I would sign up for destructive uploading tomorrow given a faithful scan: the upload continues the loop better than my aging brain will, so refusing would be valuing the substrate over the self.

My worry is that some subset of these cannot all be true, but whenever I try to localize the clash it moves on me. Which pairs genuinely conflict, which merely look as though they do, and how severe is each conflict? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, indicate what would have to give in each case, and then tell me which single revision would resolve the most at the lowest cost — and what I would be giving up in making it.
