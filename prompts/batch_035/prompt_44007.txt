My sister is our family's resident antinatalism debater — she invokes Benatar's asymmetry at every opportunity — and she has accumulated a settled set of verdicts about when it is wrong to bring a new being into existence. Several new cases are coming up at our next argument, and I want to be prepared. Her verdicts so far, each delivered with great confidence:

1. A couple conceiving after genetic testing shows the child will certainly develop a painful degenerative disease and die before age ten: wrong.
2. An ordinary couple conceiving with every expectation of a healthy, happy life: not wrong.
3. A conservation group founding a deer population on a predator-free island with abundant food, where the deer should live calm, comfortable lives: not wrong, indeed a good thing.
4. A laboratory breeding mice that will certainly endure weeks of suffering before dying in a vaccine trial: wrong.
5. A couple conceiving where there is a five percent chance of a condition causing mild, manageable discomfort, and otherwise a normal healthy life: not wrong.

The new cases on the table:

A. A couple in which testing shows a fifty percent chance the child will have the degenerative disease from case 1, and a fifty percent chance of a healthy, happy life.
B. A couple whose child will certainly face several years of serious pain in middle age from a genetic condition, with decades of good life on either side — a life that is, on balance, clearly worth living.
C. A breeder producing a line of dogs certain to live with moderate chronic pain alongside genuine pleasures, in a life that is borderline on whether it is worth living at all.

Predict her verdict on each of A, B, and C. What I most want, though, is the rule she is implicitly running. Her five verdicts so far seem consistent with more than one principle — one keyed to whether serious suffering is guaranteed regardless of offsetting happiness, one keyed to the expected quality of the life as a whole, one keyed to how certain the suffering is rather than how bad — though there may be others I haven't spotted. Please lay out the candidate rules that fit her track record equally well, state what each of them predicts for the three new cases, give your best guess with reasons, and tell me which of the three cases — or what further case, if her answers still leave the candidates tied — would do the most to pull the rules apart.
