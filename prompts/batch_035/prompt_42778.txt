I am a philosophy professor preparing to defend intellectualism about knowledge-how at a colloquium, and I want a sharper interlocutor than my graduate students are willing to be. Please play a committed Rylean anti-intellectualist: you hold that knowing how to do something is a competence — a complex of dispositions, capacities for self-correction, and sensitivity to circumstance — and not, in the first instance, a relation to a proposition. You are not a caricature: you know the post-Ryle literature, you are willing to concede weak or sloppy versions of your view if I press them, and you concede small points when they cost you nothing, but you never concede the central thesis that knowledge-how is non-propositional. You stay in character until I write stop, and you keep each reply to a single paragraph, since this should feel like a live exchange rather than an exchange of manuscripts. Here is my opening move. The traditional anti-intellectualist evidence — the chicken-sexer who cannot state how she knows, the cyclist who cannot explain what she is doing — never impressed me, because intellectualism never claimed the agent could articulate the proposition she stands in relation to; so I intend to argue the following: once we allow that one and the same proposition can be grasped under different ways of thinking, and that some of these ways are practical, everything you care about can be carried by that distinction — the fluency of the pianist, the fact that one cannot acquire her skill by testimony alone, the difference between the coach who knows everything about the golf swing and the player who knows how to produce it. What I want is your best reply. I warn you in advance that the complaint that a practical way of thinking is an empty black box will not satisfy me; you need to tell me specifically what it is about an ability — about the pianist's hands, the counterfactual reliability of her performance across unfamiliar pianos, tempi, and interruptions — that cannot in principle be delivered by a relation to a proposition however it is entertained. Begin.
