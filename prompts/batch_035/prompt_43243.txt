so I run a monthly philosophy night at a bar — real mixed crowd, a couple of people who took one phil course a decade ago, mostly just curious adults, and next month I drew the topic off the hat: "is it better never to have existed?" What I actually want to get them to is the procreation asymmetry: the thought that there's a reason not to create a life that will go badly, but no corresponding reason to create a life that will go well — so bringing a happy person into existence is permitted but not required, in a way that doesn't seem to have a parallel anywhere else in ethics. Plan the session for me, don't just explain the view.

I get 45 minutes and this crowd will tolerate pure abstraction for maybe ten before someone checks their phone. Walk me through the order the pieces have to come in: I'm guessing they need the distinction between making an existing person's life go better and bringing into being an additional person whose life goes well, before the asymmetry even sounds like a claim rather than a typo — but tell me if thats the wrong first move, or if something else has to come even earlier. Then the carrying example: I want one concrete scenario I can keep coming back to all night, not possible-world talk. I've been considering a couple deciding whether to have a second child, one who'd predictably be happy — is the second kid's future happiness a reason to have her, in the same way her older sibling's happiness is a reason to feed him? Tell me if that's the wrong carrier and what would work better, and what the example has to have and has to lack to do its job.

Predict the misconceptions in the order they'll actually arrive, and where in the sequence each one needs to be headed off. I already know one regular will say "so you're saying nobody should have kids" within five minutes, someone else will insist that a world with a billion extra happy people is obviously better and that anyone who disagrees just hates humanity, and there's always a person who hears "no reason to create happy lives" as "happy lives are worthless," which is not the same thing and will wreck the room if I dont catch it. Also: what should I openly refuse to get into at this level? My instinct is the repugnant conclusion and the whole non-identity problem are both one-way doors, but you tell me.

And end with one question I can put to the room that would show whether they actually got the asymmetry — something where the right answer is "the two cases are disanalogous in exactly this respect" and not just a feeling. fyi by the last ten minutes several of them have had a couple drinks, so the close has to be robust to that.
