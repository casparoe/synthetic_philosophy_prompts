Heres a question I've been chewing on. In the color phi phenomenon, you show someone two stationary spots of light in the same place, one after the other — classically red then green — and they report seeing a single dot moving, changing color somewhere in the middle. But there was never any dot in the middle, and never any color change at a location. Dennett contrasts two ways to describe this: the "Orwellian" story, where the subject never consciously saw the motion at all and only has a false memory of it, written over the real experience moments later; and the "Stalinesque" story, where the brain holds the second flash back, fabricates the middle of the trajectory, and only then lets the whole thing become conscious, so the experience of motion was genuinely had, just later than you'd think. So here's my question: what's your actual credence that the Stalinesque (or broadly postdictive, backdated-consciousness) picture is basically right about phi specifically — i.e. that the motion-and-color-change experience is constructed after the second flash and arrives in consciousness as a package, rather than being a false memory of an experience that never occurred? Don't give me "it depends" — I want a number and the decomposition behind it.

My guess is the terms at least include: (1) the probability that there even is a determinate fact of the matter about when a brief experience occurs — Dennett's own line is that the Orwell/Stalin question is confused because there's no finished "show" in the brain, so if that's right, the main question never gets off the ground; (2) the probability that the phi report requires an actual motion representation constructed in the brain, versus being confabulation off the two discrete flashes plus a bias in how people describe stuff; (3) the probability that postdictive effects demonstrated in vision lab setups tell us anything about experience outside the lab, versus being artifacts of weird timing and unusual stimuli. Weight those however you want, add terms I've missed, give one line of justification each, then combine them into your number.

Two complications I want folded in, not tacked on. First, the cutaneous rabbit: taps at the wrist, then near the elbow, then the shoulder, and subjects report feeling taps "hopping up" the forearm, at locations where nothing was ever tapped, before the elbow tap was even delivered. Does the fact that postdiction apparently works across different body parts and a longer timescale raise or lower your credence for the Stalinesque story about phi specifically, and why? Second, a stipulated experiment — assume for the sake of argument it's been run clean, no need to wonder if it really happened: subjects in a phi setup are asked to press a button the instant they first become aware of any color change, and the presses reliably land somewhere between the two flashes, systematically earlier than any backdated fill-in should be able to explain. Does that result sink the Stalinesque view outright, or just show that the button press and the after-the-fact verbal report are tapping different drafts, so the Stalinesque advocate has an out?
