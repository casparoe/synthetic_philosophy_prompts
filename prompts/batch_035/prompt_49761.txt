My friend Maya and I just had a three-hour argument over dinner about moral relativism and I need to stress test my own side now because she planted some doubts that won't go away. For context, I'm not defending the "anything goes" strawman she kept attacking. My view is more like: moral truth is relative in roughly the way motion is relative to a frame of reference. There's no frame-free fact about whether you're moving, and I want to say there's no framework-free fact about whether, say, honor killings are wrong -- but within a framework there's a fully real fact, just as within a frame there's a real fact about your velocity. So I can say lots of moral claims are true, full stop, as long as "true" is silently indexed to the framework doing the saying.

Tell me what would actually count against this. Not the caricature version, this version. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I especially want the ones Maya raised, because I answered them at the time but I'm not sure I answered them well:

1. The reformer problem. If rightness is fixed by the framework, how do I make sense of people like abolitionists, who we normally think of as seeing a truth their own society was getting wrong? Was Frederick Douglass just resolving an inconsistency within his framework, like debugging? That feels like it loses something important about what he was doing but I can't say what exactly.

2. The reflexive problem, which Maya saved for last and which kept me up. If moral claims are framework-relative, what about the claim "moral claims are framework-relative"? Is metaethetics exempt somehow, and can that exemption be principled rather than convenient? If its not exempt, my own view is just one more frame's opinion, and why should Maya care?

3. Frame individuation. What even counts as one framework? My society, my subculture, my family, me personally? The view seems to slide toward subjectivism or toward global majoritarianism depending on where I draw the line, and I don't have a principled place to draw it.

4. Her sharpest point I think: she noticed I kept slipping into saying things like "so the Nazis were wrong, obviously" without the index, and when she flagged it I said everyone slips, its just pragmatics. Is that an honest defence or am I insulating the view with patches every time it fails to say what I actually want to say?

For each defeater, tell me whether a committed relativist of my kind would concede it counts against the view or would absorb it with some amendment -- and which absorptions are legitimate philosophy versus just epicycles. And at the end I want your honest verdict on whether my position, once its been qualified enough to survive everything above, still predicts or rules out anything at all, and whether that unfalsifiability is a problem for a view of this kind or whether metaethical views generally get a pass there. I'd also like to hear if there's a defeater neither of us thought of that's worse than all of these. Don't hold my hand, I can take "your view is dead" as an answer if thats where it lands.
