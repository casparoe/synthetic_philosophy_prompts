For thirty years I worked as a stratigrapher, mostly on Quaternary deposits, and since retiring I've been following the debate over formalizing the Anthropocene with a mixture of professional interest and unease. There is a pattern in that debate I don't have a word for, and I'd like to know whether philosophy has one.

On paper the dispute is ordinary stratigraphy: a working group has to pick a boundary and a marker — a golden spike in a core or section — and the candidates are the usual ones, from an early-Holocene onset associated with the spread of agriculture, through mid-nineteenth-century industrial signals, to the mid-twentieth-century radionuclide and carbon spikes. But the choice of date tracks the chooser's view of what humanity ought to be held responsible for far more closely than the evidence does. Colleagues who want industrial capitalism indicted prefer the industrial dates; those who think the whole enterprise is a way of blaming the present favor the earliest onset, on which we have been geological agents for millennia and nothing special happened recently. Nobody is faking the data; each side argues in good stratigraphic faith. But the empirical markers are flexible enough to accommodate any of the preferred dates, and what fills the gap is a moral or political attitude toward the present. The classification presents itself as a purely empirical boundary decision while its content is quietly fixed by what rides on it practically.

Is there an established concept for this configuration? I have come across talk of essentially contested concepts and of persuasive definitions, but neither seems right: the concept itself is not the prize being fought over, and nobody is revising a word's meaning — they are selecting among genuinely defensible empirical criteria, with the selection driven from elsewhere. If there is a name, I'd like to know how the phenomenon differs from those nearby ideas; if there is not, please define and name it carefully enough to distinguish it from both, and say where its edges are — for instance, whether it still counts when the practical stakes only bias rather than determine the choice.
