At a recent meetup of the rationalist group I've been part of for years, a discussion about wireheading drifted into whether pleasure is even one thing, and two regulars got locked into a genuine argument. (1) Maya said the whole wireheading dystopia presupposes that pleasures are commensurable, but that presupposition looks false: the pleasure of finally seeing the trick in a proof, the burn of a good chili, being moved by a piece of music, a hot shower after a cold hike — these don't seem to share any phenomenal property, so the word 'pleasure' is a ragbag label, and 'maximize pleasure' is about as coherent a goal as 'maximize stuff.' (2) Jonas replied that the neuroscience of wanting versus liking — the finding that 'liking' reactions can be traced to a small set of hedonic hotspots in the brain, dissociated from the dopamine systems that handle craving — suggests pleasure is a natural kind after all, a common currency the brain actually deals in. (3) Maya said that at best shows rats' reaction to sweetness is a natural kind; the class the neuroscientists study is far narrower than the class the hedonist needs, and if human aesthetic absorption involves sadness and loss, pointing to shared hotspots just relocates the heterogeneity rather than explaining it away. (4) Jonas countered that the heterogeneity is all in the objects and accompaniments, not the pleasantness itself: every one of those episodes has a common core, a felt 'more of this, please' quality — the tears at the symphony are pleasant tears, and we can tell because people seek them out. (5) Maya answered that 'seek out' is a test of wanting, and Jonas himself had insisted wanting and liking come apart — the person who craves the burn or puts on the song that makes them sob may want the overall package without every component being pleasant, so by his own favorite neuroscience his test for the common core is broken. Neither would budge, and as a bystander I genuinely couldn't referee. Please adjudicate the exchange step by step: who is right at each move, and what, if anything, are both of them missing?
