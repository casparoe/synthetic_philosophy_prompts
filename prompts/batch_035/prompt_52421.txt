At a development workshop last week, a discussant dismissed a paper on disease ecology and economic outcomes with a wave of the hand and the words "institutions matter," delivered as though it were a knockdown reply — as if naming the favored cause ruled the rival one out. I'm a third-year PhD student in political economy, and I left the room uneasy, because I have heard that same four-word phrase invoked to do a striking range of different jobs: to summarize a correlation, to assert a causal priority over geography and culture, to justify conditioning aid on constitutional reform, and, on at least one occasion, to imply that any non-institutional explanation is not merely incomplete but somehow conceptually confused.

My suspicion is that "institutions matter" compresses several logically distinct theses into one slogan, and that the discussant's dismissal only goes through on some of them. But I can't yet articulate the menu cleanly, and I need to before I respond to the referee report that made the same move. Could you lay out the different precise claims the phrase could be expressing? I'd find it useful if you would, for each candidate reading, classify it as a platitude (true but incapable of settling anything), a substantive and contestable thesis, or a claim that is probably false — and then, most importantly, tell me which of the readings would actually license using the phrase to dismiss a geography-based explanation outright.

A few distinctions I suspect belong in the menu, though I'm not sure how to carve them: "matters" as in figures somewhere in every adequate causal story, versus is the dominant proximate driver of variation, versus is the fundamental determinant that shaped the other candidates; "matters" as an explanatory claim about why nations diverged historically, versus an interventional claim about which lever policy can actually pull; and the claim that institutions are an independent cause versus the much weaker claim that they are a channel through which deeper forces operate. The endogeneity worry seems central here: the influential cross-country arguments that treat institutions as causally prior typically lean on historical episodes meant to break the entanglement with geography and culture, and whether one finds that identification strategy convincing may be exactly where the slogan's work gets done quietly. I'd like the readings spelled out in enough detail that I can see which ones the discussant could defend and which ones he would have to abandon, and also your view on which reading people who repeat the phrase most often actually intend — and whether that is the reading they can support. Please lay this out as a structured list I can work from in revising my response to the referee.
