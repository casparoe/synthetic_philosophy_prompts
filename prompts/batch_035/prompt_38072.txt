I teach an upper-level seminar on formal epistemology, ten students, one 80-minute session. These are the kids who can churn through Bayes factor exercises in their sleep and who've already survived the Monty Hall unit without crying, so standard demos bounce off them. This week we covered Jeffrey conditioning — updating on evidence you're not certain of, rigidity, the whole deal — and I could just tell it went in one ear and out the other. When I asked whether you should treat a blurry glimpse of a card the same as a clean look at it, most of them shrugged yes, and one said something like "it's still evidence, so just condition on it." That's the exact mistake I want to kill.

Design an in-class betting game that makes them feel why it's a mistake. The idea I keep having is some kind of noisy observation setup — a card flashed for a fraction of a second, or a confederate "witness" who tells the truth only 70% of the time, or dice read through a bad phone camera — where students have to commit to bets on the underlying proposition, and the ones who update as if the evidence were clean systematically bleed points to the ones who weight it by its reliability. But you're the designer, feel free to throw my idea out. I want concrete rules, materials I can prep in an afternoon, a payoff scheme that doesn't involve real money but stings anyway, and a timeline that actually fits in one session with the debrief included.

For the debrief: it should map specific moments in the game onto specific parts of the formalism — where the 70%-reliability enters the update, which beliefs should stay put (rigidity) and when the game quietly violates that assumption, what happens when students start doubting the reliability figure itself. Also please be honest about what a game like this just cannot reproduce, so I know what I have to supply by hand. And tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — my classes always have one kid who refuses to bet anything, and another who tries to game the noise channel instead of learning anything.
