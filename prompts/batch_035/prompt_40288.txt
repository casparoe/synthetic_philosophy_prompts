Covering econ for a magazine means I end up with a lot of half-digested views about what "utility" even is, and my editor recently poked at one of them and now I'm worried the whole package doesn't hang together. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) When economists say a consumer prefers x to y, that should just mean the consumer picks x when both are affordable. There's no extra inner mental fact that the choice is reporting on — preference talk is shorthand for choice dispositions, nothing more.

(2) Utility is purely ordinal. Asking how many "utils" a sandwich gives you is a category error, and comparing utility across people is meaningless.

(3) I'm fine with expected utility theory — putting numbers on outcomes and weighting by probabilities doesn't secretly sneak cardinal utility back in. I'm pretty sure. (This one has always felt slightly wobbly next to (2) but I've never worked out whether it should.)

(4) The utility functions in macro models, the ones with the fixed elasticities and so on, are picked for the math being tractable, not fit to any actual choice data — and I've written that this is fine, they're just modeling tools, and it doesn't undercut anything.

(5) I believe in adaptive preferences — someone who's grown up with nothing can genuinely, sincerely want things that are bad for them, and I've argued in print that this sometimes justifies policy overriding what people actually choose.

(6) Sending $1000 to a poor family rather than a rich one is better, full stop, and I'd defend that as a claim about welfare, not just as my aesthetic preference for equality.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally damning, and for each one say what would have to give and what giving it up costs. Also flag any pair that only looks like a tension but dissolves once you think it through — I suspect there's at least one of those. I honestly don't know which of my commitments I'd drop, so don't pick one for me, just map the damage.
