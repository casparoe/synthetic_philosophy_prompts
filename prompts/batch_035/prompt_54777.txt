My brother left his copy of Ecce Homo at my place, and out of pure boredom I ended up reading the "Why I Am So Clever" section, where Nietzsche goes on at length about what and when you should eat, where you should live, climate, recreation, and how sitting as little as possible is essential to thinking well. Kind of a weird thing to read while hungry. Anyway I mentioned it at dinner (over my sad plate of white rice) and my two friends got into it, and I couldnt fully follow who was winning.

Maya, who's halfway through a philosophy PhD, said the diet stuff isn't an embarrassing footnote but the philosophy itself: by the late work, Nietzsche thinks your moral and intellectual judgments are symptoms of your physiology, so going on about your meals is just doing his philosophy consistently -- revaluing values all the way down to breakfast. Dev, who's a dietitian and allergic to grandiosity, said that's backwards: this is a chronically ill guy with migraines and a wrecked stomach mistaking his own n=1 body for the human condition, dressed up with standard 19th-century health faddism, and the fact that he keeps insisting everything is true only of HIS body just proves it can't be philosophy, since philosophy is supposed to say something general.

Maya answered that the singularity IS the doctrine: prescribing one menu for all of humanity is exactly the herd-morality move, the ascetic priest thing to do, so refusing to generalize is the point, not a bug. Dev said that's a get-out-of-jail-free card -- whenever one of the rules sounds silly (the bit about sitting, the opinions on tea and coffee) it's "intentionally personal," and whenever it sounds profound it's philosophy; you can't lose an argument you've rigged that way, and a genuine experiment on yourself has to have some way of failing. Maya's last move was that Dev is proving her case: dismissing it all as biography is itself a value judgment, the very move Nietzsche diagnoses -- deciding in advance that bodies and digestion aren't serious subjects for thought.

Can you go through the exchange move by move and tell me who's right at each step, whether one of them comes out more right overall, and what (if anything) both of them are missing? I'd honestly like to know whether I should finish the book or just finish the diet.
