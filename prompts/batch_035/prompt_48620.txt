so here's my situation: I'm a science journalist (physics and cosmology beat, occasional math) and I've been asked to write a personal-essay-ish sidebar for a package we're doing on whether math is discovered or invented. Started drafting it and realized my own position is a mess, and I'd rather find out from you than embarrass myself in print. heres what I actually believe, as near as I can tell:

1. I'm a pretty hardened scientific realist. When our best theories need some entity to make the explanations work — electrons back in the day, maybe dark matter now — I think that's a real reason to believe in the thing, not just a bookkeeping convenience.

2. But I do NOT believe in numbers as objects. All this platonist stuff about a timeless realm of abstract entities that we somehow perceive... no. I'm not sure I can even say what that would mean without my physics-writer brain gagging.

3. Despite (2), I think mathematicians discover things rather than invent them. When the Monster group was found, it wasn't authored, it was sitting there. And if a proof has a gap, then it was wrong all along, even before anyone noticed the gap — nobody gets to make it right by fiat.

4. I think 2+2=4 isn't just true but had to be true, and would still have been true if humans had never evolved. (This one bugs me the most when I think about it too long.)

5. I think Wigner's old point about the uncanny effectiveness of math in physics is a genuinely deep puzzle that demands an explanation, and I get annoyed when people wave it off.

6. I think the continuum hypothesis has a real answer, whether or not anyone ever knows it — it's not that it became true or false based on what axioms we feel like adopting.

7. People keep telling me that believing math is knowable a priori — no experiments needed — is inconsistent with my general naturalism, since for me everything else is ultimately known through experience. I've never seen the contradiction, frankly.

8. Oh and one more: I think math is a human cultural practice, like a language, that we built up over millennia — and I also think that has basically nothing to do with whether mathematical claims are true, which I hold to be as objective as anything in physics.

So: is this package consistent? Where I'd especially like your help is figuring out which of these are actually pulling against each other and which only look like they conflict, because honestly I can't tell anymore, and I don't want to give up any of them if I don't have to. Rank whatever tensions you find by severity, say for each one what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me, and if there's a single revision that would clean up the most, tell me which. Don't go easy on me — the piece runs with my name on it.
