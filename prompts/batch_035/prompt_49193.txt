I got into this after reading about the Pasadena game — where the good and bad outcomes alternate and shrink in a way that makes the infinite series converge only conditionally, so that merely reordering the outcomes changes the "expected value" to anything you like. Once I saw that, I started noticing the problem everywhere I looked: St. Petersburg variants, heavy-tailed payoff distributions in real forecasting, charitable cost-effectiveness estimates where the tails dominate, and ethical theories that just add up welfare across lives and assume the sum means something.

Since I only have a few evenings a week and no access to the academic literature beyond what leaks onto forums, I need to choose what to think about carefully. So here is what I want from you: a prioritized list of the open questions in this general area — the Pasadena problem and its relatives, undefined or order-dependent expectations, and what any of it implies for how a bounded, mortal agent should actually choose — ordered by which ones most deserve my attention. For each question, tell me why it comes before the others: which questions are upstream of which (e.g., does whether tails can be legitimately truncated depend on settling anything about aggregation first?), which are tractable for someone who can follow calculus and some analysis but not measure theory, and which are interesting-but-downstream — worth understanding but not worth grinding on myself because nothing else hangs on them.

Please commit to an actual ordering rather than surveying the field neutrally, and at the end tell me honestly what would change your mind about your top choice: which argument, consideration, or thought experiment, if it worked, would promote a different question to first place — and where in your reasoning you think that's most likely to happen.
