I have to write a character reference for my friend Elena's custody hearing next month, and a psych-major friend of mine has me half convinced I'd basically be writing fiction. Over beers he laid this out:

(1) Having a character trait — honesty, kindness, reliability — means being disposed to act that way consistently across situations, so the trait should be the best predictor of your behavior.
(2) In the classic studies, it isn't: Milgram's subjects kept shocking people when told to, Darley and Batson's seminarians stepped around a slumped victim mostly depending on whether they were in a hurry, and Hartshorne and May found that kids who cheated on one test mostly didn't cheat on the next.
(3) If situational variables predict behavior better than any trait does, there's no evidence the trait exists.
(4) So global character traits basically don't exist, and nobody is "a caring person" in the sense virtue ethics needs — he says this is what kills virtue ethics as a moral theory, not just letter-writing.
(5) So my letter would assert an empirically refuted psychology to a judge.

Then I went home and noticed the argument seems to prove way too much. Beliefs are also supposed to be stable dispositions — if you believe smoking causes cancer, you judge that way across contexts. But framing studies show people's judgments flip with irrelevant wording, and order effects change what people conclude from the same evidence. Run steps (1)-(3) on beliefs instead of traits and you get that nobody beleives anything — including my friend's belief in the studies he just cited me. I texted him this and he didn't have an answer.

So which step is the bad one? Or is the argument okay and something else has to give — maybe the letter only needs a weaker claim than step (4) ever touches? I'd like to keep enough to honestly tell the judge that Elena is reliable, since five years of her showing up says she is. And if I've mangled what the studies actually show, or the philosophy, just tell me that straight — don't be polite about it.
