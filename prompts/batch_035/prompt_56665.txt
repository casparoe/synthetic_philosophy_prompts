I've spent way too many evenings down a rabbit hole on how philosophers explain causation, and I want to run something past you because I genuinely can't tell anymore whether there's a real disagreement here or just people talking past each other.

The way I understand it: the counterfactual people (Lewis and that crowd) say A causes B when, if A hadn't happened, B wouldn't have either. Fine. Then there's the interventionist bunch, Pearl and Woodward types, who say you understand causation by asking what happens when you reach in and wiggle A while holding everything else fixed. Also fine, on the surface. But from what I can gather the two camps have been sniping at eachother for decades and nobody converts. The counterfactual side gets wrecked by preemption cases (the backup shooter stuff, where B would've happened anyway through another route, so the counterfactual comes out false but we still want to say the first shooter caused it), and the interventionists get told they can't say anything about things nobody can actually intervene on, like your sex or the gravitational constant, plus their whole setup just quietly helps itself to the same counterfactuals it was supposed to replace.

Here's my amateur theory, and I want you to take it seriously but also to take it apart: I suspect the fight never gets resolved because the two sides aren't doing the same job at all. One side is doing metaphysics, asking what causation IS in the world, and the other is doing methodology, building a toolkit for scientists and statisticians to find causal structure in data. If that's right, they only look like opponents because they both use the word cause, and the stalled debate is basically an accident of vocabulary. But I might be missing something, since the interventionists do seem to make claims that sound metaphysical when you push them.

So diagnose it for me. Is it a verbal dispute, two different projects under one name, or a genuine substantive fight I'm underestimating? Use what the sides actually say, not a history lecture.
