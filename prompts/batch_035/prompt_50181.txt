I'm a physicist by training, and for the last couple years I've been reading philosophy of time as a hobby, which is how I ended up stuck on the block universe debate. You know the argument from special relativity, the one people usually trace back to Rietdijk and Putnam in the sixties: two observers moving relative to each other slice spacetime into "nows" at different angles, and for one of them some distant event is in their present while for the other it's still future or already past, and by chaining observers like that you can connect basically any two events, so the conclusion is supposed to be that everything is equally real and there's no privileged global now — presentism is dead, QED. But then the presentists fire back and it goes around again. Some say fine, there's a privileged foliation we just can't detect it (the neo-Lorentzian move), some point at cosmological time as a foliation that's sitting there for the taking, and some have the subtler reply that "real relative to an observer" just isn't a coherent thing to say — reality isn't the kind of thing that takes a frame as an argument, so the whole arguement has a typing error baked into it. And the eternalists answer, and the wheel turns. Everyone has been looking at the same equations for a hundred years.

My working theory is that this was never a physics disagreement at all, and the sides differ about what the word "now" is supposed to do: whether it's an indexical like "here," which carves the world no more deeply than my location carves the Earth, or whether it's an ontological fence between what exists and what doesn't. On that reading they're answering two different questions under one name and should've gone their seperate ways decades ago. But I'm honestly not sure, because the more philosophical replies — about whether the experience of passage needs an explanation, or whether "real for" talk can be rehabilitated via causal structure — feel like they're engaging something substantive.

So: diagnose this for me. Is it a verbal dispute about "now," a genuine metaphysical fight hiding under the physics, a disagreement about what gets to count as an explanation, or something else? Use what the sides actually say, not a tour of the literature, and be honest about whether anything — quantum gravity handing us a preferred foliation, some new distinction, a reformulated question — could actually settle it, or whether we should just expect this one to grind on forever and why.
