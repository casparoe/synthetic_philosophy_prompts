Quick context: I'm the grad student rep on the constitutional review committee of our university's student association, and a mess just landed in our lap. Last semester a referendum passed 68-32 to stop all student-fee funding for any group that takes a public stance on "controversial political issues" — it was pretty transparently aimed at defunding a small pro-Palestinian group and a pro-life group, though the campaign framing was all about "neutrality." Two weeks later the Judicial Council — five students appointed by past association presidents, none of them elected to anything — struck it down as a violation of the charter's guarantee of viewpoint-neutral funding. Now half the campus is screaming about tyranny by unelected randos overruling a supermajority, and the other half is saying this is literally the one moment the charter exists for. The committee has to vote Thursday on whether to propose an amendment stripping the Council's power to strike down referenda (making their rulings advisory only), and I somehow got assigned to write the recommendation memo everyone will read beforehand.

Please draft it. I need a clear bottom line — recommend stripping the power, keeping it, or something in between like a supermajority override — with actual reasoning, not vibes. And it has to honestly engage the strongest argument on whichever side it comes down against. For me the hard part is the precommitment thing: the whole point of writing something into a charter in advance is that you can't trust majorities to protect small unpopular groups precisely at the moment when everyone wants to gang up on them, so "68% voted for it" maybe shouldn't settle the matter. But it also genuinely bothers me that 5 appointed people with no accountability killed something 68% of voters passed. The memo should be readable by my fellow committee members — smart students, but not con law people — so no jargon dumps, keep it to maybe a page and a half.
