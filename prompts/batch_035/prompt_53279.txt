I'm putting together a syllabus module on philosophy of medicine, and before I have the students read the naturalists and the normativists I want them to write the spec first — I don't want them grading theories against criteria they never articulated. So help me with the spec. The purpose matters here: the account doesn't have to settle metaphysics once and for all, it has to be good enough to do real work for deciding what a public health system should treat and pay for, and what counts as illness for things like sick leave or legal excuses.

Give me the desiderata for an adequate account of health and disease — what it has to deliver and why, case by case. At minimum I'd want coverage of the classics: whether a condition being statistically abnormal in a reference class makes it a disease (and what to do about athlete's slow resting hearts or the fact that basically everyone's back is a mess by evolutionary-design standards), whether dysfunction in the biological-function sense is necessary, whether the account can explain why infertility counts but choosing to be childless doesn't, and whether it can handle disagreement over things like ADHD or whether grief became a disorder when the DSM folks loosened their criteria. But add whatever I'm missing.

Then two things I really want from you: first, flag which desiderata are camp prejudices in disguise — i.e., things only a naturalist or only a normativist would demand — versus which are genuinely load-bearing for the practical purpose, and mark each one negotiable or not. Second, tell me if any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, because if it is, that's the actual lesson of the module, and which single requirement does the most to knock out the main contenders. My sister is a GP and keeps insisting the whole debate is academic since "disease is just what we treat," so also tell me whether that lazy view satisfies the spec or fails it. Table format is fine.
