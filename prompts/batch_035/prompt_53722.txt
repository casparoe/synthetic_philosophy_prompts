I've come to hold a fairly strong version of the eliminativist line on qualia — the strategy sometimes called quining qualia, associated with Chase and Sanborn. The position, as I hold it, is not that experience lacks character, but that the concept of a quale bundles together four marks that no property could jointly satisfy: ineffability, intrinsicality (not being constituted by relations, dispositions, or discriminations), privacy, and unmediated immediate apprehensibility. The concept is incoherent, so nothing answers to it; and the appearance that something does — the intuition of an intrinsically qualitative residue over and above the discriminative, affective, and representational facts — is a cognitive artifact, comparable to other cases where a theoretically motivated posit turns out to reify a structural feature of our concepts. My version is constitutive rather than empirical: I don't merely predict that no science of the mind will ever need irreducibly phenomenal vocabulary; I think the concept fails a priori, so that classic deployments of it — inverted spectrum cases, the knowledge argument, the zombie conceivability argument — trade on the incoherence rather than tracking a possibility. I distinguish this from reductive realism (which keeps qualia and naturalizes them) and from illusionism (which keeps the phenomenal- seeming talk and reinterprets it); I want qualia talk quined outright.

Tell me what would count against this. I want the arguments, possible cases, and dialectical moves that should move me, sorted from fatal through serious to merely awkward. Some candidates worth assessing rather than assuming: a demonstration that one or more of the four marks is optional to the concept, so that the cluster survives pruning into something coherent; a possible case the quiner cannot describe without the phenomenal residue doing genuine work, not merely generating an intuition; the phenomenal concept strategy, if it vindicates reference to phenomenal properties while staying physicalist; the objection that the illusion of qualia is itself something with phenomenal character, so the view undermines its own positing of an illusion. Distinguish what a committed quiner would concede as counting against them from what they would dismiss — say, Moorean appeals to how things obviously seem. Say what the position predicts that its rivals do not, so I can tell a genuine test from a stalemate. And assess honestly whether successive retreats — quining ineffability first, then intrinsicality, then privacy, until the thesis is just immediate apprehension relabeled — have insulated the view from any possible loss, and whether that insulation, if real, is disqualifying or is the right fate for a negative metaphysical thesis of this kind. The persuader that would count most with me: a case where the phenomenal residue is needed to state a finding, rather than to honor an intuition.
