For a seminar writeup I have to lay out my actual position on whether you can get knowledge or justification from aesthetic testimony, and before I commit to it I want to know if it even hangs together. Here's the package:

(1) pure deference is illegitimate in aesthetics — if the only basis for my belief that a poem is moving is that a critic I trust says it is, my belief isn't justified, even if she's right; (2) I have no similar scruple about science or history — I believe in Higgs bosons purely on testimony and that seems totally fine; (3) aesthetic judgement is response-dependent, i.e. being beautiful is partly constituted by how idealized responders respond, so there's no aesthetic fact that outstrips what creatures like us can register; (4) aesthetic expertise is real — trained critics genuinely track the aesthetic facts better than novices do; (5) I'm fine using critics as pointers, so if someone tells me where to look in a painting or what to listen for in a recording, and I then look/listen and form my own judgement, that judgement is legitimate even though testimony got me there; and (6) I don't think it poisons my judgement of the Divine Comedy that I only picked it up because centuries of critics told everyone it was great.

Audit this for me. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look that way, rank the real ones by severity, and for each say what would have to give and roughly what it costs. Don't just flag everything — I specifically want to know which commitment, if any, is doing most of the damage.
