Quick one that's been bugging me for a while. So we all agree (well, most of us) that whether a physical system "implements" a computation seems to depend on how you choose to map its physical states onto the states of the computational formalism. And there's this old line of argument that if you're allowed a sufficiently baroque mapping, then basically any physical object with enough stuff going on can be described as implementing any finite computation you like — a rock, a wall, the weather system. If that's right, and if moral status supervenes on which computations a system implements, we're in a proper mess: either everything with the right sort of complexity carries a bit of moral status, or the connection between computation and moral status was never the real story in the first place.

So here's what I want from you. Give me your credence in this proposition: for any given physical system, there is a determinate fact of the matter about which computations it implements, robust enough that a rock does not implement a pain computation under any legitimate interpretation. And I don't want vibes — I want the decomposition. At minimum I'd expect terms something like these, plus whatever else you reckon belongs:

1. P(some non-triviality constraint on implementation is correct — e.g. that the mapping has to respect causal and counterfactual structure, not just the actual sequence of states)
2. P(that constraint actually succeeds in ruling out the baroque mappings, given its defenders have never fully agreed on what the constraint even is)
3. P(if moral status ever attaches to a computation at all, it attaches to the implementation rather than to something coarser like the bare input-output function)
4. whatever residual term you think the proposition needs to be true.

Give each a number and a one-line justification, then combine them however you think is honest — if you reckon they're not independant, say so and adjust rather than just multiplying. Then tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or finding would move that term the most.

For calibration: my own credence sits around 0.6, whilst a mate of mine who's a much more committed functionalist says 0.9 because he thinks the causal-structure constraint obviously does all the work on its own. Tell me whether you think the disagreement between us actually lives in term (2), as he insists, or somewhere else entirely — my suspicion is it's hiding in term (3) and neither of us has noticed.
