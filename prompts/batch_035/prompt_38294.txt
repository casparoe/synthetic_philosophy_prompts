For a seminar on self-knowledge I've been asked to write up my position on introspection so the rest of the group can poke holes in it, and honestly I'd like to know where the holes are before they find them. Here's roughly where I stand: (1) when I carefully attend to my current experience -- how this headache feels, whether there's a blind spot in my visual field -- my sincere introspective reports can't be wrong. Not just reliable; error-proof, at least for careful, present-tense phenomenal reports. Maybe "incorrigible" is too strong a word for it, but it's something like: however it seems to me is how it is. (2) Introspective reports about my own attitudes -- what I believe, why I did something -- are a different story: usually right but genuinely fallible, as all the confabulation stuff shows (people inventing reasons for choices, not noticing their preferences got swapped, that kind of thing). I accept that literature. (3) But what those studies seem to really show is that when introspection errs, it errs silently: confabulation feels from the inside exactly like successful introspection. There's no inner alarm bell that goes off. (4) I picture introspection as a genuine cognitive faculty, like perception but aimed inward -- an inner scanner -- and I think a mental state's being conscious is at least roughly constituted by its being available to that scanner. (5) I'm a physicalist about the mind, so introspection is just something brains do, no homunculus reading a screen. (6) In practice I trust my introspective access a lot: I'm confident I moved cities for the career reasons I think I did and wasn't just rationalizing restlessness, and I'd tell others to extend the same confidence to their own retrospective self-explanations. (7) And I think I can usually tell, just by introspecting, when I'm currently rationalizing -- wishful thinking has a distinctive feel, and I've caught myself at it before.

Go through this set and tell me which pairs of commitments are actually in tension, which only look like they're, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest fix would be for each. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I genuinely don't know where the worst problem is, so don't soften anything -- if two of these can't both be held, say which one has to go.
