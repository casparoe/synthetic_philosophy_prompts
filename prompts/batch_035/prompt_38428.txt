Longtime rationalist-adjacent lurker here, been reading this stuff since the old SSC days, and I thought I knew what counted as a race to the bottom, but when I actually go case by case my verdicts are a mess and I can't find the principle behind them. Here's my list:

- Two superpowers building nuclear arsenals way past anything deterrence could possibly need, each only building because the other does. Trap, obviously, the canonical one.
- AI labs cutting safety evals to ship first. Everyone at each lab individually hates it, no one can stop unilaterally without just losing. Trap, and the one I actually care about.
- Delivery platforms squeezing drivers harder and harder because any platform that pays decently gets undercut. This is definately a trap, even though I don't think any individual platform is run by monsters.
- Coke and Pepsi spending billions on brand ads that mostly just cancel each other out. Trap, I guess? But honestly its way down my worry list, and that bugs me, because structurally it looks the same as the others.
- Two biotech firms racing to be first to patent the same drug, duplicating the whole R&D effort, winner takes all. This one I say is NOT a trap, even though it has trap DNA all over it — duplicated spend, winner-take-most, unilateral restraint means you simply lose. The race is doing work: society gets the drug sooner, and parallel attempts hedge the risk. So being a race to the bottom can't just be about structure.
- Countries competing on corporate tax rates to attract businesses. Trap, even though my free-market friends keep telling me it's just competition and states should have to earn it like anyone else.
- Kid's birthday party escalation in my neighborhood, each party having to top the last one. This is where I get embarrassed: structurally it's the Coke/Pepsi ad war (positional, canceling, low stakes) but I say it's NOT a trap, it's just... people, and honestly some of the parents seem to enjoy the arms race. Some days I'm tempted to say the same thing about the ads, which is why I need help.

So is there one principle that gets all of these, or close? My current attempt is something like: it's a trap iff everyone would be better off if all sides stopped simultaneously, AND the racing activity isn't itself producing anything anyone values at the margin. That saves the patent race (the racing produces the drug) and maybe the birthday thing (people like hosting the parties), but I don't trust it — it feels stitched together, each clause added to save exactly one verdict.

And here's the case I keep going back and forth on, which I'd like the principle to actually rule on: pro cycling back when everyone doped. By my patent-race clause it shouldn't be a trap — the race produced something outsiders valued, faster times, bigger spectacle, more revenue for the sport as a whole. But in my gut the Armstrong era is Moloch at his purest. So either my principle is wrong, or the patent race is a trap too, or my gut is wrong. Which is it?

Can you state the principle (or a couple of candidates) precisely, say which clause comes from which case, tell me which of my verdicts it has to overturn, and what it predicts for the doping one? Not looking for a reading list, just the actual analysis.
