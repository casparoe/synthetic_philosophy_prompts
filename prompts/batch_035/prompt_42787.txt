I'm a program officer at a family foundation that gives away about $15M a year. Last quarter the trustees, after some reading, rewrote our mission statement to say we exist to "secure the long-run flourishing of humanity." Nobody has told me what that means for my actual job, which is triaging ~120 letters of inquiry a quater, twenty minutes each, mostly from orgs I've never heard of. Write me a procedure I can actually run at that speed: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where it ends in a decline, where it punts to the trustees because the question is one of values rather than facts, and which steps are really just judgment calls dressed up as procedure.

The part I suspect matters most: the new mission statement is vague enough that it doesn't settle whether we think the far future merely matters or whether it dominates everything, and I'm fairly sure the trustees don't all mean the same thing by it. I'd want the procedure to surface that difference rather than paper over it — ideally it tells me which kinds of grants turn on it, so I know when to stop and ask.

Then run it on three applications I more or less have on my desk right now: (1) $400k to train biosecurity fellows; (2) $60k for an adult literacy program in the city where we're headquartered (this one is politically untouchable, the founder's daughter is on the board and loves it); (3) $250k to digitize and preserve audio archives of endangered languages. And be straight with me — if the procedure gives an answer I won't like, especially on the literacy one, I want that answer anyway.
