I'm a PhD student straddling epidemiology and machine learning, and a disagreement I used to regard as a harmless notational squabble has become load-bearing for my thesis. On one side sits the counterfactual tradition running through Lewis: c causes e when, had c not occurred, e would not have — with the famous apparatus of possible worlds, small miracles, and non-backtracking similarity to keep the counterfactuals from depending on the future. On the other sits the interventionist tradition running through Pearl's do-calculus and Woodward's account: c causes e when some suitable intervention that sets c would change the distribution of e. My cohort treats these as two dialects, and I watched a seminar devolve into exactly this: the causal-inference people sketching a DAG and saying "just read the intervention off the graph," the philosophy people objecting that this is circular, since interventions are themselves causal processes and you have defined causation in terms of itself, and neither side moving for two terms. Meanwhile my actual problem — whether the causal effect I estimate in one trial population carries over to a target population, where nobody can literally intervene on the confounders — seems to demand that I know which construal of causation the math is tracking.

My working hypothesis, offered as nothing more than a hypothesis, is that this is not a substantive disagreement about one thing but two projects under one word: the counterfactual side is doing metaphysics, trying to say what causal structure is in the world such that anything is true to say about it, while the interventionist side is articulating a tool — a semantics for what an agent who can wiggle some variables is entitled to infer — and that the standard objections (circularity against the interventionists, the impossibility of clean interventions and the backtracking problem against the counterfactualists) each land because they are addressed to a project the other side isn't running. But I can't tell whether this is a genuine diagnosis or just an uneasy truce, and my advisor is of no help: she says the do-operator "just is" the definition of causal effect, said in the tone of someone who thinks disagreement ended sometime in the 1990s.
