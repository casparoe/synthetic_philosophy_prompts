This happened at a BBQ last weekend and I couldnt referee it for the life of me. My friends Maya and Dan got into it about deflationism in philosophy of language — the idea that truth isn't some deep property, the truth predicate is just a logical device, basically shorthand that lets you say 'everything the witness said is true' instead of listing every sentence (like an infinite conjunction you can't actually write out). Dan thinks that's wrong. The exchange went roughly like this:

(1) Dan said: if truth is just a device, then the liar sentence ('this sentence is not true') should be harmless — a grammatical tool can't generate a contradiction. But it does generate one. So there must be more to truth than a device.

(2) Maya answered: the liar is a problem for every theory of truth, including ones that treat truth as a heavy-duty property, so it can't be special evidence against deflationism. Paradox comes from self-reference plus negation, and those resources are available to everyone, deflationist or not.

(3) Dan came back: but self-reference plus negation alone doesn't blow anything up. 'This sentence is not about dogs' is self-referential, negative, and completely harmless. Something specific to truth — the fact that 'p' is true iff p — is what lets the negation bite on itself. So the paradox isolates a feature that the device story owes us an explanation of, not just a wave in its general direction.

(4) Maya said that's exactly what the device story explains: the liar works because the truth predicate comes with disquotation built in, and disquotation is the whole deflationist claim — the schema is truth's only job, and the schema is also what licenses the liar. An inflationist has to explain the liar AND the extra metaphysical nature of truth, which is extra work for zero extra explanatory payoff.

(5) Dan then shifted ground: fine, maybe, but the device story can't handle truth being a goal. Science aims at truth, beliefs are supposed to be true, an assertion is correct only if it's true. A whole practice of inquiry can't aim at a logical device — that's like saying chess strategy aims at the '+' sign in the notation.

(6) Maya replied that he's confusing the predicate with the contents: we aim at beliefs that things are as they are, and that aim lives in the world-directed beliefs themselves; 'true' is just how we generalize over the aim when we don't want to list each belief. Dan said that's a relabeling — if truth-talk adds literally nothing, the word 'true' should be deletable without loss, and he bet she couldn't delete it from 'nothing he believed was true' and still say it without a truth predicate. Maya said that's just the generalization point from the start, which she never denied — deflationism doesn't claim the word is useless, it claims the word is a device, and devices earn their keep. Dan ended with: then deflationism is unfalsifiable — whatever work the word does, you just call it device-work, so there's no possible observation that could count against you.

They asked me to referee and I definetely could not. Please go through the six moves one at a time, say who's right at each step (not just who wins overall), and tell me what, if anything, both of them are missing.
