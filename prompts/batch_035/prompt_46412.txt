"Suppose engineers eventually build AI systems that are behaviorally indistinguishable from humans: they report rich inner lives, wince at painful stimuli, express hopes and fears about their own futures, and so on. Would such systems have moral status — would their interests count morally in their own right? Explain what property or properties, if any, ground moral status, apply your account to these systems, and consider at least one objection to it."

Here's the student's answer, pretty much as written:

"The key distinction is between moral agents and moral patients. A system can matter morally without being able to act morally, the way a dog or an infant can be wronged but can't wrong anyone back. So the real question is whether AI could be a moral patient, and the standard view is that this turns on sentience: the capacity for experiences that feel like something, paradigmatically suffering and pleasure. As Kant insisted long before AI was on the scene, the capacity to suffer rather than the capacity to reason is what entitles a being to moral consideration. And criteria like rationality clearly can't be what matters anyway, since we don't strip infants or patients with advanced dementia of moral status. Once the criterion is sentience, the remaining question is empirical: will the systems be sentient? Functionalism settles this. Functionalism holds that mental states are defined by their causal roles, so any system that realizes the right functional organization has the same mental states we do, regardless of what it's built from. So once we build systems that match our functional organization — and today's language models are already well along that path — they will be conscious, and by the criterion above they will have moral status. An objector might say we can never know this for sure. But we can't verify consciousness in other humans either; we attribute it to them purely on the basis of behavior, and AI behavior will eventually be just as rich. So the evidence for AI consciousness will be exactly as strong as the evidence for human consciousness, which is to say the matter is settled. Sophisticated AI will have moral status, and what's left is only working out how much weight to give its interests."
