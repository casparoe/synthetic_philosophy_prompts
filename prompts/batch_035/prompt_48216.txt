ok so i have zero academic background, this came out of an actual argument with my neighbor, and i've been chewing on it for months. i want an essay that actually takes a side at the end, not one of those "on the other hand, on the other hand" things.

the setup: me and my neighbor Deb share a quiet weekend. i sometimes practice drums on saturday (hobby band, and honestly practicing feels like a chore, i'd usually rather skip it). she sometimes runs her leaf blower on sunday (she likes a tidy yard). so any given weekend looks like one of four things:

1. total peace and quiet
2. me drumming saturday, quiet sunday
3. quiet saturday, her blower sunday
4. both happen

i rank them 1 > 2 > 3 > 4. quiet is best, if there's gonna be noise i'd rather it be mine, her blower bugs me more than almost anything, both at once is the worst. yes i actually asked her: she ranks them 2 > 3 > 4 > 1. she LOVES my drumming (retired music teacher, says it reminds her of work), she'd rather blow than not when i'm quiet, but she won't run the blower over my drumming cause she wants to hear it, and total silence bores her. people are weird, me included.

now here are two rules that both seem like the absolute floor of any fair way for us to settle weekends together:

- rule one (unanimity): if we BOTH prefer one weekend over another, then our two-person "group" should prefer it too. nobody can be overruled when we're literally agreeing.
- rule two (own business): each of us is the final boss of our own activity. questions about whether *i* drum get settled by my preference no matter what she thinks, same for her and the blower. no committee needed to decide what i do with my own sticks.

sounds bulletproof, right? run it on our rankings:

- blower question with me quiet (weekend 1 vs 3): she'd rather blow, so by rule two, 3 beats 1.
- drum question with her quiet (weekend 1 vs 2): i'd rather skip practice, so by rule two, 1 beats 2.
- weekend 2 vs 3: we BOTH prefer 2 over 3, so by rule one, 2 beats 3.

so 3 beats 1, which beats 2, which beats 3. a circle. whatever weekend we "pick", there's another one that we jointly or rightfully prefer instead, so the choice always has a foot out the door. and apparently this isn't just us being weird: a friend who did econ in college says an economist named Sen proved in the 70s that with even two people and two personal decisions you can always construct exactly this kind of loop, it's called the liberal paradox.

that's the essay i want: which of the two rules has to give? or is the way out deeper than picking one to sacrifice — maybe a group doesn't need one coherent ranked list of preferences at all (but then what does it even mean to choose something *together*?), or maybe rule one shouldn't count preferences that are really about the OTHER person's business, like deb preferring my drumming isn't really a preference about her own life (but then who gets to say which of someone's preferences are "really theirs"? and does that quietly gut rule one?). take those escape routes seriously, weigh them, and land on a verdict and defend it. and as a bonus tell me if, under your verdict, i still have to practice.
