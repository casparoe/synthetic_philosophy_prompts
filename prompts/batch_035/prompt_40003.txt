Please give me your credence that free-floating rationales exist: cases where, as a matter of strict truth rather than metaphor or convenient shorthand, a state or trait obtains *in order to* produce some effect E, even though no reasoner — no agent with purposes, no designer, nothing that could formulate or grasp a reason — ever had E as an aim, the "in order to" arising entirely from a blind selection process. The paradigm is "the heart beats in order to circulate blood": the explanation looks teleological, yet there was no teleologist.

Decompose your number along at least the following lines, assigning a probability and one line of justification to each, then give the combination:

1. Whether "X in order to F" claims are ever strictly true, rather than compressions of a selection-historical etiology that is itself entirely non-teleological.
2. Whether such truths require an actual selection history, or whether a structure that would perform E, and would have been selected for E had it arisen, already has the function of E even with no history — the swampman question: does a molecule-for-molecule duplicate of an organism, produced by a lightning strike with no evolutionary past, have the same functions, and, if the original had beliefs, the same beliefs?
3. Whether "rationale" here is semantically parasitic on reasoners, so that a rationale without any reasoner resembles a debt without anyone ever having owed anything — grammatically well-formed but necessarily false — or whether the parasitism runs the other way, our reasoned purposes being a late, special case of a more general selectional "for".
4. Any further terms you think belong; consider, for instance, whether the contrast case of a trait fixed purely by drift — where the same selection-historical machinery applies but no rationale seems present — shows that the line between rationales and mere histories can be drawn non-arbitrarily.

Then tell me which term your final number is most sensitive to, and what would move it most: a settled verdict on the duplicate asymmetry in (2), a semantics for "in order to", or something else. If you suspect part of the question is verbal — that "strictly true" versus "metaphor" is not a well-defined contrast here — say which part, and explain what cash value the question retains once that part is set aside.
