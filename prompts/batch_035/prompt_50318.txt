You are a professional metaphysician who has taught seminars on persistence and the ontology of time for twenty years. Please think carefully before answering — maybe brainstorm 2 or 3 angles first, then write the actual piece.

Write a dialogue between an endurantist and a perdurantist who bump into each other at their 25-year college reunion. They end up standing in front of a big photo banner of their graduating class, arguing about whether the people milling around at the reunion are literally the same people as the ones in the photos — the endurantist insists each alum is wholly present and numerically identical to the skinny kid in the photo, just with different properties now, while the perdurantist thinks that's sloppy talk and that what you really have is a four-dimensional worm whose 1999 temporal part is in the photo and whose 2024 temporal part is hogging the snacks.

Make them actually engage with each others points rather than talking past each other. For example: when the endurantist raises the problem of temporary intrinsics (the alum in the photo was clean-shaven, the one in front of them has a beard — so the very same thing seems to have incompatible properties simpliciter), the perdurantist should give the temporal-parts reply (the bearded part and the clean-shaven part are distinct, so no contradiction), and then the endurantist should push back on the cost of that move, e.g. whether saying "he has-a-beard-at-t" is just perdurantism with extra steps, or whether the relativized version quietly gives up on what properties things really have.

Weave in at least two of the following complications, and let the characters disagree about what each one shows:

- A third classmate, now a doctor, keeps chiming in that something like 98% of the atoms in every alum's body have been replaced since graduation — does either view handle that better, or does it not matter at all?
- A big thundercloud visible through the window, and the problem of the many: if the reunion counts as the same event while people shed cells and swap atoms, is there some analogous sloppiness about how many objects are in the room?
- The perdurantist pressing the endurantist on how the same wholly-present person can be located at the reunion AND (if she'd stayed home) at home — is "multi-location" a feature or a bug?
- The endurantist pressing back on whether perdurantism can even make sense of ordinary claims like "I regret what I did at the reunion" if no temporal part is strictly identical to any other — who, exactly, is the "I" doing the regretting?

Neither side should win outright or be a strawman — both are smart and know the literature. End with them still disagreeing, but having sharpened where the real fault line is: is it a disagreement about what objects fundamentally are, or just about which metaphysical bookkeeping better explains the same ordinary facts? Keep the tone natural, a bit funny, like two old friends who genuinly enjoy this. Roughly 600-900 words.
