My mate Priya has this argument she's dead smug about and I can't find the exact spot it goes dodgy. (1) Suffering is bad, and if something is bad then reducing it makes the world better, all else equal. (2) No amount of happiness makes up for torture — a world with a billion blissful people plus one person being tortured is worse than the same world minus the torture, no matter how much extra bliss you pile on top. (3) If happiness can never make up for suffering, then in any choice between creating happiness and removing suffering, removing the suffering wins. (4) The only guaranteed way to remove all suffering forever — including all the animal and wild-animal suffering nobody will ever even notice, let alone fix — is to painlessly end all sentient life, like a button that puts every living thing to sleep and nothing ever wakes up again. (5) So we should press the button. (6) Pressing the button is obviously monstrous. Priya says she'll bite the bullet on (6) and that I'm just being squeamish, but I reckong that's a bullet nobody can actually bite.

The bit I keep staring at is (3). She acts like it just falls out of (2), but (2) sounds like it's about happiness and torture in the same world — or even the same person's life? — while (3) gets used to compare one group's happiness against a completely different group's suffering. And she slides from "torture can't be compensated" to "any suffering at all beats any happiness", which feels like a bigger claim than she paid for. Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? For the record I'd really like to keep (2) — the thought that you can't just out-joy somebody else's torture — and (6). Is there a way of caring mostly about suffering that lets me keep both, or am I stuck chosing?
