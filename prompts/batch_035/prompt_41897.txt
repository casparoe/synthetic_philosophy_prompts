hey, so I'm helping put together the final for a grad seminar on formal models of rational agency, and I want one take-home question on the Löbian obstacle. the students have all seen Löb's theorem stated before, so the question can't be answerable by reciting it -- it has to make them actually work through why a proof-based agent can't trust itself. Concretely: an agent A that proves theorems to decide what to do, facing a future copy/ successor of itself that runs the same search procedure, and wanting to prove something like "if I follow my procedure, the outcome is fine". Students need to see that each individual step of the reasoning can be verifiable while the self-trust conclusion still isn't derivable, that the obstacle is about consistency/trust rather than capability (the agent isn't dumb, it's stuck), and why the obvious fix -- just add "my own search is sound" as an axiom -- is either circular or, if you push it, blows up in the Gödel's-second-theorem way. I also want a part where they explain why this matters outside pure logic, e.g. for two agents who can read each other's source code trying to coordinate but unable to prove that the other's proof-checking is trustworthy -- so it doesn't feel like a curiosity. The question should be phrased so everything needed is in the question itself plus standard grad-level logic; don't lean on any specific paper or author, and no citations in the model answer.

Deliverables: the question as it would appear on the exam (phrased for ~90 minutes of work), a model answer of around 600 words, and then a marking scheme of exactly five numbered criteria, each one or two sentences -- no intro or conclusion around the five items, just the list. Each criterion should name a specific move that separates an A answer from a B answer, and where you can, say in the same breath how students typically mess that particular part up (like conflating "can't prove I'm sound" with "should believe I'm unsound", or treating the axiom fix as obviously fine). thanks!
