I've been around the rationalist blogs since the original Sequences — long enough to have watched the same arguments about uploads, AI moral status, and whether a thermostat has beliefs cycle through every few years — and somewhere in there I settled into a position I'd like stress-tested. I'm not asking for a tour of the literature; I want an audit of my own package. Here is what I actually believe, as best I can state it:

1. Functionalism is correct: mental states are individuated by their causal-functional organization — by what they do within the system — not by the material they are made of.

2. Multiple realizability is real, and in a strong form: the very same mental state can be realized in neurons, in silicon, and in substrates we haven't imagined, and there need be no physical property shared by all of a state's realizers other than the functional organization itself. Substrate independence is the whole point of the view.

3. A sufficiently faithful whole-brain emulated on different hardware has the same mind as the original. I'd extend this even to a neuromorphic chip that runs the same causal organization a thousand times slower: the mind is there, just running slow, and I don't think consciousness is indexed to real time.

4. But there is a substantive fact of the matter about what implements what, and it is not liberal. The result — due to Putnam, and recycled endlessly in forum threads ever since — that any open system can, with enough cleverness in the state-to-state mapping, be described as implementing any finite-state machine, is a reductio of unconstrained realization, not a discovery about how widespread computation is. A rock does not implement your calendar app.

5. Alongside that, a case verdict I'm confident in: a giant lookup table that maps every possible input-history to the same output as the emulated brain — behaviorally indistinguishable, with no internal structure doing any work — is not a mind. It's a database. If functionalism entails that it is conscious, so much the worse for functionalism; but I don't believe functionalism entails that.

6. Finally, psychology is an autonomous special science: its generalizations needn't be reduced to physics in order to be true, and functionalist claims about the mental are established at the psychological level of description.

What I'd like: go through these pairwise and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look to be in tension, and for the real ones rank them by severity and spell out what the cheapest repair costs — I want to know what each repair gives up, not merely that one is available. I genuinely don't know where the problems are, so don't soften the verdict on my behalf.

Two requests about how you report the state of the art. Where the field is genuinely split — for example, over whether the triviality result actually goes through once the realization relation is properly spelled out, or over what the right thing to say about table-driven implementations is — please mark that as contested rather than presenting one side as the settled view. And where something really is near-consensus among people who work on this, say so plainly: I'd like to know which of my worries are live debates and which are my own confusions.
