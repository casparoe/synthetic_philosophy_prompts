I have no training in philosophy. The farthest I've gone is working through Wittgenstein's On Certainty by myself after a friend recommended it, alongside a few discussions of Moore's old proof of the external world, and for the past month I have been testing my friend Rosa with cases to see what she does with doubt. I now have a track record of her verdicts that I cannot fully make sense of. So far she has said: I know I have two hands, and no evidence is needed for that; I know the world existed long before last Tuesday; in a classic case where a belief is true only by luck, she denies knowledge; she denies I know my lottery ticket will lose even though the odds are one in three hundred million; and, on two occasions, instead of answering, she treated the question itself as broken — first when I asked whether she might be a brain in a vat, and again when I asked whether she knows she is not dreaming right now. In both cases she refused to say yes or no and seemed to think the question misfires.

Next week I intend to run Moore's proof on her: I will say to her that I know I have two hands, that if I have hands there is an external world, and that therefore I know there is an external world. My question is whether she will accept that conclusion. I am less interested in her answer than in the rule she appears to be running, because several rules fit her verdicts so far about equally well as far as I can tell. Perhaps she thinks some things are simply known without any support, and knowledge flows through the if-then to the conclusion. Perhaps she holds something like Wittgenstein's own line, that the background certainties are not really objects of knowledge at all, in which case the conclusion should misfire for her the way the vat question did. Perhaps she would say she is entitled to the conclusion without knowing it. Or perhaps her answers just track how much doubt is in the air, so it all depends on how I set the question up. I also cannot decide whether her lottery verdict fits the Wittgenstein line or cuts against it. Please tell me what each of these rules predicts about the Moore proof, give me your best guess as to what Rosa will say along with your reasons for preferring that guess, and propose the further case that would best tell the candidate rules apart — I have one candidate, whether she knows she has never been on the moon, but I am not confident it splits anything. Please also separate what is genuinely contested among people who know this literature from what is basically settled, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus. Lead with your prediction in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restatement of my question.
