I've been reading LessWrong and adjacent forums since the Sequences were posted, and I've noticed that every acausal trade thread contains at least five people talking past each other: one means Hofstadter-style superrationality, one means FDT-style cooperation with logically correlated agents, one means bargaining with agents in distant parts of an inflationary multiverse, and one just means ordinary trade that happens to be mediated by simulations. I want to write a reference post that sorts this out, and the centerpiece should be a decision tree that starts from a commitment everyone in the audience already shares — roughly, "I choose actions by some decision theory" — and ends at a named position on acausal trade.

Build the tree from the logical space, not from a list of labels. Each internal node should be a question answerable from one's own decision-theoretic convictions, without knowing which leaf it leads to, in something like this order: (1) does your decision theory ever recommend acting for the sake of an agent with which you share no causal interaction whatsoever — and if so, is the relevant link evidential, logical, or mediated by an actual causal channel such as ancestor simulations?; (2) must the counterparty model you well enough to predict your compliance — is there a threshold of mutual modeling below which "acausal trade" is vacuous?; (3) must the stakes be astronomical (as in multiverse-wide cooperation across causally disconnected regions) for cooperation to be rational, or does it bite even for small payoffs between ordinary agents? The leaves should include at least: the superrationalist who cooperates with any agent reasoning the same way; the FDT-er who cooperates only where the correlation is logical and exact; the multiverse-wide cooperator who treats acausal deals as one instrument among others for large-stakes altruism; the skeptic who holds that every real case smuggles in a causal channel and the pure cases never bite; and the view that acausal trade is real but is an equilibrium among superintelligences only, with humans at most able to pre-commit into it. At least one pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate should be flagged as such, with the extra question that would separate them, rather than pretending the tree is sharper than it is; likewise, note any combination of answers no leaf occupies, and say whether nobody is there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.

End the entire response with a final line beginning "Verdict:" naming, in one sentence, the leaf you would land on if you answered the tree honestly.
