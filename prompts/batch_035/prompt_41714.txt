I've just been assigned to co-author a 2050 outlook for our ministry's strategic foresight unit, and my senior colleague keeps defusing my Popperian doubts with the same analogy: long-range social forecasting is like weather forecasting. Chaos caps point predictions, he grants, but climatology still yields lawlike knowledge of the slow-moving background — and on his telling, demographic momentum, epidemiological transition, and capital-stock turnover are our climate, which is why trend extrapolation plus scenarios is a legitimate product and Popper's attack on laws of history misses us entirely.

I'd like an audit of the analogy rather than a verdict on the report. Give me the correspondence table — what plays the role of sensitive dependence on initial conditions, of the forecast horizon, of the climate itself — then the breaks. Two I suspect matter: our outlook, once published, is read by the very ministries it describes, the feedback Popper labeled the Oedipus effect (though he later conceded that expectation-driven effects show up in biology too, so it may not cleanly divide social from natural systems); and his other argument, that the principal driver of long-run social change is the growth of knowledge, which no one can predict without already possessing it. Which of the disanalogies are fatal for his use of the analogy and which are idle? Does his conclusion — that our product is legitimate, Popper notwithstanding — survive once the breaks are on the table, or was the analogy carrying it the whole time? And if the weather picture fails, is there a better comparison — evolution, epidemics, self-referential systems — and what would it predict about the half-life of a 2050 outlook?
