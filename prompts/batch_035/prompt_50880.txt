I'm a science and ideas columnist, and for April Fools' week my editor lets me run one satire piece. This year I want to skewer how the scale argument from the insect-sentience debate gets mangled once it leaves the seminar room: you take a small probability that insects feel anything, multiply by the truly astronomic number of insects, and suddenly anyone with a product to sell can claim the moral math came out their way. So: please write a corporate press release from a fictional pest-control company, VerminZero, that has rebranded as the ethical choice in the industry because it now sells 'sentience offsets' — for every million bugs its treatments kill, it funds half a grad student to study whether insects can suffer, and homeowners can buy 'conscience credits' at checkout. The humor has to come from getting the arguement right — the expected-value multiplication, the way moral uncertainty can be pointed whichever way pays, the comfortable asymmetry between tiny probability and vast numbers — not from making the company sound dumb. Keep it around 450 words, formatted as exactly five numbered sections, no intro or conclusion, deadpan corporate voice throughout. Then add a short note after the piece telling me which claims about insect cognition and sentience it leans on are real but genuinely contested among researchers, and which are pure invention for the joke, so I don't accidentally present a live scientific fight as settled.
