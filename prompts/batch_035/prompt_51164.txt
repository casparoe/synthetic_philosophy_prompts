Before I wade into the literature on manipulation I want the spec first. I keep running into arguments about whether recommender systems and dark patterns manipulate people, and both sides just point at one case and gesture, so: what would a theory of manipulation (as oposed to legitimate persuasion) actually have to deliver? Give me the desiderata -- the cases it has to get right, the distinctions it can't collapse (manipulation vs. coercion, vs. lying, vs. merely effective or eloquent persuasion), and the verdicts that seem pretty fixed (subliminal ads in, a heartfelt appeal to our friendship out?), each with the case behind it. Two I'd especially want on the list. First, the two-defaults problem: the same psychological mechanism gets opposite verdicts depending on who set it and why (a country making organ donation the default vs. a checkout flow defaulting you into a subscription), so can a theory defined purely by how the influence works survive that, or does it have to smuggle in the influencer's motives? Second, the truthful manipulator: a doctor who uses only accurate statistics, but chosen and framed to move you through fear rather than through your own weighing of reasons, seems manipulative without hiding anything -- which is trouble for any view where the wrong-making feature is covertness. Then tell me which pairs of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied; I have a feeling that "classifiable by mechanism alone" and "getting the symmetrical-default cases right" are jointly unsatisfiable, and if so, that itself is a result about where the moral weight actually sits. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's prejudice. And then, briefly, how the three main proposals going do against the list: (1) manipulation as bypassing the target's rational capacities, (2) as hidden or covert influence, on the thought that if the target knew about it it would lose its grip, and (3) as exploiting some pre-existing weakness or trait of the target's. Keep the scoring quick -- the list is the part I actually want.
