I write novels, mostly in close first person, and I've talked myself into a corner over Wittgenstein's private language argument. My characters spend whole chapters alone with their sensations, and I keep putting words to those sensations that nobody in the story ever hears. From what I understand, Wittgenstein held that a language for what only the speaker can know -- the diary case, the beetle in the box -- couldn't get a grip, because there'd be no standard for whether the word was used correctly. Some people tell me this means inner speech is parastic on public language; others say the argument is really about rule-following and says nothing about inner life as such. So: what are the most important implications of the private language argument for how I write interiority? Specifically, can a character's wholly unexpressed sensations be truthfully rendered in words at all, is first-person narration closer to reporting or to expression, and should the argument change how I write a narrator who has no audience for her words? I want what the argument actually supports, not vibes.
