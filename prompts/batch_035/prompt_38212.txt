She's drawn to the normative-powers view: roughly, promising is an exercise of a power to alter your own normative situation, similar to consenting or authorizing, and that's what generates the obligation — not the downstream effects, not anyone's expectations. I keep landing on the reliance/convention side: something like Hume-style conventions plus the thought that what you really owe people is not to betray the expectation you deliberately created. And we each know our standard moves by heart. I say her view has an embarrassing bootstrap problem: the power is only binding if there's already a norm saying you ought to use your normative powers correctly, and I don't see where that prior norm comes from without smuggling in exactly the consequences and practices she's trying to explain away. Then we go around again.

What I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict for either of us.
