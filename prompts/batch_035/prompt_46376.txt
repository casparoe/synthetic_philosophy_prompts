My cousin works as the hearing officer for our city's parks department, and she is the one who interprets the ordinance reading "No person shall operate a motor vehicle within the boundaries of any city park." Her rulings so far: an ambulance cutting through the park to reach a collapsed runner — permitted; a motorcyclist riding a loop for fun — prohibited; an ice cream truck that parked by the playground to sell — prohibited; the groundskeeper's ride-on mower — permitted; a golf cart run by the parks department itself to shuttle visitors with limited mobility — permitted; a man walking alongside his motorcycle, engine off, after it broke down — permitted. Three complaints are now pending before her: a contractor, hired by the parks department under a concession agreement, running the same golf-cart shuttle for a fee; a fully electric, nearly silent food truck; and a wheeled autonomous delivery robot bringing sandwiches from a shop across the street to the park café. Predict her ruling on each of the three. Then say which interpretive rule best fits her record — a purposivist reading aimed at quiet, safety, and the park's recreational mission; a narrower textual reading of "motor vehicle" with exceptions only where the ordinary meaning runs out; or something else entirely — and, since more than one rule fits the earlier verdicts, identify which of the pending cases actually separates those rules, and describe one further hypothetical case that would separate them most cleanly.
