For a workshop on the metaphysics and ethics of virtual environments, I've been asked to circulate a short statement of my position so the other participants can respond to it. Before I send it around, I want to know whether what I actually believe is internally coherent, because I can't tell whether I have a consistent view or am just using the word "real" in several senses at once. Here is the position:

(1) I am an anti-realist about virtual objects. There is no sword, no pile of gold, no plot of virtual land; there are only bit-patterns and the conventions that let players talk as though there were things there. "I own the sword" is a convenient shorthand and nothing more.

(2) I nonetheless think virtual property is genuinely stealable. When someone hijacks a player's account and strips their characters, or when a guild officer empties the guild bank and disappears, a real theft has occurred, and I would support treating it as legally punishable theft.

(3) I hold a phenomenological account of harm: something harms you only if it makes a real difference to your mental life — distress, anguish, loss you actually experience. No experiential footprint, no harm.

(4) I think nothing that happens within a game's rules can wrong you, because a game is a stipulated space: being repeatedly "killed" by another player in an MMO, however enraging in the moment, is not a harm to you, full stop.

(5) I think non-consensual sexual contact in VR — one user's avatar groping another's, of the sort widely reported on social VR platforms — is a real harm of the same moral category as physical assault, not merely a threat or an unwelcome reminder of possible physical assault.

(6) I think my closest online friendships, forged entirely in virtual worlds, have exactly the value that physical friendships have, and I would not trade them away.

Go through these and tell me which combinations are genuinely inconsistent, which only look inconsistent, and how confident I can be about that sorting. I am especially unsure whether (4) and (5) can both be true, and whether my reasons for (4) are compatible with my reasons for (5), or whether I would have to find new reasons for one of them. For each genuine tension, tell me what would have to give, what it costs to give it, and whether the cost is bearable. Finally, tell me whether there is a single revision that would resolve more of the trouble than any other, or whether I am stuck making several small concessions. Please order the tensions by how much damage they do to the overall position rather than treating them as equally serious.
