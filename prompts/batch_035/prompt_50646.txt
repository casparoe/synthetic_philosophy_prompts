I'm writing a short essay for a graduate seminar on formal epistemology, on whether Jeffrey conditionalization really helps with the problem of uncertain evidence, and I'd be grateful for comments on my draft before I rewrite it. My thesis is that it helps, and moreover in a stronger sense than usually acknowledged: not only does it relax the requirement that evidence be certain, it also tells you, starting from your prior, how confident to be in the evidence itself. My sketch goes roughly as follows. On orthodox Bayesian conditioning you update by conditioning on a proposition only when you are certain of it, but real perception is seldom like that. Jeffrey's move — in his stock example you examine a piece of cloth by candlelight and come away merely more confident, but not certain, that it is green — lets the agent input a new probability directly over a partition (green versus blue, say) and redistribute the rest of their credences by holding fixed the conditional probabilities, the so-called rigidity condition. Orthodox conditioning then falls out as the limiting case where the probability assigned to one cell of the partition goes to one. So far, so standard; this much I expect is unproblematic. But I want to press two further claims. First, since each Jeffrey update can be seen as a projection of the old distribution onto the constraint the new partition probabilities impose, updating on one uncertain item of evidence and then another should land you in the same place as updating in the reverse order — so the formalism is order-insensitive, which matters because evidence typically arrives piecemeal and one would not want the order of arrival to change one's final credences. Second, the formalism is not merely a bookkeeping device: given the prior and the partition, Jeffrey conditioning actually computes the probability the evidence itself should receive, so the notorious question of where the inputs come from is answered from within the system rather than by appeal to some extra-statistical faculty. My instructor hinted that both of these further claims have drawn fire, and I'd like to get the objections right before I revise.

Please comment on the draft in two registers. First, assess its accuracy, flagging anything mistaken or misleading as stated; I suspect my two further claims are where the trouble lies, but I'd like to know precisely what is wrong with each, why, and what a defensible replacement claim would look like. Second, tell me whether the thread is interesting enough to be worth developing, and if so how you would sharpen the framing. Please write continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — the way one would write an email or a letter.
