Construct a pair of matched scenarios for the situationist critique of virtue ethics, built to the following specifications.

Purpose. The pair is a test of the situationist inference: the move from aggregate behavioral data, collected under a situational manipulation, to the conclusion that character traits are absent or explanatorily idle. The case should show that such data can be generated with equal fidelity by a world in which character is idle and a world in which character does all of the causal work while remaining invisible to the study. A pair that cheats fails: it fails if the character-dependence is visible in the recorded data, or if a mechanism is stipulated without a behavioral rationale behind the numbers.

The observable layer. Describe a small field study of the kind situationists actually run: an ordinary, low-stakes setting (an unattended payment box in an office kitchen, a library self-checkout, a transit honor system — choose one), a trivial situational cue of the sort used in real studies (a poster of watching eyes, a mirror at eye level, a signature line), no explicit moral instruction, and stakes of a few dollars. Specify the measured variables — payment rate before and after the cue, plus one secondary measure such as an exit questionnaire, chosen so that it does not distinguish the two worlds — and give the aggregate results as numbers.

The two worlds. Then give two candidate underlying realities, one paragraph each, identical in cue, stakes, setting, and every recorded variable, differing only in the distribution of individual dispositions. In World A the cue bypasses character: nobody has a relevant standing concern, and the cue nudges everyone slightly toward paying. In World B the cue recruits character: a minority with a genuine but imperfectly expressed concern for honesty is reminded by the cue and pays near-perfectly thereafter, while the indifferent majority is untouched by the cue and drifts upward only slightly, for non-moral reasons (say, the poster makes the box easier to notice). The honest minority's pre-cue shortfall must have an ordinary explanation (hurried, no cash at hand), which you must state. Provide individual-level probabilities for each world and show that they aggregate to the same before-and-after figures; this demonstration is part of the deliverable, not optional.

After the pair, in order:

(i) Say what a situationist would conclude from the study alone, and whether that conclusion is correct in World A and incorrect in World B.

(ii) Identify the single feature of the case that does the work, and say what happens to the pair if that feature is removed — in particular, if the researcher adds a baseline trait measure and looks for an interaction with the cue.

(iii) Give the situationist's strongest attack on the pair — at minimum, that recruitment is either observable in principle, in which case the underdetermination is an artifact of measurement choices, or unobservable in principle, in which case it is idle — and say whether the attack succeeds.

(iv) State candidly what World B costs the virtue ethicist, given that its honest minority needed a cue to express its concern, and say what a virtue ethicist should concede about the reliability with which virtue expresses itself in mundane conditions.

Keep the scenarios realistic — no trolleys, no demons, no ideal observers — and keep the whole thing tight enough to read in one sitting.
