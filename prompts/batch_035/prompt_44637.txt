I've been asked to write a short position piece on infinity for a philosophy of physics reading group, and before I submit it I'd like a consistency check, because several friends who read drafts each told me a different pair of my views was incompatible, and they didn't agree with each other about which. Here is where I stand. (1) Cantorian set theory is entirely legitimate as mathematics: actually infinite totalities are coherent objects, and comparing infinite sizes by bijection is the right way to compare them. (2) But I think mathematical coherence settles nothing about what can be physically instantiated — that's a separate question — so I'd describe myself as a finitist about physics, not about mathematics. (3) I'm nonetheless persuaded by the Hilbert's-hotel-style arguments that a physically instantiated actual infinite — an infinite hotel, an infinite library, any infinite collection of concrete things — would be absurd, and I conclude that the physical world contains no actual infinites. (4) At the same time, I'm comfortable with spacetime being a continuum, with infinitely many instants between any two instants; I tell myself that points aren't concrete objects in the way hotel guests are, so this is compatible with (3). (5) I hold, as an a priori matter rather than as a finding of physics, that an infinite past is impossible: no series of events could have been formed by successive addition in such a way that infinitely many of them are already behind us — you cannot traverse an actual infinite. (6) By contrast, an endless future seems perfectly possible to me, because the future is at most a potential infinity: no future totality exists yet. (7) And I accept relativity as it's standardly taught, including that there is no privileged cosmic present and that the division of events into past and future is frame-relative.

Rather than giving me a general lecture on infinity, please treat my set of commitments as the object of audit. Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be — I suspect some of my friends' objections are confused, and I'd like to know which. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad; for each, say what would have to give and what the cheapest fix is, including what that fix would cost me elsewhere in the set. Finally, tell me whether there is some single revision that would clean up most of the problem, since I'd much rather revise one commitment than four, and if so which one.
