Bit of an odd request, so bare with me. I'm a novelist, and my current book has a secondary character who's basically a fictionalised Elizabeth Anscombe — a Catholic moral philosopher, chain-smoking, terrifying in a seminar, completely immoveable once she's decided something. For plot reasons I need a scene set roughly now, decades after her death, where somebody hands my character an after-action report on a fully autonomous weapon: a loitering munition that selects its own targets, and a strike where the software classified a wedding party as a gathering of combatants and hit it without any human in the loop. She has to respond on the spot, in character. So: what would the real Anscombe most plausibly have made of lethal autonomous weapons? Does the fact that no person intends any particular death — the intention being baked into code and procurement decisions made years earlier — break her double-effect framework, or does it slot straight in? And would she treat the engineers who wrote the targeting classifier as morally on a par with the bombardier at Hiroshima, or different in some way she'd insist matters? I'd rather the scene not be a strawman, so if there's some wrinkle in her actual thinking that would make her hesitate, or surprise the reader, that's gold for me. And if you think my premise is off somewhere, please say so.
