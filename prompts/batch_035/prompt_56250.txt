My lab has been stuck for two years on the tool AI vs agent AI fight and I want to see what an actual resolution document would look like. One camp says we should build systems that answer questions, complete tasks on request, and hand control back -- no persistent goals, no long-horizon autonomy -- because staying in the tool regime is what keeps us safe, and anything that plans over hours or acts in the world without check-in is where the real risk lives. The other camp says that's both hopeless and backwards: hopeless because users will just chain tool calls into agentic behavior anyway, so the safety win is an illusion, and backwards because a well-trained agent with explicit objectives is actually more predictable and steerable than a tool whose goals are hidden in a prompt and change with every request. Write the joint statement these two camps should be able to sign after a real adversarial collaboration: the numbered list of claims both accept (including about what "tool" and "agent" actually track -- maybe they agree the distinction isn't really one thing), the disagreements stated exactly, with each one worded so its own side would sign it, the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether the dangerous properties (goal pursuit, situational awareness, resisting correction) come bundled with agentic architectures or come from scale/training regardless of architecture, and another is which regime gives humans better oversight in practice -- and for each crux a concrete test or argument both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If you think part of the dispute is just verbal (e.g. they mean different things by "autonomy"), say so and say which part. End with a disagreements table.
