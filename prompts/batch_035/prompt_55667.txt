bit of an odd ask for me, I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, so go easy on the jargon. but here's my situation: I keep getting into arguments where someone compares the current superpower rivalry to the run-up to World War I, and I want to actually understand what's doing the work in that comparison.

the story I've pieced together, partly from books like The Guns of August (barbara tuchman's famous one about the first month of WWI, came out in 1962), goes something like this: in the summer of 1914 a bunch of great powers that had spent years building rival alliance systems and war plans stumbled into a catastrophic war basically in a few weeks, mostly because of rigid mobilization timetables, mutual distrust, and leaders who felt locked in once the machinery started moving. the analogy people draw now is: rising power, incumbent power anxious about losing its spot, tangled alliances, weapons that get more destructive every year, automation shrinking decision windows -- so obviously we're "sleepwalking" into WWIII the way they slept walked into WWI. there's even a catchy phrase for it, the "Thucydides trap," I think coined by a political scientist named graham allison (spelling might be off), whose whole pitch is that when a rising power threatens a ruling one, war is the usual outcome -- Athens vs Sparta being the original case, from Thucydides' history. he's been arguing for years that China vs the US fits the pattern.

the kicker is that the analogy apparently has a good track record of actually changing history. the folklore is that president kennedy read tuchman's book during the cuban missile crisis in october 1962 -- the thirteen days where the US and USSR came closest to nuclear war -- and that it shaped how he handled it, helping him resist advisers who wanted air strikes and push for the blockade-plus-backchannel route instead. so this one analogy is credited with helping avoid a nuclear war. meanwhile the same analogy, applied to today, is used both ways: some people say it means we should be maximally firm with the rival (never appease, remember munich, stand firm or you get walked over) and others say it means we should slow everything down and give rivals off-ramps (remember 1914, nobody wanted the war and everyone got it anyway). same analogy, opposite prescriptions, which already makes me suspicious.
