The exam question was: "Explain the setup of Davidson's radical interpretation: what the interpreter starts from, what a theory of interpretation is supposed to do, and why Davidson thinks the principle of charity isn't just a helpful heuristic but something an interpreter can't avoid relying on. Then explain what this is supposed to show about global skepticism, i.e. the worry that most of our beliefs might be false."

Here's what the student wrote:

"Radical interpretation imagines a linguist confronted with a speaker whose language she shares nothing with. Unlike Quine's radical translation, the interpreter isn't translating but interpreting: she is constructing a theory that makes the speaker's utterances and beliefs intelligible at once. The interpreter's data are the speaker's attitudes of holding true sentences, observed in concrete circumstances. Because beliefs are attributable only in relation to sentences held true, and sentences mean what they do only given the beliefs they express, meaning and belief must be fixed together, which is why Davidson is a holist about both. To get from the data to a theory, the interpreter uses Tarski: a theory of truth for the speaker's language generates T-sentences like the one for snow, and a theory that delivers such sentences counts as a theory of meaning. Since the data are just patterns of assent, the interpreter first correlates the speaker's hold-true dispositions with the stimulus conditions that prompt them, as Quine's field linguist does, and once these correlations are in place the T-theory can be read off of them. The principle of charity then says the interpreter should assume the speaker's beliefs are by and large true and coherent, and where the speaker's beliefs conflict with the interpreter's, the interpreter should trust her own view of the facts. This is no mere convenience: if you attribute mostly false beliefs, the hold-true data can no longer be explained by what is actually the case, so the interpretation loses its footing. Charity is therefore constitutive of interpretation. The payoff against the skeptic is that interpretation itself guarantees that most of a speaker's beliefs are true, so the skeptical hypothesis that most of our beliefs are false has been empirically refuted by how interpretation actually works."
