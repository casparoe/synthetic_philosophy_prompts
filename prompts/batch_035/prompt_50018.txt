for the last session of my philosophy of mind reading group — we've spent weeks on Dennett and the colour phi phenomenon, and I want to close with a poem instead of another discussion. 14 to 20 lines, no fixed form. I want it to enact the dispute rather than just describe it: in the first half, describe the illusion itself — two spots flashing in alternation, red then green, a bit apart, and the subject seeing something move between them, the colour seeming to shift mid-path before the second flash has even occurred. then, near the end, have the poem bring back one of its own earlier lines — but slightly revised, so the reader genuinely cant settle whether the first version or the revision is what the poem "really said." dont have the poem tell us which one is true, and dont have it referee between the Orwellian and Stalinesque readings; the point is that the question of when the experience actually happened may have no clean answer, and the poem should leave that open the way our discussions kept doing. it should still work as a poem for someone whos never heard of Dennett. use british english throughout (colour, realise, etc.)
