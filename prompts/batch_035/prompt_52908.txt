I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background, but I run a lot of A/B tests and got pulled into statistics arguments during the replication-crisis coverage a few years back. Over time I've ended up with a set of positions that the people around me treat as obviously inconsistent, but I can no longer tell which of their complaints are real and which are just tribal signaling. Here is the set. (1) I'm a Bayesian at bottom: probability is degree of belief, rational inference is updating by Bayes' rule, and it's legitimate to update as data arrives rather than waiting for some pre-set endpoint. (2) At the same time, I distrust informative priors in scientific contexts: when a published result turns on a substantive prior my confidence in it goes down, and I think analyses in science should mostly let the data speak. (3) At work I enforce fixed sample sizes, no peeking, alpha 0.05, and I have told my teammates, in all seriousness, that peeking at a running test invalidates it — I mean that as a claim about right practice, not merely as company policy. (4) I think the replication crisis was caused by researcher degrees of freedom — flexible analyses, optional stopping, trying specifications until something clears the bar — and not by p-values being defective as such; the famous misreading of a p-value as the probability that the null hypothesis is true is a user error, not a flaw in the tool. (5) Even so, I believe the right long-term fix for science is to move away from null-hypothesis significance testing toward Bayesian machinery — posteriors and Bayes factors in place of p-values. (6) Somewhere along the way I read the claim that the evidential content of a dataset should not depend on the researcher's intentions — in particular, not on whether they planned to stop at a fixed sample size or planned to stop once the numbers looked good — and that struck me as obviously correct.

Does this package hang together? Please go through it pair by pair: tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they conflict, and rank the real tensions by severity rather than presenting everything as equally bad. For each real tension, say what would have to give and roughly what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. I honestly don't know where the problem is, if there is one, so please don't assume I'm attached to any particular item: the two people who argue with me about this — a coworker who thinks everything frequentist is a scam, and a friend in academia who defends NHST to the death — each insist the fix is obvious, and I've stopped believing either of them.
