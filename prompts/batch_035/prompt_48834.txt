For the past two years our department has run a termly workshop on anthropic reasoning, and its two most stalwart participants have dug into opposite trenches on the doomsday argument. One — call her the Carterian — holds that the doomsday reasoning is sound in outline: given that roughly a hundred billion humans have lived before me, and treating my birth rank as a random sample from the pool of all humans who will ever live, the posterior tilts sharply toward a comparatively early doom; her quarrel is only over how strong the tilt is and how it interacts with ordinary empirical evidence. The other — the self-indicationist — holds that the argument is undone before it starts, because conditioning on finding oneself to exist at all shifts the priors back in favor of hypotheses under which there are more observers, and that this shift exactly or approximately cancels the doomsday update. They have reached the stage of arguing past each other: the Carterian accuses her opponent of double-counting the evidence of her existence, and the self-indicationist accuses the Carterian of treating a sampling assumption as if it were a constraint on how the world must be. At the end of last term I agreed to referee a proper adversarial collaboration between them, and I want to give them — and subsequently my graduate seminar — the joint statement they should have been able to produce.

Please draft that statement. It should contain, in order: a numbered section of claims both parties can sign — I would expect them to be able to agree on the Bayesian skeleton (that the dispute is about priors and conditioning, not about any error of arithmetic), on the irrelevance of the exact figure for past births, on the fact that their disagreement ramifies into other anthropic puzzles (they should each sign something about the application of their position to the Sleeping Beauty problem and to fine-tuning, even though the analogy is contested), and on the empirical modesty point — namely, that neither party's conclusion licenses a prediction of doom so precise as to be testable against near-term demographic data. Then a disagreements table with rows for each residual disagreement, each row phrased twice: once in wording the Carterian would accept for her own view and once in wording the self-indicationist would accept for his. I want the cruxes isolated and made operational. At least one crux I expect to be the reference-class question — whether the Carterian's sampling premise requires choosing a reference class (all humans? all mammals? all observers?), whether that choice is a free parameter, and whether the doomsday conclusion survives arbitrary widening or narrowing of it. Another, I suspect, is what exactly the fact of one's own existence is evidence of, and to whom. For each crux, specify at least one argument, thought experiment, or stylized case that both parties have already agreed bears on it, and record each party's prediction in advance of working it through — I have in mind stipulated cases where a coin flip determines whether a world contains ten observers or a thousand, since both parties have conceded that such sealed-universe cases isolate the formal issue from the empirical mess. If any portion of their dispute strikes you as verbal — for instance, if part of the disagreement dissolves once they distinguish sampling assumptions from indexical claims — say so explicitly and identify which rows of the table would survive the disambiguation. Please keep the tone of a document both parties could actually put their names to: no adjudication of who is right, no advocacy, and no formulations so bland that neither party would dissent from them.
