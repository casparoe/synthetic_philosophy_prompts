The deliverable I need is the itemized assessment, not a general impression. Here's the answer, typed up as-is:

"Contextualism and pragmatic encroachment both say that whether you know depends on more than just truth-related factors like evidence. The difference is about whose context matters. Contextualism, held by philosophers like Jason Stanley, says that 'knows' is context-sensitive like 'I' or 'tall': the standards that a knowledge attribution must meet are set by features of the attributor's conversational context, so if I say 'Bob knows the bank is open on Fridays,' whether that's true depends on how demanding the standards are in my conversation. Pragmatic encroachment, defended by Keith DeRose and also Fantl and McGrath, keeps 'knows' with a single invariant standard, but makes that standard partly practical: how much is at stake for the subject — how much their actions depend on being right — changes whether they know. So in DeRose's bank cases, the couple who urgently needs to deposit a check before leaving on a trip doesn't know the bank will be open, because their stakes are high, while the relaxed couple who could easily come back tomorrow does know. Since stakes are part of the subject's context, and context includes practical context, pragmatic encroachment is really just a special case of contextualism where the context that matters is the subject's rather than the attributor's. This means the two views always make the same predictions about the truth of any given knowledge attribution — they only disagree about the underlying mechanism. A case that could distinguish them would be one where the attributor's conversational standards and the subject's practical stakes come apart, e.g., a low-stakes speaker attributing knowledge to a high-stakes subject. But since the two views agree in all such cases, no such case can actually distinguish them, and the choice between the views is basically stylistic. Both views also avoid skepticism, since the skeptic can simply be told that in her high-stakes context she doesn't know, while we in ordinary contexts do."
