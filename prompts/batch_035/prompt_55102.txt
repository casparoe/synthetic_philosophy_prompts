I teach an upper-level seminar on the metaphysics and epistemology of modality that closes with two-dimensional semantics: Kaplan's character/content distinction, Stalnaker's diagonal proposition, and then the Chalmers–Jackson revival. For the final exam I want one essay question, answerable in about ninety minutes, on whether the disagreement between Stalnaker's original use of the two-dimensional apparatus and epistemic two-dimensionalism is substantive or merely verbal. The question must not be answerable by reciting the primary/secondary intension machinery. Students should have to say what, precisely, the two frameworks disagree about: whether Stalnaker's diagonal propositions and Chalmers's epistemic intensions are the same function under different names, and, if not, where they come apart — in what the rows of the matrix are (possible worlds serving as contexts versus epistemically possible scenarios), in whether the diagonal content of a statement like "water is H2O" is knowable a priori, and in what each framework counts as a "strong necessity." The question should also force a verdict on Stalnaker's complaint that the epistemic application changes the subject, because the only things accessible a priori in the neighborhood are metalinguistic claims about words and their uses rather than intensions of the original statements, and a verdict on what turns on the answer: whether the apparatus can underwrite a general link between conceivability and possibility, and whether the epistemic reading threatens reductive physicalism or merely relabels the necessary a posteriori.

Please supply three things: the question exactly as it would appear on the exam paper; a model answer of about 600 words that argues for a position rather than surveying the literature; and a marking scheme. For the marking scheme, give me exactly five numbered items with nothing before or after them: each item should be one or two sentences, name one must-hit move, state the marks it is worth out of a total of 20, and identify the standard way students fail to make that move.
