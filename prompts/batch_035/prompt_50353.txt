I help run a reading group that just finished Naming and Necessity, and I want a handout that sorts members by what they actually believe about origins, not by whether they like Kripke. Please build a decision tree where every node is a concrete case someone can answer without knowing any of the labels — e.g. could the person who'd have resulted from my mother's egg and a different sperm from my father have been me, or would that just have been someone very like me who was never born? Cases to work in somewhere: the same gametes but conceived three months later; a frozen embryo implanted a decade late; a lab-built person with my exact DNA sequence but no causal chain back to my actual gametes; and an IVF mix-up where my embryo got swapped for a genetically distinct one at the clinic. The leaves should be the named positions — strict gamete-pair essentialism, genetic-essentialist views on which the DNA is what matters, cut-off views where origins can differ a little but not much, and full origin inessentialism — plus, if the logical space contains them, combinations nobody actually holds (say so, and take a guess at whether that's because the combo is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored). Two extra constraints. First, a couple of the questions should be answerable differently by a counterpart theorist than by someone working in standard quantified modal logic — half the group is Lewis partisans and I want the handout to expose that difference, not paper over it. Second, flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really separate; I'd rather print a caveat then pretend the cases do more work than they do. Seven or eight cases total, each kept to a sentence or two.
