I've been in the LessWrong-adjacent akrasia threads since forever — the ones where half the commenters freeze their credit cards in blocks of ice and the other half are running Beeminder and taking modafinil to stop doomscrolling — and it's slowly driven me nuts that people use "akrasia" to mean like five totally different things. Some people clearly mean ignorance (if you REALLY grokked that the pizza was bad, you wouldn't eat it), some mean your discount curve doing exactly what it evolved to do, some mean a genuine contradiction inside the will, and a suspicious number mean "I have a preference and am sad about it," which barely seems like a theory of anything.

So for my blog I want a "which theory of weakness of will do you actually hold?" quiz. 8 to 10 questions, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options, and then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions: the Socratic view (akrasia is always some kind of not-knowing), the partition-of-the-mind views where one motivational system genuinely loses to another, the hyperbolic discounting view where the problem is just preference reversal across time and bundling is the fix, the deflationary "rational preference revelation" view (there's no weakness, only discovering what you wanted), and a willpower-as-strength view if you can keep it distinct from the others.

Cases I want in there somewhere:

- hard precommitment: person hands a friend $500 payable if they smoke a single cigarette, signs up for a commitment app, that whole deal. The key question is whether the precommitment itself is clean rationality or something that needs its own defense
- procrastination on something you endorse the whole time you're procrastinating it — no appetite, no craving, just akrasia about a value
- addiction where the person sincerely reports wanting to quit and keeps failing anyway
- a compulsive checker — does compulsion even count as akrasia or is it a neighbouring thing
- someone whose reflective values changed but whose behaviour hasn't caught up, e.g. they stopped believing years ago and still can't make themself leave the church
- a "breaks the seal" case where one donut matters mainly because of what it predicts about tomorrow

No question should just ask "is akrasia real" or anything where you need to know the theory names to answer — someone should be able to take the quiz cold and get sorted by what they say about cases, not by which label they've already adopted.

Two more things. Flag any pair of positions your questions can't actually seperate — I suspect the hyperbolic discounting view and the willpower view will collapse into each other unless one question is specifically about whether temptation is a force resisting you versus just being an ordinary preference that happens to be judged from a later moment. And include at least one case where two positions give the same verdict for opposite reasons; I want the scoring key to exploit that to split them, or else own up that it can't.
