Write a parody of enterprise software release notes, in the deadpan register of a corporate developer portal, roughly 600 words, for a product called Mind. The successive major versions correspond to Daniel Dennett's four kinds of creatures, and since some readers of the changelog may be new to the product line, define each build inline: Mind 1.0, the Darwinian build, ships with hard-wired responses, all learning done by the vendor across product generations, at the expense of individual users who field-tested the bad versions; Mind 2.0, the Skinnerian build, adds within-lifetime learning by reinforcement, contingent on timely rewards and punishments; Mind 3.0, the Popperian build, adds an internal simulator so that candidate actions can be discontinued inside the sandbox instead of in the wild; Mind 4.0, the Gregorian build, adds the ability to import thinking tools designed by other minds — culture as a third-party app store. The humor must come from getting the epistemology right, so that a reader who knows Dennett's generate-and-test tower laughs rather than winces: each entry should register the genuine costs and pathologies of its rung — the 1.0 entry candidly noting that users who installed it badly were discontinued and that no individual user ever benefited from the fixes; the 2.0 entry reporting mis-learning from coincidental rewards and dependencies on prompt feedback; the 3.0 entry flagging divergence between the simulated environment and the real one, so that simulations die in the user's stead only when the model is good; the 4.0 entry acknowledging that third-party tools are of uneven quality, that some of them jam rather than sharpen thinking, and that the build is useless in regions without other 4.0 users — a vendor lock-in on the customer base itself. Stay deadpan throughout, with no winking at the reader, and respect the fact that the versions are cumulative rather than replacements: the changelog should make clear that 4.0 creatures still run their 1.0 instincts and 2.0 habits underneath. Close with a Known Issues section and a deprecation notice that, on inspection, turns out to be impossible for any user to act on.
