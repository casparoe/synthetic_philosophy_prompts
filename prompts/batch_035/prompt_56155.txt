Write a satirical internal memo from the COO of a giant corporation, addressed to all employees, announcing a reorg of the Executive Boardroom -- the one room every department's reports get routed through, where only a single slide deck can be on the screen at any moment, and whatever's on the screen gets broadcast to the whole company while everything else waits in the queue. The humor should come from getting global workspace theory right, not from generic office jokes: the bottleneck and why it's supposedly a feature, the winner-take-all competition between departments desperate for their ten seconds of broadcast time, being "famous" (i.e., on the screen and reportable to the CEO) as the only path to funding and follow-up actions, and the thousands of regular employees who do serious work every day that never makes it to the room and thus, per the org chart, never happened. Give a recurring role to the newly created Phenomenal Experience division, which insists it's contributing something vital that no metric, auditor, or org chart can detect -- play Block's access/phenomenal distinction straight without ever naming it, and let the reader decide whether the division is doing real invisible work or just has great branding. End with Facilities refusing to close a work order asking why the boardroom exists at all; the ticket keeps getting reassigned to itself. Maybe 600 words, deadpan corporate memo voice, with the reorg framed as bold and overdue. A cognitive scientist should recognize every gag; someone who's never heard of GWT should still find it funny. Don't wink at the reader.
