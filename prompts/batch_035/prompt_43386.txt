so, full disclosure up front: I'm a novelist, not a philosopher, and I'm in over my head, which is exactly why this needs doing properly rather than me winging it.

the background: my next book is about a woman who spent her whole career as a planetary astronomer, and in 2006, at the IAU general assembly in prague, she watched a floor vote kick her life's object of study out of the planet club. (pluto, obviously.) while researching her I fell into thomas kuhn, and now, partly because of the book, I've somehow ended up refereeing between two friends who have been arguing about this stuff for years and have long since stopped hearing each other. my editor suggested they try an adversarial collaberation and produce one joint document, and I said I'd commission it, and they said fine, but I have to write up the terms of reference first. that's what I want from you.

the two of them: one is a kuhnian of the later, weaker variety — he talks about local incommensurability and something like a no-overlap principle, where after a revolution the old and new taxonomies can't quite be mapped onto each other in certain places, but he does NOT hold the wild reading on which nobody across a divide can understand anybody at all. the other is a scientific realist of roughly the popper-putnam-davidson flavor: theory comparison is always possible in principle, translation failure is an empirical hiccup and not a deep fact about science, and if you can't say which of two theories is better, that's a fact about you, not about the world. (her words, more or less. she's like that.)

what I want is the document they'd both actually put their names to. specifically:

1. a numbered section of agreed claims, about the history of science as such — real cases both accept happened and meant something: copernicus, oxygen vs phlogiston, the way mass means something slightly different in newton versus einstein. and if they can agree on anything about what the 2006 vote actually was, I want that in there too, because it's the case I actually care about for the book.

2. a disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated so that its own side would sign the wording. I've been burned before by phrasings where one side gets to describe the other's view for them, so I want every row basically self-attributed.

3. the cruxes. I suspect one is whether the pluto thing is a miniature paradigm shift or just bookkeeping — a definitional stipulation by a committee, which even my kuhnian friend admits might be too boring to count. another is what to say about the truth of sentences like the claim that there are nine planets, printed in a 1990 textbook: false all along? true then and false now? true only relative to a taxonomy? that one matters to me enormously, because my protagonist has to be able to insist her old papers weren't lies. and if any part of their whole fight turns out to be verbal — I've started to suspect the word incommensurable itself does a lot of sneaky work — I want that said out loud, with which part and why.

4. for each crux, one concrete case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I'd suggest ceres for the pluto crux: it got called a planet when it was found in 1801 and was quietly dropped from the list later in the century once the asteroids started piling up, all without any vote or manifesto. if the two of them can't agree on what to predict about how that case differs from the 2006 one, that failure itself goes in the document. honestly it would be the most useful thing in it for me, because my book is about a person, not a committee, and I need to know whether her world changed or just her filing system.

keep it in plain english — these two slide into jargon when unsupervised and my editor will cut it. no bibliographies, no reading lists, no citations; I want claims and reasoning I can check against the book I'm writing, not homework.
