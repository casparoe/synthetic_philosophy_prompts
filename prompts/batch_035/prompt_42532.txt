I have been reading the rationalist blogs and forums since roughly 2009, long enough to have followed the modesty debates in real time and to have accumulated a mental catalogue of named argumentative pathologies. There is a configuration I keep encountering in actual disagreements, though, for which I cannot find a settled term, and I would like to know whether one exists anywhere in epistemology or argumentation theory — and if not, I would like a serious attempt at coining one.

The pattern has two steps. First, B answers A's object-level argument not with a counterargument but with a diagnostic: people who produce arguments of this shape, with this flavor of confidence, have historically been wrong, so the existence of the argument is itself evidence against its conclusion. Second, when A objects that the diagnostic was gerrymandered — that the reference class was assembled by looking at who turned out to be wrong, which makes its track record circular — B replies that only someone in the grip of an inside view would claim the class was rigged against him, and treats the objection as a further datum. At that point every move available to A gets absorbed: the object-level case, the complaint about the class, and even silence all count as evidence for B. The most recent instance that set me off: a friend with a detailed model of a technical forecasting question was told, in sequence, that models of this kind have a poor track record, and that only a person in the grip of such a model would challenge how the track record was assembled. He had no remaining response that was not evidence against him, including the response of pointing that out.

I know several near misses, and none quite fits. Kafkatrapping shares the self-sealing structure but concerns moral guilt and aims at extracting acknowledgment; here nobody is accused of anything, the user is sincere, and the output is a credence. A fully general counterargument is general because it is logically vacuous; this one is general through its two-level design, in which the diagnostic covers first-order evidence and, separately, all reactions to the diagnostic. Reference class tennis is a contest between two outside views; here there is only one, and the dispute is over whether it is challengeable at all. And Popperian unfalsifiability attaches to theories, whereas this is a dialectical posture that renders someone else's position unfalsifiable by proxy.

So, three questions. Does this configuration have an established name, in the academic literature or in the community corpus? If not, coin a term with a definition that cleanly separates it from each of the near misses above. And most importantly: what principled marker, if any, distinguishes the legitimate use of an outside view — where the class was fixed and validated in advance, out of sample — from this degenerate form? I am suspicious that the only available marker is retrospective, and I would like to be wrong about that, but I want the answer to argue the point rather than console me.
