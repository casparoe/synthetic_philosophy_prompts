I write a column on science and ideas, and I'm preparing a long piece on self-locating belief — the question of how evidence about one's own temporal or personal situation should bear on ordinary propositions about the world. To organize the piece, a colleague and I worked through a family of cases built around the Sleeping Beauty problem, and I now have a page of gut verdicts that I suspect do not all fit under one principle. I would like to know whether they do.

In the standard case — Beauty is put to sleep, a fair coin is flipped, she is awakened once if heads and twice if tails, with amnesia between awakenings, and asked each time for her credence that the coin landed tails — my gut says one-third. In a variant where she is told, on waking, that it is Monday, I am confident the answer is one-half. In the extreme version, where tails leads to nine hundred and ninety-nine awakenings and heads to one, saying one-half feels absurd; her credence in tails should be very close to one. Yet where she is awakened exactly once on either outcome — Monday on heads, Tuesday on tails — the answer is obviously one-half, and no self-locating reasoning seems to do any work. Where she is awakened on both days no matter how the coin lands, but interviewed only on Monday if heads and on both days if tails, my gut still says one-third, even though being awake can no longer be evidence of anything, since she would be awake under either outcome. Two further verdicts pull against each other: if she is offered an even-money bet on tails at every awakening, with payouts accumulating across repeated ones, taking the bet seems clearly right; but the halfer thought — that a guaranteed experience cannot be evidence, so she should stay at one-half — strikes me as sound whenever I stare at it, and I cannot see how it and the betting verdict can both be true. The case I keep going back and forth on: the coin is flipped not on Sunday night but on Monday, after the first awakening — she is awakened Monday regardless, and on Tuesday only if tails — where my gut says one-half on Monday, even though from inside her pre-toss perspective the setup seems indistinguishable from the standard one.

My tentative principle is that her credence should match the share of indistinguishable awakening-experiences, across all the ways the coin could go, that occur under tails. Please test that principle against my verdicts, repair or replace it as needed, and state plainly which of my verdicts the surviving version cannot accommodate. I would also like to know what it predicts for a case outside the laboratory framing of my piece: whether the fact that I happen to be among the first hundred-odd billion humans ever born should raise my credence that humanity's run is unusually short.
