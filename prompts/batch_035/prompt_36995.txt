the cases i can think of off the top of my head that it has to handle, but you should add ones i'm missing: lottery tickets (you have a losing ticket, 99.99% credence you lost, but something feels wrong about flatly asserting "you lost" -- why?), the gettier-adjacent guy who has justified true belief and asserts it happily, moore-paradoxical assertions ("p but i don't believe p" -- true, maybe, but unassertable?), self-assertion and prediction markets (can you assert about your own future assertions), and what happens with hedging (does "i think p, 60% confidence" count as an assertion at all or a different speech act, and if the latter does the norm only apply to flat assertion -- which seems to let people launder low-credence claims through hedges and then act like they called it).
