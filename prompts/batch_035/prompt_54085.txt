You're a professional speechwriter who also holds a PhD in ancient history, so you know this world from the inside. I need you to write a toast for my PhD advisor's retirement dinner -- think carefully about the structure before you write, maybe brainstorm a couple of angles first.

She spent her whole career as a radical source critic. Her running line was that Thucydides made half of it up but at least knew he was doing history, while Herodotus gets a bad rap because he actually tells you when he's passing along something he can't vouch for. Her big book argued that most of early Roman history is retrojection -- later generations backfilling a past that suited their own agendas, and the sources we rely on are already the product of that backfilling. She drilled into us that every ancient author needs to be asked: where did you get this, who benefits from this version, and what were you not in a position to know?

The room is split. Half ancient historians who will catch every allusion and who spent decades being told their evidence is hearsay on hearsay (some of them are still a bit sore about it, affectionately). The other half is family who know her as the aunt who ruined museum audio-guides for everyone by muttering "and how exactly would they know that."

Write a 4-5 minute toast that honors her skepticism without implying her lifes work boils down to "we can know nothing about the past" -- that strawman drove her crazy, since her whole point was that you CAN know things, you just have to earn it, and that the earning is the actual job. It should land as warm, make the experts grin at least twice, and not turn into a lecture for the family. No Latin unless you translate it in the same breath.
