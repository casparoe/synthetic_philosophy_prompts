You are a political philosopher who specializes in theories of distributive justice, with a side practice writing institutional satire in the style of Kafkaesque government correspondence. Write the FAQ page of a fictional agency, the "Directorate for the Benefit of the Worst-Off," published alongside its annual report announcing that the gap between rich and poor has been narrowed by a record margin while making no one better off.

The directorate is officially and fiercely prioritarian: it insists that its sole concern is the moral importance of benefits to those who are worse off, weighted by how badly off they are, and that it takes no interest in equality as such. But the FAQ's answers should keep betraying a telic egalitarian underneath: the agency celebrates narrower gaps for their own sake, books the better-off's losses as "accrued equality" in its ledger, and reassures readers that no one was harmed because — and here the technicality should be airtight — the worse-off's positions were left exactly as they were, which the report describes as "a holding pattern of great moral significance." The humor must come from the actual dialectic rather than from invention: Parfit's leveling-down objection and the utilitarian's challenge that one state of affairs is worse for everyone and better for no one; the difference between caring about the badly off and caring about the gap; whether the better-off's losses appear anywhere in the social welfare function at all; the distinction between the badness of a person's position and the badness of their life; and the persistent temptation to treat "no one is worse off and someone is better off" as the only test the directorate could ever fail. Include at least one question from a fictional utilitarian auditor demanding to know why total welfare fell, one from a confused member of the public, and one from an internal sufficientarian colleague who has been reassigned. At some point the directorate should announce a leveling-up pilot program and explain, without embarrassment, why it was discontinued.

Constraints: the directorate must never state a falsehood about the facts, only about its own motives; every evasion should be a technically true sentence. A specialist in distributive justice should find the satire fair — recognizing that pure prioritarianism does not actually endorse leveling down, and that the joke is the agency's slow slide into valuing equality while denying it — and a general reader should still find it funny. Roughly 700 words, formatted as numbered Q&A entries.

Begin directly with the piece itself, with no preface, commentary, or framing of any kind.
