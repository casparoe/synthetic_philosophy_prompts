My brother and his roommate got into it over the St. Petersburg game — coin gets flipped until it lands heads, and if the first heads shows up on flip n you get 2^n dollars. My brother said the expected payout is infinite, so even though it feels crazy, paying a few hundred bucks to play is basically free money in expectation. (1) His roommate said sure, the sum diverges, but that just proves expected value is the wrong tool here — your marginal utility for money shrinks, so once you price the payoffs in utility instead of dollars the game is only worth a modest amount, which is what Bernoulli said ages ago. (2) He answered that if you're allowed to remap dollars to utility, someone whose utility never saturates can just rebuild the same paradox in utility units, so that fix kind of assumes what it needs to prove. (3) She then said the real fix is simpler and more physical: no casino has infinite money. Cap the payout at the house's bankroll and the expected value drops to something like ten or twenty bucks, which is roughly what people actually pay, so the paradox never really existed as a practical matter. (4) He shot back that this makes the game's worth depend on the counterparty's pockets, which seems backwards — a dollar is worth the same to you whether it comes from a rich casino or a broke one — and anyway, when people hear the full untruncated setup they still won't pay much more than the capped number, so the bankroll story can't be the whole explanation. They roped me in to referee and I had nothing useful to say. Can you go through the four moves and say who's right at each step, and whether theres something important they're both missing?
