Sicily feels different: the assembly debated it properly, took a vote, and even gave the skeptic Nicias a command, yet it was the biggest catastrophe of the three — though I can't decide whether a terrible decision reached by fair procedure is a failure of democracy or just a failure that happened under it. My tentative principle was roughly that democracy fails when passion overrides debate — that covers Mytilene's first vote and gets overturned by the correction, but it says nothing about Sicily, and it scores Socrates's orderly trial a success. Is there a principle that captures all three of my verdicts, or close to it? If not, which verdicts should I give up, and in particular, should the corrected Mytilene decision count for the system or against it?
