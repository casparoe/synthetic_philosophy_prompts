I write novels, and my friend Dana — also a novelist, the kind who writes food scenes you can practically taste — has this habit of ruling on whether a person's experience of a thing changed, or just their attitude about it. Her track record so far: her ex, who has always said cilantro tastes like soap, she says his experience of it is genuinely different from hers, not some herb phobia. Her dad, who hated bitter beer at 25, drinks IPAs happily at 60, and swears it tastes exactly as bitter as it ever did and he just doesn't mind it anymore — she ruled no change in the experience itself, only in the liking. Her aunt, who insists tomatoes tasted better in the summers of her childhood — she ruled the aunt misremembers, the experience back then was probably the same, the memory is doing the flattering. Our friend Priya, who was on a medication for a month that made everything taste like coins — that, Dana ruled, was the experience itself changing. And her mother, who after cataract surgery said the streetlights finally looked white instead of yellowish — changed, obviously, Dana said.

Now I've got three new people for her, and I want to predict her rulings before I ask (I'm writing a novel with a perfumer in it, so this is research, sort of). One: a sommelier I know who says that after ten years of training, the same cheap wine she drank at 22 tastes completely different now, she can hear the whole song where before it was just noise — and she claims no bodily change, only training. Two: a friend who came out of a long depressive stretch and says that for months music sounded flat, the same songs he'd always loved, and then one day it opened up again. Three: me. My favorite color used to be a certain orange, and lately it looks duller to me, and I honestly cant tell whether my eyes changed, my memory is flattering the orange, or I just wore the color out.

What I'd like from you: what rule or rules seem to fit Dana's record (I suspect there's more than one that fits it equally well), what each of them predicts for each of the three new cases, your best guess at her actual rulings and why, and — since I get to pose her one more hypothetical to settle which rule she's really running — what hypothetical would split the candidate rules most cleanly? For what it's worth, I once read about a coffee taster who could never tell whether the coffee had gotten worse or his memory of it had, and that's sort've the vibe here. Dana, as far as I know, has never read a page of philosophy.
