I'm in a graduate seminar on consequentialism and its critics this semester, and we just wrapped up a three-week unit on Bernard Williams's integrity objection -- the Jim and the Indians case, George the chemist who can't get a job except in CBW research, the doctrine of negative responsibility, the idea that my projects are what gives my life meaning and a morality that just folds them into the best aggregate outcome seems to alienate me from my own agency. My professor has now assigned us a weird final project: we have to simulate an adversarial collaboration, which apparently is this thing where two people who disagree hard agree on a joint statement of what they do and don't disagree about, plus predictions they register in advance so they can't weasel out later.

Here's my setup: one party is a sophisticated indirect consequentialist in roughly the Railton mold, who thinks the best outcome is produced by agents who mostly don't reason like consequentialists in daily life, and who wants to absorb the integrity objection by saying the theory operates at the level of what dispositions and decision procedures it's best to have, not what you consciously deliberate with. The other party is a Williamsian critic who thinks this concedes too much or too little -- if the theory only kicks in at the level of justifying whole systems of agents, the Williamsian wants to know what it was supposed to justify about any particular act in the first place, and whether the husband who saves his drowning wife is really just lucky that his attachment happened to track the optimal arrangement. We also read Williams's later remark about the husband saving his wife, where in one sort of context bringing in the utilitarian justification alongside the fact that she's his wife looks like one thought too many, so you should work that in too.

Please draft the joint statement these two would actually sign: a numbered section of claims both accept (about what negative responsibility amounts to, about which of Williams's cases do or don't show what, about the distinction between decision procedures and criteria of rightness), then a disagreements section in table form where each disagreement is worded so its own side would endorse the phrasing -- the consequentialist shouldn't get to describe the integrity objection as a psychological complaint and the Williamsian shouldn't get to describe consequentialism as alienating by definition. Then lay out the cruxes, and for each crux say what argument, case, or consideration both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. And if you think part of their fight is verbal -- e.g., over what counts as a justification as opposed to an explanation of an act -- flag which part and say why. Keep it in American English please. My seminar paper is 15 pages so something substantial is fine, and honestly the more precise you are about the Jim case the better, since my professor grades hard on getting the case details exactly right.
