Me and a friend argued til like 2am about Brexit and I'm still chewing on it. Her claim was that the whole 2016–2019 saga is basically a case study in the populist conception of the will of the people: one people, one voice, a 52–48 split gets converted into a single unified mandate, and anyone who gets in the way -- the judges in the Miller case (and the tabloid that branded them enemies of the people), MPs blocking no-deal, the people marching for a second vote -- gets recast as an elite frustrating the popular will rather than part of the demos. I kept pushing back that this is just what referendums do, majoritarian politics, not populism per se. So: analyze Brexit through the lens of the will of the people. Who was supposed to count as the people, what the theory says each actor was doing, and which parts of what actually happened it genuinely explains. But also be honest about where it breaks -- e.g. the 2019 election produced a landslide for get Brexit done with a different coalition than the 52% (loads of Leave-Labour seats flipping, etc.), so was that the same will of the people or a different one, and can the theory even tell? And tell me if the theory ends up explaining too much, where literally any outcome counts as the will of the people surfacing or being blocked.
