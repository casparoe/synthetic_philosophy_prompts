I had better say who is asking. I spent a working life as an experimental physicist and have, since retiring, taken up philosophy of probability. I keep encountering the Judy Benjamin problem and its cousins: an agent with settled prior probabilities is told, not a plain fact, but a conditional — in the canonical case, that if she is in Red territory, there is a three-quarters chance she is at headquarters — and must revise on that alone. Conditioning on the material conditional gives a result everyone finds absurd, and the various repairs on offer each satisfy some intuitions and betray others. Rather than sign up with one of the schools, I want the specification written out first.

Write the desiderata: what any rule for updating on an asserted conditional must deliver. Cover at least the following, with the case behind each: it must not raise the probability of the antecedent when only the consequent clause is being vouched for; it must agree with ordinary conditionalization whenever the testimony can be recast as a straight conditional probability or a likelihood; it must keep the speaker's reliability separable from the content of the conditional, since in the laboratory a technician's remark that if the gauge is drifting the fault is thermal is partly evidence about the gauge and partly evidence about the technician; it must give either a determinate answer or an honest interval when the assertion is qualitative ('probably', 'in most cases'); and it must not turn on an arbitrary choice among rules that fit the same constraints. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are prejudices of particular camps, and say whether some subset is jointly unsatisfiable — that is the part I most want to see, since the literature appears to keep discovering one further intuition that no single rule can accommodate. Fix adequacy against this purpose: a rule good enough that a working scientist could apply it to testimony, not one that settles every dispute about conditionals. Please write in British English, and give no bibliography — I will trace the references myself.
