I am a retired physical chemist who came to philosophy late, after a career spent treating every measured quantity as a value plus an error budget. I have been reading the literature on well-being, and I find that my laboratory habits transfer poorly: philosophical positions are usually argued for, but rarely costed, and I miss the discipline of being forced to say how much of a conclusion rides on each auxiliary assumption. I would like you to give me that treatment for one case.

The case is a version of the experience machine, with the parameters fixed so that the usual evasions are unavailable. A woman of thirty-five is offered the remainder of her life in a machine that will simulate experience indistinguishable from ordinary life: she will seem to have friends, work that succeeds, and a partner who loves her, and she will seem to make free and consequential decisions. The operators guarantee, and are stipulated to be perfectly reliable, that the simulated life will contain somewhat more pleasure and somewhat less pain than her real life would, that she will never at any point suspect the deception, and that her entering the machine imposes no costs on anyone else beyond their missing her presence. Stipulate further that she has a stable, reflectively endorsed second-order desire that her projects actually succeed in the world and that her intimate relationships be with people who really know her — and that this desire would persist, in the machine, as a desire about her apparent life that is systematically false in the machine and would have been true outside it.

The proposition I want priced is this: that declining the machine and living out the real life would be better for her, all things considered — better for her own sake, prudentially, not morally, and not better for the world.

Give me your credence as a number, and then show the decomposition, in the manner of an error budget. At minimum I want to see: your probability that hedonism is the correct theory of well-being, and, conditional on hedonism, the probability that the real life is better for her (note what the stipulation about quantities of pleasure does to this conditional); your probability for the desire-satisfaction family of views, and, conditional on those, the probability that satisfaction of the second-order, world-involving desires is a genuine component of well-being rather than something the machine's false satisfaction can adequately substitute for; your probability for objective-list theories, and, conditional on those, the probability that contact with reality, or actual achievement, is on the list. Include any further terms you think belong — for instance, a term for the possibility that our intuitions about this case are contaminated by status-quo bias, or by the distaste for deception that I notice in myself and do not trust; there is empirical work suggesting people's answers to machine-type cases shift substantially when the case is described so that their real future life would be the worse one, and I would like to know how much weight you give to the thought that the intuition is tracking the truth about well-being rather than tracking the framings. Then combine the terms explicitly and give the final number.

Two further stipulations, each with its own conditional, if you are willing: first, if the machine is described as one she would enter knowingly each evening and leave each morning, with no deception at any point, does your credence that the real life is better rise, fall, or stay fixed, and which term in the decomposition does the change come from? Second, if instead of a woman of thirty-five the subject is a person about to be created, choosing once and for all between a whole machine life and a whole real life of equal apparent content, does your number change, and should it?

Finally, tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to, and what would move it most. For calibration: my own credence is around 0.55, held with low confidence, and I suspect the whole of my uncertainty is concentrated in the second-order-desire term rather than in the choice among theories. Tell me whether you think that is where my number is most likely wrong, and in which direction.
