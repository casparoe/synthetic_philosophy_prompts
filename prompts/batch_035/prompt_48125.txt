I'm leading my book club through Kazuo Ishiguro's The Remains of the Day next month, and I want to frame the discussion around a problem from the philosophy of causation rather than the usual themes: causation by omission. The standard puzzle is that omissions seem to cause things — the gardener's not watering the plants killed them — even though absences can't literally do anything, and since everything that didn't happen is a candidate cause, something has to select the salient omissions, and on the most developed views what does the selecting is norms: expectations, duties, standards of what a person in that role ought to do. Stevens is a man made almost entirely of omissions: he does not intervene when Lord Darlington orders the dismissal of the two Jewish maids even as Miss Kenton declares it a sin and threatens to resign; he stays at his post serving downstairs while his father is dying upstairs; on the evening Miss Kenton comes to his study to announce her engagement, he declines to be disturbed from his reading. I'd like a reading that asks whether the novel dramatizes the norm-relative account of omission-causation: Stevens organizes his own life around failures measured against professional duty alone, while the reader is forced to weigh the same non-events against moral and human norms, so that his tragedy is a mismatch between two different systems for picking out which absences matter. Two complications I'd like taken seriously rather than waved away: whether Lord Darlington's order pre-empts Stevens's non-intervention, so that on some analyses Stevens isn't a cause of the maids' dismissal at all — and whether the novel itself takes a side there; and whether a norm-relative account of what his omissions caused does something distorting to Miss Kenton, whose own agency in choosing to leave and to marry becomes invisible. Please also say honestly where the novel resists the lens — whether Ishiguro presents Stevens's failures as causes of what follows, or only as objects of regret — and whether the book ends up contributing anything to the philosophical debate that the counterexamples in the literature don't. Which scenes should I point the group to?
