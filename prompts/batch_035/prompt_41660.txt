so my friend and i have been going back and forth on origin essentialism for like a month and it's gotten weirdly personal. the question, roughly: could I have come into existence from a different sperm and egg than the ones I actually came from? kripke-style views say no — your actual origin is essential to you, so the guy who'd have been born from a different sperm is at best a sibling-like counterpart, never you. my friend says that's obviously crazy, since the alternative zygote would've been 99.999% similar to me, raised in the same house, etc., and identity shouldn't hinge on which one of millions of sperm won the race.

for reference: my credence that I could NOT have come from a different gamete pair is about 0.7. hers is maybe 0.15. I want your number on the same proposition, but honestly the number is the least interesting part — what I want is the decomposition. I'd expect the credence to factor into things like:

1. whether personal identity over time in the early, pre-personal stages (zygote, blastocyst) is even a factual matter or partly conventional — parfit-ish stuff about how maybe there's no fact of the matter whether "I" existed at the one-cell stage at all
2. whether de re modal claims about contingent beings like us can coherently vary the origin at all, or whether varying the origin just describes a different possible world containing someone else
3. whether the kripkean route — necessity a posteriori, rigid designation for organisms — actually extends to origins or was hand-waving even by his own lights, since he basically just asserted it with the famous "could this very chair have been made of ice" intuition pump
4. and whether the whole question is answerable from the armchair or whether it's a verbal dispute about what we mean by "you" dressed up as metaphysics

also, two wrinkles I'd want you to weigh in on since they're where my friend and I actually fight:

- the twinning problem. zygotes can split, and one zygote clearly can become two people. if identity with the zygote is that fragile, doesn't that undercut the thought that the specific gamete pair matters so much? or does it show nothing, because origins could still be essential even when the origin-stage thing isn't identical to the person?

give me a rough probability for each sub-question (and any others you think belong in the decomposition), how they combine to your final number, and tell me which single term the number is most sensitive to. also: given your decomposition, where do you think the 0.7 vs 0.15 split between me and my friend most likely lives — which sub-question would we be disagreeing about if we made all our other assumptions explicit?
