I'm a science and ideas journalist, and I keep noticing a pattern in how fringe claims spread that I can't find a proper name for. When a high-profile misinformation claim gets debunked — by a major news outlet, a prominent scientist, a well-trafficked fact-check — the claim's reach grows. The debunking is the vector: the fringe claim travels inside the rebuttal, reaching millions of people who would never have encountered it on its own, and the people behind the claim know this, so they actively court debunkings, sending their material to scientists and journalists precisely in the hope of being publicly refuted. It's not the Streisand effect, which is about attempts at suppression drawing attention; here nobody is suppressing anything, and the refutation is accurate and well-intentioned. It's also not the backfire effect, which concerns individuals failing to update their beliefs; this is about the aggregate reach of the claim, which can grow even if most of the people exposed to the debunk correctly reject it. Is there an established term for this configuration — where a claim's transmission is boosted by its critics rather than its supporters, and where refutation functions as the main channel of replication? If memetics or any adjacent literature has a concept for it, I'd like to know how it's defined and how it differs from the things I mentioned that it's not. And if there's no established term, I'd be grateful if you could coin one — with a definition precise enough that I could apply it in an article without it collapsing into 'bad publicity is good publicity'.
