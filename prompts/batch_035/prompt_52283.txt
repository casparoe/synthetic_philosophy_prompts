At a dinner party last weekend, two friends of mine, Maya and Dev, got into it over what emotions actually are, and as a bystander I honestly couldnt tell who was winning. It started when Maya mentioned her mom: her mom was furious for a week at a neighbor who she thought had snubbed her at the grocery store, and then found out the neighbor literally hadn't seen her — and the anger evaporated on the spot. Maya said that shows emotions are judgments: her mom was angry because she judged she'd been wronged, and the moment the judgment went, so did the anger. She added that it runs the other direction too — you can talk people out of anxiety by changing what they believe, which wouldnt make sense if emotions were just bodily stirrings. Dev said hold on — he's terrified of turbulence. Not mildly; white knuckles, even though he genuinely believes, and can recite the statistics, that flying is safer than the drive to the airport. His fear argues directly against his own judgment, and it wins. He said that makes emotions look more like perceptions: perceptions can persist while you know better — the Müller-Lyer lines still look unequal after youve measured them — and his fear keeps presenting the flight as dangerous after he's decided it isn't. Maya said maybe there's a stale, subpersonal judgment hanging around, one he can't introspect, and the fear is tracking that. Dev shot back that this makes her theory unfalsifiable: any emotion that contradicts the person's stated beliefs gets "explained" by a hidden judgment nobody can ever independently check for, so what would ever count as evidence against her? He also pointed at the phenomenology — fear isn't just the verdict "this is dangerous," it's an urgency, a racing heart, a pull to do something — which is why people take a drink or do breathing exercises before a flight, and that calms the fear without changing a single belief. Maya answered that bare feelings can't be the whole story either, because emotions are about things: you're afraid of the turbulence, not merely having palpitations, and a body that's just churning doesn't point at anything — whereas clearly his fear is pointed at the plane. And there they stalled, both appealing to me, and I had nothing.
