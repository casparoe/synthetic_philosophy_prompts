In my philosophy of mind course we just started the hard problem of consciousness, and a guest speaker argued that we've been framing it backwards. The brain, he said, is not a producer of consciousness but a receiver, like a radio picking up a broadcast: the music doesn't originate in the circuits, and the station exists whether or not any set is tuned to it. On this view the hard problem is a pseudo-problem — our puzzlement about how physical processes could give rise to experience arises only because we've wrongly assumed the brain generates consciousness rather than tuning into it. As evidence he cited two facts from neuroscience: localized brain damage degrades specific contents of experience rather than dimming experience globally, and changing brain chemistry changes the character of experience. Both, he said, are exactly what you'd expect from a damaged or mistuned receiver, and neither shows the signal originates in the apparatus. My professor was sympathetic to taking it seriously but remarked that the analogy seemed to be doing all the work in his argument, and she noted that versions of this idea — the brain as a kind of filter on some wider mind — have a long pedigree. I have to write a response paper and would like to audit the analogy properly. Could you lay out the correspondence table — what is supposed to play the role of the signal, the station, the receiver, and the mistuning — and then say where the mapping breaks? I'm especially interested in which features of radios the analogy quietly imports that brains seem to lack, and I have two candidate disanalogies I'd like assessed: first, a radio works the moment it's built, whereas the capacities for experience appear to come online gradually, in step with neural development; second, a receiver's sensitivity to damage is fixed by its circuitry, whereas the fine-grained mapping between particular neural events and particular contents of experience looks like something the brain itself implements. Finally, does the speaker's conclusion — that the hard problem dissolves once you adopt the receiver view — survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or does the view merely relocate the problem from the receiver to the station?
