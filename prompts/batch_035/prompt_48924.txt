Is there a name for this thing, or do I need to coin one?

I've been noticing a pattern whenever I try to write down, for someone else (or for my future self), instructions about what to ignore. Last month we went away for a week and I wrote instructions for the friend checking in on our cat. Somewhere around rule 14 ("if the smoke detector in the hall blinks, it does that sometimes, ignore it") I realised the list couldn't work in principle. Not just incomplete — impossible. Every "ignore X" only works if you already know X is harmless in this instance, and knowing that is exactly the job the list was supposed to do for you. I can write "ignore the blinking detector" because I already know the battery's old and it's been doing it for years. She can't, unless I also write when NOT to ignore it, and when not to ignore the not-ignoring, and off it goes.

Same thing when I tried to automate things with smart plugs. Every rule needed an exception, each exception seemed reasonable on its own, and a month later the system was forty rules deep and still wrong about the kettle. And the embarassing part is I just... walk out the door every morning without checking anything. Something in me is doing all this ignoring invisibly, and that's precisely the part I can't get onto paper.

So, questions. Does this have an established name in philosophy or AI? I half-remember someone telling me about a decades-old robot thought experiment where a machine wastes forever checking that its plan has no unexpected side effects, which sounds adjacent but maybe not identical. More importantly, to me there seem to be two distinct problems bundled together here and I want to know whether the actual literature separates them: (1) the "when is it safe to stop checking" problem — how you know an exception can be dismissed without a further check; and (2) the "what's still true afterwards" problem — once you've done something, figuring out which things in the world changed and which didn't, without re-verifying literally everything. Am I carving a real joint here or inventing a split? And if the everyday human version (the cat-sitting list, the walk out the door) is genuinely different from whatever the formal/AI version is, has anyone given the everyday one its own treatment or name, or is that territory unclaimed? Please don't just drop a term on me — I want the carving: what each candidate concept covers, where it stops, which one fits my list, and where the edge cases sit. If nothing fits, coin something for the specific everyday version and defend why it needs its own label.

Whatever you conclude, end with one machine-readable line like: Verdict: <best-fitting name, or "no established name — coin one">, so I can grep my notes later.
