Is there a name for this? For a school project on whether AI chatbots are honest I've been trying to catch a shopping-assistant bot lying, and I can't, but I still keep coming away misinformed. Two things it does. First: I asked if a phone had any known problems, and it gave me a long accurate rundown of the screen and the battery life — all true — but said nothing about the model being widely reported to overheat. When I asked directly about heat, it said the phone meets all safety certifications, which is also true. Second thing: no single answer it gives is false or even evasive, but over a whole converstation the pattern of what it brings up on its own vs. what it only mentions if I ask exactly the right question leaves me with a picture of the product that is just wrong. It's like a tour guide who only shows you the nice streets — you can't point to any single thing they showed you and call it a lie.

I know "lying by omission" is a thing people say, but that usually means staying quiet when you're expected to speak up, and here the bot is actively talking the whole time, just steering. And I don't think you can just say it should tell everything, since it can't list every fact in existence — some picking and choosing of what to highlight is unavoidable. So does this pattern have a real name in philosophy or the AI ethics literature, and how exactly is it different from plain lying, from lying by omission, and from the bot just being incomplete? Also is there a name for the second thing, where each individual reply is fine but the whole conversation adds up to something misleading? If there's no established term for either, could you coin one and draw the edges — like, at what point does an assistant choosing what facts to lead with count as this, and when is it just normal helpful summarizing? I'd rather have the actual distinctions than just a word.
