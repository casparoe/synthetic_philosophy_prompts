I'm putting together a sorting quiz for my philosophy discussion group's next session on free will, and I want it built around Galen Strawson's Basic Argument (Galen Strawson, not his father P. F. Strawson — I'm not thinking of the reactive attitudes paper). In case it helps you calibrate: the argument runs roughly that to be truly responsible for what you do, in the way that would make you genuinely deserving of praise or blame, you would have to be responsible for how you are in the relevant respects — your character, motivations, and so on. But to be responsible for how you are, you would already have had to have done something that made you that way, which would require an earlier self with a different character that you'd also need to be responsible for, and so on, until you'd have to have created yourself — been the cause of your own existence "from nothing" — which is impossible. The upshot Strawson draws is that no one is ever morally responsible in the strong, desert-involving sense. Our group is split between people who think this is obviously right, people who think it's a seductive word game, and people who accept it and simply don't care, so I need an instrument that will actually sort us.

What I want is a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz of eight to ten questions. Each question should be a concrete case or thought experiment with two or three answer options, phrased so someone can answer it without knowing any of the position labels — the quiz should sort by what people say about cases, not what they call themselves. The branch points I care about are at least these: whether the person accepts the ultimacy requirement (that genuine desert requires responsibility for how you are in the relevant way), whether they accept that the regress to causa sui is inescapable (agent-causal libertarians think an agent can originate a choice without inheriting character from anything prior — your quiz should test whether someone takes that escape, and whether they notice Strawson's rejoinder that even a self-created being would be responsible for its self-creating choice only if it were responsible for how it was when it made it), whether ordinary responsibility can be preserved once the strong sense is given up, and whether the "extended self" reply works — the idea that responsibility attaches to the temporally stretched-out person who shaped their own character over time, rather than to any momentary slice. The leaves should include at least: Strawson's own impossibilism (true desert is impossible, and we should admit it), hard determinism about desert paired with a consequentialist or communicative justification for keeping some blame and punishment practices anyway, compatibilism that simply rejects the ultimacy requirement as a manufactured standard, the extended-self view, and agent-causal libertarianism. Also flag any two leaves your quiz can't really separate, and note any combination of answers that no one plausibly holds, with a one-sentence diagnosis of whether that's because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored.

Please deliver the whole thing as a JSON object with four fields. Field "questions": an array of objects, each with "case" (a string containing the scenario) and "options" (an array of two or three strings). Field "scoring_key": an object mapping option-combination patterns to leaf names. Field "positions": an array of objects, each with "name", "core_claim", and "awkward_commitment" (the single commitment a defender of that leaf has the hardest time swallowing). Field "unoccupied": an array of objects with "answer_pattern" and "diagnosis" as defined above, plus a final field "cannot_separate" listing any leaf pairs the instrument fails to distinguish and why. Keep the cases vivid but short — a sentence or three each — and make sure at least one question forces the answerer to confront the regress directly rather than skating past it on intuitions about blame's usefulness.
