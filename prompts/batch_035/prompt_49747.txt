I'm on the patient-family advisory council at the hospital where my mom was treated last year (she's fine now, thanks for asking). The hospital is rolling out a new "standardized overnight care" policy: at 8pm all visitors leave, and the night nursing staff takes over everything — feeding, turning, repositioning, comfort stuff. The stated reasons are safety, liability, and fairness: not every patient has a family member who can stay, and the nurses' union reps have been pretty firm that families doing overnight care is a coverage gap the hospital is quietly exploiting. Some nurses also told us privately that families hovering at night actually make their jobs harder in ways people don't appreciate.

On the other side, a bunch of us family members have been arguing against it. One woman on the council said her husband, who has advanced dementia, stops eating entirely when she's not the one holding the spoon — a stranger holding the spoon is a different thing entirely, he won't do it. My own view, and I want this to be in the memo because it's the reason I'm leaning the way I am: I've come to think there's a difference between care as a *task* and care as a *relationship*, and the policy treats them as the same thing. A task can be handed off like a relay baton. A relationship can't be handed off — it's more like a rope, where the whole thing goes slack if either end lets go. If I'm right, then "the nurses will do the same care at night" is sort of technically true and completely missing the point. I don't know if anyone in the philosophy world has said this before, probably someone has, but I came to it myself sitting in that room and I think it's the crux.

So here's what I need. Draft the memo I'll circulate to the council before our vote in three weeks. It should recommend a concrete bottom line — endorse the policy as-is, endorse it with specific exceptions (and say what they'd be and who qualifies), or push it back to the hospital with conditions. Whatever you recommend, I want the reasoning to take the nurses' side seriously, not just as a legal footnote — the fairness point about patients without families is a real one and I don't want us looking like we only represent people who happen to have devoted relatives. If you think my task-vs-relationship thing is doing real work in the argument, use it, but also tell me straight if it's weaker than I think it is, because if it's going to get torn apart at the meeting I'd rather find out from you first. Keep it to about a page and a half — these people are volunteers and nobody reads more than that.
