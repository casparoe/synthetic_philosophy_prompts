my sister's husband works in end-of-life care and lately he's gotten really into this greek philosopher, epicurus. he pulled the letter up on his phone and there's a line in there — 'death is nothing to us' — and the bit that goes 'when we are, death is not come, and, when death is come, we are not.' he's been using it two ways. one, to tell my mom (she's 76 and has gotten really scared of dying this year) that her fear is basically a mistake, like being afraid of a room she'll never walk into — nobody is ever 'there' to have death happen to them. fine, whatever, i can feel the pull of that one.

but the second use is the one i cant swallow. he says the same logic means it doesn't matter whether my mom goes at 76 or at 96. if there's nothing bad about being dead, then 20 more years of living isn't something she'd be 'missing out on', since she won't be there to miss anything. and he adds that the endless time before she was born didn't hurt her either, so the endless time after should be the same. i feel like the one-liner must be smuggling in a bigger claim somewhere, but every time i try to pin down where it goes from 'you won't feel anything' to 'so the lost years aren't a loss', he says i'm just demonstrating how powerful the fear of death is, not that death is bad.

so what i want is this: take 'death is nothing to us' apart and lay out the distinct actual claims that one sentence could be making, from the small safe ones (you won't experience being dead) up to the huge ones (nothing about your death is bad for you, not even dying young, not even the part where your family sits at your funeral) — and for each one, say whether it's plausible and whether it actually gets him the two things he's using it for. especally tell me if there's a reading that keeps the calming-my-mom part but blocks the 76-vs-96 part, because that's the one i'd bring back to him. and please be straight with me about what's settled versus still genuinely fought over among people who study this for a living — if most of them think death IS bad for you even in the strong sense, say that plainly instead of treating it like a free-for-all where every reading is equally respectable.
