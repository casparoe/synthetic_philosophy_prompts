I run a small hobby philosophy blog and want to make one of those "which theory of material constitution do you actually hold?" quizzes. Write me eight questions, each a concrete case — a Lumpl/Goliath style squashing, the clay existing on the shelf before the sculptor starts, Tibbles and his tail, the ship of Theseus, a person vs their body, maybe a statue created and destroyed in the same instant — with two or three answer options each about how many objects are in play and what survives what. Then give me a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the real positions: coincidence (statue and clay are two things sharing a location), constitution-is-identity, sortalism, four-dimensionalism where they differ only in temporal extent, nihilism, relative identity, and the deflationary "this is just a verbal dispute" option. The whole point is sorting readers by what they say about cases, not which label they like, so no question can just ask "do you think the statue and the clay are two objects?" — the options have to be about what happens in the scenario, phrased for general readers with zero jargon. Two more things: flag any pair of positions the quiz can't actually separate no matter how someone answers, and identify at least one answer combination you'd expect nobody to end up in, with a sentence on whether that's because it's incoherent or just unattractive. Its fine if one or two questions are invented variants rather than the classics, as long as they do real sorting work.
