I've just spent an entire evening arguing with a friend about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism, and I've come away more convinced than I should probably be of a particular line of resistance. My view is now this: the heterogeneity objection is overrated, because it conflates a pleasure's feel with its objects and accompaniments. There is, I want to say, a single common phenomenal tone present in everything from eating a good peach to solving a proof to schadenfreude; what varies is everything that tone is attached to — the sensory qualities, the intentional objects, the conative states running alongside it. My friend thinks this is introspectively wishful: she says that when she actually attends to her pleasures, she finds no shared residuum at all, just a motley, and that the "common tone" is a posit theorists need rather than something felt.

I'd like you to take the role of a philosopher of mind who specializes in well-being and the phenomenology of affect, and tell me what would genuinely count against my position. Please start by brainstorming the full space of possible defeaters before organizing them. I'm interested in: which introspective findings, which cases (false or deceived pleasures, bodily versus attitudinal pleasure, anticipatory pleasure), and which results from other areas — say, about whether pleasure is even a natural kind in psychology — would tell against the common-tone view; which of these a committed defender of my position would concede as counting against it versus dismiss as a misunderstanding; and whether the view can be qualified into unfalsifiability, for instance by retreating from a shared feel to a mere determinable, and whether that retreat would be a problem for a view of this kind. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about how exposed my position actually is — don't just confirm me.
