You are to act as an experienced mediator of adversarial collaborations, combining the temperament of a professional philosopher with a working knowledge of screenwriting practice. Think carefully before drafting, and begin by brainstorming the candidate formulations each side could sign without wincing before committing to any of them.

Draft the joint statement emerging from a year-long adversarial collaboration between two camps that have argued past each other for decades over what we owe the real dead when we dramatise them:

- **The fidelity camp** — broadly, the view that representing a real, identifiable dead person carries a standing duty of accuracy: fabrication about the dead wrongs them (or their surviving communities), and audiences are owed a truthfulness the marketing rarely provides.
- **The licence camp** — broadly, the view that dramatic representation is art, not testimony; the dead have no interests left to injure, audiences knowingly import genre conventions, and compression, conflation and invention are the price of narrative form. What matters is fidelity to the shape of a life, not to the record.

The statement must contain the following, in this order:

1. **Agreed claims.** A numbered section of claims both camps can sign. These must include, but not be limited to: the shared datum that under English law the dead cannot be defamed, and that both camps regard this legal fact as settling nothing either way; and an agreed working distinction between three sorts of departure from the record — (a) compression and conflation dictated by form, (b) invention of particular events, and (c) what the collaboration has come to call *composite falsehood*, where every scene is individually defensible but the cumulative portrait is false (e.g., depicting a subject only ever alone and thereby implying a solitude the record contradicts). Both camps should be able to accept this taxonomy as marking real differences in kind, even while disagreeing about which of the three matter morally.

2. **A disagreements table.** For each remaining disagreement: the precise formulation, worded so that its own side would accept it; whether the other side regards the disagreement as genuine or as resting on an ambiguity (and if the latter, which term is ambiguous — the camps suspect at least one dispute turns on the word "wrongs," as between wronging a subject and wronging truths about them, and the table should say whether they ended up agreeing); and which side, if either, conceded a purely verbal component during the year.

3. **Cruxes.** At least three, and each must be a sub-question such that settling it would move at least one camp. At least one crux must turn on the case the collaboration used as its stress test throughout the year: a biopic of a real, deceased scientist that invents, purely for narrative tension, a scene in which she plagiarises a junior colleague — a scene that is not a composite falsehood, not a compression, but a straightforward invention that lowers the subject in the audience's eyes. The fidelity camp believes its view explains the wrongness here straightforwardly; the licence camp must either accept the case as a counterexample to unrestricted licence or articulate the principle that distinguishes it from permitted invention, and the statement should record exactly how that articulation went and where it now stands. For each crux: the concrete case, argument or piece of evidence both camps agree would bear on it, each camp's prediction recorded in advance, and — crucially — what each camp has signed as the consideration that would genuinely change its own position, phrased so that it is not a face-saving concession of a possibility the camp already holds to be remote.

4. **A fragility note.** A closing section, drafted last, in which the signatories identify where the agreed formulations above are most likely to be slanted toward one camp, which agreed claim is least stable under future pressure, and what the next collaboration should test first. The statement should be candid that the fidelity camp's most likely failure mode is smuggling evaluative weight into ostensibly neutral taxonomy, and the licence camp's is retreating to an unfalsifiable notion of fidelity to a life's "shape."

Constraints: write in British English; keep the whole statement under roughly 1,800 words; do not ask for citations, bibliographies or references to specific published works, and do not lean on any named philosopher or film — the collaboration is to be argued from the cases and principles given here, not from the literature. Where you introduce a coinage or a distinction of the collaboration's own making (as with composite falsehood), flag it as such rather than presenting it as established terminology.
