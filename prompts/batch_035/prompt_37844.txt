I'm a novelist and I've got a problem that's half craft, half I-don't-know-what. Two years ago I abandoned a draft of a novel very publicly — told my agent, my writing group, everyone that the structure just wasn't working, the timeline kept collapsing under me. That was the story I gave and honestly the story I lived by. Last month a friend from the writing group, a bit drunk, said she never bought it — she said the book's protagonist was obviously my late mother and I bailed because it got too close. I laughed it off, but since then I've been quietly testing myself and it's unsettling: when I sit down and ask myself why I really abandoned it, what comes up first is the structural stuff, sure, but it doesn't quite feel load-bearing the way I remember it, and the mother thing, which I'd honestly never consciously registered, has a horrible click to it now, like it was there the whole time.

Here's my actual question. I want to go back to the draft, and to do that well I need to know what happened. But how do I know which story about myself to trust? The structural failure story is what I told myself at the time, in the moment, while it was happening — surely that counts for something? Or does telling yourself a story repeatedly just sand it smooth until you can't feel what's underneath it? And is the "click" of my friend's explanation any better evidence — people get that same click from horoscopes. Basically: is there a principled way for me to figure out which account is the real one, or at least which one to act on, or am I stuck just picking the more useful fiction?

Give me your honest read on the situation, but please end with a last line beginning "Verdict:" and then a short phrase — e.g. which account I should treat as the working truth, or what I should do about the draft.
