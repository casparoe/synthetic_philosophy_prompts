I chair a three-person committee at my climbing club that decides on small expedition grants (up to £400, a handful of applications a year). Our rulebook says a grant is approved if and only if (i) the trip is safe enough and (ii) it gives something back to the club, and it says we settle each of those two questions by simple majority. On last month's application both questions passed 2-1: me and Dara thought the trip was safe, and me and Farah reckoned the payback (a route guide for the club website) was genuinely useful. But a straight show of hands on the actual question — fund it or not — came out 2-1 against, with Dara and Farah each fine with the premise the other one doubted, but not wanting the thing funded overall. So by our own rulebook the grant should go ahead, and I think it's daft to ignore that, but Dara says if two of the three of us dont want to fund it, that should be that — and I can't pretend I don't notice that each of us only favours whichever proccess happens to give the answer we wanted anyway. There's also an awkward practical bit: we have to write to the applicant, and if we reject, every reason we could put in that letter ("we had safety concerns", "we didn't feel it benefited the club enough") is the opposite of what the committee, by majority, actually decided on that exact question.

So where does this leave us? Should the criteria-by-criteria route win because we agreed to it in advance, or is switching to a bottom-line vote after the fact perfectly legitimate? And is there even a fact of the matter about what "the committee" thinks here, given its a different answer depending on how you ask?
