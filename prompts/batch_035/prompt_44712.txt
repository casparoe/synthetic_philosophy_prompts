So this is probably a weird ask but bear with me. I'm a backend engineer and I sit on the grants committee of a mid-sized open source protocol DAO (roughly 500 token holders, maybe 60 who ever show up to vote on anything). Our current setup is the usual thing: people post grant proposals on the forum, committee members vote, everybody sort of vibes it. Honestly it's gotten pretty political — the same five whales swing every vote and there's a whole meta-game of who knows who on discord.

At our last meeting someone dropped a proposal to switch to futarchy for grant decisions, i.e. instead of voting on proposals directly, we'd run prediction markets on whether funding a given grant will move some success metric, and fund the ones the market says will help. The proposal author is a Hanson fan, keeps saying we should vote on values but bet on beliefs, and wants the metric to be a 12-month trailing average of monthly active developers (with some correction for self-reported identities). The committee is split and I got voluntold to write up a recommendation before next month's call.

Some stuff that's bugging me and that I'd want you to actually deal with: our community is small enough that I can imagine one or two rich accounts just pushing the market price wherever they want, since manipulating the market is way cheaper than the grant amounts. Also the active-dev metric feels gameable (people spinning up bot contributors to move it). And there's the whole thing where the people most able to trade accurately on a grant's prospects are... the grant applicants themselves and their friends.

Please draft the memo I'd circulate to the committee. Be concrete: if you think we shouldn't do it, say so plainly and say what (if anything) is worth salvaging for a pilot, like maybe markets that only advise the committee rather than decide. If you think we should, say under what exact conditions. Don't give me a "on the one hand / on the other hand" doc where I have to make the call myself — I want your actual position and your reasons for it, including how you'd answer the whale-manipulation worry and the metric-gaming worry if you think they're answerable. Assume the readers are smart but none of them have read a word of political philosophy, so no jargon without a plain-language gloss. Thanks in advance.
