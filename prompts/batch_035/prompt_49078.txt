I'm writing a philosophy of mind essay and want feedback before I commit to the whole thing. The idea: Dennett's multiple drafts model is always sold as the antidote to the Cartesian theater — no single place where consciousness happens, no inner audience, just parallel content-discriminations that get edited until some acquire privileged status. My claim is that the model quietly smuggles the theater back in, just one level up. Call it the narrative theater: if what makes a draft conscious is that it wins a place in the ongoing narrative stream — the one that gets reported when the subject reports — then that stream functions as the audience. The editorial metaphor already implies a readership: drafts are only worth editing if someone is reading the final product. So something has to read the narrative for narrative fixation to matter, and when you ask what that reader is, you either get a regress (a meta-narrative that needs its own reader) or a homunculus in narrative clothing. Dennett of course has replies — the stream is just a matter of which contents end up available for report and control, no reader required — and the second half of my essay argues that this reply either succeeds (in which case the whole narrative/editorial apparatus was doing no real explanatory work and could be swapped out for plain talk about access) or it fails (in which case the theater is back). So the model is unstable between a deflationary reading and a theatrical one, and neither reading gives you what the model promised.

Things I want from you: (1) Is this a fair reading of Dennett or am I beating a strawman? (2) Is it actually interesting, or is it just the standard "consciousness-of requires a further subject" objection wearing a trenchcoat? I want to say it's genuinely different because it targets the narrative metaphor specifically rather than awareness in general, but maybe Im kidding myself — if the narrative stuff is just illustrative garnish for Dennett, then attacking the garnish proves nothing. (3) Do you think this overlaps with critiques of Dennett that already exist? Not asking for a reading list, just a sense of whether I'm reinventing the wheel conceptually, and if so whether the two-level framing gives me enough of a new angle to keep going.
