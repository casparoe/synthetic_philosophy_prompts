My roommate and I spent half the evening arguing about whether mixed motives dilute moral worth, and I want a thought experiment that would actually settle something between us rather than another round of dueling intuitions. She holds the strict Kantian line: an action has full moral worth only insofar as it is done because it is right, so the purer the dutiful motive, the greater the worth, and warmth of affection can only ever be an inert passenger or a mild contaminant. I counter that once the duty-motive is present and doing real causal work, affection should add rather than subtract: the friend who visits you in the hospital both because she ought to and because she wants to see you seems to me strictly better off, morally, than the one who comes solely from a sense of obligation. Every hypothetical we generated merely stipulated the motive mixture rather than generating it, so we never made progress.

Construct the case to these specifications. It should be a realistic, one-paragraph scenario — caregiving, a workplace, a friendship — in which the agent performs the right action, and her dutiful motive and her affectionate inclination are each individually causally sufficient for what she does, with her aware of both. Add the twist we kept circling but never built: the affection itself was deliberately cultivated by the agent years earlier, in part precisely so that she would reliably act well when duty alone might have flagged.

Then walk through what my roommate's strict view must say, what my own view must say, and what a third party should say who suspects that strategically cultivated affection is just self-management wearing the costume of virtue. Tell me which feature of the case is doing the work — the overdetermination, the cultivation, or their combination — how each of us could most plausibly resist the verdict you reach, and whether deleting the cultivation clause collapses the whole thing back into Kant's own example of the benefactor who acts from duty when sympathy runs dry, or whether something survives the deletion that our dinner argument missed.
