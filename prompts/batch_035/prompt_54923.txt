I write about AI and animal behavior for a general-interest magazine, and I've let myself get roped into running a 45-minute session at a science festival — about 20 attendees, smart laypeople, no philosophy backgroud. The theme is whether a colony can have a mind, loosely inspired by the Hofstadter dialogue where an anteater chats with an ant colony he calls Aunt Hillary. I refuse to lecture. I want a game where each person plays a dumb ant following one purely local rule — no one sees the big picture, no one knows the goal — and the room as a whole ends up doing something recognizably smart, like finding the best path or making a collective "decision." Then a debrief that maps what actually happened onto the real thing: ants as neurons, colony-level signaling, and whether calling that a mind is insight or just a metaphor we enjoy. Materials should be index cards, colored stickers, and a stopwatch, nothing fancier. Tell me the rules, the roles, the debrief questions, and what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it. Also be upfront about where the game cheats — what real ants do that twenty humans in a room can't reproduce. And at the end, say how confident you are in the design and which part you'd least bet on working. Keep the whole thing under 600 words.
