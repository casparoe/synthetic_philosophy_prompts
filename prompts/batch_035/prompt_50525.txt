I've been writing down my views on what makes a being matter morally, mostly to get them straight for myself, and I want a consistency check before I ever defend any of this out loud. Here's the package: (1) moral status depends only on the capacity to suffer and enjoy things — species membership is irrelevant, it's capacities or nothing; (2) that capacity comes in degrees, so status is a gradient, not an on/off switch — the death of an elephant is worse than the death of a mayfly, and not just because elephants live longer; (3) as a practical proxy for where a species sits on that gradient I lean pretty hard on neuron counts — it's basically how I decide wether donating to insect welfare beats donating to chicken welfare stuff; (4) all humans have full and equal moral status, flat — newborns, people with microcephaly, advanced dementia, no sliding scale withing our species, and I get itchy when people start ranking humans against animals; (5) I don't think a given pain hurts more in a bigger brain — pain intensity seems roughly conserved across mammals of wildly different sizes, and my picture of consciousness is something like a serial bottleneck where extra neurons just means more content competing for the same limited attention, not a louder experience; (6) a sufficiently sophisticated AI could in principle have more moral status than a human, if its capacities genuinely exceeded ours. So: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest repair in each case? I'd rather you ranked the tensions by severity than just flagged everything, and be concrete about what each fix would cost me.
