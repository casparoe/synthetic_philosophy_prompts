position piece for my distributive justice seminar is due friday and before I hand it over I want to know if my views actually survive contact with each other. Context: what pulled me into this whole debate in the first place is the sheer gap between how the global rich live and how the global poor live — that contrast seems to me to be the central injustice. But here's the package I've ended up defending:

(1) Prioritarianism — a benefit of a given size counts morally more the worse off the person recieving it is.
(2) I take the levelling-down objection to be fatal to egalitarianism: making the best-off person worse off with nobody made better off can't be an improvement, not even a tiny one. This is basically why I'm a prioritarian and not an egalitarian.
(3) I'm on board with the seperateness-of-persons critique of utilitarianism — there's no collective whose wellbeing is the sum, so one person's serious loss can never just be cancelled out by gains to other people.
(4) The priority weight is fixed by how badly off someone is in absolute terms, not by how they stand relative to anyone else — otherwise it's just egalitarianism with the serial numbers filed off.
(5) Case verdict I'm committed to: take 3 units of wellbeing off a very well-off person and deliver 2 of them to a very badly off person (a unit leaks away in the transfer). I say the resulting world is better.

I can't shake the feeling that something here pulls against something but I honest to god can't see where. Go pair by pair: which tensions are real, which only look real, rank them by severety, and for each real one tell me what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost me. Don't assume you know which commitment I'd drop first. Also please write the answer in British English.
