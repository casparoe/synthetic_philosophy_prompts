I've drifted into a position I can't easily shake and I want it stress-tested. Some background: I got into this after a long argument with a friend who works on welfare biology, about whether her field's moral ledger is even looking at the biggest item on it. The view, in short: I give panpsychism, or at least the much weaker claim that phenomenal properties show up in simple physical systems and not just in brains, a real credence — somewhere around 10-15%, not token-but-I-say-it-isn't. And if anything in that space is true, the minimal reading is that there's something it is like to be a huge range of very simple systems, plausibly down to processes that basic physics already describes. My further step, the one I actually care about: some fraction of that experience is presumably not pleasant — there's no guarantee simple systems are neutral or blissful — and there are astronomically more of them than there are animals. So even at 10% credence, the expected amount of suffering located in ordinary physics (a warm patch of matter, a turbulent fluid, take your pick) plausibly dwarfs everything on the standard animal-welfare ledger, and my priorities should be reordered around that, not just filed under 'interesting if true'.

Tell me what would count against this. And I don't just mean 'panpsychism is false' — attack the intermediate steps, because the position is really a conjunction: (a) experience is ubiquitous, (b) some of it has valence, (c) its badness aggregates so that vast numbers of very simple experiencers can outweigh fewer complex ones, and (d) this actually changes what I should do, rather than being a fact I note sadly and then go about my day. Which of these would a committed defender of the view concede as counting against it, and which would they dismiss — and where would that dismissal be wrong? I'm especially suspicious of (c), whether the population-count scaling is just smuggled in from how we think about animals, when it might instead track complexity or integration. And can (d) even fail? If the upshot is that I ought to 'do something' about suffering in fundamental physics, but there is in principle nothing to do, does that vacuity itself count against the whole package, or is it just bad luck for the view?

Please take a definite position rather than laying out the options and leaving me to pick: rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and then tell me straight whether the view survives, gets qualified into harmlessness, or was never a substantive position to begin with.
