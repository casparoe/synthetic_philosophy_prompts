(a) How much work can be extracted per cycle, assuming quasi-static operation? **Student's answer.** "(a) The expansion is isothermal and quasi-static, so the work done on the piston equals the heat drawn from the bath. Since the volume accessible to the molecule doubles, the work extracted per cycle is W = kT ln 2, all of which comes from the bath as heat.

(b) Taken alone, the engine converts heat entirely into work in a cycle, which would violate the Kelvin–Planck statement of the second law. What saves the second law is the measurement: before the piston can be used, the demon must determine which half the molecule is in, and acquiring this one bit of information inevitably dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat. This was Szilard's own resolution in his 1929 paper, and it is what Landauer later generalized into his principle: any information-processing operation dissipates at least kT ln 2 per bit. Since the measurement cost exactly balances the work extracted, the total entropy of the universe is unchanged over the cycle.

(c) The example shows that thermodynamic entropy and information-theoretic entropy are essentially the same quantity. The entropy the gas loses is exactly compensated by the information the demon gains, so the net entropy change of the universe over a full cycle is zero, and the whole process is, in principle, reversible."
