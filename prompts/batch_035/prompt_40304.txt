The essay question was: 'Elster's Ulysses, bound to the mast, is the classic image of precommitment: an agent, while still rational, arranges things so that a foreseen lapse of rationality cannot issue in action. Assess whether constitutional constraints — a bill of rights entrenched against ordinary majorities, say — are genuinely analogous to the mast, and whether the analogy survives the most serious disanalogy you can identify.'

Here is the student's answer:

'Elster uses the episode of the Sirens from the Odyssey to analyse self-binding. Ulysses knows that when he hears the Sirens' song he will want to steer towards it, and that this desire will be irrational, so he has himself bound to the mast before the temptation arrives. This is precommitment: acting in a calm moment to neutralise the anticipated consequences of a hot moment. The wax earplugs work the same way — Ulysses stops his own ears as well, so that the crew cannot be tempted to follow his orders to change course.

The constitutional analogue is straightforward. A society writes a bill of rights while calm, and this binds later majorities who are swept up in panic — wartime hysteria, say, or a moral panic about a minority. Just as Ulysses' earlier self knows better than his later self, the founders are more rational than later majorities, because the founders have had time to deliberate whereas later majorities act under passion. So the founders' entrenchment of rights is rationally justified in exactly the same way as the mast: the earlier decision should win whenever the two conflict.

The main objection is Jefferson's point that the dead should not rule the living: the future majority is a different set of people, whereas Ulysses is the same man at the mast as he was when he gave the order. But this is a superficial objection, because in law a people is a corporate agent that persists over time, just as a person does; successive majorities are temporal parts of the same continuing entity. Once we see the polity as a single agent persisting through time, the personal and constitutional cases are structurally identical, and the dead-hand objection dissolves. Note also that this is precisely how precommitment works: it operates by changing the future agent's values, so that the future majority internalises the founders' commitments and no longer wants to do what it was restrained from doing.'
