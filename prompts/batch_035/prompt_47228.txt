I advise a small funder that can support roughly eight researchers for three years on conceptual and theoretical work in AI alignment — decision theory, epistemics, formal frameworks — not empirical ML. The funder must decide how to divide effort between two broad framings of the safety problem: an average-case framing, in which misalignment is a tail risk to be managed in expectation (probabilities over failure modes, cost–benefit trade-offs between capability and safety, expected-utility reasoning about deployment decisions), and a worst-case framing, in which one seeks guarantees that hold across every environment in some class (adversarial assumptions, robustness-style bounds, proposals like quantilization, and more recent attempts to say what a "worst case" even means for an agent that learns from data). I want a defended agenda out of this, not a survey.

Specifically, please do the following:

1. Rank the open conceptual questions in this area that most deserve those three years. For each, explain what makes it worth the funding: how many other questions depend on it, how tractable it is for philosophers and theorists rather than ML engineers, and how neglected it currently is.

2. Somewhere in the ranking, address the upstream question of whether the dichotomy itself is well-posed. Consider: whether "worst case over all environments" is coherent for agents that must generalize beyond their training distribution; whether probabilistic safety cases implicitly rest on distributional assumptions that need their own justification; and whether, in the limit of good epistemics, the two framings converge or remain genuinely opposed prescriptions.

3. Say explicitly which alleged implications of choosing one framing over the other are overrated. For instance, does the choice really change what interpretability or oversight research is worth supporting, or does that recommendation follow from either framing equally well?

4. Describe what evidence or developments over the next few years — technical results, deployment incidents, shifts in the field — would most move items up or down your list.

Throughout, defend the ordering rather than merely presenting it: state the criteria you are using, show the trade-offs between them, and frame things so that I could disagree with your ranking by pointing at a specific premise rather than at your conclusions. Please write your response in American English.
