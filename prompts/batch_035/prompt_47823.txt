So I've been arguing with my brother about voting rights at Thanksgiving and realized my own views are kind of a mess, so let me lay out my gut verdicts and see if there's a principle under there somewhere. Here's where I land on cases:

16 year olds voting in local elections, like school board: fine, honestly they probably know more about the school system than I do. 16 year olds voting national: also fine I think, though less sure. 14 year olds voting at all: not fine. Long-term resident non-citizens (lived there 30 years, pays taxes, kids in local schools) voting in local elections: fine. Same person voting in national elections: hmm, not fine? But I can't really say why, which bothers me. Tourists voting: obviously not fine. Someone on a 6-month work visa: not fine. A citizen who moved abroad 40 years ago and never came back, voting by mail in home country elections: fine — I've heard some countries even let expats pass citizenship to kids who've never visited, and that seems fine too. Weird that the guy next door who's lived there three decades gets less than them though.

People currently in prison: fine, they're still getting governed. Felons who've finished their sentences: obviously fine, the disenfranchisement thing is nuts. Someone with advanced dementia voting while a family member "helps" fill in the ballot: not fine, even though I can't point at what's missing, and I know some places allow assistance at the polls and that seems ok in milder cases, so there's a line in there I can't draw.

On the compulsory side: mandatory voting with a modest fine, like Australia has: fine, not my preference but fine. Mandatory voting enforced criminally: not fine. Requiring everyone to pass a civics test before voting: not fine, no — that history is ugly and also who writes the test.

So: is there a principle that gets all or most of this? It feels like it's something about capacity plus stake in the community, but capacity seems to do no work in the 14 vs 16 case, and stake seems to do no work in the expat case, and I genuinely don't know which one is doing the work in the local-vs-national thing for non-citizens. I'd take either (a) a principle that saves every verdict even if it needs extra clauses, or (b) a simpler one that tells me which verdicts to drop — and if you go with (b), I want to know which of my verdicts goes and why that one rather than another. Also tell me what the principle predicts for a couple I didn't list — non-citizens voting national after 10 years, or a rule that says no one over 80 can vote for the first time — and whether those predictions should embarrass me.
