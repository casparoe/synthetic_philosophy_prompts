Here's my opening move. In physics the centre of mass is causally idle -- nothing in the wheel rests on it, it doesn't push back on anything, it's a bookkeeping point. But my self looks load-bearing in a way your analogy can't capture: when I'm half a mile from the end of a run and about to quit, something in me digs in and finishes; when my therapist asks who's doing the worrying, the answer actually changes how the worrying goes. So if the self is just a posit like a centre of mass, who exactly is digging in? It seems like you've explained the self by comparing it to something that, unlike the self, has no causal role at all. Either your analogy quietly smuggles the causal self back in, or it explains the self away at the cost of leaving my half-mile of grit unaccounted for.
