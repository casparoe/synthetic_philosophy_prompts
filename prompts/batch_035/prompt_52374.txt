My flatmate and I argued about this for like two hours last night so I'm hoping someone can settle it. The thing we kept circling: she says she "knows" stuff in some situations and not in others while literally having the same evidence. Concretely: she'll say she knows the back door is locked because she checked it an hour ago and nobody's been home, but when she's about to leave for a weekend and I ask her whether it's locked, she says she should go double check, and when I point out that she checked an hour ago she says yeah but this time it matters. Same memory, same door, same hour — but when the cost of being wrong goes up, she stops counting it as knowledge. And it's not that she forgot checking or got new evidence, she just refuses to call it knowledge anymore.

We also noticed it does something weird when the stakes are mine instead of hers. If I'm the one relying on her door-checking because I'm the one coming home late, she's fine saying she knows it's locked, even though a wrong belief would now cost *me* a lot — the word seems to track what it costs her to be wrong, not what it costs whoever she's talking to. Except other times it seemed to track something else again, like how bad it would be to have said "I know" out loud and then be caught out, which is embarassment about the speech act rather than the stakes of the thing itself. And there was one case we could not agree on: if the mistake is cheap to undo — say she can check a camera on her phone and let someone in remotely if it turns out she was wrong — she happily goes back to saying she knows it's locked, even though being wrong is just as likely as before. So is this pattern tracking stakes at all, or is it really tracking whether the error is fixable?

Her theory is that it's all just vagueness — that "know" is a threshold word over confidence, high stakes make people second-guess themselves, so her actual confidence genuinely drops and there's nothing deeper going on. I half buy that, but it doesn't seem like the whole story, because she'll admit her confidence is exactly the same as an hour ago and still refuse to say she knows. So: does this have an actual name in philosophy, and if so, which concept is it exactly? I've heard people say something about how words like "know" shift depending on who's talking and why, but that doesn't sound right either, since here it seems to be about her situation and not mine. Whatever the nearest concept is, does it distinguish the sub-cases we stumbled into — stakes for the believer vs stakes for the listener, costly-to-be-wrong vs embarrassing-to-have-claimed, fixable vs unfixable errors — or does it just lump them all together? If nothing out there carves it exactly this way, I'd take a name and definitions for the different versions too, because "my flatmate won't say she knows the door is locked" is getting old as a way to refer to this.
