The exam question was: "The 1859 Preface to A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy says the economic base conditions the legal, political and ideological superstructure. Does historical materialism therefore claim that the base explains everything else? In your answer, distinguish (a) the claim that the base determines which superstructures come into existence from (b) the claim that it determines their specific content, and assess what work, if any, Engels's later letters do for the thesis."

Here is the student's answer, typed out as recieved: "Historical materialism is often summed up as: the economic base explains everything else. On the standard picture the base is made up of the productive forces — the technology, tools, raw materials and labour power available to a society. The relations of production, i.e. who owns and controls the means of production, belong on the superstructure side of the line, since ownership is ultimately a legal relation, constituted by property law. The superstructure is then everything else: the state, the legal system, religion, philosophy, the dominant ideas of the age. Marx says in the 1859 Preface that the base rules only in the last instance, with the superstructure able to react back on it, so the base's influence is real but not exclusive. Does the base explain everything else? If the last-instance qualifier is taken seriously, the thesis cannot be that the base is the only cause at work, because Engels's letters explicitly allow the superstructure to influence the base in return — political institutions can speed up or slow down economic development. But if the superstructure feeds back into the base, and property relations sit in the superstructure, then the very relations that were supposed to constitute the economic structure are themselves superstructural. It follows that the base explains the superstructure only through the superstructure, and a thesis that explains something only by way of the thing it was supposed to explain is not doing any explanatory work. So the strong reading — the base explains everything — collapses, and what is left is the weak reading: the base sets limits within which superstructures vary, without determining their content. Since last instances never actually arrive in history, the weak reading is probably all historical materialism ever needed, and it is also all Marx can defend."
