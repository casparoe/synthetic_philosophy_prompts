Got into an argument with a friend about the "why Britain and not China" question — industrial revolution happens first in Britain, China had printing, big cities, advanced markets, centuries of technological lead before that, so why there and not there? — and he deployed an argument I can't refute but also can't accept. Goes like this:

(1) An answer to a contrastive question — why X here rather than there — has to cite some feature present here and missing (or weaker) there.
(2) Take any candidate from the literature: coal near the population centers, high real wages making labor-saving machines worth building, a continent of rival states vs. one big empire, patent law, merchant culture, whatever. For each one you can find some other time and place that had the same feature and no industrial revolution — cheap coal elsewhere, merchant-friendly cultures elsewhere, fragmented rival states elsewhere, sometimes for centuries.
(3) So no candidate is sufficient for industrializing.
(4) And since industrialization happened exactly once, there's no way to establish any of them was necessary either.
(5) But a factor that is neither sufficient nor necessary explains nothing.
(6) So there is no answer to why Britain and not China — the question only feels answerable because we already know how it turned out and are retrofiting.
(7) Worse, the same goes for every one-off in history: the fall of Rome, the outbreak of WWI, you name it. So either that whole kind of history is non-explanatory, or something above is wrong.

He also tacks on that the choice of contrast is arbitrary anyway — why Britain vs China rather than Britain vs the Netherlands, which was richer and still didn't industrialize first — so the question is badly posed even before you try to answer it.

I'm fine with a weak reading of (6), that a lot of the popular answers in this debate are just retrofitting. But I don't buy that the question itself is fake. Which premise should I give up, and what does giving it up cost? My instinct says it's (5), and that contrastive explanation works differently from sufficient-cause explanation, but I'd want the price spelled out — if a factor can explain the divergence without being sufficient for industrialization, what stops literally anything from explaining it?
