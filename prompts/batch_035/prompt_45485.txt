I've become pretty convinced by the Rietdijk-Putnam style argument for the block universe, and I want someone to stress-test it. Quick recap of the view so we're on the same page: in special relativity there's no fact of the matter about what's happening "right now" far away from you, because observers moving relative to each other slice spacetime into nows differently, and their slices through a distant region can disagree about which distant events have "already" happened. Putnam's move (building on Rietdijk back in the 60s) was to say that if my "now" includes a distant event that's in your future, and things that are real relative to real observers are real simpliciter, then your future is real -- so tenseless eternalism, the whole 4D block, just follows from the physics. No flowing time, no objective becoming, the future is as fixed as the past.

So what would actually count against this? I want the full map of where the argument is exposed, not just the standard textbook pushback. Some things I'd especially like you to address:

1. Physics defeaters. What would it take for the relativity premise to fail in the relevant way -- e.g., a detectable preferred rest frame, or a Lorentzian interpretation where the one true foliation exists but is hidden? Do cosmic time in cosmology, or GR stuff like Gödel's rotating universe where there's no coherent global now at all, help the block view or complicate it? And does anything about quantum mechanics (collapse, dynamical collapse models, whatever) give objective becoming a foothold again?

2. The philosophical step. Even granting the physics, the argument needs an extra premise about "real for an observer" transferring to "real, period." How exposed is that step? I've heard people say it smuggles in an assumption about simultaneity being an equivalence-like relation that spreads reality around -- is that a real flaw or a nitpick? Would a defender of the argument concede this is where all the work happens?

3. Rivals. There's the "point presentist" move where only the here-now is real, so distant-now talk never comes up, and various "relativized presentism" or "perspectival" views. Do any of these actually fit the physics AND keep enough of a flowing-time picture to be worth wanting, or are they just the block universe in denial?

Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones eternalists themselves would concede as counting against their view versus which they'd wave off, and give me an honest verdict on whether the argument has been qualified into something unfalsifiable -- and whether that even matters for an argument that's supposed to follow from physics rather than be tested like an empirical claim. For what it's worth, what I'd personally find most persuasive is a physically serious story where becoming is objective, not just a "maybe the premises are too strong" dodge.
