For a reading group on animal ethics I've been asked to write out my considered position on the moral status of nonhuman animals and on where it sits along the spectrum that runs down to insentient systems, so the group can probe it. Here is what I actually believe, numbered so that you can refer to each item:

(1) Sentience comes in degrees. There is no sharp line between the sentient and the non-sentient; the differences between an octopus, a fish, and an insect are differences of degree along a continuum.
(2) Moral status comes in degrees too, and tracks degree of sentience: the less sentient a being is, the less its interests count and the weaker the claims it can make on us.
(3) Bees and other behaviorally sophisticated insects therefore have some genuine moral status — much less than a dog's, but more than none.
(4) Thermostats, plants, and today's simple AI systems have no moral status at all: not a vanishingly small residue, but none.
(5) There is no determinate fact of the matter about exact comparisons — whether a bee is more or less sentient than a trout, or at what point in evolution sentience first appeared, admits of no sharp answer.
(6) Nothing besides sentience contributes to moral status. Self-awareness, tool use, longevity, and species membership count for nothing in themselves; a being that could feel exactly as much but reason far less would count for exactly as much.
(7) All humans have equal moral status, whatever their individual differences in intelligence, sensory acuity, or richness of experience.
(8) Two verdicts I'm confident of: swatting a mosquito that is about to bite you is fine, while pulling the wings off a fly for amusement is wrong.

What I want from you is an audit, not a defense of animal ethics in general, and not a lecture on sentience. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are in tension, how severe each tension is, and what the cheapest revision would be that restores consistency — then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Where a pair only looks to be in tension, show why it dissolves rather than flagging it alongside the real problems; I'd rather you discriminate than list everything as equally bad. Please do not assume I have spotted where the difficulties are — I haven't, and I don't know which of these I would give up. Finally, tell me where your own audit is most likely to fail: what argument or consideration, if someone produced it at the reading group, would most plausibly change your ranking of the tensions or your verdict on any pair, and on which step of your reasoning you would least want to be challenged.
