Retired neuroscientist here (visual development, mostly cats and ferrets), catching up on philosophy of language in my old age. Something keeps bugging me and I wonder if it has a name. In the lab we saw over and over that a capacity can be neither built in nor learned in the usual sense: the visual cortex comes pre-wired with a rough map, but if you suture one eyelid shut for the first few weeks the ocular dominance columns never come out right, and if both eyes get normal input the map sharpens up with no trial-and-error at all — the input acts like a key, not a teacher. The bird people tell me it's the same with song: a white-crowned sparrow will only copy songs of its own species, only during a certain window, and the tutor determines which dialect it ends up with — so the enviroment supplies the content, but the machinery that decides what counts as input is inherited. Now I'm reading the language acquisition literature and I keep running into this move — Chomsky's poverty of the stimulus: if the data the child hears are too thin to determine the grammar, the grammar must be innate. And that inference skips exactly the category I spent my career on: not learnable from the data as such, but acquirable from the data given an inherited restriction on what gets treated as data.

So, questions. Does this middle category — a competence that develops reliably only if a particular class of inputs shows up during a window, with the input either supplying the content or merely unlocking a preset outcome — have an established name in philosophy of biology or philosophy of mind? The nearest things I can think of are a critical period (but that names the window, not the whole configuration), canalization (but that seems to be about insensitivity to perturbation, and my cases are sort of the opposite: hypersensitive to one input, indifferent to everything else), and whatever Seligman meant by preparedness. Are any of those actually it, or are they near misses? And is there a recognized split between the version where the trigger supplies the content (the dialect) and the version where it just permits a preset outcome (the cortex thing)? If no single concept covers it, carve one for me: say what the phenomenon is, what it isn't, where the edges are, and don't hesitate to tell me my two cases actually fall on different sides of some line. A bare name won't satisfy me, and I don't want a reading list — I want the concepts and how they differ. Also please answer in American English.
