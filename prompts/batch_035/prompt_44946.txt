So I've ended up holding this view about free will and I want you to stress-test it properly.

The view: what free will amounts to (the interesting kind, the kind worth wanting) is basically the absence of sphexishness. If you don't know the term, Dennett got it from an old ethology story about the digger wasp. The wasp drags her paralyzed prey to the burrow, leaves it at the threshold, goes in to inspect, comes back out, and drags the prey in. But if you nudge the prey a few inches away while she's inside, she comes out, finds it, drags it back to the threshhold — and goes in to inspect again. You can apparently do this to her over and over and she never skips the check. Dennett called behavior like this sphexish: procedurally rigid, insensitive to whether a step has already been done, running the routine regardless. The opposite would be flexible: adjusting what you do to the particulars of the situation, including situations the routine wasn't built for.

My claim is roughly: an agent is free, and apt for praise and blame, to the degree that it's flexible rather than sphexish — and most of the traditional free will debate is kind of beside the the point. Determinism doesn't make you sphexish. A system could be fully deterministic and still respond intelligently to novelty; a system with genuine randomness baked in could still be sphexish, just with dice rolled into the routine. So the line that matters for responsibility is sphexish/flexible, not determined/undetermined.

What would count against this? I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Things I'm already half-worried about:

(a) Cases where we clearly do hold people responsible (or clearly excuse them) in ways the sphexishness line seems to get backwards. The person who acts out of a rigid, unbreakable habit — is she sphexish, and does that prediction match how we actually treat her? The person who is flexibly, ingeniously pursuing something they couldn't help pursuing — where does the view put them?

(b) Whether the view can handle the hard cases the traditional debate was built around at all: compulsion, addiction, manipulation. Is a manipulated agent sphexish? Flexible? Does either answer come out right, or does the view just lack the resources to say anything about them?

(c) The suspicion that I haven't actually sidestepped the compatibilism debate but just relabeled one side of it and smuggled its problems back in. If the sphexish/flexible distinction is itself doing the same work as the old determined/free distinction, I want to know.

And be honest with me about whether the view can be qualified into unfalsifiability — the phrase "to the degree that" is doing a lot of work above and I can feel it. If the view ends up able to accommodate any case by adjusting degrees, is that fatal for a view of this kind, or is degree-talk just the honest thing here? I'd also take any observation or thought experiment that you think should move me even if it doesn't fit my (a)-(c) list.
