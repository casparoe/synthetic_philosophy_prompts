So this is a thing I keep chewing on and I want to see if I can actually nail down what would count as solving it. I've been reading philosophy of language stuff on and off for years, mostly via blog posts and the occasional paper a friend links me, and Bradley's regress / the unity of the proposition is the one topic where every explanation I read feels like it dissolves right when I try to hold onto it. And recently I've been noticing that people in the neuroscience-in-AI space talk about something called the binding problem -- how does a brain (or a big neural net) represent "red triangle and blue square" as bound pairs rather than a soup of red, blue, triangle-ness, square-ness -- and it at least *looks* like the same puzzle at a different level of description: you have a bunch of contents, and the question is what holds them together into one thing that can be true or false. Maybe that analogy is load-bearing for my interest and maybe it's a distraction, feel free to say which.

What I want is the desiderata: a spec sheet for a theory of propositional unity. What does any adequate account of what makes 'Socrates is wise' a proposition rather than just the set {Socrates, wisdom} have to deliver? Give me the requirements with the case or puzzle motivating each one, and tell me which are non-negotiable versus which are just the aesthetic preferences of particular camps. Things I suspect belong on the list but want you to sanity check: it has to distinguish a genuine unity from a mere aggregate (the list vs. the proposition), it has to handle non-symmetric relations without generating a new instance-of-the-relation entity that itself needs relating (this is where the regress bites, right?), it has to explain false unities at least as well as true ones, it can't explain predication by helping itself to predication somewhere in the machinery (no smuggling), and there's some kind of ontology budget consideration -- how much weird new furniture is the theory allowed to buy, and should a regress that never terminates but is somehow 'benign' count as paying up or defaulting.

Then flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied. My hunch is that no-smuggling and terminate-the-explanation are jointly unsatisfiable -- every theory either bottoms out in a brute fact ('the fact just is a unity', or the Tractatus-style move where the proposition pictures the world and the picturing relation is itself unexplained) or lets the regress run and argues it's harmless -- and if that's right the spec sheet itself is basically the result, which I'd find satisfying honestly.

At the end, briefly, no more than a paragraph each, how the main contenders do against the list: Armstrong-style states of affairs where instantiation is not itself a relation, the Tractatus picture-theory line, trope-bundle approaches, and the deflationary 'there is no problem, propositions are just shadows of our sentence practices' move. I care way more about the criteria and their motivating cases than the scorecard, that's the part I'll argue with people about.

Oh and if the binding problem analogy at the top is actually doing real work, I'd love a sentence on which desiderata transfer to the neural version and which don't -- like whether the false-unity requirement even makes sense for a brain. If it's a mess, just say so, I can take it.
