I build and eval LLM agents for a living — the sort that get long-horizon goals, can spawn subagents, write to their own scratchpad and memory, and get checkpointed between episodes. Last week a philosopher friend (writing his dissertation on intention, so he's got skin in the game) hit me with Kavka's toxin puzzle over beers and wouldn't let it go: a billionaire offers you a million dollars at midnight tonight iff, at midnight, you intend to drink a toxin tomorrow — a day of horrible sickness, no lasting damage. The catch is that the money is conditional only on the intention, and everyone knows this in advance: you never actually have to drink, and you can even tell yourself the night before that you'll take the money and skip the toxin. But, my friend insisted, intending to drink while planning not to drink isn't intending, so the question is whether rationality can ever require you to genuinely form an intention you'd be better off never acting on. I kept saying "this is just a commitment/verification problem, we'd solve it with escrow" and he kept saying "no, you've missed the whole point," and neither of us could settle it because I don't actually know the philosophy well enough to say what I'm translating and what I'm flattening.

So here's what I want: restate the puzzle and its main dialectic faithfully in my vocabulary — policies vs. plans, internal states vs. outputs, self-modification between timesteps, reward specified on latents instead of behavior, sandbagging under evals, checkpointing and rollback, credible-commitment mechanisms like escrow or verifiable delay, whatever genuinely fits. Give me enough of the real two-sided debate (including what the analogy to Newcomb-style cases is doing for the two sides) that I can tell where the translation is doing work rather than decoration. Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about: which pieces of the puzzle map cleanly onto something like "the reward depends on a latent the verifier can inspect and the agent can rewrite right after inspection," which pieces only translate with distortion, and which pieces have no expression in my vocabulary at all. And give me your honest read on the residue — when something won't translate, is that because the agent-engineering frame is missing a concept (something like 'intending' that isn't just 'having a plan state at time t'), or because the puzzle was leaning on an ambiguity that my frame would have caught? Bottom line I want answered: is my "it's just commitment verification with rewritable internals" take basically correct, or did my friend earn his beer?
