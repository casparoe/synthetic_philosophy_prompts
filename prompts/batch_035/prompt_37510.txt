I'm a control systems engineer with no philosophy training beyond airport-bookshelf level. My working vocabulary is setpoints and reference tracking, feedback and feedforward, disturbance rejection, model-predictive control, plant versus controller, sensor noise, saturation, stability. Restate Daniel Dennett's compatibilism — the position he develops from Elbow Room through Freedom Evolves, on which the free will worth wanting is the evolved capacity to anticipate consequences and govern one's behavior by foresight rather than impulse — in that vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Within the same translation, restate the two positions he is arguing against: the libertarian demand for an agent who could have done otherwise in some sense stronger than counterfactual input-sensitivity, and the hard determinist's claim that if outputs are fully determined by prior states, then whatever we call freedom is an illusion. Then give me the residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which come across only with distortion, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all. Two suspicions I want confirmed or corrected as part of the report: first, that the question whether I could have done otherwise becomes, in my terms, a question about what the same controller would output under different inputs — something we answer by counterfactual simulation and never find spooky; second, that the libertarian's extra demand looks, in my terms, like a request for a controller output that is not a function of anything, which in my world is not freedom but a fault. Finally, tell me what to make of the residue. I notice that my vocabulary evaluates controllers only against a specification handed in from outside; it has no way to ask whether the specification deserves tracking, and no standing inside the loop — and I suspect that is exactly where the worth-wanting part of Dennett's position lives. So say whether the residue points to something my vocabulary is missing, or to something that was less clear than it looked in the philosophers' original.
