quick backstory so this makes sense: I'm a retired physicist (35 years on superconductors, mostly low-temperature stuff) and since retiring I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole. Right now I'm stuck on Hofstadter.

In Gödel, Escher, Bach there's that dialogue "Prelude... Ant Fugue" where the Anteater explains that individual ants are dumb as bricks but the colony as a whole carries symbols — the ants are just the "substrate," and the real action happens up at the symbol level. Then in I Am a Strange Loop (2007) he recasts the whole thing as the "Careenium": a huge billiard-table world where balls careen around down at the bottom, while up at the symbol level there are "simms" and "simmballs." His pet example is a dog chasing a ball: at the neural level the dog is just atoms shoving atoms, but at the symbol level it's the *simmball* that pulls the dog across the yard. And Hofstadter asks, half-jokingly: do the symbols push the substrate around, or does the substrate push the symbols? He wants to say there's a genuine downward pull, but a naturalized one — no spookiness, the higher level is "just" what the lower level adds up to, except that the "just" is doing a lot of work.

Heres my problem. My whole career I worked on superconductivity, where everybody talks as if Cooper pairs "cause" zero resistance, and nobody blinks. But if you push on it, all the pushing is done at the bottom, by electrons and lattice ions. The pair-level talk is fantastically useful bookkeeping, but I've never been able to convince myself it does causal work of its own. So when Hofstadter says symbols push the substrate around, I can't tell if he's telling me symbols are like Cooper pairs (compressed description, no oomph of their own) or something genuinely more. And if it's more, I want to know where the extra lives.

I'd like you to write me a dialogue between two characters who hash this out. One who thinks the symbol level genuinely pushes — a Hofstadterian, call her Maya, a cognitive scientist. And one who thinks the substrate does all the causal work and the symbol level is bookkeeping — a hard-nosed reductionist, call him Dave, a philosopher of physics. Important: Dave should NOT be a strawman. "It's just bookkeeping" is a real position with real arguments, and he should know them cold.

Ground rules for the dialogue:

- Real back-and-forth. Maya has to answer Dave's specific objections (causal closure of the physical, overdetermination — if the symbols are "doing" something, and the neurons are also doing it, who did it?) and Dave has to answer Maya's points, not just restate his position louder.
- They should actually use the careenium/simmball machinery, and also the superconductivity analogy, since that's where my intuitions live. At some point Maya should have to say whether the simmball differs in kind from a Cooper pair, and if so, where the difference lives. (Self-reference? Loopiness? The substrate caring about the pattern?) And Dave should get to press on whatever she says.
- Concrete test cases would help. E.g. me raising my arm because I "want to signal the waiter" — is the waiter-symbol doing any of the pushing, or is that a story I tell afterwards? Or a robot car that swerves because of a pedestrian-symbol in its planner.
- Nobody wins by cheating. If one of them lands a genuinely strong point, let the other concede or adapt instead of pivoting. And it's fine — honestly, it's probably best — if they end in an honest stalemate. What I most want is for them to pin down where the real crux is, and ideally what would settle it: an experiment, a conceptual clarification, or just a matter of taste in how much you want the word "cause" to cover.

Length: long enough to get into the weeds, but they're talking to each other, not lecturing. And given my background, don't be shy about the physics — I can take it.
