I've been reading up on the IIT wars and I honestly can't tell anymore whether this is a live scientific dispute or two camps talking past each other. On one side you have the IIT people deriving everything from phenomenological axioms — experience exists, it's structured, informative, integrated, closed under its own causes — and then claiming those axioms carry over to the physical substrate, so that a system's phi tells you its capacity for consciousness. On the other side, functionalists and illusionists who say a theory on which a big grid of idle logic gates could in principle have more integrated information than a human brain — while a system that perfectly mimics a brain's input-output behavior using purely feedforward connections has phi of exactly zero — has obviously misfired somewhere, and some of them went so far as to sign that public letter a few years back calling IIT pseudoscience. What gets me is that both sides point at the same facts: the cerebellum has most of your neurons yet you can lose big chunks of it without losing consciousness, anesthesia, split-brain patients, and each side says the facts favor them. It's been over a decade and nobody has budged an inch. So what kind of disagreement is this actually? A hidden empirical question we just can't run the experiment for yet, a verbal dispute about what "explanation" or "falsifiability" should mean for consciousness specifically, or two groups answering different questions under one heading — one asking what the physical substrate of experience is, the other asking which systems we're entitled to attribute experience to? My tentative view is that the fight is really about what a theory of consciousness is for, and that the empirical stuff (grids, cerebellum) is downstream of that. But I'd like a real diagnosis grounded in what each side actually claims, and then an honest answer about what would count as progress: an experiment, a theorem showing what phi can and can't be computed from, a clean split of the field into "theory of what consciousness is" versus "clinical test for who has it," or nothing at all? Don't just survey the positions for me, I've read the surveys.
