I sit on a university committee drafting regulations on when a doctoral student may be dismissed for "unsatisfactory progress". One colleague insists we must replace that phrase with a precise threshold — fewer than two accepted publications by the end of year three, say — on the grounds that any residual vagueness licenses arbitrary treatment. A second colleague replies that the arbitrariness merely relocates: a student with two publications then counts as dismissible whilst one with two and a single book review does not, and no amount of refinement removes that cliff edge. I find myself drawn to a third position: that some students simply are borderline cases, such that dismissing them would be neither clearly permissible nor clearly impermissible, and that our regulations ought to acknowledge this penumbra rather than pretend it away. Is that third position coherent, or does it collapse into one of the other two once we ask the hard question — namely, whether a given borderline student is, in the event, dismissed or not? I would value your assessment, particularly of whether a rule-governed procedure can tolerate indeterminacy in its application without thereby tolerating inconsistency in its outcomes.
