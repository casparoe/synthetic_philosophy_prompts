I am a novelist three-quarters of the way through a book I no longer know how to end, and my difficulty is not one of plot but of judgment, so I am writing to you in the hope that philosophy can settle what my instincts cannot.

The novel concerns a retired schoolteacher, a widow in her seventies, who for the past eleven years has been compiling what she calls the definitive register of the county's hedgerows: every species, every laying and cutting date, every gap and restoration, entered in a fair hand and cross-referenced by parish. She works on it most of every day, and she is happy — not serenely resigned but genuinely, demonstrably absorbed, more alive than she was in thirty years of teaching. The trouble is that no one wants the register. The county archive declined the first three volumes. There is no second hedgerow surveyor in the world. When her son, my narrator, puts this to her — that she is the sole reader and likely the last — she replies that the register would be no less worth making if the county were emptied of people tomorrow. He takes this as proof that she has drifted into a private fantasy; I am no longer sure he is right, and the final chapter must take a side.

So my questions are these. Is her absorption a form of good fortune that most of us merely envy, or a capitulation to pointlessness that has been dressed up as devotion? Does it matter that the object of her love could not, by any stretch, be called important — that from any point of view wider than her own, hedgerows simply do not bear the weight she has placed on them? Or is that wider point of view itself the fantasy, a perspective no one actually occupies and from which nothing whatever would count as mattering? And a related worry about my narrator: his insistence that her project needs an audience to be worthwhile — is that clear-sightedness, or a failure of imagination about the many ways a life can go well? I could end the book either way; what I cannot do is end it sincerely unless I know which ending is true rather than merely the gentler one. Please tell me what you actually think, and why.
