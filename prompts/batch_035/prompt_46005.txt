I got into an argument at a reading group about when hypocrites lose their standing to blame, and my friend produced a chain of reasoning that ends with nobody ever having standing to blame anybody, which seems to prove too much but I can't locate the broken step. It goes like this:

(1) You have standing to blame someone for a wrong only if you're not yourself guilty of the same thing. This is just the hypocrisy thought — the "look who's talking" response.

(2) "The same thing" can't mean the identical act in the identical circumstances. If it did, you'd lose standing to blame a plagiarist only by plagiarizing that very paper, and real hypocrites would be nearly impossible, which contracdicts the plain fact that we do discount the blame of people who preach one thing and do another.

(3) So "the same thing" has to be read broadly: being guilty of wrongdoing of the same general kind, or at least of comparable gravity.

(4) Read broadly, the test is one everybody fails. No one gets through life without some dishonesty, some unfairness, some unkindness — for every ordinary vice there's a prior offense of your own sitting somewhere in the same moral neighborhood.

(5) So nobody has standing to blame anybody for ordinary wrongdoing.

(6) But blame is sometimes obviously in order, and standing isn't distributed evenly: the person who doesn't lie seems to have a kind of authority to blame the chronic liar that the liar's fellow liars lack.

(7) Therefore (1) is false: guilt of the same thing doesn't remove your standing.

The troulbe is that (7) can't be right either, or "look who's talking" would never be an apt reply to anything, and it plainly is. My friend has concluded that standing was never a real category and we should only ever talk about the sincerity of the blame. My own hunch is that something quietly widened between (3) and (4) — that "the same thing" means one thing when it's doing the work of explaining why we discount hypocrites and something much weaker when it's being used to convict everyone — but I can't spell out exactly what changed or why the slide felt innocent. So: which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If you think more than one premise has to go, say which and why. And please answer in American English.
