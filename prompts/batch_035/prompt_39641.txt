Write the joint statement that a committed presentist and a committed eternalist could both sign after a year of good-faith adversarial collaboration on the metaphysics of time. The document should contain, in this order:

1. **Agreed claims.** A numbered list of claims both parties can sign. These should include the semantic data both accept (that tensed sentences convey information that can't be recovered from their tenseless paraphrases, whatever the explanation turns out to be); an agreed characterization of what the dispute is *not* about (e.g., neither side is asserting that we can causally interact with Caesar, or that the past is somehow spatially distant); an agreed statement of the shared phenomenology of temporal experience, with each side's gloss on it explicitly bracketed as disagreement; and an agreed map of nearby positions (growing block, moving spotlight) that neither party holds, so the two signatories aren't conflated with views they reject.

2. **Disagreements, in a table.** Each residual disagreement stated twice — once in wording the presentist would accept for their own view and once in wording the eternalist would accept for theirs — where possible also a formulation in terms both accept. Include at least: the ontological status of non-present entities; whether cross-time relations (Caesar being earlier than my typing this, my admiring Marcus Aurelius) need relata that exist; and whether the passage of time is a feature of the world or an artifact of temporally embedded observers.

3. **Cruxes.** Identify the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one signatory. I want at least these four addressed, plus any others the drafter deems load-bearing:
   - The truthmaker problem: what makes past-tense truths like "there were dinosaurs" true, if nothing dinosaur-related exists?
   - The triviality worry: whether presentism, stated carefully with tense operators, asserts anything an eternalist must deny, or whether the dispute partially dissolves into one about the ideology of quantification and tense.
   - The argument from special relativity: whether relativity's denial of absolute simultaneity undercuts a global present, and — a sub-crux — whether adopting a Lorentzian preferred frame is a philosophically acceptable response or an ad hoc rescue.
   - Prior's relief observation: that when an ordeal ends, we are relieved in a tensed way, apparently about the ordeal's *being past* rather than about it ending earlier than this utterance — and what, if anything, this constrains ontology rather than merely language.
   
   For each crux, state the case or argument both parties agree would bear on it, and record each party's prediction in advance of that test.

4. **A verbal/substantive split.** If part of the dispute strikes the drafter as verbal — e.g., a dispute over whether the word *exists* in quantified tense logic expresses one thing or two — say which part, and why it is verbal rather than merely difficult.

Constraints: nowhere may the joint statement conflate presentism simpliciter with commitment to temporal passage, or eternalism with commitment to a block universe plus no genuine change — each signatory's position must be characterized as they would characterize it, not as their opponent caricatures it. Both signatories are professional metaphysicians who know the literature, so the document should engage the strongest version of each objection, not textbook versions. Reach formulations that are neither vacuous (agreeing only that time is in some sense real) nor slanted (as when the presentist's view is described as denying that Caesar "really" existed, in a sense of *really* the presentist would reject).
