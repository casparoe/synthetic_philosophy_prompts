i'm running the last session of an undergrad seminar on philosophy of time and we end on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument — the thing where two observers pass each other at different velocities, their planes of simultaneity tilt differently, so what counts as "now" for one of them already includes the other's future (say, the reading of some distant dial, or whether a certain decision has been made yet), and Putnam says once you chain these "distant simultaneity" facts together you get that everything future is already real, so the block universe falls out of special relativity basically for free.

i want to close with a poem instead of a summary. write me a sestina. the six end-words should be chosen so that the form does the argument's work: words like "now," "future," "past" (you pick the full set) that keep recurring but never in the same position, so that as the stanzas rotate, which moment counts as present keeps shifting depending on whose frame the stanza is in — the rotation of the end-words enacting the tilting of the simultaneity planes. i'd like at least one stanza to be grounded in the concrete setup (two observers passing, a distant event each of them slices differently) rather than all abstract.

the envoi is where i want the twist: compress all six end-words into three lines so it reads as the block — every moment equally there, no privileged now — but ALSO, if you can manage it without wrecking the poem, let the envoi register the standard objection that Putnam quietly needs his "is real for" relation to be transitive and simultaneity-relative facts maybe don't chain like that. so the ending should feel like the block won, but with a hairline crack visible. don't resolve it. our seminar ended exactly there and that's honestly the note i want to end on.

it should be accurate enough that someone who teaches philosophy of physics would nod along, but still work as a poem for students who half-zoned out during the Lorentz diagrams. no name-dropping Putnam or Rietdijk in the poem itself — save that for after, when i tell them what they just heard.
