I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for well over a decade, and I've ended up with a pile of views on the philosophy of mind that I've never stress-tested together. Before I write this up for my local meetup, I want an audit. Here's the set:

(a) I'm a physicalist: there's nothing to minds over and above the physical organization of the system.

(b) I think the Churchlands were basically right about the direction of travel: "belief" and "desire" are theoretical posits of folk psychology, and as cognitive neuroscience and ML interpretability mature, they will likely go the way of phlogiston -- not reduced, but eliminated, with the real cognitive categories cutting across the folk ones in ways that make belief-talk inapplicable rather than merely coarse-grained.

(c) At the same time, I take Dennett's intentional stance to be an indispensable predictive instrument, and I'll keep using it everywhere -- for other people, for animals, for myself, and for chess engines -- regardless of how (b) turns out. It earns its keep pragmatically, the same way "sunrise" earns its keep for navigators who know the Earth orbits the Sun.

(d) I'm committed to the core rationalist practices: beliefs should pay rent in anticipated experiences, my credences should be calibrated (I keep a forecasting track record and treat improving it as one of the highest-leverage things I do), and big decisions should go through explicit expected-utility reasoning.

(e) I think misaligned AI is the most serious global risk this century, and I mean this in a robust realist sense: future systems will genuinely have goals that conflict with ours, where having a goal is a real property of the system and its internals, not just a convenience of our descriptions of it. If someone told me goal-talk was merely our projection onto optimizers, I'd take that as a substantive downgrade of the risk model, not a harmless reframing.

(f) What makes an entity a moral patient, at least in the part of the picture I'm confident about, is that things matter to it -- that it has states it seeks and states it avoids, that deprivation and frustration can befall it. This is what grounds my vegetarianism, and I expect it to ground my views on machine suffering as AI systems get more sophisticated.
