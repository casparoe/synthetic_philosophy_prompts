Something from my financial econ + epistemology reading group that I can't shake, and I want an actual diagnosis, not hand-waving. You're a formal epistemologist who moonlights teaching market microstructure, so don't hold back.

The setup: two risk-neutral agents with common priors, each gets a private signal about whether some stock will go up. Purely speculative trade — no hedging motive, no liquidity needs, no cash constraints, they just want to maximize expected money.

(1) A trade happens only if it's common knowledge between them that both are willing: you can't buy unless someone will sell to you at that price.
(2) If my agent is rational, her willingness to sell at price p is itself evidence she recieved a bearish signal; and if she knows the counterparty is rational, his willingness to buy is evidence he got a bullish one. Each side should condition on the other's willingness to trade.
(3) Once both condition on the fact that the trade is on, Aumann-style their posteriors coincide, and no risk-neutral agent takes a bet she knows the other side was happy to take too.
(4) So between rational common-prior agents, speculative trade never occurs: the willingness to trade annhilates the reason for trading.
(5) But a huge chunk of real equity volume is institutional, fast, and looks exactly like informed speculation, and those people are rich and not obviously stupid.
(6) So either traders are systematically irrational, or priors are heterogeneous — rational agents can just "agree to disagree" before any evidence ever arrives.
(7) If priors are heterogeneous, prices reflect priors as much as information, so markets can't be the clean information-aggregation engine Hayek wanted; if traders are irrational, same conclusion for a worse reason. Either way the epistemology of markets is in trouble.

The step I keep poking at is (2), but honestly I can't articulate what's wrong with it. Which premise or step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost — is there a reading where the theorem is intact but real markets still get to keep their epistemic role?
