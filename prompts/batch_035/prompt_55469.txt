I'm a wireless communications engineer by training and trade — link budgets, modulation schemes, error-correcting codes, channel capacity under power constraints are my daily bread — and I've recently gotten interested in evolutionary biology, where I keep bumping into the literature on costly signaling and the so-called handicap principle. As I understand it, the core claim is that signals like a peacock's tail or a stag's roaring are honest *because* they are costly: only a genuinely high-quality male can afford to waste resources on them, so the cost itself does the work of keeping the signal believable. There was apparently a long fight about this — the original proposal was mocked for implying that nature solves its credibility problem by burning resources, and it was only later rehabilitated by game theorists who showed that differential costs can sustain an honest signaling equilibrium — and there also seems to be a live question about whether the costs are best seen as wasteful at all, versus some framing on which the system as a whole is efficient. My instinct is that I already own all the concepts I need: transmitted power as a proxy for the handicap, the signal-to-noise ratio and detection theory for the receiver's discrimination problem, capacity-per-watt trade-offs for the alleged wastefulness, maybe jamming and adversarial interference for something.

So here's the request. Restate the main positions in this debate — the handicap claim itself, the game-theoretic rescue of it, and the dispute over whether the costs represent waste — in my vocabulary: power allocation, channel capacity, coding, detection and estimation, power- and bandwidth-constrained channels, whatever else fits. Do it claim by claim, as faithfully as you can. Then give me the residue report I actually care about: which parts translate cleanly, which parts have to be bent or distorted to fit, and which parts of the biological argument have no expression at all in how I think about communication systems. I have my eye on one candidate for the residue in particular — in my world, transmitter and receiver share a codebook by standard, and the designer wants them both to succeed, whereas in the biological case the two sides seem to have partially conflicting payoffs and no standards body, so the "code" itself is whatever each side's evolution has left it with — but don't let me dictate the answer. Tell me honestly whether the untranslatable remainder points to something my vocabulary is missing, whether it's something engineers have actually dealt with under another name, or whether the biologists' debate is less substantive than it looks once you demand the kind of precision I'm used to. And in particular: is "honesty" even a well-posed notion when there is no prior agreed mapping from signals to states, as there is in every system I've ever designed?
