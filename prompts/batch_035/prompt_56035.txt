Two years into an aesthetics reading group and the art-and-morality fight has gone completely static, and I'd rather have a diagnosis than another round of it. The positions, roughly: the hard autonomists hold that moral defects just aren't aesthetic defects — for them Triumph of the Will is great filmmaking with an evil message, and the evilness sits on a seperate scoreboard entirely. The ethicists push the merited response argument: a work that instructs you to enjoy cruelty, and is only any good if you comply, is flawed in itself, and their favorite case is a comedy that's only funny if you find the victim's humiliation funny. The moderates say a moral defect counts aesthetically only when it breaks the response the work prescribes — you're meant to sympathize with a hero the writing itself makes repulsive, say. At this point everyone knows everyone's counterexamples, nobody has moved in a year, and any new case we throw at it gets assimilated within about ten minutes. My own hypothesis is that it's verbal: the autonomists are scoring formal achievement while the ethicists are scoring the overall value of engaging with the work, and 'good art' is doing double duty between the two. But it's just a hypothesis — pushed hard enough, both sides seem to genuinely disagree about the cases and not just the words. So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Verbal, substantive-but-hidden (maybe a difference about what evaluation is even for — guiding your engagement with a work vs ranking works against each other?), or two projects running under one heading? Diagnose it from what the sides actually say, and tell me whether anything would count as progress here — a distinction, a new kind of case, a change of question — or whether this one just goes on forever.
