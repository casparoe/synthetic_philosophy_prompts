I serve on the renewal panel of a national research funder. Every three years the panel decides whether to continue five-year block funding for a dozen or so long-running, multi-investigator research programmes, spanning fields from condensed-matter physics to community ecology. Each panellist gets about ninety minutes with a programme's dossier before the panel meeting, and the decision, worth several million per programme, is made by majority vote. Our current practice is that each panellist argues from overall impression, which in practice rewards good storytelling, famous names, and glossy dossiers. I want to replace this with a written procedure, grounded in Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes, that any panellist could run in those ninety minutes without having read a word of philosophy of science.

The procedure should translate Lakatos's distinctions into questions a panellist can actually answer from a dossier and a short interview with the programme's leadership. In particular: the hard core versus the protective belt, as a question about which commitments the group treats as non-negotiable, and whether recent work has been absorbed by adjustments at the periphery or by quietly rewriting the core; progressive versus degenerating problem shifts, as a test of whether the last funding cycle produced genuinely new predictions or unexpected connections to neighbouring fields, versus whether each surprise was retrofitted — a new free parameter, a newly carved-out exception, a redefinition of what the programme counts as its subject matter; and the positive heuristic, as a question about whether the group has a concrete story about what to do next that goes beyond applying the same method to one more case. For each step the procedure must state what a sufficient answer looks like, when the honest output is "indeterminate", and which steps are judgment calls that the table cannot eliminate. It must also contain two safeguards Lakatos's own views demand: an explicit warning that a single review cycle cannot settle a programme's fate (degenerating-looking programmes sometimes revive, and prematurely killing them was, on Lakatos's account, a mark of institutional irrationality), and a rule specifying how many consecutive cycles of apparent degeneration must accumulate before the procedure will output "do not renew" at all.

Then apply the procedure to the three programmes below, which I have disguised but kept structurally faithful to real cases on our docket:

1. Programme A, materials modelling: a twenty-year effort to predict the behaviour of a class of functional materials from first principles. Each anomalous sample over the years has been handled by adding a fitting parameter or restricting the promised domain of application; but last cycle the group made a bold prediction of an unusual effect, which was confirmed — in the same paper, however, they quietly redefined the target class of materials to exclude two cases that had misbehaved for a decade.
2. Programme B, community ecology: the core commitment is a single optimisation principle said to govern how organisms allocate effort across competing demands. It has produced no risky prediction in over a decade, but its defenders argue it unifies a wide range of phenomena under one formal scheme, and I confess I find that unification impressive.
3. Programme C, founded five years ago in cognitive science: its central model has already been substantially revised twice, its output so far is mostly formal development with little empirical contact, and its leadership argues the field it addresses is too young for the group to be judged by the standards applied to mature programmes.

Please deliver the procedure as a table with the columns Step; Question put to the dossier; What a sufficient answer looks like; Output (one of progressive, degenerating, indeterminate); Judgment call (yes/no, and if yes, what remains for the panellist to decide). Then give the three worked applications, each followed by a JSON object conforming to this schema: {"programme": string, "verdicts_by_step": [{"step": integer, "verdict": string}], "cycles_since_genuine_progress": integer, "overall": one of "progressive", "degenerating", "indeterminate", "too-few-cycles", "panel-judgment-required", "rationale": string, "unavoidable_judgment_points": [string], "recommendation": one of "renew", "renew-with-conditions", "do-not-renew", "defer", "confidence": one of "high", "medium", "low"}. For each JSON object, indicate which fields a panellist could fill mechanically from the table and which require deliberation the table cannot substitute for. Do not soften the verdict on Programme B on account of the sympathy I expressed for it; if the procedure's answer there is one I will not like, I want to see that plainly, along with what the procedure canonically cannot do about it.
