so I'm putting together my position on scientific realism for a talk and I want someone to poke holes in it before I stand up in front of people. here's the package:

(1) I'm a realist about our best theories: mature scientific theories are at least approximately true, including what they say about unobservables, and my main reason is no-miracles — the empirical success of science would be a giant unexplained coincidence otherwise.

(2) the pessmistic meta-induction doesn't scare me much, because I'm a structural realist about theory change: what gets carried over when a theory is replaced is the mathematical structure, and that's the part that was doing the predictive work all along. the rest — the pictures, the mechanisms, the little stories about what the stuff is like in itself — gets thrown out, and I'm fine saying that part was false.

(3) some case verdicts, for conreteness: Fresnel's ether theory was on the right track — his diffraction equations are basically still standing in optics, so that counts as a success even though the ether itself turned out to be junk. Phlogiston, on the other hand, was a dead end that never referred to anything. And caloric — I actually think Carnot squeezed real, lasting results out of a theory about a heat fluid that doesn't exist, and I'd even go as far as saying caloric latched onto something; it's just that what it latched onto turned out to be energy-ish rather than a fluid.

(4) separately, I'm very sympathetic to Hacking's entity realism move: once you can use the things — build apparatus that works only if electrons are there, make the screen light up — their existence is settled no matter how wrong the surrounding theory turns out to be. electrons have been real since well before quantum electrodynamics, through several total rewrites of the theory around them.

(5) last one: I think a physicist in 1890 who reasoned exactly the way I do — realist for the same reasons, structural realist for the same reasons — was right, not merely reasonable. she wasn't a dupe. her version of realism was true then the way mine is true now.

does this hang together? which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest fix in each case? rank them by severity rather than just listing them all as equally bad. and if one single revision would clean up most of it, say which — but honestly I don't know where the problem is, so don't go easy on any of them.
