My friend and I have been arguing about what it takes for something to count as fragile. Mostly this means I cook up cases and she rules on them, and she rules fast. Her record so far: an ordinary wine glass on a shelf, fragile, obviously. That same glass hooked up to a lab gadget that fires the instant anything is about to hit it and instantly heat-treats it, so it would never actually break no matter what you did — still fragile, she says, because the glass itself hasn't changed, it's the gadget doing the cheating. The same glass sealed in so much bubble wrap and foam that you could drop-kick it down a flight of stairs — still fragile. A pub glass that's been properly heat-strengthened, the real process, no gadget involved — not fragile, she says, it's just different stuff now. And a toughened pub glass sitting in a room with a poltergeist that would smash any glass the instant something touched it, so this one WOULD break if you struck it, just not because of the glass — not fragile, she says, since take the poltergeist away and nothing happens.

Now I have a new case and I genuinly can't call it. It's an ordinary glass, ordinary fragile glass through and through right now, no gadget, no wrap — but it's been engineered so that its own internal structure flips into a super-tough state within nanoseconds of an impact starting. All internal, that's the whole point of the case. It never breaks: by the time your blow actually lands, it's already tough. Will she call it fragile? Honestly the yes/no matters less to me than the rule she's implicitly running — as far as I can tell there's more than one rule that fits her whole record, and they come apart on exactly this case. So: lay out the candidate rules, say what each one predicts here, tell me which you'd bet on and why, and give me the further case we should run past her next to settle it. And if there's a rule that fits everything she's said but is somehow dumb, I want to hear about that too — I'd like to at least know what we're ruling out.
