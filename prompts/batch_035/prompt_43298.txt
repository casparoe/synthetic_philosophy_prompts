I'm a backend engineer with no philosophy training, but I've got myself obsessed with this idea of "sphexishness" (I think Dennett came up with the term, after the digger wasp) and I can't work out what I actually think about it, so I'm hoping you can help me find the pattern in my gut reactions.

Quick background in case you've forgotten: the wasp, Sphex, drags her paralysed prey to the burrow entrance, leaves it outside, goes in to check the burrow, comes back out, and drags the prey in. If a researcher nudges the prey a few centimetres away while she's inside, she comes out, re-draggs it back to the threshold, and goes in to check again. Nudge it every time and she'll do the loop forever. She never catches on. Sphexish = running a fixed routine no matter what the world is telling you.

So here are my verdicts on a bunch of things, rated by how sphexish they feel to me:

1. The wasp itself: fully sphexish, obviously.
2. My deploy script at work: it's got retries with backoff, checks exit codes, falls back to a secondary region, the works. My colleagues call it "robust". I say it's still completely sphexish. It's a fancier loop, but it can be fooled in exactly the wasp way — if the network flaps in the wrong pattern it'll retry forever. Very flexible-looking behaviour, zero actual flexibility.
3. The chess engine I play against, which plays a different reply to the same opening every game. Still totally sphexish — the variety comes from a random seed or opening book or whatever. That's variety by design, not flexibility.
4. My teammate Sarah: same coffee order for six years, same route to work, never seen her deviate. But somehow not sphexish at all? I think because if the road were closed she'd just take another one. She'd adapt.
5. Another teammate, Dev: whenever a build breaks, he runs the same three commands in the same order regardless of what the error message says. Watching him, it looks EXACTLY like my deploy script. But I really don't want to call him sphexish, because when someone pointed it out to him he laughed and now he actually reads the error. So he was sphexish until someone noticed for him? Or he was never sphexish and just had a bad habit?
6. Our on-call alerting system, which has flapping detection — it escalates to a human if the same alert fires too many times in a row, so it sort of notices that it's in a loop. I genuinely cannot decide whether that's less sphexish than my script or just my script plus one extra if statement that *we* wrote while noticing.

Here's the tension I can already see in my own verdicts: I give the script and the chess engine no credit whatsoever for their counterfactual sensitivity — the script takes diffrent branches on different exit codes, which is at least a bit like Sarah taking another road — while Sarah gets full marks even though her observable behaviour is the most repetitive thing on the list. And I want sphexishness to come in degrees and to matter for free-will-ish stuff (the more sphexish, the less room your "choices" actually leave you), but if Sarah's routine costs her nothing and the chess engine's genuinely varied play costs it everything, I don't think degrees alone can be doing the work.

My best attempt at a principle: a thing is sphexish to the extent that it can't step back and notice that it's in the loop. The wasp never notices; my script never notices its own retrying, it only notices its inputs; maybe the flapping detector kind of notices?; Sarah obviously can. But then Dev bugs me — before anyone told him, was he stepping-back-capable but not doing it, and does capability you never exercise count? And is the flapping rule really noticing, or is it just one more reflex?

So, questions: is there a principle that actually captures all six of my verdicts, or close? If not, which verdicts should I throw out and why? What does the surviving principle predict on the case I keep going back and forth on — a chatbot that gives slightly different answers to the identical question each time, and that, if you point out it's repeating itself, apologises and genuinely restructures its answer: is that Sarah-level, Dev-level, or just the alerting system again? And lastly, is my gut even right that this connects to free will, or have I smuggled in a way bigger claim than a wasp can support?
