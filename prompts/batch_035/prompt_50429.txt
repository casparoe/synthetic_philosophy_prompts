I'm putting together my positions on advance directives for a bioethics discussion group and want a consistency check before I present. Here's the package:

(1) Advance directives should be treated as fully binding. If a competent person writes down that if she ever has advanced dementia she doesn't want to be fed by tube, that should be honored even when the demented patient is visibly content, enjoys her meals, and would obviously choose to keep living if asked.

(2) I'm pretty anti-paternalist in general. Competent patients' current refusals rule. It horrifies me when a hospital overrrides a clearly competent elderly woman's treatment refusal on the theory that she's confused or being fatalistic.

(3) I don't buy the line that dementia patients are just bundles of pleasure and pain. Even fairly impaired patients still have values, things they care about, reasons of a sort. Their wanting to keep living is a real preference held by a real valuer, not a symptom to be managed, and their current wishes deserve actual weight -- not the pretend weight where you note them down and then do what the directive says anyway.

(4) On personal identity I'm squarely in the psychological continuity camp, and I'm prepared to say that in very advanced dementia the connections get so sparse that it's genuinely indeterminate whether the original person still exists.

(5) My ground for (1) is autonomy, not best interests. The directive is the earlier self governing its own future, like a Ulysses contract -- it's not that honoring it is good for the patient, it's that the choice was hers to make.

(6) When surrogates have to decide without a directive, I'm a substituted judgment person: do what the patient would have wanted, not what's best for her now.

(7) And patients should be able to revoke or amend their directive at any point while competent, even capriciously -- no "she made her bed" rule.

I keep having the feeling that some of these are quietly fighting each other but I can't pin down which. So: which ones are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest fix for each? Please rank the tensions by severity instead of handing me a list of six equally fatal contradictions, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most.
