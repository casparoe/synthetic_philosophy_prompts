I'm a master's student working through the value problem for knowledge, and I've gotten tangled in the way the literature carves it up. Background, so you know where I'm coming from: the issue originates in the Meno, where Socrates concedes that a true belief will serve you just as well as knowledge for the purposes of getting where you want to go — but he compares true beliefs to the legendary statues of Daedalus, which are said to run away if not tied down, and says that tethering them, by working out the reason why, is what makes them stay put and become knowledge. So the value question is why the tethering is any improvement, and my seminar instructor has been insisting that this single question is really at least three questions in disguise: what she calls the primary problem (why is knowledge more valuable than any mere true belief?), the secondary problem (why is knowledge more valuable than any proper part of itself, given that it seems to consist of a true belief plus something else?), and the tertiary or unification problem (why is knowledge more valuable than any epistemic standing that falls short of it?). I can more or less reproduce the definitions, but I can't reliably see the philosophical work the boundaries are doing — especially because some of the readings use the term swamping problem for the secondary problem, while others seem to use swamping for something subtly different, and I can no longer tell whether the swamping challenge is a problem specifically for reliabilism (where the extra component is the belief's being produced by a truth-conducive process) or for any theory whatsoever that analyses knowledge as true belief plus some further condition.

So here is what I'd like. First, explain the three problems so that I can keep them apart, using one worked example throughout — say, a doctor who truly believes a patient has a given condition, first as a lucky guess, then on the basis of a reliable but opaque diagnostic instrument, then on the basis of understanding the underlying pathology — and show, for that example, exactly which contrast each of the three problems targets and how the swapping of the value of truth over the added component is supposed to make the extra part worthless once the belief is true. Second, give me your view on whether the swamping problem is a local problem for process-reliabilism or a general problem for compositional analyses of knowledge, and say why. Third, since I'll be writing a term paper on this: please be scrupulous about dividing what is settled among epistemologists from what is genuinely contested. In particular, flag whether the primary problem is actually regarded as solved, since I have the impression that some authors take virtue-theoretic or knowledge-first responses to have dissolved it while others regard those as relabellings rather than solutions — and where the taxonomy itself or its standard attributions are disputed, say so rather than presenting one reading as the consensus.
