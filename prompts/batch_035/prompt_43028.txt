My two roommates, Dee and Marcus, had a blowout in our kitchen on Sunday that lasted like two hours, and they finally agreed to let me ask somone smarter than me. Background: Dee grew up in a Hindu family, Marcus just took a philosophy of religion class. It started because Marcus said karma is basically a cosmic theory of moral desert — you get what you deserve, across lifetimes. Dee said that was a Western misreading: in her tradition's telling, karma is more like an impersonal moral law of nature, no judge, no ledger, just actions ripening into consequences, and calling that "desert" smuggles in a punisher nobody posited. Marcus pushed back that a law which specifically tracks the moral quality of your intentions cant be neutral physics — gravity doesn't respond to whether you *meant* to push someone — so the pairing of cruel acts with future suffering is itself the desert claim, with the punisher deleted but the punishment kept. Dee answered that this confuses consequence with desert: people deserve better or worse than they get all the time, and a theory that only says deeds have downstream effects says nothing about what anyone is owed. Marcus said then karma is trivially true — of course actions have effects, so does everything — and the only contentful version is the desert one, which historically got used to tell the starving and the caste-bound that they earned it, which is exactly what you'd expect a desert theory to do.

Then it got weirder. Dee said the misuse objection is sociological, not conceptual (people misused Darwinism without refuting biology), and that desert doesn't need a deserts-assigner anyway — if you freely do wrong, you deserve bad outcomes whether or not anyone is keeping score. Marcus went deeper: in the classical picture the chain of lives is beginningless, there's no first lifetime, so every deed that's supposed to ground your current desert was itself the output of conditions from a previous life you didn't choose, and desert can't be bootstrpped out of an infinite regress with no freely chosen deed at the bottom. Dee fired back that this proves too much: nobody chose their genes or upbringing either, so if that regress kills karmic desert it kills everyday desert talk everywhere, and Marcus surely doesn't want to conclude that no one ever deserves anything. Marcus's last word was that everyday desert at least has a starting point (you, here, in this life), while karmic desert needs a grounding deed at every single node and has none.

I could genuinely see both of them landing punches and neither conceded anything. Please go through the argument move by move — who's right at each step, not just overall — and tell me what, if anything, both of them are missing. And since this involves what a whole set of traditions actually hold, please keep straight what's pretty settled among people who study this stuff versus what's genuinely contested, and don't hand me a contested reading as if it were the consensus.
