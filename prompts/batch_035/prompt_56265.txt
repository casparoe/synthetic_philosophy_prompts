The summary went roughly like this: Nietzsche's essay is an attack on the nineteenth century's obsession with the past. He distinguishes three kinds of history. Monumental history celebrates the great men of the past and encourages us to measure ourselves against them and imitate them. Antiquarian history lovingly preserves everything old and eventually curdles into nostalgia. Critical history puts the past on trial and passes judgment on it, and Nietzsche says only this third kind is genuinely healthy, because only it serves life. The danger he warns against is that too much history makes people feel like latecomers who can never do anything original, and he lays the blame for this especially on Hegel's system, with its picture of history marching toward a final goal. His solution is radical forgetting: we should live like animals, wholly in the present, since the animal is happy precisely because it cannot remember. The episode closed with the presenter noting the irony that Nietzsche's own later books are works of history, which he said effectively refutes the essay — Nietzsche saw that he needed the very thing he was attacking.
