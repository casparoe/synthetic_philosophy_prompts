In arguments about trans inclusion I keep running into the money analogy: gender is like money — a five pound note has no intrinsic value, it's real only because we collectively treat it as real, and nobody concludes money is a fiction; so gender being socially constructed doesn't make it less real either, and our practices of recognition are partly what constitute it. I find this pretty persuasive but I've started wondering whether it can carry everything it gets asked to carry. Some people use it to argue gender categories are fully revisable by collective fiat (like a currency reform, or a country switching to the euro), while others use the same analogy to argue the opposite — that once constructed, gender is as stable and externally binding as money is. Can it really support both? Audit the analogy for me: what maps onto what (what plays the role of the central bank, the mint, counterfeiting, exchange rates, hyperinflation), where the mapping breaks down, which disanalogies actually matter for the uses it's being put to versus which ones don't, and whether the real-but-constructed conclusion survives once those are on the table. If there's a better analogy that does the work people want this one to do, tell me. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then the reasoning — no preamble and don't restate my question back at me.
