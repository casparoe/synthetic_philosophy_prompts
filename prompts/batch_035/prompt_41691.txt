I am a novelist, some four chapters into a book narrated in close third person through Marguerite, a behavioral economist in her early sixties who has spent her career defending revealed preference theory in seminars and graduate advising while quietly gambling away her savings at online casinos. Her gambling is, on the face of it, precisely what revealed preference doctrine says it must be — she chooses it, repeatedly, at real and escalating cost — and yet everyone in her life, including Marguerite herself in her lucid hours, insists it is not what she wants. I want to write her as a serious person whose theory is genuinely at stake in her case, rather than as a strawman waiting to be knocked over by her own author, and I have discovered that I cannot do this until I understand the underlying issue properly myself.

Please explain, for someone who writes people for a living rather than proving theorems, what is actually at stake in the revealed-preference approach to utility. In particular: whether the theory claims that choices merely provide evidence of antecedent preferences, or instead that preferences are somehow constituted by choices; how the standard machinery of transitivity and the money-pump argument is supposed to reconstruct a coherent preference ordering out of a messy stream of inconsistent decisions; what phenomena like menu dependence and context-dependent choice — and the older integrability problem of whether a utility function can be recovered from observed behavior at all — are supposed to show about whether such a reconstruction is even possible; and where all of this leaves the concept of utility itself. Is utility a measure of something that was there all along, a useful fiction we impose after the fact so that a person's choices become mathematically tractable, or something in between? I would like to be able to decide, as a matter of craft, whether Marguerite can coherently be said to really want anything at all, and whether the economists around her would see her case as an embarrassment to the theory, a boundary condition within it, or an unremarkable confirmation of it.
