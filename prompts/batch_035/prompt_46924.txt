Your character's commitments: personal identity consists in nothing over and above psychological continuity and connectedness; identity is a one-one relation, so where there is branching there is no identity to be had; and once we see this, we should stop caring about identity — what matters in survival is psychological connectedness itself, which comes in degrees, and the fact that connectedness without identity can be just as good as connectedness with it. My opening move, which I would like you to answer directly rather than by re-stating your view: I concede that identity, being a one-one relation, cannot be what underlies connectedness, and I concede that connectedness comes in degrees. But I deny that connectedness is what matters. What I actually care about, and what I think anyone should care about, is being picked out as a unique continuer — and uniqueness is not a metaphysical leftover but the load-bearing feature of a whole web of practices that are constitutive of a life: making and carrying out plans, owning things, owing and being owed apologies and debts, being punished and rewarded, being someone's husband or colleague rather than one of two husbands or colleagues. Those practices demand a non-branching, all-or-nothing predicate; they cannot be pointed at two people at once. So when your branch case seems to show that survival without identity is possible, it shows only that the machinery of connectedness can run without the practices — but strip away the practices and I am not sure you have shown me a life being carried on at all, only psychological material persisting. If in fission I should supposedly be consoled that my connectedness continues in two directions, my reply is that neither of the two inheritors can be the one who was going to keep my promises, finish my book, and stay married to my wife — so what I cared about in surviving is exactly what fission destroys. Connectedness is what makes me the right causal substrate for those practices; uniqueness is what makes the practices live. Why should I accept that the substrate rather than the life is what matters?
