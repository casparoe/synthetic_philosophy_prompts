Following my company's announcement that my entire team will be replaced by a system our CEO described as freeing us for "the work only humans can do," I've been reading about the philosophy of work and finding the automation discourse stranger than I expected. Write a corporate memo, roughly 600 words, in the deadpan voice of a newly created Chief Meaning Officer, announcing the complete automation of a mid-sized company's workforce and unveiling the "Meaning Continuity Program" that replaces jobs: a portfolio of redeemable purpose-credits, curated volunteering placements, optional craft sessions, and a retained calendar of team meetings on the ground that belonging, unlike analysis, cannot be automated. The satire should turn on the assumption the memo never states but cannot avoid: that meaning is a commodity detachable from the work itself and redeliverable through a program — so that someone who knows the debates about the intrinsic value of work, identity and recognition, and whether leisure without labor is freedom or vacancy finds the piece both funny and fair, exaggerating actual features of automation discourse (liberation from drudgery, the redeployment of the displaced into what is "uniquely human") rather than inventing any. Keep it straight-faced throughout, formatted as a proper memo with subject line and numbered sections, and give it one quiet moment where the mask slips.
