i'm running a discussion section for an intro phil of mind course and want a one-page decision tree students can walk through on their own after we cover Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument. the tree should start from a commitment basically everyone in the room already has — that every physical event with a cause has a sufficient physical cause at the physical level — and work downward to leaves. each node has to be a question answerable from intuitions about cases, never from knowing a position's name, so nothing like "do you accept non-reductive physicalism?"

the node questions should be built around concrete scenarios, roughly these: a patient whose arm goes up from a muscle spasm vs. one who raised it on purpose, and whether anything causally differs between them; a molecule-for-molecule zombie twin who behaves identically but (we stipulate) has no inner life; a case where we hold the neural event fixed and ask whether any mental residue is left over to do causal work; and a two-shooters-simultaneously case to probe how comfortable they are with one effect having two independently sufficient causes — if they shrug at that, the overdetermination branch of the exclusion argument loses its bite, so the tree should route them accordingly.

the leaves need to include: type-identity reductionism, anomalous monism, realization-based non-reductive physicalism, Kim's later functional-reduction view, emergentism with downward causation, interactionist dualism, epiphenomenalism, and Russellian monism. include one extra leaf marked "unnamed" for any coherent answer-combination nobody seems to actually occupy, with a sentence on whether thats because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. flag explicitly any pair of leaves the tree can't genuinely seperate — I suspect anomalous monism vs. realization-based non-reductive physicalism will be the hard one, and if so say so instead of inventing a fake fork for it.

attach a short key: for each leaf, one sentence on what it commits you to and one on its most awkward commitment (the thing its defenders wince about when pressed). keep the whole thing to one page plus the key — students wont read more than that.
