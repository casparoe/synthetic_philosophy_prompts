I teach a mid-level course on well-being -- 16 students, one 75-minute session. Next week we hit Nozick's experience machine, and honestly the machine by itself has gotten stale: everyone's heard of it, half of them think the answer is obvious, and the discussion dies in five minutes. I want an in-class game that makes them feel the pull from the inside, before they realize what question we're actually asking.

Here's what I've been mulling: the machine is supposed to be an argument against hedonism, but the cases that really trip my students up are the ones that split hedonism from desire-satisfaction theories -- lives where the experience is great but the world quietly fails to cooperate (the partner who secretly despises you, the achievements that turn out to be fake, the person whose desires all get satisfied but only in ways they never find out about). So I'd like the game to walk them down that ladder: start with ranking lives purely by what they feel like from the inside, then have facts leak out gradually, and see where their rankings crack and why.

Constraints: no real money, no one has to role-play being personally deceived by me (they hate that), and it has to run on index cards and envolopes. I'd like some scoring or payoff structure so that at each stage there's a defensible "right answer" under each theory -- that way in the debrief we can look back at round 2 and ask each of them "which theory were you actually using there?" And I don't want the machine mentioned by name until the very end, if at all.

Plus the usual stuff: a debrief that maps what happened onto hedonism vs. desire-satisfaction vs. objective-list views, a note on what will predicatably go sideways the first time I run it, and honesty about which part of the philosophical problem a game just cannot reproduce (I'm guessing something like the weight of actually being asked to plug in can't be staged, but tell me if you disagree).
