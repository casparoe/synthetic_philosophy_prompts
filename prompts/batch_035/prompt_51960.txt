I'm a journalist who writes about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm preparing a long essay on the 2021 boom and bust of NFTs and cryptocurrencies. Rather than the usual manias-and-bubbles framing, I'd like to run the episode through Aristotle's account of money and exchange: his distinction between use and exchange in the Nicomachean Ethics, the natural art of household wealth-getting set against unlimited chrematistics in the Politics, the observation that money was introduced to facilitate exchange but came to be pursued as the object of exchange, and his special loathing of interest as money breeding money. Please give me a disciplined case analysis: which features of the crypto episode his framework genuinely illuminates (assets whose value is almost pure exchange value, wealth-getting pursued as an end in itself with no natural terminus), where his lens misdescribes or simply goes silent (digital scarcity, self-custody, the projects where people used tokens rather than held them), and what the episode does to Aristotle in return — whether it vindicates his worry about unnatural wealth-getting or reveals it to be a complaint about scale rather than kind. Assume my readers are educated generalists who have never opened a page of Aristotle; keep the Greek to the terms they'll need, and don't let the essay become a lecture on ancient economics.
