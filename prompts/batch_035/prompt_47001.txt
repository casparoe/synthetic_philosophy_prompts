hey, so i run a tiny philosophy blog and i want to make one of those "which theory of pleasure do you actually hold?" quizzes, aimed at readers who've heard of hedonism but never really dug into the heterogeneity problem -- the old worry that the pleasures of eating an orange, listening to Beethoven, finishing a hard proof, and lying in the sun seem to have nothing felt in common, so what exactly is the "pleasure" that hedonism says is the only intrinsic good?

you're basically a professional philosopher of mind who also writes well for a general audience, so please take that role seriously. think for a bit before you write -- brainstorm which distinctions actually do the sorting work before you commit to a quiz structure, because the whole point is that the quiz should sort readers by what they say about cases, not by which label they like.

the format: 8 to 10 questions, each one a concrete case with 2-3 answer options (options should be plain-English descriptions of what's going on in the case, never names of theories). then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the positions. the positions i need as leaves:

- the classic "hedonic tone" view: pleasure is one distinctive feeling-tone all pleasant experiences share
- disjunctive / family-resemblance views: the pleasant experiences form a heterogeneous bunch united by resemblance, not a shared ingredient
- the felt-betterness style view: what unites them is that each is experienced as better, in a distinctive felt way, than the alternatives available at that moment (so it's a comparative phenomenal property, not a separate tone)
- attitudinal accounts: pleasure is not a sensation at all but a stance you take toward states of affairs -- being pleased *that* something is the case -- with bodily pleasures as maybe a special case or maybe a problem case
- the deflationary "distinct existents" view: there is simply nothing interesting in common, and hedonism has to be reformulated or abandoned

cases i'd like you to work in (you can add others): morphine patients who report deep contentment while denying they feel any distinct sensation of pleasure; masochistic pain that is somehow also wanted and satisfying; the "false pleasure" case where someone is pleased that their friend won an award, but they were lied to and the friend lost; a flow state like rock-climbing where the person later says it was the best part of their week but during it they felt no distinct pleasure at all, just absorbed attention; and the runner's high vs. quiet satisfaction contrast. make sure at least one question does real work separating the felt-betterness view from plain hedonic tone, since those are easy to conflate, and at least one separates attitudinal accounts from everything sensation-based.

a few things i care about:

1. no question should be answerable just by knowing the philosophy jargon -- phrase everything so someone who's never heard of any of these views has to reveal their actual intuitions
2. after the scoring key, flag which pairs of positions the quiz can't cleanly separate (e.g. can it really distinguish the disjunctive view from the deflationary one?) and suggest which single extra question might fix it, or say honestly that no single question can
3. point out any combination of answers that doesn't map onto any real leaf, and note whether that's because the combination is incoherent or just unexplored

also -- the blog audience skews skeptical, so dont make any question sound like it's pushing the reader toward one answer. don't add a reading list or cite sources, the quiz has to stand alone. go ahead and think out loud about the tree structure first if you want, but the final deliverable should be the finished quiz i can paste straight into the blog. thanks!!
