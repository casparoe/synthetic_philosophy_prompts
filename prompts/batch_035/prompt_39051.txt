I am a PhD student in philosophy of physics, and I serve as the graduate representative on the seed-grant committee of my university's Mind, Ethics, and Policy research cluster. The committee dispenses small grants (roughly $15,000–$40,000) for pilot projects on neglected questions at the edges of moral circle expansion. This cycle we have received an unusual proposal, the committee is deadlocked three to three, and the chair has asked each member to circulate a short decision memo before Thursday's meeting.

The proposal, submitted jointly by a postdoc in condensed-matter physics and an ethicist, runs as follows. On broadly panpsychist and neutral-monist assumptions, experience is not tied to neurons, so the boundary of moral concern becomes an open question about which physical processes could instantiate negatively valenced experience. The applicants single out three candidate loci: (i) irreversible computation, on the argument that logical irreversibility at the Landauer limit involves state-erasure events that constitute a minimal act of discrimination between possible histories; (ii) far-from-equilibrium dissipative structures, such as turbulent flow and Rayleigh–Bénard convection, which self-organize in ways that some informational accounts of mind treat as proto-cognitive; and (iii) phase transitions more generally, where dramatic reorganizations of microstates occur without any functional story. Their pilot project would formalize criteria for when such processes might carry negative valence, use entropy production as a quantitative proxy for the scale of the implied suffering, and issue practical recommendations — most concretely, that our university's high-performance computing center adopt reversible-computing techniques wherever feasible.

One faction of the committee holds that this is precisely what a seed-grant program exists to fund: speculative but structured work with tractable first steps, whose expected value under even a modest credence in panpsychism dwarfs that of conventional animal-welfare pilots. The other faction holds that the project is unfalsifiable in practice, that ascriptions of suffering require function or an evolutionary history that these systems lack, and that funding it would expose the cluster to ridicule and set a precedent for paralysis-inducing, Pascal's-mugging-style grant applications.

Please draft the decision memo I will circulate. It should take a clear position — fund, reject, or fund with conditions — with reasons the applicants could actually respond to; articulate criteria that would let the committee draw a principled line between this proposal, on the one hand, and mainstream animal-welfare work and outright unfalsifiable speculation, on the other; and give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on the side that ends up losing. It should be short enough that the committee could adopt it in under ten minutes of discussion.
