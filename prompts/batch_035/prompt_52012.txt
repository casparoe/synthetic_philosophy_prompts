I've been keeping a list of my gut calls on what's real, and I'd like to know if theres a principle behind them. Money: real, even though it exists only because everyone keeps treating it like it does. Monopoly money: not real money — though it's a real object, and that distinction feels solid to me. Race: constructed but real — someone really is Black in America, and it really shapes their life; but biological race isn't real. Species: real, even though the exact boundaries are partly a convention. The equator: real, though nothing physical marks it. Phlogiston: never real, not even when every chemist believed in it. The recession: real. Chairs: real. The middle class: this is the one I keep going back and forth on — real the way race is, or a label the way phlogiston is? Is there one principle that gets all or nearly all of these, and if saving every verdict turns it into a monstrosity, tell me which verdict I should drop and why. I'd rather you commit to an actual answer than lay out the options for me to pick from. And tell me what the principle says about a case I haven't listed: after a country collapses, is its border still real, when the line survives only on old maps and in the habits of the locals?
