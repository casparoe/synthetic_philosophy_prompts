I am a doctoral student in early Christian studies, drafting the methods chapter of a dissertation on the historical Jesus. My supervisor, who trained in the era when the criteria of authenticity — embarrassment, dissimilarity, multiple attestation, coherence, and the rest — were the working machinery of the field, has since declared the criteria era over and insists that the chapter justify any method I use rather than inherit one. Before defending any particular criterion, I want to know what a criterion of authenticity would have to deliver to be worth using at all. Please write that specification.

The requirements should address, at minimum: what kind of thing a criterion is supposed to be — a rule for sorting authentic from inauthentic material, a device for weighting testimony, or something else entirely; the independence problem, since a criterion like multiple attestation works only if the sources it counts are genuinely independent, and the judgments that establish independence may themselves rest on conclusions about what is authentic; the selection problem, since what survived was preserved by communities with theological interests, so the historian works from a filtered sample rather than a representative one; consistency of application, since a rule whose output varies with the historian applying it does no discernible work; and the base-rate problem — if a criterion flags some feature of the tradition as odd enough to be authentic, odd relative to what population, and who fixes that population?

For each requirement, state the case or consideration behind it and mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference that a particular school in the field might waive. Then give me the part I most want: identify any subset of the requirements that cannot be jointly satisfied — in particular whether non-circularity can coexist with applicability at all, and whether a demand for distinctive material (the material that would explain why Jesus was remembered in the first place) can coexist with a demand not to distort the man by prizing only what is unusual in his first-century Jewish setting. If some subset is jointly unsatisfiable, say what follows: whether the criteria are repairable instruments, or whether settling authenticity by criteria is misconceived from the ground up, and what the field's critics of the criteria have put in their place. Close by naming the single requirement that, if enforced, would disqualify the most criteria in current use, and why.
