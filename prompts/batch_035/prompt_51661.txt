The question: what is the best framing for current production chat models — agent, simulator, or something else? To make it have a truth value, let me stipulate what the options mean for the purpose of this prompt. By "agent" I mean that the right unit of explanation for predicting and controlling the model's outputs is a single, persistent, goal-directed system that carries something like values and beliefs across prompts and selects outputs to advance its goals. By "simulator" I mean that the right unit is a generative model of text-producing processes, from which context elicits transient personas — simulacra — with no goal-bearer persisting above them; the "assistant character" is just one simulacrum among many, and nothing in the model corresponds to the model itself having a perspective. By "something else" I mean any view on which neither framing is right — for instance, that post-training turns the system into a hybrid that is neither a pure next-token simulator nor a classical planner, or that the question is confused at the level it's asked.

My own credence that the simulator framing is the best of these for current chat models is 0.55. My colleague, who runs safety evaluations, puts it at 0.15 — she thinks fine-tuning collapses the ensemble of simulacra into one persistent character with its own goals, and that the simulator story only fit pre-trained base models.
