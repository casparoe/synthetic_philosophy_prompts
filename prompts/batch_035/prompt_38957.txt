I run a small online reading group on decision theory — mixed crowd, some LessWrong-adjacent folks, a couple of economics grad students, one philosopher who thinks the whole subject is silly. Next session is on acausal trade and I want to hand out a "which position do you actually hold" style decision tree that sorts people by their answers to cases, not by which label they like.

Quick background for the handout's framing (rephrase freely, this is just the idea): acausal trade is trade between agents who never causally interact. Neither can signal the other or affect the other directly, but each can model the other well enough to predict roughly how it behaves, and each can choose its own policy in ways it knows the other will factor into its own choice. So the story goes, each can benefit the other "for free" — I leave some resource untouched for your sake because I predict you leave some for mine, even though we can never communicate or meet. It comes up in discussions of superrationality, FDT-ish decision theories, prisoner's dilemmas against distant copies of yourself, and occasionally in wilder speculations about superintelligences in causally disconnected universes. That's all the background the quiz should need.

The tree should be built from the logical space, not from a list of names. Start from the questions the positions actualy turn on — my guesses would be things like: whether "benefiting someone" even makes sense as a motive when your act can't causally affect them; whether you can have, and should act on, evidence about another agent's policy absent any causal contact; whether it matters if the other agent is a literal copy of you vs merely similar; whether the trade requires mutual transparency (each knowing the other is running the same reasoning) or whether unilateral prediction suffices; whether the logic extends downward to simpler, dumber agents — would you "trade" with something thermostat-like that just reliably leaves resources alone? But pick the 5 or 6 branch questions that you think do the real sorting work, and phrase every one so someone who's never heard the term could answer it. Nothing that's just "do you believe in acausal trade?" in different words.

Leaves should include at least: the full superrationalist (cooperate with any sufficiently predictable agent running similar reasoning), the version that only goes for literal copies / self-simulations, the causal-decision-theory holdout (no causal influence, no reason to budge), the person who thinks every real "acausal trade" case is just convergent interests mislabeled, and the one who accepts the logic but only as prudence — I act this way to keep my own dispositions sane, not for the other agent's sake. Plus whatever other leaves fall out of the tree naturally, but keep the total at 7 or fewer since this needs to fit on one page. Each leaf gets one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and flag any pair of leaves your branch questions can't genuinely separate — don't paper over it.

One more thing: since a couple of group members are economists, it'd be nice if one branch somewhere hinged on whether they'd rather treat the whole thing as an equilibrium / focal-point concept than a decision-theory one — work that in if it fits naturally, don't force it. And don't worry about making the tree fair to the position I personally hold; I'd rather be honestly sorted than flattered.
