My department is drafting a policy for how to respond to fundraising appeals that promise a small probability of enormous benefit — campaigns for life-extension research, asteroid deflection, and the like. Since Pascal's mugging demonstrates that an expected utility maximizer is obliged to comply with any demand backed by a sufficiently large promised payoff, I've concluded that we need to discount tiny probabilities outright. My proposal is to treat any probability below one in a hundred million as exactly zero while leaving larger probabilities untouched. Will this solve the mugging problem while still allowing us to rationally buy insurance and fund vaccination campaigns, or does it create worse problems than the one it solves? I'd also like to know whether a sufficiently persistent mugger could simply inflate the promised payoff until it clears our threshold.
