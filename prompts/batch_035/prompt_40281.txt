I'm a retired condensed matter physicist (35 years, mostly on phase transitions), and somewhere along the way I picked up a philosophical position I can't fully defend, so you're going to help me stress-test it. My grandson showed me one of those YouTube videos about Hilbert's hotel a couple years back, I went down the rabbit hole, read a bit about Cantor and the different sizes of infinity, and I've come out with what I'd call a moderate finitism. Here's the position: potential infinities are fine, obviously -- there's no largest natural number, and unbounded processes don't bother me. But completed/actual infinities, things that just ARE infinite all at once, I don't believe in. In physics that means no infinite universe, no actually infinite past, no physical quantity that takes an infinite value -- the infinities I saw my whole career (in QED, in critical exponents near a critical point) were always limits, subtractions, idealizations, never something measured. In math I'm happy to say the hierarchy of infinite cardinals exists as bookkeeping inside the formalism, a useful fiction, but not as a discovery about some actually infinite realm. I'm not one of those people who thinks huge finite numbers are suspicious -- my quarrel is only with "completed" infinity, not with big finite things.

Take the role of a philosopher of mathematics who also knows enough physics to not be bluffed by it. Think carefully about this one, and I'd like you to start with some brainstorming: before you rank anything, spell out what exactly my version of the position commits me to, because I suspect I've stated it sloppily and some defeaters might only hit versions I don't hold.
