I am a novelist, and I would like help diagnosing something about how my profession is treated, because the pattern looks inconsistent to me and I suspect it presupposes a theory nobody states.

The critical culture I was trained in treated the author as irrelevant to the work's meaning: Barthes's obituary for the author, the intentional fallacy as a term of abuse, the reader's freedom as a doctrine. When a review misread my second novel, I replied publicly that the ending meant a particular thing because that is what I had constructed it to mean, and the unanimous response was that this was beside the point — my intention has no authority over what the text means. Fair enough; that is the doctrine as I learned it.

And yet the same literary culture, in nearly the same breath, treats me as the owner of the novel's meaning everywhere else. Book clubs write to ask what I meant by the ending, and my answer settles the argument. Reviews interpret my fiction through my biography as though the life were the key to the text. When interviewers ascribe my narrator's politics to me, or readers demand that I account for something my antagonist does, my protest that the character is not me is heard as evasion: I am held responsible for the content of sentences I demonstrably wrote on purpose. My contract gives me approval over cuts, jacket copy, and adaptations — a legal recognition that the work's integrity is mine to defend. And in scholarly practice, when editors prepare editions of dead writers, they use letters and manuscripts to restore what the author is presumed to have intended, sometimes undoing changes the author himself approved in proof.

So the question: what view of a text's meaning, and of an author's relation to it, does this combined practice presuppose? Is it flatly inconsistent, or is there a principled distinction between the contexts where my intention is authoritative and the contexts where it counts for nothing? Can actual intentionalism, hypothetical intentionalism, or some two-level view — on which what a text means and what its author is answerable for are different questions — actually generate the observed pattern? Please reconstruct the implicit theory, separate what the practice strictly requires from what it merely sits comfortably with, and tell me what would have to change, and on which side, for the practice to become coherent.
