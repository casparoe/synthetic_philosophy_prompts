My dad is 81 and has had Alzheimer's for about six years now. Back when he was still sharp, maybe fifteen years ago, he sat me and my siblings down after his brother died following a long decline in a nursing home, and made us promise we'd never let him linger like that — he was really emphatic about it, said being alive without his mind was his worst nightmare, and that the person in that bed wouldn't really be him anymore, just a body carrying his name. We all promised. We even have it in writing somewhere, a letter he wrote to us telling us not to feel guilty about letting him go.

Here's the thing though: dad today is, honestly, pretty content. He's in a memory care facility, doesn't remember the promise or really understand what's happening to him, spends his days feeding the birds outside his window, listening to old country records, doing jigsaw puzzles badly and happily. Last month he got a bad pneumonia and the doctors asked whether we wanted aggressive treatment or just comfort care. When we asked him directly — he was still lucid enough to answer in the moment — he clearly said he wants to get better, he wants to live. My older sister says the promise controls: the man who wrote that letter knew exactly what he valued and did his deciding while he still could, and honoring him now means honoring that decision, not polling the version of him that can't follow a train of thought for two minutes. My younger brother says that's insane — the actual living person in front of us, the one who smiles at the birds, is telling us what he wants, and overruling him in favor of a piece of paper means we're respecting a ghost instead of a man. We went with the antibiotics this time and he recovered, but it's eating at me because there will be a next time, and I'm the one with the healthcare proxy.

I flip between their positions constantly and it's not helping anyone. I'd genuinely take either a principled way to weigh these, or a straight-up verdict — if you think one of my siblings is basically right and the other is wrong, tell me that plainly instead of giving me a wishy-washy both-sides answer, because I need to actually decide something here. And push back if I'm framing the whole question wrong in the first place — like if asking "which dad gets to decide" is already a confused way of putting it, I'd want to know what the real question is instead.
