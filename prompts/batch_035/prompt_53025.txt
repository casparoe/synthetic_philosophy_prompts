Quick bit of background since it shapes the question: I spent close to forty years as a condensed matter physicist and never once thought hard about time. It was the parameter t in the Hamiltonian, and the relativity of simultaneity was just coordinate bookkeeping — a fact about how you slice spacetime, not about what exists. Now that I'm retired and reading philosophy for the first time, I've run into the Rietdijk–Putnam argument (special relativity plus relativity of simultaneity supposedly shows the future is already fixed and real in exactly the way the past is — the block universe), and what bugs me isn't the argument itself but the state of the debate around it. This thing has been going for nearly sixty years and, as far as I can tell, nobody has moved an inch. The physicists I worked with mostly shrugged and accepted the block universe as the obvious reading of the math; the philosophers of time keep insisting the argument commits some fallacy, usually something about the words "real" or "determinate," or about simultaneity being a convention rather than a discovery. My old colleagues say the philosophers don't understand the physics; my philosophy reading group at the community center says the physicists don't understand that "real" was never a physical term to begin with.

So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is there a hidden physical question lurking in here — like whether any fundamental violation of Lorentz invariance (quantum gravity people mutter about this) would hand the presentist a preferred slicing of spacetime and let them off the hook — or is it purely verbal, with no experiment that could ever settle it? A woman in my reading group claims it's all equivocation: physicists use "real" to mean "appears in the model," philosophers use it to name some metaphysical primitive, and Rietdijk–Putnam just slides between the two. I half buy that, but not entirely, because it seems like something substantive rides on whether there's an objective, frame-independent fact about what's happening "now" halfway across the galaxy, and the man in my group who's a presentist is surely not just confused about vocabulary. Diagnose the impasse for me using what the two sides actually say, and be honest about whether anything — a result from quantum gravity, a cleaner distinction, a better thought experiment — could genuinely end it, or whether this is just how it's going to be for the rest of my retiremnet. I'd rather know I'm watching a permanent stalemate than keep waiting for a resolution that isn't coming.
