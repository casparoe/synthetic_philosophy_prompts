A philosophy podcast I listened to recently explained the bootstrapping or "easy knowledge" problem roughly as follows: 'You can justify anything on the cheap by appealing to evidence that already assumes what you're trying to establish. Want to confirm that your car's gas gauge is reliable? Just track its record: notice the gauge reads empty, look in the tank and see it's empty, repeat this a few times, and infer that the gauge is reliable. But this is circular, because the conclusion -- the gauge is reliable -- is already contained in the premises, and a circular argument gives you no justification at all for anything you didn't already have. It follows that induction can never justify itself, which is why Hume's problem of induction has no solution. Moore's famous proof of the external world -- "here is one hand" -- is the same trick in different clothing, which is why almost no epistemologist accepts it.'
