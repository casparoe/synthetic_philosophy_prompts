I've recently joined my company's hiring-equity review board — appointed just last month, which is why this has landed on me so suddenly. At our last meeting the board upheld a sanction against a director of candidate acquisition, based on the annual report showing that women received offers at a notably lower rate than men overall. Her team has now appealed with department-by-department numbers showing that within every single department, women received offers at a *higher* rate than men; the aggregate gap appears to be driven by women applying in larger numbers to the departments with the lowest offer rates. We must decide at next month's meeting whether to uphold or rescind the sanction, and I've been asked to draft the written opinion circulated in advance.

Please draft that opinion for me, structured as follows, in this order: a Summary of the recommendation (about 100 words); The Numbers, a plain restatement of both the aggregate and the departmental findings, with a sentence on how both can be true at once (about 150 words); What the Statistics Alone Can Settle, which should carefully distinguish what a reversal under disaggregation proves about confounding from what it leaves open — including whether the departments applicants sort themselves into is itself affected by fairness problems upstream (about 350 words); Options Considered, at least three, including upholding the sanction and rescinding it (about 250 words); and Recommendation and Next Steps, concrete and actionable (about 150 words). Throughout, note explicitly how confident you are in each major claim. Close the memo with a short final section, titled Where I Am Least Sure, naming the single point you are least confident about and what evidence would change your mind on it. The other board members are not statisticians, so keep any formal apparatus minimal and explain whatever you use.
