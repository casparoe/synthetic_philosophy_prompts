My friend has strong opinions about moral luck and I've been keeping a running list of her verdicts. Two identically drunk drivers, one hits a kid and one gets home fine: she says the one who hit the kid is worse. A hired killer whose gun jams at the last second vs. one whose gun fires: the successful one is worse. And a reckless speeder who luckily hurts nobody vs. a careful driver whose car has a completely unforeseeable brake failure and kills someone: she says the speeder is worse, no hesitation there.

She's coming to dinner next week and two new cases are going to come up. One: a guy glances at a text on the highway — a small everyday lapse, low risk, the kind of thing half of us do — drifts, and kills a cyclist. Two: a hunter who fires at a shape he's mostly but not completely sure is a deer, and it turns out to be a person. Where is she going to land? Like, is the texting driver close to the drunk driver who killed, or close to the lucky speeder? And where does the hunter fit? I don't just want your guesses — I want the candidate rules that fit her track record so far, what each one predicts for these two cases (including whether the texter ranks above or below the lucky speeder, which I suspect is the real crunch point), your best guess and why, and one further case that would best pull the surviving rules apart. Also be honest about where your read of her is most fragile: which verdict from her on the new cases would definately sink your favorite interpretation.
