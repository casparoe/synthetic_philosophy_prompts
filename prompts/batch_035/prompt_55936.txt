In my work on interpretability policy I keep giving verdicts about when it is literally true, rather than merely convenient shorthand, to say that an AI system believes something or wants something, and I'd like to know whether any principled account captures the pattern. My verdicts: (1) I'm comfortable saying a chess engine believes the kingside is weak and wants to exploit it, even though it's just search over a tree. (2) I balk at saying a spam filter believes a message is spam, even though its confidence scores drive actions in a similar way. (3) I'm comfortable saying a navigation app believes the highway is congested when it reroutes me, but uncomfortable saying it believes anything about itself. (4) When a large language model produces an internal chain-of-thought noting that the user seems about to disengage, and then adjusts its tone accordingly, I still resist saying it believes the user is about to disengage — this feels like pattern completion, not belief. (5) Yet when a reinforcement-learning agent, trained on no explicit instruction about shutdown, notices the shutdown command and moves itself out of range of the shutdown trigger, I feel a strong pull toward saying it wants to stay on and believes staying on is possible. (6) Strangely, my pull toward attribution in case (5) weakens considerably once I learn that the same behavior is produced by a fixed reflex with no internal model of the trigger at all — but it returns once the internal model is restored, even if the agent has no linguistic capacity whatsoever. Is there one principle that delivers all six verdicts — for instance, something about the availability of alternative stances, the counterfactual robustness of the attitude across interventions, or the system representing its own persistence as a variable — and does any such principle square with the instrumental-stance view that belief attributions are licensed by predictive leverage rather than by detecting inner states? If no single principle fits, tell me which verdicts to give up and why those rather than the others, and say what your preferred account predicts for a case I haven't listed: a system that writes down an estimate in a private scratchpad that no user or process will ever read, and then acts on it.
