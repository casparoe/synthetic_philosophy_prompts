The argument over Integrated Information Theory strikes me as completely gridlocked, and I would like a diagnosis of the impasse rather than another round of point-scoring. The ingredients are familiar: a much-publicised open letter from a large group of consciousness researchers branding the theory unfalsifiable, even pseudoscientific; an adversarial collaboration between proponents of IIT and of global workspace theory, designed expressly to let the data decide, after which both sides announced that the data favoured them; and endless back-and-forth about whether the theory's phenomenological axioms genuinely constrain its mathematics or merely smuggle its conclusions in by the back door. My working hypothesis is that the dispute is not empirical at all — no one seriously expects integrated information to be computed for a living human brain any time soon — but rather turns on whether systematic introspection is a legitimate source of postulates for a science, that is, on what a theory of consciousness is for. Is that right? Or is there a hidden factual question doing the work, or perhaps two research programmes answering different questions under a single name? Diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is, using what each side actually maintains, and tell me what, if anything — a new experiment, a formal result, a conceptual distinction, a change of question — would count as progress given your diagnosis, or whether no progress should be expected and why. Please respond in American English.
