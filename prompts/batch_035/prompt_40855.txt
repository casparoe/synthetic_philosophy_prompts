i'm teaching russell's theory of descriptions in my intro logic class next week and i want to open with something that'll actually stick. write me a villanelle about the present king of france. the two refrains should be the apparent contradictories — one line saying the present king of france is bald, the other that he isnt. the trick is that the villanelle form keeps dragging those two lines back and making them look like they have to clash, and the poem's real job is to show that by the closing couplet, when the refrains finally sit side by side, the fight has dissolved rather than been won: on russell's analysis neither line comes out true, because there is no king of france for either of them to be about. so the surface grammar, which makes it look like a contradiction, should give way over the course of the poem to the logical form underneath. id love it if the middle stanzas quietly showed the work the word the is doing — the smuggled in existence and uniqueness claims — and if you could slip in a nod to negative existentials or meinong as an easter egg for the one student who actually did the reading. accuracy matters more than beauty here, though ill happily take both. this is going up on the projector before i say a single word, so it has to work as a poem even for the kids who havent read a page of russell — they should feel the puzzle, and the kids who catch the resolution should feel clever about it
