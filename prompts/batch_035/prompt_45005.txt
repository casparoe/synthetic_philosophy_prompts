I'm taking an upper-level epistemology seminar this term, and we just finished a unit on Nozick's tracking theory and the problem of epistemic closure. There's a set of study notes circulating in my group, put together by a student who took the course last year and did well, and several of us have been treating them as gospel. But when I tried to reconstruct Nozick's position from them for our problem set, what I produced felt off, and now I can't tell whether my discomfort is because the notes are subtly wrong or because I misunderstood something. Here is the relevant paragraph, quoted in full:

Nozick's insight was that knowledge isn't true belief plus internal justification, but true belief that tracks the facts: you know that p when p is true, you believe p, and, in the closest worlds where p is false, you believe not-p. This tracking condition is essentially what later writers call safety — it's the idea behind Williamson's line that knowledge is the most general factive mental state — so Nozick really anticipated the safety-based accounts by a couple of decades. The upshot is that knowledge fails to be closed under logical consequence, and this is what answers skepticism: you track your having hands (if you had no hands, you wouldn't believe you had them), so you know you have hands; and since you know that having hands entails that you're not a brain in a vat, you are thereby entitled to conclude that you're not a brain in a vat, so the skeptical argument never gets off the ground. The only cost is that some deductions from known premises fail to preserve knowledge, which is a small price given the anti-skeptical payoff.

Some of this rings true to me — I do remember the lecture emphasizing that Nozick's conditions are counterfactual rather than justificatory, and I recall the grandmother example, where she believes her grandson is well and it's true, but she wouldn't believe otherwise if he were ill because the family would keep it from her, so she doesn't track and doesn't know. But a friend in the seminar insists that the notes are conflating two conditions that the literature keeps sharply apart, and that the anti-skeptical conclusion doesn't actually follow from anything Nozick says.
