So a few weeks ago I dropped a mug and it didn't break, and ever since I've been weirdly obsessed with what it even means to call something fragile. My friend Dan took a philosophy of science class in college and when I brought it up he gave me this explanation that I've been chewing on ever since: a disposition is supposed to be just a conditional in disguise -- something is fragile exactly when it would break if it were struck, soluble exactly when it would dissolve if dropped in water, and so on. But he said philosophers discovered counterexamples called finks and masks that kill this analysis. A fink is named after the philosopher who came up with the idea (some guy called Fink I guess?), and it's a process that kicks in the moment the stimulus shows up and undoes the disposition before it can do anything -- like a protective sorcerer who casts a spell making the glass permanently sturdy the instant you go to strike it, so the glass really is fragile but never breaks. And a mask is basically the same move again, just slower or using stuff instead of magic -- like glass packed in bubble wrap, which he said means the glass stops being fragile while it's wrapped. His big takeaway was that since finks and masks can crop up anywhere, the conditional analysis is completely dead, and the real lesson is that dispositions can't be analysed at all -- they're primitive powers we just project onto objects, kind of a leftover vitalism for everyday properties.

Something about this feels too tidy but I honestly can't tell where. Is the summary fair to the philosophy here? Which parts would a metaphysician accept, which are simplifcations, and which are just confused? I'm espeically unsure about the bubble wrap bit -- it seems strange to say my glass stops being fragile just because it's padded, since if I unwrapped it it would smash just fine, but Dan insists that's the whole point of masks. And does his conclusion actually follow, that dispositions are primitive and unanalysable? Or do the counterexamples only show the analysis needs patching up rather than abandoning? Go through his summary piece by piece and tell me what I can trust and what I should push back on next time I see him.
