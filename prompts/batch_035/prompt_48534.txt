I've been rereading Hofstadter's *Gödel, Escher, Bach* — mostly the dialogue interludes, which I remember fondly — and I finally sat down to work through the Typogenetics chapter properly. My sense is that Hofstadter is trying to set up a two-way isomorphism: molecular genetics read as a typographical formal system (strands as strings, enzymes as operations acting on them), and, in the other direction, Gödelian self-reference read as a kind of self-description that foreshadows how a molecule can carry the instructions for building the very enzymes that copy it. But the chapter is oddly paced and I keep losing the thread of which pieces of the analogy are meant to do real work and which are just decorative.

Could you lay the isomorphism out carefully for me? In particular, I'd like to understand (a) how the "folding" of a typoenzyme is supposed to determine which operations it performs on a strand, and why Hofstadter needs that indirection at all rather than just stipulating a fixed operation per enzyme; (b) where exactly the self-replication story connects to Gödel-style self-reference — is the self-reproducing strand genuinely analogous to a self-referential sentence, or is it closer to a quine in the programming sense, and is there a difference between those two? I've played around with writing quines myself, and my intuition is that a quine reproduces its source but doesn't say anything *about* itself in the way Gödel's construction does, but I'm not sure the distinction survives in Typogenetics, where copying a strand arguably is a statement about the strand.

Two follow-ups if you can address them. First, how does Hofstadter's toy system compare to von Neumann's earlier self-reproducing automata — do they succeed at evading infinite regress (the instruction-copying regress) by the same trick, or different ones? Second, a practical question: if someone implemented Typogenetics as a genuine artificial chemistry and let it run in a simulated primordial soup, would you expect it to exhibit open-ended evolution, or does the way the system's translation table (base pairs to amino-acid-like operations) is fixed by fiat rather than inherited foreclose the interesting Darwinian dynamics? I'd rather have your honest assessment of where the analogy is tight and where it creaks than a defense of Hofstadter on all fronts.
