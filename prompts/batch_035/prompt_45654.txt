I'm a software engineer, never taken a philosophy class in my life, but my brother-in-law and I got into it at Thanksgiving about when it's okay to just take someone's word on a moral question instead of figuring it out yourself. I've been going through my gut calls and I can't find the pattern. Here they are, tell me if there's one principle that covers them:

- Before signing with a defense contractor, I ask my most thoughtful friend whether it's wrong to build targeting software. She says it's fine, I take her word and sign. Totally fine by me.
- Same job, but this time I don't even ask — I just know she'd say it's fine, so I skip the agonizing and sign. Feels a little worse somehow? Can't explain why.
- Checking a website that rates charities on effectiveness before donating. Obviously fine, that's just being responsible.
- On a jury, another juror who's clearly smarter and more thought-through about it says the defendant deserves leniency, and I switch my vote without working through the evidence myself. Not fine — that one's mine to decide.
- It's 11pm, I'm exhausted, deciding whether to tell a friend his partner is cheating. I text my sister, she says tell him, I tell him. Fine, honestly. I don't feel bad about that one at all.
- My manager says the dark pattern we're shipping is fine, standard industry stuff, and I go along without pressing him on it. Not fine, and no amount of "I trusted his judgment" gets me off the hook.
- A hospital ethics committee tells us it's time to take my uncle off the ventilator, and we do it, without me forming any independent view of my own. Fine? I think fine.
- Voting exactly how my well-informed friend votes on the local ballot measures. Kinda lazy but fine.

The jury one and the 11pm text one feel really different to me but look basically identical on paper — in both I'm rubber-stamping someone else's moral read of a situation that was my job to figure out. And there's a case I keep circling that made me realize I don't have a principle: suppose I could hire a "personal ethics advisor," like an accountant but for moral questions — all my hard calls route through them, they're wise and incorruptible. That sounds grotesque to me, but it's hard to see what exactly is wrong with it if the friend and the ethics committee cases are fine. So: is there a principle that saves all my verdicts, or do I have to give some up, and which ones? And what does the principle say about the advisor?
