I'd like a dialogue built on the absent-minded driver setup, so let me spell it out so were all on the same page: a driver is heading home on a highway with two identical intersections where he can exit, but he's so sleep-deprived he can't remember whether he's already passed one. Payoffs: exiting at the first intersection dumps him into road construction (payoff 0), the second exit is the good one (payoff 4), and if he misses both he ends up in the next state over (payoff 1). The night before, at his kitchen table, he worked out the optimal plan on a napkin: at any intersection, continue with probability 2/3 — expected payoff 4/3, which beats the 1 he'd get by never exiting at all. Now he's actually sitting at an intersection, no memory, and he's torn between two voices. Write the dialogue between them: the kitchen-table planner, who says stick to the napkin, roll the die exactly as planned, no second-guessing; and the in-the-moment driver, who argues that being at an intersection right now is evidence he can use — he can ask which intersection this probably is, and conditional on that, the plan's die-roll may not be what he'd choose now. Make both sides genuinly sharp: the planner should press on why present-tense reasoning can't be trusted here, and the driver should press back on whether a plan is still rational once you're standing in the middle of it. Then let one side actually win on the merits — no cop-out truce.
