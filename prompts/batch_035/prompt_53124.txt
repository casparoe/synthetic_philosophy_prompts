I help run a discussion group on personal identity that's been meeting for about two years, and the gradual replacement debate has completely frozen over. Quick background so we're on the same page: the scenario is that your neurons get swapped out one at a time for functionally identical silicon, so at every stage you wake up in the morning, make coffee, insist you're fine. The pattern camp says obviously you survive — only causal/functional organization matters, and the swap preserves it. The biological camp says the thing that survives is the living process itself and an upload is a copy, no matter how gradual the transition is. Chalmers-style fading qualia arguments get deployed, Parfit's "identity isn't what matters anyway" move gets deployed, people bring up the "for all you know you're already partially replaced by cochlear implants and pacemakers" point, and nobody budges an inch.

Here's what bugs me though. As far as I can tell both sides agree on literally every observable. The completed upload will say it's you, remember your childhood dog, be terrified of "dying" a second time, and pass any test anyone can devise. Both sides predict exactly that. Both sides also agree the half-replaced brain behaves normally mid-process. And the fading qualia argument cuts both ways — if consciousness faded during replacement, the reports would stay fixed by the silicon function anyway, so the biological camp can't even claim they'd expect the person to notice anything going wrong. So there's no observation either side expects to differ on, and yet people genuinely convert in both directions, and one guy nearly quit the group over the single-remaining-neuron case.

My working hypothesis is that this is mostly a verbal dispute about the word "survives": the patternists mean something like "the pattern continues with the right causal history," the biological people mean some further fact they can't cash out observationally, and once you disambiguate, the fight dissolves. But I'm honestly not sure, because the biological people insist they're not doing word games — they claim there's a fact about what it's like to be the original versus the copy that no report from either can settle, since the reports are held fixed by the functional organization in both cases. To complicate things further, one member has lately been arguing we should reframe the whole debate around second-person stuff — whether the upload's spouse should keep sharing the bank account, whether the wedding vows still bind it — and that the metaphysics is downstream of what we'd do, not the other way around. The patternists say that's dodging the question.

So diagnose the structure of this for me. Is it (a) verbal, (b) substantive but empirically open in principle, (c) substantive but nonempirical, or (d) two different questions — identity conditions on the one hand, and what we should care about on the other — travelling under one heading? Ground the diagnosis in what the sides actually say: the fading/dancing qualia reasoning, the Parfitian move that identity per se was never what mattered, the "you're already partly artificial" appeal, the organic-continuity intuition. If it's verbal, say what the disambiguation looks like and whether the biological camp is right that something gets lost in it. And then tell me what would count as progress on your diagnosis — a distinction, a case both camps would have to agree on, some kind of coherence or impossibility result, or nothing — and why.

Also: tell me straight where my verbal-dispute hypothesis is weakest, if it is weak, and what consideration would flip you to a different diagnosis. I want to know the point in your own reasoning most likely to fall apart, not a confident survey of the positions.
