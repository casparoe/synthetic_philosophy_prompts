I'm preparing a short position piece on supertasks for a philosophy of physics reading group and would like a consistency check before I circulate it. My commitments, as I currently hold them, are:

(1) Thomson's lamp is not a paradox but an underdetermination problem: the specification of infinitely many switchings before the two-minute mark, together with the facts about the lamp at every moment before the mark, fails to determine whether the lamp is on or off at the mark. In the spirit of Benacerraf, the limit state is a further fact not settled by the sequence of states.

(2) The lamp scenario, so described, is metaphysically possible: there is no contradiction in a lamp's occupying exactly that sequence of states, and what prevents its realization is contingent physics rather than logic.

(3) The physical world is finite: only finitely many objects exist and only finitely many events ever occur; actual infinities are never physically instantiated.

(4) Continuous motion does not conflict with (3). A body's passing through continuum-many positions in a finite interval is one task with continuum-many parts, not an infinity of discrete tasks that must be completed one after another, and so it is not an actual infinity of events in the sense (3) forbids.

(5) By contrast, an ordinary dropped basketball with coefficient of restitution less than one undergoes infinitely many distinct bounces in a finite time before coming to rest, and this requires no idealization beyond perfectly standard mechanics — a mundane supertask occurring in our world.

(6) The world is deterministic in the following sense: the complete physical state at all times up to, but not including, some time t, together with the laws, determines the state at t.

Please audit this set. Which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what exactly would have to give in each case? Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most at the least cost. I honestly do not know where the worst trouble lies, so please do not pull your punches on any of the six.
