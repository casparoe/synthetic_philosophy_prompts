A historian friend of mine keeps firm score on which historical episodes deserve the word "collapse" and which were merely wrenching change. Her track record so far: the Western Roman Empire in the fifth century — collapse; the ninth-century abandonment of the southern Maya lowlands — collapse; the eastern Mediterranean around 1200 BC — collapse; the Black Death in fourteenth-century Europe — not a collapse, whatever the death toll; the handloom weavers ruined by mechanization — not a collapse; the Meiji dismantling of the samurai order — not a collapse. Three new cases are coming up. First, Easter Island before European contact: deforestation ended monumental moai production, but a smaller Rapanui society endured and adapted. Second, Angkor in the fifteenth century: the enormous hydraulic city was emptied, yet its population redistributed into smaller settlements and the Khmer kingdom survived at reduced scale. Third, a hypothetical: a wealthy society in which fertility falls so far that the population halves over a century while living standards, literacy, and institutional continuity hold steady. Predict her verdict on each of the three cases; lay out at least two rules that fit her track record equally well but come apart on these; tell me which rule you think she is actually running, and why; and propose the further case that would best discriminate between the candidate rules.
