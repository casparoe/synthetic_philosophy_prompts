I have no academic background, though I've spent the last twenty-five years as a working musician: self-taught, no conservatory, trained instead by bandmates, fake books, transcribing records, and a few thousand gigs. Lately I've been thinking on my own about something I heard in a lecture series: Schopenhauer's idea that music is fundamentally unlike the other arts. As I understand him, painting and poetry represent the world at one remove — they show us images of particular things or of the general forms things take — while music skips that step entirely and is supposed to be a direct portrait of the will itself, the underlying striving that he thinks everything is. He also makes more specific claims along these lines: that melody tells the story of a striving that meets obstacles, is deferred, sometimes satisfied, and that satisfaction is never final; that the bass and the inner harmonic structure correspond to the lower, less conscious levels of that striving; that dissonance is unfulfilled wanting and resolution is fulfillment; and that this is why music moves people of every culture and era in a way painting and poetry, which require literacy in their subject matter, do not — music, on his view, isn't expressing particular emotions so much as expressing the basic grammar of wanting and getting that all emotions share.

I'd like you to restate this position in the vocabulary I actually use on the bandstand, as faithfully as you can, claim by claim. That vocabulary includes: chord function (tonic as home, dominant as the chord that demands motion, pre-dominants as the road between them), tendency tones and how they resolve, voice leading, the tritone and its resolution, suspension and appoggiatura, cadences and their relative strength, modulation and relative keys, bass movement, register and density. Say which of his claims map onto something real in my vocabulary, which map only after being bent or shrunk to fit, and which have no expression in it at all. Then give me your honest view on the residue. My suspicion, from the cheap seats, is that everything my vocabulary can carry reduces to a claim about tension and resolution within a musical style — and that styles differ enough that the "universal grammar of wanting" part, which is the whole point for Schopenhauer, is exactly what my vocabulary cannot say without turning into either psychoacoustics or a metaphor. So tell me: is that because my vocabulary is missing something, or because his claim was never as clear as it sounded? And if there is a version of his claim that survives, state it in a way that would still distinguish it from the boring observation that dissonance sounds restless and resolution sounds like relief.
