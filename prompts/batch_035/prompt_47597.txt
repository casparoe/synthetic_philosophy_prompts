I've pretty much bought Quine's line in Two Dogmas that the analytic-synthetic distinction is a dogma, and confirmational holism along with it. But lately something bugs me: the argument seems to come down to saying any statement can be held true come what may if we adjust elsewhere in the web, and no statement is immune to revision. Carnap-style people seem happy to concede exactly that -- they just say the revising happens at the level of choosing a linguistic framework, and within a framework the analytic truths are stipulated and perfectly well defined. So maybe holism doesn't refute the distinction, it just relocates it from sentences to frameworks, and Quine never actually answered that move. What would genuinely count against my Quinean view here? I'd want to know what an explication of analyticity would have to look like to be a real counterexample (does it need to be totally non-circular, and is that even a fair demand for a fundamental term -- we don't demand non-circular definitions of "object" or "cause"?), whether the standard counterattacks that Quine must rely on some notion of meaning himself in order to run his argument have any bite, and whether the view has been qualified into something that can't lose. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me which ones a committed Quinean would just shrug off.
