Here is a version I worked out myself. Suppose a machine stamps out metal cubes whose edge lengths all lie between 1 and 2 centimetres, and you pick one cube at random from its output. What is the chance its edge is under 1.5 centimetres? If you assume every edge length in the range is equally likely, you get one half. But if you assume every volume between 1 and 8 cubic centimetres is equally likely, you get roughly a third, since the edges below 1.5 correspond to the volumes below 3.375, which fill less than a third of the volume range. And nothing stops you there: take surface area, or the mass, or the logarithm of the edge, and you get a different answer each time. What struck me is that the second assumption doesn't feel like an error you can catch the way you'd catch an arithmetic slip.
