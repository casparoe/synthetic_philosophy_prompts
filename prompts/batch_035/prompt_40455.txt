After a long argument with a friend about dispositions, finks, and masks, I have her track record settled and want help preparing for tomorrow's round. Her verdicts so far: the glass watched over by Martin's sorcerer, who would make it sturdy the instant before any blow, is still fragile; the poison is still poisonous even though the drinker's blood already contains an antidote; the sturdy glass is not fragile even though a wizard would make it fragile just before any strike; and the glass whose own surface has been fused into an unbreakable glaze is not fragile. The new cases we will fight about: (1) a pane that is intrinsically sturdy but sits in a frame with a sensor that detonates a small charge, shattering the pane whenever anything brushes it; (2) a venom that was synthesized together with a binding agent chemically inseparable from the toxin, so that the antidote is part of the very substance; (3) the glazed glass again, but now with a sorcerer who would strip the glaze the instant before any blow. Predict her verdicts on all three. More importantly, tell me which rules fit her existing verdicts equally well — for example, an intrinsic-basis view with a clause about where the defeater is located, or a conditional analysis with exception clauses for finks, mimickers, and external masks — say which of the three new cases does the most to discriminate between those rules, and describe a further case that would settle the matter between them.
