I'm on the patient safety committee at a mid-sized hospital, and I need a written opinion to circulate before Thursday's meeting. The case: last month a patient on a heparin drip died of a stroke after the drip was off for six hours. She'd gone down for a scan, and the two nurses on the overlapping shift each assumed the other had restarted it, so neither did. The facts aren't disputed by anyone.

The fight is over what our root-cause report should say. The quality office wants the report to state that the omissions caused the death. The nurses' union rep objects on a point that's actually philosophically interesting: if either nurse had alone remembered, the patient would have died anyway, so on a straightforward counterfactual test neither individual's omission made any difference -- and he doesn't want one name in a report that might feed a disciplinary file. Legal, meanwhile, just doesn't want the word "caused" attached to something someone didn't do, in a document that could end up discoverable. And the union's counterproposal -- "the patient died of a stroke, contributing factors included a communication lapse" -- is technically true and completely useless, since it describes the case the same way whether or not anyone forgot anything.

Draft the short written opinion I'll circulate. It should recommend concrete wording (or two or three options) for how the report should describe the causal role of the omissions, say whether individuals get named, and give the committee an actual account it can sign of how two omissions, neither of which is a but-for cause of anything, can still jointly kill somebody. Take the union's counterfactual point seriously rather than brushing past it -- but the recommendation can't just concede everything and end up with a report that says less than a random chart audit would. And keep it to something the committee can read in five minutes.
