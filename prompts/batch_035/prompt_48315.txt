As someone who has watched the Lucas–Penrose arguments get re-litigated on rationalist forums for over a decade, I've noticed the exchange always ends the same way: someone invokes Gödel, someone replies that the machine could be inconsistent, and the thread dies with both sides exactly where they started. I'd like a parody that dramatizes that impasse rather than declaring a winner.

Write a customer-support FAQ, roughly 650 words, in numbered form, for a fictional AI company whose chatbot is being besieged by a persistent user — call him the Correspondent — who demands a refund on Lucasian grounds: he can see the truth of the chatbot's Gödel sentence, the chatbot cannot, so the product was sold under the description "understanding" and does not have it. The company's answers should be deadpan corporate-legalese, but each one must quietly get the actual dialectic right: the theorem applies only to systems that are consistent or sound, effectively axiomatized, and strong enough for arithmetic, so the FAQ's central running joke should be that every answer forces the Correspondent to claim all three of himself in writing, and the company holds each claim up to the light — that he cannot survey his own axioms, that he has no procedure for distinguishing which of his beliefs are axiom-equivalents and which are derived, and that "seeing at a glance" the Gödel sentence of a system as complex as anything brainlike is precisely the point in dispute. Include one entry for the Penrose variant, the one about non-halting computations, but keep it from ballooning. And include at least one answer where the corporate confidence slips — say, a note that the standard reply, that we can't prove our own consistency either, cuts both ways, and that legal has advised against putting that in writing.

The humor has to come from the logic, not from the Correspondent being a crank. A philosopher of mind who thinks Lucas was wrong should find the piece fair; one who thinks Lucas was right should wince in recognition rather than at a strawman.
