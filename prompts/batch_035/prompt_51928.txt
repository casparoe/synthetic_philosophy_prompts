I've been noticing something about how internet memes actually spread vs how the Darwinian theory of culture says they should, and I can't figure out if the memetics people ever gave it a name.

So the setup: a meme starts out as an elaborate artifact. A genuinely well-drawn comic, a skillfully edited video, a joke with a really specific setup. And what ends up taking over is never that version. The versions that win are the degraded ones — stick-figure redraws, crooked phone pics of the original, the variant where you can barely tell what the joke was. At first I assumed the slick version was just losing to funnier competition, but that's not what's happening. The crude version wins *because* it's crude. Nobody feels intimidated remixing something that looks like it took ten minutes in MS Paint. The polished original dies partly because it looks finished, like there's nothing left to add, while the ugly copy is basically an invitation to make an even uglier copy. The trait being selected for isn't appeal, it's something like "easy to reproduce badly" — the copy is deliberately lossy and the lossiness is the advantage.

Here's why this bugs me. I remember Dawkins talked about successful replicators in terms of longevity, fecundity, and copying fidelity. And in genetics that framing makes sense — mutations are mostly errors, and the replication machinery exists to suppress them. But what I'm describing is almost the mirror image: transmission is lossy on purpose, the degradation is functional, and fidelity looks like it's actively selected *against* past a certain point. It's also not just "selection for transmissibility" in the generic sense, I don't think — a polished, gorgeous version of a meme can be extremely transmissible (people share it constantly) while still failing to *propagate* in the sense of spawning variants. Sharing and copying-with-variation come apart, and the second seems to be what actually drives the long-term lineage.

So my questions. Does this exact thing — selection for reproduciability-by-untalented-copiers, where degradation is adaptive — have an established name in the memes-and-Darwinian-culture literature or in the more academic cultural evolution stuff? If it's been named, define it for me and tell me what the boundaries are. If it's only been partially covered by existing concepts (like general selection for transmissibility, or whatever the nearest analogs are in cultural evolution theory), tell me where those concepts stop and this thing keeps going. And if there's genuinely no term, coin one: give a definition precise enough that I could tell whether a case falls inside it, and then probe the edges. Like — does it apply to cover songs that simplify the original? Folk tales getting sanded down over generations of retelling? Recipes? Or is there something specific about internet meme culture (everyone can produce and publish, not just consume) that makes this a genuinely new regime rather than an old phenomenon at internet speed?

I don't want just the label, I want the carving: what exactly the phenomenon is, what it's not, and where the line falls. A name with no definition attached is worse than useless here.
