Write roughly 600 words of mock meeting minutes for a fictional body — the National Bureau of Standards' "Ad Hoc Committee on the Elimination of Vagueness" — which parliament has tasked with stamping out vagueness in public life by setting exact cutoffs: the precise number of grains that makes a heap, the number of hairs whose loss makes a man bald, the exact centimeter at which "tall" begins for purposes of the school-bus discount. The comedy should come from the actual dialectic around the sorites, not from zany bureaucracy: every cutoff the committee fixes is separated from the ones next to it by a boundary that looks suspiciously vague itself, so they keep appointing subcommittees to draw the boundaries of the boundaries, and the minutes should record this escalating regress in perfect deadpan. At some point a member should propose the supervaluationist workaround (declare something a heap if it's a heap on every precisification the committee has issued so far), and by the end someone should move the epistemicist resolution — the original cutoff was always perfectly sharp, the committee's job was only ever to discover it, and nothing in principle stops them from failing forever — which should pass with visible relief. I'd also like one lone dissentr who keeps insisting the predicates were working fine before the committee showed up, and gets told his objections will be minuted to an appendix no one reads. Format it as numbered resolutions followed by a short "Outstanding Actions" section listing the subcommittees, which should itself quietly exhibit the problem. A philosopher working on vagueness should be able to spot epistemicism, supervaluationism and contextualism being sent up (and sent up fairly), but a lay reader should just find it funny without knowing any of that.
