I'm in a reading group that's been doing values this term, and I've been asked to write up my own position on hard choices so the others can tear into it. Before I send it around I want to know if it even hangs together. Here's the set:

(1) Incommensurability is real and objective. Some options just can't be ranked by better-than — not because we lack information, and they're not equal either. They're on a par. Like comparing a career as a lawyer and a career as a poet, where no amount of one good adds up to the other.

(2) When options are on a par, either choice can be fully rational, and you can make it rational by committing — by putting yourself behind one option so that you now have most reason to go with it. That's how hard choices get solved rather than merely endured.

(3) Practical reason is receptive, not creative. Reasons come from the world and from things like loving, promising, caring — you can't conjure a reason into existence just by deciding. Nothing gets to count as a reason because you chose it.

(4) After you've chosen between on-a-par options, feeling regret is irrational, strictly. There was no better option, so there's nothing you failed to get. Regret would only make sense if you'd actually passed up something better.

(5) Even in hard choices, flipping a coin is a cop-out. If both options are choiceworthy you should commit to one, not randomize.

(6) Old habit from my econ days, but I still believe it: the choices of a fully infromed, consistent chooser can always be represented as maximizing a complete preference ordering, and that representation isn't just bookkeeping — it captures something real about what choice reveals about preference.

Go through these and tell me which ones actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how bad each real tension is. Rank them rather than listing everything flat, and for each one tell me what would have to give and what giving it would cost me elsewhere. If there's a single revision that would clean up most of the mess, name that one. I honestly can't tell whether some of these are in genuine tension or whether I'm just using the same words (reason, rational) in different senses and confusing myself.
