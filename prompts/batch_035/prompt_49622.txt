My husband and I have spent the past several weeks debating whether to have a third child, and the discussion has drifted from the practical (space, money, sleep) into territory neither of us can quite map. I'd like help thinking it through, because I've come to suspect the disagreement isn't really about our family at all.

His case rests on a kind of ledger. Our two children are, by any reasonable standard, happy, and their lives are worth living. If we add a third — wanted, and as well cared for as the first two — the world will contain more good in it than it otherwise would: three flourishing lives where there would have been two. On his view, declining to have her, when we could easily raise her well, leaves the world with a deficit it would not otherwise have, and redirecting our spare resources toward children in need doesn't cancel that, since those children would exist, and be needy, either way. My case rests on an asymmetry I can feel but cannot defend rigorously: it seems to me that no one is made better off by a child's being created — there is no one waiting for the chance to exist, and had she never been conceived, there would have been no one to miss the life she never got. Improving the life of a child who already exists helps someone, in a way that creating a new and happy child does not, even though I cannot point to anyone whom the creation helps or harms.

When I floated this at a dinner with friends, one of them pressed my husband's ledger to what I found a troubling conclusion: if every additional moderately happy life adds good to the world, then couples like us owe the world as many children as we can decently raise, and — more unsettling — a very large number of lives barely worth living would together outweigh a smaller number of wonderful ones. She suggested my asymmetry was the only thing standing between us and that result, and I could not tell whether she was right. So my questions are these. First, is there a principled way to hold that creating a happy life differs in kind from improving an unhappy one — different enough that it is not simply one more entry on the same ledger, to be traded against entries of the same sign — and can that distinction be stated so that it does not collapse under the pressure my friend applied, and without falling back on the claim that additional lives eventually make things worse? Second, what is the strongest version of my husband's view, so that I am not refuting a strawman: if the ledger is right, what exactly follows for a couple in our position, and where, if anywhere, does it stop?
