So I'm putting together my position statement for a grad seminar on epistemology after Wittgenstein, and I want a sanity check on whether what I believe actually hangs together before I present it to people who will definitely poke holes. Here's roughly where I've landed. (1) I think the core insight of On Certainty is right: some propositions -- "I have two hands", "the world existed long before I was born", "my name is such-and-such" -- aren't beliefs I hold on the basis of evidence but hinges that the whole practice of giving and asking for reasons turns on. They're not conclusions; they're the scaffolding. (2) Because of that, I deny that we *know* our hinges -- knowledge seems to require grounds, and a hinge is precisely something not grounded, so it's not that I'm confident I know them, it's that the question of knowing them can't get a grip. (3) I'm nonetheless a fallibilist about everything in the sense that nothing is immune to revision in principle, and the history of science backs this up: "the Earth sits at the center of the cosmos" or "heavy things fall faster than light things" plausibly functioned as hinges for whole communities before they got dislodged. (4) I take hinge certainty to be a kind of primitive, animal trust, closer to how a child relies on its parents than to any judgment I make. (5) I firmly reject epistemic relativism: our current hinge-set is not on all fours with, say, a conspiracy theorist's, whose hinges include something like "the official story is always a cover-up" -- I want to say our framework is epistemically *better*, not just different. And (6) I think Moore was actually onto something with the hand proof: saying "here is a hand" is a perfectly legitimate move against the skeptic, even though, per (2), I deny we know the hinge propositions Moore was leaning on.

Please act as an expert on post-Wittgensteinian epistemology and really think this through before answering -- maybe brainstorm first and sort the pairs before you commit to a verdict. What I want is an audit, not a survey of the literature or a reading list (I have no use for citations here, just the arguments). Which of these six commitments are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest fix for each real one? A few things I'd especially like you to address: whether the fallibilism in (3) can survive alongside the idea that doubting a hinge isn't even intelligible *from within* a practice, and whether (5) is compatible with (1) and (2) -- if hinges are exempt from epistemic assessment because they don't answer to grounds, what exactly is left over that makes ours better than the conspiracist's, and does whatever it is smuggle hinges back inside the space of justification? Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally fatal, and at the end tell me which single revision would clean up the most, since if I have to give something up I'd rather it be one thing than a piecemeal retreat.
