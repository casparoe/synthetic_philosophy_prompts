I'm giving a talk next week at our campus AI-safety reading group (about 15 people, mixed backgrounds) and I want a Q&A prep sheet. My thesis, roughly: in present-day reinforcement learning the reward signal is not itself a valenced state — it's a scalar that nudges weights, closer to a grader's mark on an exam than to the experience of pain — so the standard question "can RL agents suffer?" gets asked at the wrong place. The reward number doesn't feel like anything; if suffering ever shows up in these systems it would have to be carried by something else, like a persistent internal state that gets repeatedly frustrated while the system keeps trying, and I want to claim that current systems lack anything with the right structure for that. I hold this less confidently for setups where an agent maintains a rich predictive world-model and is trained under objectives that conflict, since there the "frustrated expectation" story at least gets a grip.

The audience: roughly six RL engineers (including one who worked on robotics control and thinks anthropomorphizing the loss function is the main intellectual sin in this debate), two philosophers of mind who are committed functionalists and will say that "it's just a number" begs the whole question, one person whose home turf is animal welfare ethics and will push on moral uncertainty, and the rest are grad students who mostly go along with whoever spoke last. The engineers will want me to treat reward like a teaching signal and be done with it; the functionalists are the ones I'm actually worried about, because their line is something like: if the persistent frustrated state does all the causal work you attribute to disvalence, why isn't that just valence under another name — and if you admit current systems have it "partially," your criterion starts condemning systems you clearly don't want to condemn.

So: draft the prep sheet. List the objections in roughly the order they'll actually come, phrased the way each subgroup would phrase them (the engineer version and the functionalist version of the same worry are different questions and I need both). For each one give me a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft. But please don't hand me a menu of options with "it depends on your meta-view" — commit: tell me which single objection does the most damage, what I should concede up front to buy credibility, which concessions are cheap versus which ones cost me the thesis, and the one reply that has to land for the talk to survive. And include the question I'm hoping nobody asks — I have a feeling it's the one about whether my "persistent frustrated state" criterion, applied consistently, also starts to cover things like a spam filter or a thermostat that keeps failing, and I'd rather see it coming than meet it cold. If you think my framing itself has a flaw — e.g. that "the reward signal isn't valenced" is a distinction that does less work than I think — say so directly rather than building the prep sheet around a broken foundation.
