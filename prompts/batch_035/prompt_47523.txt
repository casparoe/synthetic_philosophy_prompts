quick context: i'm a physics grad student, and a journal club meeting on the 2015 loophole-free Bell tests turned into an argument that has now spread to our group chat and refuses to die. the topic is what Bell's theorem actually rules out. one side — basically the position you find in most textbooks and pop-science accounts — says the experiments killed "local realism," so we get to choose: keep locality and give up realism, or keep realism and give up locality, dealer's choice. the other side, which i gather is closer to Bell himself and to philosophers like Tim Maudlin, says "realism" was never defined, doesn't appear as an assumption anywhere in the derivation, and the theorem just refutes locality — the correlations violate local causality, period, and "realism" is a placeholder physicists use for "whatever else besides locality you'd have to drop," which isn't an assumption you can inspect but a leftover. there's also a middle move i only half understand, due to Jarrett i think: split the factorization condition into parameter independence and outcome independence, claim the data only forces you to give up outcome independence, and note that parameter independence is the part that no-signalling needs anyway. the friend defending the textbook line keeps asking "if realism isn't an assumption, what exactly do you think you're giving up when you keep locality?" and the friend on the Maudlin side keeps answering "the question is malformed," and this has been going in circles for a month.

i want a diagnosis of the disagreement rather than a verdict on who wins. my working hypothesis — offered as a hypothesis, i could be totally wrong — is that it's partly verbal: "give up realism" functions as a description of the remaining option space (you need *something* non-local-ish, so call the missing thing realism), not a naming of an independent axiom, and if so the two camps might literally agree about every counterfactual claim and still fight about the sentence. but i suspect it's not *only* verbal — the Jarrett split suggests there's a real formal question hiding in there about which independence condition does the work, and maybe also a difference in what the two sides count as an "assumption" at all (something written into the derivation vs. something you need to believe to find the derivation's conclusion livable). so: is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or two groups answering different questions — one about what the mathematics assumes, one about what a reasonable person should conclude — under one heading? and given the diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here: a sharpened definition of local causality, a theorem, an experiment, or just an agreement to stop using the word "realism"? for what it's worth, "maybe the experiments are wrong" is off the table for us — we all accept the loophole-free results — so please don't spend space on that.

one epistemic request: please keep a clear line between what's settled among people who actually know this literature (the math of the inequality, the experimental violations, the closure of the detection and locality loopholes, Bell's own stated view) and what is genuinely contested (whether the outcome/parameter independence split carves nature at a joint or is just bookkeeping, whether "local realism" is a meaningful category at all, what follows for quantum foundations). don't present one camp's reading as the consensus when the other camp is made of specialists too — the whole reason our group chat is stuck is that everyone keeps citing their side's review article as if it were the field's last word.
