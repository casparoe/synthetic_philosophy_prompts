And here is the student's answer, reproduced verbatim from the script, slips and all; I have corrected nothing.

Nozick's tracking analysis, set out in Philosophical Explanations (1981), says that S knows that p when four conditions hold: p is true; S believes that p; if p were false, S would not believe that p (sensitivity); and if p were true, S would believe that p (adherence). The conditions are relative to a method: it is via the method by which S actually arrived at the belief that S must track p.

The analysis denies closure because sensitivity is not preserved under entailment. Take my case. Tomas passes the open door of the shared office and sees his colleague Priya at her desk, and so comes to believe p: Priya is at her desk. He also knows that p entails q: that the person at the desk is not Priya's identical twin Ravi, who is visiting the department that week and is famous for pranks. Does Tomas know p? In the closest worlds where p is false, Priya has stepped out to the kitchen and her chair is empty, so he would not believe p. Moreover, his belief is sensitive in the stronger sense required: in every nearby world in which he believes p, p is true. So he knows p. Does he know q? In the closest worlds where q is false, Ravi is sitting at the desk in Priya's cardigan, and Tomas would believe just the same as he actually does; so his belief in q is not sensitive, and he does not know q. Note that he does not thereby lose p: knowledge fails to be transmitted down an entailment, but it is not destroyed at the source.

Nor is that the end of the downward slide. The failure of closure runs down as well as up: because Tomas cannot rule out Ravi, he cannot so much as know the weaker disjunction, that either Priya is at her desk or Ravi is impersonating her, since to know that he would need to know which.

On balance, the denial of closure is a virtue of the analysis. It lets the analysis answer scepticism without giving up ordinary knowledge: we know that we have hands, we do not know that we are not handless beings envatted, and the analysis never saddles us with the awkward conjunctions that Closure would otherwise force upon us. The cost is that we must accept some things we do not know, but that is a small price next to losing everything.
