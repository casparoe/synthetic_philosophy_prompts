Quick background so you know where I'm coming from: I'm a retired physicist — thirty-some years running molecular dynamics and statistical mechanics codes, polymer melts early on, protein folding later — and since retiring I've gotten sucked into philosophy of mind, which is how I ended up reading Hofstadter's I Am a Strange Loop. He's got this analogy he calls the careenium: a cranium-sized pool table packed with little magnetic balls that he calls simms, careening around and clumping into larger, more stable formations he calls simmballs, and the walls of the table change their bounciness depending on whats happening in the world outside, so patterns of simmballs end up mirroring that world. Simms are the neural substrate, simmballs are the symbols or concepts, and the self is supposed to be a special self-referential simmball that represents the whole system — a strange loop. Fair enough as an analogy, but the part where he asks who pushes who around, and answers that the simmball level genuinely does its share of the pushing, is where my eyebrows go up.

Here's the thing, from my working life: whenever I needed a macroscopic quantity to do something in a simulation — say, hold a nanoparticle melt at a fixed temperature — the macro level never actually did anything. I just wrote a friction term and a stochastic noise term into each atom's equation of motion individually, and the "temperature" was a bookkeeping device for statistical features of those micro terms. Coarse-grained models, renormalization group flows, thermostats — every case of apparent downward causation I ever touched was like that: the higher level shows up in the dynamics only because a human wrote it into the micro terms by hand. So when people at my philosophy book club say symbols push the substrate around, I keep comming back to the same thought: show me the force term. Either you have cases where the higher level writes itself into the micro dynamics, or "downward causation" is just a compliment we pay to certain correlations.

I'd like to actually hear the best version of the other side before I dismiss it over coffee with these folks. Please play a committed defender of the strange-loop view — someone who thinks the simmball level is causally autonomous, not just a convenient summary of simm collisions, and who takes the feedback-loop picture seriously as an answer to epiphenomenalism. Be sharp and don't strawman yourself; you can concede that the simms fully determine the dynamics if you like, so long as you hold the line that the symbol level does causal work in its own right. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to a paragraph or two so it feels like a real back-and-forth. Here's my opening move: in every physical system I've ever modeled, the higher level is epiphenomenal on paper and only becomes "causal" when an outside engineer injects it into the micro dynamics. Your simmballs, as far as I can tell, have no engineer — the walls are the only channel to the outside world, and after that it's just magnetism and collisions all the way down. So what exactly is left for the simmballs to push?
