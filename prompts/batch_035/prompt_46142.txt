I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized social platform and somehow that means I also sit on the escalation review board, the group that rules on the edge cases the classifiers and front-line mods won't touch. Our written policy leans hard on Mill — the doc basically says we restrict speech for harm to others, not for offense — so every week we end up doing little harm principle rulings whether we like it or not.

Here's our track record from the past year, roughly:

1. Guy posts a local election official's home address plus "someone should pay her a visit" — removed.
2. A detailed thermite recipe, no target, no threats — allowed.
3. Strobe GIF dropped into a streamer's chat by a user who knew she had epilepsy — removed.
4. Execution videos shared with no commentary — allowed. We are not the feelings police.
5. Coordinated fake one-star reviews claiming a bakery gave people food poisoning — removed.
6. A seller of some bleach-based "cure" posting in a cancer patients' forum — removed.
7. We kept up a blasphemous cartoon even after its poster recieved death threats; we banned the threateners instead.

Now this week's docket, and this is where I'd like your read:

A. A community trading fasting and "thinspo" tips, aimed at, tbh, mostly teenage girls. No named victims, nobody is told to do anything, and no strictly false factual claims — just tips and encouragement plus the occasional lecture about discipline. Front-line mods keep escalating it, we keep punting.

B. A user runs a bot that archives and reposts, with snarky commentary, every deleted tweet from a certain politician. All true content, public figure, no threats. One catch: among the deleted tweets was one where she doxxed her own home address, which she deleted after getting threats, and the bot reposts that one too. A journalist has already cited the archive twice.

C. Someone posted a neutrally worded list of the home addresses of every judge in one state, compiled entirely from public records, right after a controversial ruling. No threats, no commentary at all, just the list.

For each of A, B, and C: what should we rule if we want to be consistant with ourselves? What rule are we actually running — I have my own guess, something like "identifiable victim plus foreseeable harm," but the cancer-forum case bugs me, and idk, there seem to be several rules that fit our record equally well and they don't agree on these three. Tell me which precedent is the closest analog for each, and where our record genuinely underdetermines the answer, say what principle we'd be adopting with each choice (including what it commits us to on the cases we've already decided). And if you can design a case that would split the candidate rules cleanly, give me that too — I want to bring it to the next review board meeting and watch what happens.
