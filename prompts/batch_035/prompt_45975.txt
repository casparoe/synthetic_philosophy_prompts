My department runs a proseminar in philosophy of medicine, and for the past two years two of our faculty have been arguing the naturalism-versus-normativism dispute about health and disease past each other. One holds a broadly Boorsean line: disease is statistically subnormal biological functioning relative to an age-and-sex reference class, and while judgments of badness or disvalue are real, they belong to a separate, normative layer and are no part of what disease itself is. The other holds that our disease concepts are through-and-through evaluative, that what we call pathological partly encodes what we have reason to avoid or remedy, and that the naturalist's "dysfunction" is either covertly normative or useless for explaining what actually drives our classifications. I have been assigned to write up their standoff, and rather than another literature review I want the document a genuine adversarial collaboration between the two of them would produce.

Draft that joint statement. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both parties can sign, including claims about the shared cases — the removal of homosexuality from the DSM in 1973, the DSM-5's elimination of the bereavement exclusion for major depressive episodes, the addition of gaming disorder to the ICD-11, and the status of asymptomatic conditions like undetected atherosclerosis or the sickle-cell trait in malaria-endemic regions; (2) the precise formulations of their disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording — in particular, they should state exactly where they stand on the cases above, since I suspect each case pulls them in different directions; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one of them — and, for each crux, a concrete case, thought experiment, or empirical result both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about that case written down in advance. I myself suspect one crux is whether the naturalist's reference class and "species-typical design" can be specified without any evaluative choices smuggled in, but I want your independent diagnosis, and I also want you to flag any part of their dispute that turns out to be merely verbal.

Please be disciplined about symmetry: neither side's formulations should be slanted, and neither should be strawmanned into something neither actually holds. If the naturalist's position needs qualification (say, to handle the 1973 case), say what the qualification costs them rather than papering over it, and likewise for the normativist. Structure the document with the numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table, then the cruxes with recorded predictions, then a short section on what, if anything, both agree would actually settle the dispute. End with a final line of exactly this form: "Deepest crux: <one sentence>."
