I'm a data analyst by day and I've gotten obsessed with a question that keeps coming up in my work and I can't find the right vocabulary for it. When you compress a noisy time series, at some coarse granularity the noise disappears and a clean pattern shows up -- but at a finer granularity the "pattern" is just one arbitrary wiggly line among many, and you could have smoothed it a dozen different ways. It feels weird to say the pattern isn't real: it predicts things, other analysts independently find it, you can be wrong about it. But it also feels weird to say it's real the way an electron is real, because its identity seems to depend on which questions you're asking and how much error you're willing to tolerate. The same thing shows up everywhere once you look: a "recession" isn't any particular set of transactions, a "fork" in chess is invisible at the level of pixels on the board but is the single most important fact for a player, and a handful of stray pixels doesn't stop a low-res image from being a picture of a face. In each case there's something that is genuinely out there -- it supports prediction and you can miss it or hallucinate it -- but only relative to a grain of description and a tolerance for error, and different observers with different predictive needs might carve the same underlying stuff into different such things without either of them being wrong.

I've tried to map this onto concepts I know about and none of them quite fit. "Emergence" usually suggests higher-level things with causal powers of their own, but here I'm not claiming the pattern does anything over and above the pixels or transactions, just that it's an objectively better or worse compression of them. "Social construction" is off too, since a face in a noisy bitmap isn't constructed by us agreeing on it -- it would still be the best compressible description even if no human cared. And "supervenience" is arguably true of these things but doesn't capture the part I find interesting, namely that whether a given lump of low-level stuff counts as *one* instance of the pattern or as noise depends on the tracker's error budget, so to speak. So my question: is there an established concept or literature in philosophy for entities or patterns that are real but only relative to a grain of description and a practical standard of tolerance? If there's a name for this exact thing, I want to know what it is and how it's standardly spelled out. If the nearest concepts each capture only part of it, I want to know which parts each one captures and where each one runs out.

One request on how you answer: please keep straight what's actually settled among philosophers who work on this stuff versus what's genuinely contested -- e.g. if there's a classic paper that everyone treats as the locus classicus but its interpretation is fought over, or if some people think these patterns are fully real and others think they're a useful fiction, I'd rather hear that disagreement laid out plainly than get one side presented as the consensus. I've been burned before by confidently stated one-liners from pop philosophy books that turned out to be one guy's contested gloss on a much messier debate. And if the honest answer is that there's no clean established term and I'd have to coin one, that's fine too -- just give me a careful definition and tell me where its edge cases are, so I can tell whether my noisy-time-series case and my chess case are even instances of the same thing.
