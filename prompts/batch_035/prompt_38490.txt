After forty years in condensed-matter physics, I have spent the past three years reading philosophy of science seriously, and I would now like to write something of my own. I am planning a paper of roughly 8,000 words to develop over six months and submit to a workshop on scientific realism, and I need a proposal I can work from. My background is mathematical but not formal-logical; I have never written for a philosophical audience.

My half-formed idea comes from something physicists do constantly but, as far as I can tell, philosophers of science treat only in passing. We never simply say a superseded theory was false; we say it is the limiting case of its successor — Newtonian mechanics as the low-velocity limit of special relativity, the classical ideal gas as the limit where quantum effects and finite particle number vanish, the ray-optics limit of wave optics. These relations carry a built-in measure of error: a parameter (v/c, the ratio of thermal de Broglie wavelength to interparticle spacing) whose smallness bounds the discrepancy, so "close to the truth" has an operational meaning that does not look like a similarity measure chosen by fiat. That is exactly what the standard accounts seem to lack. The content-based ordering Popper proposed famously collapsed once Tichý and Miller showed that, on his definition, no false theory can come out closer to the truth than another; the similarity-based programmes that followed have, I gather, their own trouble, namely that the likeness of two structures depends on how the vocabulary carves them up. My suspicion is that the physics practice supplies a metric that is fixed by structure rather than convention — but I can already see the obvious rejoinder, which is that the choice of parameter is itself a choice of language (are we measuring v/c, or rapidity, and does the answer depend on that?). Whether that rejoinder is fatal, or just forces care about what "small parameter" means, is precisely what I cannot settle alone.

Please write the proposal accordingly: the question stated precisely enough that it could in principle come out either way; a section on why the existing approaches leave it open, treating both the content-based and the likeness-based lines fairly rather than as foils; a method section telling me honestly what I would need to learn (I am prepared to work through the formal literature on truthlikeness measures, but tell me which parts a physicist could realistically master); the possible conclusions, including the one I expect least — that the limiting-case practice is a local convenience of physics with no general lesson for truthlikeness; and explicit failure conditions for the project. Two pages. I will add references myself; do not include a bibliography or quotations from the literature, since I want to check every textual claim against the originals before it goes in.
