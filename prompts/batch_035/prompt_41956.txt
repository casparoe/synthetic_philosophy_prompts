"The meta-problem of consciousness was originally formulated by Daniel Dennett, roughly in the 1990s, though it got its current name later. It is the problem of explaining why we think there is a hard problem of consciousness at all. That is, instead of asking why physical processes give rise to subjective experience, we ask why physical processes give rise to reports, judgments, and intuitions about there being such a thing as subjective experience. Dennett's point was that these reports are just more physical behavior, so a complete cognitive science should be able to explain them the same way it explains any other behavior.

The relationship to the hard problem is that they run in parallel. Just as the hard problem asks for a bridge from physical processes to consciousness, the meta-problem asks for a bridge from physical processes to our problem-reports about consciousness. Chalmers later developed the idea and proposed a symmetry thesis: if the explanation of our problem-reports in the meta-problem is of the same kind as the explanation of consciousness in the hard problem, then solving the meta-problem solves the hard problem. The catch is that we cannot know in advance whether the two explanations are the same kind, so the symmetry has to be assumed rather than established.

Illusionists like Dennett take the optimistic view here. On their view, once we can fully explain why humans report puzzlement about experience, there is nothing left over to explain. If people only believe in qualia because of some quirk of how our introspective systems model ourselves, then the hard problem was never a problem about the world, only a problem about our concepts, and a solved meta-problem leaves no residue.

Finally, the symmetry has an important implication for physicalism. If the meta-problem turns out to be solvable, this demonstrates that physicalism is true, since a physical explanation of our judgments about consciousness is exactly what physicalism predicts. Conversely, if the meta-problem is unsolvable, physicalism is false. So the meta-problem can be read as a new argument in the old debate between physicalists and anti-physicalists."
