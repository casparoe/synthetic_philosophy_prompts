My roommate and I spent basically all of Thursday night arguing about the shutdown problem (we're both ML grad students, yes, this is what our social life has come to). His take is that "corrigibility" is just a confused grab bag of intuitions and the only real question is a boring safety-capability tradeoff: you can have an agent that's useful or an agent you can switch off, pick one. I think that's way too quick — some of the things people demand of a "shutdownable" agent look like genuine requirements to me, others look like aesthetic preferences of a particular camp, and I suspect there might be an actual impossibility result hiding in there rather than just a soft tension. Before we go another round I want the full spec written down so we're at least arguing about the same thing.

So: what would an adequate solution to the shutdown problem actually have to deliver? Candidates I can think of off the top of my head — the agent must actually shut down when the button is pressed, not merely fail to physically resist; it must have no incentive to cause, prevent, or influence button presses, including indirectly through the humans who might press the button (so no "get the human to trust me so they never press it" behavior); it should stay useful in the meantime rather than being lobotomized into harmlessness as the price of indifference; it should remain shutdownable under self-modification and when it starts encountering situations unlike training; and the whole construction shouldn't leak weird behavior (I vaguely remember utility-indifference-style agents that compulsively check the button or pay to keep it in a particular state). But I don't trust my own list. Give me the requirements with the case behind each, mark which are non-negotiable versus which are camp preferences, and tell me whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — that part I care about most, since if it exists my roommate wins the arguement in a way I'd rather concede honestly than bluff my way out of. Then, briefly, a few sentences each on how utility indifference and the off-switch-game / assistance-game style approach do against the list, and which single requirement knocks out the most candidate solutions.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — whether the desiderata can all be had at once, and which requirement does the most work — then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restating my question back at me.
