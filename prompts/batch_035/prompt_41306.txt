My brother thinks he's proved that mowing down pedestrians in GTA is genuinely wrong, and not just a bit gross. His argument: (1) Something has interests iff it persistently pursues goals and switches to a distinct regime when those goals are badly thwarted. (2) Modern NPCs do exactly that — they seek things out, flee damage, and there's a whole separate panic mode when their plan falls apart. (3) You can wrong something that has interests. (4) So you can wrong an NPC. And he adds (5): none of this turns on being simulated rather than "real", since simulation is just a substrate and substrate can't carry moral weight on its own.

What bugs me is that, by his own lights, a Tamagochi has interests, the Pac-Man ghosts have interests, and plausibly my Roomba's little error beeps count too. He just shrugs and says fine, they have marginal interests and get wronged marginally. But I can't accept that stomping a Goomba is even slightly wrong, so something has gone wrong somewhere — I just can't spot which premise is the dodgy one. And if I deny (5), he'll say I'm committed to thinking a perfect simulation of a child wouldn't matter either, which I definitely don't want. So: which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me?
