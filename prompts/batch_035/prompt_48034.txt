My roommates Maya and Theo had a long argument on the porch last night and I want someone to referee it properly, because they keep restarting it.

The backstory: Maya works at a small company and has thought hard about whether her team's new product plan will flop. She walked her reasoning out loud — market size, the two competitors, the deadline they're already slipping — and said she's about 85% confident it flops. That's where it started.

(1) Theo said that number is too high given her history: she's called three of their past projects dead-on-arrival, two of them succeeded anyway, and there's a whole psych literature showing people are systematically overconfidant about exactly this kind of judgment. Her track record is evidence about her evidence, he said, so she should knock the number down until it matches what someone with her track record deserves.

(2) Maya said no. She can see each step of her reasoning right now, and each step looks as solid as anything. A track record is a statistic about her past, and it can't outweigh what she can literally see in front of her — like a forecast saying 20% chance of rain while rain is visibly falling on her head. Discounting would leave her believing her evidence points one way while believing the other, which she said is just being irrational twice over.

(3) Theo said that's precisely what an overconfident person would say — she'd also feel the clarity of each step, that's the whole problem. If the meta-level evidence says her object-level judgments run hot in this domain, then lowering isn't ignoring her evidence, it IS her evidence working.

(4) Maya said fine, then where does it stop? By his logic she should also check whether her checking was overconfident, and then check the check, and it never bottoms out — plus the studies he keeps citing are themselves things she's taking on fallible testimony, so why is it only her confidence that gets audited?

(5) Theo said it bottoms out wherever the evidence is most reliable: the finding that people overrate their forecasts in domains like this is far better established than her private sense of clarity, so that side wins and that's the end of it.

(6) Maya's last word was that she'd already discounted for overconfidence when she settled on 85 instead of 95 — hasn't she paid the toll already? And Theo said: how does she know the discount itself wasn't too small, given who applied it?

They went to bed mad. Please go through the exchange round by round and say who's right about what — including whether Maya's move in (2) is the incoherence Theo takes it for or a defensible stance (I gather philosophers actually disagree about this "akrasia" stuff), and whether either of them is missing an option that would dissolve part of the fight, like just making the number itself carry the uncertainty instead of treating the track record as a separate thing to obey. And then tell me who comes out more right overall.
