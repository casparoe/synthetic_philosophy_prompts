I've been reading LessWrong and the surrounding blogosphere since like 2012, and free will arguments are one of those topics that come up again and again (determinism debates, acausal decision theory hand-waving, compatibilism flame wars in the comments) without anyone ever really sorting out what position people actually *hold* vs which flag they fly. So for my blog I want a "which free will view do you actually have?" quiz, and I'd like you to write it.

Setup: you're a professional philosopher who works on free will and has taught intro courses where students call themselves compatibilists because they like the vibe. The quiz should sort readers by their reactions to cases, not by their self-labels. Don't ask anything like "do you think determinism is compatible with free will" — that's the thing we're trying to diagnose.

Requirements:

1. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. Cases should include at least: a manipulation case along the lines of covert neuroscientists rewiring someone's motivations from birth (this is the classic pressure point between compatibilists and incompatibilists, use it well), a Frankfurt-style scenario where a device would intervene to ensure an action but never actually fires, a Dennett-flavored case where someone's impulsiveness is mildly corrected and they end up *more* free than before, and one coercive-pressure case where the person technically could have refused but everything about the situation made refusal absurd. Invent the rest yourself.

2. Build the logical space first and then place the positions in it — don't just enumerate the famous names. The axes that matter for my purposes: (a) whether free will requires ultimate sourcehood or just appropriate responsiveness to reasons, and (b) whether the thing we *call* free will is a fixed target or something we're allowed to revise as we learn more. That second axis is important because I want the leaves to include not just libertarianism, hard determinism/hard incompatibilism, and garden-variety compatibilism, but also Dennett's "varieties worth wanting" deflationism and a revisionist position (Vargas-style, where the concept gets partially rebuilt), plus the impossibilist line that responsibility demands something not even libertarianism can deliver. If a slot in the logical space is unoccupied, say so and guess why (incoherent? just unattractive? nobody got around to it?).

3. Include a scoring key mapping answer patterns to leaves, and explicitly flag any pair of positions the quiz can't seperate — I'd rather have an honest "these two will come out the same on my instrument, here's why" note than pretend the quiz is sharper than it is.

4. At the end, tell me how confident you are in the quiz's discriminating power overall, and single out the single question or branch you're least sure actually cuts between the positions the way you assigned it — I want to know where the instrument is weakest before I post it.

Please think it through carefully before writing, maybe brainstorm which distinctions do the most sorting work per question first, since some cases split the space three ways and others only confirm what earlier questions already established. Keep the question wording plain enough that a smart reader who's never taken a philosophy course can answer honestly — remember a lot of my readers' entire exposure to this is one Eliezer sequence and a wiki article.
