"(a) It means about 80% of the variation in height among the teenagers in the sample is associated with genetic differences between them, and the rest with environmental differences. Heritability is a statistic about a population, not a fixed property of height itself.

(b) No, Jordan is wrong. Heritability doesn't split any one person's height into a genetic chunk and an environmental chunk. It's about variation across the sample, so it can't be applied to a single individual like that.

(c) It came out lower because in Country Y the environment varies more between families. When environmental differences are bigger, environmental variance makes up a bigger share of total variance, so the genetic share, which is what heritability measures, shrinks.

(d) Mostly yes. Since 80% of height is genetic in Country X, food can only influence the remaining 20%, so a nutrition program could only shift average height a little. A heritability that high basically tells you the trait doesn't respond much to environment.

Also, since height is highly heritable in both countries, the fact that people in Country X are taller on average than people in Country Y is probably mostly down to genetic differences between the two populations."
