In my decision theory seminar we recently covered counterfactual mugging and XOR blackmail side by side. I can reproduce the standard verdicts — the updateless agent pays in the mugging but refuses in the blackmail — but I want to understand what feature of the two scenarios actually drives the difference, since both involve a predictor whose conduct is correlated with your decision and evidence you would rather not have. A classmate claims the cases are structurally identical, and that the verdicts come apart only because Omega runs a full simulation of you, so that your action is logically tied to the payoff in the other branch, whereas the blackmailer merely conditions on a prediction of your action. Please assess this claim: does the distinction really turn on logical dependence versus mere evidential correlation? And what should we say about a variant our TA raised, in which the blackmailer also runs a full simulation of your decision before deciding whether to send the letter — does refusing remain correct there, or does the case collapse into a mugging in which you ought to pay? I would like a clear account of what, exactly, updateless verdicts are tracking in these problems.
