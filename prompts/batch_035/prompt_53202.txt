You are a professional epistemologist, used to designing teaching and diagnostic material. I need your help building a classification quiz, and I'd like you to think carefully and brainstorm before writing, because the diagnostic cases are the entire point.

Background: a friend and I argued late into the night about Gettier cases, and we discovered something frustrating — we agreed on all the labels and disagreed about nearly every concrete case. We each self-identified as "externalist about knowledge," yet we split on almost every example we discussed: he said the driver in fake barn county knows there's a barn ahead, I said he doesn't; I said the person looking at the distant sheep-shaped rock knows there's a sheep in the field, he didn't. Our positions, whatever they are, clearly aren't the ones we call them by.

So: please write a "which view of knowledge do you actually hold?" quiz, 8–10 questions, each a concrete case (variants on the fake barn, the sheep in the field, a lottery, a stuck fuel gauge — mix familiar cases with new ones you invent). Each question should offer two or three options phrased only as verdicts about the case ("knows" / "doesn't know" / "knows, but only because..."), never mentioning any theory or any -ism. Then provide a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions: the no-false-grounds condition, causal/reliabilist theories, relevant-alternatives or safety conditions, no-defeater views, and the knowledge-first option of rejecting the whole Gettier-industry as misconceived.

Two requirements beyond the quiz itself. First, at least two questions should be built to split positions that usually travel together — for instance, a case where the belief is true through luck in a generally reliable way, set against a case where the belief is safe but formed through a falsehood, so that people who give the same label in the abstract are forced apart. Second, flag honestly any pair of leaves your quiz cannot in principle separate: if no case you can devise distinguishes, say, the safety theorist from the relevant-alternatives theorist, say so explicitly rather than manufacturing a fake discriminator. I'd rather know where the tree runs out than be handed a quiz that pretends to be sharper than it is.
