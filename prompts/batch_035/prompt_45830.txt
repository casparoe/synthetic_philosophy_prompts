So heres the situation I'm in: I'm a science journalist, I've been on the mind-uploading beat for about a decade now (I did the long profile of Continuum Bio back when everyone still called the trial speculative), and next month my best friend Dana becomes one of the first thirty people enrolled in their gradual-replacement program. The way it works is they swap out your neurons for functionally identical implants a few thousand at a time over roughly two years, and you're awake and chatting and reporting on your own experience the whole way through. She's throwing herself a "see you on the other side -- or the same side, whatever" party this weekend, and I've been asked to give the speech. Help.

Here's what makes it tricky. Dana is a hardcore Parfitian, has been since undergrad -- she genuinely believes the question "will it still be me?" is an empty one, that what matters is psychological continuity and connectedness rather than identity, and she made me promise the speech won't reassure anyone by insisting she'll survive, because she thinks that's the wrong thing to even care about. Her mother will also be there, and she's convinced the trial kills her daughter and leaves behind a very good copy of her; I can't write a speech that treats that fear like it's stupid, because it isn't, and I've spent ten years interviewing smart people on both sides without ever coming down myself. The room will also have a handful of Continuum engineers and at least two colleagues of mine who'd love a quotable line, so nothing I say should sound like I'm either shilling for the company or doing a hit piece on it. I want maybe five minutes: something that takes Dana's view seriously without steamrolling her mom, that doesn't pretend there's a settled answer where there isn't one, and that lands as a toast to my friend rather than a column. Dana keeps going back to the thought experiment where you replace the neurons one at a time and ask at every step whether anything changed, and the intuition that nothing ever could change, because you'd never notice -- I suspect that belongs in there somewhere, but I can't figure out how to say it to a mother who's terrified. And whatever you do, no Ship of Theseus joke -- Dana banned it explicitly.
