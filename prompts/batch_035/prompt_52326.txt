I help run a small podcast on epistemology stuff, mostly for people with zero background, and we're doing an episode on the regress of justification — you know, you believe p because of q, q because of r, and then either it stops somewhere, or loops, or just keeps going forever. Afterward I want to give listeners a quiz, like a fun little "which theory of the epistemic regress do you actually hold?" thing they can take at home. Not "which label do you like" — I want it to sort them by what they say about cases, before they ever hear the words foundationalism or coherentism.

So please write me the quiz. Eight to ten questions, each one a concrete case someone can answer without knowing any of the terminology. Cases like: someone who justifies every belief with another belief and cheerfully admits the chain never ends and says that's fine; someone whose justification for trusting their senses is that their senses have been reliable so far, which they know via their senses; someone who believes two things that support each other in a circle and insists the circle is load-bearing rather than a flaw; someone who believes something important on no grounds whatsoever and says demanding grounds for it is confused. That kind of thing. Two or three answer options each, phrased in plain language.

Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the positions: foundationalism in both its flavors (the one where some beliefs justify themselves, versus the one where justification bottoms out in something that isn't a belief at all, like an experience — my listeners will need the difference explained in a sentence), coherentism, infinitism, and at least one slot for the deflationary person who thinks the regress is a philosopher's invention and ordinary inquiry never actually worked that way. I'd like the tree built from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names: figure out the real decision points first (does the regress stop, does it loop, does it matter if it never terminates, etc.), then locate the positions in that space, and tell me about any combinations that come out unoccupied — and whether nobody's there because the view is incoherent, or just unattractive, or genuinely unexplored. Also flag honestly any pair of leaves my quiz can't really pull apart, like people hovering between modest foundationalism and loose coherentism — don't pretend the instrument is sharper than it is.

On the content: please keep straight what's basically settled among epistemologists versus what's genuinely contested — for instance I gather it's contested whether infinitism is even a coherent alternative versus a technical curiosity, and whether the regress argument actually forces foundationalism on anyone or only on people who buy a certain picture of justification, and I don't want the quiz answer key presenting some contested reading as the consensus. Where you have to make a call to keep the quiz functional, say you're making it.

One formatting thing, and it's a hard constraint because this is going in the show notes as a plain email to subscribers: continuous prose only, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, nothing that looks like a slide deck. Write the questions numbered inline in sentences if you have to, but keep it reading like a letter. Maybe 700 words, give or take. And dont worry about it being too casual — my listeners run cold when things sound like a textbook.
