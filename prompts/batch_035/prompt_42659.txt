Write a corporate press release, roughly 600 words, in the flat, damage-controlling voice of a real crisis-communications release, in which a mid-sized technology company announces that its board has adopted a formal surrogate goal: an expensive precommitment to retaliate disproportionately against any party that so much as begins modeling an extortion attempt against the company, adopted purely so that no rational actor will find extortion worthwhile in the first place. The humor should come from getting the decision theory right rather than from caricaturing it: the release should distinguish threats from offers; explain why the commitment must be binding, costly, and publicly verifiable to have any deterrent effect; have the CEO insist the policy was adopted updatelessly, before anyone had actually threatened anything, and that the company would have been prepared to retaliate in every branch where threats emerged; and acknowledge, in a cheerfully buried sub-clause, that the company is now obligated to carry through on the retaliation in branches where it is pointlessly destructive — including a pending case against a teenager who ran an Instagram poll titled "should we extort them lol." Include a quote from a resigning board member, a classical decision theorist, who thinks the whole policy is a self-inflicted wound: capitulating to no one, exactly as planned, at considerable expense. The satire's target is the practice of commitment racing and surrogate goals itself — the way precommitments can deter, but also invite new extortion from anyone who learns they exist, and can lock you into acting on threats you'd rather ignore — so a reader versed in updateless decision theory should find the piece fair rather than confused, and a reader who isn't should still follow the logic. Keep it deadpan throughout; no winking. End with a short afterword (outside the parody) noting where the piece takes liberties with the ideas and separating what is settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on decision theory — in particular, that whether surrogate goals help at all, and whether adopting them invites the very extortion they're meant to deter, are open questions, and the afterword should not present either answer as the consensus view.
