I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and my personal identity reading group just revisited Dennett's Where Am I?, where the narrator's brain sits in a vat while his body walks around connected to it by radio. Strip the story down and consider a tidied version of the setup: a patient's cerebrum is removed and kept alive in a support vat, wired to the original body — brainstem intact — by a high-bandwidth radio link; after a year of apparently normal life, the link is severed, the now-cerebrum-less original body is kept alive on life support with, let us stipulate, no psychological life of its own, and the cerebrum is connected instead to a lab-grown body with a fresh brainstem. Give me your credence that the person who existed before the operation is the one who wakes up in the lab-grown body — that is, that personal identity tracks the cerebrum rather than the human animal left on the support system. Please don't reply that it depends on your theory of identity; I want the number and its decomposition. I'd expect terms at least for: the probability that some psychological-continuity account of personal identity is correct; the probability that animalism is correct and, given it, that the animal we are stays with the brainstem-plus-body rather than following the cerebrum; the probability that the scenario is coherent as described, without some hidden impossibility in consciousness being severed and reconnected across the year-long radio link; and a term for the possibility that identity in such a case is indeterminate rather than determinately one way or the other. Add anything else you think belongs. Assign a rough probability and a one-line justification to each term, combine them into a final credence, and then tell me which term you are least confident in and which single stipulation change — say, gradual neuron-by-neuron replacement instead of wholesale cerebrum transfer — would move your final number the most, and roughly how far.
