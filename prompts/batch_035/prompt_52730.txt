Quick backstory so you know where I'm coming from: I'm 42, a driver ran a red light on me last April, and the TBI left my working memory basically cooked. My whole life now runs through a notes app, a to-do app with aggressive reminders, and a set of routines my rehab psychologist helped me build. When she mentioned the extended mind thesis I went and read up on it -- Otto's notebook, the parity principle, the whole deal -- and I'm not gonna lie, it was comforting. On that view my mind wasn't so much diminished as relocated. But I don't want to lean on a philosophy of mind position just because it's flattering, so stress-test it for me: what would actually count against it?

Not the one-line textbook objections -- I want to know which findings, cases, and arguments should genuinely move someone who believes it, and how the defeaters rank from fatal to merely awkward. Things I'd want covered: whether the parity principle is too permissive (if constant availability and automatic endorsement are all it takes, my wife and half my house start looking like parts of my mind too, and at some point has the thesis become trivially true and stopped being interesting); whether this is even an empirical claim that results on cognitive offloading and tool use could bear on, or whether it's just a choice about where to draw a line that no experiment could ever touch; the circularity worry that we only know what counts as cognitive processing by looking at brains in the first place, so extending "cognitive" outwards begs the question; and whether anyone's proposed a mark of the cognitive that isn't either circular or just prejudice in favor of the skull. Also the meta question: which of these would a committed extended-mind person themselves concede counts against them versus wave off, and if the thesis has been qualified into unfalsifiability, is that a defect for a claim of this type or just how metaphysics of this kind works?

There's a practical edge too: my employer is making noise about wiping personal phones under some BYOD policy, and if I actually believed the phone is part of my cognitive system that changes how hard I push back -- "you deleted part of my mind" is a different conversation than "you deleted my notes." So don't be gentle with the thesis just because it's been good for my morale. If it only survives by never risking anything, tell me that, and tell me what I should beleive instead.
