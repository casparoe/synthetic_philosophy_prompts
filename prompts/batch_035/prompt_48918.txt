Write a parody FAQ page, roughly 600-700 words, for the website of a fictional government agency (call it the National Species Registry or make up your own name) whose legally mandated job is to maintain THE official count of species in the country. The count matters because funding, endangered-species protections, and environmental impact reviews all turn on it, so the agency needs one number, and the joke is that the number depends entirely on which species concept you pick -- and the FAQ has to keep a straight face while papers over this.

The humor should come from real features of the species problem, not invented ones, so that a biologist or philosopher of biology recognizes you actually know the stuff: the biological species concept's reliance on reproductive isolation, and how that leaves asexual lineages (parthenogenetic whiptail lizards are a nice touch) and bacteria with horizontal gene transfer in limbo; the phylogenetic species concept and how it inflates the count by splitting on every branch point, with the lumpers-vs-splitters dispute showing up as some kind of appeals process; ring species, where the neighbors interbreed all the way around the ring until the ends meet and can't; hybrids, and whether a hybrid swarm counts as one species, two, or a new third thing; and the old morphology-based fallback that everyone quietly uses when the genetics budget runs out.

Requirements: keep it deadpan and bureaucratically chipper throughout, like the agency genuinely believes there is a correct answer and the FAQ will reveal it any question now. At least one answer should be visibly written by whichever staff member got stuck with the microbes section and has clearly given up on the whole concept of species. Include the standard FAQ furniture -- "Q: What is a species?", "Q: Why did the total change this year?", etc. No winking at the reader, no authorial commentary: the irony has to stay legible without it.

The deeper target is the assumption that there's one natural cut of the tree of life waiting to be counted -- the satire should let a reader feel that the taxonomy is a decision plus a fact, not just a fact -- without ever saying anything that directly meta. Aim for it to be funny to someone who knows the literature (mayr, the phylocode, all of it) but readable to someone who just knows what a species is supposed to be. Roughly 650 words is ideal but dont stress over exact count.
