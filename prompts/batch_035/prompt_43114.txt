ok so last night my friend (she's a PhD student in evolutionary biology) and I had this massive argument that started because I said peacock tails were obviously just a mistake evolution hadn't gotten around to fixing. She then spent like an hour explaining costly signaling and the handicap principle to me — the idea that a signal is believable to the receiver *precisely because* it's expensive to produce, so the peacock's train works as an honest advertisement of genetic quality because a mediocre peacock literally cannot afford to grow and haul around a giant useless tail. The wastefulness isn't a bug, it's the security deposit against bluffing. I told her that sounded like a just-so story and she said people did dismiss it that way for decades (apparently some very cranky fights in the 70s-90s) until the math people worked out when costly signals can actually be stable. Anyway I lost the argument but came away kind of obsessed, and now I want to turn it into comedy.

Write an agony-aunt advice column, roughly 600 words, called something like "Dear Aunty Signal." The letter-writers are animals struggling with their display behaviors, and Aunty's answer to nearly everything is some version of "make it more expensive." Letters I'd like to see (feel free to adjust): a gazelle who can't stop stotting — doing those stiff-legged jumps right in front of lions, basically yelling "I'm too fit to catch, don't bother" — and wonders if this is a cry for help; a peacock whose train is wrecking his commute and his lower back; and at least one letter from the receiver's side, like a lion complaining that the gazelles keep doing show-off jumps at him and it's honestly getting into his head, or a peahen asking how she's supposed to know the tails aren't all bluff.

The jokes have to come from getting the theory right, not from making fun of it — a specialist should laugh at Aunty's overextensions, not wince at yours. Keep her internally consistent: she's a true believer who applies handicap logic to everything, including places it shouldn't go. And crucially, include one letter where the right answer ISN'T "add more cost" — for instance an animal whose signal is reliable because it's structurally impossible to fake (something like a size or body-shape cue that just IS the quality being advertised, so the cost is beside the point) — and let Aunty get flustered or double down, while an attentive reader can see where the actual theory stops. Deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader, no name-drops or citations needed, and pls give her funny sign-offs ("Yours in honest advertising" or similar).
