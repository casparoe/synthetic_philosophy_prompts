Two friends of mine have been arguing for literally years about whether philosophy makes progress and I've given up on them ever resolving it directly. One is a pessimist: after two and a half thousand years there's still no consensus on free will, consciousness, or induction, while physics settled most of its founding questions within a few centuries — by any reasonable standard that's failure. The other is an optimist: philosophy makes progress precisely by dissolving its own questions, so when a question gets genuinely solved it gets handed off to a science (natural philosophy became physics, chunks of philosophy of mind became cognitive science) and everyone forgets it was ever philosophy, meaning the ledger can only ever show losses. My hunch is they aren't really disagreeing about the facts at all but about what "progress" even means — convergence on consensus vs. elimination of questions — plus maybe a counterfactual: whether the sciences could have gotten where they are without philosophy doing the conceptual ground-clearing first.

Write the joint statement they should produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want: a numbered section of claims both can sign (about what the historical record actually shows, what "consensus" would even look like in philosophy, etc.), then a disagreements table laying out the cruxes — the consensus criterion, the dissolution criterion, that counterfactual about philosophy's contribution to science — each phrased so its own side would accept the wording, plus for each crux the kind of argument or case both would agree bears on it and each side's prediction written down in advance. And if any part of their dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why. Keep it under 500 words.
