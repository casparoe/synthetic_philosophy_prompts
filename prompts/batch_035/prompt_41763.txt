I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology, and for the past several months I've been keeping a notebook of my verdicts across the Sleeping Beauty family of cases, in the hope that they would cohere into a position on self-locating credence. I now suspect they don't. Here is the list.

In the original problem (fair coin flipped Sunday night; heads: one awakening on Monday; tails: awakenings on Monday and Tuesday, with full amnesia between them; Beauty knows the protocol but on any given awakening doesn't know which day it is), my verdict is 1/3. When she's told during an awakening that it is Monday, I say 1/2; told it is Tuesday, 0. All three feel firm.

In the duplication variant — on tails, Beauty is copied overnight and each person is woken once, on Monday or Tuesday respectively, each believing herself to be Beauty and knowing the protocol; on heads, the one Beauty is woken once — I say 1/2, and this verdict feels firmer than my 1/3 in the original, even though I cannot find any difference between the two situations accessible from the inside. A friend presses this as an argument: whatever answer the original problem gets, the duplication variant must get the same one, since no observation could tell the subject which situation she is in. I can't answer him, and I can't give up either number.

In what I think of as the extreme version — tails: a million awakenings — I hold two reactions at once. Near-zero credence in heads feels right when I picture the per-awakening betting setup, where each awakening is offered a wager on the coin at even odds. And 1/2 feels right when I reflect that the coin is fair and nothing in her evidence is correlated with the toss. I notice that these two reactions respectively underwrite my two main verdicts, so neither can be dismissed without cost.

There is a sailor's-child case in the same family — a fair coin determines whether a sailor's child grows up alone on one island or alongside a sibling on a second; you know you are one of his children but not which — and my verdict there is 1/3, matching the original problem. Once the experiment is over and Beauty is told everything on Wednesday: 1/2, trivially.

The candidate principles I've tried: assign equal credence over centered possibilities and read off the uncentered propositions — this delivers 1/3, 1/2, and 0 in the first three cases, but also 1/3 in the duplication variant, which I don't accept. Or hold that a rational agent's credence in the outcome of a fair coin can never legitimately move without evidence of the toss — which delivers my duplication and Wednesday verdicts, but not the 1/3 I'm committed to in the original.

What I would like is a principle, or a small set of candidates, stated precisely enough to compute with, that captures as many of these verdicts as possible. Please tell me which clause each verdict motivates, what the principle predicts for cases I haven't listed — in particular the variant where the coin isn't tossed until after the Monday awakening, so that the Tuesday awakening occurs only on tails, and whether, if the per-awakening betting setup is offered, the principle says her credence must match the odds she would accept — and give me an honest accounting of which of my verdicts cannot be saved. If my 1/3 and my duplication 1/2 really are inconsistent, tell me which one to surrender and what keeping the other costs.
