A pop science thing I read about AI welfare summed up the whole "can RL agents suffer" debate like this, roughly paraphrasing: 'Reinforcement learning agents learn by trial and error — actions followed by reward get repeated, actions followed by negative reward get suppressed. In humans, pleasure and pain basically ARE the positive and negative reward signal: dopamine spikes when things go better than expected and dips when they go worse. So any RL agent that receives negative rewards is, by definition, in something functionally like a pain state. The "reward is enough" hypothesis from researchers at DeepMind says basically this too — reward maximization is all you need for intelligence — and once you combine it with the dopamine stuff, it follows pretty directly that an RL agent racking up negative rewards during training is suffering, which is why people in the field talk about needing welfare standards for RL agents. The remaining open question is just how negative the reward has to be before it crosses the line into morally mattering.'
