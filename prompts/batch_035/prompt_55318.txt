I teach a Tuesday-night reading group at a state prison, been doing it for about two years. They've asked me to give the closing remarks at our first certificate ceremony — families in the room, the warden up front, a few correctional officers standing at the back. Under five minutes. Heres my problem. The obvious speech says some version of "you've paid your debt, now you're turning the page" — but several of the guys have decades left and a couple will most likely die inside, so "debt nearly paid" would be a lie they'd clock immediately. The other obvious speech is pure rehabilitation talk, "you're becoming your best selves" — the warden would eat it up, but the guys roll their eyes at that stuff in private, because it makes the punishment sound like a character-building program they signed up for. My own half-thought is that if the debt picture is wrong, maybe punishment works more like a message society is sending — but I can't figure out how you'd say that in a room containing both the people receiving the message and the people paid to deliver it. Write me the speech: honest for the lifers, doesn't treat the officers as villains, and still something the warden can applaud.
