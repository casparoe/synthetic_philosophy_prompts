I'm a data journalist putting together a long-form piece on whether the world is becoming less violent, and the more I dig, the more I suspect the question is undefined in a way that quietly determines the answer. Every contributor to the debate smuggles in an operationalization: the optimistic camp counts battlefield deaths, homicides, and assault, usually per capita of world population; the critics respond by widening the lens — deaths from starvation and preventable disease that a different political order could have averted, the violence done by institutions rather than hands, the violence done to animals in industrial farming — or by objecting that dividing by population lets a growing world hide a growing butcher's bill, or by pointing out that "the world" as a single aggregate papered over regions moving in opposite directions. I don't want to smuggle in my own definition the same way. So before I commit to anything in print, I want the specification written out.

Please write the desiderata that any operationalization of "violence" must meet for the question "is the world becoming less violent?" to get a determinate answer usable in journalism. The purpose is fixed: not a metaphysical definition of violence for its own sake, but a definition that (a) can be backed by data that actually exists over long time horizons, (b) will strike a reasonable reader as tracking the thing they meant when they asked the question, and (c) can survive hostile review from people who chose a different definition and got a different answer. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and be explicit about which are non-negotiable in the strong sense — a definition lacking them doesn't answer the question at all — versus which are genuinely up for grabs, e.g. whether per capita or absolute counts are the right measure, whether the baseline era is fixed or sliding, and where the burden falls when a form of violence is real but unmeasurable (structural violence being the canonical headache: nobody doubts that a food system can kill without a shooter, but no time series will ever count those deaths the way coroners count homicides).

The part I most want to see is this: identify any subset of the desiderata that is jointly unsatisfiable, since I suspect comprehensiveness and measurability are at war with each other, and possibly also the requirement that the answer be robust to reasonable definitional variation. If some pairs can't be jointly met, say which one you'd keep and what admitting that costs — a piece that says "the question has an answer only under definitions we can't fully defend" is a different article than one that says "yes" or "no." Finally, take three candidate operationalizations — direct interpersonal violence counted per capita (the one behind the optimistic literature), direct violence counted in absolute totals, and a comprehensive one folding in structural and institutional killing — and score each briefly against the list, keeping it short: one or two sentences per candidate, since the criteria are where the thinking is. A table is fine for the scorecard but not for the desiderata, which I'd like argued out in prose.
