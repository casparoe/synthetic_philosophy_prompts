I'm in an intermediate epistemology course and we just hit the lottery and preface paradoxes, and I want to build one of those 'answer some questions, find out what you actually believe' decision trees for our section's discussion wiki. The kind where every question is a concrete case you can answer without knowing any of the position names, so it sorts people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like.

Rough spec:

- The entry point is the paradoxes themselves: a fair million-ticket lottery where for any single ticket you'd confidently say 'it will lose', plus the fact that not all tickets can lose; and the author version where you believe each chapter of your own book and also believe the book surely contains an error somewhere.
- The first split should be which step of the paradox the person refuses: the probability-to-belief step ('sufficiently high probability suffices for belief'), the conjunction/agglomeration step ('if you rationally believe each, you can rationally believe the conjunction'), the assumption that rational belief sets are consistent, or the claim that lottery and preface propositions aren't *believed* at all (maybe they're merely accepted, or endorsed for action, or just high credences with no belief present).
- Somewhere in the tree there needs to be a node running the lottery/preface asymmetry: does the person's verdict differ between the two cases, and if so, why — stakes, the difference between statistical and case-by-case evidence, or something else. People who treat the cases exactly alike should end up in different leaves from people who insist there's a principled asymmetry.
- I also want a betting node: 'would you take an even-money bet that this ticket loses' vs 'would you say you believe it' — something that separates people who think high credence is enough for belief from people who think belief requires more. That's where a leaf should go for the view that lottery propositions get high credence without being beleived, plus whatever that view then has to say about knowledge.
- Leaves should be named positions where they exist: rejecting conjunction closure for rational belief (Kyburg-style), the no-belief-in-lottery-propositions family (including the 'justified in asserting but don't believe' version and whatever contextualist-threshold variants fit), the 'inconsistent but rational' line where rational belief sets are just allowed to be inconsistent, epistemic-utility / stability-style views if they fit, and a leaf for 'no position survives, the concept of rational belief is broken' if people end up there. Also include unoccupied combinations instead of skipping gaps in the logical space — one sentence on why each gap is empty (incoherent? unattractive? just unexplored?).
- Flag any two positions the tree genuinely can't separate with the available questions instead of pretending the questions do it.

Format: I need this as structured data for the wiki, so give me a JSON object rather than prose. Schema:

{
  "nodes": [{"id": string, "type": "question" or "leaf", "text": string, "yes_child": string or null, "no_child": string or null}],
  "inseparable_pairs": [[string, string]],
  "empty_cells": [{"answer_pattern": string, "diagnosis": string}]
}

where "text" for questions is the case (one or two sentences max, people will do this on their phones) and for leaves it's the position's name plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. "inseparable_pairs" lists leaf names the tree can't really pull apart with a one-line reason each, and "empty_cells" covers answer combinations that lead nowhere real.

Aim for 9-12 leaves. And please sanity-check at the end that no question basically gives away which position it leads to — the whole point is that someone can answer honestly without knowing what they're being sorted into.
