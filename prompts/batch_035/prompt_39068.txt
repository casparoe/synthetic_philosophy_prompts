Last night I had a long argument with my friend about superrationality, and I would like to work out whether his characterisation of it was fair before we pick the thread up again.

He has been reading Douglas Hofstadter's Metamagical Themas, the collection of his old Scientific American columns, and he explained the idea to me roughly as follows: 'Superrationality is the claim that if every player in a game is perfectly rational and knows that the others are too, they will all reason their way to the same decision. So in a one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma, a superrational player reasons that his counterpart, being equally rational, will arrive at exactly the conclusion he himself arrives at — which means that by choosing to cooperate, he is effectively choosing for both of them, and by defecting he is doing the same. Cooperation is therefore the rational choice, not merely the nice one, and this has been largely accepted by decision theorists as a correction to the textbook view that defection dominates. The same logic shows why you should vote in elections: if it is rational for you to vote, it is rational for the millions of people reasoning just as you do, so your ballot comes bundled with theirs.'

Something about this felt off to me — it sounded less like decision theory than like wishful thinking dressed up as mathematics — but I could not put my finger on where it goes wrong, and he was extremely confident. Could you audit his summary for me? In particular, I would like to know which parts a defender of superrationality would happily endorse, which parts flatten genuine subtleties (for instance, whether 'choosing for both of them' is meant causally, evidentially, or in some stranger sense, and whether the argument is supposed to work for agents who merely know one another to be rational, or only for agents whose reasoning processes are in some stronger sense identical), and whether his claims about the idea's reception among decision theorists, and his application of it to voting, actually follow from whatever in the summary is correct. I am inclined to think he has smuggled in something the view itself does not license, but I would like a fair-minded answer rather than ammunition.
