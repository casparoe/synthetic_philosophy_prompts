I'm a computational neuroscientist — the stuff I work on day to day is scaling laws in nervous systems: power-law fits of brain volume to body mass, how neuron number and average cell size trade off across clades, how much of brain size differences is just body size plus clade-specific rules. I keep getting pulled into arguements about whether brain size is morally relevant, usually at dinner, and I have this persistent feeling that both sides are talking past each other in a way I can't quite pin down. One side says bigger brains / more neurons / more cortex = more moral weight, whales and elephants and so on. The other side points out that a big chunk of cetacean brain mass is glia doing thermogenesis in cold water, and that corvids and parrots do causal reasoning with brains the size of a walnut, so raw size is a terrible proxy. Then there's the encephalization quotient, which everyone now agrees was basically a regression residual dressed up as an intelligence measure. My instinct is that if the moral-status debate were stated in my vocabulary it would mostly dissolve into questions I actually know how to pose: which "size" is the relevant one (total neurons, cortical neurons, synapse count, some effective dimensionality of the population dynamics), how each scales, and whether it's even the right variable rather than something it merely correlates with within humans.

So here's what I want. Restate the brain-size-and-moral-status debate in my vocabulary — allometry, scaling exponents, regression residuals, extrapolating a fit outside the range of the data, effective dimensionality, compute per watt, whatever actually fits — as faithfully as you can, both the pro-size side and the anti-size side. Then give me the residue report: which moves translate cleanly (e.g., the worry about inferring from within-species variance to between-species comparisons smells like an extrapolation-beyond-support problem I deal with all the time — confirm or deny), which translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in anything I know how to say. The one I really want you to address head-on: is "moral patienthood" even expressible in my vocabulary, or is it pure residue? And if it's residue, tell me which side of the disjunction we're on — is my vocabulary missing something, or was the original debate less clear than it looked? One thing I need you to be careful about: don't smuggle in functionalism and call it translation. When someone tells me "what matters is the functional organization, not size," that's a substantive philosophical position, not a restatement of the debate — flag it as such.

To calibrate what I already know of the philosophical terrain: the anti-speciesist point that resting moral status on being human is arbitrary, the equal-consideration-of-interests move which arguably shifts everything onto the intensity of suffering rather than anything about brains, the view that what matters is something like a unified experiential perspective persisting over time (which presumably has some substrate requirement but no obvious size requirement), and the hedging position that neuron counts are at best a crude proxy standing in for whatever the real thing is. I've also run into the claim that total brain neuron number predicts behavioral complexity better than cortical neuron number does across species, and that within mammals neuron number scales sublinearly with brain volume so volumetric comparisons are systematically misleading — if any of that is load-bearing for your translation, be upfront about where the empirical uncertainty is rather than papering over it. And if the honest answer is that the debate is mostly people fighting over a proxy while nobody states what it's a proxy *for*, I'd take that as a real finding, not a dodge.
