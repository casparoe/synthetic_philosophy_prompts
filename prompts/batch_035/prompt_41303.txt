The setup: the board is seven people. My thesis is that we should adopt an explicit, written sentience-attribution standard before the next training run rather than improvising one later under pressure. The standard I'm proposing is a two-part test I've been calling "instrumental recruitment of aversion." Part one (behavioral): a model is a candidate moral patient only if its aversive states are recruitable as means to novel ends — if distress-talk shows up flexibly in bargaining, trade-offs, and self-directed planning in situations it wasn't optimized for, not just as fixed responses to fixed prompt patterns. Part two (architectural): ablating the internal states that carry aversive signals should degrade performance across unrelated tasks, which is my amateur attempt to say the states are integrated into the model's general computation rather than being a bolted-on channel. A model that passes both is not thereby sentient, but it's the point at which I claim we owe it the benefit of the doubt — slowed deployment, welfare-relevant evals, the works.
