quick context before the actual question: i'm on the city council of a smallish city, and this year we finally passed a rental inspection ordinance (landlords have to get units inspected every three years, they fought it hard). it passed 5-4 after months of hearings. last week a county judge blocked enforcement while a landlord lawsuit works through the courts, and honestly i'm pretty steamed about unelected judges freezing something our elected council passed.

at the community meeting about all this, one of the guys who supports the lawsuit stood up and said this is exactly what courts are FOR -- he brought up the thing one of the chief justices said at his confirmation hearing years back, that judging is like being an umpire: you don't make the rules or pitch or bat, you just call balls and strikes, and the rulebook is the law, so blocking our ordinance wasn't a power grab, just a called third strike on an illegal pitch. my brother-in-law, who's a public defender and physically incapable of agreeing with anyone, says the umpire comparison actually cuts the other way: the strike zone is whatever the umpire calling the game says it is, nobody elected him to have a strike zone, and half the arguments at home plate are about the zone itself, not the pitch.

i keep flip-flopping depending on who i talked to last, so i want to actually think it through instead of just being mad. can you audit the umpire analogy for me? like:

- lay out the mapping explicitly -- who's the umpire, what's the rulebook, what's a pitch, who's the pitcher vs the batter, what's a manager arguing a call, is there even a "game" here and who's keeping score?
- where does the mapping break? things i keep half-noticing: the "rulebook" the judge is applying was itself written and amended over time partly by judges, umpires don't get to decide which games are worth officiating but courts pick which doctrines to build up, baseball added instant replay review and there's nobody standing above the courts reviewing their zone, umpires call the same known zone in every game while our ordinance's legality turns on open-ended phrases. what else?
- which breakdowns actually matter for the question i care about -- whether it's legitimate for an unelected judge to freeze something an elected council passed -- and which ones are just pedantic gotchas that don't touch the legitimacy question?
- and the bottom line: once the disanalogies are on the table, does the umpire comparison support the guy at the meeting, my brother-in-law, or neither? and if neither, is there a better analogy out there for what judicial review actually is, that could carry more of the weight?
