quick context before the ask: my friend Maya and I spent most of saturday night arguing about whether you can actually *know* a painting is good from someone else's word. She says of course you can — her whole taste in music came from critics she trusts, and she'd fight you that Coltrane is great even for records she hasn't heard. I kept pushing back that there's a difference between believing it and knowing it, that aesthetic stuff feels like it has to be earned first-hand somehow, and she accused me of confusing snobbery for epistemology. It got heated over dumplings. Anyway it turns out this is now my actual problem, not just a bar argument.

I'm on the public art committee for our city's new central library, and we have to pick ONE of two pieces for the plaza by the end of the month. Option A is a water feature by a competent regional artist that all seven of us have walked through and seen with our own eyes; the honest consensus is that it's fine, pleasant, kind of corporate. Option B is a large-scale sculpture by a much bigger name, but it's currently installed in a sister city abroad, none of us can travel before the deadline, and the only evidence we have is a long email from a curator we all personally trust (she picked the pieces in our east wing and was right about all of them) calling it the best public work she's stood in front of in a decade, plus photographs, which she says don't do it justice. The committee has split exactly along the line of my fight with Maya: our retired gallerist says she will not vote for art she hasn't seen, full stop, because testimony about aesthetic value is cheap and everyone's cousin has an opinion; the librarian thinks that's insane given that we trust testimony about literally everything else the city buys, including the structural steel.

There's also a wrinkle making this harder: our founding bylaw says the committee shall exercise "its own aesthetic judgment" in selecting works, and the gallerist claims voting for B sight-unseen violates that outright, while others read it as boilerplate. Please draft the decision memo I'll circulate before the vote — it needs to actually recommend one of the two pieces with reasoning the losing side could respect, and it has to deal honestly with the strongest version of the gallerist's worry, not just wave it off (though feel free to show if you think it *should* be waved off, as Maya would put it). If it helps, I'd also want the memo to touch on the difference between us getting aesthetic knowledge from the curator's email versus just justified belief, and whether it matters that once the thing arrives we'll all eventually see it anyway — is deciding on testimony really different from just deferring our judgment, and does that bear on the bylaw thing at all. Keep it under 900 words, something my fellow committee members will actually read.
