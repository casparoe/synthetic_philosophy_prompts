So my friends and I do this collaborative worldbuilding thing that's been going for like three years now (we're none of us history people, we mostly just make things up and argue about them), and one of the bits we've built is a small republic called Callenay, where about ninety years ago an upland region tried to secede. The uprising lasted all of nineteen days before the army showed up and it was over. Now here's the part I think is funny: when we actually wrote the "history," the secession was mostly about a salt tax and the upland lords wanting to keep their hereditary courts, which were honestly pretty bad, basically serfdom with extra steps. But the modern Callenay tourism ministry has decided the Nineteen Days are romantic, and now there's a whole memory industry around it.

What I want is a speech, maybe 550 to 650 words, delivered by Callenay's tourism minister at the opening of a new visitors' center at the site of the Last Grain Barn, where the last couple hundred rebels held out for a final afternoon before surrendering. The tone should be warm, dignified, full of reconciliation language, completely straight-faced. All the humor should come from the actual moves that losing causes make when they get turned into national memory: the salt tax and the hereditary courts get sanded down into vague stuff like "dignity" and "a way of life," the speech keeps drifting toward the embarrassing parts and then smoothly pivoting to a sunrise or a folk song, a doomed afternoon gets remembered as a kind of victory because the losers "never stopped being who they were," schoolchildren get trotted out to recite something, and of course there's a gift shop announcement near the end. The minister can even be a distant descendant of one of the losing lords, which he mentions once, fondly, without noticing it might matter.

The idea is that someone who knows how real lost-cause mythmaking works would recognize every single move in the speech, but the speech itself never winks or breaks character, it just commits fully to being a tourism speech. Please no headings, bullet points, bold text, or anything like that — continuous prose, the way an actual speech would be written out — and use American English spellings throughout. Also dont make the minister secretly sympathetic or have him slip and reveal he knows it's all inflated, that would be too easy, I want it deadpan all the way down.
