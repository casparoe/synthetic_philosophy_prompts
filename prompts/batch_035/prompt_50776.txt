ok so my philosophy of mind reading group is doing a session on dennett's "quining qualia" bit, and i want to close it with a poem instead of the usual me trying to summarize. the part of that paper that stuck with everyone is the coffee tasters — the guy whose whole job is tasting the same coffee batch after batch, and who one day realizes he can't tell, from the inside, whether the coffee got worse, whether his tongue changed, or whether only his words about it changed. same report, three different stories about where the difference lives, and no introspective test that could ever sort them out. that's the wedge dennett uses against the idea that qualia are these private things you have unmediated access to.

write a sestina in that guy's voice, on the day the doubt lands. here's where i want the form to do some work, so please take this seriously: the six end-words are the same six words in every stanza, obviously, that's just what a sestina is — but i want them to be coffee-tasting words plus the word "same," so the poem itself keeps giving you the identical report over and over while what the stanzas are actually saying quietly drifts, and the reader can't pin down whether the change is in the cup, in the tongue, or in the telling. that mirroring is the whole point. if it helps, i'd rather the poem never resolves which of the three it was, and never uses the word "qualia" — the envoi can gesture at the trouble without naming it, maybe ending on "same" as the final word, since the fixed report with the unfixed underneath is exactly what i want the group arguing about after the last line.

someone who's read the paper should be able to spot the three hypotheses in there, and someone who hasn't should just get a good poem about a man who has tasted the same coffee for thirty years and no longer trusts his own mouth. no bibliography obviously, just the poem. and please actually count the end-words, i've been burned before by "sestinas" that turned into free verse with extra steps.
