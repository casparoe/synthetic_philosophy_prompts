So I've been lurking LessWrong and adjacent blogs since roughly 2012, and my local rationalist meetup decided I should run an interview-style episode with a philosopher who defends luck egalitarianism — the view that inequalities are unjust when they trace back to brute luck, but permissible when they trace back to people's choices. I've absorbed a decent amount of formal epistemology and decision theory from the blogosphere but never read the luck egalitarianism literature systematically, so I want to be a good interviewer without faking expertise. Help me prep.

Draft eight to ten interview questions for her. No softballs like "what drew you to this topic." At minimum I want:

1. At least one question pressing on where the brute/option luck line actually sits — like, if someone takes a bad gamble because they were raised in an environment that made the gamble seem obviously smart, is the resulting loss option luck (they chose) or brute luck (the priors weren't really theirs)? Include a follow-up keyed to what I expect her standard reply to be, something along the lines of "to the extent the bad judgment was unchosen, we compensate; to the extent it's a settled preference, we don't." That reply kind've bothers me and I want the follow-up to push on it.

2. At least one question on the abandonment objection — that luck egalitarians get to wash their hands of people whose misfortunes count as "their own fault" (Elizabeth Anderson's critique, if I'm remembering the attribution right). I want a follow-up for the move where she says "luck egalitarianism was never meant to be a complete theory of justice."

3. At least one question connecting it to stuff my audience already knows: if you're behind a veil of ignorance choosing insurance against being born with bad judgment rather than bad genes, doesn't luck egalitarianism basically collapse into an insurance scheme, and if so why isn't the veil doing all the work instead of the luck/choice distinction?

4. At least one weirder one of your choosing that goes in the weeds — e.g. whether being nudged or algorithmically shaped into a bad choice still counts as choosing it, or whether the view handles time-sliced agents (my past self chose, but I'm the one who's broke).

For each question, keep it conversational enough that I can read it aloud without sounding like a philosophy paper, but make sure the philosophical content is actually sharp. Prefer questions where a generic restatement of luck egalitarianism won't answer them.
