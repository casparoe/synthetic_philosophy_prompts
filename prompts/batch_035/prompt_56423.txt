Two probability theorists have been arguing for years about the Judy Benjamin problem introduced by Bas van Fraassen: a soldier on patrol is lost in war-games territory divided into a Blue Army region and a Red Army region, each subdivided into a Headquarters Company Area and a Second Company Area. Suppose her prior is uniform over the four areas. A radio operator then informs her that, if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that she is in the Red Second Company Area rather than the Red Headquarters Company Area, saying nothing about what holds if she is in Blue territory. One camp holds that the message, together with her prior and requirements of coherence and minimal change, determines a unique rational posterior — via a rule such as minimum cross-entropy or some principled competitor. The other camp holds that the announcement constrains only the conditional odds within Red territory and is otherwise too unspecific to determine any particular redistribution over the four areas, so that any determinate rule silently encodes extra assumptions. Draft the joint statement these two camps could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should contain: the claims about the case both accept (including what Judy may not conclude — for instance, that she is in Red territory, or that her probability of being in Blue territory must stay unchanged at one half — and why Jeffrey conditioning on a partition does not straightforwardly apply); the precise residual disagreement, each formulation phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; the cruxes — among them whether the message is best read as a Stalnaker-style conditional to be accommodated by an imaging-style update, or merely as a constraint on conditional odds, and whether rationality can underdetermine a posterior at all; and, for each crux, a concrete test both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance — such as how rival rules behave under successive announcements, or whether a rule commutes with subsequently learning that she is in Red territory. Format the output as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
