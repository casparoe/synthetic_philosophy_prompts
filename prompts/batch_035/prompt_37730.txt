I'm in a metaphysics reading group and this week we did F.H. Bradley's Appearance and Reality (1893), chapter III, "Relation and Quality." I mostly followed it, but a couple sentences near the start have me completely stuck. He writes:

"The arrangement of given facts into relations and qualities may be necessary in practice, but it is theoretically unintelligible. The reality, so characterized, is not true reality, but is appearance."

and then a bit later:

"Our conclusion briefly will be this. Relation presupposes quality, and quality relation. Each can be something neither together with, nor apart from, the other; and the vicious circle in which they turn is not the truth about reality."

So I get that he's unhappy with carving the world up into things standing in relations, but I can't reconstruct the actual argument. Why exactly does he think relations presuppose qualities AND qualities presuppose relations, and how is that supposed to be a vicious circle rather than just two dependencies that cancel out into an innocent interdependence? Like, plenty of concepts seem co-dependent without being incoherent -- you can't explain "left" without "right". And then someone in the group said this circle is what spins off into the regress people call Bradley's regress, where you need a further relation to tie a relation to its relata, and then another relation to tie THAT one, forever. Are the circle argument and the regress argument literally the same argument, or two separate moves? Because the regress I can at least picture, while the "neither together with, nor apart from" bit reads like gibberish to me.

Also our TA mentioned in passing that some people think the regress is benign rather than vicious -- it never terminates, but nothing is left unexplained at any stage, so who cares -- and other people think the regress gets going even for facts, like the proposition that Socrates is wise, so that no fact can be built out of an object, a property, and a relation between them. Can you untangle which of these is supposed to be Bradley's actual target, and whether the "benign regress" reply is any good against him? And if the regress does work, what does Bradley think reality is like instead -- I gather it's something about everything being one big non-relational whole, but that part of the seminar kinda went over my head.

Bonus points if you can put the argument into premise-conclusion form so I can stare at which premise I actualy want to reject.
