I'm giving a colloquium in three weeks defending what I suspect is a minority report in my department: that Gödel's ontological argument is a genuinely interesting piece of philosophy, not just a curiosity that a great logician tinkered with late in life and everyone politely ignores. The conclusion "necessarily there exists a God-like being" is, on my view, the least interesting part.
