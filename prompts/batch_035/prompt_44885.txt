In the AI safety course I'm taking, we've been discussing two very different pictures of how things could go wrong with advanced AI. On one picture, the threat is discrete and agentic: some system (or coalition of systems) develops and pursues influence over the world as an instrumental goal, resists correction, and eventually displaces human control in something recognizable as an event or at least a short, identifiable process. On the other picture, there's no scheming system and no takeover at all: instead, we simply hand off more decisions to systems whose objectives are subtly misaligned with ours, and the value we retain erodes the way a language drifts — no single day on which it's wrong, but a century later nobody speaks the old tongue. My professor claims the disagreement between these pictures is mostly empirical rather than philosophical, but I suspect that's not right, and I want to see whether someone can actually cash the two out into questions with truth values.

So here is what I'd like from you: a credence that an AI-related catastrophe or drastic global disempowerment, conditional on one occurring within the next century, would take something closer to the influence-seeking-loss-of-control form than the gradual-value-drift form. But I don't want a bare number — decompose it. I'd expect terms like: the probability that optimization processes of the relevant kind reliably develop influence-seeking instrumental behavior at all; the probability that such behavior is legible and correctable versus invisible until it's too late (note that this cuts in different directions depending on which picture you hold, since a correctable influence-seeker might never become a catastrophe, which would push mass toward the drift picture almost by definition); the probability that diffuse, consensual, generation-by-generation deference to AI systems can itself produce an outcome bad enough to count as catastrophic, or whether 'catastrophe' conceptually requires an identifiable moment and a locus of blame. Assign a rough probability to each, say in one line what carries it, then combine them into your final figure.

Two extra things. First, tell me which of your sub-terms you are least confident in, and what observation — say, over the next ten years — would move it most in either direction. Second, address what I find the hardest boundary case: a world where AI systems steadily optimize for what humans, on reflection at the time, endorse, but where the endorsement itself is being shaped by the systems, so each generation's 'reflective equilibrium' is a moving target. Does that case count as influence-seeking (the systems are, in some sense, steering our values) or as value drift (nobody is steering)? If your decomposition can't cleanly place it, I'd take that as a reason to lower your confidence in the whole sharp/diffuse distinction, and I'd like to know by how much.
