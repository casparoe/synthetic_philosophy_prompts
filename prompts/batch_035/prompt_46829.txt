I'm grading a problem set for a graduate seminar on formal epistemology, and I'd like a second opinion on one submission. The exam question read as follows:

"Alice has a prior credence function P over a finite algebra of weather propositions, including R (rain), S (snow), and B (the barometer reads 'storm'). Through a vague feeling of humidity rather than any observation she can articulate, she becomes 70% confident of R without becoming certain of anything. (a) State Jeffrey's updating rule as it applies here, and state the rigidity condition; what kind of assumption is rigidity, and does it need independent justification? (b) Suppose instead that her uncertain evidence makes her 70% confident of the disjunction R∨S. Does Jeffrey's rule still apply, and if so, with what partition? (c) After the soft update, she looks at the barometer and becomes certain of B. Does the order of the soft and the hard update affect her final credences? (d) Could her soft update have been reproduced by ordinary conditioning on some proposition of her original probability space?"

The student answered:

"(a) Jeffrey's rule says P_new(H) = 0.7·P(H|R) + 0.3·P(H|¬R) for every H in the algebra. The rule is licensed by rigidity: the credences conditional on R and on ¬R are unaffected, so only the weights on the two cells change. Rigidity needs no independent justification here: since the evidence bears directly on R, only the probabilities of R and ¬R can shift, while everything conditional on them stays put. Note that if the new value of R were 1, the rule reduces to ordinary conditioning, so Jeffrey conditioning is a genuine generalization of Bayes' rule. (b) Yes, the rule still applies. We take the partition {R∨S, ¬(R∨S)} and set P_new(H) = 0.7·P(H|R∨S) + 0.3·P(H|¬(R∨S)), so every proposition's new credence is a mixture of its old credences conditional on rain-or-snow and on its negation. (c) The order does not matter. Jeffrey conditioning is built to mesh with ordinary conditioning: whether Alice first becomes 70% confident of rain and then observes B, or first observes B and then becomes 70% confident of rain, she ends up with the same final credences, since rigidity guarantees that the two updates commute. (d) Yes. There must be some proposition E in her original space — whatever the humid-feeling evidence ultimately amounts to — such that conditioning on E would raise R's probability to exactly 0.7 and shift every other proposition's probability in exactly the way the soft update does; Jeffrey's rule is best seen as a bookkeeping device for cases where we cannot identify E."

Please assign the answer a grade on a 0–10 scale and justify it with point-by-point comments on parts (a) through (d), indicating for each whether the reasoning is correct and, where it is not, identifying precisely which step fails and why. Where the student says something correct but easily mistaken, say so explicitly, so I can calibrate partial credit.
