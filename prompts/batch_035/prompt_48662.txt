My uncle Frank was a paramedic for like 30 years, and now that he's retired he has VERY strong opinions about when a person is actually dead. He's not religious about it or anything, he just thinks most people are sloppy about the question. Over the years here's basically his track record as far as I can piece it together:

- ICU patient on a ventilator, no brainstem reflexes at all, nothing on the scans, two doctors confirmed it days apart — dead, no question. His words were along the lines of "that's a body they're keeping warm, the man is gone." And he's fine with taking that guy's organs.
- His own dad: heart attack at home, the crew worked on him for 40 minutes, never got a rhythm back, called it at the scene — dead, obviously, and nobody did any kind of brain test. Frank says that's how most deaths actually go and nobody fusses about it.
- A Schiavo-type case: woman in a permanent vegetative state after a stroke, eyes open, sleeps and wakes, breathes on her own, but never coming back — alive, per Frank. Taking her organs would be killing her, in his book.
- A guy resuscitated after ~10 minutes down, badly brain damaged, in rehab, occasionally seems to track you with his eyes — alive, obviously.

So he's not running some simple "consciousness = life" thing, fine. But at Thanksgiving the argument moved onto two new cases and I couldn't get a straight answer out of him, and now it's bugging me. I want to figure out what rule he's actually running before I push him on it.

Was he dead at the two minute mark? Because I've read that his whole brain can't be physically destroyed by then — that takes longer. So if "dead" means "whole brain done," the organs came out of a man who wasn't dead yet. But if "dead" means "circulation stopped and nobody's going to restart it," then he was dead right at two minutes. Which is it for Frank?

New case 2, the plainer home version. Grandma's heart stops at home, everyone agreed beforehand that there'd be no resuscitation, so nobody does anything. Is she dead the moment the heart stops and it's settled no one's restarting it? Or a few minutes later, when the brain actually dies? Frank's dad got 40 minutes of CPR — they tried.
