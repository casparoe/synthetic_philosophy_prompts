Retired physicist here, reading philosophy of math for fun in my old age. Something keeps bugging me that I don't have a word for. In my career the same handful of mathematical objects showed up everywhere -- harmonic oscillators, group representations, variational principles -- and often the math was invented decades before anyone dreamed it would describe anything real, and then it just did, to ten decimal places. But when I read quantitative work in ecology or economics, the math feels totally different: every model is local, it works on the dataset it was built for, gets patched with case-specific corrections, and nobody in those fields has ever been shocked that some piece of pure mathematics turned out to describe their subject unprompted. Everyone quotes Wigner about the effectiveness part, but the thing I want a handle on is the asymmetry: math is ridiculously effective in physics and only grind-it-out effective everywhere else. Does that asymmetry itself have a name, or work on it specifically rather than just the physics half? If not, coin a term and tell me where the edge cases sit -- I genuinely can't decide whether chemistry falls on the physics side of the line or the economics side.
