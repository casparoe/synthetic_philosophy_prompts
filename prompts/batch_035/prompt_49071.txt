I teach a seminar on the theory and philosophy of history, and before my doctoral work I spent several years as an applied econometrician, so my instinctive vocabulary is that of estimands, estimators, sampling frames, selection bias, model misspecification, and researcher degrees of freedom. Every year we work through the classic objectivity debate: Ranke's programmatic claim, in the preface to his 1824 History of the Latin and German Nations, that the work "seeks only to show what actually happened [wie es eigentlich gewesen]"; Carr's rejoinder in What Is History? that the historian's facts are not given but chosen, carved out by a selecting mind that decides both which facts matter and in what rank they stand; and Hayden White's Metahistory, which goes further and argues that the very form of a historical account -- whether it is emplotted as romance, tragedy, comedy, or satire -- is a narrative choice the material itself never dictates. My quantitatively trained students keep pressing me with the same question after seminar: is this not simply the familiar territory of sampling error, specification searches, the garden of forking paths, and underdetermination of theory by data -- things we were taught to worry about in any applied statistics course? I want to give them a better answer than a shrug, and I suspect the honest route runs through a careful translation.

So here is the assignment I would set you, and please take it as a serious translation exercise rather than an invitation to improvise analogies. Restate each of the three positions in my econometrician vocabulary, as faithfully as the vocabulary permits, and tell me explicitly what the translation does. Ranke as an aspiration to recover a fixed historical estimand with a properly specified estimator -- but then which of his commitments survive the move, and is his emphasis on primary sources, diplomatic dispatches, and eyewitness reports better captured by the notion of a sampling frame, by measurement error structure, or by neither? Carr as a claim about selection of facts and the inescapability of priors -- does his famous point that one studies the historian before studying the facts map onto anything a statistician would recognize, or does the analogy distort his relativism into something more benign than he intended? White as a claim about model class and underdetermination -- emplotment as a specification choice not identified by the data, in the way functional form or narrative structure of an analysis is underdetermined by the evidence. Then give me the residue report I actually care about: what in this debate has no counterpart in the statistics vocabulary at all. In particular, White's claim that narrative form is an aesthetic and ultimately moral choice -- a choice among ways of giving meaning to suffering rather than a choice among candidate descriptions of a fixed reality. If that residue is real, I want your view on which side is deficient: does the econometric vocabulary lack the resources to say something the historians are right to insist on, or does it reveal that the philosophical dispute is less substantive than the historians suppose?

One constraint on how you answer. Please keep scrupulously separate what is settled among scholars from what is genuinely contested, on two levels. First, about the historical figures themselves: specialists dispute, for instance, whether Ranke's phrase was a naive copy-theory of representation or a more modest methodological manifesto about source criticism, and whether Carr's position is better read as soft relativism or as something stronger; do not present a contested reading as the consensus one, and flag where you have had to choose one. Second, about the translation itself: where a mapping between the historiographical and the statistical vocabularies is itself a matter of reasonable disagreement -- where a statistician might insist that specification choice is a technical, answerable matter while White would deny that any such answer exists -- say so plainly rather than smoothing it over, and indicate which of your translation decisions you would defend only tentatively. I would rather end with a clearly marked open problem than a tidy table that pretends the residue away.
