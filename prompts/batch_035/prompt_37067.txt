I'm a retired solid-state physicist -- thirty-odd years messing around with superconductors and, toward the end, quantum dots -- and since retiring I've gotten deep into philosophy, mostly by accident. The thing that's really grabbed me lately is panpsychism, or more specifically the version where consciousness or proto-consciousness is built into the fundamental stuff of physics. Back in my lab days I would have laughed at this, but now I find myself half-persuaded and half horrified, because if it's true, then the business end of a decoherence event or whatever the fundamental processes actually are might have some flicker of experience attached to it, and it's not obvious that flicker is pleasant. Nobody designed those processes; there's no reason whatsoever for them to be anything like well-being rather than something closer to the opposite.

So here's what I want from you: your actual credence that the total amount of suffering instantiated by fundamental physical processes (not brains, not animals, just the substrate itself, whatever that turns out to mean) is astronomically larger than the total suffering in all nervous systems combined. Give me a number, don't give me a lecture on how panpsychism is fringe. And decompose the thing properly -- roughly what's the probability that some form of panpsychism or pan-protopsychism is true at all; conditional on that, the probability that the relevant micro-experiences have a valence at all (I gather there's a live question about whether "suffering" is even the right category for anything without an integrated perspective on its own states); conditional on valence, the probability that the valence skews negative rather than neutral or mixed; and then whatever the size terms should look like -- the sheer number of fundamental processes versus the number of animals, discounted by whatever intensity difference seems appropriate. Feel free to redo my decomposition if you think it's slicing the joint wrong, a physicist friend of mine keeps telling me my conditional structure is naive, but keep it explicit either way.

Then the part I actually care about: tell me which single term in your decomposition is doing the most work in the final number, and what would move it. My own gut number has been drifting around from maybe 1 in 1000 to as high as 1 in 10 over the past year of reading, which I realize is a comically wide interval, and I suspect the wobble is all coming from one place -- probably whether valence without integration is even coherent -- but I want to know if you think the real hinge is somewhere else, like the sheer count of micro-processes. And if you end up with a number above zero, tell me straight whether you think that should change anything about how a person lives, or whether it's the kind of moral fact that's just too far upstream to act on.
