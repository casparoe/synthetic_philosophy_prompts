I sit on my department's appointments committee (seven of us) and I've just spent two hours arguing about our last meeting with my friend Priya, who does social choice theory and thinks we made a hash of it. Our statutes say a candidate gets promoted only if the panel judges both that their research record is excellent AND that their teaching contribution merits promotion. For this candidate, four of us voted yes on research, four voted yes on teaching — but they weren't the same four. Only three said yes to both. When our chair asked for a single up-or-down vote on promotion itself, it came out 4-3 against. Priya says the premise votes are the ones that count, since that's what we actually deliberated about, and the final vote is just an artefact of how the chair framed the question; I'm not so sure, since the final vote is what we're each accountable for. The chair has asked me to write the recommendation memo that goes to the faculty board, and I have to actually land on one: promote or don't. Draft the memo — a clear recommendation, the reasoning behind it, and a serious treatment of the losing side of the argument (whichever that ends up being). Please write it in British English, and don't just name-drop Condorcet at the board — most of them have never heard of him and it'll come off smug.
