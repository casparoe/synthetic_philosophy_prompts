So I've been reading up on futarchy, the Robin Hanson idea where you vote on values and bet on beliefs — voters pick the national welfare metric, and prediction markets decide which policies will actually move that metric, and whatever the market says wins.

Here's what I don't get though. If prediction markets are basically perfectly calibrated — like, that's the whole efficient-markets insight, right, that market prices aggregate everyone's info better than any committee could — why keep the voting part at all? Why not just run one big market on "which national goal should we adopt" and let it decide the values too? The market would be way better than a bunch of rationally irrational voters at figuring out what we actually want, same way it's better at figuring out which policy works. Seems like Hanson is being inconsistent here, keeping elections in the loop when his own argument says markets beat them at everything.

Second, smaller question: since these markets are already out there (things like Intrade and PredictIt on elections and such), can't we just look at their track record to see if the market side of futarchy would work, and skip all the theorizing?

A friend of mine says I'm making some kind of category error here, mixing up prediction with preference, but I don't see it — a market for goals is still just a market, it'll still get the right answer. Can someone walk me thru where I'm going wrong, or am I actually right that futarchy collapses into pure market rule?
