I write about science and ideas for a mid-size outlet, and every year we do a live evening event for subscribers — about 25 adults, mixed backgrounds, 90 minutes with a break, zero math beyond high school. This year I want to do actual infinities: Hilbert's hotel, Cantor's diagonal, and why finitists think the whole thing is a parlor trick. Design an activity where the room actually runs the hotel — people as guests with room-number cards, a front desk, buses pulling up — so they physically do the one-new-guest shuffle, then the infinite bus, then the countably infinite fleet of buses interleaved by a grid, and then a diagonalization moment where they try to compile a master roster of every possible guest list and one guest constructs a list that can't be on it. No lecture up front; nobody hears the word "countable" until the debrief. The debrief has to map each moment onto the real math and take the finitist objection seriously — the game only ever runs finitely many moves, so "we shifted everyone" is a supertask the rules smuggle in, and I want you to be blunt about which parts of the idea no finite activity can show and I just have to assert. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong with 25 tired adults at 7pm and how to handle it. Materials should be name tags, chairs, and cards — no projector.
