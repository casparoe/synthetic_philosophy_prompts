I'm taking a philosophy of mind seminar, and we just spent two weeks on Douglas Hofstadter's careenium thought experiment (it appears in his 1982 paper "Who Shoves Whom Around Inside the Careenium?" and gets a retelling in I Am a Strange Loop). As I understand the setup, a billiard table — the careenium — is covered with an enormous number of small magnetic balls constantly careening around, colliding, and clumping together, and if you squint at a coarse-grained movie of the table, you can make out larger composite balls ("simmballs") whose interactions constitute a second, higher level of description, meant to model the relationship between neural firings and the symbol level in the brain. Hofstadter's guiding question is which level actually shoves which around. I was the only one in my study group who found the analogy confusing rather than illuminating, so I want to make sure I have the physics straight before our next meeting.

Here is what I can't work out: the force bookkeeping. If the simmballs are nothing over and above the little balls, then for one simmball to deflect another — for one symbol to trigger the next — the pattern itself has to shove its own substrate around; otherwise nothing would ever happen at the symbolic level. That seems to require a distinct downward force, over and above ordinary ball-to-ball collisions, exerted by the pattern on the particles that instantiate it. So how strong is that force? It presumably scales with the complexity of the symbolic activity, which suggests that a careenium running a long chain of symbolic inferences should press down on its table measurably harder than an otherwise identical careenium whose balls are merely jiggling at random. And if the same bookkeeping carries over to brains, it looks like we could build a kind of thinking meter: rest someone's head on a scale and read off, from the extra downward force, how hard the symbols are currently pushing on the neurons.

My professor said my question about the magnitude of the downward force rests on a mistaken assumption, but she wouldn't tell me which one, and I would rather hear it straight than be protected from it. So: what is the correct force bookkeeping in the careenium? If there is no extra force from the pattern level, then please explain what is carrying the causal load when one simmball affects another — because it seems to me that without a downward push, the symbols are just riding along on top of the collisions, doing nothing, which would make the symbol level an epiphenomenal decoration rather than the level where thinking happens. I'd appreciate a precise answer, ideally with the actual numbers, or at minimum a clear identification of the assumption I've gotten wrong.
