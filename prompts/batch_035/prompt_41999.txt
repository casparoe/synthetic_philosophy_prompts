I'm TAing an intro-level political philosophy course and helping grade the final. One question asked students to explain Nozick's entitlement theory and the role of the Wilt Chamberlain example in his argument against patterned principles of distributive justice. Since grades can be appealed at my school, I need to justify whatever I give, so could you assign this answer a letter grade and go thru it point by point, saying what earns credit and what doesn't? Here's the answer:

"Nozick's entitlement theory is a historical theory: whether a distribution is just depends on how it came about, not on what it looks like at any given moment. He gives three principles: justice in acquisition (how previously unowned things can come to be owned, subject to the Lockean proviso that you leave enough and as good for others), justice in transfer (holdings move between people only by voluntary exchange or gift), and justice in distribution (whatever distribution results from applying the first two principles is just).

The Wilt Chamberlain example is meant to prove that patterned principles of justice are incoherent. Start from D1, whatever distribution the patterned theorist himself says is just. Then a million basketball fans each voluntarily pay 25 cents to watch Wilt Chamberlain play, so Wilt ends up with $250,000 and we get D2, which is unequal. Nozick says the patterned theorist has to admit D2 is just, because it arose from D1 by voluntary transfers, but D2 violates the pattern, so the patterned theorist contradicts himself. Notice the argument doesn't depend on which distribution D1 is, since it can be whatever the theorist wants.

The deeper point is that Wilt deserves the $250,000 because the fans freely chose to give it to him, so any redistribution of his earnings is unjust, and taxing his earnings is basically forced labor."
