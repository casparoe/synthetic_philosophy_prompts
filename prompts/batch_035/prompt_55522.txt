Quick background so you know where I'm coming from: I'm a retired physical chemist (34 years in polymer research) who got properly hooked on philosophy after retiring, and of all the rabbit holes I've gone down, the one about the ethics of speech has grabbed me most. For my sins I also help moderate the online forum for our regional science society — about 400 members, mostly retired academics like me, with a debates section that gets surprisingly spicy. Lately we've recieved a steady drip of complaints, a couple a month, about posts on immigration and religion, plus one member who keeps insinuating a colleague faked his climate data. Our constitution just says offensive material may be removed, which tells us exactly nothing, and the three of us on the committee have been deciding on gut feel, which I don't need to tell you is bad science.

Here's what I'd like. I've been reading about how the American First Amendment tradition and the European approach carve this territory up differently — the American allergy to viewpoint discrimination versus the European willingness to restrict in the name of dignity and public order, things like Holocaust denial bans and incitement laws that would never survive in the US — and I want that turned into something we can actually run at our scale, with no lawyers involved. Please write me a decision procedure for whether a post comes down: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer at each one, where the procedure should stop with a firm no (leave it up), and where it has to hand the call back to the committee because it's a value judgement rather than something a rule can settle. I want to be honest with myself about which steps are just my preferences dressed up as principle.

Please give it to me as a numbered list of exactly five steps, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion — a question plus its pass/fail standard is enough per step. And please, no citations or reading list, I've already got the books. If you can manage it, fold into a step or two how it handles one of our recurring trouble cases: a member who describes a religious minority as a cancer on the country, but never quite says anything should be done to anyone.
