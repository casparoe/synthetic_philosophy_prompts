Retired physicist here (semiconductors, forty years, don't ask), now reading philosophy of physics for fun. Something keeps bugging me about how the Maxwell's demon debates actually go. Every time someone cooks up a version of the demon that looks like it beats the second law, the defenders never deny the setup — they extend it. The one-cylinder engine looks like a winner until you count the cost of the demon finding out which side the molecule is on. Fine. Then someone gives the demon an unbounded memory so it never has to erase anything, and the reply becomes: well, evenually you have to clear the tape, and that's where the bill comes due. Then someone lets the demon dump its waste someplace cold, and the reply becomes: you forgot to count the entropy of the dump. The law never loses; the ledger just gets relocated to whatever part of the apparatus was left out of the original description. Is there a name for this move — rescuing a principle from a counterexample by enlarging the accounting boundary until the books balance? I gather it's related to Duhem-Quine somehow, but that's about choosing which hypothesis to give up, and this feels different: here the defender gets to decide what counts as "the system" only after seeing the attack. Does this have an established name in thermodynamics or philosophy of science, and if not, can you define it properly — in a way that keeps it distinct from ordinary auxiliary hypotheses, from honest boundary-drawing, and from just moving the goalposts? I'm not after a bare label, I want to know where the edges of the thing are.
