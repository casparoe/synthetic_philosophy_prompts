So my roommate and I keep getting into this argument about AI safety stuff. He insists "alignment" is one well-defined technical problem, like a Rubik's cube you just have to solve, and I keep saying the term is doing like five different jobs at once and nobody agrees which one they mean — sometimes it's "the model does what the developer intends," sometimes "it does what humanity would want on reflection," sometimes it's just "it's not actively evil," and half the time people are talking past each other completely. To annoy him, I want to publish a parody piece on our dumb group blog. Could you write a job advertisement, around 700 words, posted by a fictional alignment research org (give it a blandly aspirational name — "the Harmony Institute" or something you think is funnier) that is deadpan corporate HR-speak throughout?

The jokes should come from the org's fine print quietly taking positions on whether alignment is one problem or several, without ever saying so outright. For example: separate listings for an "outer alignment researcher" and an "inner alignment engineer" who will work on different floors and are contractually discouraged from communicating; a "mission" paragraph that equivocates between the developer's intentions, humanity's reflectively-endorsed values, and plain goodness, ideally within a single sentence; a bullet about "robustness to adversarial examples" with a footnote admitting this might belong to a different department that was quietly defunded. Somewhere include a "how we measure success" section that accidentally concedes they can only tell whether the model passes their evals, not wether it's actually aligned — the Goodhart joke, but never named as such. If you can work in the tension between value learning and corrigibility, or the awkward question of whether alignment research is even meaningfully distinct from capabilities research (maybe the "qualifications" section asks for the same skills both jobs would need), that would be great, but don't cram it in at the expense of the piece reading smoothly.

Two constraints on the voice. First, it should read like it was written by committee — maybe a stray bracketed [LEGAL: please review this clause] or a sentence that changes direction halfway through. Second, absolutely no winking at the reader: the ad has no idea it's funny. Someone who knows the actual debates (the outer/inner split, mesa-optimization, instrumental convergence as the reason the mission statement sounds ominous if you reread it slowly) should feel that the author understands them, while someone who just knows what a chatbot is should still find it a plausibly weird job posting. No real organizations or people named, and definately no footnote citations — the org believes in itself far too much for that.
