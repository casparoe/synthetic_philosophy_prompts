In our team's design review for a conversational assistant, a colleague defended the following position: performative utterances are side-effecting API calls, and constative utterances are read-only queries. On her picture, when someone says "I hereby resign" or "I promise to deploy the fix tonight," they are invoking an operation that mutates a shared state -- employment, obligation -- whereas "the server is down" merely reports it. Each performative, she says, comes with preconditions (you must be the person authorized to resign; there must be a boss and an institution behind the words), and when a precondition fails you don't get a false statement but an exception: the utterance misfires, as she put it, the way an unauthorized request returns a 403 rather than a wrong answer. She was using this to argue a policy point about our assistant: the model should never emit promise-shaped or apology-shaped or warning-shaped output, because a system without credentials, intentions, or an institutional role has no business calling side-effecting endpoints -- it would be like a lambda invoking the payment API with no service account.
