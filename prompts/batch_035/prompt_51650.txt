I'm a senior in high school, and for our philosophy club each member has to submit a written profile of their own epistemological views so the rest of the group can challenge it. Before I turn mine in, I want someone to check it for internal consistency, because I have the uncomfortable feeling that not everything on it can be true at once, but I honestly cannot see where the problem lies. Here is the profile:

1. The regress of reasons must terminate somewhere: I cannot actually possess an infinite chain of justifications, and a reason that only ever gets you another reason would justify nothing.
2. Where the regress terminates, it should terminate non-arbitrarily — the stopping point should be a belief I could not possibly be wrong about, not merely one I hold very firmly.
3. I am a fallibilist: I take my perceptual and memory beliefs to be justified even though I know from experience that perception and memory sometimes fail, and I accept that any of them could turn out to be false.
4. Circular justification is never legitimate: if I believe A on the basis of B and B only on the basis of A, then I have not justified anything.
5. Even so, checking a claim against multiple independent sources genuinely strengthens it — two witnesses who agree without conferring give me more justification than either alone, and comparing my memory against a dated photograph beats memory on its own.
6. When two of my beliefs conflict, I resolve the conflict holistically: I revise whichever belief fits less well with everything else I believe, treating my belief set as one system to be balanced rather than a stack of separate cases.
7. Justification flows in one direction only, upward from the foundations: a belief is justified by what it rests on, never by how well it hangs together with beliefs it does not rest on.

Please tell me which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each conflict really is — rank them rather than treating them all as equally damaging. For each, say what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair would cost me elsewhere in the list. I am not asking you to pick the one item I should delete; I want to understand what each commitment actually commits me to.
