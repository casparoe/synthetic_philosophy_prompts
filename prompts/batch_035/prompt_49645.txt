I've basically come to believe that archives are instruments of power all the way down — that what a state or institution records is never a neutral sample of what happened, and that the silences in the record are themselves produced by power, so the people who got written about (taxed, jailed, counted, converted) are visible to us only through the eyes of whoever held the pen. On my view, "recovering marginalized voices" from archives is mostly an illusion: you're reading the shape power imposed on them, not them. What would actually count against this? I want to know what an archive would have to look like for the view to be embarrassed — records that contradict administrative convenience, ordinary people documenting themselves in ways that survived without any state sponsorship, whatever. And I want you to confront the worry that the view has quietly become unfalsifiable: every silence confirms it (power suppressed that), every abundance confirms it (power wanted that displayed). If it is unfalsifiable, is that fatal, or is it more like a lens meant to reorient how you read rather than predict anything? Rank the possible defeaters from things a committed defender should concede matter to things they'd shrug off, and be honest about which category is bigger.
