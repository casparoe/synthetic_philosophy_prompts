I'm in a decision theory seminar this semester and we've now spent like five weeks on Parfit's hitchhiker and its cousins (medical Newcomb, the cases where you'd pay in advance to bind yourself), and I've noticed the whole debate has completely stalled. Everyone knows everyone elses arguments, the same four or five cases get recycled every single week, and literally nobody has budged. Quick setup so we're on the same page: you're dying in the desert, you promise the driver a fat reward once you're in town, she only rescues you because she can tell you're the kind of person who'll actually pay, and then once you're safe in town keeping the money looks like the smart move. The camps seem to be: the causal decision theory people, who say that at the moment you're in town not paying IS rational and the earlier intention was just a lucky thing to have had; the evidential-ish people, who say you should pay because paying is evidence you're the type who gets rescued; and a third camp, going back to Parfit, saying it can be rational to intend now to do something later that will be irrational when you do it -- rational irrationality basically. My working theory is that these camps aren't actually disagreeing about one thing: the causal people are scoring single acts at single times, the policy people are scoring whole selves or whole lifetimes, and the evidentialists are maybe smuggling in epistemology about your own future behavior. But maybe that diagnosis is too charitable to everyone and lets them all off the hook. So: diagnose this for me. Is it a verbal dispute about the word rational, a substantive dispute in disguise (like a hidden question about whether rationality is forward-looking or about your whole life), or three different questions being fought over under one name? Ground the diagnosis in what each camp actually says when defending itself, not just a survey. And then be honest about whether anything could move it -- a genuinely new kind of case, a formal result, some distinction nobody has drawn yet -- or whether its bedrock intuitions about what rationality is even for, and my seminar is doomed to go in circles til december.
