Me and a friend got into Hilbert's hotel stuff the other night and I realized I have pretty firm gut reactions to every case, but they don't obviously hang together. So: is there a principle (or a couple of principles) behind them, or am I just being inconsistent? My verdicts, such as they are:

- Hotel with every room occupied; the clerk moves everyone from room n to room n+1 to squeeze a new guest into room 1. Verdict: fine, nothing was created, it's just shuffling.
- Same hotel, but now a coach with infinitely many passengers arrives, everyone doubles their room number, and the passengers fill the odd rooms. Verdict: also fine. Somehow less weird than the first one, even.
- Before any of this I'd have said the hotel was full — "no vacancy" would be true. After absorbing the new guest it's full again. But I also want to say it was never full the way a finite hotel is full, since it could always accomodate one more. I think I'm using "full" in two ways here and I'd like that sorted out.
- Suppose a second, identical hotel gets built next door. How many rooms do the two hotels have between them? Same as the one hotel had. But ask me whether the town just doubled its hotel capacity and I say yes instantly. Those two can't both be about counting, right?
- As many even numbers as numbers: yes, fine, bijection, I'm on board.
- But now a library with one book per natural number, the even-numbered books red and the odd ones blue. "How many red books are there?" — as many as there are books, period. "What fraction of the collection is red?" — half. And I don't think the second answer is just me being dumb. Same as I'd say a jar with infinitely many alternating red and blue marbles is half red marbles, whatever cardinality says.
- All the even-roomed guests check out and the hotel still has exactly as many guests as before: yes. Five guests check out and now the hotel has fewer guests and is genuinely less full: also yes, and I definately hold both even though I can't defend the pair.
- Finally, a coach arrives carrying one passenger for every point on a line, so uncountably many. My verdict: this one the hotel actually cannot take, and I suspect it's the first case where "no vacancy" is true in both of the senses from the third bullet.

So what's the principle? My half-formed guess is that "how many" and "how much" are different questions that happen to coincide for finite stuff and come apart at infinity, and that "full" splits into every-room-occupied versus can't-take-another, but I want it said properly. And I want you to tell me straight which of my verdicts has to go if saving all of them would cost too much. One more thing while you're at it: what does the principle say about a hotel whose rooms are labeled by every fraction between 0 and 1 — same number of rooms as the ordinary hotel, or more? My gut says way more, and I'd like to know if my gut is wrong there too.
