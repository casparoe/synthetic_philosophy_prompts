I am preparing a paper on the timing of conscious experience — specifically the debate between what Dennett calls the Orwellian and Stalinesque interpretations of the colour phi phenomenon — and before I engage with any particular theory, I want the specification a theory has to meet. Some background so we are on the same page: when a red disc is flashed at one position and a green disc at a nearby position in rapid alternation, subjects report a single disc moving smoothly between them that changes colour about halfway across, i.e. they report an experience as of something at a place and time that was never stimulated (Kolers and von Grünau's experiments in the 1970s). Dennett contrasts two stories about what happens: the Stalinesque story, on which the brain delays completion of the conscious experience until after the second flash arrives, so that the editing happens before the show is broadcast to consciousness; and the Orwellian story, on which the intermediate colour is genuinely experienced after the fact, but the subject's memory is subsequently rewritten to include it. Write the desiderata: what any adequate theory of the timing of experience must deliver in order to adjudicate or dissolve this dispute — which phenomena it must get right (colour phi itself, the cutaneous rabbit, the flash-lag effect, backwards masking, Libet's results on the backwards referral of sensations, and the more basic matter of how succession and duration can be experienced at all), which distinctions it must respect (between when an experience occurs and when it seems to have occurred; between revising an experience and revising a memory of an experience; between experienced time and neural time), and which structural constraints it must obey (no backwards causation in the production of a report or action, compatibility with rapid online behaviour such as catching a ball, consistency with what is known about the physiology of perception). For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it and state whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp — I particularly want your view on whether Dennett's demand that a theory posit no 'Cartesian theatre' is a genuine constraint on all comers or a theoretical prejudice. Then flag any pair or subset of the desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied: I have a suspicion that the requirement that there be a determinate fact of the matter about whether the Orwellian or Stalinesque story is true may be jointly unsatisfiable with the causal constraint together with the colour-phi data, and if so the list itself amounts to a result, which I want made explicit. Finally, in no more than a paragraph each, indicate how Dennett's Multiple Drafts model, Libet's time-on theory, and a trajectory-estimation view of the sort Rick Grush has proposed fare against the list — but keep the weight on the criteria, not the scoring. A table is acceptable for the list itself, though the reasoning behind each requirement should be in prose. Please write in British English.
