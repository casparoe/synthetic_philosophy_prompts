Act as a philosopher of physics with a solid background in thermodynamics and the theory of computation. Write an essay on the following controversy: does the standard information-theoretic resolution of Maxwell's demon genuinely explain why the demon cannot work, or does it smuggle in the second law rather than establish it? The standard story runs roughly as follows: for the demon to sort molecules, it must measure and record their states; any finite memory must eventually be erased; and erasing one bit necessarily dissipates at least kT ln 2 of heat into the environment (Landauer's principle), so the entropy the demon appears to remove from the gas is merely relocated, not destroyed. A sceptical line replies that this is circular: Landauer's principle is itself derived from the second law, or from statistical-mechanical assumptions equivalent to it, so the purported resolution assumes exactly what it was meant to prove, and amounts at best to bookkeeping rather than an independent explanation. Weigh both sides carefully. In doing so, consider what a physical demon would actually have to be made of, whether microscopic demons might fail for reasons having nothing to do with information (for instance, a one-way trapdoor so small that it fluctuates thermally along with the molecules it is meant to deflect), and whether the defender of the standard story can reply that, once the second law is formulated information-theoretically, it becomes the deeper principle from which everything else — including the fate of any would-be demon — follows. Reach a definite verdict: either the information-theoretic resolution is explanatory in a non-circular sense, or it is not, or it is explanatory only under a specific construal of what counts as an explanation — say which, and defend the claim. Before writing, brainstorm the main considerations on each side and set out a brief plan. End with a paragraph on what would most plausibly change your mind about the verdict and where your reasoning is most vulnerable. Write in British English throughout.
