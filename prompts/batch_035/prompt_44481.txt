Write me a parody piece — a manufacturer's warranty / terms-of-service document, about 700 words, numbered clauses, from a fictional company that sells rigid designators. Like you buy a name from them and it's guaranteed to designate the same object in every possible world where that object exists. The jokes have to come from the actual machinery of the new theory of reference, so someone who knows the literature laughs instead of winces, while someone who doesn't can still read it as an absurdly over-lawyered warranty. The fine print should quietly take positions, roughly like this, but you can rearrange: a clause stating that failure to designate in worlds where the referent doesn't exist is not a defect and does not void coverage; a clause on the necessary a posteriori, where once the customer discovers an identity like Hesperus being Phosphorus, the identity turns out to have been true all along in every possible world and the company is not liable for the customer's earlier ignorance or for the necessity being retroactive; a contingent-a-priori clause involving reference fixed by pointing at a physical standard (the old Paris meter bar or something like it), where the act of fixing reference is covered but whether the standard itself holds up is explicitly the customer's problem; a clause on causal chains of transmission, where names acquired through hearsay refer to whoever the chain actually terminates in, even if every single belief the customer holds about the bearer is false — I'd love a Gödel-and-Schmidt style example in there; and an exclusion stating that names introduced purely by definite description are sold as-is, descriptivist use voids the warranty. If you can work in a dispute-resolution clause specifying which possible world disputes are settled in, great. Deadpan throughout — never explain the joke, never wink. No need to name-drop any actual philosopher, and please don't put anything in quotation marks that purports to be a real quote, I don't want fabricated quotes floating around. Also fine to include one of those "what this warranty does not cover" sections listing things like fictional names, names of contingent artifacts whose origin the customer has wrong ideas about, etc — your call on what else belongs in there. Its not supposed to read like a philosophy paper wearing a costume, its supposed to read like a warranty that happens to have metaphysical consequences it is completely unbothered by.
