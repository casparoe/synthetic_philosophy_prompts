My friend and I have been going back and fourth on this and I've talked myself somewhere I don't want to be. It started with me noticing that the stuff I call "pleasure" doesn't feel like one thing at all. Scratching a mosquito bite, finishing a crossword, getting a text back from someone I like, sitting in a hot bath -- describe what any of these feel like and you end up describing the itch, the clue, the person, the heat. There's no leftover feeling that shows up in all four once you subtract the specifics. Fine. But then my friend pressed on it and we ended up with an argument neither of us can get out of:

(1) Hedonism -- the idea that a good life is just one with more pleasure and less pain in it -- only makes sense if "pleasure" names one single thing, whatever it is.
(2) The best way to find out what pleasures have in common is to look inward at the experiences themselves.
(3) When you look inward, the things we call pleasures share no felt quality -- every one is just its own texture (the sweetness, the relief, the thrill) plus our saying "that was nice" afterward.
(4) If things share nothing but our applying the same word to them, then the word doesn't pick out anything real.
(5) So there is no such thing as pleasure.
(6) And if there's no pleasure, hedonism isn't even false exactly, it's not about anything -- like a theory that the best life is the one with the most zorple in it.
(7) Worse, every claim about something being "good for you" or "enjoyable" goes down the same hole, since they all lean on this one word.

The conclusion feels like too much to me. But I've gone through the steps one by one and I beleive all of them?? My friend thinks (5) is just right and that we're basically meat that says "yum" at things. I'd rather keep (1) and (3) if I possibly can, because they're the ones I actually checked. Which step is the weak one, and what do I have to swallow if I give it up?
