"Many philosophers claim that special relativity supports the block universe (eternalism), via the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. (a) Reconstruct the argument as clearly as you can. (b) Present the strongest objection to it that you know of. (c) Assess whether the argument survives that objection."

And here's what the student wrote:

"(a) Special relativity says simultaneity is frame-relative. Take two observers passing each other — the Andromeda setup, where one of them has the alien fleet still deliberating in their 'now' while the other already has it launched. They disagree about which distant events are simultaneous with the moment of passing. Putnam adds the principle that whatever is real for an observer is real, period: no frame's 'now' gets to be the metaphysically privileged one. Since simultaneity within a frame is transitive, chaining observers together passes realness along until every event — past, present, and future — is equally real. That's the block universe, and it comes straight from the physics, not from any prior metaphysics.

(b) The strongest objection is Gödel's. Gödel pointed out that the 'real for' relation, once it's built out of frame-relative simultaneity, isn't transitive when you mix observers from different frames. So the chain that's supposed to spread realness across the whole manifold never gets started, and the argument collapses at step one.

(c) The objection fails. Whatever the relation does across frames, within any single frame simultaneity is an equivalence relation, and the argument only ever needs pairwise comparisons between neighboring observers — each comparison is legitimate within its own frame — so transitivity gets restored where it matters. And the conclusion is momentous: special relativity shows the future is fully determined. Every future event is as fixed and causally necessitated as the past, so open futurism is refuted by physics, and libertarian free will goes with it. The argument survives; determinism is just the price of accepting relativity."
