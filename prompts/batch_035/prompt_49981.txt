I write about science and ideas for a general audience, and I'm working on a piece about whether history gets "locked in" -- the path dependence question, whether the same outcomes would have arrived anyway. The trouble is the famous examples bundle two different things: the outcome could easily have been otherwise, AND once it happens you can't get out. I want a thought experiment that pries those apart, and it should be something mundane -- one organization, not a whole civilization.

So: build me a scenario set in one workplace -- say a newsroom or a hospital picking some internal standard (a content system, a records format, I'll leave it too you) -- and give it two variants. Variant A: the outcome is deeply contingent, small early stuff decides it, but exit is cheap -- anyone can switch later without much pain. Variant B: the outcome was basically overdetermined, lots of independent pressures all point the same way, but once you're in the exit costs are brutal. One paragraph each, no perfect foresight, no secret conspiracies making it happen.

Then do the analysis part. Run each variant through the two features -- contingency and irreversibility -- and tell me which one actually deserves the name "path dependence" and which deserves some other name (and coin that name, I need it for the piece). Say which feature is doing the work in each variant, what happens to the case if you strip that feature out, and finally how someone who thinks the same outcome would have arrived anyway -- a convergence person -- would attack variant B, and whether that attack actually lands or just changes the subject.

As you go, flag how confident you are in the main claims, and single out the one step in the analysis you're least sure of. I'd rather print hedges I understand than crisp claims I can't back up.
