Hi — quick bit of background so you know where I'm coming from. I'm a retired physical chemist (35 years, mostly spectroscopy), and since retiring I've fallen down a philosophy rabbit hole. Back in grad school in the 70s everyone read Thomas Kuhn, and I remember being struck by his picture of science: long stretches of puzzle-solving normal science punctuated by abrupt revolutions in which even the meaning of central terms shifts, and the people on either side of the shift talk past each other. Lately I've been reading about the philosophy of psychiatry, and I keep running into Ian Hacking's looping effects — the thought that when we classify people, say with a psychiatric diagnosis, the people classified change their behaviour and self-understanding in response to being classified, which changes the class itself, which changes who gets the label, and so on. Hacking apparently took this to mark a deep difference between the natural sciences and the human sciences, and if I've got him right he resisted being read as just doing a Kuhn for the human sciences — though plenty of commentators seem happy to call him exactly that, so maybe I'm mangling him.

So here's what I'd like from you: a comparison of Kuhnian paradigm shifts and Hacking-style looping effects. My working guess is that they are different kinds of instability — Kuhn's revolutions seem to be about an entire community's framework being replaced at once, while a looping effect looks like a continuous feedback process that can run within a single classification scheme, quietly reshaping it from the inside — but I'm not confident that's the right way to carve things up, and I might be flattening one or both of them. And the part I really want your judgement on: think of DSM categories like autism or ADHD, where the criteria get revised every decade or two, and meanwhile parents, teachers, and clinicians get better at spotting the label, kids get identified earlier, the diagnosed population grows, and the next revision responds to that grown population. Is that process best described as Kuhnian paradigm change, a Hacking loop, both operating at different levels, or neither? Please make the case for whichever answer you favor rather than just listing possibilities.
