Draft the joint statement that a market-oriented climate economist and a committed degrowth advocate could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Their dispute concerns the decarbonization strategy of a wealthy industrialized country over the next twenty-five years: the economist favors a rising carbon fee (reaching roughly $200 per tonne of CO₂ by 2045) with full per-capita dividend recycling and a border adjustment on embodied carbon, supplemented by technology subsidies; the degrowth advocate argues that any price-based path leaves aggregate energy and material throughput intact, and instead supports hard caps on aggregate resource use, statutory working-time reduction to around thirty hours per week, and the deliberate winding down of carbon-intensive sectors even at the cost of aggregate GDP contraction.

I want the following components. First, a numbered section of claims both parties can accept — these should include physical facts about the remaining carbon budget, points about the observed strengths and weaknesses of existing emissions trading systems, and whatever minimal political-economy claims they genuinely share. Second, a disagreements table in which each substantive disagreement is stated twice, once in wording the economist would sign and once in wording the degrowth advocate would sign, covering at least: whether absolute decoupling of emissions from GDP can proceed fast enough under decarbonized growth; whether relative prices can deliver absolute reductions at the required pace without quantity constraints; whether a shrinking economy can sustain political consent and welfare-state financing; and whether "prosperity without growth" is a coherent objective for a whole society. Third, identify the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one party — and, for each crux, the specific case, historical episode, or piece of evidence both parties agree would bear on it, with each party's prediction about that evidence written down in advance (for example, what each expects a sustained high carbon price combined with dividends would do to aggregate energy demand, and what each expects from episodes of involuntary recession-driven emissions declines). Finally, if you judge that any part of their dispute is merely verbal — for instance, whether "degrowth" denotes a planned contraction of GDP or a change in the social objective function away from GDP — say which part and why, and note it as settled in the agreed section rather than carrying it into the table.
