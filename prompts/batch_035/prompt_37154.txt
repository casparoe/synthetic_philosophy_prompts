Write me a corporate FAQ / complaints-handling policy, around 650 words, for a fictional budget airline (call it whatever you want, something like Dialethiair or Paraconsistent Pacific) that has officially adopted dialetheism as its customer relations philosophy. Some of their flights both departed and did not depart. A gate can be simultaneously the correct gate and the wrong gate. Many refunds are both owed and not owed.

The humor has to come from getting the logic right, not from "lol contradictions are crazy." Specifically:

- The airline is paraconsistent, NOT trivial. A single true contradiction in their operations does not entitle every passenger to every form of compensation, and the FAQ should be smug about this — e.g. under their previous classical management, one contradictory departure board legally obliged them to pay out literally everything to everyone (ex falso quodlibet / explosion), which is officially cited as the reason they switched logics. This should read like a genuine corporate humblebrag about operational efficiency.
- At least one bit exploiting the fact that they reject the law of non-contradiction but are still perfectly happy to use excluded middle whenever it suits the bottom line. (That's real: even the standard paraconsistent logics keep excluded middle.) Like, every seat is either occupied or unoccupied when they're checking you in, somehow.
- Something on negation: the policy should carefully distinguish "your complaint is false" from "your complaint is not upheld," because negation doesn't behave classically for them and they know it.
- A section on the customer who files the complaint "this complaint is not upheld" and what the staff handbook instructs them to do. Liar-adjacent, but keep it deadpan corporate — an escalation path, a reference number, a form.
- A clause where they quietly clamp down on inference: passengers are not permitted to chain together "if the flight is delayed then you recieve a voucher" with "the flight is delayed" to demand a voucher, and the policy should give some hand-wavy corporate justification. You don't have to name the actual technical issue (the detachable conditional problem in the standard paraconsistent systems), but a logician should be able to tell that's what's being gestured at.

Keep it deadpan the whole way — no winking at the reader, no "isn't this absurd," the joke is that corporate policy language is already like this and these people are just weirdly rigorous. The airline shouldn't be evil or incompetent exactly; they're applying their logic with more care than most airlines apply anything.

Format it as numbered sections like a real FAQ, with a fake revision number and effective date. It should be readable to someone who knows zero logic, but someone who actually knows the Priest literature should think the satire is fair and not a strawman — nothing in it should be a misrepresentation of what dialetheists actually claim (e.g. don't have them say contradictions are "all true" or that anything goes).

keep it under 700 words for the FAQ itself, and no preamble, just the document. typos in the doc are fine, it's an airline.
