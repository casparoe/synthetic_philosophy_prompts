I'm preparing a position statement for a graduate seminar on Davidson's philosophy of language, in which each participant has to lay out and defend their considered views on radical interpretation and its consequences. Before I circulate it, I want an audit of whether the positions I've committed to can all be held at once. Here is the set:

(1) Radical interpretation is constitutive of meaning: what a speaker's words mean, and what she believes, is fixed by nothing over and above the best interpretive theory of her linguistic and non-linguistic behavior in her environment, where "best" is governed by the constitutive ideals of charity and rationality. There is no further fact about meaning that a correct interpretation could miss.

(2) Truth-chararity is a strong constraint: a correct interpretation must find the majority of a subject's beliefs true. An agent most of whose beliefs are false is not a possible object of interpretation at all.

(3) Semantic externalism, on the strength of the Twin Earth style of case: sameness of internal psychological state does not guarantee sameness of meaning, since what a speaker's natural kind terms mean depends partly on the environment she is embedded in.

(4) In Burge's arthritis case, I side with the externalist: the layman who reports that his arthritis has spread to his thigh means arthritis — his community's term, fixed by the community's usage — and is simply mistaken, even though an interpreter working from his individual behavior alone could charitably assign him a slightly different concept, one covering inflammations of the thigh, under which most of his utterances come out true.

(5) A community of brains in vats, or a village systematically deceived by a Cartesian demon, is interpretable in completely ordinary terms: they speak about tables, trees, and one another, and the right interpretation of them assigns them mostly false beliefs.

(6) Medieval villagers whose central explanatory framework — witchcraft, demonic causation — was almost entirely false were nonetheless fully interpretable; understanding them posed no in-principle obstacle.

Please go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be. For each genuine tension, indicate how severe it is, what the cheapest repair would be, and what that repair costs elsewhere on the list. Please don't simply relay Davidson's own answers — half the reason I'm asking is that I don't see how his usual moves can be made to work given everything else I've signed up for. Equally, don't tell me everything is in tension: I suspect some of these sit together quite comfortably, and I want to know which and why. Conclude by telling me which single revision to the list would clean up the most, in case something has to go before I circulate the statement.
