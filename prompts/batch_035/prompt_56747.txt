Had a stupidly long argument with a friend last night and I can't stop thinking about it. It started with her saying that if her parents had conceived a month later, that kid would still have been her — same parents, different month, so a different egg and a different sperm. I said no way, that would be some sibling-ish stranger who happens to look a lot like her, and she said that's crazy, obviously it would be her, just with a different body — the same way she'd still be her if she'd been born with different hair or a different nose. Then it got wierd, because when I pressed a variant — same egg, different sperm — my own intuition suddenly hardened: THAT one really feels like it couldn't be me, that's my half-sibling at best. But when she flipped it, same sperm but a different egg, I noticed my intuition got a lot weaker, even though biologically that's exactly as big a change. And then she brought up cases like a frozen embryo implanted in a surrogate, or identical twins splitting from the same fused cell days after conception, and neither of us could even say which origin fact is supposed to be doing the "you-making" work there — the egg, the sperm, the fused cell, the uterus, some combo?

So here's what I actually want to know, and I'd rather get a real answer than a label I could google. There seem to be two things going on. One is the underlying view we were fighting about: that some fact about your biological origin is essential to you in such a way that a person formed from a different sperm and egg just wouldn't be YOU — she thinks that's obviously false, I think it's obviously true, or at least I did before my intuitions started wobbling. The second thing is the pattern I can't shake: whenever someone tells one of these stories, whichever origin fact is being held fixed in that version silently becomes the one that feels like "the real me," and the strength of the feeling shifts depending on whether the change comes through the mother's side or the father's side or through gestation instead. Do either of these have established names and literature? If so, tell me what the nearest concepts are and how what I've described differs from each — and if the flip-flopping pattern in particular isn't an established thing, name and define it yourself, with some boundary cases to show where it starts and stops. Like, does someone conceived from the same embryo but carried by a different woman fall inside it, or someone with the same egg but donated sperm? Don't just hand me a term and move on — I want to know what the phenomonon actually is, what it isn't, and where its edges are, because right now I honestly can't tell whether my intuitions are tracking something real or just whichever half of the story got told last.
