My aesthetics reading group has been stuck on the aesthetic testimony debate for what feels like two years now. Quick setup for anyone lucky enough to have missed it: the pessimists say you can't get aesthetic knowledge, or even justified belief, from testimony — if I tell you a film is moving, that shouldn't make you believe it's moving; you have to watch it yourself. The optimists say testimony transmits justification for aesthetic claims like it does for everything else, and there's no principled difference. At this point every meeting is a rerun: cases about wine and novels, the circularity worry (how did the testifier find out in the first place?), and the pessimists' fallback that relying on other people's judgments somehow undermines the value of your own engagement. Nobody has moved an inch. My working theory is that the two sides aren't in an genuine epistemic disagreement at all: the optimists are answering a question about justification transmission, while the pessimists are really defending a claim about what aesthetic life is for, and each side mistakes itself as disagreeing with the other about the same thing. Is that right? Or is there a hidden substantive question both camps are skating around — say, whether aesthetic properties are response-dependent in a way that matters for who can come to know them — and the impasse is a sign the question is ill-posed rather than just hard? I want a diagnosis based on what each side actually claims, plus an honest answer about whether anything (a new kind of case, a conceptual distinction, reframing the question) would count as progress here, or whether we should expect none.
