I'm a longtime reader of the rationalist blogs and forums, and for the last few years a running argument with my friend Maya has been our version of what philosophers call the special composition question: when do some things compose one further thing? Maya is a nurse with strong opinions and no philosophical training, and over many day hikes she has issued verdicts about what exists that I've been collecting like data points. She is not a nihilist (she thinks she exists) and not a universalist (she denies that arbitrary collections compose), so both of the textbook escapes are off the table, and what's left is trying to reverse-engineer her actual rule. She has never stated one; she just rules, case by case, with total confidence, and the cases don't line up in any way I can make clean.

Her verdicts so far, all delivered firmly: her own body — the cells and bones compose one thing, her (yes); her body together with the armchair she's sitting in (no, obviously, she says); six pebbles strewn along a trail (no, nothing composed of them); the same six pebbles after hikers stack them into a cairn (yes, the cairn exists and the pebbles compose it); a murmuration of starlings wheeling overhead (no, there are just the birds, nothing composed of them); a coral head, which is thousands of polyps on a shared skeleton (yes, one thing); her car (yes); her car with her in the driver's seat (no); and a pile of fallen leaves in her yard (no, there is no such thing as the pile, just leaves — even though she happily granted the cairn, which strikes me as a stacked pile of rocks).

Next weekend two new cases are queued up, and I'd like your prediction before we meet. First: a river — specifically, does the water currently flowing through that stretch of river, together with whatever is suspended in it, compose one thing (the river)? Second: a campfire — do the burning logs, the charcoal, and the flames in the fire pit compose one thing (the fire)? Please take the role of a professional metaphysician who has taught the composition literature for years: begin with some quiet brainstorming, then lay out the candidate rules that actually fit her whole track record (my hunch is a stability-or-cohesion rule and an everyday-kind rule both fit, but I can't tell which, and the coral verdict nags at me), say explicitly what each rule predicts for the river and for the fire, note any of her existing verdicts that already strain each rule, give me your best guess for each new case with your reasons, and — most important — tell me what further case, ideally one we could actually encounter on a hike or around a campsite, would best discriminate between the candidate rules, since I'd like to settle this with her once and for all. Please be rigorous: rules she could plausibly be running implicitly, not merely logically possible ones, and flag honestly if her verdicts may be too sparse or too inconsistent to pin down a single rule.
