I'm a physicist by training — my day job is in nonlinear dynamics, and I have taught statistical mechanics — and I have just come away from a long argument with a close friend who is writing a dissertation on Nietzsche. We argued over coffee after his department's colloquium about the eternal recurrence: the thought Nietzsche puts in the mouth of a demon, who confronts you with the claim that this very life, with every event in the same sequence down to the smallest detail, will recur innumerable times, forever, and asks how you would take that news. My friend holds that the cosmological reading is serious — that Nietzsche meant it as a claim about the world — and that alongside it runs a practical reading, on which the thought functions as a test of how one lives. My contribution was to say that this is Poincaré recurrence with rhetorical packaging; his was to call that philistinism. But when I added that the second law looks like it blocks literal recurrence anyway, we found that neither of us could say precisely which condition fails, and that unfinished point is what bothers me.

Translate the whole thing into my vocabulary. Treat the cosmological reading as a set of claims about a dynamical system: accessible state space, phase-space volume, measure-preserving flow, recurrence times, ergodicity, dissipation, coarse-graining, attractors. Show me which of Nietzsche's claims become precise necessary conditions in those terms — finite accessible state space in the relevant respect, measure-preserving fundamental dynamics, and so on — and which get distorted in translation. My suspicion is that the demand that the life recur identical in every detail is where the trouble sits: in a continuous state space an exact return to an initial condition is measure zero, so the claim has to be weakened to recurrence within any finite resolution, and I want to know whether the weakened claim can still carry the weight Nietzsche puts on it, or whether the reading that survives is so coarse-grained it no longer deserves to be called recurrence of *this* life.

Then give me the residue report. Does the practical reading — the test, the demand that one live so as to be able to affirm the eternal return of this life — have any counterpart at all in my vocabulary, or is it pure residue? If it is residue, say whether that reflects a defect in my vocabulary (a physicist simply cannot say what affirming is) or a lack of clarity in the original claim. And there is a question my friend and I never reached: he insists the practical reading survives even if the cosmology is false, since a thought experiment can do its work regardless of the physics. Does the translation support that independence, or does the practical reading quietly presuppose the very same coherence conditions — sameness of life, determinate sequence — that make the cosmological reading doubtful? Finish by telling me, given the translation, which of us should concede what, and on what grounds. No philological detail about publication history, please; treat the thought itself as the object of study.
