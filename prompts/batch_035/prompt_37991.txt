For about a year I've held a revisionist view of dreaming, in the lineage of Malcolm's Dreaming (1959) and Dennett's 1976 response to it: dreams are not experiences had during sleep. On my version, what occurs during sleep is a sequence of brain activity that, in the moments around waking, is rapidly composed into a first-personal narrative; the sense that I "lived through" the dream last night is an artifact of how the memory is written, not a record of an episode of awareness. The only direct evidence anyone ever has for a dream is a waking report, and a waking report cannot, in principle, distinguish between an experience that unfolded during sleep and content assembled at or near the moment of reporting. I find this cleaner than the orthodox view, and I like that it treats the phenomenology of dreaming as a construct rather than a stream.

I would like a map of this position's exposure. Specifically: which observations, experimental results, or lines of argument would genuinely embarrass it; which awkward consequences a committed defender would concede but absorb; and which objections only look threatening because they assume the very thing in dispute. I am aware of lucid dreaming, and I have heard of experiments in which sleepers allegedly answered arithmetic questions from experimenters using pre-agreed eye signals — please tell me honestly how much pressure that puts on the view, and whether it shows that experiences can occur during sleep, or only that some content is generated at the border of sleep, where the view can happily allow it. Are there any predictions my view makes that the orthodox view does not, or vice versa, such that evidence could adjudicate between them rather than being absorbed by whichever view is already preferred? I also want an honest audit of how the position has evolved: as held by its actual defenders it has been qualified repeatedly — content composed at waking, in the moment of report, during the sleep-wake transition — and I would like to know whether these qualifications have insulated it from refutation altogether. If they have, is that fatal for a view of this kind, or simply the price of a constructivist position about experience more generally? Please rank the defeaters from fatal through serious to merely awkward, and be candid about which ones a revisionist could dismiss without cheating.
