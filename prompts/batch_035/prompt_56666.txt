A few years back Warner Bros shelved the Batgirl movie -- like $90 million spent, film basically finished, and they just didn't release it, took the tax write-off and buried it. They did roughly the same thing later with the Looney Tunes Coyote vs. Acme movie, which eventually got sold off for way less than it cost to make. The studio logic is textbook sunk-cost discipline: the 90 mil is gone no matter what, so if releasing would cost more in marketing and residuals and brand damage than it would bring back in, you eat the loss and never let anybody see it. Econ 101 says this is correct, honoring sunk costs is THE fallacy.

But both times the public reaction was furious. People called it an insult to the filmmakers, a waste, said stuff like at least dump it on streaming, it's basically free money. And it hit me I have no idea what theory of waste or rationality that backlash is actually running on. So that's my question: what does the outrage presuppose?

Work through it for me. Does the angry public implicitly believe that money already spent gives you a pro tanto reason to see a thing through -- i.e., the exact thing we're all taught is a fallacy? Or is there something coherent underneath that isn't really about sunk costs at all -- like a view that the finished film belongs to the crew who made it rather than the studio that paid for it, or that the studio's own spending carries information (they judged it worth 90 million at some point, so who are they to now claim its worth nothing?), or maybe something about what counts as wasting a thing in the first place. Maybe the two sides are using waste to mean different things, one about forgone value going forward and one about effort created and then destroyed.

Then give me the actual analysis I'm after: reconstruct the implicit theory behind the backlash, separate what that reaction strictly presupposes from what it's merely compatible with (a few different views of waste or of rationality might all license the outrage), and note whether the studio decision and the public reaction are even disagreeing about the same question or talking past each other. Finally, tell me who is actually being rational here, the bean counters or the angry internet -- if either. If you think the public is on to something, be honest about whether that generalizes to honoring sunk costs elsewhere, or whether it only works because a finished film is a weird special case. Keep it under 450 words. Don't just split the difference and refuse to pick a side.
