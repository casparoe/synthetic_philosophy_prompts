hey, so I help run a little reading group on philosophy of medicine (mostly students, couple of docs) and I want to build a "what do you actually think a disease is?" quiz for our next session. The group keeps talking past each other using "disease" and "illness" interchangeably and I want to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like. So no question should just ask "are you a naturalist about disease?" or "do you think disease is value-free?" — every question has to be a concrete case where the positions actually come apart.

Here's roughly what I want:

Eight to ten questions, each a specific case with two or three answer options (the options should be phrased as verdicts about the case, not as slogans). Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto leaves: Boorse-style biostatistical naturalism (disease = part functioning below species-typical design), Wakefield's harmful dysfunction hybrid, plain normativism (disease = whatever the sufferer or society reasonably objects to), and the skeptical/eliminativist option ("disease" is doing no real work, just talk about undesirable conditions). If you think there's a fifth live position that should be a leaf, add it, but justify why it's not just a subtype of one of the four.

The cases have to include at least these, since they're the ones our group keeps fighting about: a) an otherwise healthy couple who are infertile but genuinely don't want children anyway — do they have a disease? b) someone with sickle cell trait living in a malaria-endemic region, i.e. a "subfunctioning" part by the species-typical standard that plausibly benefits them in that environment; c) an elderly person who is, by Boorse's standard, riddled with "disease" just from ordinary aging, but feels fine and is thriving; d) a person who's deaf, is part of Deaf culture, and considers their condition a difference not a defect; and e) prolonged grief — whether intense grieving a year after a bereavement is a disorder or just being human. You can swap in others (I've been toying with using male pattern baldness and "gaming disorder" too, use them if you think they pull their weight).

Two extra things. First, at least one question should test whether someone thinks disease judgments can be *objectively wrong* — like, is there a fact of the matter about whether the doctors who called homosexuality a disorder in the 1970s were mistaken, as opposed to just having ugly values? Second, after the scoring key, flag honestly which pairs of leaves the quiz can't reliably seperate — I expect normativism and the hybrid will get tangled on most cases, and if so just say which question would need to exist to split them, rather than pretending the quiz does it.

Keep the questions answerable by someone who's never read a word of philosophy of medicine — plain language, no jargon in the question text itself. And it's going on a handout so keep the whole thing under a page. Definately don't turn it into an essay with a quiz bolted on, the quiz IS the deliverable.
