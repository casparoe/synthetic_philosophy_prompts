I've spent over a decade writing about science for a general readership, and somewhere along the way I settled into a habit that has hardened into a conviction. Whenever I have to translate a researcher's causal claim into plain prose — this pesticide causes declines in pollinators, this gene causes disease, austerity caused the excess deaths — I gloss it as: hold the rest fixed in the right way, intervene on the one factor, and the outcome's probability changes. I've come to think that is not a journalistic simplification but the whole content of causal talk in the sciences. Where no intervention is so much as in principle available, I now suspect the sentence is either elliptical for something about a manipulable variable upstream, or else it isn't really a causal claim at all. And I've stopped believing that "if X hadn't happened, Y wouldn't have" adds anything: plain counterfactuals seem to me to borrow whatever content they have from facts about hypothetical interventions, not the other way around.

I'd like to know what would count against this. Not the obvious ritual objections, but the cases that should genuinely worry me, ranked from fatal to merely uncomfortable. A few I already half-see: causal claims in cosmology and fundamental physics, where nothing outside the system is positioned to intervene on anything, yet the vocabulary of causal structure seems to be doing real work; causation by omission and by double prevention, where the "cause" is an absence and it is unclear what one would wiggle; and the preemption cases — the backup assassin ready to fire — where the counterfactual comes out false but the causal claim seems true, which makes me wonder whether my interventionist gloss survives by quietly re-importing the machinery it was supposed to replace.

There is also a structural worry I'd like assessed: whether the notion of an intervention can itself be specified without smuggling causation in, and if not, how damaging the circularity is. I want to know which of these pressures the view's serious defenders would themselves concede as counting against it, which they would absorb by stretching "in principle possible intervention" until it covers everything, and whether that stretching has insulated the position from refutation altogether — and, if it has, whether that is a fatal flaw or just what a conceptual analysis of this kind is entitled to look like.
