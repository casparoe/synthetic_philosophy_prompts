i've gotten myself stuck in an argument about whether consciousness has anything like a "presentation time" — the idea that for any conscious content there's some moment (and maybe some place) at which it gets shown to the inner audience, versus the dennett-style picture where content-fixation just happens at lots of parallel points and there is no showtime, no theater, only drafts that get edited and later narrated. my interlocutor keeps citing experiments at me (the cutaneous rabbit, color phi, postdictive masking, the readiness potential stuff) and i keep citing the same ones back, and i've realized we don't even agree on what a theory here is supposed to DELIVER. so before i read any more papers on the timing of awareness i want the spec.

write me the desiderata: what any adequate account of the temporal structure of consciousness has to get right. i'm thinking it at minimum has to handle the cutaneous rabbit (taps at the wrist then elbow then shoulder get experienced as a sequence of hops traveling up the arm, including locations where nothing was ever stimulated), the color phi case (a red dot flashing here, a green dot flashing there, and subjects report seeing a dot moving and changing color midway — a color they report confidently even though a change was never shown at that midpoint), backwards masking and postdiction generally (later stimuli changing how earlier ones were experienced), and the Libet-style finding that brain activity precedes both the reported intention and the action. it has to say something about whether subject reports are data or noise — some theories end up calling basically every timing report a confabulation, which seems like it costs too much. it has to distinguish, or explicitly refuse to distinguish, the time a content is neurally determined from the time it is experienced as occurring, and it should tell me whether "when did this become conscious" is even a question with a determinate answer or whether that determinacy is an illusion generated by memory.

now the interesting part. i suspect the desiderata split into three piles: (1) non-negotiable, e.g. no theory gets to say the subject in the phi experiment is lying about the intermediate color, since they typically can't tell they've been fooled at all; (2) things only a theater-ish person treats as requirements — like "there must be a fact of the matter about the exact onset moment of a conscious content" or "all the contents of one moment of experience must be co-presented somewhere"; and (3) things only a multiple-drafts person treats as requirements — like "no single place or time gets to be privileged as where consciousness happens." and i want you to actually mark which pile each one goes in and defend the sorting, because i bet some things i think of as neutral are secretly camp badges.

also, here's a desideratum i made up and i want your honest read on whether it's a genuine requirement or whether i'm smuggling in a conclusion: call it probe-relativity. suppose in some phi-like setup you can flip the subject's report about the intermediate color (whether they say it was greenish, or yellowish, or that nothing was in between) just by changing when and how you probe them after the fact — same physical stimulus, same subject, different retrospective question, different reported content. a theory should either (a) explain which report is veridical and why, (b) say both are veridical but about different things, or (c) say there's no fact of the matter until the probe happens. is "must not default to calling whichever report is inconvenient an illusion" a real constraint, or does (c) already hand the game to the multiple drafts side? i genuinely cant tell.
