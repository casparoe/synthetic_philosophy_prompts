I've spent months recording my gut verdicts on Sleeping Beauty-style cases, and I'd like to know whether any single principle captures them, or whether I have to give some of them up. (1) Standard setup — fair coin on Sunday, woken Monday, and again on Tuesday if tails, with amnesia in between and everything told to her in advance: on waking she should be 1/3 on heads, not 1/2. (2) When she's told it's Monday, she should go back to 1/2; told it's Tuesday, to 0. (3) If the experimenters mount a red light over her bed on Monday only, then waking under the red light should raise her credence in heads to 2/3 — I bite this bullet, but it feels like a lot of bullet. (4) If tails means a million awakenings rather than two, I can see the road from (1) to a credence in heads of nearly zero, and I refuse to travel it — though I can't say where between 1/3 and ~0 the counting is supposed to stop. (5) Applied to the world rather than the lab — one hypothesis says a trillion observers exist, another says only a handful, and both fit our evidence equally well — I don't think our existence favors the teeming universe, even though it seems to stand to (4) roughly as (4) stands to (1). (6) I'm untroubled by the outside observer who watches the whole experiment and stays at 1/2 while Beauty says 1/3: two agents with the same background knowledge can rationally disagree about a fair coin. Is there a principle — about what her awakening is evidence of, or about how centered possible worlds are to be counted and weighted — that delivers (1)–(3) and either delivers or principledly blocks (4)–(6)? Please state it precisely enough to compute with, apply it to the variant where tails creates a million temporary copies of Beauty instead of waking her a million times, and tell me what it says about whether Beauty on Sunday night should regard her own Monday credence as rational from where she stands. Finally, be honest about which of my verdicts, if any, the best such principle cannot save.
