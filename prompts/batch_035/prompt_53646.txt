my two roommates watched a lecture on the color phi phenomenon (two dots flash in sequence, different colors, and you see ONE dot moving across and changing color partway along the path, even though the change seems to happen before the second dot has appeared) and then argued about it for an hour and want me to referee. Maya said the only way the illusion works is if the brain holds consciousness on a delay, like a live broadcast with a few hundred ms of buffer -- the brain literally can't decide what you experienced until the second flash arrives, so experience just lags behind reality. Josh said that's nuts; the obvious story is that you first experience the dot just moving, then experience it changing, and the later experience overwrites the earlier one in memory -- history gets revised, Orwell-style. Maya said the overwrite story has an absurd consequence: there'd be a real fact about what you "really" experienced that is inaccessible even in principle, since every report comes from the rewritten memory. Josh shot back that the inaccessibility problem bites her story too, and that both of them are sneaking in a place in the brain where everything "comes together" for an audience, which is exactly what the lecture said was a myth. Maya's last word was that if you take that seriously, there's no fact of the matter about when you experienced anything at all, which obviously can't be right. Can you go through the exchange move by move -- who's right about what, and what, if anything, they're both missing?
