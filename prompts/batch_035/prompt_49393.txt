I'm giving the toast at my grandparents' sixtieth wedding anniversary next month, and I've been struggling with what to say. My grandmother is a lifelong, devout Catholic; my grandfather is a lifelong atheist socialist who thinks organized religion got nearly everything wrong — and after sixty years, each is exactly as unconverted as on the day they married. Neither ever pretended otherwise. The family joke is that they never agreed on a single moral question, yet they built a life together that neither could have specified in advance and neither would now trade back.

The idea I'd like to carry the toast — one my grandfather, a retired economist, will appreciate, and the rest of the family can feel without needing the vocabulary — is that their marriage was a trade rather than a truce: that compromise between genuinely different value systems can be positive-sum, with each party ending up richer in ways they could not have achieved alone, and without either one having sold out. Please write it so that it honors both worldviews, with no suggestion that one of them was humored or quietly converted along the way, and without collapsing into relativism or "all beliefs are equally valid" — they would both hate that. Keep it to about four minutes when spoken.
