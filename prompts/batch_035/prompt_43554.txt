every time the "does the model really believe things" thread comes back around on the forums, someone does the Dennett move: we already say the thermostat believes it's too cold in here, the vending machine wants payment before it gives you the chips, the chess program doesn't want its queen taken, and nobody has a meltdown about that. so, the argument goes, belief-talk was always just a predictive instrument -- the intentional stance -- and asking whether the LLM REALLY believes anything is the same category error as asking whether the thermostat really believes. the followup is usually one of two flavors: either you shrug and accept that beliefs are everywhere (thermostats included), or you say there's no fact of the matter at all and the question dissolves.

I've been treating this as basically airtight for years but lately it feels like the thermostat and the LLM are doing very different amounts of work in the argument and nobody checks the mapping. off the top of my head: the thermostat has like one belief, and crucially the stance is *optional* for it -- an engineer can just give you the circuit description and lose nothing predictive. for a big model nobody has the mechanical alternative, we literally cannot predict it except by treating it as talking about things. also the thermostat's "belief" never gets *deployed* by anything, whereas model outputs look like stancetaking all the way down (the model models US taking the intentional stance toward IT). and I half remember Dennett himself had a later thing about beliefs being "real patterns" which is maybe where the deflationary reading oversimplifies him? like maybe the point wasn't ever "nothing really believes" but something more annoyingly in the middle.
