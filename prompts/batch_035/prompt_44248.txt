Before I take a side on whether the knowledge argument refutes physicalism, I want a specification of what an adequate theory of Mary's post-release gains must deliver — the argument gets argued badly on all sides, and I suspect the real disagreements are about what counts as success. Write the desiderata: which variants of the thought experiment the account has to handle (for instance, a release in which the first colored object Mary sees is a blue banana described to her as ripe, or a Mary who is color-blind rather than color-deprived, or a Mary who grew up in the room and has never seen her own face); which distinctions it must respect (between acquiring new information, new concepts, new abilities, and new acquaintance; between not knowing a fact and lacking a mode of access to it; between what Mary can deduce and what she can imagine); what it must say about whether Mary's stipulated omniscience about the physical facts already includes indexical and self-locating knowledge, and whether including or excluding it changes the argument; and what it owes us about the inference from "Mary learns something" to "some facts are non-physical" — is that an application of a closure principle, a suppressed premise about fact individuation, or a non sequitur? Give the case or consideration behind each requirement, and mark which are non-negotiable and which are prejudices of one camp (physicalists and dualists want different things from the case, and part of the deliverable is separating genuine adequacy conditions from wish lists). Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — for example, whether an account can both explain Mary's surprise and deny that anything she gains is truth-apt, or whether treating her pre-release state as genuinely omniscient is compatible with her being surprised at all. Then, in a closing paragraph only, run the ability hypothesis, the phenomenal-concept strategy, and Jackson's own later recantation against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases, not on the scorecard.
