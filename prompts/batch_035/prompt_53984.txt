The Frege-Geach problem for expressivism, but as a "why has this debate stalled" prompt, written casually:

So I've been in a metaethics reading group for about a year and a half, we did the whole expressivism arc, and I've come out of it genuinely confused about what the Frege–Geach debate even is at this point. Not confused about the problem itself — I get the negation problem fine (if "stealing is wrong" just expresses disapproval of stealing, what does "stealing is not wrong" express, disapproval too?), and I get why embedding in conditionals and disjunctions is supposed to be even worse, because validity of arguments like modus ponens seems to need the premises to have contents that stand in logical relations, not just to vent attitudes.

What confuses me is the state of the debate. Blackburn's quasi-realism tried to handle it with higher-order attitudes toward combinations of attitudes. Gibbard went and built an entire semantics around plans/norms so that moral sentences have genuine contents that can be embedded and reasoned with. Schroeder wrote a whole book ("Being For") claiming to actually solve the problem once and for all by analyzing wrongness as, roughly, being for blaming-for-it, so the logic comes out valid. And yet as far as I can tell nobody on the other side went "oh, ok, solved, moving on." Instead you get the same recurring counterargument in slightly different clothes: every fix quietly reintroduces a compositional semantics with propositional-looking contents, so either (a) the view collapses into cognitivism wearing a funny hat, or (b) there's still some embedding context where the translation breaks. And then Dreier's "creeping minimalism" thing on top: once the expressivist helps themself to deflationary truth and deflationary belief, the view starts looking indistinguishable from the minimalist realist anyway, so what exactly are we fighting about?

So here's my actual question. Is Frege–Geach maybe not the real disagreement at all, just the stress point where a psychological thesis (moral judgment is conative, hence motivational internalism comes for free) collides with the demands of compositional semantics? In other words, is the debate stalled because everyone is fighting a semantic proxy war while the substantive disagreement — about the direction of explanation between judgment and motivation, about whether the conative state does double duty or whether you need belief plus desire like everywhere else in psychology — never actually gets on the table? Or is that too charitable to the expressivists, and there's a genuine technical/semantic question here that just happens to be really hard?

Diagnose the stall for me: is it verbal, is it two questions under one heading (semantics vs moral psychology), is it a fight over starting intuitions where neither side can argue for theirs? And what would even count as progress — if Schroeder-style formal solutions exist and nobody treats them as decisive, what does that tell us about what the objection was really tracking? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not a survey of who said what, and an honest take on whether this thing is resolvable or whether we're all just gonna keep rerunning it forever.
