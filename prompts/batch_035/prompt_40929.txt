My flatmate is a physics guy who took one metaphysics class and now thinks he's solved personal identity. Here's his argument, numbered so you can point at the part that's doing illicit work:

(1) You could not have come from a different sperm and a different egg. Whoever would have resulted from that other pairing would have been your parents' child, sure, but not you. This is the Kripkean intuition and I have to admit it feels right to me.

(2) Which sperm fertilizes the egg is a matter of absurdly fine timing. Conception a second earlier or later and it's a different sperm, a different recombination of chromosomes, the works. Not controversial, just fluid dynamics.

(3) More generally, the actual world is chaotic in the technical sense: any difference in the state of things at one time propagates outward and eventually ramifies everywhere — including into the microscopic details of which sperm meets which egg, and, given enough time, into which potential parents ever encounter each other at all.

(4) So take any possible world in which anything before your conception differs from actuality — the Berlin Wall falls a day later, some butterfly flaps differently in the Cretaceous, whatever. By (3), the causal chain terminating in your conception is disrupted there, and by (1), whoever gets conceived in that world, if anyone, is not you.

(5) Therefore no possible world in which any pre-conception event differs from actuality contains you. Every historical counterfactual anyone has ever entertained with a pre-conception antecedent — if the Mongols had taken Vienna, if the Spanish Armada had landed — describes a world with a completely different cast of people in it.

(6) He actually likes (5) and wants to bite the bullet: he says it means all big historical counterfactuals are secretly about a planet of strangers, and the ordinary feeling that "I would still have gone to my school" is an illusion we get from ignoring the causal plumbing. I think (5) proves way too much and that something has gone wrong, but I cant put my finger on where. Step (4) is where I feel the sleight of hand but I don't know what it is exactly.

He also runs a variant that I find even stranger. Suppose your parents' actual gametes get frozen and implanted two years later than they actually were — same sperm, same egg, different uterine environment, different year, different everything else. He claims the butterfly reasoning says any world that different contains different people, so the resulting child isn't you; but premise (1) says same gametes, so it is you. The two principles give opposite verdicts on this one case, and he treats the clash as more evidence for his radical (5) rather than as a reductio of something upstream. I suspect that's a clue but I'm not sure of what.

So: which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? Being upfront about my priors — I want to keep (1), it's the one thing here I feel in my bones, and I also want it to come out that my grandfather could have caught a different train in 1962 and I'd still exist. If that combination turns out to be unavailable, tell me which one I have to surrender and why, and don't be diplomatic about it. And please actually commit — I don't want a menu of respectable positions with the pros and cons of each, I want you to name the broken step and defend the diagnosis, including why that step looked fine to two people arguing about it for three evenings. If you think the argument is actually valid and the real question is which premise is cheapest to drop, say that and pick one.
