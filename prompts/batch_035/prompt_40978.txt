I've been covering tech and science long enough that editors now send me every "how we got stuck with this" story that crosses the wire, and it's gotten me keeping a private list of gut verdicts on lock-in cases. Some of these I'm confident about, others I flip on weekly. So here's the list. QWERTY: genuine lock-in — the layout was (so the story goes) shaped around typewriter jamming, the jamming problem died a century ago, and we're all still pecking at it, so I think a rerun of history plausibly gives us a different layout. Standard railway gauge: locked in, allegedly inherited from cart-wheel ruts, but I also think almost any reasonable gauge would have worked about as well, so I can't decide whether to call that lock-in that "matters." VHS over Betamax: a near coin flip that got cemented by rental stores stocking one shelf, but the destination "a home video format" arrives either way, so contingent at the product level, inevitable at the category level — I like that framing but I'm not sure it survives contact with my next case. Electric vs gasoline cars in the 1900s: this is the one I keep going back and forth on. My instinct is that the internal combustion engine won on real merits (energy density, range), but then I remember that early electric cars were genuinely competitive until a bunch of contingent stuff (cheap Texas oil, the electric starter, roads built for long distances) broke the tie, and every time I try to explain why that case is different from VHS I get stuck. AC over DC: I think that was straight technical superiority, Edison just lost to a better technology, no lock-in story worth telling. And from my own trade: the inverted pyramid news story and AP style, which I write in daily and which I honestly cannot tell anymore whether it's a convention that does real work or pure inertia nobody could defend from scratch.

My friends think there's a principle buried in here, something like: an outcome is "locked in" when a switching cost that no individual can justify paying gets built up by small historical accidents, and it's "inevitable-ish" when some selection pressure would have pushed roughly that way anyway. But when I test it, it seems to say VHS was both locked in and inevitable, which my gut accepts, and that QWERTY might actually have been fine all along, which my gut hates. So is there a principle that captures my verdicts, or close to it — and if not, which ones do I have to give up? I'd also want it to make a call on a case I deliberately left off my list: whether the specific form of something like short-form vertical video was contingent or whether, once smartphones and boredom existed, something TikTok-shaped was always going to show up. Please actually commit to a formulation, don't just list considerations — and be honest about which of my verdicts your principle can't save.
