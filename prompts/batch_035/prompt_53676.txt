me and a friend keep going in circles on whether Malthus was "right but early" so before I reply to him again I want to lay out my actual position and have someone audit it. here's what I'm committed to:

(1) If a theory makes a dated prediction and the date passes without the prediction coming true, the theory is falsified, full stop. If you said population would press against subsistence within a couple of generations and it didn't, you were wrong, and nothing we later discovered changes that verdict.

(2) Malthus's core mechanism is sound: a population whose fertility isn't held down by something will eventually press against subsistance, and historically something always has held fertility down — famine, disease, war, delayed marriage, or lately deliberate limitation.

(3) So the right verdict on Malthus specifically is: right about the mechanism, wrong about which check would dominate, and wrong about the timescale. Like a forecaster who gets the mechanism right and the schedule wrong.

(4) I think "right but early" is a legitimate verdict, not a face-saving one. If the theory was really a conditional all along — "unless there's an energy subsidy unlike anything in history, population outruns food" — then when the subsidy arrives, pointing at it isn't an ad hoc rescue, it's just reading the theory correctly.

(5) Whether a rescue counts as ad hoc depends on what was in the theory before the miss. The rescue that saves is one that articulates a conditional that was already there; the rescue that doesn't save is one that invents the conditional after the fact. And I think we can usually tell these apart.

(6) I also hold that the verdict on a theory is a function only of its predictions and how things turned out — not of what the theorist could reasonably have known at the time. Malthus isn't less wrong for having no way to foresee the demographic transition or fossil fuels; epistemic excuse isn't a third verdict sitting alongside right and wrong.

(7) last thing, more of a hunch: I think the modern collapse in fertility under growing wealth counts as vindication of Malthus's "preventive checks" category, even though the substance of the check (contraception, small families by choice, prosperity itself) is nothing like what he described.

what I can't figure out: (1) and (6) together say the dated miss falsifies, period, and nothing downstream can soften it — but (4) and (5) let the verdict on the miss turn on things like what was conditional in the theory all along, which smells like exactly the post-hoc sensitivity that (1) is supposed to forbid. And is (3) even a distinct position, or is it just an average of (1) and (4) wearing a disguise? also can't tell whether (7) is a real tension with (2), since (2) says the mechanism is sound while (7) seems to say the modern case works through a channel that bypasses it entirely.

go through the pairs, tell me which are in genuine tension, which only look like it, and rank then by severity — I'd rather know the least costly single revision than get a list of everything that could possibly be questioned. and don't be polite about it, my friend won't be.
