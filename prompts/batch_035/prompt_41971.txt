Quick background so the draft makes sense. The microdomain is analogy puzzles over strings of letters. You're told "abc changed to abd" and asked what "ijk" changes to; most people answer "ijl", applying the rule "replace the rightmost letter by its successor". The interesting cases are the ones where the default reading breaks. My favorite: given "abc → abd", what does "xyz" become? The letter z has no successor, so you either wrap the alphabet and say "xya", or — and this is the move I care about — you quietly re-conceptualize "rightmost" as "leftmost" and answer "wyz", letting the whole mapping slide along with it. Changing a concept mid-solve, under pressure, so the analogy survives: that's what this literature calls conceptual slippage.

Here's the draft:

---

**Slippage as repair, not fluidity**

One thing the microdomain makes visible is that slippage never happens when it doesn't have to. Given "abc → abd; ijk → ?", nobody slips. "Rightmost" stays perfectly rigid, because nothing forces it anywhere. Slippage shows up exactly where the default mapping fails: the target letter has no successor, the string has no letters, the obvious answer is barred by the setup. This pattern suggests slippage isn't a general, ambient fluidity of concepts — a lubberly willingness to re-conceptualize at any moment — but a repair mechanism, a last resort deployed when the default hits a dead end.

If that's right, then what analogy-making measures is not how fluid concepts are but how well a system *detects that the default has failed* before anything slips. The slip is downstream of a verdict. The interesting work happens at the moment of recognizing "this mapping is not going to make it", and the slippage itself is almost epiphenomenal — the system's way of cashing out a decision that has already been made.

This yields a phenomenological prediction I find a bit odd but kind of compelling: the *depth* of an analogy should track the pressure it took, not the number of slips. "xyz → wyz" should feel more creative than "ijk → ijl" even though the first involves exactly one slip and the second involves none. And the converse: a system that re-conceptualizes freely, at will, with no resistance, should produce analogies that feel flat — because with no pressure there's no achievement. Depth is coordinated slips that each had a reason. Random flexibility is the opposite of depth.

Maybe the same holds outside the microdomain. Most everyday concepts are like "rightmost": rigid, because nothing forces them. The deep metaphors — the ones that feel like discoveries — might just be the ones where a concept was forced to slip and couldn't dodge. The feeling of profundity is the feeling of pressure releasing.
