Been going through trolley variants ever since the umpteenth LW thread on them, trying to figure out whether my intuitions are anything more than pattern-matching, and I'd like someone to actually do the reflective equilibrium thing on my verdicts. Here they are:

Switch — divert the trolley from five onto the side track with one guy: fine, obviously. Footbridge — push the big man off to stop it: not fine, also obviously. The loop case, where the side track loops back around and it's the one guy's body physically stopping the trolley: fine?? I keep wanting to say yes, and I can hear the double effect people screaming. Transplant surgeon harvesting one healthy patient for five: not fine. Terror bombing civilians to break morale: not fine. Strategic bombing a munitions plant knowing civilians nearby will die: grimly fine.

The one that really bugs me: in one version the big guy is standing on the side track as a mere side effect of my diverting (the trolley would derail on its own from hitting a barrier), and in the other version his body is what stops the trolley. He dies either way, I divert either way, and these feel different to me. I suspect they shouldn't.

So is there a principle that gets all of these? Plain utilitarianism loses immediately on footbridge and transplant. The doctrine of double effect handles switch vs footbridge but my loop verdict seems to torch it. Killing vs letting die never even gets off the ground on the two bombing cases. The usual rationalist move is "your intuitions are confounded by proximity and visceralness, drop the ones that conflict," but that proves way too much — it would make transplant fine if the surgeon does it by remote drone. Is my loop intuition just noise I should bite the bullet on, or is DDE circling something real that needs a better formulation? And whatever principle you land on, tell me what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on: the big man volunteers — he sees what's happening and jumps himself.
