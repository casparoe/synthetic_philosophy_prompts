I'm applying for a one-year dissertation fellowship and need to turn a half-formed idea into a two-page proposal on conceivability as a guide to metaphysical possibility. The seed of the idea: when philosophers claim that zombies are conceivable, or that a world just like ours but lacking secondary qualities is conceivable, they rarely specify what kind of mental act the conceiving is supposed to be. My hunch is that "conceiving" should be factored along two independent dimensions: the mode of representation (quasi-perceptual, imagistic simulation versus purely descriptive, propositional supposition) and the grain of the representation (how much of the scenario's internal structure is actually fixed versus left schematic). My working hypothesis is that the notorious thought experiments get their intuitive force from particular combinations of these factors — for instance, that zombie intuitions trade on imagistic mode at coarse grain, while the classic identity cases involving natural kinds get theirs from descriptive mode at fine grain — and that mismatches between how a scenario is conceived and what kind of possibility is then inferred to can explain away a good number of the standard disputes without a global error theory about modal intuition.

The existing taxonomies in the literature — the one distinguishing prima facie from ideal conceivability and positive from negative conceivability, and the separate suggestion that there are imagistic versus descriptive ways of grasping a scenario — gesture at parts of this, but as far as I can tell nobody has asked whether the two-dimensional space of mode-by-grain systematically predicts which conceivability claims survive scrutiny, which collapse under reflection, and which persist but stop doing argumentative work. That's the gap I want the proposal to build around.

Please produce the two-page proposal with the following components: the research question stated precisely enough to have a determinate answer; why the currently available positions leave it open; the method, spelled out month by month (I imagine something like constructing a matrix of well-known thought experiments coded by mode and grain, formulating predictions about which should generate disagreement or instability, and testing those predictions against how the debates actually unfold, but improve on this or replace it if you see a better route); what a positive result would look like and what it would show; an honest statement of what would count as the project failing; and a realistic assessment of whether one year is enough for it.
