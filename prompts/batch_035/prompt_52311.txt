I'm a PhD student in cognitive science, and the final chapter of my dissertation is on the symbol grounding problem. Rather than ending it with the usual recap paragraph, I'd like to close with a poem. Write a villanelle spoken by a purely formal symbol system: a system in which every term is fixed only by its relations to other terms, so that its knowledge of the world amounts to a dictionary it can never put down. Let the first refrain voice the system's complacency — something like the thought that shuffling symbols is all that understanding has ever been — and let the second voice the doubt it cannot quite suppress: that not one of its tokens has ever touched the thing it stands for, and that from the inside there is no telling a mind from a roomful of rules executed by a clerk who understands nothing. The form should do real philosophical work: the refrains must return verbatim, the way symbols in a closed network only ever meet more symbols, and the rotating tercets should be the system's successive failed attempts to reach something outside itself — a texture, a color, a thing with edges — attempts that keep collapsing back into more words about words. In the final stanza, where the two refrains at last share a quatrain, let them meet without settling anything: end with both thoughts held side by side rather than a choice between them. Someone who knows the literature should recognize the shape of the debate — the definitional circle, the shadow of the Chinese Room, the hope of some bottom-up route from perception to categories — and someone who doesn't should still find a good poem about being stuck inside a dictionary.
