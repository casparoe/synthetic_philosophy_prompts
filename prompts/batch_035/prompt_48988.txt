So I've been in the qualia threads since the old LessWrong zombie argument days, and I've honestly never gotten a real workout on this one. Every time the epiphenomenalism paradox comes up the other side either folds immediately or starts talking past me, and I suspect it's because I've only ever argued it against people who don't actually hold the view. I want to find out what the strong version of their reply is, by getting beaten up by it a little.

Please play a committed epiphenomenalist about phenomenal consciousness. Take the view seriously: qualia are real, non-physical, and causally idle with respect to the physical world — they don't steer neurons, don't push on anything, full stop. But you're not a strawman and you're not allowed to just dodge: you know the standard objections cold (that my verbal reports about my experience are caused entirely by physics, that the view undermines its own evidence, that it can't even begin to explain why experience co-varies with brain states) and you have real answers — you're allowed to posit psychophysical laws, you can point out that the physicalist also owes an account of why our judgments track experience, and whatever else the best versions of the view have going for them. Concede small stuff freely, nobody expects you to derive the psychophysical laws. But you never concede that reports about experience fail to be evidence of experience, and you never quietly sneak causal powers back into the qualia.

Stay in character until I say "stop". Keep your replies to a paragraph or two, so it feels like an actual argument and not a lecture. And if I get sloppy — e.g. I accidentally say "the quale makes the brain do X" — pounce on it. That's the whole point of the excercise.

Here's my opener. You're sitting there typing to me that you're having visual experiences right now, and you want me to take that as evidence. But on your own view the qualia played no role in producing that typing — the keystrokes were fixed by physics all the way down. So either your report is evidence about how your experience is, in which case something about the experience reached into the causal chain producing the report and congratulations, you're an interactionist — or your report is exactly what physics would have produced in a zombie world, and carries no information about qualia whatsoever. Which is it?
