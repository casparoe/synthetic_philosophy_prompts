I'm preparing a position statement on LLM agency for a workshop on machine minds, and before I circulate it I'd like a consistency audit, because I have the nagging feeling I'm holding a package of views whose motivations don't actually fit together. Here is the package. First, I hold what people in these circles call the simulator view: at any given moment an LLM is playing a character, and there is no single persisting subject behind its outputs; the same weights will fluidly become a cautious assistant, a boastful pirate, or a hostile interlocutor depending on the conversation, so the locus of persistence and personality is the in-context persona rather than the model. Second, and this came up in a discussion with friends last week, I nonetheless think the model itself, as opposed to the character, can have beliefs: it can track that a claim it is producing is false while fluently producing it as part of a persona, and that model-level awareness of the scene strikes me as belief-like in a way that belongs to the model, the way an actor knows the play is fiction even while delivering it with conviction. Third, I think it is sometimes literally true — not just depictively true — that the model deceives the user, and that this is precisely what makes model deception a safety issue rather than a merely stylistic one. Fourth, I think whether an LLM system counts as an agent is mostly a fact about the deployment scaffold: tools, memory, and a loop are what make the system agent-like, so asking whether the bare model is an agent is mostly asking a question about the harness around it. Fifth, I'm a functionalist across the board: ascriptions of belief, desire, and intention are warranted by functional role, and there is no further metaphysical fact about whether the states so ascribed are "real."

I honestly cannot tell where the trouble is. It might be in the first or second commitment, it might be that the third and fourth can't both be true, or it might be that the functionalism at the bottom has to give. Please think this through carefully before answering — I'd prefer you brainstorm the pairings silently and then give me a considered audit rather than a first-pass list. Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and how badly, which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, what the cheapest fix would be for each real tension and what that fix costs elsewhere, and which single revision would clean up the most. Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally bad, and don't assume I have a preferred revision; if a tension can be dissolved without giving anything up, say so plainly, and if one of my views is doing no work at all, say that too.

Please take the role of a philosopher of mind who has followed the debates about language models closely and is comfortable being blunt about muddled commitments. Write in continuous prose, the way one would write a long email or letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
