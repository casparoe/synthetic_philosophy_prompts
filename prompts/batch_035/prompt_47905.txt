At a recent seminar afterparty, two friends of mine got stuck on the color phi phenomenon. The setup, for context: a red spot is flashed briefly on the left side of a screen; roughly a tenth of a second later, a green spot appears on the right. Subjects reliably report a single spot moving across and changing color somewhere in the middle — and, oddly, they say the color seemed to start changing before the second flash occurred, as though the experience had already incorporated an ending the brain had not yet received.

(1) Maya said the natural explanation is what she called the Stalinesque one, borrowing Dennett's labels from Consciousness Explained: consciousness runs behind the world. The brain buffers the raw input, edits the footage in the projection room, and only then screens it, so the color change is spliced in before the show airs — which is why it can appear to happen "early." (2) Theo countered that she had the phenomenology backwards: the subject did experience the change at the midpoint in real time, and only the later record was falsified, an Orwellian revision in which the archive is rewritten after the fact. He added that since all we ever have to go on is what the subject says, and the subject only consults their memory, the two accounts predict exactly the same data forever — including the subject's sincere insistence that they saw it early. (3) Maya answered that indistinguishable predictions don't collapse the distinction: whether the edit happens before or after "presentation to consciousness" is exactly what's at issue, and reframing the debate in terms of reports quietly changes the subject from experience to testimony. (4) Theo replied that "experience as it was, prior to any report" is a posit no one could ever cash out: there is no access to the stream except through probes, so his position isn't a rival empirical theory but the only empirically respectable stance, and Maya's demand for a fact of the matter smuggles in a Cartesian theater where the real show happens. They eventually turned to me to referee, and I honestly couldn't. Please go through the four moves in order, say who is right at each step — not merely who wins overall — and identify anything both of them are missing.
