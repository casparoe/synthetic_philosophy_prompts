I'm a Bayesian statistician by training and trade — I spend my days specifying priors, checking posterior predictive fits, and fighting about model class selection — and my philosophy reading group has just worked through Wittgenstein's On Certainty. I understood maybe half of it, and I suspect the half I understood is the half that maps onto things I already do. So: translate the book's central claims into my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then report what doesn't survive the translation.

Specifically, take these claims: that certain propositions — Moore's paradigm cases like there being a hand here, or the earth having existed long before my birth — function not as objects of knowledge or justified belief but as hinges on which the whole practice of doubting and testing turns; that some empirical propositions get "hardened" so that they serve as rules for judging others rather than as candidates for refutation by them; that a doubt that doubted everything would not itself be a doubt; and the river-bed image from section 97: "The mythology may change back into a state of flux, the river-bed of thoughts may shift. But I distinguish between the movement of the waters on the river-bed and the shift of the bed itself; though there is not a sharp division of the one from the other."

Render each in Bayesian terms. Is a hinge just a parameter pinned by a prior with a point mass — a flagrant Cromwell's-rule violation that I've been trained to avoid — or is that already a distortion? Is the hardening of a proposition like moving it up a level in a hierarchy, from something the data can speak to into the hyperparameters and likelihood structure that decide what the data can speak to at all? Is the remark about total doubt an observation about model classes — that updating within a class presupposes the class, so no amount of posterior drift can be a doubt about everything at once? Is the shifting river-bed what happens in model criticism, when accumulated misfit finally forces you to change the model class itself, with no principled threshold for when that becomes warranted? Where you have to fudge to make the translation go through, say so.

Then the residue report, which is what I actually want: which of Wittgenstein's claims translate cleanly, which only with distortion, and which have no Bayesian expression at all. I especially want your view on the claim that a hinge proposition is not believed, known, or assigned any probability — that it has a grammatical or normative status rather than an evidential one, more like part of the language in which the likelihood is written than like anything in the prior. If that is the residue, tell me whether it exposes a blind spot in the Bayesian vocabulary or whether Wittgenstein's talk of a special non-epistemic status for hinges is less clear than it looks and my machinery is doing him a favor by forcing him into probabilities he'd resist.
