If you pick the chord by choosing two random points on the circumference, you get 1/3. If you pick a random radius and then a random point on it and draw the chord perpendicular to it, you get 1/2. Same question, three different answers, so the principle of indifference -- the idea that you should spread probability equally when you have no reason to favor one outcome over another -- is flat-out inconsistent. And since indifference was the only thing that could ever tell you which chord distribution counts as 'the' random one, the whole idea of a probability before you've collected data collapses. Probabilities are just choices we make, which is basically what the frequentists were saying all along, and it also means single events don't have any objective probability, period."
