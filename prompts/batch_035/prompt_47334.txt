I'd like a villanelle on Galen Strawson's Basic Argument against ultimate moral responsibility, written so that the form carries the philosophical weight rather than merely decorating it.

The argument in compressed form: you do what you do, in the situations you face, because of the way you are — your character, dispositions, values, will. To be truly responsible for what you do, you would have to be responsible for the way you are. But you cannot be responsible for the way you initially are: you did not choose your starting constitution, and any later self-shaping was carried out by a self you did not choose to have, so the demand just regresses; the only escape would be to be causa sui, which is impossible. Note carefully what the argument is not: it is not an argument from causal determinism, since indeterminism is no help — a random swerve in the formation of your character is not something you are responsible for either — and Strawson holds that even an omnipotent being that revised its own nature would still be acting from an initially unchosen base, so the conclusion is meant to be a priori and to apply in any possible world.

Let the two refrains be the argument's two steps — the move from action to character, and the move from character to self-creation — and let their alternation and final meeting in the closing couplet enact the regress: the poem should keep trying to get outside itself to find a starting point and keep being returned to the first refrain, so that the convergence of the two lines at the end reads as both the argument's completion and its trap. Somewhere along the way I'd like the two standard escape attempts given their due and shown their failure: the partial self-shaping reply (that you did after all shape yourself somewhat through your earlier choices), which only pushes the question back a step without ever reaching a first step; and the randomness reply, which dissolves responsibility rather than grounding it. I'd also like the poem to acknowledge — perhaps within a single stanza, without surrendering the argument — that its target is ultimate, desert-entailing responsibility rather than the everyday, answerability-style responsibility that compatibilists can keep; the speaker might even be a compatibilist who grants the argument entirely and refuses to be alarmed, or a hard determinist who wishes it weren't so. Someone who knows the Basic Argument should be able to identify each move and say why the refrain structure suits it in particular rather than any old regress; someone who doesn't should still get a good poem about trying to lift yourself by your own bootstraps.

Please give the poem first, followed by a short prose note of a few paragraphs explaining your choices about the refrains, what you assigned to each tercet, and any places where you had to compress or simplify the argument for the sake of the form. Use no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables anywhere — just the poem and then the paragraphs, continuous prose throughout, the way one would write a letter.
