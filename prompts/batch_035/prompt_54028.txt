I'm a functional-safety engineer: my working vocabulary is requirements documents, design rationale records, FMEAs, and the as-designed versus as-built distinction. In my world a component's function is fixed by its requirements — a failed hydraulic pump still has the function "provide pressure," which is why I can write it up as a malfunction rather than as a functionless lump of metal. Philosophers of biology argue over what it is for a biological trait — the myelin sheath, heterozygous sickle hemoglobin — to have a function, and there are two main camps: the etiological account, on which a trait's function is what past natural selection kept it around for doing, and the causal-role account, on which a trait's function is whatever it currently contributes to some capacity of the containing organism. Restate this debate in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, without smuggling in my framework's assumptions and presenting them as translation, and then give me the residue report. Which parts of the debate map onto my distinction between design intent and operational role? Where does the translation distort — for instance, my requirements have authors and selection has none, and I can't see what would count as a design history file for an organism? Most importantly: can the notion of malfunction, on which my entire practice rests, be expressed at all in a purely causal-role vocabulary, where function is fixed by what a part actually does now? For each piece of residue, tell me whether it points to something my engineering vocabulary cannot say or to something in the philosophers' dispute that is less substantive than it looks.
