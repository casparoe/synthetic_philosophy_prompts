For the past three years I have been one of the volunteer moderators of an online gaming community of about forty thousand members, and last year we moved most of the routine enforcement to an automated system that applies our escalation ladder — warning, one-day mute, seven-day mute, permanent ban — based on reported messages. Writing and repeatedly tuning that policy is what got me thinking about machine ethics in general: not the movie version, but the boring, practical version — how anyone would ever build a system that has to make the kinds of judgment calls we make every week, when the letter of the rules and the right answer come apart. I have no philosophy training; everything I know about this I worked out on my own, so please write for a smart layperson with no assumed background.

Before I go looking at what researchers have said on the subject, I want the specification first: what does any approach to putting ethical reasoning into a machine have to deliver before it even counts as a candidate? Give me exactly five numbered requirements, each a sentence or two, with no introduction and no conclusion — each item should state the requirement itself plus the case or failure mode that makes it a requirement. So you know what I mean by the problem, here are the situations that keep me up at night: members learning the exact wording of our rules and gaming them while technically complying; cases where applying the written policy to the letter produces an outcome everyone on the team agrees is unjust; situations where two things we care about genuinely conflict and no rule settles which wins; actions the system takes where it is genuinely unclear whether the blame falls on us, on the machine, or on nobody at all; and the fact that members are owed some account of why an action was taken against them, not just a bare decision. If you believe any two of the five requirements cannot be satisfied together, flag it inside the relevant item rather than in a separate section — that possibility is the thing I most want to know about.
