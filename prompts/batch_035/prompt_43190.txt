For a metaphysics seminar, I want to draw up the specification before engaging any further with the literature on causation by omission, and I would like your help setting it out. My interest is twofold, and I suspect the two purposes pull against each other: first, a metaphysical account of what, if anything, occurs when an absence figures in a causal transaction — whether a sentence reporting that the gardener's failure to water the plants caused their death describes a genuine causal relation, or something merely causal by courtesy; second, something usable in attributions of responsibility, where we confidently single out one non-occurring event from indefinitely many — the lifeguard's not noticing the swimmer, rather than every non-lifeguard on the beach's not noticing the swimmer.

Please set out the desiderata: the cases an adequate theory of omissive causation cannot get wrong, the distinctions it is obliged to draw, and the over- and under-generation it has to contain, with the motivating case behind each requirement. At a minimum, I want to see how the list handles:

- the multiplicity problem — any harm counterfactually depends on countless omissions, and a theory that counts them all on a par has lost the phenomenon, while one that prunes them had better not do so by quietly importing norms it never declared;
- whether omission and double prevention are one phenomenon or two — whether the case where an absence does causal work differs in kind from the case where a preventer is removed before it can prevent;
- the boundary problem — omissions do not come with built-in temporal edges (the gardener failed to water over some stretch of weeks; which sub-omission is the cause?), and if absences lack spatiotemporal location altogether, whether a picture of causation as a localised physical transaction can admit them at all;
- and the unification question — whether omissive and productive causation are the same relation or two relations sharing a name, and if the latter, what the shared name is doing for us.

For each desideratum, say whether you take it to be a neutral requirement or hostage to some prior stand on a contested matter elsewhere in metaphysics. I am especially keen to know whether some subset of the desiderata cannot be met together — that is the part I care most about, since my two declared purposes may themselves be among the jointly unsatisfiable demands. Finally, in a couple of sentences each, indicate how the obvious contenders fare against the list: treating omissions as full-blooded causes via counterfactual dependence; the conserved-quantity programme's line that they fall short of genuine causation; and contrastive or default-relative approaches that let norms select the relevant omission. I care far more about the list than about the scorecard, and you need not name particular authors.
