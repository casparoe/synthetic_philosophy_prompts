Is there an established term for a pattern I keep running into in arguments about animals? Over the winter a stray cat turned up in our yard, half starved, and we fed her without a second thought — everyone we told approved. A few weeks later the field behind our house froze hard for weeks, and I found myself watching the rabbits and birds out there getting visibly thinner, and it struck me that it would never occur to me, or apparently to anyone, to feed them, even though their suffering was plausibly worse than the cat's and just as easy to see. When I brought it up at a family dinner, my brother-in-law said the difference was obvious: the cat was "our problem" because it showed up at our door. That didn't satisfy me. If my neighbor went away and his dog was starving in his yard, I'd be blamed for doing nothing — and nobody starved that dog on purpose either. So it isn't just about whether the harm was deliberate. The pattern seems to be that I'm required to help an animal when its suffering traces back somehow to the human world — a door, an owner, a road, a fence, a window — and released from helping when the suffering comes from cold, hunger, disease, or predation with no human fingerprint anywhere on it. And the release isn't because the suffering is smaller; a deer starving through a hard winter probably suffers more than a neglected dog.

I've read a little philosophy in my spare time, so I know there's a standard distinction between doing harm and allowing harm, but that doesn't seem to be exactly it: in both the neighbor's-dog case and the deer case I would be merely allowing, yet one omission makes me a monster and the other doesn't even register as an omission. So this looks like a separate asymmetry sitting on top of the doing/allowing one, something like: the strength of a duty to rescue depends not on how bad the suffering is or how cheap the rescue would be, but on whether the causal chain runs through some human hand, however indirectly. Does this have a name of its own in the literature? I'm not after just a label — I want to know how it relates to the plain doing/allowing distinction, to the idea that we're specially responsible for harms we cause, and to whatever people say about the difference between natural and human-caused suffering. If there's no established concept that captures it exactly, please say so, define the thing precisely enough that it doesn't collapse into those neighboring ideas, work through the edge cases (a wild animal hit by a car, feral cats descended from pets, a bird that crashes into my window), and propose a name that marks off the phenomenon rather than the slogan.
