So here's my situation: I'm in a reading group that has spent the better part of a year on metaontology, and the whole thing has stalled in a way that's making me wonder whether there's even a real disagreement left under the labels. The debate, as we've been tracing it, is about whether the classic existence questions in ontology — are there numbers, is there a table in addition to some particles arranged table-wise, do compositions exist — are substantive questions with determinate answers. One side takes them perfectly seriously: the old Quinean picture on which answering existence questions is part of taking our best theories at face value. Amie Thomasson is on this side in an odd way, since she argues existence claims are actually easy — you can settle them from uncontroversial premises and application criteria, so the "realist" about tables wins, but cheaply, which annoys the metaphysicians who wanted the win to cost something. On the other side you've got the neo-Carnapians and deflationists, who think the whole dispute rests on a confusion about what existence talk is for, and that once you see that, there's nothing left to fight about. And then Ted Sider is kind of off doing his own thing, agreeing with the deflationists that bare existence is boring but insisting the real substantive question is whether quantificational structure carves nature at the joints — and that this question is sharp enough that the Carnapian move can't touch it. After a year, each side restates its position, everyone nods, nothing moves.

My working hypothesis, offered as just a hypothesis, is that the disagreement is partly verbal — the two camps mean different things by substantive, where for one camp it roughly means there being a fact of the matter prior to our linguistic choices, and for the other it means something like being settled by armchair criteria we all accept — but that there's a residue that isn't verbal and isn't going away: whether there's any sense in which reality has a structure that our quantifier could fail to match, and whether Sider's question, as stated, is a genuine contrast or quietly parasitic on the very notion of joint-carving being substantive, which is the thing in dispute. But I honestly can't tell whether that residue is a substantive metaphysical question, a disguised empirical one (would physics settle it? do Quineans and Carnapians actually differ over any possible observation?), or just bedrock temperamental differences about what an explanation owes us. Diagnose it for me: what kind of disagreement is this, actually — verbal, factual, about standards, about which cases count as data, or two groups answering different questions under one heading? Use what the sides actually claim, not just the caricatures. And then tell me what, if anything, would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a precisification of substantive, a formal result, a change of question — or whether no progress should be expected, and why.

One request about how you frame the answer: I want you to keep clear, throughout, the line between what's settled among people who know this literature and what's genuinely contested. E.g., whether Thomasson's easy arguments are valid given their premises is basically settled; whether those premises are doing all the interesting work, whether Sider's joint-carving question is substantive or itself needs a deflationary treatment, and whether the Quinean inheritance was a mistake from the start — those are all live, and I don't want any of them presented as the consensus view. If the honest answer in places is that we don't know, say that rather than picking a side for me. And please write in American English. Also, minor thing, feel free to correct me if I've misdescribed any of the positions above — I've absorbed a lot of this secondhand and could have the details slightly off, and I'd rather know than have you politely build on my mistake.
