I'm a philosophy PhD student working on a dissertation chapter about moral worth — when an action deserves credit not just for being outwardly correct but for being done for the right reasons — and as a side project I've been collecting verdicts from non-philosophers to see whether ordinary judgments line up with any of the standard theories. My main subject is my roommate, a cell bio PhD with zero philosophy background but unusually consistent opinions. Her verdicts so far: the cold benefactor who feels nothing for the people she helps and helps only out of a sense of duty — has moral worth, yes; a tourist who helps a lost crying child out of spontaneous compassion, never once thinking about duty — also yes; someone who donates to charity mainly so people will think she's generous — no; someone who helps a person purely to avoid feeling guilty about herself later — no; and the one that surprised me, a hiker who is moved by an injured stranger's pain, sincerely believes she's doing right, insists on carrying him down the mountain, and (unknown to her) aggravates his spinal injury — she said yes, that still has moral worth even though it turned out badly.

Next week I want to run two new cases by her and I'd like to have predictions ready beforehand. (1) A woman who hides her undocumented neighbor from an immigration raid purely because she is moved by the neighbor's terror, while sincerly believing that, as a citizen, she is doing something wrong. (2) A doctor who attempts a risky last-resort treatment moved entirely by her patient's desperation, genuinely unsure whether it's even permissible — no firm belief in either direction. What will she say about each? Honestly I care less about the yes/no's than about the rule she seems to be running: I can see at least two different rules that fit everything she's said so far but come apart on at least one of these new cases, and I'd like to hear what you think the candidate rules are, which of the two cases (or what further case, if neither is ideal) would do the best job of telling them apart, and your best guess along with your reasons for preferring it. Also please flag it if you think her existing track record is already inconsistent with every plausible rule — that would be very good to know before I build a whole chapter on her.
