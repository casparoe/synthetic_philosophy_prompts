I adjudicate disputed coverage claims for a mid-sized regional health insurer. Our policies promise coverage for the "medically necessary treatment of disease or injury" and leave "disease" undefined, so the contested claims land on me: a handful each month, each requiring a written rationale that can survive both internal review and external appeal. The medical directors pass these to me precisely because the question is conceptual rather than clinical — the clinicians can tell me what is wrong with the patient and what treating it would do, but not whether the condition counts as a disease.

Please write me a decision procedure I can run on a single claim in about twenty minutes, grounded in what philosophy of medicine actually has to say about the concept of disease: the naturalist idea that disease is the failure of a part to perform its biological function, the normativist idea that disease is a condition we judge bad, and the hybrid views that try to combine the two. Write as a philosopher of medicine who has had to make these theories usable by non-philosophers, and think through before you write which distinctions must survive the translation into a checklist — for instance, function relative to which reference class (the whole species? the patient's age and sex?), and who is entitled to judge that a condition is harmful — and which distinctions can safely be dropped.

The procedure should set out the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure stops with a no, and where it has to hand the decision back to me because the question is one of values rather than facts. Then run it on three claims: (1) therapy for a patient with disabling misophonia — rage and panic at ordinary chewing sounds, with no identifiable mechanism failing; (2) testosterone treatment for a healthy 58-year-old man complaining of fatigue, whose levels are in the low-normal range for his age; and (3) treatment of premenstrual dysphoric disorder — and here I'll tell you up front that I personally incline toward the view that if nothing is broken it isn't a disease, and I expect the procedure to disagree with me on this one. Show where it does, and what I would have to accept if I follow it.

Be explicit about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and say plainly what the procedure cannot settle. Lead with the bottom line: the first sentence or two should state the core decision rule, then give the full procedure, the worked applications, and the reasoning. No preamble, and don't restate this request back to me.
