I'm taking phil of mind this semester and we just covered Frank Jackson's knowledge argument, so the whole black-and-white room Mary thing. For my creative writing component I need a short story (~800 words) that actually engages with the argument instead of just illustrating it, and I had an idea I can't quite execute myself. My concept: instead of following Mary when she leaves the room, the story follows a second researcher — lets call her Marie — who has been in the room her whole life too, and who stays inside after Mary leaves. Marie keeps running experiments on Mary from the inside: she gets Mary's verbal reports, measurements of her galvanic skin response, fMRI data, everything physical you could want, but she never gets the red tomato herself. The interesting question the story should pose is what, if anything, Marie is missing that Mary got, and whether her enormous complete physical dossier on Mary amounts to knowing what red looks like. I'd like the ending to be ambiguous in a specific way: a careful reader should be able to interpret it as vindicating the ability hypothesis (Marie lacks a skill, not a fact) or the phenomenal concept strategy (she lacks an old fact under a new mode of presentation) depending on how they read the final scene, and ideally the reader can't decide which one the story itself endorses. Bonus if you can work in the awkward bit that Marie, having spent her life studying Mary, might end up knowing *more about* color vision than anyone who has seen red — that knowing-about vs knowing-what distinction is basically what I want the story to quietly chew on. Keep the science at least plausible-sounding and please don't have any character lecture another about qualia, my TA specifically flagged that as lazy 🙂
