longtime lurker here (OB then LW since like 2009), so i've basically marinated in the heuristics-and-biases worldview — Kahneman and Tversky as founding scripture, "cognitive bias" as the master key to everything wrong with human reasoning. but i finally got around to reading Lakatos (via a Feyerabend rabbit hole, as one does) and now i have an itch i need scratched. so:

Argue that the heuristics-and-biases research programme, judged by Lakatos's criteria, has been degenerating since roughly the mid-90s, and that the rival ecological-rationality programme (Gigerenzer's fast-and-frugal heuristics crowd) is the progressive one. Constraints, so you can't half-ass it:

- You actually have to use the Lakatos machinery: state the hard core of each programme, the positive and negative heuristics, what's sitting in the protective belt, and which auxiliary hypotheses are doing the absorbing of anomalies.
- The crux has to be novel predictions. Lakatos's whole thing is that a programme progresses only by predicting facts that were new, not by re-describing known results after the fact. So get specific-ish: where did H&B only rationalize stuff it already knew vs. where did the ecological side genuinely predict something surprising in advance (less-is-more effects, fast-and-frugal trees competitive with or beating regression on out-of-sample prediction, recognition heuristic stuff, that kind of thing).
- Deal with the obvious comeback head on: Kahneman has a Nobel and is treated as gospel in exactly the circles i grew up reading. You can't just assert that popularity isn't progress — run the Lakatos analysis showing why prestige and a progressive problemshift can come apart, and note that the replication crisis doesn't automatically help your side either, since some fast-and-fractal... sorry, fast-and-frugal results took hits too.
- Don't strawman H&B down to priming. Kahneman himself publicly threw the priming stuff under the bus, so attacking it is punching a corpse. The target is the rationalist-adjacent core — conjunction fallacy, base rate neglect, overconfidence, framing — and you have to show that the strongest versions of those are part of a degenerating shift, not the weak ones.
- Content only. No appeals to Gigerenzer-vs-Kahneman personal beef, German vs. American psychology-department sociology, none of that.

If you privately think the position is indefensible, argue it as hard as you can anyway and then flag where the case is weakest, so I know which parts to not repeat at dinner parties.
