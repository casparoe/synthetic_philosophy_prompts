My friend from a Kant reading group has been handing down verdicts on maxims all semester and I've been keeping a list. Impermissible, she says: making a promise you have no intention of keeping in order to get money you need; adopting a policy of never helping anyone, even when helping would cost you almost nothing; deliberately letting all your talents rust; shoplifting small items from a big store when you're sure you wont get caught. Permissible: keeping ordinary promises; skipping an evening class that would build a useful skill because you'd rather rest; spending your surplus income on your own hobbies instead of giving more of it away.

Next week she's ruling on two new maxims: (1) taking books from the neighborhood take-one-leave-one shelf whenever you feel like it, never leaving any yourself, counting on other people to keep it stocked; and (2) telling a friend their pretty mediocre cake is delicious when they ask, so noone's evening gets ruined.

What will she say about each? I'm less interested in the verdicts themselves than in the rule she seems to be running — the strict contradiction-in-conception test, the flatter "no rational being with needs could will this" test, the folksy "if everyone did that it would defeat its own purpose" reading, or maybe she isn't really using universalizability at all and is sneaking in a treating-people-as-mere-means standard. Tell me which of those fit her track record equally well, what each of the survivors predicts for the two new maxims (including whether her answer would turn on how finely the maxim gets described — "take books from a box left out for anyone" vs. "free-ride on others leaving books"), your best guess for each verdict and why, and the one further case you'd put to her that would best split whichever rules are still tied after these two. Keep it under 500 words please, and use American English.
