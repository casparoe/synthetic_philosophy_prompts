My roommate read Parfit's Reasons and Persons over the summer and now survival cases come up at basically every dinner. I've started writing down her verdicts because I want to predict her on a new case before she's made up her mind on it. So far her track record: standard teletransporter (you're scanned, destroyed, replica wakes up on Mars with all your memories) — she says that's survival, and that it has everything that matters, not just some watered-down version of it. Gradual replacement of your neurons with silicon chips one at a time — survival, and she thinks it's not even a hard case. But a freak case where a random quantum fluctuation produces an exact duplicate of your brain on Alpha Centauri with no causal link to you at all — no survival, she's super firm on that, the lack of any connection between the two kills it for her. The machine malfunctioning and producing two identical copies of you — survival, she says both of them have everything that matters even though neither one is strictly "the" original her. And a person in late-stage dementia whose memories and personality are mostly gone, body still alive — she says what matters does not survive, even though the biological organism obviously does.

The new case we're going to fight about next week: the machine scans your brain and transmits the data, but there's a data error and the replica on Mars gets built from a backup copy of a scan taken ten years ago, before a decade where you changed a lot (career change, marriage, new friends, whatever). So the person who wakes up on Mars is psychologically continuous with your ten-years-ago self, and causally downstream from that scan, but they share relatively little with who you are today — and then the present-day you gets destroyed as usual. Before we have this argument I want to know what I'm working with: what verdict is she likely to give, what rule (or rules) is she actually running from those earlier verdicts — something like "psychological continuity with the right kind of causal chain," versus "enough overlap with your current psychology," versus something else entirely — and which of those rules disagree about the backup case. And if you were going to design a single new case to hand her that would most cleanly separate the candidate rules, what would it be? She'll smell it if I'm just reciting Parfit at her btw, she hates that.
