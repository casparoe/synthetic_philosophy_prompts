So I've been around the rationalist blogosphere since the old LessWrong days, and the "outside view" thing has always bugged me. Kahneman loves that story about the Israeli curriculum team — the one where everyone on the team says their textbook will take two years, the outside view (how long did other teams take?) says seven, the team goes "but we're special" and then it takes eight. Fine. But the thing nobody tells you is which outside view. "Teams writing textbooks" vs "teams of our size" vs "projects led by this guy" — pick a different class, get a different answer. That's the refrence class problem, and as far as I can tell it's just unsolved, and the superforecasting crowd mostly changes the subject when you press them on it.

Meanwhile, over in actual philosophy, there's Goodman's new riddle of induction — grue and bleen, the stuff that's green up to some time T and blue afterwards (or the other way around). Same data, incompatible projections, and nothing in the data itself tells you which predicate to extrapolate. Honest question: is this the same problem as the reference class problem wearing a different hat? Like, "which reference class does my project go in" and "which predicate do I project my sample into" both look like "the evidence underdetermines the generalization, so you have to smuggle in something from outside the data."

So compare the two for me. The reference class problem as it shows up in forecasting and reasoning about the future, versus Goodman's riddle. Are they literally the same problem at different scales? If yes, does Goodman's entrenchment story — roughly, that we project the predicates that have a track record of successful past projections, which honestly sounds like kicking the can down the road — give the forecaster anything actionable, or is it just a fancy restatement of "use the class that's worked before"? If no, tell me what the actual differnce is, because I can't see it, and I've read a fair amount of Tetlock's superforecasting stuff.

And since I'd rather not get confident nonsense: flag what would change your verdict, and point at where your reasoning here is most likely to fail. Like, is there some known kind of case where the reference-class choice and the predicate choice come apart? If someone showed me that, I'd update toward them being genuinely different problems — would that sort of thing do it for you too, or would you need something else?
