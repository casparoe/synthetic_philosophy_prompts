In my epistemology reading group the anti-reductionist guy keeps hammering one move: testimony is like perception. You don't run a background check on your eyes before trusting them, you just see, and (he says) you just take people's word for things in the same default way -- so testimony is a basic source of justification, not something that has to be backed up by induction on track records the way the reductionist wants. Someone in the group countered that perception at least has a causal mechanism that's been tuned to track the world, while any chump with vocal cords can testify, so the analogy is dead on arrival. Can you actually audit the analogy for me? Also, if the analogy is doing badly, is there a better one people have tried (testimony as instrument reading? as a channel like a chain of informants?) and does it do the same work any better?
