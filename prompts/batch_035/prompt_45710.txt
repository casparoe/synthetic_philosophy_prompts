i'm a novelist, a bit stuck, and i need a spec more than i need a theory. The book: my narrator, mid-fifties, decides to become a hedonist after her divorce — sincerely, methodically, keeping a notebook of her pleasures like a birdwatcher. The problem is that after writing her notebook entries for two hundred pages I still don't know what she's collecting. The peach at 2am, the three hours lost to revison, the ugly little glow of winning an argument against her sister, the relief when the phone finally rings, the warm grief of reading her dead mother's handwriting — I've been calling all of these "pleasure" and I suspect that's just me being lazy with a word.

so before she goes off and reads philosophy (she will, in the book, it's that kind of novel), help me build the requirements list. What would a theory of pleasure actually have to deliver to count as adequate? I want the cases it has to get right — the everyday ones and the weird ones: absorption you only notice as pleasant in retrospect, masochistic pleasures, the good cry, anticipatory pleasures, pleasures of relief that are barely distinguishable from the ceasing of pain. And the distinctions it can't blur: enjoying something vs being glad it happened, the pleasure vs its causes, bodily vs whatever the non-bodily ones are. For each requirement tell me why it's a real demand and not just me being precious — what goes wrong for a theory that ignores it.

Then, briefly, how the main contenders do against the list: simple Bentham-style hedonism where it's all just pleasant sensation with different flavors, Mill's move about higher and lower pleasures, and the modern attitude theories where pleasure is roughly taking a certain attitude toward a state of affairs. Keep the scorecard short, I care about the list.

The part I most want: tell me honestly if some of the desiderata can't be satisfied together — if unifying all my narrator's notebook entries into one natural kind is a mug's game and the heterogeneity is itself the finding. Because that would actually change the last chapter, and right now I have no idea what she concludes.
