The chapter's summary of Hofstadter went roughly like this:

"Hofstadter's favorite image is Escher's Drawing Hands, the print where two hands emerge from the paper and sketch each other. He calls this a tangled hierarchy: instead of a clean chain where one level acts on the level below, the levels loop around so that neither is really on top. This is his model of the self. In the brain, symbols acquire their meaning by referring to one another, and the self is nothing but the special symbol that refers to itself, so the 'I' is really a circular construction rather than a thing. Since Gödel proved that any sufficiently powerful formal system can turn around and talk about its own rules, and thereby escape those rules, Hofstadter concludes that the mind, being such a system, can never be fully described from the outside — no objective science of consciousness is possible in principle. And unlike the Escher print, where the paper and the artist sit outside the loop and quietly anchor it, the brain has no such outside level at all: everything in it is tangled together, which is why Hofstadter says the self is ultimately an illusion."
