My friend and I argued late into the night about functional decision theory, and I want to get to the bottom of the disagreement before we pick it up again. Both of us accept that FDT pays in counterfactual mugging and refuses in XOR blackmail; what we cannot agree on is whether the distinction between the two cases is principled or ad hoc. In both cases an event outside your causal control has already been determined (the coin landed heads; the termites either have or have not infested the building), a perfect predictor has already conditioned on what you are about to do, and the big-ticket outcome is causally untouched by your current choice. My friend insists the difference is that in the mugging your refusal can reach back into the tails world, where the simulated version of you determined whether you receive $1,000,000, whereas in the blackmail nothing you do counterfactually bears on the termites. But I do not see why the same does not apply to the blackmailer's decision to send the letter: the predictor simulated you there too, and the refusal that pays off is the refusal of the letter that never arrives. Please explain, as precisely as possible and without formal machinery, what FDT takes the relevant difference between the two cases to be — whether it is a genuine difference in the dependency structure or merely a difference in which counterfactual worlds the theory instructs you to evaluate — and tell me honestly whether my friend's contrast can be maintained without smuggled assumptions.
