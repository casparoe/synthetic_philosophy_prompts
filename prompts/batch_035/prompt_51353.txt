Sports federations have never managed to explain, in a principled way, how they draw the boundary of the female competitive category, and I want to know what their actual practice presupposes about categorization in sport.

Here is how the practice works. Where an athlete's eligibility for the female category is contested, federations settle the matter with a hormone test: the athlete is eligible if a measured hormone level stays below a threshold, and the threshold itself was fixed by looking at where such levels fall across some population of women, chosen so that nearly all women fall below it and nearly all men fall above. Notice what this makes of category membership: not a fact about the athlete taken alone, but a placement of the athlete relative to a comparison population — and nothing in the rules says which population is the right one. Elite female athletes? All women? Women in that event? Athletes with the same underlying condition? Different choices classify the same athlete differently, and the regulations never say which is correct. I gather statisticians call a structural version of this the reference class problem; whatever the label, the phenomenon is plain.

Sport classifies athletes in other ways nearby, and not consistently with that. Weight categories place you by an absolute reading on a scale: no comparison population, just a line, and any line is as good as any other so long as everyone knows it in advance. Age groups are fixed by date of birth. Para sport assigns classifications by degree of impairment relative to other impaired athletes, and re-bands them by sport. The male category, meanwhile, has no test at all: anyone may enter the open category. And anti-doping sets limits for some substances by appeal to what a normal athlete's body would produce — another population-relative line, this one drawn around the reference class of clean athletes.

So: what theory of category membership does the eligibility practice presuppose? That the female category is something like a natural kind whose boundary the hormone test is a fallible detector of — or that membership is constituted by placement relative to a chosen population, so that the test does not discover the boundary but draws it? Does the practice presuppose any answer at all, or is it underdetermined, the natural-kind reading and the statistical reading licensing the very same rules? And is the mixture coherent — absolute lines where measurement is cheap, population-relative lines where it is not, no line at all for the open category? Reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely tolerates, and tell me what would actually change in the regulations if sport openly adopted the constitutive view: would eligibility disputes become debates about where to draw a line rather than about what someone is, and would the role of medical expertise shrink accordingly?
