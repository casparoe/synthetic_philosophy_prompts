so I'm filling out an advance directive and it turns out that forces you to actually decide when you'd count as dead. before signing anything I wrote down what I believe, and I'd like someone to stress-test the whole set. here it is:

1. whole-brain death is death. the rationale is the organism-as-a-whole thing: the brain is what integrates all the systems, so once the entire brain is gone what's left on the ventilator is a corpse, not a patient. and I think the higher-brain version is a mistake — permanently unconscious patients are alive, full stop.

2. the dead-donor rule is a bright line and I want it held: no organs come out of anyone who isnt dead, no matter how long the waiting list is or how good the outcome would be.

3. I'm a registered organ donor and I'd be fine with donation after circulatory death — the protocol where they pull the ventilator, wait a couple minutes (some places two, some five) after the heart stops, and then recover the organs. seems obviously okay to me.

4. withdrawing the ventilator from a permanently unconscious patient is letting them die, not killing them, and I think that distinction does real moral work — it's not a euphemism we hide behind.

5. I accept that the Harvard committee and the people behind the 1981 uniform death law were partly motivated by wanting organs and freeing up ICU beds, and I still think they landed on the right answer. death is a biological fact that the law recognizes; the law doesn't get to define it into whatever is convenient.

for what it's worth, the cases that bug me most are the brain-dead pregnant women who've been kept on support for months so the baby can be delivered — something about that feels off but I cant say what. maybe that's nothing.

go through my five and tell me which ones are actually in tension and how badly. rank the tensions by severity, don't list them like they're all equal. for each real one say what would have to give and what giving it costs me, and flag any that only look like tensions. and if theres a single revision that would clean up more than its share, tell me which one. under 700 words please — I want an audit, not a treatise.
