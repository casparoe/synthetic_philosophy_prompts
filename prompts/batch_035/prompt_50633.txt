My roommate took philosophy of mind last semester and came out of it a self-described intentional stance person — she read the Dennett stuff and now brings it up constantly, usually to tell me I'm over- or under-attributing beliefs to something. Over the last few months I've kept a running list of her verdicts on whether it's *literally true* (her phrase) that a system believes things. Dogs: yes, obviously, our dog believes the ball went under the couch. Thermostats: no, even though apparently her professor offered the thermostat as a minimal case of a believer — she thinks that's a bit that got out of hand. A chess engine that destroys her every game: no, it just does tree search. AlphaGo after move 37: yes, that was apparently the moment she "couldn't help it." Frogs doing their bug-catching thing: no. The spam filter on her email that adapts over time: no. Me: yes, though she has her doubts on days I forget where I parked. An ant colony that collectively finds the shortest path to food, which no single ant can do: no, and she was firm about that one even for the colony as a whole.

We're having people over next weekend and I want to lock in some predictions before they arrive so I can score them later. The new cases on the table: (1) a self-driving car that runs an internal model of pedestrians — it reasons about whether a given pedestrian will probably wait before stepping out, and plans around that; (2) a chatbot that gives her genuinely good advice about her sister's situation, in fluent back-and-forth conversation, but which she's read is brittle — tiny rephrasings of the same question break it completely; (3) a thought experiment her professor mentioned: replace a person with a giant lookup table that is behaviorally indistinguishable from that person down to how they argue about thermostats and dogs. What will she say about each?

I'm less interested in the yes/no answers than in the rule she's actually running, because I can see at least two or three that fit every verdict so far. Something like: the intentional stance is only *literally* true when it's the best way to predict the thing (which fits her no on the chess engine but yes on AlphaGo, since nobody could predict move 37 from the inside); versus something like a real-internal-structure view, where brittleness and shallowness matter even if the surface behavior is great, which would treat the chatbot very differently; versus maybe a view where the marker is modeling *other* minds, which would decide the self-driving car case and maybe not the others. Those rules split on my three new cases in different ways, I think, but I'd want you to work it out rather than take my word for it. So: predictions for the three cases, the candidate rules that fit her track record, which of the three cases does the most to separate the rules, and if none of them cleanly does, what extra case I could spring on her at the party that would nail it down.
