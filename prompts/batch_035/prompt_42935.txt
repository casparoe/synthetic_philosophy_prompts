So I've been hanging around the rat-adjacent corner of the internet for like a decade now — read the Sequences back in the day, follow all the usual blogs, and one thing I keep coming back to is that nobody has done the reading I actually want of Groundhog Day. Everyone does the Buddhism take or the eternal-recurrence take or the Nietzsche take. But to me the movie is basically the situationist critique of virtue ethics run as a controlled experiment, and I want someone to actually engage with it on those terms.

Hear me out. The whole gimmick of the film is that the situation is held almost perfectly constant: same town, same day, same people, same weather, and crucially no consequences that persist across loops. Nobody remembers anything except Phil. And yet his behavior changes enormously — from the cruel, cynical version of Phil at the start to the person who catches the kid falling out of the tree every single day and learns piano and ice sculpture. On its face that's the anti-situationist thought experiment: fix the situation, vary only the person, get massive behavioral change. A virtue ethicist should be thrilled. It's character as the residual after you regress out the situation.

BUT — and this is the part I want you to take seriously rather than just dunking on — the situationist has a reply, and it's a good one: the situation is not actually constant across iterations. The one variable that moves is that Phil learns his actions are consequence-free and unobserved-by-anyone-who-matters, and later he learns them in a different way (the town "remembers" within a loop, he can't be punished, etc.). Situationists have always claimed moral behavior is extremely sensitive to observation, consequences, and incentives. So maybe the early cruelty and the later kindness are both functions of a situational parameter rather than a change in some inner character thing. And then there's a counter-reply available to the virtue ethicist, which is that something does persist across loops: his memory, and more importantly his habits — the Aristotelian idea that you become what you repeatedly do is kind of literally dramatized, since each loop is a fresh situation but his skills and dispositions carry over. So is habit a character trait or just situational residue? That's the crux as far as I'm concerned, and I want a reading that engages with it at the gears level, not vibes.

Also, as a blog reader I cannot help but bring up the replication crisis: a bunch of the classic situationist evidence (Milgram is more solid, but the prison study and the seminary students stepping over the slumped guy because they were told to hurry have aged badly). How much should we weight that literature, and does the movie's argument depend on the strong or weak version of situationism?

What I want: a proper critical reading of Groundhog Day through virtue ethics and the situationist critique. Be faithful in both directions — don't make the film a mere illustration of the debate, and don't twist Harman/Doris-style situationism to fit the plot. I know you can't quote the film, that's fine, just describe the relevant scenes (the loops of self-interested exploitation, the suicides, the pivot to self-improvement, the final day where he's caught in the web of a whole town's expectations). Say where the film resists the reading — an honest critic has to flag the parts that don't fit, like the question of whether the final Phil would survive a return to a world with real stakes and an audience of millions watching him on TV again, which the movie conveniently lets us not see.

And since I'm going to be collecting a bunch of these readings in one place: after the essay, give me a structured summary as a JSON object with these fields — "central_claim" (one sentence stating the reading), "scenes_for" (array of objects, each with "scene" naming the moment and "what_it_shows" explaining how it supports the anti-situationist reading), "scenes_against" (same structure, for moments that support the situationist or complicate it), "situationist_best_reply" (the single strongest counterargument to your central claim, one sentence), "verdict" (one of "pro-virtue", "pro-situationist", or "both-miss-something", followed by a clause explaining why), and "what_the_film_adds" (one sentence on what the movie shows that the journal articles arguing this stuff can't).
