been reading up on the libet stuff for a while now (started with a podcast, spiralled from there) and I've ended up holding a bunch of positions that I suspect dont all fit together. here's roughly where I've landed, and I'd like a proper audit rather than a lecture:

(1) the readiness potential in libet's task is genuine unconscious preparation for the movement — I don't buy the Schurger-style line that it's just background noise fluctuating in some accumulator that the movement happens to tip over.

(2) the clock method for the W judgment is basically sound — when people report the spot position at which they first felt the urge to move, they're tracking something real about when awareness of intention actually occurs, not producing an artifact of the clock.

(3) the veto is the underrated half of the paper. consciousness arriving late but still in time to call the movement off shows that consciousness has genuine causal power over action, and that's the part that actually matters to me.

(4) on free will generally I'm a compatibilist: what matters is that actions flow from your reasons-responsive mechanisms, not that a conscious event starts the causal chain. no agent causation, nothing spooky.

(5) wegner was basically right that the feeling of consciously willing something is often a post-hoc attribution we construct, and the feeling itself can't be trusted.

(6) the libet paradigm itself is a sideshow for free will, because flexing your wrist whenever you feel like it isn't an action in any sense that matters — no reasons, no deliberation, nothing that free will is supposed to be about.

(7) the decoding work out of the haynes lab, where you can read a left/right choice off fMRI patterns several seconds before the person says they decided, is the real deal — that's genuinly about free will, and it's what the libet results were gesturing at all along.

some of these feel uneasy next to each other but I honestly can't tell which pairs are actually inconsistent and which just sit awkwardly next to each other. go through them and tell me which tensions are real and how bad, which ones dissolve on a second look, and what the cheapest fix would be for each. and if one revision would clean up the most, say so — dont be diplomatic about it, I want to know what to throw out.
