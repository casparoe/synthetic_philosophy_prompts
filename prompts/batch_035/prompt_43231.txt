Here's something a friend of mine (physics grad student, very smug about it) walked me into, and I can't find the exit. It's an argument that determinism refutes itself via prediction. He says it's basically Popper's old argument but he's added a chaos bit at the end which I actually think is the most interesting part.

(1) Determinism is the thesis that the complete state of the world at any one time, plus the laws of nature, uniquely fixes the state of the world at all other times.

(2) If determinism is true, the future is predictable in principle: a predictor that had the exact state and the laws could work out everything that will happen, including everything everyone will say and do.

(3) The predictor doesn't need to be a ghost hovering outside the world. Determinism is a claim about this world, so picture the predictor as a machine inside it, reading off the world's state and applying the laws.

(4) Sooner or later someone walks up to the machine and asks: what will I do one minute from now? The machine prints an answer.

(5) The person looks at the printout and, out of sheer contrariness, does the opposite.

(6) The machine's prediction was then wrong about that person's action. But by (2), a predictor with the full state and the laws can't be wrong about anything, if determinism holds.

(7) The obvious reply is that the REAL predictor would have predicted the person reading the printout and doing the opposite, so it would have printed the thing the person then does. But that just resets the loop: whatever it prints, the person can read it first and do the opposite. So no machine inside the world can correctly predict, among the things it predicts, actions that depend on its own predictions.

(8) So if determinism is true, there is a chunk of the world's future (decisions downstream of predictions) that is not predictable in principle by any embedded predictor. But (2) said determinism entails predictability in principle. Contradiction.

(9) His chaos addendum: and it's worse than that, because even for systems that never see a printout, no measurement is infinitely precise, and in a chaotic system any finite error in the initial conditions blows up exponentially, so any predictor made of parts, with finite resolution, loses the thread eventually anyway. So embedded prediction fails twice over: once by self-reference and once by precision limits.

(10) Therefore determinism is false. Or — his fallback, which he claims amounts to the same thing — even if determinism were true, nothing inside the world could ever verify it or even use it, so the thesis is empty for us.

I have a feeling the whole thing turns on some slide in what "predictable in principle" means between (2) and (8) — like it starts meaning "there's a mathematical function from state plus laws to future" and ends meaning "there's a gadget in the world that can hand you the answer before it happens." But I can't quite nail the slide down, and my friend just says "if determinism can't cash out into even in-principle prediction, what did you think you were claiming?"
