I've been recording my reactions to pest-control decisions, in no particular order, and I'd like to know whether a single principle captures them — and, if one of my verdicts has to go, which. Fumigating a house for termites, killing millions of them: clearly fine. Setting snap traps for mice in a kitchen: fine. Spraying a wasp nest by the back door while the wasps are home: fine. Glue traps for mice, which leave them to die slowly over days: not fine. Using an anticoagulant bait that kills rats over several days, where a marksman could shoot them quickly instead: not fine. Spraying harmless ants on a counter purely because they look unpleasant: not fine. Netting pigeons out of a warehouse instead of killing them: good, of course. Yet I feel almost nothing when a friend squashes an individual spider who was doing no harm, and my verdict there doesn't seem to fit with the ant one. There's also a case I keep going back and forth on: buying parasitoid wasps to control aphids, where each aphid is eaten alive from the inside — but there are thousands of them, and the alternative is spraying. Can you state a principle (or a couple of competing candidates) that fits as many of my verdicts as possible, say which verdict it sacrifices if it can't fit them all, and tell me what it predicts about the parasitoid wasps and about whether I should prefer live-catch traps that release mice into unfamiliar fields where they usually die anyway? Please answer in British English, in under 350 words.
