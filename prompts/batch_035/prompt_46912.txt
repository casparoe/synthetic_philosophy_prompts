Construct a pair of scenarios for the reductionism vs anti-reductionism debate about testimonial justification. The two scenarios should be identical except for one variable: whether the hearer has positive, reductionistically-usable reasons to trust the speaker (track record, background knowledge of the domain, corroboration she could cite), with everything else held fixed — including the speaker's actual sincerity and actual reliability — so that anti-reductionism says the belief is justified in both cases while reductionism says justified in one and not the other. Constraints: everyday and realistic (a coworker, a pharmacist, a stranger at a bus stop — no philosophy lectures, no textbook witnesses), the report should be something the hearer has to act on so the verdict matters, and the no-reasons case can't just be "the hearer is weirdly incurious" — the absence of positive reasons has to come from the situation (unfamiliar domain, one-off encounter, no way to get a track record) rather than the hearer failing to check something she obviously could've checked. Also keep the speaker in the no-reasons case from being in any way suspicious — no apparent motive to lie, no strangeness — so the case doesn't collapse into a defeater case where everyone agrees.

After the scenarios, walk through what each theory says in each, tell me which feature is doing the work, and then deal with the reductionist's most natural line of attack: that in real life adults always bring a lifetime of background evidence about how testimony usually goes, so a genuine no-positive-reasons case can't be constructed at all. Say whether your case survives that attack or whether it quietly smuggles background evidence in, and if it does smuggle it in, say what that shows about how hard it is to run the anti-reductionist's argument in practice.

Note on what counts as cheating: don't stipulate the verdicts — they have to fall out of the theories. If you find yourself writing "and of course she has no reason to believe him," stop and make the case generate that instead. For context: this is for a seminar session on testimony, and the pair of cases is meant to replace the usual move of just asserting that the anti-reductionist licenses more belief than the reductionist.
