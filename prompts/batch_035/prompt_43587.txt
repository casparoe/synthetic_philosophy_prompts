I'm marking finals for an upper-level course on philosophy of probability and want a second opinion on one script before I commit to a mark. The exam question was:

'The Sleeping Beauty experiment runs as usual: a fair coin is tossed on Sunday night; if it lands heads, Beauty is interviewed on Monday only; if tails, she is interviewed on Monday and Tuesday, with a drug erasing each interview from her memory; she is told all of this in advance. In this version, whenever she is interviewed there is a note on the table in her room, and the note always truthfully states which day it is. (a) Before she looks at the note, what credence in heads should she have, on the halfer view and on the thirder view? (b) She reads the note and it says Monday. What credence in heads should she now have, on each view? Show your working. (c) Is the halfer's answer to (b) a cost of the view? Discuss.'

Here is the student's answer:

'(a) The halfer, following Lewis, says her creadence in heads on waking should stay at 1/2: Beauty knew on Sunday that she would be woken at least once no matter how the coin lands, so being awake now is not news, and her credence carries over unchanged. The thirder, following Elga, says 1/3.

(b) Thirder: on waking she distributes 1/3 over each of the three awakening-centres (heads-Monday, tails-Monday, tails-Tuesday). The note eliminates tails-Tuesday, leaving two equally weighted centres, so P(heads | note says Monday) = 1/2. Had the note said Tuesday, heads would be 0. Halfer: on waking she holds 1/2 on heads and 1/4 on each of the two tails centres. The note eliminates tails-Tuesday, so heads conditionalises to (1/2)/(1/2 + 1/4) = 2/3.

(c) The 2/3 is the real cost here, though it usually gets misdescribed. Beauty ends up fairly confident the coin landed heads on the strength of a note that never depended on the toss at all — whether a note is on the table is fixed by the protocol, not by the coin. Lewis, in his reply to Elga, eventually conceded this point and agreed that once the day is fixed the halfer should come round to the thirder's 1/2, which is why the 2/3 result is rarely pressed against him. But the thirder's 1/2 is nothing to crow about either. It follows immediately from assigning equal thirds to the three centres on Sunday, so the whole halfer/thirder dispute collapses into a prior choice over centred possibilities: go in with equal thirds and every thirder verdict in (a) and (b) is trivial; refuse them and nothing follows. All the interesting work is done at the prior, and Elga's indifference principle is that prior claim wearing a disguise.'
