I'm a retired immunologist, three years into a philosophy rabbit hole I fell into at retirement, and my neighborhood reading group just worked through the separateness-of-persons objection to utilitarianism — the Rawlsian complaint that the utilitarian treats the tradeoff between my pain and your pleasure the way a person treats tradeoffs within her own life, and that this is exactly what she may not do, because there is one subject who lives a single life but no subject who lives our two lives. Fine. But then I constructed the following and now I can't find the exit, and I suspect it proves too much.

(1) What makes it legitimate to accept a loss in one part of a life for the sake of a greater gain in another part of the same life is that there is a single subject who gets both — one point of view that is compensated.
(2) The separateness objection says this is what is missing when the utilitarian imposes a loss on me for a larger gain to you: no single subject is compensated.
(3) But there is no single subject who experiences both my headache this morning and my good mood tomorrow either — the experiencing-now and the experiencing-tomorrow are numerically distinct centers of experience, causally and memory-connected at best, exactly as my toothache and your toothache are numerically distinct.
(4) So the tradeoff prudence makes between my pain today and my pleasure tomorrow aggregates across distinct subjects just as much as the utilitarian's tradeoff does.
(5) If aggregating across distinct subjects is what's illegitimate, then ordinary prudence is illegitimate too, and I was irrational to undergo my flu shot last week.
(6) Prudence is not illegitimate.
(7) So the separateness objection, as stated, either condemns prudence or smuggles in some further difference between the intra-life and interpersonal cases that isn't about subjects at all.

I'd rather reject something in the neighborhood of (1) or (3) than accept the rejection of prudence — (6) I'm keeping. But I honestly can't locate the slide, and my group includes one Parfit enthusiast and two utilitarians who are enjoying watching me squirm, so they're no help. Pick the step you'd reject, tell me why it looked fine and what rejecting it costs, and don't give me a menu of everyone's positions — commit to one diagnosis and defend it.
