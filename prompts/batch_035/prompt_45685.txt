I'm a software engineer with fifteen years of building and maintaining large production systems, and I've read just enough philosophy of biology to be dangerous: I know there's a standing fight between etiological (selected-effect) accounts of biological function, on which a trait's function is what it was selected for doing in its lineage, and causal-role (systems) accounts, on which a trait's function is the contribution it currently makes to some capacity of the containing organism. Restate this debate in my vocabulary, one account at a time.

For the etiological side, translate it into terms of why code exists rather than what it does: the original commit, the use case a module was written to serve, the spec-as-intention even when nobody ever wrote a spec down, and git history as the analogue of selection history. For the causal-role side, use the call graph, the contract a module happens to satisfy for other modules right now, and what static analysis would tell you about a system's organization. I also want you to bring in Hyrum's Law -- the observation that, given enough users, every observable behavior of a system will eventually be depended on by somebody, promised or not -- wherever it genuinely fits, and to say whether it belongs to the etiological side, the causal-role side, or marks the slide between them, as when a trait originally selected for one job becomes load-bearing for another and can no longer be deleted.

Then give me the residue report. I especially care about dysfunction: the etiological side is supposed to explain how a heart can be diseased, failing at its function, while still pumping away, and I want to know whether my vocabulary's bug-versus-working-as-designed distinction carries that, given that our systems have authors and intended uses while selection has neither author nor spec. So tell me which parts of the philosophers' disagreement have no counterpart in my terms, and whether the residue reflects something my vocabulary cannot express -- an authorless notion of normativity, perhaps -- or reveals that the disagreement is less substantive than it looks. Be explicit about wherever you had to distort the translation to make it fit.
