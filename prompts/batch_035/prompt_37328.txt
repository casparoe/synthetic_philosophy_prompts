Okay so last night my friend and I got into a three hour argument about Lewis on convention and I think it left me with something like an actual thesis idea, but it's pretty half-baked and I want to see if it can be turned into a real proposal for my MA dissertation (one year, but realistically the writing is all in the spring semester).

The argument went like this. She says Lewis's account of convention is basically an account of coordination plus arbitrariness: a convention is a solution to a coordination problem where everyone prefers to conform given others conform, and where some alternative regularity would have done just as well -- the arbitrariness bit is supposed to be essential, it's what makes it a convention rather than something else. Her point was that this is hopeless as an account of social norms, because norms mostly aren't arbitrary: the norm of keeping promises, norms of fairness, whatever -- once they exist, there are *reasons* to stick with them, they're not just one equilibrium among many that we happened to land in. So Lewis's machinery explains at most the boring etiquette-type stuff, and norms need something else entirely.

And my pushback, which I couldn't quite articulate at the time but have been chewing on since, is that maybe the arbitrariness condition is doing two totally different jobs that we should split apart. There's arbitrariness of *origin* -- which equilibrium we happened to fall into, and here the fairness norm really might have gone another way, some other division of the bargaining surplus could have stabilized -- and then there's arbitrariness of *maintenance* -- whether, holding the norm fixed, people would be just as happy conforming to alternatives. And once a norm acquires punishment practices, second-order enforcement, internalized guilt, all that, it stops being arbitrary in the maintenance sense: the alternatives get worse as the norm gets entrenched, not better. So maybe the friend's examples aren't counterexamples to Lewis at all, they're cases where origin-arbitrariness persists while maintenance-arbitrariness erodes, and the interesting question is whether the self-perpetuating machinery can carry normativity on its own or whether you need the origin-arbitrariness too, for some reason to do with the logic of conventionality.
