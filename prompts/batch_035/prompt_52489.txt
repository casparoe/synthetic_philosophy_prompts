i want a thought experiment built to order that stress-tests the procreation asymmetry on risk. quick setup so we're on the same page: the asymmetry says creating a person whose life would be miserable makes the world worse, but creating a person whose life would be happy doesn't make it better. everyone states it for certainties. i want a case where the trouble comes specifically from uncertainty.

the scenario: a couple at a fertility clinic with exactly one viable embryo, which carries a condition such that theres a small chance (you pick, somewhere in the 1-5% range) the child's life would be not worth living and a large chance it would be genuinely flourishing. constraints on the case:

- realistic, clinic or genetic-counseling setting, no sci-fi, no perfect predictors
- the risk has to be unavoidable, so the choice really is gamble vs abstain — no test can screen it out, and dont let them just adopt instead
- the badness of the bad outcome has to be shown, not just labeled "miserable" — and dont stack the deck by making the downside vivid and the upside generic
- the numbers should be knowable-ish (its a known variant, its been sequenced) even though the outcome hasnt happened

then the walkthrough and the meta. give it to me as exactly five numbered items, each a sentence or two, no intro, no wrap-up: (1) and (2) set up the case; (3) the asymmetry's verdict, applied the way it's normally applied — to outcomes, after the fact — walking through both branches and spelling out why it comes out condeming the gamble; (4) the total view's verdict plus what ordinary intuition says; (5) the honest note: which feature of the case is doing the work (hint: the asymmetry was never built for cases that are partly good and partly bad from the same act), what happens to the verdicts when you dial the probability of the bad outcome to zero or to one, and the defender's best move — switching to ex ante evaluation, i.e. judging the gamble before anyone knows how it lands — together with what that move costs them, e.g. whether it forces them to start counting expected welfare and quietly converge on the total view.

one more thing, since the purpose fixes what counts as cheating: this is meant to test whether the asymmetry survives being applied under uncertainty at all. so all the verdicts have to fall out of the principle's actual commitments — dont stipulate that the asymmetry theorist "feels terrible about it," and no inventing auxiliary principles that were never part of the view. if you think the case cant actually be built to spec, say so in item 5 rather than fudging the walkthrough.
