I've worked in removals for over twenty years, and there is something that happens whenever two people carry a wardrobe up a staircase that I have never been able to name. I don't mean the physical side of it. I mean this: neither of us issues instructions, yet we adjust to each other continuously, and afterwards neither of us could say exactly what he did — the carrying seems to belong to the pair rather than to either man. And if one of us puts his end down without warning, the other's complaint is not merely "that was careless" but "you can't do that to me" — as though something had been owed, even though nothing was said, promised, or signed. What is more, that sense of being owed does not appear everywhere that people rely on each other. When two strangers reach a narrow gate at the same moment and each waits for the other, each plainly relies on the other's restraint, and yet if one pushes through first the other only grumbles; nobody feels betrayed. There is a similar fuzziness at the edges: two colleagues gradually start tidying a van together, and at some hard-to-locate point wandering off for a smoke stops being a personal choice and becomes the rebuke-worthy thing, but I could not tell you when the change happens.

Does this cluster — the pair-owned doing, the rebuke without a promise, and above all the line between reliance that carries that entitlement and reliance that doesn't — have an established name in philosophy or anywhere else? I am not after a bare label. Please answer in four sections, in this order: (1) The nearest established concepts, and what each would say about my cases (about 200 words); (2) Where my cases break away from each of those concepts, taken one at a time (about 250 words); (3) Your verdict: either one of those concepts in fact covers the phenomenon, defended against the cases in section 2, or a new name and a plain-words definition of the dividing line I am chasing (about 200 words); (4) Two boundary cases that test that verdict, one falling on each side of the line (about 100 words).
