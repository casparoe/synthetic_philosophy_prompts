i keep reading that the combination problem is what sinks panpsychism, but nobody seems to agree on what would even count as solving it. before i engage with any particular attempt, i want the spec. what does a solution to the combination problem actually have to deliver? give me the requirements list, with the case behind each one.

stuff i'd want covered, but go beyond if you like:

- does it have to be a *mechanism* of combination, or is a brute "phenomenal unity just bottoms out" fact allowed? and if brute facts are fine for the panpsychist, on what grounds do they still get to complain about brute emergence?
- does a solution owe us a stopping condition -- why do the micro-experiences making up me combine into one mind while mine and yours don't combine into a group mind? is silence about that a genuine gap or an unfair demand (after all, nobody demands the physicalist explain why two brains don't merge)?
- what should it say about split-brain patients? severing the corpus callosum looks like it takes one experiencer and makes two, so can a theory of combination that can't run in reverse really be a theory of combination at all?
- do we need to keep the palette problem (many simple qualities, one unified field containing those qualities) separate from the subject-summing problem (many centers of experience, one experiencer), or are those the same problem in different hats? i suspect a theory that dissolves one can be totally silent on the other and i want to know if that's a disqualifier.
- and the one i care about most: is "must be no more mysterious than the emergence it was meant to replace" a genuine constraint or just a debating point?

also happy to be told that some requirements people throw around -- like "must be empirically testable" -- are camp prejudices rather than real desiderata. and flag any pair of requirements you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, that's the part i most want to see. keep the weight on justifying the criteria; the scorecard against the live candidates (structuralist approaches, constitutive holism, the deflationary "there's no combination because there's only one consciousness" move) can be quick and dirty -- a table is fine if you want.
