Is there a name for the thing where believing one conspiracy theory makes you believe all of them, even the ones that contradict each other? My uncle is really into this stuff and over winter break we had a long argument that kind of broke my brain. He thinks the moon landing was faked, but he ALSO thinks NASA is hiding recovered alien technology. I pointed out those can't both be true — if the moon landing was faked then the space program is basically a movie studio, but if they have alien tech then why would they need to fake anything — and he literally didn't even pause. He just said either way it proves they're lying to us. It's like the actual content of each theory doesn't matter, only the direction: anything that points toward "the official story is false" counts as evidence for every other "official story is false" belief, and contradictions between them just bounce off.

I took a psych elective last year so I know about confirmation bias, but this doesn't seem like the same thing. Confirmation bias (as I understand it) is about how you treat evidence for a belief you already hold. This is more like all of the conspiracy beleifs function as ONE giant belief, so they can't really contradict each other because the details are almost decoration. There's also a second weird part: he'll say the government is a bunch of idiots who can't do anything right, and in the next breath that this same government has run a perfect, leak-proof coverup for 60 years. So it's not just that he has a lot of weird beliefs, it's that having them together should create friction and somehow doesn't.

So my question — is there an established term for this pattern, in philosophy or psychology or wherever people study why people believe weird things? Or if there's no exact term, what's the nearest concept and how would you carve this one out so it doesn't just collapse into confirmation bias or "some people are gullible"? I'm not looking for just a label, I want to understand what the mechanism actually is (does believing one conspiracy make the next one easier to believe? is it a worldview thing where "nothing is as it seems" is the load-bearing part?) and where the edges of the phenomenon are — like, does it apply to non-conspiracy stuff too, such as political beliefs that also seem to come in bundles?
