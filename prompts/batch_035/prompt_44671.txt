I'm in a third-year philosophy of mind course and our seminar has basically fractured into two camps that have been arguing past each other for the whole term. Camp one is basically on board with eliminative materialism: propositional attitudes like beliefs and desires are posits of a folk theory, the theory will go the way of phlogiston, and mature neuroscience will just not have anything that answers to "believes that p". Camp two thinks that's wildly overconfident: belief-talk has survived every round of scientific psychology so far, gets vindicated rather than refuted by it, and the eliminativist can't even state their own view without helping themselves to beliefs. We've been set an assignment: write the joint statement these two camps could actually put their names to if they did a proper adversarial collaboration, and I'm struggling to draft it fairly.

Please draft it for me. What I need is:

1. A numbered section of claims BOTH camps could sign — I'm guessing they actually agree on a lot (that folk psychology is a theory in some sense, that it's highly successful in everyday prediction, that neuroscience keeps marching on, that nothing in current neuroscience yet eliminates belief-talk), but I want the wording to be tight enough that neither side feels embarrassed signing.

2. A disagreements section, ideally a table, where each disagreement is phrased twice — once so the eliminativist would endorse it and once so the realist would — so we can see whether they're really disagreeing or just using words differently.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move a side. Our tutor keeps hinting that one crux is whether "belief" picks out a natural kind or just a cluster that science might refine rather than abolish, and another might be what exactly the phlogiston/demon analogy is supposed to establish. Feel free to include that as a crux or to shoot it down as irrelevant.

4. For each crux, at least one argument, case, or piece of evidence that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance (e.g. if belief-desire style explanations keep getting absorbed into computational psychiatry or machine-learning research, what does each side expect that absorption to look like — smooth refinement or replacement?).

5. A short section on which parts of the dispute, if any, are merely verbal — e.g. whether a heavily revised folk psychology still counts as "keeping belief" or is already elimination under another name — and say why.

Also if you could flag wherever you think either camp would refuse to sign my wording, that would honestly save me a seminar of pain. Please write the whole thing in British English, and don't be afraid to tell me if one of the "disagreements" I listed is actually fake news that both camps already agree on.
