So I run a small cognitive-science reading group (bright undergrads, no prior reading required) and next term we're doing Dennett's tower of generate-and-test — the Darwinian / Skinnerian / Popperian / Gregorian ladder, from blind selection through trial-and-error learning, to previewing moves in an internal model before your body pays for them, to all that plus mind-tools like labels and concepts handed to you by your culture. I want a diagnostic quiz, and the point is to sort candidate systems by what they actually *do*, not by which rung the textbooks file them under.

Ten questions, each a concrete behavioural test on a specific system, with 3-4 answer options describing what the system does (not what it "is"). The systems should span the ladder hard: a thermostat, E. coli doing chemotaxis, Physarum slime mould rerouting through a maze, a Sphex wasp checking its burrow (the classic sphexishness trap — looks smart, is hardcoded), a rat in a Skinner box, a scrub-jay or crow caching food with re-caching behaviour when it knows it was observed, a chess engine running lookahead, a toddler who asks a question they've never been asked before, and a large language model doing whatever it is LLMs do.

Two things the quiz must handle properly. First, the mimicry problem: Dennett's own caveat is that each rung can be faked from below — a population of Darwinian creatures can look like one learning Skinnerian, and a creature with a bogus hardcoded internal model can look Popperian until the world punishes its model. So at least two questions need to be designed so the wrong-run mimic fails them. Second, my own suspicion, which I'd like the quiz to test rather than assert: that LLMs might be Gregorian without being properly Popperian — absolutely soaked in mind-tools, but with no cheap internal preview step you can catch them skipping or doing. If a question can't separate that from a genuine Popperian-plus-mind-tools agent, say so honestly.

Finish with a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto leaves (all four rungs, plus the "not even Darwinian" basement and a "sphexish" leaf for hardcoded mimicry), and a short section flagging any pairs of leaves the quiz genuinely cannot separate from behaviour alone — I'd rather know where my instrument is blind than pretend it isn't.

Answer in British English please (behaviour, mould, etc.). And don't just rehash the four definitions at me at the start — assume the quiz-taker hasn't read Dennett, but the quiz-writer has.
