So this is kind of a heavy thing to bring to a birthday dinner recap but here we go. I turned 35 last month and it landed weird — like the odometer flipping over — and since then I keep catching myself wondering if any of what I do actually means anything. I do data entry at an insurance company, I'm not curing anything, nobody will remember me in 200 years, the universe is famously not returning my calls. Apparently I've been obvious about it, because at my birthday dinner two of my friends got into it on my behalf, and they've been going back and forth in the group chat since.

Dev is our resident Camus guy. His line: the whole question is rigged. Nothing has meaning viewed from nowhere (he kept saying sub specie aeternitatis, he's very proud of that phrase, I think he got it from Nagel), and the mistake is thinking that's a discovery about the universe rather than a demand we're making. Once you accept there's no cosmic significance coming, you have three real options — give up, take what Camus calls the leap into philosophical suicide (religion, any system that promises the universe actually cares), or just live with the absurd openly and keep going anyway. Revolt, freedom, passion. He said Camus literally ends the Myth of Sisyphus by saying you have to imagine Sisyphus happy, so it's not like Camus is offering nothing — the point is the meaning isn't out there, it's the defiance plus lucidity.

Marta, who has read her Susan Wolf, said revolt is a mood, not a meaning. Her claim was Dev is collapsing two questions: does your life matter to the cosmos (no, obviously, everyone agrees, even Aquinas would say our significance comes from somewhere else) and does your life have meaning at all, which needs two things — you being genuinely engaged with what you do, and what you do being worth engaging with. A nurse treating patients in an indifferent universe has a meaningful life. And on Dev's picture, she said, anyone sufficiently caught up in something — a guy who spends forty years obsessed with cataloguing bottle caps and feels great about it — has a meaningful life, which just seems wrong. Attitude can't be the whole story.

Dev said that's not his picture — Camus isn't saying get passionate about anything, there's something about lucidity and living with your eyes open, refusing the comfortable stories. And anyway intensity of commitment is doing more work than Marta gives it credit for.

Marta said intensity is a measure of how strongly you feel, not whether what you feel tracks anything worth doing — by that logic the more vividly deluded you are the more meaningful your life. Then Dev did his Nagel move: Nagel argued the sense of absurdity doesn't come from the universe being silent, it comes from our ability to always step back and view our own lives from the outside, and nothing fully survives that step back. So Marta's precious objective worth is just whatever happens to survive HER step back, and eventually she'll step back further and lose it too. Her view collapses into the same hole.

Marta's comeback was that this just shows the absurd is a feature of having a reflective mind, not a fact about the world, so it can't be evidence against meaning down here — and also that meaning comes in degrees, it's not all or nothing, so the fact that some perspective diminishes it doesn't erase it. Then the cake came out.

Honestly I want someone to referee this properly because it's now genuinely about my own life and I can't tell if Dev is offering me something real or just a stylish way of standing in the void. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what, where either of them is talking past the other, and what (if anything) they're both missing? And if there's some version of this I could actually live with at my desk job on Monday, I'd take that too.
