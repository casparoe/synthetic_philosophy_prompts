My friend (who did a year in Munich) spent all of last night arguing that German folk morality is basically rule-following for people who don't trust their own judgment, and that the American way -- individual conscience over blind rule-following -- obviously gets the better of it. I went to bed annoyed and now I'm trying to figure out what I actually think by going through cases and seeing if there's a pattern.

Crossing against a red light at 3am on a completely empty street: fine, and I think most Germans would say fine too, which already bugged my friend. Jaywalking in front of a kid: not fine, because you're teaching them that rules are optional. A judge letting a starving man off for stealing bread because conscience demands it: honestly I think that's worse than the judge who says the law is the law and takes his conscience to the legislature instead. Skipping a pointless form requirement that harms no one: fine by me, but my friend says the fact that I even reason like that is the whole problem -- if everyone privately decides the form is pointless, the form stops meaning anything, and Germans understand that in their bones. The wartime bureaucrat who just followed orders versus the one who broke the law to hide people: obviously the second is better, which seems like a clean point for conscience. But then again the American who overrides the rule because his conscience says so is usually wrong about what his conscience says, while the German who follows the rule even when it's inconvenient at least isn't trusting the worst person for the job to interpret it -- himself.

So: is there one principle here that captures which cases each side really wins, or is my friend's actual claim (and my tentative one) just that each culture's rules happened to fit their domains better, so it's all contingent? And if you can state a principle, what does it say about the case I keep flip-floping on -- whistleblowing on your employer when the leak itself breaks the law, i.e. following your conscience against the rule that says follow the rules? I'd take a principle that costs me one of my verdicts if it's clean.
