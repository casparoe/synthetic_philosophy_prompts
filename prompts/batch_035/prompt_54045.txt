I run a philosophy of mind reading group working through the Malcolm–Dennett exchange on whether dreams are experiences at all, and I want a one-page decision tree that takes each member from their bedrock intuitions to a position in that debate. Build the tree out of cases rather than labels — for instance: a lucid dreamer who agrees in advance to signal with left–right eye movements when she notices she is dreaming and does so while the EEG confirms REM; a sleeper woken mid-REM who reports a long, narratively coherent dream as though it had unfolded over the twenty minutes before waking; someone who sincerely reports a vivid dream on a night with no REM at all; and the ordinary morning report itself, which each theory must say is either memory of an episode or something composed on waking. Every node should be a question a member can answer without knowing which leaf it feeds, and the leaves should include Malcolm's view that dreams are not experiences occurring during sleep (so that dream-talk reports waking behavior, not sleep episodes), Dennett's alternative on which dream content may be fixed only at the moment of waking, the orthodox view that dreams are experiences had during sleep, and the view on which dream reports are reports of imaginings rather than perceptions. Please flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate, and note any unoccupied combination of answers with a brief diagnosis of why nobody sits there — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. No citations or reading lists; the tree itself should carry the distinctions.
