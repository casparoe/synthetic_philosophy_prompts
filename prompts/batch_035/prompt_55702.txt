She also holds, on reflection, that humanity's track record of surviving past catastrophes is itself strong evidence against assigning any high probability to near-term extinction. Next month she votes on whether risks from advanced artificial intelligence belong on the existential list. Predict her verdict, but I am more interested in the rule she seems to be running: is she counting as existential only those threats for which we have historical near-misses or natural analogues, or any threat with a plausible mechanism for killing everyone that current science could in principle deliver, or something about which technologies have actually been built rather than merely projected? Her engineered-pandemic and nanotechnology votes pull in opposite directions here, so please say which candidate rule fits her record best and why, what each rule predicts about her vote on AI, and which further case — say, a lab-grown pathogen far deadlier than anything found in nature, or a hypothesised physics disaster with a real mechanism but no precedent whatsoever — would best tell the rules apart.
