Is there a name for this pattern? I keep hitting it in people close to me and honestly in myself, and I can't find the word.

The setup: someone believes something. You show them clear evidence against it. And in the moment they actually get it — no fighting, no nitpicking, they seem sincerly persuaded, sometimes even a bit shaken, like "god, I can't beleive I thought that for years." And it's not an act. Ask them an hour later and they can still tell you what the evidence was and what it showed.

Then nothing downstream changes. A week later the old belief is running their decisions exactly as before. And here's the part I most want a name for: when you say "we talked about this, you agreed with me," they don't deny it. They'll say yeah, I remember that. And then just continue as if the update never happend. The correction got accepted out loud but never installed anywhere.

Concreet example so you know what I mean: a relative of mine was sure a specific supplement was fixing his joint pain. His doctor walked him through the trial data, and he got it, said fair enough, seemed genuinely annoyed at himself for spending the money. Four weeks later he's ordering it again and telling people it works. When I reminded him of the appointment he said "yeah, I know what the data says" — and kept ordering.

I know about motivated reasoning and that's not it, because motivated reasoning is the resisting part and there is no resisting here. Nobody talks themselves out of the evidence. It goes in, gets acknowledged, and then quietly doesn't count. I also came across "belief perseverance" in psych, but that's usually about people hanging on after the *evidence* gets discredited — here they concede the point and it still doesn't stick. The closest everyday phrase I have is "in one ear, out the other," but that sounds too passive, like nothing registered, and something clearly did register — they can recount the whole convo.

So, questions. Does any field have an actual concept for this? If the self-deception literature or philosophy of mind has something I'd take that. If nothing established fits, I'd rather you just say so and coin a term — but then define it by what it's NOT, because I keep confusing it with (a) ordinary self-deception, (b) akrasia — they "know" the better thing and don't act on it, except this seems weirder, like the knowledge isn't located anywhere it can push on anything, and (c) plain forgetting, which it definitly isn't. The question I keep coming back to: where is the belief even living in the meantime? It's not stored as an opinion, it's not stored as a memory, but it's clearly stored somewhere, because it keeps regenerating. If you can carve that out for me I'll be happy.
