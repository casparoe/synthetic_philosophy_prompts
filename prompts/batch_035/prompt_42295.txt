I've been working through the special composition question in a metaphysics reading group this term, and I've come out the other side a fairly confident universalist: whenever there are some things, there is something composed of exactly those things. My reasoning is mostly eliminative. Every restricted answer I've seen draws what strikes me as an arbitrary cutoff — composition when there's contact, or fastening, or fitting, or when the parts are caught up in a life — and the arbitrariness doesn't go away when the cutoff is sharpened; it just gets relocated. The remaining options seem to be nihilism (which I find incredible about tables and organisms), or admitting with the brutal composition crowd that it's just a brute fact where composition stops, which strikes me as giving up on the question rather than answering it. I'm also moved by the thought that if composition were restricted, it seems like there'd be a sharp cutoff, and sharp cutoffs in an otherwise vague world smell bad.

Lately, though, I've started to worry that my position may not be risking anything, and that this is a flaw rather than a strength. When someone objects that there mustn't be an object scattered across my chair and the moon, or something made of a trout's front half and a turkey's rear half, I hear myself replying that of course there is such a thing, it's just boring and mostly ignorable — and when someone says the view makes object-counting trivial and answers too easy, I reply that the quantifier is cheap and that's fine. A friend in the group accused me of having insulated the view until it can't lose, and compared my attitude toward the junk fusions to the way we ignore all the unnamed mereological sums rather than deny them. That stung a bit, since I got into universalism precisely by complaining that the restrictions were unfalsifiable.

So here's what I want from you: a map of my view's exposure. What would actually count against unrestricted composition — which intuitions about cases (the scattered junk objects, artifacts, the idea that I am not one of countless co-located thinkers with slightly different parts), which arguments, and which pressures from elsewhere in metaphysics, like the sorites reasoning that's supposed to favor universalism possibly backfiring, or worries about how the view interacts with grounding and about which objects are fundamental? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which of them a committed universalist would concede as genuine costs versus explain away, and be honest about whether the sort of deflationary moves I've been making leave the view testable at all. And if you conclude it is well insulated, tell me whether that's a vice for a metaphysical thesis of this kind or just an acceptable feature of a claim that's supposed to be cheap and unifying.
