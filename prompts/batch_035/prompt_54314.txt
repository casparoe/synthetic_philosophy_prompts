I'm writing a short position piece for a seminar on language and metaphysics, and before I circulate it I want a sanity check on whether my views about vagueness actually form a package or whether I'm just holding a bunch of things that each feel right on their own. Roughly where I stand: (1) vagueness lives in language, not the world — nothing out there is "really" borderline; heaps and bald men are only borderline because our words leave the boundary undecided. (2) I keep classical logic for vague discourse — every instance of excluded middle is true, no classical tautology fails — and the sorites premise (if n grains make a heap, so do n−1) comes out not true. (3) For a genuinely borderline case, "that's a heap" is neither true nor false — I'm not an epistemicist, and the idea of a secret sharp cutoff we somehow can't ever know has always struck me as desperate. (4) I accept higher-order vagueness all the way up: "definitely," "borderline," even "true" are themselves vague, and there's no final level where things finally get sharp. (5) Meaning supervenes on use — nothing over and above the communitys usage patterns makes it the case that "heap" applies to this pile rather than that one.

I can already hear a friend of mine who's a committed epistemicist smirking about at least one of these, so I'd rather be told now than in seminar. What I want from you: go through the commitments pairwise and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, but don't treat everything as equally bad — rank them by severity. For each real tension, say what would have to give and roughly what it costs me elsewhere. And please separate the ones that only look like tensions from the ones that are real: I have a feeling at least one item on my list that sounds contradictory is actually fine on reflection, but I honestly can't tell which, and I'd rather you discriminate than flag everything. To finish, tell me which single revision, if I had to make one, would clean up the most.
