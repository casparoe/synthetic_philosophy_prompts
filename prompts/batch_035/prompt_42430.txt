I'm a grad student presenting at a workshop next month where most of the audience are longtermist EA researchers -- a couple of decision theory PhDs, several people who work on existential risk at big orgs, plus one old-school utilitarian who's been citing Singer since before it was cool. My thesis is basically the cluelessness worry: for nearly all real-world acts, the far-future effects swamp and roughly cancel, so the expected-value differences between our options are dominated by unresolvable uncertainty, and this isn't noise we can average away -- it's structural. My own twist (the part they'll hate): I argue cluelessness compounds. Every choice conditions the environment for all later ones, so even if uncertainty about a single act looks symmetric, over a whole life the standard "just be risk-neutral and let the little stuff wash out" move fails twice over.
