i'm a phd student in political theory and my dissertation chapter is on epistemic arguments for democracy, and before i go any further I want the spec nailed down: what does a Condorcet-style jury theorem have to actually deliver before it can do the work it's almost always asked to do, namely justify giving majorities authority over collective decisions? Everyone in my field quotes the theorem and then quietly smuggles in repairs — competence above 1/2, independence, sincere voting, a procedure-independent fact of the matter to track — and I want the full requirements list with the case behind each one. Some candidates I can already see the shape of, but I want them done properly: it has to handle correlated votes (voters share media environments and sometimes just copy each other, and the correlated-votes results going back to Ladha and Berg suggest reliability degrades fast once common causes enter), it has to handle heterogeneous and possibly endogenous competance (voters differ, and how informed they are may depend on the institutions the theorem is supposed to justify), it has to say something about small bodies like juries and committees where the asymptotics never kick in, and it has to respect the distinction between tracking facts and aggregating values, which people keep blurring. Mark for each requirement whether it's non-negotiable for the theorem to have any content at all, or just a pet assumption of one camp, and say why.

The part I actually care about, and the thing I keep getting tangled with my advisor on: is there a subset of the desiderata that is jointly unsatisfiable? My worry is that the usual repairs — weight votes by competence, filter out the badly informed, restrict the franchise to the issue-aware — improve accuracy only insofar as they move the procedure away from equal votes, so that "reliable" and "still recognizably democratic" can't both be secured by the same construction, and once you see that, the theorem is an argument for an epistocracy of the well-informed wearing a democratic hat. If that tension is real, say so and say which side has to give; if it's not, show me where the repair keeps equality intact. And tell me which single requirement does the most work knocking out the candidate repairs — I suspect it's the endogenous-competence one but I'm not sure and don't want to build a chapter on a hunch. no scorecard of existing positions, just the criteria and the satisfiability verdict, I'll do the applications myself later. tbh a table is fine if that's easier.
