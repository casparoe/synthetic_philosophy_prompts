I'm a software engineer at a mid-sized company, and I've been asked to give our leadership team a realistic estimate for how long it will take to migrate our ageing monolith to a service-oriented architecture. The trouble is that two people whose judgement I respect have given me figures that point in opposite directions. Our CTO keeps citing an industry study he read years ago claiming that roughly two-thirds of large IT modernisation projects run badly over budget or get abandoned altogether, so he wants us to plan as though we're one of the unlucky ones. My team lead, on the other hand, points out that of the four comparable migrations our company has attempted over the past decade, three finished close to schedule and one was cancelled — and that those four share our codebase, our staffing constraints, and our management culture in a way no industry-wide study ever could. My own instinct is that the team lead's numbers are too few to trust and the CTO's are too generic to apply to us at all, but I can't articulate why without hand-waving, and I'll have to defend whichever figure I put in front of the board.

For context: the project is genuinely large (an estimated eighteen months of work), our company has a decent but not stellar record of finishing what it starts, and the industry study apparently lumped together everything from banks rewriting core systems to retailers replacing e-commerce platforms. I took a statistics course at university, so I'm comfortable with the idea of a sample and a population; what I can't work out is what the right population even is here, and whether there's any principled way to decide rather than just picking the grouping that suits the answer I already want. A friend of mine suggested the whole question is undecidable and I should just present both figures and let the board choose, but that feels like an abdication.

So: please give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. And I'd like you to actually commit — each point should stake out a definite claim or recommendation, not a balanced survey of options. In particular, take a clear position on which evidence should carry more weight and why, tell me whether my instinct to distrust both figures is sound or just timidity dressed up as rigour, and if you think my friend's "present both and let them choose" suggestion is the honest answer, say so plainly and explain what I'd be refusing to take responsibility for.
