Two philosophers have been arguing for a decade and have finally agreed to run a proper adversarial collaboration: one holds that all values are comparable — for any two options, one is better, worse, or they are exactly equal, and saying an option is "on a par" with another is just a confession that we haven't finished measuring — while the other holds Ruth Chang's view that hard choices reveal a fourth relation, parity, and that when options are on a par we choose by constituting ourselves through rational commitment rather than by being led to a verdict by reasons. Draft the joint statement they should produce. I want: (1) the claims both can sign, including about the phenomenology of hard choices, the failure of standard arguments that all hard choices are really just choices under uncertainty or imprecision, and what would count as a case where values are clearly not on a par; (2) a table of their precise residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording — pay particular attention to whether "parity" names a substantive fourth relation or merely an epistemic state of the chooser, since each side suspects the other of smuggling a verbal dispute in here; (3) the cruxes — I'd expect one to be whether the role of self-creation in hard choices is rationally defensible or merely psychologically comforting, and another to be whether comparability is a requirement of practical rationality or just a metaphysical assumption inherited from consequentialism; and (4) for each crux, an argument or thought experiment both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance (e.g., what each expects to happen in a variant of the forcing-case examples: whether making one option minutely better along one dimension destroys the hard choice or leaves it intact). If either side would in fact concede part of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why. Format: numbered agreed-claims section, followed by the disagreements table, followed by cruxes with predictions.
