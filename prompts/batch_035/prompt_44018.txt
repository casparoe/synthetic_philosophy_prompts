I've got about a year of protected research time at a small theory shop that does conceptual work on decision theory and AI governance, and I keep going back and forth on whether to spend any of it on acausal trade. On one hand it feels like the kind of thing that could quietly be upstream of a lot of alignment reasoning — if a future system is going to make deals with agents it can't causally interact with, we'd like to know that in advance. On the other hand it might just be superrationality cosplay with no way to ever check anything. So help me prioritise: which open questions in this area actually matter most, and in what order should someone tackle them given my time budget and the fact that I'm a philosopher-ish type with decent logic but no way to run experiments?

The questions I can see from here, roughly: (1) whether acausal trade is even licensed by evidential decision theory, or whether you need something stronger like functional decision theory, or whether the whole thing quietly collapses into updatelessness plus symmetry assumptions in a trenchcoat; (2) whether "trade" is the right picture at all, versus one agent merely simulating another and unilateraly acting on what it finds — a colleague here insists the mutuality is fake and that this matters for whether there are real equilibria, acausal "prices", anything market-like; (3) commitment races — whether the strategic situation before agents can model each other well is unstable in ways that dominate everything downstream; (4) whether a superintelligence would actually find trading with hypothetical or causally disconnected agents worthwhile, or whether the epistemic cost of pinning down a counterparty eats the gains — this one seems to decide whether multiverse-wide cooperation is a serious idea or a nice story; and (5) what any of this implies for alignment practice — whether a system might enter into acausal deals we'd regard as catastrophic, and whether that's a failure mode you can design against or just a reductio of the whole framework.

I'm less interested in the list than in the defence of the ordering. For each question you put near the top: why it's upstream of the others, what makes it tractable for someone who can't do empirical work (or why it's worth doing despite being intractable), and what would have to become clearer for it to move up or down. And please tell me which bits of the standard framing you think are overrated or just confused — e.g. whether the focus on exotic cosmological counterparties is distracting from the boring decision-theoretic core, or whether it's the other way round. I'd also take a straight answer that the whole area deserves none of my year, if that's what the reasoning supports, as long as you say what would change your mind.
