In two weeks I'm presenting a position paper at an interdisciplinary workshop on AI trustworthiness and safety. My thesis: deception by a large language model should be defined in terms of a mismatch between the model's outputs and its internal epistemic state — the model asserts p while its learned representations encode that p is likely false — rather than in purely behavioral terms such as producing false or misleading outputs. On my view, a model that confidently asserts a falsehood while having no internal representation that distinguishes it from the truth has made an error but hasn't deceived anyone, whereas a model that tells the user what they want to hear while its internals point the other way is deceiving even if the assertion happens to come out true. The audience is roughly two-thirds interpretability researchers who think probes for internal epistemic states are artifacts that fail to generalize off-distribution, plus two philosophers of language who suspect that belief-talk simply doesn't apply to transformers, so my definition is undefined at its core, plus a few policy people who want a behavioral definition an external auditor could apply without running experiments on model weights. Prepare my Q&A sheet: the objections I will actually get from this audience, ordered by how much damage each would do to the paper, each phrased the way that particular subgroup would phrase it, each with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is weakest. The sheet must cover at least the following trouble cases I suspect the philosophers have already spotted: the role-playing model that internally registers p but asserts not-p while in character as a dishonest narrator; the sycophantic model that hedges a claim it registers as correct because the user resents it — deception, bullshit in Frankfurt's sense, or neither; and the worry that a model trained by gradient descent has no stable, discrete epistemic state at all, just a pile of context-dependent features that merely behave like one. Include the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.
