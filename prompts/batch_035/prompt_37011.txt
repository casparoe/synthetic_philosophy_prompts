I'm a novelist stuck on a scene, and I need someone to reverse-engineer a character from her track record. The character, Petra, is closely modeled on a college friend of mine — call her M — and I've been seeding Petra's dialogue with the actual opinions M has vented at me over the years, so she feels like a person rather then a ventriloquist dummy. Now the plot has handed Petra a vote on the city council, and I've realized I don't actually know what M would say, and I don't want to cheat by making Petra conveniently vague in the moment.

So here's M's record, as best I can reconstruct it. (1) She said she'd save her own drowning kid rather than two strangers' kids, and didn't think this needed defending. (2) She thinks it's completely fine — basically the point of having a country — that our government protects our own workers' jobs even when the foreigners losing out are just as badly off. (3) On her own giving, though, she's ruthless: borders are, in her words, magical lines, and her donations go wherever a dollar does the most good, and she teases her sister for giving to the local food bank instead of abroad. (4) When I once suggested you surely owe a bit more to a fellow citizen you've never met than to a foreigner you've never met, she said a stranger is a stranger, doesn't matter what passport. (5) During a hurricane abroad she insisted our embassy evacuate citizens who'd lived there for decades and, in one case she knew of, dodged local taxes the whole time — thats just what an embassy is for — while also being adamant that a woman who'd lived, worked, and paid taxes here for thirty years without papers shouldn't be deported over a paperwork error.

The scene: a bad flu season, the city has a few thousand surplus vaccine doses (the federal allocation overshot local demand), and two petitions come in — one from recently returned expat citizens who want doses reserved for our own people, and one from a clinic serving exactly the sort of long-term undocumented residents from item (5). Petra has to pick or split. What would M say? And honestly, the answer matters less to me than the reasoning: what rules actually fit the whole track record (I can see at least two — one where what matters is participation in a shared cooperative scheme, one where it's citizenship as such, and maybe a third where the state may play favorites but owes everyone a floor of decent treatment) — what does each predict for the vaccine scene, which one would you bet on for M and why, and is there a further case I could drop into a later chapter that would really force the rules apart? Please write in American English (the manuscript is US-set even though M keeps saying queue), and since this is a reconstruction of one person from five data points, be upfront about how confident you are at each step and flag the part of the reconstruction you'd bet against.
