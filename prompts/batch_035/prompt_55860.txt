So that's what I want: restate Lakatos's picture of how scientific research programmes live and die in the vocabulary of software engineering, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report — what translates cleanly, what has to be distorted to fit, and what has no expression at all in my vocabulary.

Here's the twist that makes me think this isn't just an idle translation exercise. My working suspicion is that the residue will be concentrated in one place: Lakatos's notion of a *progressive* programme isn't just "changes keep unlocking new capabilities," because in software that's trivially achievable — any codebase can keep shipping features — whereas Lakatos wanted some notion of *novel predictions*, of the programme successfully anticipating results it wasn't designed around, which is much closer to generalizing to unseen inputs than to feature velocity. If I'm right, then my vocabulary quietly substitutes a proxy for the actual criterion, and arguments about whether a programme is progressive would come out wrong in translation. I'd like you to check that suspicion rather than indulge it.
