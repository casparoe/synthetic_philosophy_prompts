I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background — my relevant training is a Bayesian statistics course and too many years spent arguing with flaky test suites — so please write for that reader. Here is the setup, then the question.

Our integration suite is flaky: when the build is genuinely broken, it fails about 90% of the time; when the build is fine, it fails about 4% of the time. Straight conditioning handles that layer fine. The problem is the layer above it: the dashboard sometimes shows stale results, I occasionally misread a log line, and on some mornings I end up only about 80% confident that what I saw was a failure at all. My teammate has been reading philosophy of probability and says the right move in that situation is what he calls Jeffrey conditioning: treat the uncertain observation as a probability distribution over the possible observations, and update each hypothesis using that distribution. My instinct is different: model the whole pipeline — the test's behavior, the dashboard's caching, my reading of the output — as one joint distribution and condition on the event that I ended up believing I saw a failure. To me his rule looks like a convenient approximation you reach for when you can't or won't model the pipeline, not the normatively correct update.

We've managed to state the disagreement precisely. Let P be the claim that, for a rational agent who is less than certain what they observed, the correct update is Jeffrey conditioning on the observation, rather than conditioning on a full model of the process that produced the uncertain evidence. My credence in P is about 0.35; his is about 0.7. Please give me your own number, and more importantly the decomposition. Some terms I'd expect to see, though add or restructure as you see fit: (a) the probability that the two procedures can actually give different answers from the same prior and the same uncertain observation, as opposed to the rule being a notational variant of full conditioning; (b) the probability that, where they diverge, the full-pipeline conditioning is the one the agent's actual evidence supports; (c) the probability that there are realistic cases where the pipeline genuinely cannot be modeled, so that something like Jeffrey conditioning is needed as a standing norm rather than a stopgap; and (d) the probability that successive uncertain updates should be required to be order-independent, since I gather that Jeffrey conditioning doesn't always have that property and I'm not sure whether to care. Give each term a number and a one-line justification, then combine them into your overall credence.

Second, tell me what argument or concrete scenario would move your overall number the most. Third, as a sanity check on your position: assuming the dashboard is stale 2% of the time regardless of the build's state, and that my chance of misreading the output is independent of everything else, does your view say his rule and my full-pipeline conditioning give different final credences that the build is broken in my case? If they agree there, say what you'd have to change about the case to make them come apart.
