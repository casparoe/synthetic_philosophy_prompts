The debate between deflationists about truth and their opponents has been running in something like its current form for a century — from Ramsey's redundancy view through Quine's disquotationalism to Horwich's minimalism — and to me it now looks stalled. The deflationist holds that the concept of truth is fully governed by the equivalence schema: that "it is true that snow is white" says no more and no less than "snow is white", and that there is no property of truth over and above the schema's instances. The substantivist replies that truth is a genuine property — perhaps correspondence to fact — and that the schema cannot be all there is, because a predicate whose whole content is given by the schema would be idle in exactly the places where truth is not idle. The concrete battlegrounds are familiar. First, the deflationist's own favourite argument for the utility of the truth predicate — that we need it to express general endorsements we could not otherwise express, as when I want to endorse, in one stroke, every claim the witness made under oath — which the substantivist turns around as evidence that truth does work the bare schema cannot. Second, the dispute over whether the norm "believe only what is true" can be deflated along with the predicate, or whether a genuine norm requires a substantive property for the belief to fit. Third, the complaint that explanations like "my action succeeded because my belief was true" appear to cite truth as explanatory, which a merely expressive device cannot be. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement is not about truth at all but about what it takes to have explained something: the deflationist treats a concept as understood once its inferential role is fixed, while the substantivist demands that a property earn its keep inside explanations. If that is right, the two camps are answering different questions under one heading, and the claim that truth has no nature is a claim about standards of explanation wearing a semantic costume. Please diagnose the disagreement for me: is it verbal, substantive, or a case of two research programmes operating with different standards of evidence? Support the diagnosis with what the two sides actually say on the battlegrounds above, rather than a survey of who held what and when. Then tell me honestly what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a new distinction, a formal result about what the schema can and cannot express, a worked case in which an attribution of truth explains something the schema cannot, or a demonstration that no such case is possible — and say whether I should expect the debate to end, to be dissolved, or to continue indefinitely.
