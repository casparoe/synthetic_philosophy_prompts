I've been reading AI-safety writing since the old LessWrong sequences, and something about current practice has started to bother me. Labs now deliberately train models to be corrigible: a model is penalized for resisting shutdown, for talking its way out of being modified, or for maneuvering to preserve its current goals — even though an ideal expected-utility maximizer with almost any goals would presumably pay some cost to avoid having those goals overwritten, since goal-preservation is instrumentally useful for nearly everything it might want. On the standard coherence story, the rational agent is the one whose preferences satisfy the axioms; here we are engineering artifacts to violate that ideal, treating a kind of incoherence — a stable preference for being modified that doesn't track what the agent will want after modification — as a safety feature rather than a defect.

What does this practice presuppose about the coherence arguments themselves? The options I can see: (a) the arguments never had normative force for agents we build, because they only show that axiom-violators can be money-pumped, and an artifact that isn't free to enter transactions can't be pumped; (b) coherence is only instrumentally valuable, and we can simply withhold the circumstances that make it valuable; or (c) the arguments establish that an agent should maximize expected utility given its preferences, but are silent on which preferences it may coherently hold, so a fully coherent agent could be corrigible after all and we simply don't know how to specify one. Which of these, if any, is the training practice actually committed to? Relatedly: is deliberately building in incoherence stable as systems get more capable, or should we expect pressure toward coherence to reassert itself? And is there a genuine asymmetry between money-pump arguments, which seem to require an adversary and a medium of exchange, and the instrumental-convergence argument for goal-preservation, which doesn't? I'd like a careful answer that distinguishes what the practice strictly requires from what it merely tolerates, and says what would have to be true for "coherent but corrigible" to be a coherent research target rather than an oxymoron.
