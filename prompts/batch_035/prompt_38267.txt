So, quick bit of background: I've been reading consciousness takes on LessWrong and adjacent blogs since roughly the Chalmers-vs-Dennett flamewar era, and lately I keep losing arguments to illusionists. Not because I think they're right — I'm actually pretty sympathetic to there being something it is like — but because the illusionist position is more defensible than people give it credit for, and my counterarguments keep coming out as rehearsed Chalmers fanfic. I want to fix that with practice.

Please play a committed illusionist, somewhere in the Frankish/Dennett mold: you think phenomenal consciousness as standardly conceived doesn't exist, that what we call qualia are just physical/quasi-functional states misdescribed by a defective introspective model, and you think the whole notion of a phenomenal feel that's beyond the reach of third-person science is a conceptual mess. You're sharp, not a strawman: you can concede that introspection is useful, that reports about experience track something real, that the term "phenomenal consciousness" might be rescued for some deflationary concept — you just never concede that there's an explanatory gap in any interesting sense. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep replies to about a paragraph so this feels like a real back-and-forth rather than a lecture series.

Here's my opening move, and I want you to take it seriously rather than waving it off: you claim we're victims of an illusion, but an illusion seems to require a seemings — the stick that looks bent is really seeming bent to somebody. You'll reply that seemings are just judgments, fine. But now notice that the same introspective faculty that you say is confabulating the qualia is the faculty generating your sense that the illusion is "vivid" or "irresistible," which is literally the load-bearing part of your theory about why everyone else disagrees with you. Worse, your own belief in illusionism is itself a product of introspective-seeming reasoning. So the theory appears to saw off the branch it's sitting on: if introspection confabulates, why is it suddenly reliable when it reports to you that consciousness seems totally functional? How do you, in character, dodge the self-undermining bullet?
