At a philosophy reading group, two members got into it over Lewis's modal realism and never resolved it. Maya's opening move was that the theory is a spectacular violation of parsimony: it posits infinitely many concrete spatiotemporally isolated worlds, none observable from ours, just to analyze our talk about possibility — so Occam's razor alone should kill it. Dev answered with what he said was Lewis's own reply: parsimony is about the number of *kinds* of entities a theory posits, not the number of entities; we happily believe in infinitely many numbers or electrons without feeling the razor, and Lewis's ontology adds no new kinds, since everything in the other worlds is made of the same stuff, obeying the same laws, as things here. Maya came back that number can't be wholly irrelevant — if a theory said every electron was accompanied by an invisible duplicate electron, that would count against it even though it posits no new kinds — and that a razor indifferent to number is toothless against bloat, since any theory could pad itself with copies for free. Dev replied that the duplicate-electron theory is bad not because of parsimony but because its extra entities do no explanatory work, whereas Lewis's worlds earn their keep by giving a unified, reductive account of modality, counterfactuals, and content — so the complaint is really about net theoretical cost-benefit, not about parsimony at all. Maya's last word was that 'no explanatory work' is just the razor under another name, and that the trade is terrible anyway: the appearances to be saved — that things could have gone otherwise — are far less puzzling than the infinite concrete worlds doing the saving, so the explanation is more mysterious than what it explains. Please adjudicate this exchange move by move: say who is right about which point, where each side's strongest argument is, whether Dev's qualitative/quantitative distinction really does the work he claims for it, and what, if anything, both of them are missing. Finish by saying who comes out ahead overall and why.
