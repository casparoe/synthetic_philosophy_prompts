Is there a name for this thing? I keep noticing a difference between two kinds of made-up facts, and I want to know if the difference has its own term. Some made-up facts seem held up by each person's belief individually, and they wobble as soon as people quietly stop buying in — a rumor that a bank is insolvent, a fad, someone's reputation as a good mechanic. If enough people privately decide it's false, it just becomes false, no announcement needed. But other made-up facts seem to grip you from the outside. A friend of mine is Black in a country where that has real consequences, and no amount of her not believing in race changes anything. Money is similar in a weird way — it only works because everyone takes it, but no single person, and honestly not even a large group, can just opt out. The shopkeeper who concludes that dollars are just paper still has to accept them. And seeing through it does nothing: you can fully understand that the whole thing is held up by collective attitudes and it still applies to you with full force.

Is there a term for facts of that second kind — jointly created but individually inescapable? People keep telling me "socially constructed" covers all of it, but that seems way too broad, it lumps the fragile stuff and the rock-solid stuff together as if they were the same phenomenon. "Coordination problem" also isnt quite right — with a coordination problem you can at least imagine everyone agreeing to switch at midnight, like when countries change over their currencies. Some of these dont seem switchable even that way. What I want is the actual carving: what exactly makes the second kind different, where the line between the two falls (if there even is one line and not a spectrum), and whether the philosophers who write about money and race have separate vocabulary for the two. If there's no established term, feel free to coin one, but please dont just hand me "social construct" again and call it a day.
