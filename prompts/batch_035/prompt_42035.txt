I've been keeping a running list of cases where I want to say a thing has a reason for doing what it does, even tho nobody -- no mind, no designer, not even a committee -- ever reasoned anything out. Dennett has this phrase, free-floating rationales, for roughly this. Anyway here are my gut verdicts and I want to know if there's a principle underneath them or if I'm just equivocating on the word "reason."

The heart beats in order to pump blood. Verdict: yes, real rationale, even though no one gave the heart one and the heart has no idea what it's for.

The reason we drive on the right in the US is... nothing, it's just what everyone does, history locked it in. Same for why English kept "children" instead of "childs" -- accident of persistence. Verdict: no rationale in either, just convention.

But the reason a rumor mutates as it spreads is that the scary memorable versions survive retelling better. Nobody designs the mutation. Verdict: I can't help hearing a rationale there. The rumor is "trying" to spread in the same sense the heart is "trying" to pump.

Here's one I flip on constantly: the reason the dollar is worth anything is that everyone accepts it because everyone accepts it. No one intends that, no one could've planned it, and yet it feels like there's a rationale -- the norm exists FOR something (coordination), unlike driving on the right, which exists for nothing. Or am I wrong to split them? They're both just conventions that persisted.

A sunset doesn't happen in order to end the day, obviously no. But the reason rivers meander is... no, that's just least-work physics, no rationale either. Worth noting my brain even offers that one.

The weird one: there's this classic case about birth control pills and blood clots -- the reason women on the pill have lower net clot risk is that the pill prevents pregnancy, and pregnancy raises clot risk. So there's a reason the pill has this overall effect, but every piece of the causal chain is a byproduct, nothing in the story is for anything. Verdict: the explanation is fine, but there's no rationale anywhere in it. A reason-shaped explanation with no rationale at any level. Maybe that's allowed and my list conflates two things?

And the case I actually care about: people say stuff like the LLM's next-token prediction is the "function" of its outputs -- nobody at inference time intends prediction, the model doesn't intend anything, but the outputs exist because they were selected for predicting well. Does that make the rationale free-floating in my sense, or does it float back to the humans who did the selecting? The heart's selection history has no reasoners anywhere in it; the LLM's has reasoners at the origin. Does that break it or not?

So: is there a principle that gets all or most of these? (selection history? something being used? something being relied upon by something else?) State it precisely enough that it gives verdicts on cases I havent listed, tell me which of my verdicts you'd overturn and why, and run it on the LLM case and the dollar case specifically, since those are the two where my intuitions fight each other. If my heart/rumor verdict and my children/no-rationale verdict can't both survive the same principle, I want to know which one you'd sacrifice.
