Is there a name for this? I work on interpretability at a small ML safety-ish lab, and we keep having the same fight about whether a model is "using the same mechanism" in two situations, and I've slowly realized everyone on the team means something different by it. Concretely: in one family of prompts a particular attention head does the language-ID work (figuring out whether its french or english, say), in another family a different head does it, but the way information flows between layers looks identical if you squint. Half the team says same mechanism (same computation, different parts doing it), the other half says different mechanism (different parts, end of story). And it actually matters: our anomaly detector is supposed to flag behavior that's weird *for the mechanism the model usually uses*, and which answer you give decides whether the new head counts as anomalous or boring. By-parts, it's a flag; by-computation, it isn't.

I'm pretty sure this is an old fight that philosophers had decades ago, maybe something adjacent to whether the same mind can run on different hardware, but I don't know that literature. And to be clear, a bare label won't help me — I want to know what the candidate notions of "same mechanism" actually are, which (if any) has real support behind it, and where each draws the line, because right now "same mechanism" is doing like three jobs in our codebase and I suspect at least one of them should be a different word. Also one of our guys keeps saying it's just type vs token and I feel like that's not quite it but can't put my finger on why — if he's wrong, tell me why, and if he's sort of right, tell me what it's missing.
