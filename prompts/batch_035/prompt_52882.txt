I'm a novelist — five books in, mostly domestic realism — and I'm in a fight with my editor that I can't resolve. My current manuscript follows a friendship between two women across roughly fifteen years, and the friendship erodes: small betrayals, long silences, a move to another city, reconciliations that each give back a bit less than the last one. My editor (who I love, but still) says readers need a scene they can point to as the moment the friendship ends, and she wants me to engineer it — raise the stakes chapter by chapter so the break actually lands. Without that, she says, the last section will feel mushy, like I declined to make a choice.

Here's my problem: I genuinely beleive there is no such moment in the story I'm telling. Every time I try to isolate a candidate scene, I notice they could have survived it — they've survived worse earlier in the book — and when I look one scene later, it's already over in a way I can't pin down any better. It's not that the friendship never ends. By the final chapter they haven't spoken in years and neither one grieves it. It clearly ends, and yet no scene is the ending. My editor says that's a cop-out, that if I can't name the scene it's because I haven't found it yet, and that in real life there IS a moment — some actual last conversation that was the last one — and the fact that nobody involved could identify it at the time is just an epistemic limit, mine or the characters'.

So we've deadlocked, and I want to know who's actually right before I do another rewrite. Is she right that every ending like this has a fact-of-the-matter moment, even an unnoticed one — that there's a determinate answer to "which scene was the last one where it was still alive"? Or am I right that a claim like "by chapter forty the friendship was already over" is somehow neither true nor false, the way it feels weird to say that when chapter thirty-nine and chapter forty-one look basically identical? And then the practical question, which honestly matters most: if she's wrong and there's no moment, can a novel still dramatize an ending that happens at no particular point — is there a structure for that — or is that just muddle that readers will punish me for?
