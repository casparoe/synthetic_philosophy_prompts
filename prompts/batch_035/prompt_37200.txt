I've been trying to figure out what I actually mean by "the probability of X" by going with my gut on a bunch of cases, and it's a mess, so help me out. A coin I've flipped maybe ten thousand times, coming up heads just about half the time: the probability of heads is clearly 1/2, and that fact is out there in the world, it's not about what anyone believes. A different coin flipped a hundred times that comes up heads 62 times: my gut says the probability of heads on the next flip is somewhere above half, maybe 0.55ish, which is weird because there's no "true frequency" for a single upcoming flip. My doctor telling me there's a 70% chance my thyroid nodule is benign: the thing is already benign or not, it's just that I don't know which, but the 70% still feels like more than a statement about my ignorance -- it's tracking something about the world, like the base rates for nodules that look like mine on a scan. An atom of some isotope where the half-life is precisely known: the probability it decays within the hour seems like an objective fact about that atom, and it feels like even a being who knew everything about the current state of the universe couldn't do better (putting aside hidden-variable stuff). But now the Rubicon: someone tells me the probability that Caesar crossed the Rubicon is 0.7, and my gut says that's just wrong, it's 1 or 0, and the 0.7 is merely their confidence. Yet a fair coin already flipped and hidden under a cup: I'm totally comfortable saying the probability it's heads is 1/2, even though that's exactly as settled as Caesar. And at trial, when an expert says the probability the defendant left the print is 0.9999, I nod along, but when a pundit says the probability the defendant is guilty is 0.8, I wince -- that one seems like it shouldn't be called a probability at all. Is there a single account of what probabilities are -- frequencies in the long run, degrees of belief, propensities or objective tendencies of individual setups, whatever -- that delivers all these verdicts? If not, which of my verdicts should go, and what's the least ugly package left standing? Also please tell me what the surviving view says about a case I haven't made up my mind on: engineers saying a first-of-its-kind reactor design has "about a 10% chance of a failure event in year one," where there's no track record, no known physics of chance like decay, just one machine that will either fail or not.
