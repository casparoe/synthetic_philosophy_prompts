In my philosophy of mind seminar we have just finished the unit on multiple realizability and functionalism, and for my term paper I have been trying to state precisely which physical systems could realize a mind, by first cataloguing my own verdicts on cases. My tentative principle was that sameness of functional organization — sameness of causal roles — is what matters, not the substrate. My verdicts: an octopus, whose nervous system is organized almost nothing like mine, obviously has pains and beliefs. A patient whose neurons are replaced one at a time with silicon components duplicating each neuron's input–output profile remains conscious and remains the same person. My own brain emulated in software and run a thousand times slower than real time still hosts a mind. A lookup table that maps every finite interaction history to the outputs my emulated brain would produce is not a mind, even though it is input–output equivalent to one. A billion people passing slips of paper so as to implement the state-transition table of my emulated brain do not thereby compose a mind, though I waver on this. And a paramecium, or an elevator controller, which behaves in some respects like a minimal agent, has no mental states at all.

The trouble is that the lookup table and the slow emulation each preserve the causal organization at some grain, and I cannot say which grain matters without the principle becoming either too permissive (letting in the table) or too restrictive (ruling out the octopus). Is there a single principle — a principled way of individuating functional states — that captures all six verdicts? If not, which of my verdicts should I give up, and why that one? Please also tell me what your principle predicts for two cases I have not decided: a mind implemented redundantly across three noisy copies of an emulation that outvote each other, and a robot whose every behavioral capacity is realized by enormous but perfectly ordinary clockwork. I would rather have an honest principle that overturns one of my verdicts than a patched-together one that saves them all.
