I sit on the grants committee of a donor network in which roughly thirty members pool their money and allocate it to charities by majority vote. Over the past two years the membership has split into two nearly equal blocs: one prioritizes long-termist projects (biosecurity, AI safety), the other near-term animal welfare. Neither bloc can muster a majority alone, and our meetings have decayed into mutual obstruction — each bloc votes down the other's proposals, we disburse less every cycle, and members are quietly drifting away.

Two members have now proposed adopting a formal "moral trade" protocol — an idea I first encountered on rationalist forums, where it is usually credited to Nick Bostrom. Under the proposal, each bloc commits in advance to voting for the other's top-ranked proposals up to a fixed share of the annual budget, in exchange for reciprocal support. The appeal is supposed to be that each bloc can expect, by its own lights, more of what it values from the traded package than from the status quo of mutual blocking — with no shared moral metric required, since each side evaluates the deal entirely by its own axiology.

Our chair has solicited written opinions ahead of next month's meeting, and I would like to submit one. Please draft a decision memo recommending whether the network should adopt the protocol, either in some defensible modified form or not at all. It should (a) give a clear, actionable recommendation with concrete terms — for instance, what share of the annual budget each bloc's guaranteed support would be capped at, and for what commitment horizon; (b) engage honestly with the strongest objection on the losing side, voiced by one of our members, that trading votes on moral convictions is a kind of corruption, akin to congressional logrolling, in which principles are treated as bargaining chips; and (c) address the boundary case that worries me most: whether the same logic would license trading with a bloc whose commitments we consider abhorrent, and if not, say precisely what distinguishes our situation from that one.
