Quick backgroun, then the ask. I teach epistemology, and our department is doing one of those end-of-term symposia where everyone submits a one-page statement of their current views and then gets grilled by colleagues for twenty minutes. Mine is below. Before I send it in I want an honest internal audit, because I have a feeling something in here doesn't hang together and I can't tell what.

1. Knowledge is true belief that arises out of cognitive ability — the credit view, roughly. What attracts me to it is that it answers the Meno problem: knowledge is better than a lucky true belief because getting it right is your doing, it's attributable to you as a cognitive agent.

2. The extra value of knowledge over mere true belief is not merely instrumental. If knowledge were better only because it tends to lead to more true beliefs later on, that seems to me like giving the game away — the Meno problem just comes back at one remove.

3. Veritism about epistemic value: the only thing with final epistemic value is believing truly (and maybe disbelieving falsely). Everything else — methods, virtues, evidence — is instrumentally valuable insofar as it gets you truth.

4. Process reliabilism about justification is basically right. A justified belief is one produced by a belief-forming process that's reliable across the relevant range of cases.

5. Testimonial knowledge is easy and everywhere. If I ask a stranger on the street for directions and she tells me the truth, I know where the station is, full stop. And notice that the credit for getting it right belongs overwhelmingly to her, not to me — that doesn't seem to stop me from knowing.

6. In the fake barn county cases, the subject doesn't know, even though the belief is produced by the very same reliable process that works perfectly fine in normal counties.

So: go through these pair by pair. Which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look like it until you press on them, and which are just obviously compatible? Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one tell me what would have to give and what that revision would cost me with respect to the other commitments on the list. If there's a single change that would clean up most of the mess, say so — but only if it's actually true, not just to give me a tidy answer. And since you're the auditor here, be specific about which commitment does the damage in each conflict, because I honestly can't see it.
