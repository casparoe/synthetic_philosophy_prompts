Is there a name for this pattern? It's happened to me twice recently. First time: I read a study, beleived the finding, then came across a debunking of the study (supposedly serious methodological flaws), and my confidence dropped a lot. Later it turned out the debunking itself was wrong — the person doing it had misread the statistics. But my confidence never fully recovered, even once I knew, on reflection, that the original evidence was as good as it ever was. Second time: a smart friend disagreed with me about something, I lowered my confidence accordingly, and then found out he'd only read a bad summary of the issue and had no idea what the actual arguments were. My confidence stayed down anyway, like the disagreemnt had poisoned the well regardless of its merits.

I gather there's stuff written about "defeaters," but this seems different: the defeater is itself defeated, and yet part of its effect survives its own debunking. So my questions: does this residue have an established name, and how is it supposed to be different from ordinary undercutting defeat? And a related case I keep wondering about — when the debunking can't be checked at all (the study is retracted and the data is gone, so no one can ever tell me if the retraction was justified), is that the same phenomenon or a different one? If there's no established term, I'd like you to coin one with a proper definition and boundary cases, not just a label.
