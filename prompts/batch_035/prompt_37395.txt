I want this argument to actually be a real philosophical live wire, not Tamsin spouting winions (my autocorrect insists that's not a word, whatever).

The point of the case is to test whether our statue-and-clay intuitions track the objects themselves or just the way we describe them — that's what fixes what counts as cheating. So no fair writing a case that just stipulates the verdict; it has to generate the verdicts from how normal people think.

Specs: two scenarios, identical in every material fact — same clay, same hands, same physical process — but differing ONLY in the narrative framing of the process (one framed as reshaping, one framed as destroying and making anew), and the framing difference should be something a normal person with no philosophy background would find natural, not contrived.
