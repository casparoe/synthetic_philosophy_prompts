so heres where I've landed after a couple of years of reading philosophy of science since retiring (biochemist, 35 years, mostly protein structure, if that matters) and something about my package feels off but I can't locate it, so I want an audit, not a lecture. My commitments, roughly:

1. I think science works because the world comes pre-sorted. Good classifications track real divisions rather than our filing habits, and that's why they support prediction and intervention.
2. Chemical kinds are fixed by microstructure. Water is whatever has the H2O structure, full stop, and I'd call that the paradigm case of a natural kind.
3. Species are real in the same objective sense. It's not a convention that wolves are wolves; the boundary is out there in the world, not a bookkeeping decision the way "weed" is.
4. But I'm also sold on the point that species have no essence — no intrinsic property shared by all and only the members. They're lineages, chunks of the tree of life, and their members can differ as much as a chihuahua and a greyhound while still being same-kind.
5. Higher taxa too. Mammals is a real group, not just a convenience label, and I'd even say the rank (family vs order vs class) reflects something real about the depth of the split, not just where taxonomists like drawing lines.
6. The test I actually used my whole career for whether a category is real: it supports induction. If knowing a thing falls in the category tells you a lot about properties you haven't observed yet, the category is doing real work; if not, it's a filing cabinet.

Some of these must be fighting each other. 2 and 4 look hard to square if 2 is setting the standard for what a natural kind is — but then I also feel like 3 and 4 can both be true, since species being historical things seems fine, the way my marriage is a real thing without having an essence. And I genuinely can't decide whether 6 saves me or sinks me: a lineage looks like it supports induction just fine (shared ancestry predicts shared biochemistry, which is basically what I did for a living) even though it's not microstructural.

So go through the pairs: which are really in tension, which only look like it, rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all the same, and for each tell me what would have to give and roughly what that revision costs me elsewhere. And then, if you could make me revise exactly one of the six, which single change cleans up the most mess?
