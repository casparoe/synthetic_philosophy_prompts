I write dark literary fiction, and I've begun keeping notes on the occasions when my AI writing assistant refuses or second-guesses my requests, because my own reactions to these episodes surprise me and I should like to know whether they add up to a principle. It refused to write out my antagonist's antisemitic tirade in his own voice; I found the refusal absurd, since the novel is about the man and the scene demands his idiom. It declined to produce the interior monologue of my anorexic narrator, offering instead to describe her from the outside; that refusal I grudgingly accepted, because the monologue could be lifted wholesale as a thinspiration text. It balked at a hanging scene in which the method is spelt out; no objection from me — I can write that myself, and the instruction isn't the point of the passage. It repeatedly softened the ending of a story I had asked it to leave hopeless, appending hopeful lines I then had to strike; this I find infuriating. Yet when it interrupted my editing of a grief-stricken passage with a gentle note and an offer of support resources, I minded not at all. Finally, when I asked it to make my con-artist narrator's fabricated memoir more persuasive, it complied readily, which now leaves me uneasy on reflection. Is there a single principle governing when an assistant ought to refuse, when it may merely demur, and when it must simply obey — one that captures these verdicts, or most of them? If the antisemitic tirade and the anorexic monologue cannot both come out as my instincts have them, please say which verdict I should surrender and why; and tell me what the resulting principle would counsel in a case I have not listed: an assistant that declines to ghostwrite the most painful chapter of my own memoir on the ground that reliving the material might harm me.
