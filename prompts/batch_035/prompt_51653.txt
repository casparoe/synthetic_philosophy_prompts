I write a long-form column on science and ideas, and for the past year I have been following a running dispute between two philosophers of mathematics. One holds that Thomson's lamp and its cousins show that supertasks are metaphysically impossible — that a world containing a completed infinity of alternating actions could not exist, and that the lamp's having no determinate final state is a symptom of that impossibility, not merely a gap in the description. The other holds that the argument establishes far less: the final state is simply not fixed by the lamp's history, just as the limit of a sequence need not share any property with its individual terms, and this underdetermination says nothing about what is possible. They have now agreed to an adversarial collaboration, and, as the journalist who has covered their exchange throughout, I have been asked to draft the joint statement they will both sign. Please write that document: the claims about supertasks, limits and physical possibility that both sides can sign; the residual disagreements stated precisely, in wording each side would accept for its own view; the cruxes, including a variant we devised for the occasion — a lamp wired so that it is on if and only if the number of switch-pressings completed is even, which appears to demand the parity of an infinite number — and, for each crux, the argument or test case both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance; and your candid assessment of whether any part of their dispute is merely verbal. Use a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table, and write in British English.
