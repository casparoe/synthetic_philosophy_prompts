I'm a phil mind PhD student and I've got a draft (well, draft-ish, its more like structured notes with a few sections actually written out) for a paper on Dennett's quining qualia essay, and I'd love comments before I embarass myself at our department's work-in-progress seminar next week. The angle I'm going for is kind of a new framing so I especially want to know if the framing is doing any real work or if its just relabeled Dennett.

The basic idea: the standard reading of the Chase/Sanborn coffee case (you know, the two coffee tasters -- one whose qualia shifted but he judges the taste the same because his standards shifted with them, the other whose qualia stayed the same but his standards drifted so he judges it as changed) is that it shows intrapersonal comparisons of qualia are impossible, so qualia explanatory idle. Fine. But I want to say something stronger and I'm not sure it works: that the case is best understood as a *measurement failure*, and once you see it that way, qualia come out not just idle but as an incoherent posit, like positing a quantity that has no possible measurement procedure that could even in principle converge on stable values. My thought is that psychometrics went through this exact thing -- a construct (like general intelligence or whatever) can only be a posit if there are reliability and validity conditions it could meet. If every attempt to measure it gives you values that drift independently of the construct itself, you don't just have an unmeasured construct, you have nothing. The construct is *constituted* by the measurement practices, in some sense, and when those practices can't be stabilized, the posit fails.

So the essay sections I have so far:

1. The standard reading: Chase/Sanborn shows introspective comparisons underdetermine whether qualia changed, so qualia do no explanatory work. (This is just Dennett, I'm aware.)

2. The measurement turn: I want to argue the case is a test-retest reliability failure for the "instrument" of introspection. Normally when an instrument is unreliable you say the quantity is there but we can't measure it. But for qualia the catch is that introspection isn't measuring something independently characterized -- there's no independent handle on the quale at all, its not like temperature where you can compare mercury expansion against alcohol expansion against your finger in a bucket. So the reliability failure infects the quantity itself. When the only possible access route is one that demonstrably drifts, the notion of the "true" value of the drift vs. the "true" value of the thing measured becomes indeterminate.

3. Objection I'm worried about: the phenomenal conservative says this conflates the epistemology with the metaphysics. Sure we can't *know* which of Chase/Sanborn's situation we're in, but one of them is true, and that's enough for the posit to be coherent. I want to reply that this is like saying there's a fact about whether a point on a featureless homogeneous plane is "really" moving -- a state of affairs with no possible difference-maker. Does that reply work or is it question-begging? Like, the conservative will just bite the bullet and say some facts are inaccessible.

4. A bit where I try to make the incoherence charge more precise with what I'm calling "drift independence": the worry isn't just that judgments drift, it's that judgment-drift and quale-drift are explanatorily symmetric, nothing breaks the tie even counterfactually, and posits in that situation are the ones that get quined (I gesture at something similar happening with absolute position in Newtonian spacetime, or maybe with dispositions prior to their stimulus conditions? not sure the dispositions one works).
