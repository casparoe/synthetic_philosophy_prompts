ok so I'm not a philosophy person at all — took one elective in college and mostly remember the professor's tie — but a while back I read a summary of Kant's idea that you should only act on rules you'd be okay with everyone acting on, and its been rattling around my head ever since. The thing that strikes me is that if you made it an actual test, it would work like a real bureaucracy: people would show up with their rule worded in whatever way makes it pass, some rules would get rejected because a world where everyone follows them couldn't even exist (like everyone making promises they intend to break whenever it's convenient — promising itself would stop meaning anything), while others only get rejected because the world is possible but lousy, like nobody ever helping anyone or nobody bothering to develop their talents. Apparently Kant himself sorted these into two different kinds of failure, which to me sounds exactly like a government office with two different rejection letters in the drawer.

So heres what I want: a FAQ page, maybe 600-700 words, from a fictional government agency — call it the Board of Universalization, or something better if you think of it — in a country where the test is literally law: before you act on a personal rule, you have to get it cleared for universalization. Write it deadpan, in the tired voice of an agency that has been processing applications for decades. The jokes should come from the real known problems with the test, not invented ones: applicants writing their maxims absurdly narrow so they squeak through (the FAQ's advice on how to phrase your rule, plus some clerkly grumbling that every application somehow contains the phrase "when I specifically feel like it"); the split between rules rejected outright because the universalized world can't exist and rules rejected only because no rational person could want that world — the FAQ should have separate sections for these, maybe "Category A: The World Breaks" and "Category B: The World Holds, Unfortunately," without any section explaining which failure is philosophically worse; and the quiet fact that the Board pretty much always ends up approving whatever the applicant was going to do anyway, since no application in its history has ever been decided without the adjudicators first checking how they feel about the case — put that in somewhere without ever saying it out loud. My brother-in-law claims the whole test is just people dressing up their gut feelings in fancy language, and part of me wants the piece to leave room for him being right without tipping its hand.

Two more things. Don't name Kant anywhere in the FAQ itself — the agency should talk as if it invented all of this and finds the philosophy a bit beneath it. And somewhere near the end, I'd like one question from an applicant asking who reviews the Board's own rules while it's on duty — answer it in the flattest, most bureaucratic way you can manage.
