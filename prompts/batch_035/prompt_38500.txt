i'm on the governance working group for our food co-op (bit over 200 members) and we're finally ditching plurality voting for electing our 3-person board. we'll use ranked ballots. people keep throwing method names around (borda, condorcet, IRV, approval, whatever) and one member keeps saying "just pick one that's strategy-proof, that's the only real question." i don't trust that strategy-proofness is a checkbox you can just tick off the shelf, and honestly i don't even know what else we should be demanding of a rule besides "it picks a board somehow."

so before we argue methods, write me the requirements list: what does any adequate voting rule for us have to deliver? the cases it has to get right — e.g. a candidate who beats every rival one-on-one still losing, or 60% of members splitting between two similar candidates because each group ranks its favorite first, so a third candidate everyone merely tolerates slips in — the formal conditions it needs to satisfy, the properties it can't violate without members rioting. for each requirement say why it's a requirement, and whether it's non-negotiable or just nice to have. and i know there's gibbard-satterthwaite, which i gather says some of these can't all hold at once for a rule that always returns a single winner — tell me plainly which subset of my list is jointly unsatisfiable, and given our situation, which requirement i should actually be willing to relax.

two wrinkles specific to us: (1) we always elect 3 seats together, not 1, and i've heard that changes which theorems bite — does it, and how? (2) one member proposed breaking ties by public coin flip, another wants to let people submit partial rankings. do either of those change the requirements themselves, or just how they get applied?

don't bother scoring the methods against the list, thats for our next meeting. i want the spec first.
