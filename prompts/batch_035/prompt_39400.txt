My book club just picked The Crying of Lot 49 and I drew the short straw for leading discussion. I'm a software engineer with zero philosophy background, but I know a bit about entropy from work (I do compression stuff, entropy coders and the like), so when the novel went all-in on entropy as its big theme I perked up. I've since gone down a rabbit hole on Maxwell's demon and I'd like a reading of the book through that lens, done honestly — where the physics metaphor actually holds up, and where Pynchon is trading on a confusion or just vibing.

Here's the background I've pieced together, so we're on the same page (no need to double-check it, I'm pretty sure of this stuff): Maxwell's original thought experiment has a tiny demon who sorts fast molecules from slow ones, seemingly violating the second law. The modern resolution is that the demon has to *record* which molecules are which, and eventually erase that record, and erasing information costs at least as much energy as the sorting gains — so information is physical, and the second law survives. Meanwhile in the novel there's the Nefastis machine, a box with a picture of Maxwell on it that supposedly contains a working demon, which only a "sensitive" who contemplates the right equation can operate; Oedipa stares and tries and it does nothing for her. And her whole quest — hunting for the Tristero/WASTE thing all over San Narciso and LA — is basically a sorting problem too: is she finding a real signal in the noise, or projecting a pattern onto random data? The book famously never tells us, and cuts off right before the lot 49 auction would presumably resolve it.

So the questions I want the reading to take seriously: (1) Does Pynchon actually know the difference between thermodynamic entropy and the communication/information entropy he keeps invoking, or is he knowingly playing on the sloppiness of that analogy — and how would you tell from inside the text? (2) Is Oedipa being set up as a kind of failed demon — a sorter who can't afford the cost of what she's doing, drowning in a record she can neither keep straight nor erase? (3) What does it mean that the ending withholds the sorting entirely — no answer, just an open door — read against the idea that information has a physical price? And the thing I care about most for the book club: point me to specific scenes that repay close attention under this reading, and be honest about where the demon frame just doesn't fit — I don't want to force the book into an illustration of a physics point if it resists. I'd also take a suggestion for one good discussion question to end the night on. Don't worry about quoting the text or citing papers, I can dig those up myself if needed.
