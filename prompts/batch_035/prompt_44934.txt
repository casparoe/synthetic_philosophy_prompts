Here is the angle I'm considering, and I'd like you to develop, correct, or push back on it. On a reductionist view, testimonial belief is justified only insofar as we possess positive, independent evidence that speakers tend to be right in the relevant circumstances; on an anti-reductionist view, testimony is a basic source of entitlement, so we are justified in accepting what we are told unless we have specific defeaters. The jurors never saw the killing; everything they know about the case is second-hand — the old man downstairs who says he heard the boy shout threats and saw him flee, the woman across the street who says she saw the stabbing through the windows of a passing elevated train, the claim in evidence that the murder weapon was one of a kind. So is Juror 8 a working reductionist, demanding positive support for every testimonial item before the jury may rely on it?
