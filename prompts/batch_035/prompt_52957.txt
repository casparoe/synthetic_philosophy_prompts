my roomate and i keep going back and forth on this and i want an actual essay to settle it, not just vibes. the question: are everyday conventions like tipping, queueing, and jaywalking genuine moral obligations, or just social facts that happen to carry social penalties? and do visitors have to follow the local ones?

here's the part i actually want the essay to dig into, because otherwise it just turns into "when in rome" hand-waving. the standard justifications people give for conventions as moral obligations seem to rely on things like: you benefit from the practice existing, you'd want everyone to comply, it's a scheme of cooperation among people who interact repeatedly. but a lot of those wash out for the one-shot visitor. picture a tourist who's in the US for exactly one week, will never return, and knows no one they'll ever interact with again. do they still have to tip? if yes, then whatever grounds the obligation has to be something other than reciprocal advantage or repeated play — so what is it? maybe fairness to the waiter who depends on the tipping system, maybe just generalizing over "what if everyone skipped out," but the essay should actually work this out rather than gesture at social contract vibes. and if the answer is no, the tourist is off the hook, doesn't that suggest the local's "obligation" is also mostly habit + fear of judgment rather than morality proper, which seems wrong?

also want it to distinguish between two kinds of convention, cause i suspect they come apart: ones where breaking them directly affects a specific person (tipping — the waiter loses income) vs ones where breaking them at the right moment harms no one (jaywalking across a totally empty street in a country where nobody jaywalks, vs crossing against the light in a city where everyone does it). does the second category even have a moral dimension or is it pure coordination that you'd be irrational to violate only because of traffic?

don't end with "it's complicated" or "depends on your framework" — pick an actual verdict and defend it, even if you have to bite a bullet. 800-1200ish words is fine.
