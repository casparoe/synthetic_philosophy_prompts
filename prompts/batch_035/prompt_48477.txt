my friend from my phil of mind class went full panpsychist this year and now he has an argument I can't beat but also can't accept. Background, as he gives it: consciousness is a fundamental feature of matter, so electrons and quarks have some tiny glimmer of experience (he says he got this from people like Philip Goff, I have no idea if that's a faithful version and honestly I don't think it matters for the argument). Then:

(1) If panpsychism is true, every micro-constituent of matter is a subject of experience.
(2) An experience can be negatively valenced — unpleasant, a tiny bit of suffering.
(3) Suffering counts morally no matter how small or simple the subject that has it.
(4) Whatever you do physically, you redistribute energy across an insane number of micro-subjects. Waving your hand, boiling a kettle, walking to the shop: each one jostles something like 10^24 molecules, and each jostle changes that subject's experience, mostly for the worse.
(5) Even if only a tiny fraction of those changes are bad ones, the numbers swamp everything at the human scale. The difference between donating to charity and committing a murder is a rounding error next to the micro-suffering in one kettle boil.
(6) If consequences are what matter morally, then all ordinary human actions are morally indistinguishable, since the differences between them are rounding errors.
(7) But that's obviously false — comforting a child isn't morally on a par with assault, and it wouldn't become so if panpsychism turned out to be true.

So either panpsychism is false or morality as we know it is gutted. My friend is weirdly ok biting that bullet — he says we should prefer actions that disturb fewer particles, which for him means sitting very still, which is suspiciously convenient for someone who hates going out. He also floated an amendment at some point that maybe only irreversible processes count, since those are the ones that "leave a mark," but that seemed to raise as many problems as it solved.

For my part I really don't want to give up (3), it feels like that's where speciesism sneaks back in the door. Something between (4) and (6) smells off but I can't put my finger on exactly where it slips. Which step is the bad one, and what do I pay for rejecting it?
