I'm in an intro philosophy course, and we just finished the section of the Philosophical Investigations containing the private language material -- the diary with the symbol for a recurring sensation, the beetle in the box, the remarks about pain. We have an exam in a week and my study group has been pooling summaries of everything. One member of the group, who is by far the most confident of us, produced this write-up of the argument, and I want to trust it but something about it nags me. Here is what he wrote, as he wrote it:

"Wittgenstein's private language argument shows that a language only one person could in principle understand is impossible. The reason is essentially that language is a social institution: meanings are fixed by the conventions and agreements of a community of speakers, so any genuine language must be shareable, and a language for describing one's own private sensations fails this test. The diary thought experiment makes the point concrete: if you try to record a recurring private sensation by writing a sign in a diary each time it occurs, you can never check later that you used the sign correctly, since only you can access the sensation, and a criterion only you can apply is no criterion at all. The beetle in the box generalizes this to all sensation words: since the contents of each person's box are hidden and could differ or even be empty, sensation words cannot mean the private inner items, so their meaning must instead be constituted by public criteria for behavior. Wittgenstein's ultimate conclusion is therefore that sensations are not really private after all -- pain just is pain-behavior plus its circumstances -- and that saying 'I am in pain' is really a report about my own behavior, which is what makes it publicly checkable."

Parts of this sound like what our professor said, and parts sound suspiciously like the behaviorism we were warned about. I'm not sure where the line falls. Could you go through the summary piece by piece and tell me which claims a careful Wittgensteinian would accept, which are oversimplifications, and which are actual misrepresentations? A few things I'd especially like sorted out: whether the argument is really about languages that happen to have only one speaker, or about something else (does Wittgenstein's "private" mean inaccessible-to-others in the ordinary sense, or something stronger?), whether "meaning is fixed by conventions and agreements" is a fair statement of the role the community plays in the argument, and whether the final two sentences follow from anything Wittgenstein actually argued or are a slide into a view he explicitly resisted. If the concluding part is wrong, please also tell me what the private language argument actually shows about pain if not that -- since that's the part most likely to show up on the exam.
