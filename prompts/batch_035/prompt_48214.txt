I'm taking a second course in metaphysics, and I'm preparing to write a term paper on material constitution — the statue and the clay — but before defending any particular line, I want to be clear about what would count as success. Please write out the desiderata: the requirements any adequate account of the statue/clay case must meet, with the consideration behind each. At minimum the list should cover the following.

First, a count: is the account committed to one object on the pedestal or two, and what settles the count? I keep running into pressure from the problem-of-the-many direction — that there seems to be no non-arbitrary fact about which lump counts as the statue — and I want that addressed rather than waved away.

Second, the modal gap: the piece of clay plausibly survives being squashed into a ball while the statue does not. But I'd like you to mark whether this is a fixed constraint that every theory must respect, or rather an datum a theory is permitted to explain away, since the temporal-parts response seems to dissolve the disagreement.

Third, the grounding demand: where could any difference between statue and clay come from if they share all purely microphysical properties? Here I want you to distinguish the modest demand for some ground or other from the stronger demand for a reductive ground, and say which you are imposing.

Fourth, generalization: must the account extend to persons and their bodies, or does that question bring in enough independent baggage (ethical and psychological, not just metaphysical) that it should be listed as optional?

Fifth, a non-relabeling condition: some criterion for telling when a proposed solution merely restates the problem in new vocabulary. This worries me most about views that take constitution to be a primitive relation, so please state under what conditions, if any, primitivism is acceptable.

After the list, please flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, identify which single requirement does the most to eliminate the standard responses — coincidence, elimination, relativized identity, and four-dimensionalism — and close with a brief note on which response comes out best against the full list. Please do not include citations or a bibliography; naming the positions is sufficient, and I will add references myself.
