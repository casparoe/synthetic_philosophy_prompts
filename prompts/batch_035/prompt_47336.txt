For about fifteen years I've read the rationalist blogs and forums, and the local metaphysics has always run roughly as follows: causation is in the map rather than the territory, the universe is just a gigantic arrangement of events spread across space and time, and a scientific theory is at bottom a lossless compression of data. Lately I've watched that last slogan harden into an actual argument, and I want to know whether the argument works. It goes like this. The history of physics is the history of shorter and shorter programs that reproduce the same observations; the best theory just is the shortest one; but a compression is a description, not a cause — nothing can be governed by its own summary. So when people say the laws of nature constrain or generate or necessitate what happens, they are taking our best compression and reifying it into something with power over events, which is a straightforward mind projection fallacy. Humeanism comes out true, and the anti-Humean picture — whether Armstrong's universals related by nomic necessitation, or anything else that treats laws as doing real governing work — comes out confused.

I half believe this, but I've started to suspect the compression analogy is carrying more weight than the argument, and that the argument would look different if the analogy were stated carefully. So here is what I want: an audit.

First, the correspondence table. What plays the role of the data to be compressed — the Humean mosaic itself, or our observations of it, and does the ambiguity matter? What plays the role of the compression algorithm, the description language, the compressor? Where in the picture does the Best System Analysis's competition among virtues — simplicity, strength, balance — actually sit?

Second, the disanalogies, sorted by whether they matter for the conclusion the analogy was supposed to support. I'll prime you with three worries I've accumulated, though you should add your own. (1) Kolmogorov complexity is defined only up to a constant, relative to a choice of universal machine; Lewis's best system is supposed to be an objective feature of the whole mosaic. Is compression a fair precisification of the Best System's virtues, or does the analogy quietly import an observer-relativity that Humeanism was never supposed to have? Which side of this the answer comes down on may depend on how large the gap between machine choices is at physics scale — say something about that. (2) Ordinary compression is of observed data, but the Humean's systematization ranges over everything, everywhere, everywhen, including the future. The summary is therefore of a whole that already contains everything it supposedly predicts, and I can't tell whether that is a deep feature of the view or a bug that relocates the mystery rather than solving it. Relatedly: can the compression picture say anything about counterfactuals — what would have happened to the uranium sample had it been larger — or does it inherit the problem that the actual mosaic contains no data about non-actual happenings? I understand Lewis had a distinctive move here involving small deviations from the actual laws; explain what it amounts to, and whether it is more or less plausible when redescribed in compression terms. (3) The anti-Humean's strongest card has always been explanation: an event seems to be explained by the law, and explanation has an arrow, from law to event, that a summary run in either direction lacks. Can the Humean recover the arrow from compression alone, or is there an assumption buried in the analogy — that compression is for prediction while explanation demands something more — that begs the question?

Third, the verdict. Once the disanalogies that matter are on the table, does the conclusion — Humeanism about laws — survive on its own, or was the compression analogy carrying it the whole time? Distinguish carefully here between two failures: the analogy failing while Humeanism stands on independent grounds, versus the analogy exposing that Humeanism itself only ever felt compelling in compression terms. And if the compression picture does fail as an argument, tell me whether there is a better analogy available for the Humean position — I have heard the mosaic likened to a finished novel and the laws to its plot summary, which at least makes the systematicity feel less like an achievement of the summarizer — or whether the honest conclusion is that this whole framing by analogy was a mistake and the debate between governing laws and best systems has to be joined directly.

Background assumptions about me, so you can pitch the level: I know what the Best System Analysis says, what Armstrongian nomic necessitation is supposed to be, and what a Kolmogorov complexity and a Solomonoff prior are; I have read the rationalist canon on reductionism and the mind projection fallacy; I have not read the journal literature on Humean supervenience and its discontents, so name-drop sparingly and only when the point genuinely needs it. I care less about who said what than about whether the argument works.
