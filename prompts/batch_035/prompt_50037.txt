I'm writing a paper on the separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism, and I need a thought experiment that does a very specific job — help me build it.

First, my thesis, so you know what the case is actually for: I want to argue that the objection's intuitive pull doesn't come from the fact that persons are numerically distinct. It comes from whether the costs and benefits can be united under a single deliberative standpoint — roughly, whether someone can be asked to endorse the tradeoff as *theirs*. Standard interpersonal cases fail that test, standard intrapersonal ones pass it, and everyone mislabels the variable.

So what I need is really three linked scenarios, same setting, one paragraph each:

1. A control case where a single person faces a tradeoff across time (moderate cost now, larger benefit later for the same person) that any reasonable theory says is fine to make.
2. The parallel case where the costs fall on distinct people instead — but built so that common-sense morality STILL clearly says to aggregate, i.e., a case where the separateness objection gives the wrong answer by ordinary lights. Something like an office rotation, a clinic scheduling thing, a shared household, whatever — no trolleys, and definately no organ surgeons, my reviewers will stop reading.
3. The money case: a mixed one where some of the cost-bearers and some of the beneficiaries are the same individuals at different times, and others are distinct people, with the utility profile held fixed across all three scenarios. If my thesis is right, intuitions in scenario 3 should track the endorsement structure, not the headcount.

Constraints: no sci-fi (no mind-melding, no memory transfer), everyone's a normal adult with ordinary self-interest, and you can't just stipulate that the tradeoff is endorsed — that's cheating. The endorsement, or lack of it, has to fall out of the setup itself.

After the scenarios: walk through what a utilitarian, a Scanlon-style contractualist, and a maximin/Rawlsian type each say about each of the three cases, and tell me honestly which feature of the design is doing the work — I suspect it's the deliberative-standpoint thing, but I want to know if I'm smuggling something else in. Then tell me how the sharpest separateness defender would attack the case, and whether they could tweak scenario 3 to flip it on me.

Start your answer with a one-sentence verdict on whether my spec is even jointly satisfiable as stated, then the scenarios, then the walkthrough. No preamble and no restating what I asked for.
