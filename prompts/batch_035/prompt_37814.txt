If utility is nothing more than a representation of choices, as revealed preference theory maintains, then whenever a consumer buys the dearer brand-name aspirin rather than the chemically identical generic, we are obliged to say she derived more utility from the brand, and the proposition that people maximise utility becomes true by stipulation rather than by anything testable. Since an unfalsifiable definition can support no predictions, how do economists justify using the theory to forecast that a fuel duty will reduce driving? A colleague tells me the answer turns on consistency conditions imposed across different budget sets, but if those conditions are imposed by fiat rather than discovered, I don't see how that rescues the theory from vacuity rather than merely narrowing it by decree. And if consistency really is all there is to rationality here, why do economists keep talking as though utility were a magnitude agents try to obtain more of?
