I'm putting together a philosophical profile for a graduate seminar on ethical theory — we've been asked to write down a set of commitments in normative ethics so the group can audit each other's sets before term papers are due. Before I circulate mine, I want to know whether what I've written actually hangs together. Here is the set. (1) I am a holist about reasons in roughly the sense the moral particularists defend: the contribution a consideration makes to the rightness or wrongness of an act depends on the total context, so the fact that an action will cause pain can count against it in one case, in favor of it in another, and make no difference in a third; and I take this to imply that no finite set of principles captures what makes acts right. (2) About promising specifically, I hold that the bare fact that I have promised always counts in favor of keeping it — never against, never falling to silence — and that this is a deep fact about the practice, not a heuristic I would abandon under pressure. (3) A case verdict I am confident about: I promised to meet a friend for coffee, and on the way I can rescue a drowning child only by breaking the promise; there I judge that the promise does not merely lose to the stronger reason but stops counting altogether, and I would judge the same in any structurally similar case. (4) On moral argument, I believe a moral argument is valid only if one of its premises is a general moral claim; moving from one case to another without such a premise may persuade, but it is not really inference. (5) I am a moral realist who thinks moral facts supervene on natural facts, and I hold that the wrongness of gratuitous cruelty is universal — there is no possible circumstance in which it is anything other than wrong.

What I want is an audit rather than a lecture. Go through the five commitments, tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like they are, and rank the tensions by severity rather than listing every possible clash as equally bad. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair would cost me; for the merely apparent ones, show me where I am equivocating between two things that only sound like the same commitment. I should say that I have no idea where the real pressure is — when I reread the list, different pairs strike me as the problem on different days, which is exactly why I am asking. I would rather hear that some of my views are simply incompatible, with a specific verdict about which one to sacrifice, than receive a taxonomy of everything anyone has ever said about the generalist side of the ledger.
