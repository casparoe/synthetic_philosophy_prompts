I've basically committed to a view that's part stuff I read and part something I made up myself, and I want to know what would count against it.

The view: whenever some things are arranged a certain way, they compose a further object exactly when together they can do something that none of them could do singly or as just a loose group — AND that new capacity persists continuously, it's not a one-off fluke. So a car is one thing because its parts continuously have the new ability (driving), a pile of sand is not one thing because "being a pile" isn't really an ability beyond what each grain already does plus where it is. I know van Inwagen goes further and says only living things compose, but my rule isn't that. The case that bugs me: some strangers stuck in an elevator all lean against the door at once and it opens — did they, at that moment, compose one object with a new ability (door-opening), and did it pop out of existence the second the door opened? I genuinely don't know what my view says and that scares me a little.

And please give me your actual verdict on the elevator case, even if it's embarrassing for my view.
