Draft the joint statement they'd actually produce on the conceivability argument. I want the claims both can sign (that the thought experiment is coherent as a piece of imaginative practice, that it's at least prima facie conceivable, that the dispute is not about whether consciousness exists); the residual disagreements stated exactly, each phrased so the side holding it would accept the wording — they've already rejected like four drafts of each other's phrasing, mostly over whether the argument begs the question against physicalism or merely takes the gap seriously; the cruxes, and I suspect they're these: whether ideal positive conceivability is genuinely achievable here or is just whatever the anti-physicalist happens to feel after reflection, whether phenomenal concepts are a real explanation of why the gap seems to appear or just a label stuck on it, and whether the water/H2O style a posteriori necessity is a good analogy for the physicalist or a bad one because that identity has an explanation in a way the psychophysical one allegedly lacks; and for each crux, the argument or development both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about it written down in advance. If part of their fight turns out to be verbal — e.g. the two of them meaning different things by conceivable — say which part and why. Format: numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table. Hard limit of 900 words; they want something a dean will actually read, not a monograph.
