so heres a thing ive been chewing on for a couple years and i want your help figuring out what actually matters in it. quick background on me: no philosophy training, i work in hvac, but i read a lot of internet arguments about ethics and i give about 5% of my income to charity, currently split between a farmed animal welfare org and a global health one (malaria nets type stuff).

my view, as best i can put it: i've basically convinced myself that suffering and happiness arent on the same scale. a really bad headache just isnt the kind of thing any amount of mild fun can make up for, the way $5 and $500 at least feel like the same kind of thing. but im not a full negative utilitarian either — the kind where you'd press a button that painlessly ends the world if it reduced net suffering — that seems insane to me. so where ive landed is: below some threshold of badness, pleasure and pain trade off against each other more or less normally, but above the threshold pain stops trading. it cant be compensated, it just has to be prevented. no amount of added happiness anywhere in the world cancels it out.

ive been collecting problems with this and i want to know which ones actually matter, since i only have so much brain time and i need to decide (a) whether i keep believing something like this and (b) whether it should change where my donations go. here they are, roughly:

1. where does the threshold sit and who says? is a bad migraine above it? childbirth? a factory farmed chicken's whole life? this feels like that heap-of-sand thing where you remove one grain at a time and never know when it stopped being a heap — but does that actually break the view, or just make it messy?

2. the threshold seems to depend on how you slice pain up. is a week of 6/10 pain one thing above the threshhold, or seven days of stuff below it? a friend of mine says duration has to matter but im not sure the threshold idea survives that.

3. small chances. if a donation has a 0.1% chance of preventing above-threshold agony for one person, versus a 100% chance of preventing lots of below-threshold misery for many people, what does my view even tell me to do? i gather the philosophy world has a name for problems like this, fanaticism or something.

4. if above-threshold pain cant be compensated, doesnt one person's torture outweigh any number of people's decent lives? that conclusion smells wrong to me but i cant see where i'd block it without giving up the core idea.

5. maybe happiness has a threshold too? the symmetric version? no idea if that fixes anything or just doubles the problems.

so rank these for me. which is load-bearing for whether the view survives, which is just messiness i can live with, and is there a problem im not even seeing thats worse than all five? and for each one, tie it back to the donation question, because at the end of the day thats what this is for. i dont want a reading list or names of papers, i want your actual reasoning about which of these i should lose sleep over.
