I help run a rationality meetup -- about 14 regulars, mostly people who've been around LessWrong long enough that they'd all nod along if you said "asymmetric information" or "Bayesian updating." Paradoxically that makes them harder to teach, because they've read about information cascades so they think they're immune to them. I want an in-class game that proves them wrong. Design something ~45 minutes where people draw private signals about which of two states the world is in (I'm thinking marbles from an urn, two urns, one mostly white balls one mostly black, each person draws one ball looks at it privately then publicly announces a guess), with some scoring so the correct-guessers get points. Then a twist I want your help building in: after the first cascade forms (which it will, person 3 or 4 just copies their predecessor), run a second round where midway through the sequence the urns are secretly switched, and see how long the cascade of copy-the-last-person persists against a wall of stale public guesses. Also worth adding: give a couple of players a small private bonus for being contrarian AND right, so we can compare their behavior with everyone else's. Finish with a debrief that walks through at what point copying the predecessor was actually the rational Bayesian move vs pure herding, and honestly note which part of the real-world phenomenon (crypto panic, restaurant choice, academic fads) the urn setup can't capture. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time -- my guess is someone will spill the beans about the urn switch to their neighbor, lol.
