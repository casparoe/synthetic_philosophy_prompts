I'm a charge nurse at a smallish hospital and we're in a genuine shortage of a certain antiviral (weird supply chain thing, doesn't matter which drug). One patient, call her Ms. A, has already been prescribed the only full course we have left, and without it she'll almost certainly die. Three other patients each need about a third of a course, and with that dose each would very likely pull through. A resident suggested we quietly split Ms. A's course among the three — basically take it from her. My gut says absolutely not, that's a line you can't cross, and most of the staff agree with me. But the resident keeps pressing: the whole reason we have rules about not taking a patient's meds is to protect patients, and here the rule protects one patient by letting three die. If protecting patients is the point, how is the rule doing its job? And he asks whether there's really any difference between "taking" her meds and just declining to treat the three, since either way I know exactly what's going to happen. I feel like there must be an answer but I can't articulate it and it's eating at me. Is my gut coherent here, or am I just being squeamish while people die?
