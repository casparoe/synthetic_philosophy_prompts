At my weekly poker night two of the regulars got into it over a side bet the host likes to offer. He has a bag with 90 chips: 30 are red, and the other 60 are some mix of black and yellow, but nobody but him knows the mix. He offers even odds on whatever color you name. Dave said he'd take red every time, never black, even though black could be up to 60 chips — his reasoning was that with red he knows exactly what he's getting, and he hates betting when the other guy knows something he doesn't. Marco said that's just dumb: whatever the mix is, black has to average out to the same 30 as red, so refusing black at the same odds is throwing money away. Dave said no — the host filled the bag himself, so for all he knows there are zero black chips in there, and a bet where the guy on the other side picked the composition isn't like betting on a shuffled deck. Marco said that's paranoia, and anyway you name your color before anything is drawn, so there's nothing for the host to rig. Then the host offered a combo version of the bet: pick black-or-yellow or red-or-yellow, and you win if a chip of either of your two colors comes out. Dave took black-or-yellow without hesitating, since that's a guaranteed 60 chips. Marco pounced on that: Dave had just said red beats black, but if you add the exact same yellow chips to both sides of that comparison you get red-or-yellow over black-or-yellow — so Dave flipped the moment yellows got attached, and his two answers can't come from any single consistent set of odds on the bag. Dave shrugged and said he's allowed to have prefernces, they don't need to be consistent, it's a game night not a math exam. They both turned to me for a ruling and I had nothing. Can you go through their whole back-and-forth move by move and say who's right at each point, and especially whether Marco's consistency point actually lands or whether Dave has some legitimate way out? Under 500 words please.
