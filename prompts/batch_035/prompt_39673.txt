A bit of background: I'm a novelist, and I've spent the last two years on a book where one of the narrators is an ant colony. Not a queen speaking for her subjects, not some Disney-ish bug with a face — the colony itself, as a character, thinking thoughts no single ant could think. Basically my rewrite of Hofstadter's Aunt Hillary, the intelligent ant colony that appears in the Ant Fugue dialogues in Gödel, Escher, Bach. So I've fallen head first into this old fight about whether a colony can be a mind, and I need help diagnosing why it never goes anywhere.

Here's the situation. My editor adores the narration; my writing group is split in half and after a year of arguing about it in bars, nobody has moved an inch, and I've started to wonder if we're even disagreeing about anything.

The two camps, roughly: the "it's a cheat" people say a mind needs a subject — there has to be something it's like to be the colony from the inside, and there isn't. The ants are tiny robots following pheromone gradients, and the intelligence I'm writing is in my reader's eyes, the way a novel has a tone even though no individual sentence does. If I narrate the colony's inner life I'm just lending it mine. The other camp says wait a minute — that's exactly what neurons are too. Dumb cells, chemical signals, no one of them knows anything, and yet here we all are arguing in bars. The mind is the pattern at the level of the whole; either the colony counts or my own brain doesn't, and I can't have it both ways. A termite mound cools itself like a lung breathes, an ant colony solves routing problems no ant can spell — so at which point does "just a bunch of bugs" become a prejudice about which aggregates we're allowed to call a "who"?

I don't want you to referee the bar fight. What I want is a diagnosis of the disagreement itself. Is it verbal — the two camps meaning different things by "mind" and never noticing? Is it a hidden empirical question (something about integration, or unified perspective) that we just can't get at from the outside? Is it bedrock intuitions about where the level of description is at which a mind lives — one side treating the colony level as a mere metaphor and the other treating my level, the single organism, as the same kind of metaphor? For what it's worth, that last one is my own working theory, but offer it only as a suspicion — please test it against what each side is actually saying, and tell me if it doesn't survive. And then: given your diagnosis, is there anything that would count as progress here — a distinction, a thought experiment, some criterion short of the impossible experiment? Or should I accept that the argument is a permanent fixture, finish the book, and let my readers have the bar fight instead?
