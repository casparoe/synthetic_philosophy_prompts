A friend of mine just finished a pop-philosophy book about falsificationism, and now he's insufferable about it. He gave me this rundown of Popper and Darwin, roughly in his own words: Popper showed that Darwinism flunks his own falsifiability test — natural selection is so accommodating it can explain whatever you happen to find in nature, which makes it closer to a tautology than a theory, since whatever survives gets counted as fittest and the fittest are by definition whatever survived. Biologists came down on him hard for this, and by the late seventies he'd publicly eaten his words and reclassified evolution as a proper science. So the punchline, my friend says, is that evolution sits in exactly the same epistemic boat as creationism — both can absorb any observation you throw at them — and that in the end Popper himself admitted falsifiability can't actually draw the line between science and non-science after all.

Some of this rings true. I know Popper at one point said natural selection wasn't a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme, and I know he later took that back, at least partly. But the last two moves feel off to me. Putting evolution and creationism in the same boat sounds like exactly the kind of thing Popper spent his career arguing against, and I don't think he ever gave up on demarcation. Still, I haven't read the actual texts, so don't just take my side of it.

Go through his account claim by claim and give each a verdict — fair, oversimplified, or flat wrong — with your reasons, and tell me specifically whether the tautology bit runs together two different worries Popper had (about the survival-of-the-fittest formulation versus about the theory's explanatory reach), because that's the part he was most confident about. And I want a committed answer, not a menu of possible readings: where his punchline half-follows from what's true in the summary, say how much of it survives, and take a definite position on what Popper actually ended up thinking about both Darwinism and his own criterion. If the conclusion only works by quietly redefining what Popper meant by metaphysical research programme, call that out.
