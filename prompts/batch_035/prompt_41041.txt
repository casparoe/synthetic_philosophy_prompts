I help run a reading group on phil of math for grad students in the math department -- math-strong, philosophy-naive. I want a decision tree that starts from something everyone in the room already accepts, that "2+2=4" is true in a way that doesn't depend on us or our conventions, and walks them to a named position on mathematical objects. The nodes have to be questions they can answer without knowing any of the labels: e.g. does accepting a theorem with quantifiers in it commit you to the existence of whatever it quantifies over; if numbers are abstract and causally inert, is there any story at all for how we could know about them (put the Benacerraf-style access worry in plain terms); suppose tomorrow's best physics somehow stopped quantifying over numbers entirely -- does that change anything about whether 2+2=4; two set-theoretic reductions of the number 3 both work and disagree about which set 3 is, is that a real problem or a confusion. The leaves must include plain platonism, full-blooded platonism where consistency alone gets you existence, structuralism, if-thenism, Field-style fictionalism where math is a useful fiction we should ideally do science without, paraphrase nominalism, and a deflationary "numbers exist but its a trivial fact" leaf. Also mark which answer combinations nobody actually defends (and say why -- incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored), and flag any two leaves the tree cant genuinely separate. Keep it to six nodes and eight leaves max so it fits on one whiteboard.
