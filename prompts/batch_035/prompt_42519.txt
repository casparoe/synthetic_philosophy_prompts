So I have zero academic background -- no philosophy degree, never even took a class -- but I've been chewing on this for a couple years on my own and I've pretty much talked myself into a position. Now I want somebody to try to break it.

Here's my view: the way a piece of tech is built decides most of what it does to society, and the "choices" the people running the companies make are mostly just the tech playing itself out. Like, once you build a feed that ranks posts by whatever keeps people scrolling, it stops mattering whether the CEO is a decent person or a jerk -- the outrage bait is baked in, and anyone who tried to run it a different way would just get outcompeted by whoever runs it the way it wants to be run. It's not that somebody sat down and decided to make the internet nasty, it's that the design quietly decided for everybody. Same with self-checkout at my grocery store: no boss had a meeting about cutting cashier jobs, a machine that does the work for free just exists now, so the decision kind of makes itself. What I guess I'm saying is that I don't think artifacts just sit there neutral until humans use them -- they come with a default politics already loaded, and the humans are downstream of it.

What would count against this? And I mean really count against it, not just "well humans still matter a little." I want to know what observations would have to be true for me to be wrong -- the same tech getting built two genuinely different ways in two different places and staying different for a long time, a company that actually chose the less profitable design and survived anyway, whatever. Rank the stuff by how decisive it would be, from "okay, I'd have to give this up" all the way down to "mildly anoying for my view but whatever." Also, be straight with me: is my version of this so bendy that nothing could ever count against it? Like, if a tech turns out bad I say the tech did it, and if a company fixes it I say they were forced to -- have I rigged the game so I can't lose? And if so, is that a fatal flaw, or is some slack like that actually fine for a big picture view of this kind? I genuinely don't know and I'd rather hear the ugly answer.

For what its worth, the thing that would probably move me most is real cases of the same invention going different directions in different hands. If stuff like that has happened I want to hear about it, even if it hurts me.
