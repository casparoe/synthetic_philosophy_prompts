I build predictive models for a living, and there's a distinction I keep needing in my work that I can't find a settled name or treatment of, if one exists.

Some regularities seem to be worth their keep no matter how powerful the predictor is. If you want to know the force on a piston over the next hour, tracking pressure and temperature is not merely cheaper than tracking every molecule; even an imagined observer who could track every molecule would find that the microstates fall into equivalence classes that lead to the same future macrostates, so the grouping reflects something about the system rather than about the observer's limitations.

Other regularities look different: they seem to exist only for predictors on a budget. In our customer data, a cheap model finds two well-separated clusters and predicts behavior off them; a larger model with more features dissolves the clusters and predicts the same behavior without ever sorting anyone into them. My spam filter attributes being-a-spammer to users and thereby predicts a lot, cheaply; an observer with the raw behavior and enough compute could predict the same messages without ever grouping people that way. My teammate says our chess engine wants to trap the rook, and that predicts the next move better than anything we can afford to compute from its source code -- but an observer with the source and unbounded resources could match the prediction without ever attributing wanting.

So what I'm after is this. Is there an established concept, or literature, for the distinction between regularities that a budget-unconstrained predictor would still do best to exploit, and regularities that exist only relative to predictors with limited time, data, or memory? And tied to it: is the second category a way of being real, or just a description of our ignorance -- though it doesn't feel like mere ignorance, since two independently built budget-limited predictors tend to land on the same groupings, and their predictions are of real events.

I won't be satisfied with a single term. I'd want the carving: the nearest named concepts and how each differs from what I'm describing; whether everything falls into the second category once you push hard enough, or whether there's a principled way to argue the piston case is different from the cluster case; where ordinary belief-and-desire prediction of people belongs, and how we could even begin to tell; and, if no existing concept fits, a proposed definition with the hard boundary cases -- a flock heading south, a face in the clouds versus a face in a crowd, water coming to a boil.
