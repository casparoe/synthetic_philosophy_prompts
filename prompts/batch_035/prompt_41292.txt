I'm on a volunteer committee for my kids' school district (about 12 of us, parents plus a couple retired teachers) and we have to recommend whether to replace our aging diesel bus fleet with electric buses over the next five years. The current buses are 15+ years old, the mechanics say they're getting expensive to keep on the road, and there's a state grant covering a big chunk of the switch but only if we committ soon. Pretty normal municipal stuff, except the meetings have turned into a philosophical standoff and I can't tell who's actually right.

One parent, let's call her Dana, has staked out this position: we don't fully understand the long-term behavior of big lithium battery packs on buses (fire risk, crash behavior, what happens to the packs at end of life), and she says "when the potential harm is serious and the science isn't settled, the burden of proof is on whoever wants to make the change." It sounds reasonable, and honestly the pro-electric people on the committee can't really answer her — they just keep repeating that the diesel exhaust is bad too, which doesn't seem to land. Dana also points out that once we go electric we're locked in for a decade, so any mistake is hard to undo.

Here's the thing bugging me though. Last year Dana used basically the same logic in the opposite direction — we were debating whether to keep treating the athletic fields with a conventional pesticide, and she wanted us to switch to the organic alternative immediately, saying the known risks were bad enough that we couldn't sit around waiting for perfect certainty. Nobody called this out at the time, me included. So my questions: is she being flatly inconsistent, or is there a version of "better safe than sorry" that's legitimate and a version that's broken, and what exactly separates them? And a smaller thing — does it matter that the diesel risks are old, well-mapped, and boring to talk about, while the battery risks are newer and fuzzier? Dana acts like fuzzier automatically means scarier, and I genuinely can't tell whether that's a real point or just a gut feeling dressed up as one. We meet again in two weeks and I'd like to walk in with something better than vibes.
