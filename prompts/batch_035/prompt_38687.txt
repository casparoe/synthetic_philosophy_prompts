My roommate and I got into it pretty hard last night about Humeanism regarding the laws of nature (we'd both been to a talk on it earlier this week, which is probably what started it). The upshot was that neither of us could get the other to budge: I'm at maybe 0.3 that Humeanism is basically right — no necessary connections between distinct existences, laws just being whatever the best systematization of the total mosaic of particular facts looks like — while he's up around 0.75, and it's frustrating because I genuinely can't tell whether our disagreement is about the actual metaphysics or about something more subtle that we're both just papering over.

Here's roughly where we ended. I pushed the explanatory-direction worry: on the best-systems view it seems like the laws can't really explain the regularities, since they're just a compressed summary of them, and physicists sure seem to treat laws as explanatorily prior. He had two replies. First, that the summary itself has explanatory value even if it's grounded in what it summarizes (he gestured at the way a good statistical mechanic can explain a gas's behavior by describing it as typical given the macrostate, without needing anything modally loaded). Second — and this is the one I keep chewing on — he accused me of smuggling nomic content in through the back door of the best-systems criteria themselves: "simplicity" and "strength" only do the work they're supposed to do if you've already picked a vocabulary in which the true theory comes out simple, and that choice looks suspiciously like it presupposes laws rather than deriving them. I couldn't give him a clean answer to that, and I suspect it's doing a lot of work for both of us.
