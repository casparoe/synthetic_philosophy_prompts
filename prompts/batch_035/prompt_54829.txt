I'm on the rules disputes panel of our university games society, and this year we ran a semester-long game of Nomic as an official society event (Peter Suber's game where changing the rules *is* the gameplay -- if you don't know it, the short version is players propose amendments to the rulebook every turn, vote on them, and even the rules about how rules change are themselves fair game). It's gone properly weird and I have to write something up before Saturday, so I need your help drafting it.

The mess: around week six a player got an amendment through that added a clause to one of the point-scoring rules saying it "may not be amended via the ordinary amendment procedure" -- a self-entrenchment clause. (Yes, I know Suber's original ruleset has immutable rules that need transmuting before you can touch them, but we'd long since replaced that machinery with something simpler, so this clause was doing its own work.) Two weeks later, a different player used a separate emergency-measure procedure that somebody had added earlier -- meant for fixing typos and contradictions -- to amend the entrenched rule anyway, AND made the change retroactive, reapportioning everyone's points back to the start of the term. The retroactive reapportionment changes who won a round that was already declared finished and prize-awarded, if that matters.

Our panel is split. Half think the entrenchment clause was binding, so the emergency amendment was void, and the old result stands. The other half think substantive entrenchment is incoherent in a game like this: the emergency procedure was itself validly enacted, and if the amendment power flows from the rules, then a rule made *by* that power can't bind a later exercise of it -- otherwise you've got an omnipotence-paradox situation with scorekeeping. There's also a third option floating around: declare the whole thing from week six onward an uninterpretable mess and restart that phase under cleaned-up rules. The society constitution says our panel's written decision is final for this game, so no dodging.

Please draft the decision memo I'll circulate to the panel. It should recommend one of the three options concretely, with reasoning the losing side could actually engage with rather than just "the panel rules thus". I'd like you to genuinely get into the self-reference stuff: whether entrenchment can bind at all when the amending power is self-derived, what the difference is (if any) between "the rule says you can't do this" and "you therefore can't", and whether the retroactivity question is seperate from the entrenchment question or tangled up with it -- I suspect it's actually two disputes wearing one trenchcoat, but I might be wrong. Feel free to lean on analogies to real legal puzzles (constitutions trying to entrench themselves, that sort of thing), just don't turn it into a jurisprudence lecture, nobody on the panel wants that.

Two more things. First, include a short section at the end where the memo itself says what would change its recommendation -- which argument from the losing side, if it actually worked, would flip the decision, and where the panel's reasoning is most likely to go wrong. We've got an ex-law student on the panel who will absolutely pounce on hand-waving, so be honest about the weak spots. Second, write it in British English throughout.

Keep it to maybe 600-800 words so the panel can read it over coffee -- this is a games society, not a court. Casual register is fine. (Apologies if I've mangled any Nomic terminology btw, Im not the one who's been winning.)
