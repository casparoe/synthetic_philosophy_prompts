I volunteer at a small natural history museum and have somehow ended up running the members' newsletter, and for the autumn issue I want to do one of those trashy magazine personality quizzes — "Which species concept are you, really?" The idea is that visitors (and our docents, who argue about this constantly over coffee) answer questions about concrete cases, and a scoring key at the back sorts them into an actual position on whether species are real and what holds one together — without any question using the technical names, so people can't just pick whichever label they like the sound of.

Can you write me 8–10 questions, each a short concrete scenario with two or three plain-language answers, plus a scoring key? These are the cases that have to show up, because they're what our docents actually fight about: the bdelloid rotifers, who apparently haven't had sex in something like 30 million years, so "interbreeding" can't be what keeps them one species; a ring species like the textbook version of the gull complex encircling the Arctic, where neighbouring populations interbreed but the two ends of the ring won't; two bird populations that interbreed freely wherever their ranges overlap, yet are so consistently different in plumage and genetics that every field guide gives them separate names; the fossil lineage puzzle — every generation of Homo interbreeds with the next, so where exactly does one species stop and another start; bacteria trading genes sideways with strangers instead of passing them to offspring; and at least one applied question about conservation law, say whether a wolf population that's been mixing with coyotes still counts as the endangered thing the paperwork is meant to protect.

For the scoring key, map answer patterns onto at minimum: the interbreeding-and-isolation view, the "if you can consistently tell them apart they're different species" view, the view of a species as one continuous lineage through time rather than a kind of thing (try to get that across without jargon), the ecological niche view, straight pluralism — different concepts for different jobs and that's fine — and an eliminativist leaf for readers who keep refusing to sort anything. If two of those positions genuinely can't be seperated by your questions, flag it rather than pretending the key does it, and if some answer combination doesn't correspond to any real position, say so instead of forcing it into a box. Audience is intelligent laypeople: no philosophy assumed, vague memories of school biology at best. And please begin directly with question 1 — no intro paragraph explaining what a species concept is, we're putting a little editor's note in front of the quiz anyway.
