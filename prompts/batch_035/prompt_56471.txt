I'm a retired physical chemist -- spectroscopy, mostly -- and I only got serious about philosophy after retiring, so bear with me if this is naive. The fight over whether LLMs understand language has been going in circles for years now: stochastic parrots on one side, and on the other people who say the models have world models or concepts or something of that kind, and nobody seems to move an inch. It reminds me of an argument we had forever in my own field about whether molecular orbitals are real things or just bookkeeping devices that happen to give the right numbers, and my working hypothesis is the LLM fight is like that -- mostly a disagreement about what we're allowed to mean by "understand" -- though I suspect there's also a buried empirical question about what's actually going on inside the network that nobody can currently get at. Diagnose the disagreement for me, using what each side actually claims: is it verbal, substantive, or several different questions (a cognitive-science question about the innards, a question about when we're warranted in saying a system understands, and maybe a bedrock intuition about embodiment and grounding) hiding under one word? And be honest about whether anything could actually end it -- interpretability results, behavioral tests, a sharper distinction -- or whether we just have to wait it out like the orbital people did.
