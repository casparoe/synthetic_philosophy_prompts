My opening move concerns the explanatory role of truth in evolutionary theory. When biologists explain why our perceptual and belief-forming systems are as they are, they say things like: creatures whose states accurately represent their environment outcompete creatures whose states misrepresent it. That appears to treat truth as a respect of comparison — a dimension along which belief-like states can be ranked, and along which selection discriminates. But on your view, an attribution of truth is just an abbreviation: apply the schema to each of an organism's beliefs, strip the predicate off, and what remains is an enormous list of sentences together with the world. So here is my challenge. Selection can only act on what there is: if truth is not a property over and above the schema, what exactly is the feature that fitness is tracking when accurate trackers win and misrepresenters lose? If you answer that the causal work is done entirely by the individual representational states and their contents, with the truth predicate serving merely as a device for generalizing over them, then I want to know what this device is generalizing over, and why the pattern it records lines up so neatly with survival and reproduction.
