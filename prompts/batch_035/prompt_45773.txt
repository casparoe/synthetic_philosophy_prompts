I've been reading LessWrong and the adjacent blogs since the early 2010s, and no debate there has stayed live longer than takeoff speeds: whether the path to transformative AI looks like a slow, continuous ramp — where AI systems that can meaningfully accelerate AI research precede anything decisive, and the world has years of warning — or a sharp discontinuity, where the first system capable of recursive self-improvement converts a modest lead into a decisive one in months or less. Two members of my local rationalist meetup have been arguing this for years, one on each side, and the argument has become almost entirely ritualized: one of them recites the returns-on-cognitive-investment argument and the other recites the bottlenecks objection, and neither ever concedes that the other's framing might partially dissolve the disagreement.

I want to commission the joint statement they should have produced from a good-faith adversarial collaboration. Please draft it with the following components:

1. An agreed-claims section, numbered, containing the substantive claims both could sign — about current ML trends, about what recursive self-improvement would require in principle, about the epistemic weight of the deep-learning era, and about what would count as transformative in the first place.

2. A disagreements table with exactly these columns:
   - id: a short identifier for the disagreement;
   - disagreement: the precise statement of where they differ, worded so that each side would accept the characterization of its own view (no strawmen, no hedging into vacuity);
   - crux: the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one side;
   - shared_evidence: a concrete case, historical episode, or thought experiment both agree bears on that crux;
   - continuous_prediction: what the slow-takeoff side predicts about that case;
   - discontinuous_prediction: what the fast-takeoff side predicts.

The cruxes you address should at least include: the shape of the returns curve on investment in intelligence (roughly log-linear versus convex); whether bottlenecks other than cognitive talent — compute, experimental data, physical lab work — throttle the feedback loop; whether many parallel copies of a human-level AI researcher can substitute for serial cognitive depth; and what the appropriate anchor is for how far human-level AI sits from AI-that-automates-AI-research.

3. A short section on which parts of their dispute are verbal rather than substantive — for instance, whether any of the disagreement survives only because they mean different things by 'takeoff' or by 'continuous' — with your reasoning stated.

One wrinkle I want handled explicitly rather than buried: they keep circling whether the 2012–2022 deep-learning period, in which capability gains tracked compute and scale with no AI-driven research loop at all, counts as evidence about the shape of the curve, or is uninformative because it samples a regime with no feedback loop in it. The statement should record, as an agreed claim or an explicit disagreement, which historical episodes are probative and why.

After the document, please append a brief structured summary as a single JSON object with this schema: fields "agreed_claims" (array of strings), "disagreements" (array of objects, each with "id", "crux", "shared_evidence", "continuous_prediction", "discontinuous_prediction"), and "verbal_disputes" (array of strings, empty if none).
