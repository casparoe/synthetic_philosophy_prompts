The gist she gave was this: back in the 1940s some American critics called the New Critics proved that asking what the author meant is a mistake, the so-called intentional fallacy — their argument being that the author died before we got to the text (this is where she quoted some French guy about the death of the author), so intentions are simply not available to readers, and anyway a poem is a public object that means whatever its words mean in the language, which is why the only evidence admissible in interpretation is the text itself and things like dictionaries and literary history. From this she concluded that intentionalism is basically a refuted position, that biographical material about an author is never legitimate evidence for an interpretation, and that if an author comes out and says 'I meant X by that passage', that tells us nothing about what the passage means — at most it's the author misreading their own work like anyone else.
