In a popular neuroscience book I picked up between semesters, the chapter on conscious will summarizes the Libet experiments roughly as follows: Libet had people flex their wrist whenever they felt like it and report the position of a rotating dot at the moment they first became aware of the urge to move. Scalp recordings showed a ramping signal, the readiness potential, that began around half a second before the movement itself, and crucially before the reported time of the conscious urge. So the brain had already decided to move while the subject still felt the choice lay ahead: conscious intention arrived only after the decision had been made, meaning it is a readout of what the motor system is doing rather than a cause of it. Libet therefore showed that free will, in the sense of conscious agency, is an illusion. Later imaging work went further, predicting which of two buttons a subject would press with high accuracy up to ten seconds in advance, and because the readiness potential is the brain's command to move, the whole causal chain runs without any need for consciousness at all. The chapter closes by suggesting that this undermines our ordinary practice of holding people responsible for their actions.

My background is in philosophy of mind, not neuroscience, so I can spot that this is written with rhetorical confidence but can't reliably sort the careful claims from the inflated ones. I'd be grateful for a sentence-by-sentence audit: which parts would a neuroscientist working on volition accept as stated, which would they wince at as a misleading but common simplification, and which are simply wrong? I'm especially unsure about the status of the readiness potential itself and whether later results genuinely improved on what Libet showed or changed the interpretation of it. Finally, setting aside the science, does the chapter's closing claim about responsibility actually follow from whichever parts of the summary survive correction — or does it depend on sliding from the corrected findings to a conclusion they don't support?
