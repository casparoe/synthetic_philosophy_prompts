the problem is about whether "better than" always carries through. like, if option A is better than B, and B is better than C, does it follow that A is better than C? everyone assumes so, same as "taller than". i think it doesn't, at least not always, and here's my bit of evidence.

take making tea. i like my tea at a certain temperature, say around 60 degrees. my mate likes his scalding, like 90. now obviously i prefer 60-degree tea to 90-degree tea, that's just a fact about me. but here's the thing: if you gave me two cups one degree apart, 60 vs 61, i genuinely could not tell you which was which. so cups one degree apart are basically tied for me — neither is better. and you can obviously build a staircase of cups from 60 all the way up to 90, one degree at a time, where every neighbouring pair is a tie. so you get: 60 is tied with 61, 61 tied with 62, ... all the way up. but at the ends, 60 is clearly better than 90 (to me). so being tied must NOT carry through the chain, otherwise 60 and 90 would come out tied, and they're not.

and i reckon the same trick works with "better" itself, not just ties. imagine choosing between two jobs. job A pays £30,000 and job B pays £30,001. is B better than A? it's more money, sure, but nobody in their right mind would say B is *better* — the difference is too small to matter against everything else you'd weigh up. so for tiny differences, "better" basically goes away. but stack up thousands of those tiny differences and you get £30,000 vs £45,000, and now there's a real question with a real answer. so how can thousands of nothings add to a something? my conclusion: the better-than relation just isn't the clean transitive thing people picture. it's more like a fuzzy mess, and the fuzziness at the small end is exactly what lets big differences exist at the far end.

where i think someone might push back on me: maybe the tea cups and the salaries aren't really cases about "better" at all. maybe when i can't tell two cups apart, they're not *equally good*, they're just... undecidable for me. like the comparison doesn't exist yet rather than coming out as a tie. and maybe that's the same for the £1: B isn't "better" than A, but it's not tied either, the question just doesn't have an answer at that grain. i half suspect this reply is right and half suspect it's just relabelling the problem, and i can't work out which half is correct. that's actually the bit i'd most like your thoughts on: does the "it's a non-answer, not a tie" move rescue transitivity, or does it just shove the mess somewhere out of sight? and if it does rescue it, what did it cost — because it seems like it costs you the idea that every comparison has a verdict, and i'm not sure everyone realises that's part of the deal.
