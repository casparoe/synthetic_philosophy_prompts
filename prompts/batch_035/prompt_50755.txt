Write a dialogue between an epistemic structural realist and an ontic structural realist, in which each tries to persuade the other that their version of structural realism is the right lesson to draw from the success of scientific theories.

Some framing you should build on rather than assume the reader knows:

- The epistemic structural realist holds that our best theories give us knowledge of the *structure* of the unobservable world (its relational patterns, its equations, its symmetry properties), while remaining agnostic or eliminative about the intrinsic natures of the underlying entities. Theories change, but structural content is preserved across theory change — or so the story goes.
- The ontic structural realist goes further: structure is not merely what we can *know*; it is all there fundamentally *is*. Objects, if talk of them is kept at all, are derivative — nodes in a network, positions in a structure, with no non-structural identity of their own.

To keep the discussion concrete, have the characters argue over the following scenario, which they may refine or challenge:

> Suppose the final physics turns out to be formulated entirely in terms of a single symmetry group. Every physical fact is a fact about which quantities are invariant under which transformations of that group, and the individual 'carriers' of the properties drop out of the formulation altogether — much as, in a graph whose only facts are its edge relations, nodes that can be swapped by an automorphism of the graph have no identity over and above their position in the network. The equations admit no way, even in principle, to say which node is 'really' which; any labelling is pure convention.

The ontic structural realist takes this scenario to be the vindication of her view: physics itself has demoted objects from the fundamental furniture of the world. The epistemic structural realist resists: perhaps the invariance of the formalism only shows that structure is what our *representations* can capture, not that the world itself is structure all the way down — maybe there is a further fact about the nodes that no theory could express. They should press on whether that 'further fact' is a mysterious leftover, whether relata can literally be eliminated rather than merely de-emphasised, and on whether the epistemic version's caution is epistemic humility or an unstable halfway house that collapses into either traditional realism or the ontic version.

Requirements:

- Both characters should be equally capable and should respond directly to each other's strongest points; neither should be a foil. Let each score genuine hits, and let each concede a point or revise a formulation under pressure.
- The dialogue should reach some kind of resolution — agreement, a precisely stated residual disagreement, or a deliberate stalemate — rather than trailing off.
- Use British English spelling and idiom throughout.
- After the dialogue, append a short author's note (a few sentences, separate from the dialogue itself) in which you state how confident you are in the accuracy of the philosophical positions you attributed to each character, and identify the single point in the dialogue where you are least sure that you represented a view fairly.
