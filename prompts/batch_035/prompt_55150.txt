I've been chewing on this argument I ran into that says nobody can ever really be responsible for anything, because you act the way you do because of how you are, and to be truly responsible you'd have to have made yourself that way, and to make yourself you'd need something to do the making, which you also didn't make, and so on back forever. Sounded like a cheap trick at first but it's been bugging me, because my own gut verdicts on cases seem to lean on the same regress that makes the argument work. Here they are.

A guy who grew up with horrifically abusive parents, never had a chance, robs a store: my gut says he's not fully to blame, because he never made himself who he is. A woman with a lovely upbringing who spends her life doing kind things: my gut still says she deserves real credit, even though by the same logic she never made herself either. A man who at 30 sees he has a vicious temper, spends 15 years genuinely working on himself, becomes calm, then once at 45 snaps and humiliates a coworker: my gut says he's MORE responsible than the average person would be, precisely because he made himself who he is. Someone whose personality turns aggressive from a brain tumor: obviously not responsible, no conflict there. A guy who knows he gets mean when he drinks and drinks anyway, then gets mean: I keep flip-flopping on this one.

So tell me: is there one principle behind all these verdicts, and if so does it force me into the full no-one-is-ever-responsible conclusion, or does it let me keep some middle position? If I have to drop a verdict, tell me which one and why that one specifically. Then tell me what the principle says about a case I haven't given: a kid raised by wolves (or just completely feral, no human upbringing at all) who kills someone at age 16. Don't give me a menu of options -- pick a principle, commit to it, and show me where my verdicts have to bend. Keep it under 400 words.
