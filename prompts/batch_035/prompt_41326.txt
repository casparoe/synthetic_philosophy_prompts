I am a novelist preparing my next book, and I need a piece of writing that will appear in it essentially verbatim, so it has to hold up to a reader who knows the subject. I would like you to draft it.

The novel follows two characters who have known each other for thirty years: Margarethe, a retired health economist who has always defended equality of welfare as the only honest metric of whether lives are going equally well, and Yusuf, a former rehabilitation-program administrator who left that work convinced that welfare is the wrong currency and that what matters is what people are actually able to do and be — capabilities, with resources at best an input. Over the years they have argued in letters, at conferences, over dinner. In the chapter I am writing, they spend a long weekend at Margarethe's lakeside cottage with the explicit purpose of producing a single document that both will sign: an adversarial collaboration, as the psychologists would call it. Neither expects to convert the other; what they want is a text that fixes, once and for all, exactly where they stand and exactly where they split.

Please draft that joint statement. It should contain, in this order:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims — things both would sign about the inadequacy of unexamined metrics, about the role of interpersonal comparison, about the difference between making people equal and compensating them for misfortune, and anything else you judge they genuinely share. Avoid formulations so abstract that neither could dissent from them.

2. A disagreements section, ideally a table, with each remaining disagreement stated twice: once in wording Margarethe would accept as fair to her view, once in wording Yusuf would accept as fair to his. Do not let either side's phrasing smuggle in a victory.

3. The cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, name a case, argument, or kind of evidence they both agree would bear on it, and record each character's prediction in advance — what Margarethe expects it to show, what Yusuf expects.

The novel gives them three recurring test cases, which I would like the statement to use rather than the usual philosopher's examples, so please build the analysis around these three townspeople: a stonemason whose chronic migraines have never responded to any treatment; a young woman whose ambitions visibly shrank after a decade of scarcity and whom Yusuf suspects of adapting her preferences to her circumstances; and an orchard owner who has spent a lifetime cultivating expensive tastes in wine and opera. Where one of these cases forces a character to concede something to the other, show that concession happening in the text. If you conclude that some portion of their dispute is merely verbal — a matter of how the word "welfare" is being used — identify which portion and why, and let the two of them agree on that much.

The register should be that of a serious public document: measured, precise, addressed to an intelligent reader who is not a specialist. Assume no citations, no references to particular books or authors — just the two positions in their own words. The finished piece should run to something substantial, because in my novel it is printed in full, and the reader must be able to feel the two signatures at the bottom having been earned rather than given away.
