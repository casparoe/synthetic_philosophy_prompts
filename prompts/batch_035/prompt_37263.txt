I keep running into the Korea example in the institutions-vs-geography-vs-culture debate (you know the Why Nations Fail crowd, they got the economics Nobel in 2024) and honestly it gets used as a mic drop and nobody ever actually checks the work. So: one peninsula, one people, one language, basically one continuous history for a couple millennia, split in 1945 at the 38th parallel -- a line drawn pretty hastily by American officers, not chosen by anyone on the peninsula -- and within two generations you have one of the richest countries on earth on one side and a famine on the other. Case closed, geography and culture refuted, institutions win. I want to take that argument seriously rather than nod along. Map out what the Korea comparison is actually doing, inferentially speaking: what has to be true for a split like this to count as a natural experiment, and is the 38th parallel close enough to as-if-random given that the north inherited the Japanese-built heavy industry and most of the mineral deposits while the south was mostly rice paddies (which, note, cuts against both geography and the "the south was always going to win" story)? What exactly does the comparison identify -- institutions as such, or the whole bundle that came with the two occupations (the war that flattened the north in the early 50s, massive US aid to the south, different patrons, different initial factor endowments)? Does adding East/West Germany to the reference class strengthen the inference or muddy it, given Germany had real regional differences before the split? And be honest about what a geography or a culture defender can still say that survives the Korean data -- e.g. that "one culture in 1945" stops being true once the halves diverge for 80 years, so maybe the comparison can't hold culture fixed the way it needs to. Where the inference breaks, say so instead of forcing it through. End with a last line of the form \answer{...} containing exactly one of these three labels -- decisive, suggestive, inconclusive -- for the Korea-as-natural-experiment argument, and make it match what your analysis actually showed.
