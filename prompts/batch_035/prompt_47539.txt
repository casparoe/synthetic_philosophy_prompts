So here's my situation: I got voluntold onto the grant panel of our county arts council (small town, ~30 applications a quarter, $2,500 grants, and every decision has to be defensible in writing because we've had two appeals in the last year). Our chair, a retired high school art teacher, had a conversion experience reading Tolstoy's What Is Art? and now wants the whole panel scoring applications his way. The old rubric was generic mush — "merit, feasibility, community impact" — and she says it let in exactly the kind of polished, empty work Tolstoy would've called counterfeit. As she puts it, for Tolstoy real art is the transmission of a feeling the artist actually experienced, sincerity is the make-or-break condition, and technical skill without a real feeling behind it is just imitation of art. Fine in principle. The problem is that we're five volunteers with no philosophy background and maybe fifteen minutes per application, so what I need is a procedure the panel can actually run: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, the points where it stops with a no, and — this is the part I care most about — the points where it can't produce a score at all and just has to hand the question back to the panel because it's a values call, not a scoring question. And please be honest about which steps are really just judgment dressed up as a checklist.

The hard parts the procedure has to face head-on: we fund work that hasn't happened yet, so there's no audience yet to observe whether the feeling gets transmitted (Tolstoy's "infection" happens in spectators, but we only see a written application and a small portfolio); sincerity is self-reported and literally every application says the artist is passionate; and clarity of an application is not the same thing as clarity of the artwork. If the procedure just ends up scoring how well people write grant applications, it's worse than the old mush and I'd rather know that now.

Then run it on three cases so I can see it work: (1) a youth theater group whose application letter is genuinely moving but whose sample script is stale and borrowed-sounding; (2) a technically brilliant conceptual sculptor whose application is cold and ironic throughout, though we have photos of last year's unveiling and people at it look visibly moved; and (3) the case I already know the panel will hate: an amateur painter, obviously sincere, mediocre craft — the kind of applicant our old rubric rejected politely and that I suspect Tolstoy's logic actually favors over polished professionals. If the procedure says fund her and not the sculptor, so be it, but I want to see the steps that get us there. Finally, tell me straight what this procedure cannot do — my guess is it can't touch Tolstoy's harder claim that the feeling also has to be one worth transmitting (he ties that to the religious perception of his own age, whatever we're supposed to substitute for that in rural Ohio in 2025), and it can't predict whether an audience will actually catch anything. Wherever it's substituting the panel's values for Tolstoy's, the procedure should say so out loud rather than hide it in a score.
