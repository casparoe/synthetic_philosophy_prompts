My professor also keeps using the phrase "geometric rationality" for the family of views where you aggregate preferences multiplicatively / geometrically instead of arithmetically.

My half-formed thought is that there might be a third reading nobody pushed in class: Kelly as the output of a bargaining problem among your temporal selves. Picture each time-slice of the bettor as a separate agent with its own preferences about what happens after it, and log-type aggregation as what a fair compromise between those slices looks like — kind of like how Nash bargaining maximizes a product of utilities instead of a sum. Then "bet Kelly" wouldn't be advice about growth rates OR a smuggled-in risk preference, it would be a fairness claim about how you divide decision authority across your own life. The question I want to write on: can Kelly-style betting actually be derived as an intrapersonal bargaining solution, and if the derivation works, does that make it normatively binding for agents who don't care about asymptotic growth at all — or have I just relabeled the log utility assumption and moved it somewhere less visible?
