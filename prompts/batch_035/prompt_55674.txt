In my philosophy of social science class we have to turn in a short position statement that the rest of the seminar then gets to attack. I've written up where I stand but before I submit I want to check whether it even hangs together, because after staring at it for a week I honestly cant tell anymore. Here's roughly what I beleive:

1. Ontologically there are only individuals. "Society" is just people doing things together — no group minds, no social facts floating above us. Durkheim was basically wrong to treat society as a thing with its own forces.
2. That said, aggregate explanations seem fine to me, and complete in themselves. "The financial crisis happened because the banks took on too much leverage" is a good explanation, and I don't think it's a placeholder waiting for someone to hand over a list of individual decisions.
3. When a company dumps waste in a river, I blame the company. Thats not just shorthand for blaming the managers — the firm is the responsible agent, and it seems like it can be responsible in ways none of its individual members are.
4. Similar with intention: when my university decided to divest, it really was the university that decided, not merely the trustees. Institutions can literally intend things.
5. For prediction I'm fine with representative-agent models — one average consumer, even though no actual person is the average consumer. They often work better than models that track what real individuals do.
6. When an outcome can be explained either way, I think the rational-choice explanation — in terms of individuals' beliefs and desires — beats the structural one.

Some of these feel like they pull against each other (1 vs 2? 3 and 4 vs 1?) but I genuinely don't know which conflicts are real and which would dissolve if I thought about it harder. Can you go through the pairs, rank the real tensions by severity, and say what would have to give in each one? And if there's a single cheap revision that cleans up the most, tell me which it is. One more thing — please don't flag everything as a tension. I have a suspicion at least one of these pairs only looks like a conflict, and I'd like to know which before I embarrass myself in seminar.
