My friend has views about what you can learn from testimony and I've been keeping a list of her verdicts. She says a colorblind person told that a scarf is scarlet can know it's scarlet. But she says if someone tells you the sunset was gorgeous you can't know it was gorgeous -- best you can get is knowledge that they were impressed. Same with jokes: she thinks you can't know a joke is funny on someone's say so, you have to hear it yourself and get it. She's fine with learning from testimony that a bridge is structurally unsound, or that a novel won the Booker. Last week two new cases came up: a chess amateur being told by a grandmaster that a certain endgame study is elegant, and a lifelong anosmic being told by a perfumer that a particular perfume is subtle and refined. What will she say about these two? I care less about the yes/no than about the rule she's actually running -- is it about aesthetic properties vs descriptive ones, about needing acquaintance through your own perception, about needing to "get it", something else -- and which of the two cases would actually discriminate between the candidate rules rather than being settled by all of them at once. And if both cases somehow come out the same way under every rule I've thought of, what further case should I try her on to split them?
