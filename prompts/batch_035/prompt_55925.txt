My friend and I argued til 2am about whether self-deception is even possible and I want to keep thinking about it. She takes the hard line: deception requires a deceiver and a deceived, and if they're the same person the whole thing collapses — you can't believe p while also knowing the evidence is against p, because the part of you that knows should win the argument. I kept pushing a deflationary picture: no secret plot, just ordinary biased processes — being selective about what evidence you go looking for, interpreting ambiguous data generously, steering conversations away from topics that would force the issue. Over time you end up sincerely believing the comfortable thing, and there's never a contradiction because the doubt never gets a chance to crystallize into a belief. She said that's not deception, that's just motivated reasoning with extra steps, and if the person never believed the uncomfortable thing then calling it self-deception is flattering — a real deceiver has to know the truth. Write a dialogue between the two of us continuing the arguement over coffee the next morning. Make it a real fight: she should press on whether my picture can handle cases like the spouse who "kind of knew all along," and I should press back on whether her view makes self-deception literally impossible, which feels like explaining a real phenomenon away rather than explaining it. Neither of us should win cleanly.
