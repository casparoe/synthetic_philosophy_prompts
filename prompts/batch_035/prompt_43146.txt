Construct a pair of cases, A and B, that jointly isolate whether retributivist desert, the communicative justification of punishment, and forward-looking consequences are genuinely independent levers, as follows. In both cases the same offender committed the same offense, and the two cases are identical in every respect except one: in A the offender remains fully lucid and capable of understanding a sentence imposed on him; in B he has, since the offense and entirely independently of it, suffered a cognitive decline so severe that he can no longer understand what a trial, a verdict, or a punishment is. Beyond that difference, the specifications are:

1. Guilt and substantial desert are beyond dispute — conclusive evidence, full responsibility at the time of the offense — and whatever one thinks about remorse, reform, or passage of time, those factors are held constant across A and B.
2. Every forward-looking consideration points away from punishment, for principled rather than stipulated reasons: the offense exploited a legal loophole that has since been closed and a professional position the offender no longer holds, so it is unrepeatable by him; the marginal general deterrence contributed by his prosecution is approximately nil, and you must make that credible rather than merely announcing it; incapacitation and rehabilitation are moot.
3. This is not a scapegoat case. The question is whether to punish someone genuinely guilty, not whether to harm the innocent for utility.

Keep it realistic — a courtroom, a nursing home, a fiduciary fraud unit, nothing theatrical. Then walk through the verdicts: the purely desert-based retributivist must punish in both; the consequentialist must decline in both; the communicative retributivist, who holds that punishment is justified only as the intentional delivery of condemnation that the offender must be able to receive, must punish in A and refuse in B. State which feature of the pair does the work and what happens when it is removed. Finally, address three pressures honestly: whether the communicative theorist's flip reveals that their view makes desert hostage to post-offense facts about the offender's mind, or instead shows that infliction of suffering the recipient cannot register as condemnation is not punishment at all but mere harm; whether the desert theorist can decline to punish in B without smuggling consequentialism back in — for instance by treating desert as merely pro tanto and defeasible when nothing of value can come of satisfying it — or must simply bite the bullet; and whether the communicative theorist can rescue punishment in B by redirecting the censure at the community, and if so, whether that redirection quietly collapses the theory into audience-focused consequentialism. A scenario that lets desert drift between the two cases, or that quietly re-imports deterrence through the back door, fails the exercise.
