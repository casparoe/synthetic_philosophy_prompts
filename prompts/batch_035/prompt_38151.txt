My flatmate and I run a small reading-group blog, and after we worked through the GEB chapter on the location of meaning (the whole frame message / outer message / inner message apparatus, the records that can't be played on any jukebox around, the rock whose fissures could in principle encode Bach) we had a row about it that lasted three evenings, so naturally now I want to outsource it to our readers. Please write a "which view of meaning do you actually hold?" quiz for us, around 9 questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. The whole point is that readers get sorted by what they say about cases rather than by which label they find flattering, so no question may just ask "where do you think meaning lives?" — the cases have to do the work.

Cases I want in there somewhere (mix in your own as needed): a message written in a script no living person can read, like Linear A; a vinyl record of some piano piece that sits sealed in a vault forever and is never played; DNA from an extinct species sitting in a freezer with no organism left to read it out; a hunk of rock whose cracks happen to formally spell out the first four bars of a tune if you squint at it the right way; and a broadcast beamed at the stars in a constructed language, aimed at receivers about whom we know literally nothing except that they presumably build receivers. Each option should be answerable by someone who has never heard of Hofstadter or of any of this literature.

Then give a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto leaves, and the leaves have to include at least these: the view that the inner message carries its meaning by itself once decoded, full stop; Hofstadter's own distributed view, where the outer message does the heavy lifting and a message is best understood as a trigger for a decoding mechanism that is already there; a hardline receiver-side view on which messages never *contain* meaning at all, they only occasion it — nothing means anything to a rock, and nothing means anything without a meaning-maker; and whatever combination of answers you think no actual position occupies, marked as unoccupied, with one sentence on whether that corner is empty because it's incoherent, because it's unattractive, or because nobody bothered. If two of the leaves can't really be seperated by the cases (I suspect the distributed view and the receiver-side view blur once you push), say so rather than pretending a question cleaves them apart.

Finally, after the key, add a short note saying how confident you are that each question genuinely pulls apart the leaves it's scored to, and flag the single question you're least sure about and why. Please write everything in British English.
