A few of us in a philosophy of mind reading group just finished Dennett's tower of generate-and-test (Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregrian creatures) and we want to turn it into something usable for a public workshop we're running next month, where most attendees won't have read any Dennett. The idea is a decision tree / flowchart that takes an arbitrary system and outputs what kind of mind it has, with every branch node phrased as a question a non-philosopher could actually answer about, say, a cockroach or a chess engine, without already knowing what the leaves are called. We figure the natural spine is something like: does the system improve its behavior only across generations, or within a lifetime; does it adjust by blind trial-and-error in the world, or can it trial things offline against some internal model before acting; and does it have access to culturally transmitted mind-tools (language, maps, notation) that let it restructure how it thinks rather than just what it thinks. But please work out the actual tree yourself, including any nodes we're missing.

Where we want you to put in the real work: we'll hand out a list of about ten systems and people walk the tree for each one. Please pick the cases so they stress the tree rather than flatter it, and at least a few should be genuinely contested -- we were thinking things like a rat in a Skinner box, a corvid caching food it can't see, AlphaGo playing move 37, a slime mold that took a longer route around salt, a large language model frozen at deployment, and a reflective adult reorganizing their own goals after reading. For each case note where the tree sends it and where you'd personally fight the verdict. Relatedly, we want you to flag what the tree can't separate rather than pretending it's clean -- e.g. whether a frozen LLM counts as Popperian, or whether its generate-and-test all happened during training, feels like a real edge the fourfold schema just doesn't resolve, and I suspect there are others.

Last thing: after the tree, include a short section on the empty rooms -- combinations of node-answers that no familiar system occupies, or that only weird artifacts occupy. Like, can something be Popperian without being Skinnerian at all (imagines alternatives but never updates from real-world feedback)? Is Gregorian-without-Popperian even coherent? For each empty branch say whether you think it's unoccupied because it's conceptually impossible, physically unattractive for evolved creatures but maybe fine for engineered ones, or just unexplored. One of my friends claims the whole tower conflates learning speed with representational depth and that two of the four kinds are really the same kind, so if you have an opinion on that, a paragraph at the end on whether the fourfold split should be collapsed would be great. Keep the node questions plain-language and jargon-free, since our audience will include high schoolers.
