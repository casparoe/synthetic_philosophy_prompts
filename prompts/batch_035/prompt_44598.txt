ok so for my philosophy elective our teacher split us into groups and each group has to make a quiz that sorts people by their actual views on a topic, and my group got "is death bad for the person who dies?" (the Epicurus thing where he says death is nothing to us since when death is, we are not). most people just answer "yes dying is bad lol" without thinking, so we want questions that are weird enough that you can't answer on autopilot, and a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto actual positions instead of just "are you an epicurean? y/n"

some stuff that has to be in there (my teacher's requirement):

- the deprivation question: is death bad ONLY because you miss out on goods you would've had? or is it bad in itself, like annihilation itself is a harm even if you'd have had a terrible rest of your life anyway. i think this one splits people who don't realize it splits them
- the symmetry point from Lucretius: we all think the time before our birth wasn't bad for us, so why is the time after our death bad for us? we need a question that catches people who accept the symmetry for the past but refuse it for the future, and the key has to say which position that combo matches (i think it's some "asymmetry" view but honestly not sure what it's called, you can tell us)
- timing: WHEN is death bad for you? there's a puzzle that it can't be bad before it happens (you're fine) and can't be bad when it happens (you don't exist) and can't be bad after (you still don't exist). maybe one question like "was the week napoleon lost at waterloo bad for napoleon LAST tuesday?" sounds dumb but i've seen people freeze on it

please also include at least 2 or 3 concrete scenario questions, like: a guy who takes a drug that makes the last 10 years of his life bliss but wipes all memory of it every night, vs a guy who lives those 10 years normally — which death is worse for whom and when. or a case where a person's death is instant and painless in their sleep at 90 after a complete life, vs a 19 year old hit by a bus. the point is the questions should sort by what people say about cases, not by which labels they like, so no question can just be "do you believe in the deprivation account"

structure we want: 8-10 questions, each with 2-3 answer options (keep the options short-ish so the quiz isn't exhausting), then a scoring key mapping combos of answers onto positions: full epicurean (death never bad for the one who dies), deprivationist, the "death is bad but not for the person" view (bad for survivors / bad simpliciter), maybe a desire-based one (death is bad only if it frustrates desires you actually had). if some answer combo doesn't fit any real position, either name it as unoccupied or make up a label and say nobody's really defended it. and please flag if any two of the positions can't actually be separated by the quiz, like if deprivationist and desire-based people answer all 8 questions the same way, we'd rather know than pretend

also the quiz is for other high schoolers so no jargon in the questions themselves (you can use jargon in the key), and if you could end with one bonus question that's more of a gut-check (like "would you take a pill that extends life to 500 years even if everyone you know dies normal lifespans") that would be cool, marked as not counting toward the score
