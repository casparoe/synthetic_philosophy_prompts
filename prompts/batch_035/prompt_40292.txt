I help run a small population ethics reading group at my university — we're mostly grad students, a mix of philosophy and econ people, and every year the exact same fight happens: someone claims everyone secretly accepts the procreation asymmetry (creating a miserable life is bad, but creating a happy life isn't good, or at least isn't owed in the same way), someone else says that's just a status-quo bias dressed up as a principle, and we never actually figure out where each person stands. So this year I want to settle it with a quiz. Like one of those dumb online personality quizzes, except it actually does philosophy.

Please take on the role of a professional philosopher who works on population ethics, and think carefully before writing — ideally do a quick brainstorm about which cases really split the positions, since that's the whole point. Then write the quiz: eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, and a scoring key at the end mapping answer patterns onto named positions. The positions that need to show up as leaves are at least: straight total utilitarianism, the procreation asymmetry itself, critical level views, person-affecting views (including the Narveson-style making-people-happy versus making-happy-people distinction), Benatar's antinatalist asymmetry, and the Epicurean line that coming into existence can't be bad for you because there's no one it's bad for. The questions have to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like — so no question can just ask whether someone accepts the asymmetry. Some cases I'd like in there: failing to create a happy child when you easily could (my friend swears people who say this isn't wrong are just weirded out rather than convinced — probe that), the couple who can conceive now and get a child with a minor disadvantage or wait and get a different, healthy child, the tradeoff between adding lots of barely-worth-living lives versus a few wonderful ones, and one case I invented that I want you to include: a hospital can spend its budget either extending an existing patient's happy life by forty years or bringing an equally happy new person into existence — if those feel different to you, is the difference about identity or about the act of creation itself? I genuinely don't know what my own answer to that says about me, so make sure the scoring key handles it.

Two more things. First, flag any pair of positions your quiz can't actually separate — I suspect critical level and lexical views collapse into each other on most realistic cases, but tell me if I'm wrong, and be honest about it rather than pretending the key is cleaner than it is. Second, check my cases: if one of my answer options is secretly two options, or if a case is doing no diagnostic work, say so and fix it — I'd rather have seven clean questions than ten mushy ones. The quiz is going out to about fifteen people who've read Parfit but argue like it's a pub, so the questions should be readable but not dumbed down.
