My cousin cornered me at a wedding about the Ship of Theseus (there was wine) and I discovered I have very strong opinions that I now suspect don't hang together at all. So I wrote down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and I want someone to tell me whether there's an actual principle in here somewhere or whether I'm just vibes all the way down.

The cases:

1. The classic setup: a ship gets repaired plank by plank over decades of sailing until none of the original wood is left, old planks chucked overboard as they're swapped out. Same ship. Obviously. Anyone who says otherwise is being difficult on purpose.

2. The Hobbes twist: the old planks weren't chucked — a scavenger has been quietly collecting them for years and reassembles them into a ship in dry dock. Here's where it gets embarassing. My gut says the reassembled one is the original, and the one that's been sailing around is a very faithful replica. I know this is in tension with #1. Saying both are "the original" seems off the table, but so does picking either one.

3. My grandfather built a stone cairn on a hill on the family land. A storm knocked it down decades ago and the stones scattered. Last year some hikers gathered up the stones and rebuilt it in the same pattern on the same spot. That is NOT the same cairn. It's a new cairn made of old stones. I feel this strongly and I cannot explain how it coexists with #2, since on paper they look identical.

4. Saw my favorite band last year. Zero original members — complete turnover maybe three times over by now. Still the same band, and I'd say I "saw them" without lying.

5. Grandpa's axe: head replaced once, handle twice. Still grandpa's axe, still hanging in the family shed, no hesitation.

6. There's that famous shrine in Japan that gets torn down and completely rebuilt every twenty years — same design, on a neighboring site — and has been for something like a millennium. Feels like one continuous shrine the whole time, honestly more "the same" than most actual old buildings.

7. The university I work at is 800 years old; no building, no person, probably not even the coat of arms is original. Same university, trivially. Not even an interesting question.

And then the case I genuinely can't settle: if a person were (set aside feasibility) taken apart and reassembled from the very same parts — is that them again? I flip on this weekly, and weirdly which way I flip seems to depend on whether I'm currently picturing the cairn or the shrine.

So: is there a principle that captures all of these, or close to it? I can see candidate hooks — continuity of matter, continuity of form or function, who's doing the replacing and why, the kind of thing involved — but each one seems to save some of my verdicts by torching others. Tell me which of my verdicts has to go, what a consistent principle would look like, what it predicts for the person case I can't decide, and ideally why the cairn and the dry-dock ship pull apart for me when they're structurally the same case.
