Every fall I teach the consciousness unit in our philosophy of mind survey, and somewhere along the way I've basically converted myself to Dennett's side of the room: no Cartesian theater, consciousness as multiple drafts, content getting fixed piecemeal with editorial processes happening all over the brain and no single place where it all comes together before the subject reports on it. I've gotten comfortable presenting the color phi phenomenon and the cutaneous rabbit as if they more or less clinch it, and I tease the "theater" imagery the way everyone in my generation does. But last week a student pushed back in a way that actualy stuck with me — she asked what would even count as evidence against the multiple drafts model, and my honest answer ("well, nothing could prove there's a theater") sounded uncomfortably like unfalsifiability the second it left my mouth. I'd like to stop hand-waving before I teach that unit again.

So play it straight with me: what would genuinely count against the multiple drafts model? I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they'd be. Consider neuroscience results — say, single neurons in the medial temporal lobe that seem to fire for one specific celebrity's face across different photos, or the discovery of some genuine convergence zone where everything a subject can report gets bound together before it's reported. Do those threaten the view, or does the view just calmly redescribe them the way it redescribes the temporal anomalies? Also consider the more philosophical trouble: the objection that denying the theater amounts to changing the subject rather than answering the question of experience, the charge that the model quietly collapses into behaviorism or deflationism about phenomenal consciousness, the thought experiments about inverted or absent qualia that the model is committed to biting the bullet on. For each defeater, say whether a committed Dennettian would concede it counts against the model or would insist it rests on a confused intuition pump, and flag the ones where you think the concession is warranted.

And please be honest about the insulation question: has the model been built so that any empirical finding can be absorbed by positing another draft or another editorial revision, and if so, is that a vice or just the price of doing business with any theory in this corner of the field? I'd like a ranking from fatal to merely awkward, and ideally something I can bring back to that student without sounding like I've been bluffing for fifteen years.
