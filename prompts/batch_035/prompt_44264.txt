My sister and I got into an argument about whether history can be objective, and she read me a paragraph from a pop-history book she'd been reading that went roughly like this: 'Ranke founded modern objective history on the motto "wie es eigentlich gewesen" — show the past as it actually was. For him that meant zero interpretation: the historian effaces himself, lets the documents speak, and a properly trained historian can thereby produce a record of what really happened, free of any standpoint. Carr demolished this by pointing out that historians select and shape their facts, and Hayden White finished the job by showing that a work of history is essentially a novel — the same events can be emplotted as tragedy or comedy, so apart from bare dates there's no fact of the matter about what happened.' I'm inclined to think the Ranke half is too rosy and the White half is a strawman, but I honestly dont know where the errors are or how subtle they are. Can you go through it claim by claim: tell me what a working historian would accept, where its overstatement starts, and whether the skeptical conclusion (no fact of the matter beyond dates) actually follows from the parts that are accurate? Also, something that keeps bugging me: people argue about how to translate the word eigentlich in the motto, some saying it means just 'actually' and others something more like 'properly' or 'essentially'. Does that translation fight do any real work in this debate, or is it a distraction from the actual disagreement between Ranke and his critics?
