My supervisor and I disagree about the logical form of disposition ascriptions, and I want to locate precisely where the disagreement lives. Her credence that dispositions like a vase's fragility have a conditional structure at bottom — that a correct analysis would eventually reduce them to suitably hedged conditionals — is about 0.75. Mine is about 0.25. Please give me your own number, but the more important part is the decomposition. Assign a rough probability to each of the following, with one line of justification, add any further terms you think belong, and then combine them:

1. That fink cases are coherent: cases where the object has the disposition, yet the conditional is false because, were the stimulus to occur, an independent process triggered by the stimulus itself would remove the disposition's basis before manifestation could occur (as in the stories where the object is wired to a device that would alter it at the very moment it would be struck).
2. That mask cases are coherent: cases where basis and disposition remain intact while some interferer blocks the route from basis to manifestation (as when a drug's disposition to kill is masked by an antidote administered alongside it), so that the conditional is false even though the disposition is genuinely present.
3. That repair strategies can in principle succeed: restricting the antecedent to suitable triggers, adding ceteris paribus clauses, or recasting the analysis in terms of what happens in close worlds where the basis persists and nothing blocks the manifestation.
4. That there is a deeper circularity problem: that finks, masks, and antidotes can only be characterized by reference to the very disposition they interfere with, so that a conditional analysis hedged with no-fink, no-mask provisos never becomes genuinely reductive.

Then tell me (a) which term your final number is most sensitive to, (b) whether the disagreement between her at 0.75 and me at 0.25 is more likely about the coherence of the cases (1 and 2) or about their significance for the analysis (3 and 4), and (c) what argument or kind of case would move your number most.
