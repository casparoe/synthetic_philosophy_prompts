The exam question:

"Explain Bradley's regress. Then assess the argument: does the regress show that the Facts theorist — who holds that a proposition's truth consists in its correspondence with a fact, where a fact's constituents are a particular, a universal, and an exemplification relation that ties them — cannot after all explain the unity of the fact, and so of the proposition? Your answer should (i) reconstruct the regress carefully, (ii) address whether the regress is vicious or benign, and (iii) evaluate at least one reply available to the Facts theorist."

The student's answer:

"Bradley's regress was first formulated by Russell in the Principles of Mathematics (1903), though it is named after Bradley, who held a similar view. The regress arises for the Facts view as follows. Suppose the fact that a is F consists of three constituents: the particular a, the universal F-ness, and the exemplification relation E that ties a to F-ness. But for these three to constitute a single fact, they must themselves stand in a further relation: a, F-ness, and E must be tied together by some further relation, E′. And E′ must in turn be tied to its relata by a still further relation, and so on without end. Every unifying relation demands a new unifying relation to unite it with what it unifies.

The regress is fatal, because any infinite regress in an explanation is vicious: it explains the unity of the fact only by positing a new entity whose unity is equally unexplained, so nothing is ever explained. Bradley concluded from this that reality must be a single all-inclusive whole in which there are no relations at all.

The same problem arises for propositions, and this shows that the Facts theorist cannot explain truth. A proposition, such as the proposition that a is F, is itself a complex that must be unified by the same kind of tie, and the correspondence theory says the proposition's truth consists in its corresponding to the fact. But if we cannot explain the unity of the fact, we cannot explain the unity of the proposition either, since the two are the same problem: the regress transfers directly from facts to propositions, and the proposition is unified only if the fact is.

The standard reply is due to Armstrong. Armstrong bites the bullet: he accepts the infinite regress of instantiation relations and holds that it is harmless, because each further instantiation is a smaller and smaller entity, so that the regress converges and there is no need for the explanation to terminate. Alternatively, the theorist can deny that exemplification is a constituent of the fact at all: the tie is not one more thing in the fact but the fact's holding together, and if there is no constituent there is nothing further in need of unification. Either way, the regress does not refute the Facts view, and the correspondence theory survives."
