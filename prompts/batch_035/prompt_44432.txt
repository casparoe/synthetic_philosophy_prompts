Write a mock FAQ, roughly 600 words, for a fictional government body called the Office for the Verification of Other Minds. The premise: since the problem of other minds can't be solved by philosophy, a bureau has been set up to handle it administratively, issuing Certificates of Sentience to humans, animals, and machines on the basis of observed behaviour alone. The satire should be deadpan throughout and legible without a wink — the humour has to come from the actual epistemology. So: the Bureau's core methodology is the Analogy From My Own Case, meaning applicants are certified on how closely their outward responses resemble the assessor's own, and the FAQ should quietly show the problems with that (infants, octopuses, and people from other cultures scoring lower for resemblance rather than for lacking minds; the assessor's own mind being the one thing the Bureau cannot verify). Include a question about whether philosophical zombies can be certified, and have the Bureau answer, straight-faced, that yes, provided they complete the questionnaire in full. Somewhere there should be a regress gag — e.g. the appeals panel also being made of other minds whose status is pending. Also cover how machines are assessed, in a way that shows the Bureau can't distinguish "responds as if it experiences" from "experiences" and has decided this is the applicant's problem, not theirs.

Keep it fair: the piece should exaggerate real features of the epistemic situation (first-person access, behaviour as the only evidence, the unobservability of experience) rather than inventing silly ones, so someone who knows the literature nods rather than winces. Aim for the tone of an actual government FAQ — numbered questions, officious, mildly reassuring.

At the end, add a short note (outside the FAQ, a few sentences) on where the piece leans on contested choices: e.g. whether you've implicitly made the Bureau behaviourist or functionalist, whether the zombie answer presupposes zombies are really possible, and where you'd expect a defender of the analogy argument or a Turing-style operationalist to say you've caricatured them.
