writing up my views on the species problem for a phil bio seminar and I want to check the package hangs together before I present it. Here's roughly where I am: (1) species are real — when two mammalogists disagree about whether some population of squirrels is a distinct species, one of them is just wrong; it's not a bookkeeping choice we make for convenience. (2) for organisms that reproduce sexually, the biological species concept is basically right: species are groups of populations connected by actual or potential interbreeding and separated from other such groups by reproductive isolation. (3) species are individuals — big spatiotemporally bounded lineages, more like organisms than like classes — so there's no such thing as the essence of Homo sapiens, and a species that goes extinct never comes back, even if something identical evolves later. (4) bacteria form perfectly good species, and not in a grudging second-class way — a staph species is as real as a bird species, even though bacteria don't interbreed the way animals do. (5) ring species like the Ensatina salamanders around the Central Valley are one species, not two, because there's gene flow all the way around the ring, even though the two ends where the ring closes can't mate with each other. So: which of these actually fight each other? I can sort of see that (2) doesn't obviously cover (4), but I can't tell whether that's fatal for (1) or whether I can keep one concept with a footnote for asexuals. And I genuinely don't know if the ring species case contradicts (2) or is just a messy case at the boundary, which every concept will have. There's also something that bugs me about (2) + (1) that I can't put my finger on — something about whether a population's species status should depend on what other populations exist. Rank whatever tensions you find by severity, tell me the cheapest fix for each and what that fix costs, and if a single revision would clean up most of it, say which one. Don't be diplomatic — if two of my commitments can't both be true, just say so.
