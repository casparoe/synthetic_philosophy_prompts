Me and two friends got into it after civics class about whether voting is actually pointless. Jake said one vote basically never decides anything — even in a close election the chance you're the tie-breaking vote is like one in a million, so voting costs you an hour for basically zero effect, and if you multiply the tiny chance of mattering by the benefit to society, the math comes out way below the cost of your time. Maya said that's a prisoner's dilemma: if everyone reasoned like Jake, nobody would vote and democracy collapses, so voting is like cooperating even when defecting is smarter for you individually. Jake said that just proves his point — you can't derive an "ought" for him from what would happen if everyone copied him, since he only controls his own ballot and everyone else's choice is fixed no matter what he does. Maya came back that people aren't strangers in a lab game, we're part of a community, and besides she'd feel guilty skipping an election, and guilt is a real cost, so for her voting is clearly rational. Jake said feelings might explain why she votes but they don't make it rational, just comfortable. Then our teacher overheard and mentioned that in our town's school board race last year the incumbent won by literally 3 votes, and Maya said that settles it. Can you go through their whole argument and say who's right at each step — and does the 3-vote thing actually change the math, or is it just a scary anecdote?
