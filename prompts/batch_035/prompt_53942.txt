I'm on the clinical effectiveness committee at a mid-size hospital network (I'm one of the docs, not a statistician, so bear with me). We're being pressured to switch our standard protocol for treating a certain kind of serious infection from drug A to drug B. The pitch deck shows that over the last two years, patients who got B had lower 30-day mortality than patients who got A, and the difference is decently sized, like 3 percentage points. The committee is mostly sold.

But one of the residents dug into it and split patients by how sick they were at admission (we have a 3-tier severity score). Within every tier, A actually beat B. All three strata, same direction. She's now convinced the aggregate number is a mirage -- B got given to healthier patients more often, or something like that, so of course its raw numbers look better.

Now we're stuck. Half the committee says the stratified numbers are obviously the real ones, that's what risk adjustment is for. The other half says hold on, the severity score is measured at admission, and there's a further wrinkle: patients who start on A and then deteriorate during the first day sometimes get switched to B, and the analyst counted people by the drug they ended up on. So the strata aren't clean either, and maybe the aggregate comparison is contaminated in its own way too.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before next month's meeting. I want it to (1) explain clearly enough for non-statisticians why both directions of the argument can be right or wrong depending on things we haven't nailed down yet, (2) give an actual recommendation -- switch, stay, or gather more data first -- and if it's gather more, specify what data would actually settle it, (3) be honest about what we should do if the data we'd ideally want just isn't collectable at our scale. Also, one committee member keeps insisting that whether you break things down by severity is "a causal question, not a statistical one" -- if that's right, the memo should explain why, and lay out exactly which causal assumption we'd be relying on either way. Keep it to about 800 words, no citations needed, and please dont be wishy-washy in the recommendation, we have to decide something by the next meeting.
