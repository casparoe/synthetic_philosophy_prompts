I'm drafting the research statement for my job-market materials on safe task-directed agents, and before I send it anywhere I want a consistency check on the package of views I've been assembling. Here are the commitments:

1. Low impact is the right safety target for capable task-directed agents, and it has to be formalizable without anything close to a complete specification of value — if defining impact required going through a ranking of world-states by goodness, the program would have failed at its stated purpose.
2. The right baseline for measuring a step's impact is what the world would have been like had the agent idled at that step, rather than the world in which the agent had never existed at all. Counterfactuals over the agent's whole existence are too global, and they punish or reward it for events it couldn't influence.
3. Reversibility should do most of the work in the measure: a change humans could cheaply undo isn't much of an impact, however large it looks on paper.
4. The measure has to be task-relative. A construction robot should be able to build a house, and building a house is a large and largely irreversible change to the world; low impact can only mean low impact beyond what the assigned task legitimately requires.
5. A low-impact agent should not be penalized for intervening in bad events it didn't cause — if it can cheaply stop a fire that would otherwise destroy the building, stopping the fire shouldn't count against its impact budget.
6. The measure should not create offsetting incentives: the agent should never gain, in terms of its impact score, by undoing or neutralizing changes made by others in order to restore a baseline.

I've held some of these for years and others are recent additions, and at this point I honestly can't tell where the real stress is — they were each adopted piecemeal, not as a system. Go through the pairs: which are in genuine tension, which only look to be, and for each genuine one, how severe it is and what the cheapest repair would cost. Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them flat, and then tell me whether there's a single revision that would clean up the most, or whether the package is fine once the right distinctions are drawn.
