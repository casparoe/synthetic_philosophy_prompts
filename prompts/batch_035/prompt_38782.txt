I've been part of the rationalist community since the old LessWrong days, and I now help run a regional forecasting group of about sixty members. Each quarter we vote to allocate a small internal grants pool (around $5,000) to one of several member-proposed projects, historically under one member, one vote. At our last meetup, two members proposed replacing this with a competence-weighted ballot: each member's vote would be scaled by a factor derived from their rolling Brier score on our calibration platform over the past two years.

The ensuing debate split the group roughly three ways, and I have to draft a recommendation before the next meeting, so I'd appreciate help thinking it through.

The proposers argue that it's incoherent for a community premised on beliefs paying rent to spend years carefully measuring who is actually good at getting things right, and then to throw those measurements away at the moment of decision. If calibration matters anywhere, they say, it matters where money is allocated.

An older member counters that the grant vote isn't a forecasting question at all. It's mostly about which projects we collectively find valuable, and no amount of predictive skill makes your values more authoritative than mine. On her view, the proposers are smuggling a competence claim into a domain where competence doesn't apply, and equal votes are the right resolution of value disagreements among epistemic peers.

A third member raised a subtler worry I couldn't fully evaluate on the spot: even granting that weighting would improve decisions in the short run, the high scorers' advantage would compound — funded members gain visibility, status, and practice, the scoreboard increasingly reflects accumulated advantage rather than current skill, and ordinary members start deferring rather than deliberating. Over time, she claims, the deliberation itself degrades, and the thing that made the group worth belonging to dies, even if the funded projects keep getting objectively better.

So my questions for you: (1) Is there a principled line between the parts of this decision that are competence-trackable and the parts that aren't, and can a balloting scheme respect that line in practice? (2) Does the deference-and-entrenchment objection show that the weighted scheme is wrong, or merely that it would need safeguards, and if safeguards, what would they look like? (3) Most importantly for my draft: is there a middle arrangement that captures what's right in all three positions — for instance something that weights votes on questions with checkable answers while leaving value judgments equal — or do these positions ultimately rest on incommensurable commitments such that no arrangement can genuinely split the difference? I'd rather submit a recommendation that names the trade-off honestly than one that papers over it.
