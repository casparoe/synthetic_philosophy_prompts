I teach an intro philosophy elective and for the unit on Sartre I'm pairing Being and Nothingness with The Sopranos, specifically the therapy storyline. The obvious hook: Tony treats his panic attacks (the ones that started with the ducks) as a medical fact about himself, something to be diagnosed and managed, while Sartre would say anguish is precisely the awareness of your own freedom, and that reclassiying it as a symptom is a textbook case of bad faith. Melfi's whole therapeutic framework keeps handing him excuses — his mother, his upbrining, his "condition" — which is either the show exposing how therapy can become a bad-faith factory, or Sartre missing something real about how formed people are by adulthood. And I can't decide whether the finale, with Tony refusing to change after all that insight, is meant as a refutation of radical freedom or just of optimism about people. Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion: which scenes make the strongest Sartrean case, where the show genuinely resists the reading, and whether Chase ends up dramatizing something Being and Nothingness can't account for.
