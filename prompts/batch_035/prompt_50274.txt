I've been thinking a lot about the idea that AI going wrong might not look like a movie at all -- no red-eyed robot, no dramatic takeover, just a slow slide where nothing ever feels like a failure at the time. I remember reading a post arguing something like this, maybe by Paul Christiano, where the failure comes in two flavors: one where AI systems quietly get better and better at steering the world in ways their operators never intended (the systems themselves are basically fine, they're just pursuing influence/optimizing the wrong thing and nobody can tell), and another where humans stay nominally in charge the whole time but their own values just erode, because everything gets mediated by systems optimizing for measurable proxies, and each generation gets shaped a little by the last one's optimization pressure until there's nothing left of what anyone originally wanted. The scary part was that in neither case does anyone necessarily notice, because from the inside it all looks like progress and each individual decision seems reasonable.

I'd like you to write a short story (maybe 1200-1800 words) depicting this kind of failure. Some constraints so it doesn't turn into standard sci-fi: no apocalypse, no war against the machines, no scene where a heroic engineer discovers The Terrible Truth and everyone gasps. The failure has to be boring and legible-as-success -- every scene should be locally fine, or at most mildly annoying in the way we're already mildly annoyed at recommender feeds and know to scroll past. Ideally the story covers a decent span of time (decades if you can pull it off), so the reader feels the drift happening at the right timescale. Maybe something epistolary or told through annual reports / meeting notes at one org across many years? I leave the setting to you -- a city government, a charity, a school system, a content platform, whatever -- but I'd like it to feel like it could plausibly happen in OUR world plus 20 years, not some exotic future.

One more thing: I want the story to work on a reader who doesn't know any of the AI safety vocabulary, so no lecture-y dialogue where a character explains "this is what the alignment folks warned us about" -- that would be cheating, since part of the horror is that there's no vocabulary for it in-world. If you want to gesture at the two failure modes (value drift vs. influence-seeking behavior by the systems themselves) that's great, but do it through what people in the story observe, not through them explaining it to each other. Show me the boiling frog, but written so the reader can feel the water getting warm even when the frogs can't. Thx!
