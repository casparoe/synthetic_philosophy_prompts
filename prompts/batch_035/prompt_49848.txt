My friend (a psychology grad student) and I stayed up way too late arguing about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism, and it's still bugging me, so help me figure out what kind of disagreement we actually had.

The topic: hedonism says wellbeing is about pleasure, but what IS pleasure such that the buzz from a cold swim, the satisfaction of finally getting a proof to work, an orgasm, and a slow dinner with someone you love all fall under it? Sidgwick basically threw his hands up at this over a century ago, saying the agreeableness of these things doesn't seem to share any one felt quality, and as far as I can tell the philosophical literature hasn't moved much since. The two main camps seem to be: (a) phenomenal accounts, where pleasures share some common felt tone (hedonic tone), and (b) attitudinal accounts, where pleasure isn't a feeling at all but something like taking a pro-attitude toward a state of affairs you're in. My friend, having actual psych training, pointed out that the affective neuroscience people talk about a common pleasure circuitry (reward, wanting vs liking and all that), and claimed that settles it in favour of a common quality. I said that even if the circuits overlap, that's at best a neural similarity, not a phenomenal one -- the felt quality of enjoying chess and the felt quality of eating cake still seem to share nothing, and if you're going to define pleasure by what lights up on a scan you've changed the subject from wellbeing to neurochemistry.

Then she offered her own move, which I hadn't seen in the philosophy stuff I've read: pleasures are unified dispositionally, not by a shared feeling or attitude -- they're the experiences we are motivated to prolong for their own sake, the ones where, mid-experience, we'd press a 'more of this' button. Nothing else is plausibly common to all of them. My instinct was that this is circular (wouldn't the motivation to prolong already have to be pleasure to do the unifying work?) but honestly I couldn't make that stick.

So here's what I actually want: a diagnosis, not a verdict on who won. Is this dispute substantive or partly verbal -- are the phenomenal and attitudinal sides just using 'pleasure' for different things (a kind of experience vs a kind of stance toward experience), and if so, which one is hedonism about wellbeing actually committed to? Or is there a real question underneath about whether wellbeing can be grounded in something so disunified? Does my friend's dispositional proposal actually break the stalemate, or is it a third entry in the same stalemate (and is my circularity complaint fair)? And what would count as progress here -- a thought experiment, some result about whether people can blindly agree that wildly different experiences are pleasurable, a distinction nobody's drawn yet -- or should we expect this one to stay stuck forever, and if so is that a sign it's ill-posed?

Please answer in British English, and keep it concrete -- tell me what each side would say about actual cases (the chess, the swim, the orgasm, the dinner), not just in the abstract.
