I have no background in philosophy; this comes from thinking things through on my own, and I've argued myself into a corner I can't get out of.

It started when I tried to say what all the pleasant things have in common — cold water when I'm thirsty, the relief of finally scratching a bite, the calm after finishing a hard project, a song that lands right. I couldn't find it, and I ended up with this argument:

1. If "pleasant" is to name something real, the pleasant experiences must share some feature; otherwise the word is just shorthand for "whatever I favor at the moment," and any view that says the good life is the pleasant one ends up saying nothing.
2. A feature of an experience must be either something the experience feels like, or something the experience makes me want or do. I can't see a third option: an episode of consciousness is either a feel or a source of inclination.
3. It isn't something they all feel like. Every candidate quality I test fails — warmth (cold water isn't warm), calm (roller coasters aren't calm), softness (a deep-tissue massage that hurts in a good way isn't soft) — and a supposed generic "pleasant feel" just puts the word back in, so it doesn't count.
4. So if anything unites them, it's on the wanting side: pleasant experiences are, roughly, the ones I want to continue.
5. But that test misfires in both directions. I want a tense scene in a film to keep going, or a distressing documentary I can't stop watching, without calling any of it pleasant; and I keep a miserable training run going while wishing the whole thing weren't happening at all.
6. So the pleasant experiences share no real feature, and "pleasant" names nothing beyond my momentary favoring.
7. And the same reasoning applies to "pain" and to every similar word for mental goings-on: nothing real is shared by the pains either — a burn, a grief, a cramp — so "pain" would be shorthand for "what I'm against at the moment."

That conclusion seems to prove too much: the burn on my hand feels bad for me in a way that looks like more than my currently opposing it. But every step seemed fine to me at first. If I have to choose, I'd most like to reject step 2, since I can't stomach steps 6 and 7 — yet I don't know what a feature could even be if it's not a feel or a pull. So: which step should I give up, and what does giving it up cost — what am I forced to admit I was wrong about, and what awkward cases does the repaired picture still have to swallow?
