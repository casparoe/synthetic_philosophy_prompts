I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I help run a reading group where two members have spent a year deadlocked on the torture-versus-dust-specks problem. One holds that harms aggregate across people, so a sufficiently enormous number of trivial harms can outweigh one grave one; the other holds there's a lexical threshold — some kinds of suffering are qualitatively different, and no number of dust specks can sum to them. To stop them from reciting stock arguments, we invented variants of our own: one where every future conscious being gets one half-second papercut versus one technician enduring fifty years of unrelievable pain, and one where all the specks land on a single person across a lifetime. They disagree about the second variant for different reasons than the first, and I want that made explicit. Please draft the joint statement they should be able to sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: a numbered section of claims both accept; the precise disagreements, each phrased so its own side would endorse the wording attributed to it; the cruxes — I suspect one is whether the anti-aggregation intuition is scope insensitivity rather than a moral datum, and another is whether aggregation across distinct persons is even a coherent operation as opposed to a metaphor; and, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If any part of the dispute is verbal — say, over what it means to sum experiences had by different people — identify it and say why. Begin the document with the single-sentence joint conclusion both parties could sign, then proceed directly, with no preamble and no restatement of this brief.
