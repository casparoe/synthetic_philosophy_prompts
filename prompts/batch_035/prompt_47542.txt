I coordinate community management for a civic nonprofit that runs a network of moderated online forums: neighborhood forums in a dozen mid-sized cities, plus themed ones on parenting, personal finance, and local environmental issues. Lately my moderators keep asking me the same question in different words: this forum has gone monochrome — everyone agrees, dissenters have left or gone quiet, and the regulars treat the forum's own consensus as settled fact. Should we intervene, and how?

From my reading I've absorbed that the literature distinguishes an epistemic bubble, where members simply lack exposure to outside voices, from an echo chamber, where members are trained to actively distrust outside voices, and that the obvious remedy — deliberately exposing people to the other side — can help with the first while strengthening the second. But none of this arrives with instructions, and my moderators are volunteers who can spend perhaps an hour per forum per month, cannot read years of back threads, and have no budget for behavioral research.

Please write a decision procedure they can actually run on a forum: the diagnostic questions in order, what counts as evidence of mere omission versus evidence of pre-emptive discrediting scripts about outsiders, where the procedure should stop and say leave it alone (a community can be homogeneous without being broken), and where it should hand the decision back to me because the question is one of our values rather than of fact. Then run it on three cases: a neighborhood forum in the middle of a zoning fight where no one has ever encountered the economic analyses the other side cites; a parenting forum where the regulars have ready-made stories about why pediatricians and public-health agencies say what they say; and a chronic-illness support forum that rejects outside reassurance about prognoses — a case where I suspect the right output is that epistemic repair is the wrong frame entirely. Be honest about where an hour-a-month volunteer will misdiagnose, and about what the procedure cannot do at all.
