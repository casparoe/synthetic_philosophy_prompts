On the other side are institutional explanations, which point to things like secure property rights and constraints on rulers rather than to what people believed. Please compare these as rival causal accounts of the same outcome. Assess whether citing a culture of disciplined, this-worldly asceticism meets that standard in a way that citing, say, credible commitments to property rights does — or whether the cultural story merely relabels the phenomenon it was supposed to explain. Then consider whether the two explanations can be turned into each other's mechanisms, for instance if Protestant congregational life produced the literacy, trust, and networks that certain institutions required, and say whether such a reduction makes the culture-first story explanatorily deeper or explanatorily empty. Conclude by classifying what kind of variable culture can honestly be in a causal explanation of social outcomes — root cause, mechanism, confounder, or mere correlation — and defend that classification against the objection that the same strictures would sink the institutional explanation as well.
