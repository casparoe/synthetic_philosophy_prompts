I have been writing novels for twenty years, and I have lately become obsessed with a philosophical idea I only half understand: Daniel Dennett's proposal that the self is a center of narrative gravity — that there is no inner witness or little person in the head, but rather an abstract posited character, brought into being by the brain's habit of spinning narratives, the way physics finds it useful to speak of a center of gravity without supposing there is a tiny heavy thing located there. I want this idea translated into the only vocabulary I actually command, which is the vocabulary of craft. Please restate the position, as faithfully as you can, in terms of point of view, protagonist versus narrator, backstory, character arc, retconning, the unreliable narrator, the difference between the character on the page and the character assembled in the reader's head, and the distinction I care about most as a working writer: a character is not a posit, it is a made thing, and it is made by someone standing outside the text. Then give me the residue report, because I suspect the residue is the whole story. Three places I want you to be especially careful. First, Dennett's metaphor has two halves, a physics half and a narrative half, and my craft vocabulary covers only one of them: in my world, characters are authored artifacts, not centers of anything — so when you translate, tell me whether the self comes out more like a center of gravity (a useful abstraction over a real distribution of stuff) or more like a character (a construction with an author), and whether Dennett can have both at once. Second, every narrative I have ever written has an author outside the story, and every retcon I have ever committed was invisible to the characters inside it; Dennett's narrative seems to need a protagonist who somehow also writes the book, and perhaps reads it too — say plainly whether that is expressible in my vocabulary or whether you had to fudge it, and what the fudging hides. Third, a published character is fixed; the self seems to revise its own canon constantly without any edition being final — tell me whether the craft notion of retconning can carry that or where it buckles. After the translation and the report on what does not survive it, give me your judgment on the live question: is the residue a defect of my craft vocabulary — something real about the self that fiction-making has no way to say — or a sign that the philosophical claim is borrowing the prestige of narrative without paying its debts? Throughout, distinguish reporting Dennett from interpreting him, and where you are flattening a controversy or guessing at his intent rather than stating something he clearly holds, mark it as such. At the end, state how confident you are in the translation as a whole, and single out the single point you are least sure of.
