I found this long review essay I was planning to lean on for framing, but before I build a whole article on it I want to make sure it's not leading me astray. The gist:

"Human violence has been declining steadily since prehistory, and the evidence starts there: the share of people who died at the hands of other people was many times higher in hunter-gatherer and tribal societies than in the state societies that replaced them. Even the 20th century, despite its world wars, killed a smaller fraction of humanity than typical tribal conflict did, so humanity's worst century still confirms the trend. Since 1945 the great powers have fought each other directly zero times, and this long peace proves that major war has become statistically obsolete. On the numbers, a person born today is far less likely to die a violent death than at any earlier point in history, so we should expect the decline to continue."

Parts of this feel a bit too smooth to me?
