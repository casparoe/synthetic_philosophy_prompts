Science journalist here -- I mostly write about animal behavior and cognition, and there's a question I keep failing to google my way out of. Every book about wasps or insect intelligence trots out the old story of the digger wasp: she drags her paralyzed prey to the burrow entrance, leaves it there, goes inside to inspect the burrow, comes back out, and pulls the prey in. But if you nudge the prey a few inches away while she's inside, she comes out, drags it back to the entrance... and does the whole inspection routine again before pulling it in. You can apparently repeat this many times and she never catches on. Some French naturalist noticed this back in the 1800s (Fabre, I think? It's been a while since I read the original anecdote). The standard takeaway is "insects are wind-up toys."

Here's my actual question: I keep noticing what looks like the same shape of behavior in humans, and I can't find a word for the human version. Instances I've seen: my dad checking the stove before leaving -- three knobs, always left to right -- and if you speak to him mid-check he goes back to the first knob and starts over, he doesn't just resume where he left off. A colleague whose leaving-the-house routine is keys, wallet, phone, patted in exactly that order, and if you hand him his keys while he's mid-pat-down he begins the whole pat-down again from the top. And the one that really spooked me: me, on a trail I've hiked dozens of times, taking the old fork instead of the rerouted one becuase in that moment the "keep walking straight" program just ran and nothing in me registered that the trail had changed.

So is there an established term for this in humans? It doesn't seem to be just habit, because habits usually flex when circumstances change -- the defining feature of this thing, at least as I've been describing it to myself, is that a small perturbation makes it re-run from the start rather than adapt. It doesn't look like OCD either, as far as I can tell: no distress attached, these are all functional people. And I have a vague memory that philosophers got real mileage out of that wasp at some point -- something about the difference between a system that merely executes and one that can notice it's executing? If there's a term, I want to know what it is, what exactly it's supposed to cover and not cover, and whether anyone thinks the human cases matter for anything bigger -- free will, conciousness, where you draw the mind/machine line. I'd rather not just get a name thrown at me; I want to know what the phenomenon actually is and where its edges are.
