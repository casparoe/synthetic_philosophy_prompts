I'm a high school senior, and since we started a unit on political philosophy this term, I've been trying to work out what I actually believe about the state -- or my school, or my parents -- restricting people "for their own good." I've been going by my gut on cases, and I'd like to know whether there's a principle underneath my verdicts, or whether I'm just inconsistent. Here are the verdicts.

My school cafeteria puts the fruit at eye level and the chips on the bottom shelf: fine, and honestly kind of clever. My city banned the sale of extra-large sugary drinks: something about that bothers me, even though I accept a seatbelt law backed by fines. I can't see what the difference is supposed to be, since both just stop me from doing something risky to myself, and the soda ban arguably interferes less. A nearby state requires helmets for adult motorcyclists: fine, I think, though I have less confidence there than about seatbells. My country changed organ donation so that you're a donor unless you check a box: fine, and I even think it's better than making people opt in. But a gym whose membership automatically renews unless you call to cancel during a narrow window: not fine -- and this bothers me, because it's the very same trick as the cafeteria shelf, steering people who aren't paying attention, and I approved of one and not the other. My parents stopping me from dropping out at seventeen: fine. The same parents reading my diary because they're worried about me: not fine, even though the diary reading is probably motivated by genuine concern and does me no physical harm.

So my questions. Is there a single principle that captures all of these, or close to it? If not, which of my verdicts would you overturn, and why those? If a principle can save every verdict only by becoming baroque -- a special clause for each case -- I'd rather know that than get a principle built to flatter me. Please state whatever principle you propose precisely, tell me which of my cases each part of it is there to handle, and say what it predicts on cases I haven't listed. In particular, I'd like to know what it says about the one I keep going back and forth on: my state is debating raising the age for buying tobacco and vaping products from eighteen to twenty-one, which would apply to me for the next few years, and I genuinely cannot decide whether that's like the seatbelt law or like the soda ban.
