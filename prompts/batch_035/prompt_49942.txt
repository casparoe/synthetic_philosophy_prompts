I'm presenting a paper at a metaethics workshop defending Quine's picture of moral values: moral claims are nodes in the web of belief, revised not by observation but by the same systemic virtues that govern scientific theory choice — conservatism, simplicity, modesty — so the fact/value divide marks a difference in insulation from evidence, not a difference in kind of epistemic standing, and irreconcilable conflicts among ultimate values are to be admitted rather than papered over. The audience will be mostly expressivists and quasi-realists, plus two or three robust realists and one Kantian; none of them work on Quine, and they will read him as either an unconfessed noncognitivist or as smuggling normativity into the "epistemic virtues" he uses to evaluate whole webs. Draft my objection prep sheet: exactly five objections, numbered and ordered by how much damage each would do, each a sentence or two giving the objection phrased the way this audience would phrase it, my strongest two-minute reply, and a note on where that reply is softest. One of the five should be the question I am hoping nobody asks. No introduction or conclusion — just the five numbered items.
