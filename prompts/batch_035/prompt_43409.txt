My brother-in-law keeps bees as a hobby, and this spring he asked me to help him settle a disagreement that has stalled a practical decision. Several of his hives are doing poorly, and the standard remedy is to remove the current queen and introduce a new one in the hope of turning the colonies around. He has no objection in principle to killing an insect, but something about this specific act bothers him, and the family is split on whether his unease is justified.

His mentor, an experienced beekeeper, thinks the unease is misplaced: the queen is best understood as a component of the colony, something like a shared reproductive organ, so replacing her is closer to pruning a tree than to killing an animal, and the thing one might be harming — if there is any such thing — is the hive as a whole, which will simply carry on with the new queen.

His daughter, a biology student, thinks this is nonsense: only individual bees could ever feel anything, since each has its own small brain, and the colony is just a name for a great many bees in one box. She adds that if his unease is about anything real, it should attach to every single bee affected, since on her view there is one unit of concern per bee and that is the end of it.

My brother-in-law, meanwhile, suspects the truth lies somewhere in between. The bees in a hive share information so intensively — through scent signals passed around the colony and their elaborate dances — that he is no longer sure it makes sense to count up what happens to them the way one would count it for, say, chickens in a coop.

I would like to help him reach a decision, and more importantly to give him a principled way of thinking about such cases. When a beekeeper replaces a queen, splits one hive into two, or merges two colonies that will then fight until one side prevails, what is the right way to think about who or what is affected? If bees can feel anything at all, how many experiencers are there in a hive — one per bee, one for the colony as such, both, or neither — and how should that answer guide what he ought to do?
