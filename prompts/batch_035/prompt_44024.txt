I run patient safety investigations for an NHS trust, and every serious incident report we produce has a seperate section for 'causal and contributory factors'. The arguments I keep getting into are about omissions — things somebody *didn't* do. The pre-op pause wasn't done before a wrong-site block; the call bell was left out of reach and hourly rounding hadn't happened before a fall at night; the on-call doctor didn't escalate to the consultant. Half my team thinks listing an omission as a cause is basically meaningless (nothing didn't happen, so how can nothing cause something?), and the other half wants to stick every conceivable non-action into the report, which dilutes it so much that none of the recommendations ever get made. I gather philosophers have actually worked on this — causation by omission, whether it turns on what *should* have happened versus what usually happens, that kind of thing — but I need something my investigators can run in under an hour per case, with zero philosophy background, and phrased so it survives being read by a coroner and by bereaved families, not just by us.

So please write me a decision procedure: the questions to ask in order about any candidate omission, what counts as a good enough answer at each step, where the procedure stops with 'don't list it', and the points where it has to hand the call over to the panel because the question is one of values rather than facts. Then run it on three cases: (1) a wrong-site nerve block that went in because the team didn't do the pre-procedure pause, though the surgeon had also marked the wrong limb beforehand, so both an act and an omission are candidates; (2) the night-time fall where the bell was out of reach and rounding hadn't happened, and we genuinely cant tell which one mattered; (3) a delayed diagnosis where the on-call doctor didn't escalate overnight and the delay probably made no difference to the outcome, but the family is (understandably) furious and wants it listed as a cause. Include at least one case where the procedure gives an answer I won't like — I suspect it'll come out exonerating somebody I'd rather it didn't. And please be upfront about what the procedure can't settle, since the report has to go out either way. Please write the whole thing in British English. Thanks!
