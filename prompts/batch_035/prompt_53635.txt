I'd like a thought experiment built to specification, on the question of whether giving priority to the worse-off insulates a moral theory against the classic utility monster objection.

Construct a realistic scenario — one paragraph, set in an ordinary institution that genuinely rations something (a hospital ethics committee allocating staff time or drug doses, a relief agency allocating supplies) — in which the "utility monster" is operationalized without science fiction: a single individual whose responsiveness to the contested resource is ordinary in kind but enormous in magnitude, so that channeling the entire budget to this one person produces vastly more total benefit than spreading it around. The case must be built so that prioritarianism comes out *more* monster-friendly than utilitarianism: the prioritarian should strictly favor giving the final marginal unit of the resource to the monster, while utilitarianism is indifferent or leans against it. The verdicts must fall out of the theories' own machinery, not be stipulated: do not build in any premise about which theory gives the right answer, and do not rig the interpersonal utility scale — the monster's outsized gains should rest on an ordinary though unusual sensitivity to treatment, not on an assumption that their utility simply counts more.

After the scenario, provide the following:

1. Two versions of the case differing only in the monster's baseline wellbeing — one in which they are among the worst-off, one in which they are comfortably well-off — so that baseline is isolated and everything else is held fixed.
2. For each version, derive the allocation each theory recommends and explain why, showing explicitly where the priority weighting does work that the utilitarian sum does not.
3. Identify which structural feature of prioritarianism is doing the work — the concave transformation of wellbeing, the additive separability across persons, or something about the interpersonal scale — and say what happens to the case if that feature is removed or blunted (for instance, by capping admissible wellbeing or by adding a sufficientarian floor).
4. Give the strongest reply available to the prioritarian: can they argue that the case works only under an assumption about the wellbeing scale they are free to reject, and does that reply preserve the theory's advertised resistance to well-off monsters without giving up its claims about the badly off?

Please write the scenario and walkthrough in prose; the only formatting requirement is that you end with a final line of the form "Verdict: X", where X is a short phrase naming the structural feature you identified in item 3.
