Longtime science-and-ideas reporter here, and I need a number, not vibes. I'm doing a piece on the insect welfare debate — the stuff that used to live on utilitarian blogs and now has actual researchers publishing on whether bugs matter morally — and my editor's one-line take was "so is it wrong to spray your kitchen for ants or not." I'd like to be able to say something sharper than "philosophers disagree."

Here's the case I want you to price: a homeowner finds an ant colony in the kitchen wall and sprays it. Stipulate the spray works instantly — the ants drop dead with zero distress beforehand. Set aside everything collateral (runoff, pollinators, human health effects, what the neighbors think). I want your credence that the act was pro tanto morally wrong, as a probability, and then the decomposition that produces it.

Some sub-questions you'll probably want as terms in there: whether insects are sentient at all, whether their experiences (if any) have enough moral weight that killing many of them counts against the act even setting aside the homeowner's convenience, and — this is the part I find genuinely weird — whether death is even bad *for* an insect, given that the standard account of why death is bad for you seems to rely on things like future-directed preferences or a life-narrative that insects plausibly lack. A painless death for a creature with no stake in its own future looks alot like no harm at all, unless there's some other story about why killing is bad. So maybe the whole thing collapses into an argument about the badness of death rather than about sentience, which would be a fun twist for the piece.

Give me a rough probability for each term, a line of justification for each, how they combine into the final number, and which single term the answer is most sensitive to. And flag it honestly if my "instant, painless kill" stipulation is doing sneaky work — i.e., whether the number would jump or crater once you reintroduce the realistic version where the poison probably isn't instant and there's distress involved. I'll take honest uncertainty over a fake clean number, but I do want numbers.
