Question for people who know the functionalism literature better than me. Everyone cites multiple realizability as the reason functionalism won over the identity theory, but I keep running into objections to MR itself and I honestly can't tell which of these are serious and which are just philosophers being annoyed. Can you rank them from most damaging to least, and say briefly why each lands where it does?

1. The neuroscience point: brain structures are heavily conserved across species, so at the level neuroscientists actually work at, the same neural mechanisms keep turning up in different animals, and the supposed alternative realizations are way more similar to each other than MR fans let on.
2. Kim's causal exclusion worry: if each physical realizer does its own causal work, the higher-level functional property is left causally idle -- so MR secures multiple realization of a property that doesn't do anything.
3. The fine-graining point: realizers only come out different if you describe the function coarsely. Once you individuate the functional role precisely enough, it ends up matched to basically one physical setup, so MR is an artifact of how coarsely you carved the role rather than a fact about the world.
4. The triviality worry: with a liberal enough way of counting inputs and outputs, nearly any physical system realizes nearly any function -- a brick, the whole chinese economy, whatever -- so functionalism risks being trivially true and explanatorily empty.
5. One I came up with and can't evaluate myself, call it the "no common cause" worry: if two realizations are genuinely physiochemically different, then no single physical kind explains why they both implement the same role. The unity of the functional kind rests entirely on our decision to describe them alike, which makes MR look like a bookkeeping convention rather than a discovery about minds. Is this just a repackaging of 3 or 4, or does it add something they miss?

Also curious whether you think any of these actually hurt functionalism as a theory of mind, or whether they only undercut MR when it's used as an anti-identity-theory argument.
