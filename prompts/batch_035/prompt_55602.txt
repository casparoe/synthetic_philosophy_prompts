For a seminar unit on the inefficacy objection — the worry that an individual's contribution to a collective harm like climate change, industrial animal farming, or a mass abstention makes no difference, and so cannot be wrong — I need a thought experiment that runs the objection backwards. Construct a case in which the agent's contribution to a collective harm is, by the agent's own evidence, as likely to slightly reduce total harm as to increase it (expected impact roughly zero, perhaps even marginally positive), yet the act still strikes us as a clear instance of wrongfully participating in that harm.

Constraints: realistic setting — a consumer market, a workplace, or local politics; no demons, perfect predictors, or firing squads. The counterfactual benefit must flow from an ordinary economic mechanism (overstock of perishables, threshold-triggered reordering, redundant supply) rather than a contrived coincidence. The agent must have no way of knowing, before or after acting, which way their act tipped the scales. And the case must earn its verdicts rather than stipulate them: a scenario that simply declares the act wrong, or rigs the facts so it is obviously harmless, fails.

After the scenario, walk through what each of three views says and why: (i) pure difference-making consequentialism, on which an act's deontic status is fixed by whether it actually makes anyone worse off; (ii) an expected-consequence view, which judges acts by the harm they can evidentially be expected to cause; (iii) a complicity or participation view, on which joining a practice whose aggregate is wrongful can itself be wrong regardless of marginal impact. Then identify the single feature of the case that is doing the work, say what happens to each verdict if that feature is removed, and give the difference-making theorist's best attack on the case — in particular, whether the familiar redundant-participant examples help her or hurt her. Begin your reply with the scenario itself, no preamble, and state each of the three verdicts in a single sentence before defending it.
