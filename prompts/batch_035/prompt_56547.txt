I'm a backend engineer and somehow let myself get talked into the policy working group of my city's public safety advisory board (long story, my neighbor chairs it and I'd said "sure, I'll attend one meeting"). The actual item on our plate: the city has to send a recommendation to the council about what to do about the escort massage parlors along the old highway strip. Three factions on the working group. One wants the Nordic-style approach: stop charging the people selling, start charging the buyers, plus fund exit services — their pitch is basically "Sweden did this in 1999 and the thinking is that demand is what keeps the whole thing running, shrink demand and the exploitation shrinks with it." The second faction wants full decriminalization like New Zealand did in 2003 — their line is that everything illegal about it stays illegal (coercion, minors, trafficking), but consenting adults buying and selling shouldn't be a police matter at all, because every version of prohibition just pushes the trade somewhere darker and makes workers afraid to call the cops when something actually bad happens. The third faction keeps pointing at Germany and the Netherlands and saying look what licensing regimes actually produced — red light districts that became tourist attractions and, their claim, magnets for cross-border trafficking, so the Netherlands walked parts of it back — and they want some heavily zoned, heavily regulated licensing system as a "middle ground." None of us are social scientists, we've each read like one article, and the faction I'm least sympathetic to made a point I can't actually refute (that exit services make way more sense under any regime than any of the actual enforcement proposals, so maybe the whole fight is downstream of the thing nobody's funding).

Can you draft the memo I'd circulate to the working group before next month's meeting? It should recommend one of the three options (or some hybrid, if you think that's honest) with concrete reasoning the other factions could actually engage with rather than feel dismissed by — especially the strongest argument of whichever side loses, that part has to be treated fairly, not as a strawman to knock down. Please also include a short closing section on what evidence would change the recommendation — like, what would the city need to see in 5 years to conclude the choice was wrong — and where the reasoning is most likely to fail, since I'm the one who'll have to defend this thing in a room full of people angrier and better informed than me. Also if any of the faction's factual claims as I've relayed them are off or oversimplified (the Amsterdam stuff especially, I'm repeating it secondhand), flag that in the memo rather than building on it silently.
