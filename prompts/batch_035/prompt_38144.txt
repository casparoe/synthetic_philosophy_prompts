Quick stats/philosophy thing I got into with my roommate, and I want you to actually take a side, not survey the options.

My TA says students who show up to the optional Friday review sessions end up about half a letter grade higher in the course. This week I'm deciding whether to go (it's Friday night, there's a thing I'd rather be doing). My roommate thinks the TA's claim is basically meaningless. His reasoning: the sessions cover nothing the lecture didn't already cover; the people who go are exactly the ones who'd have gone over that material on their own anyway; so the session is a mirror, not a motor. Then he said the part that's been bugging me: if you picture the most similar version of the world where he skips it, that's also a world where he's not the type to crack his notes on a Friday, so the grade comes out the same — going can't be what moves the grade.

Here's what feels off to me. When he picks his "similar world" where I skip, he's letting who-I-am be different there too, because in the real world whether I skip and who I am travel together — the skipping never happens on its own, it happens *because* of what I'm like. But the question I'm actually facing seems more like: everything about me — my habits, how much I'd otherwise study, the fact that I'd rather be out — stays exactly as it is, and the single difference is that this one evening I'm in that room. It feels like he's answering a different question, one where my skipping is *evidence about me*, and then using its answer for my question, where the skipping is just a thing I do or don't do.

So, committed verdicts please, no "both have a point":

1. Is the "most similar world" move the right way to figure out what happens if I skip, or is he quietly changing more than the one thing he's supposed to be changing? And if he is, what's the cleaner way to set up the comparison — spell out exactly what gets held fixed and what gets varied.
2. Given that, what should I make of the TA's number? Junk? Roughly right but for the wrong reasons? Something in between? Say which and defend it.
3. Bottom line: do I go or not, and why?

Don't hedge with "it depends on your goals" — you have enough to pick, so pick.
