I chair the Rules Committee of the Meridian Debating Society, a 400-member club whose bylaws were drafted years ago by a founder fond of Peter Suber's game Nomic — a fact that now explains most of our difficulties. Our Article VII governs amendments: any bylaw change requires a simple majority of members present and voting at a general meeting with quorum (20 members), with the proposed text circulated seven days in advance. Last month, the membership passed — by simple majority, under Article VII as it then stood — a new amendment reading: "Notwithstanding anything to the contrary in these bylaws, any future amendment to Article VII shall require a two-thirds majority of the entire voting membership, whether present or not." Call this the Entrenchment Clause.

Two weeks later, a faction filed notice of intent to repeal the Entrenchment Clause by simple majority at the next general meeting, on the following argument: the Entrenchment Clause was itself merely a simple-majority amendment to Article VII; whatever an ordinary majority can enact, an ordinary majority can repeal; and at the time the repeal is voted on, the Clause is only Article VII's current text, not a constraint standing above it. They add, less delicately, that they intend to hold the repeal vote at the poorly attended August meeting, where "two-thirds of the entire voting membership" is mathematically unreachable anyway, so the question is simple majority or nothing. Our Secretary has asked the Committee for a formal opinion on whether she should certify a simple-majority repeal as valid, or refuse certification and require the two-thirds-of-all-members procedure — and she needs it before the meeting notices go out.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate to the Committee (and, if adopted, to the Secretary and the membership). It should reach a clear, actionable bottom line — certify, refuse, or something more precise, such as a conditional ruling keyed to how the vote actually goes — and it should reason from the internal logic of self-amending rule systems rather than from any particular jurisdiction's constitutional law, though analogies to familiar doctrines like implicit entrenchment or eternity clauses are welcome if flagged as analogies rather than authority. Please engage the strongest version of the faction's argument, including the claim that a majority-procedure cannot bootstrap itself into a higher one, and the worry that if we refuse certification we are taking a side on exactly the question the Clause was enacted to settle. Address the quorum-attendance maneuver explicitly, since it converts a theoretical dispute into a practical one. The opinion must be at most 600 words, and should open with its recommendation so the Secretary can act even if she reads no further.
