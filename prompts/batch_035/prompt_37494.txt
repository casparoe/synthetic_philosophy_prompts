My friend is a committed Everettian (everything branches, nothing ever collapses) and we keep arguing about what probability can even mean for her. To her credit she actually gave me numbers over dinner — what she'd say before the split about where she'll find herself:

1. Two branches, equal amplitudes, different outcomes: 1/2 each.
2. Two branches, amplitudes √(2/3) and √(1/3): 2/3 for the heavier one. She was pretty firm its not 1/2 just because there are two branches.
3. Three branches: "lose" with squared amplitude 1/2, and "win" with total squared amplitude 1/2 — except the win gets realized by two macroscopically distinct apparatus states (a bell vs. a whistle, settled by a later decohering event), so fine-grained the weights are 1/2, 1/4, 1/4. Asked for the probability of a win however its announced: 1/2. So she's not counting the fine branches.

Next week's case, and I want your prediction before I run it past her: same 2/3–1/3 split as case 2, but in the heavy branch she'll be woken up twice (monday and tuesday, amnesia in between), and in the light branch just once. At an awakening, not knowing which branch or which day she's in: what does she say is the probability she's in the heavy branch?

Honestly I care less about the number than about the rule she's implicitly running. I'm fairly sure more than one rule fits her track record so far, and I suspect they give different answers on this one — one that never looks past the branch weights, and maybe one that counts her future awakenings. What are the candidate rules, what does each predict here, and if you could hand her one more thought experiment that would really settle which rule it is, what would it be? My guess is it takes something where she gets copied in only one branch, but you tell me.
