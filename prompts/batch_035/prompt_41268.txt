(2) I nonetheless think the principle of alternative possibilities is right — no moral responsibility unless you could have done otherwise. (4) I think Jones is fully blameworthy in the standard Frankfurt scenario — Black, the counterfactual intervener, is standing by to force the decision to kill Smith if Jones shows any sign of wavering, but Jones never wavers, Black never fires, and the whole causal history is just Jones deciding on his own. (5) I'm also pretty sympathetic to the flicker-of-freedom reply — the thought that even in Frankfurt cases there's always some tiny residual alternative left over (at minimum, the alternative involving Black actually intervening, or Jones beginning to waver and then being forced), so PAP survives Frankfurt's counterexamples rather than being refuted by them.

Now here's where I keep getting confused, and I honestly can't tell where the real problem lives. And then there's (6), which I forgot to mention and probably belongs on the list: I think what ultimately grounds responsibility is the actual sequence — whether the decision flowed from Jones's own reasons-responsive machinery — and I genuinely don't know whether that's compatible with hanging onto (5).
