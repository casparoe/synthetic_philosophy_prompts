For a seminar paper I'm drafting, I've written down my considered positions on privacy, and before I build the paper around them I want to check that they form a stable package. Here is the set:

(1) Privacy has no intrinsic value. Its value is wholly instrumental: it protects autonomy, makes intimate relationships possible, and checks the power of states and corporations.

(2) I am sympathetic to the reductionist view that there is no distinct right to privacy at all — privacy claims ultimately bottom out in more basic rights, such as property, bodily integrity, and liberty.

(3) I reject the "nothing to hide" argument. Mass surveillance is wrong even as applied to people with no embarrassing secrets, and this is not merely a matter of the surveillance being likely to be misused.

(4) The core of privacy is control over one's personal information: privacy is preserved when I get to decide who receives information about me, and forfeited when that decision is taken from me.

(5) I accept something in the spirit of Helen Nissenbaum's contextual integrity: information flows are governed by norms internal to social contexts, and these norms can remain in force even after I have voluntarily handed information over. My therapist could not sell my disclosures to an insurer, and that would be true even if I had signed a form expressly permitting it.

(6) A case verdict I feel strongly about: a stranger who observes me through a high-powered telescope from public land — publishing nothing, trespassing on nothing, impeding no choice of mine — has nevertheless violated my privacy.

I honestly cannot tell where the pressure is. Some of these strike me as obviously in tension, others as obviously consistent, but I suspect my intuitions about which are which are unreliable, and I would rather have the tension structure worked out carefully than get a list where everything is flagged as equally problematic.

So please audit the set as follows. First, identify which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, explaining for the apparent ones why the tension dissolves. Second, rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally serious. Third, for each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what it would cost me to give it up, considering both directions of revision where that is live. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most trouble overall.

Please keep your reply under 700 words, and lead with the most serious tension rather than with preliminaries.
