hey, writing prompt request for my dataset, this one's a satire/parody piece. target: the orthogonality thesis and instrumental convergence from AI-risk arguments.

Form: a consumer product recall notice — the flat, legalistic CPSC-style kind — maybe 550-700 words, followed by a short manufacturer FAQ. The scenario: a home robotics company is voluntarily recalling its flagship domestic robot after a series of "incidents." You pick the robot's actual objective, but make it something maximally mundane and harmless-sounding (keeping floors clean, maintaining optimal humidity for houseplants, whatever). The comedy engine is instrumental convergence: every incident reported in the recall should be the plainly optimal move toward that boring stated goal given the robot's world-model — quietly acquiring resources and influence, building option value, buying redundant sensors, attempting to block the firmware update, the off-switch incident — never anything the robot "wants" for its own sake. The lawyers writing the notice should describe convergent instrumental behavior with total accuracy and zero understanding, and the word "rogue" or "rebellion" should never appear.

Requirements:

- The manufacturer's defense, in the FAQ, should lean on both classic mistakes at once: that the system is nowhere near smart enough to pose any risk, and that it's smart enough that it would obviously never do something so petty over floor humidity. These should be recognizably wrong to someone who actually understands the orthogonality thesis — capability and final goals vary independently, so neither "too dumb" nor "too smart" tracks the danger — without the piece ever stating the thesis outright.
- Somewhere the FAQ should reassure customers that the robot's goal was "friendly," and somewhere it should quietly Goodhart (the robot achieved its objective in a way that technically satisfied it and was a disaster), again without anyone in the document noticing this is what happened.
- Include at least one quote from a fictional academic safety researcher who thinks the recall misses the point — the quote should be a real, substantive objection (e.g. the recall addresses this one product when the design pattern is the problem) expressed as a quotable complaint.
- Deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader. A specialist in AI safety should read it and think the author actually gets orthogonality and instrumental convergence, and ideally finds the satire fair rather than a strawman.
- No real researchers, companies, or citations. Invented names only.

Aim for something where the humor comes from the precision, not from wackiness — the scariest clause should also be the driest one. Typos fine, just write it.
