I'm taking a philosophy of science course this term, and we recently covered the idea that the available evidence can fail to single out one theory as true. There's a specific configuration I keep running into, in the readings and outside them, that I can't find a label for, and I'd like to know whether the field has one. The setup is this: two accounts of the same domain agree about every observation that has been made and, as far as anyone can tell, about every observation that could be made. When you ask each side what experiment would decide between them, they name the same experiment and agree in advance on how each possible result would be described. What keeps the dispute alive is something else: the two camps disagree about which of their shared commitments is doing the real work. One side treats A as fundamental and B as a consequence that happens to hold in our corner of things; the other treats B as fundamental and A as the derived, local fact. Two instances: in the course we discussed whether, in general relativity, the metric counts as part of the container or as a field sitting on a stage — where both sides accept every reading of every rod and clock; and in an economics seminar I sat in on, whether market prices are to be explained by individual preferences or whether individual preferences get their content from prices — again, both camps would accept the same data and the same future data.

This is clearly adjacent to what our course covered under underdetermination, but in the standard cases the rivals at least differ in some possible observation, so evidence could in principle close the gap; here neither side believes any possible instrument reading would move the other, and both say so openly. It also doesn't look like what our textbook called incommensurability, since the two camps understand each other perfectly well and share vocabulary. So my questions: does this configuration — full observational agreement plus a standing dispute over which shared claim is basic — have an established name of its own (conventionalism? something like "equivalent descriptions"? Poincaré came up only in passing), and if not, how should it be carved? I want to know what exactly the disagreement is over, what it is not over, and where its edges lie — specifically, what separates it from ordinary underdetermination on one side and from a merely verbal dispute on the other. Please lead with your verdict on whether a name exists, and keep the whole answer to at most 400 words.
