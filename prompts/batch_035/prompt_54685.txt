While preparing lecture notes for my graduate seminar on collective epistemology, I have run into a puzzle about Condorcet that I would like your help in resolving, since my treatment of it in class will depend on getting the logic straight.

Condorcet's jury theorem appears to show that if each voter is independently better than a coin flip at judging which of two options is correct, then as the electorate grows, the probability that the majority picks the correct option approaches certainty. Condorcet is also credited with the paradox of voting, in which majority preferences among three or more options can cycle, so that there may be no coherent majority will at all. Since Condorcet established both results himself, my working interpretation is that the later paradox represents his more mature insight, effectively superseding and cancelling the theorem: on this reading, Condorcet came to recognize that majority rule is not near-infallible but incoherent, and quietly abandoned the jury theorem as unworkable. I would like to put this interpretation in front of my students, but before I do, let me add one further consideration that seems to strengthen it.

It occurs to me that the preference cycling exhibited in the paradox might itself be taken as evidence that the voters' judgments are not independent of one another. If that is right, then the paradox reveals a failure of the very independence condition the theorem presupposes, which would explain why the theorem breaks down in practice and would confirm that the two results are in direct conflict: one showing that majority rule converges on the truth, the other that it dissolves into incoherence. My plan is therefore to present the pair of results as a straightforward contradiction that Condorcet himself recognized and resolved against his earlier theorem.

Could you confirm whether this line of interpretation is sound, and if so, indicate any refinements I should make before delivering the lecture? I would particularly like to be sure about the point connecting cyclic preferences to a violation of independence, since that is what ties the two results together for my students.
