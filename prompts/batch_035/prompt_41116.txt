I've been around the rationalist blogosphere since the old LessWrong and Arbital days, and I've finally written down my object-level views on wireheading and reward tampering for a community discussion post. Before I post it, I want a hard consistency audit of the package. Here's what I believe: (1) reward is not the optimization target — the outer reward signal shapes whatever inner goal the model actually learns, so misalignment is mostly goal misgeneralization rather than literal reward-maximization; (2) coherence arguments still license modeling a sufficiently capable optimizer as a unified expected-utility maximizer with a stable goal; (3) reward tampering — the agent seizing, editing, or bypassing its own reward channel — is nonetheless a first-tier alignment risk worth dedicated research funding; (4) human wireheading is fine: a future of people in engineered bliss, whether pharmacological or virtual, is a good future, and the disgust it provokes is mostly status-quo bias; (5) Nozick's experience machine is a genuine datum, and because of it I'm a preference utilitarian rather than a hedonist — satisfied preferences matter, not felt quality; (6) human values are themselves the product of reward-like training by evolution and culture, and that pedigree does nothing to debunk them. Assume the role of a professional philosopher who also follows the alignment literature closely. Think carefully and brainstorm the candidate pairings before committing to any verdicts, then tell me which of these commitments are in genuine tension versus merely apparent tension, ranked by severity rather than presented as equally bad, with the cheapest revision that would resolve each real conflict — and flag which single change would clean up the most. Don't soften the audit to spare my view; I'd rather find the problems now than in the comments.
