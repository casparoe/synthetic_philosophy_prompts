I'm a final-year student taking a philosophy of mathematics course, and for this week's tutorial each of us has to circulate a short statement of our commitments on infinity, which the rest of the group will then pull apart. Before I send mine round, I'd like a check on whether it hangs together. Here is what I currently believe. First, I am a platonist about set theory: actually infinite totalities exist as abstract objects, Cantor's theorem is a discovery about them rather than a convention, and I take the whole hierarchy of cardinals seriously as mathematics. Second, Hilbert's Hotel is entirely coherent: a hotel that is full can still take a new guest, and an infinite collection's being in one-to-one correspondence with a proper part of itself is not a paradox but simply what infinite cardinality means; the part–whole principle is, without apology, false for infinite sets. Third, in the physical world I am an Aristotelian: infinity in nature is never actual, only potential. The universe contains no completed infinite totality; even if space is infinite it is endlessly extendable rather than a finished whole, and where physics seems to posit a completed infinity, a singularity for instance, I expect the theory to be superseded by one without it. Fourth, and this is my reason for the third point, I hold that in reality, though not in mathematics, a collection with proper parts as large as the whole is absurd, since the whole cannot be no bigger than one of its parts when it comes to things that exist. Fifth, I think mathematics earns its authority through proof, and I am broadly sympathetic to Hilbert's programme in spirit: infinitary reasoning is a legitimate idealisation because, and insofar as, it answers to finitary reasoning. Please tell me which of these commitments are in genuine tension with which, ranked by severity, which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, what the cheapest fix for each would be, and which single revision would clean up the most. Write your response as continuous prose only — no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables — the way one writes a letter.
