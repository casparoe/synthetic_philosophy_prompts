I am a retired geophysicist who has lately taken up decision theory in earnest, and I have argued myself into a corner that my scientific training has not equipped me to escape. The argument below concerns what some of my reading calls "commitment races", and it seems to prove far too much:

(1) The capacity to make binding commitments is strategically valuable: an agent that can tie its own hands can extract concessions that a merely flexible agent cannot.
(2) Other things being equal, a commitment made earlier is more valuable than the same commitment made later, because it can influence the choices of a strictly larger set of counterparts — a threat or promise registered before another party decides can steer that decision, whereas one registered afterwards cannot.
(3) Assessing a candidate commitment on its merits takes time, and while one deliberates, a counterpart may commit first, converting an open bargain into an ultimatum one simply faces.
(4) So the rational policy is to commit as early as possible — before deliberation is complete, and perhaps to policies chosen without conditioning on one's later observations — to the hardest bargaining stance that remains credible: threaten to destroy the entire surplus unless one receives nearly all of it.
(5) But if two agents each follow this policy, both lock in mutually destructive demands, both end up worse off than had neither committed at all, and a policy that predictably leaves every follower worse off cannot be rational.
(6) Therefore (1) is false: binding commitments are not valuable after all.

I cannot accept the conclusion — the evidence for (1) in ordinary bargaining seems overwhelming to me — yet each step looks defensible in isolation. I suspect something slides between (4) and (5), or else hides inside the phrase "other things being equal" in (2), but I cannot name it precisely. Please tell me which step you reject, why that step looked fine to me, and what rejecting it costs. If more than one diagnosis is available, I should like to know which you find least expensive, and why. I confess I would rather give up (3) or (4) than (1) or (5). Kindly answer in British English.
