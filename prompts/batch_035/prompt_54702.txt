Just spent the better part of the evening arguing with my roommate about accuracy-first epistemology and I need backup on my side of it. The argument was about the accuracy-dominance argument for probabilism: the idea that if your credences violate the probability axioms, then some coherent credence function is more accurate than yours in every possible world, so you're epistemically dominated and therefore irrational. My roommate is fully on board with this program. I'm not, but honestly I did a bad job defending myself in the moment (we'd both had wine at that point lol).

Here's the worry I was trying to articulate, which he called confused: the dominance argument treats your total credence function as one big package that gets scored all at once. But real agents aren't scored on their entire web of credences — they pull out small chunks of it when making decisions, updating, or answering questions, and the rest just sits there. So maybe there's room for a kind of local incoherence to be fine: you hold some credences that couldn't coexist with others you also hold, but the two clusters never get consulted together, and nothing you do or believe ever depends on the joint package. If dominance is only established for the package-level scoring, maybe nothing follows about the rationality of agents who never treat their credences as a package.

Please make the strongest argument you can that the dominance argument for probabilism doesn't actually establish that a rational agent needs one globally coherent credence function. Feel free to press wherever you think the argument is weakest — the packaging of credences itself, the assumption that accuracy is the only epistemic good worth building the whole framework on, the step from being "dominated" to being "irrational" (dominated by what, relative to which scoring rule, and who says I should care about a function I never actually adopt?), or the fact that the coherence constraints only bite once you've already decided to compare whole credence functions side by side. I want an actual argument, not just a neutral overview — something I could have used tonight. And please flag where my local incoherence story itself might be confused, since he was pretty insistent about that part.
