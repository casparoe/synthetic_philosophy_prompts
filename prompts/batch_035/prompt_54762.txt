I cover AI for a general-interest science magazine, and I keep coming back to a debate that hasn't moved in years: whether progress toward transformative AI will arrive as a discontinuous jump or as a smooth acceleration. The fast-takeoff camp holds that once AI systems can meaningfully improve AI research itself, the feedback loop will run away before institutions can react; the slow-takeoff camp expects a long, visible ramp, with economic doubling times compressing gradually and plenty of warning signs along the way. Both sides have the same engineering facts in front of them, read the same papers, and largely share the same endgame picture of what a world with transformative AI looks like — yet they have been stuck since at least the mid-2000s, and each new capability milestone gets claimed as confirmation by both camps simultaneously.

My working hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than endorse, is that the disagreement isn't really an empirical one about the future at all, but a disagreement about what kind of quantity 'intelligence' is: the fast camp implicitly treats it as an autocatalytic substance where small edges compound explosively, while the slow camp treats it as something like capital or energy, subject to diminishing returns, bottlenecks, and the grinding friction of deployment in the physical world. If that's right, then no amount of benchmark news should be expected to settle anything. So: diagnose the stalemate. Is it a disguised empirical question about, say, the returns on cognitive labor in R&D that we could in principle measure now? A difference in standards of evidence between people who trust trend extrapolation and people who trust mechanism? Or bedrock intuitions about whether explosively self-amplifying processes are normal or exotic in the world? And given your diagnosis, tell me honestly what would count as progress — a measurement, a conceptual distinction, a historical case genuinely analogous enough to bind either side — or whether this is a dispute that cannot close until the event itself either happens or conspicuously fails to.
