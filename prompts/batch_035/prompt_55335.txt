For an upcoming colloquium I've been asked to write a short position statement on higher-order evidence and epistemic akrasia, and before I commit it to prose I want an audit of whether the package I've held for years actually hangs together. The commitments are these:

(1) The enkratic requirement is exceptionless: it is never rational to believe p while also believing that, on one's total evidence, one is not warranted in believing p.

(2) Higher-order evidence is genuinely defeating. If I learn that the process by which I formed a belief was biased, or that an epistemic peer I have no antecedent reason to distrust disagrees with me, I should lower my confidence, even where my first-order case, taken on its own, is exactly as strong as it was before.

(3) There is no standing obligation to audit one's own reasoning: absent specific evidence of malfunction, relying on my own judgment requires no further justification.

(4) In the self-undermining cases — where the higher-order evidence, taken at face value, would impugn the very reasoning I would need in order to apply it, as when I am told that my reasoning about reasoning of just this type is unreliable — the right response is to discount the defeater rather than to divide my trust evenly between it and my first-order judgment.

(5) In peer disagreement I owe the peer some deference but not equal deference: I may give my own first-order assessment somewhat greater weight on the strength of my own view that my case is the stronger one, and this is not automatically question-begging.

I have stated these as I hold them, without marking any as more secure than the others. Please go through them pairwise: tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be, rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them flatly, and say which single revision would relieve the most pressure. I want the discrimination, not a catalogue — flagging all ten pairs would tell me nothing.
