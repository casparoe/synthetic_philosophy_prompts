I'm a third-year PhD student putting together the position chapter for my dissertation prospectus on persistence, and before my committee sees it I want an external consistency check, because a colleague in our metaphysics reading group has started claiming that my package is unstable — indeed that parts of it are not merely in tension but jointly incoherent. Rather than defend myself piecemeal in seminar, I'd like a systematic audit of which of my commitments actually pull against each other, which only appear to, and what it would cost to restore coherence.

Here is the package. (1) I am an endurantist about substances: a persisting object is wholly present at every time at which it exists, and ordinary objects have no temporal parts. (2) I am an eternalist about time and broadly B-theoretic: all times are equally real, there is no privileged present, and I take there to be no deep methodological barrier to reasoning from the spatial to the temporal case, since on my view the manifold's dimensions do not differ in kind. (3) I hold that nothing can be wholly located at two disjoint spatial regions — a principle I deploy in my second-year paper against Aristotelian immanent universals, which would have to be wholly present in each of their many instances. (4) I take Lewis's problem of temporary intrinsics seriously, and my official endurantist response is the tensed one: an object's being bent is expressed by ordinary tensed predication, not by its standing in a bent-at relation to a time, and I take this to preserve the intrinsicness of shape. (5) I am a category pluralist: events and processes persist by having temporal parts, while substances endure; I see no pressure for a single account of persistence across categories. (6) In a symmetric fission case, where a person's career branches into two intrinsically identical descendants, I hold that exactly one descendant is identical to the original — perhaps unknowably which — and that identity is all-or-nothing rather than degreed. (7) I reject primitive thisnesses: all facts, including facts about identity over time, supervene on the qualitative character of the world. Two constraints on the audit: eternalism is not up for revision, and I want you to evaluate my colleague's specific charge that endurantism and eternalism cannot be held together without a privileged present, rather than assuming it.

Please structure the response as follows. A section titled "Summary verdict" (~100 words) stating, in a few sentences, the overall state of the package. Then "Real tensions, ranked" (~500 words), ordering the genuine tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad, and for each saying precisely which commitments are involved, what the incompatibility is, what would have to give, and what the revision would cost me elsewhere in the set — for instance, if you tell me a principle of mine must be restricted or abandoned, show me what happens to its other uses. Then "Apparent tensions" (~200 words), covering pairs that look problematic but dissolve on inspection, with the dissolution. Finally, "Recommended repair" (~150 words), identifying the single cheapest revision or repair strategy and why it beats the alternatives. Treat my commitments as data to be audited, not as invitations to survey the persistence literature.
