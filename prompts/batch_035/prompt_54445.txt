So this came up at work of all places. I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background whatsoever, but we recently had a postmortem for an outage where the "root cause" line literally said the on-call engineer didn't page the secondary team. That's it — the bad thing was something somebody *didn't do*. Half the room said you obviously can't cause anything by not doing something, the other half said of course you can, that's why we have on-call rotations, and it turned into a whole thing. Now I can't stop thinking about it.

To get a handle on it I've been running cases past my friend Priya, who has the strongest gut reactions of anyone I know on this stuff. Her verdicts so far, on whether the person's omission *caused* the bad outcome: I hire a gardener, go on vacation for two weeks, he just never waters the plants and they die — she says he caused their death, instant answer. My roommate promises to let my dog out while I'm at work, gets sucked into a game, forgets, and the dog ends up sick — caused again, also fast. But a neighbor two doors down who was travelling the whole time and never even knew the plants existed — not caused, obviously. And a passerby who can't swim, watching a stranger drown from the bank and doing nothing — not caused. She didn't hesitate on any of the four, which is what makes me think there's some rule running under the hood.
