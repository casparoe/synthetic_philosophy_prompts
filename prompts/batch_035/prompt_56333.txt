i teach a third-year seminar on philosophy of mind with a psych bent — about 16 students, one 90-minute session. Design an in-class activity where they feel first-hand that introspection isn't just fallible in the boring way (occasional mistakes), but that its errors are invisible from the inside: you introspect on why you judged or chose something, it feels completely transparent, and the real cause was something else entirely. Crucially I don't want a demo where I reveal a trick at the end — the students should design and run small pairwise experiments on each other (real choices with actual stakes in the room, some subtle manipulation of the choice setup they invent themselves), so they get hit with the confabulation finding from both sides, as experimenter and subject. The part I care most about: a second round where, after finding out they confabulated, they're asked to introspect on whether the confabulating had felt any different from genuine self-knowledge at the time — does introspection ever notice its own failures? The debrief should map specific moments in the activity onto the distinction between introspective confidence and introspective access, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which features of real confabulation findings a 90-minute classroom version just cant reproduce no matter how well it's run.
