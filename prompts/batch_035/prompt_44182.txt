I've been in a moral luck seminar for two semesters now and the same thing happens every time: everyone accepts Nagel's point that the drunk driver who kills someone and the one who gets home fine differ in how blameworthy they feel and how they're treated, everyone accepts that something like "you're only responsible for what's in your control" is a real principle, and then we just... stall. Nobody has ever changed sides. My pet theory is that the two camps aren't actually disagreeing — the anti-luck people are doing ideal theory about what a perfectly rational blamer should respond to, and the pro-luck people are describing blame as it's actually practiced and asking whether it's a mess we should keep anyway. But then there's the Gauguin stuff from Williams, where the success of the painting somehow seems to bear on whether abandoning his family was justified, and that seems like a genuinely different animal from the driver cases, so maybe "moral luck" is covering two debates that only look like one. Am I onto anything here? Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, hidden factual question, bedrock intuitions about desert that nobody can argue for, whatever you think — and tell me what would even count as progress: a distinction, some test cases, a change in the question, or nothing and why.
