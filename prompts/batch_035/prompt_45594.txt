Is there an established name for the following phenomenon, and if not, can you coin one? In reading about the end of the Classic Maya, the Late Bronze Age palatial system, and Easter Island, I keep finding that the verdict "collapse" depends on which continuities you take to constitute the society. Track the Maya royal courts, the monument dedications, and the long-distance exchange in jade and obsidian, and the southern lowlands collapse in the ninth century; track the villages in the same valleys, and they mostly keep farming the same land in much the same way, speaking the same language, under new elites. Easter Island reads as ecological suicide if you track the palm forest and the statue cult, and as adaptation to a badly degraded island if you track the people, who were still there, in the thousands, when Europeans arrived. I am not after declinism, which is a bias of the observer, and I am not asking about resilience theory; what I want named is the structural fact that the verdict is fixed not by the society's fate but by the choice of what to count as the society — a choice usually made silently, and often dictated by the evidence itself, since the institutions that perished are exactly the ones that wrote, while the ones that persisted left little for us to read. Does this dependence of a verdict like "collapse" on the choice of unit and of tracked continuities have a name, or anything close to one? If the nearest concepts each capture only part of it, tell me which part each captures and where each fails. If nothing established fits, coin and define a term, give boundary cases — a change thorough enough that "collapse" holds on any tracking, and a change that is "collapse" on exactly one — and make clear how your term differs from the ordinary evaluative question of whether a change was bad.
