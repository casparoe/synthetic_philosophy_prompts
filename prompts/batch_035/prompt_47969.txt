I spent my working life in geophysics and have taken up philosophy only since retiring, so I ask for your patience with a problem from my reading group that I cannot resolve. The topic was the ethics of founding a settlement on Mars, and one member argued as follows:

(1) It is wrong to conceive a child under conditions foreseeably much worse than those in which one could, at that very time and without delay, have conceived that same child. (2) Children born in an early Mars settlement — confined habitats, chronic radiation exposure, bone and muscle wasting in partial gravity, no open wilderness — will foreseeably be much worse off than those same children would have been had their parents remained on Earth, since remaining was within the parents' power. (3) A settlement endures only if its inhabitants raise children there, and a project whose execution necessarily involves wrongdoing of this kind is itself impermissible. (4) Therefore founding a Mars settlement is impermissible.

I do not accept the conclusion, but I cannot find the flaw, and what most unsettles me is that (1) appears to prove far too much. By (1), it would be wrong to conceive in a damp dwelling when a dry one could be had the same month, wrong to conceive in a malarial district when removal was affordable, wrong to conceive almost anywhere short of the best circumstances available at the time — which is to say, wrong nearly always. That is absurd, and I say this as someone whose parents began their family in circumstances no planner would have chosen.

So something must give. I would be glad to keep (1) in some weakened form — it seems to capture something real about parental responsibility — but I do not know what such a weakening would cost, or whether it would even rescue the settlement, since if colony life falls below whatever threshold one substitutes, the conclusion may return by the back door. Please tell me which step to reject, why that step looked secure, and what rejecting it costs in cases far from Mars. I should also like to know what would change your verdict: which consideration, if it obtained, would lead you to a different diagnosis, and where your own reasoning is most likely to fail. Please write your answer in British English.
