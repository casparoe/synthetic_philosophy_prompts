I want a probability estimate on Humeanism about the laws of nature, with the reasoning laid out term by term. Take "Humeanism" here as the conjunction of two claims: (i) everything there is supervenes on the spatiotemporal distribution of local, intrinsic, non-nomic properties — the Humean mosaic — with no holistic or modal structure in the supervenience base; and (ii) the laws of nature are nothing over and above the theorems of whichever deductive system best balances simplicity, strength, and fit in the competition to axiomatize the mosaic. Anti-Humeanism, by contrast, holds that laws genuinely govern or constrain the mosaic: they are, or involve, something modal over and above the totality of particular facts — Armstrong–Tooley–Dretske-style necessitation relations between universals, or Lange-style subjunctive necessities stronger than the facts they subsume.

My credence in the conjunction is about 0.65. A colleague who works on the metaphysics of physics puts hers at 0.2, mostly because she thinks quantum theory already falsifies clause (i): entanglement and the apparent holism of the wavefunction, she says, leave no clean mosaic of local qualities for the laws to summarize. Give me your own number, but the number is the least important part. What I want is the decomposition. At minimum, assign a probability and a one- or two-line justification to each of:

1. P(the supervenience base is Humean) — that physics, at bottom, turns out to be a mosaic of local qualities rather than requiring irreducibly holistic or modal structure in the base itself.
2. P(the best-system analysis is the right reduction, given a Humean base) — that among competing reductions of lawhood to the mosaic, the competition-based account beats alternatives like regularity theories or looser "systematization" talk.
3. P(governing is a coherent idea) — that anti-Humeanism is a genuine metaphysical alternative rather than a confusion about what "govern" could mean if it does not mean "is entailed by the best summary"; your estimate of the anti-Humean's real estate depends on this.
4. Any further terms you think belong — for instance, whether the competition among systems requires a non-arbitrary, objective weighting of simplicity, strength, and fit, and whether that weighting can itself be Humean.

Two hard cases should be weighed explicitly in your reasoning, because I suspect the whole thing turns on them.

First, the exotic-values problem. Lewis conceded that beings with alien standards of simplicity — who prize, say, syntactic economy far above strength — might systematize our very mosaic into laws radically unlike ours. If the best-system analysis lets the laws depend on facts about what counts as a good system, it looks like the Humean has smuggled in a substantive, possibly non-Humean fact about system virtue. How much does the need for a principled answer here lower your credence in clause (ii)?

Second, a case I have been chewing on that I have not seen treated directly. Consider a universe whose mosaic is exhausted by a single, extremely brief episode: a handful of particles interacting once, then nothing, forever. On the best-system analysis, the laws of that universe are fixed by the competition among systems over that tiny mosaic. But over such a sparse set of facts, almost any regularity the particles happen to exhibit could be enshrined as law by a trivially simple axiomatization — the apparatus of adjudicating between systems seems to do no work at all, and it is murky whether the Humean can say what the laws of such a universe are, or whether it has any. The anti-Humean appears to have an easy answer: the laws are whichever necessitating relations or subjunctive necessities actually obtain, no matter how impoverished the facts. Does the Humean have a principled response — and if the account goes silent precisely where the mosaic is thin, how much should that infect your confidence about the laws of our own, far richer mosaic, where the competition does have material to work with?
