My philosophy of mind reading group has been stuck on the hard problem for two months, and honestly the standard cases have worn out their welcome -- once you've done zombies and Mary to death, everyone just performs their assigned intuitions. So I want a thought experiment built to order that attacks the usual suspects from the *opposite* direction. Zombie cases pressure functionalism by having all the function with none of the phenomenology. What I want is a case where the function is largely absent but consciousness is clearly -- almost embarrassingly -- present. Here are the specs: the scenario should be clinical and realistic, no mind-reading tech, no perfect scanners, no demons. Base it on the real-world situation of covert consciousness in brain-injured patients (sometimes called cognitive-motor dissociation): a patient who at the bedside meets every behavioral criterion for an unresponsive/vegetative state, but who, over many repeated sessions of the imagine-playing-tennis style fMRI or EEG command-following paradigms, produces reliable above-chance responses -- probabilistic, statistically messy evidence of the kind clinicians actually deal with, not a clean readout of an inner life. The case must be built so that (i) a functionalist who ties consciousness to report, global availability, or rational access is under real pressure to either deny the patient is conscious or amend the theory, (ii) a higher-order theorist faces a genuinely interesting choice rather than an easy answer, (iii) an illusionist has a distinctive line available -- roughly that our confident intuition of hidden experience is itself part of what the theory says gets constructed -- and (iv) the pre-theoretic intuition remains "obviously conscious" for any normal reader. One hard constraint: the case may not simply stipulate that the patient is conscious. The consciousness verdict has to be earned by the evidence structure, the way it is at an actual bedside -- if you could get the same verdict just by writing "and she was conscious the whole time" into the scenario, you've cheated.

After the scenario, do the walkthrough: state what each of the three theories says about the case and which of their commitments forces that verdict, identify which structural feature of the case is doing the work (my guess is it's the dissociation between phenomenology and access/behavior, but tell me if I'm wrong), and say what happens to the case if that feature is removed -- e.g., if you hand the patient back a reliable communication channel, does it just collapse into ordinary locked-in syndrome and stop being interesting? Then give the strongest countermove for whichever theory comes out worst: how would a defender of it attack the inference from command-following data to experience, and would that attack generalize so far that it implausibly convicts ordinary cases of report-based ascription too? I also want an honest note on whether any part of the case smuggles in the verdict rather than generating it -- tell me where it's most likely to fail the specs, since we plan to actually run the case past a friendly functionalist at our next meeting and I'd rather know the weak point in advance than get it pointed out in front of everyone.

Throughout, please flag how confident you are in each of the verdict-attributions -- I care less about the scenario itself than about whether you've got the theorists' commitments right, since a case is only as good as the theory-readings it generates. And single out the single point you're least sure of, whether that's a theory attribution or a claim about the clinical setup. Once the prose walkthrough is done, finish with a short JSON object in this schema: {"scenario": one-paragraph summary, "verdicts": array of objects each with "theory", "verdict", "commitment_invoked" (which specific commitment of the theory forces that verdict), "confidence" (high/medium/low plus a clause why), "load_bearing_feature": the feature doing the work and what happens if it's removed, "best_counter_move": the strongest attack from the theorist who comes out worst, "least_sure_point": the specific place you think the case might fail the specs}. Keep the JSON brief -- it's a summary of the prose, not a replacement for it. And one request on tone: dont sand the case down into something so hedged it pressures nobody -- if the specs are only jointly satisfiable with a slight fudge somewhere, do the fudge and tell me exactly where, rather than quietly weakening the case.
