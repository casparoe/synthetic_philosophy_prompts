I've been trying to work out my actual views on what should and shouldn't be for sale, by listing cases and noting my gut verdicts, and I'd like to know whether a single principle captures them. A poor man selling a kidney to pay for his daughter's surgery: should be allowed. A broker arranging that same sale and keeping most of the fee: should be banned, though I can't say what changed other than the middleman's cut. Selling one's vote in an election: clearly impermissible. A billionaire spending millions on ads to influence that same election: permissible. Gouging the price of generators after a hurricane: wrong. Reselling concert tickets far above face value: fine, though I admit the structure looks similar — pay extra or do without. Paying blood donors a modest sum: fine. Paying college students to donate plasma twice a week: makes me uneasy in a way the blood case doesn't. A university admitting a mediocre applicant because his family donated a building: distasteful but tolerable, apparently, while selling the same seat outright for cash is corruption — these two verdicts I hold firmly and cannot reconcile. A life insurance policy taken out on a stranger, with the insurer profiting from betting on when I'll die: fine. Offering drug addicts cash incentives to use long-term contraception: this is the one I keep going back and forth on, and I'd like the principle to settle it rather than dodge it. So: is there one principle, or a small set of candidates, that delivers most of these verdicts? State it precisely, tell me which clause each case motivates, say what it implies for a case I haven't listed — say, paying trial subjects progressively more for riskier drug trials, or auctioning citizenship — and be honest about which of my verdicts the best principle simply cannot accommodate, so I know what I have to give up. Please answer in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, written the way you'd write a long letter.
