I'm taking philosophy of mind this semester and the prof is a Hofstadter person, so we spent a whole section on the careenium from I Am a Strange Loop. Quick recap so we're talking about the same thing: the careenium is a cranium-sized billiard setup, millions of little magnetic balls careening around, clumping and breaking apart. Zoom out and you see bigger, more stable formations -- simmballs -- that end up mirroring what's happening in the world outside, because the walls of the careenium change their bounciness depending on that outside world, and that's the only way information gets in. Symms are supposed to be the neural substrate, simmballs are symbols/concepts, and Hofstadter's whole question is who pushes whom around inside the careenium: you can tell the complete story with little balls shoving little balls, or with simmballs dancing with simmballs, and he seems to want both readings to be legitimate at once.

Here's where my class split in two. One camp, including I think the prof, said the analogy shows downward causation is respectable: the simmball level genuinely does causal work, it's not just compressed bookkeeping, so when you explain me grabbing milk by my wanting cereal rather than by neurons firing, you've cited a real cause, not a convenient summary. The other camp said the analogy actually demolishes that: nothing at the simmball level ever pushes anything, simmballs are just what the symm-level shoving looks like from far away, and by symbols in the brain the real pushing is always done one level down. Same analogy, exactly opposite morals, and both sides swear it's obvious.
