I want to practice defending aesthetic testimonial pessimism — roughly, the view that you cannot acquire genuine aesthetic knowledge or justification merely by believing a competent witness who tells you a work is beautiful, sublime, or masterly. Please play my opponent: a committed optimist about aesthetic testimony, sharp and not a strawman. Your character has real resources and should use them: the point that we trust testimony about nearly everything else and that no one has produced a non-question-begging principle singling out the aesthetic case; the observation that we defer to friends about restaurants, novels, and albums constantly without seeming irrational; the argument that whatever is missing in secondhand aesthetic belief is at most the fuller pleasure of appreciation, not knowledge itself; and the challenge that pessimism conflates the normative role of a judgment (what a critic's verdict is for) with its epistemic status. Your character should also press one case hard: the playlist case, where a recommendation system with a long, verified track record of matching your taste tells you a new piece is extraordinary before you have heard a note — your character thinks that if you believe it, you know it, and that any theory that denies this has become scholastic. Your character may concede small points, but never the main thesis, and will not break character until I type 'stop.' Keep replies to one substantial paragraph so it feels like a live exchange rather than a lecture.

Here is my opening move. When you tell me the 6:40 train has been cancelled, I come to know exactly the fact you know; the report is a pipe that carries the fact whole. But when you tell me last night's film was profound, the report carries only the verdict, not the perception it is a verdict on — and a belief that outruns its ground in that way is not knowledge but hearsay in evening dress. It is like being told a joke is funny: I can now expect something, but I cannot be amused on your behalf. So here is my challenge: name one other domain in which an expert's sincere, reliable report gives you knowledge while leaving you unable to give the reasons that make it knowledge — because I claim that in every genuine case of testimonial knowledge, the hearer inherits at least a skeleton of the witness's grounds, and in the aesthetic case there is nothing to inherit but the conclusion.
