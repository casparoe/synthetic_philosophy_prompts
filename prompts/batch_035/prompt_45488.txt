ok so I'm supposed to write the position statement for an edited volume on belief — fifteen pages, due in a month, and the editors explicitly want a line, not a survey. Before I commit it to print I want to know whether the package I've drifted into over the years actually hangs together, because I've never written it all down in one place. Here it is, more or less:

(1) Belief doesn't require certainty. It's fine — usually required, even — to believe things you couldn't prove or know, and belief itself is all-or-nothing: no half-beliefs, you either believe p or you don't.
(2) I do not believe my lottery ticket will lose. I'd bet on it, I'd act on it, but I flat out don't believe it. Belief needs a non-accidental connection to how things actually are, and bare odds can't get you that — knowing the draw is a million-to-one against me is exactly the kind of thing that could too easily have gone the other way.
(3) I do believe each individual claim in my book. I checked every one of them, most against primary sources, they're in print without a hedge, and I'd assert any of them again tommorrow.
(4) I also believe, and say so in the preface, that at least one claim in the book is false. That's the responsible thing for an author to believe; books like mine always contain at least one error, and I'd bet heavily on it.
(5) Belief is closed under conjunction, at least once you've actually considered the conjunction: someone who believes p, believes q, and has turned over p-and-q can't rationally withhold on p-and-q.

Some of these I've held for decades, some are newer, and at this point I honestly can't tell whether I have a view or just a pile of case verdicts. My instinct says the lottery case and the preface case are different in kind and not merely in degree, but every time I try to write down what the difference is it comes out sounding like special pleading — which is a decent part of why im asking.

Go through the pairs for me: which are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and for each real one say how bad it is and what would have to give. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and what it costs me elsewhere — I don't want to fix whatever the formal problem is by lobotomizing the whole view, and I definitely don't want to find out from Reviewer 2.
