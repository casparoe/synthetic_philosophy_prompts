I've been reading LessWrong and adjacent blogs long enough to have opinions on Sleeping Beauty, calibration training, and the quantum-physics sequence, and I've concluded that my views on the interpretations of probability don't hang together. Rather than argue terminology again, I want to lay out my gut verdicts on concrete cases and ask what account of probability, if any, captures them all.

For a fair coin about to be flipped, I say the probability of heads is 1/2 as a fact about the tossing setup, not about anyone's ignorance — and I would keep saying this even if I learned the underlying physics is fully deterministic.

For the same coin already flipped and hidden under a cup, I still say 1/2, but here it seems obvious that the number is a fact about my ignorance; the chance part is over.

For a particular radium atom decaying within the next hour, I think there is a real physical probability that a demon who knew the entire microstate of the universe could not improve upon — a difference in kind, not degree, from the deterministic coin.

For the googolth digit of pi, I would cheerfully bet at even odds on even, and assigning myself probability 1/2 seems fine, but I balk at saying there is any fact of the matter about the probability, in the way there plainly is about the digit itself.

For an upcoming election, subjective probabilities seem exactly the right tool — my posterior, the prediction-market price, whatever — and two people with different numbers can both be behaving perfectly rationally. Yet I also think a forecaster whose numbers are well calibrated across many elections is getting something more right than one whose numbers aren't, which seems to require probabilities answering to something beyond whose beliefs they are.

For a die I know is loaded by some unknown amount, I am simultaneously tempted to say the probability of rolling a six is 1/6 by symmetry of my ignorance, and tempted to say there is a single unknown physical propensity such that no number I assign deserves to be called the probability.

My tentative principle is roughly: probabilities are degrees of belief, constrained to match objective chances when chances exist. But this tells me nothing about the loaded die, and I can't decide whether the cup coin's 1/2 counts as matching a chance that no longer exists or as a free-floating belief.

Is there a single interpretation — frequentist, subjective, propensity, or some principled hybrid with a stated bridge between them — that delivers all of these verdicts? Please state the principle (or shortlist of candidates) precisely, say which clause each of my cases motivates, and be explicit about which of my verdicts you would overturn and why. Then apply the surviving account to the two cases I keep going in circles on: first, if Everettian many-worlds is true and both outcomes of a quantum measurement occur, what is the 1/2-probability of one of them even about — it doesn't seem to be ignorance, since I know both happen, and it doesn't seem to be a propensity, since nothing is being disposed toward either outcome. Second, what credence should Sleeping Beauty have in heads when she wakes during the experiment, and does your account of probability alone settle it, or does it need extra machinery? Finally, tell me the verdict your account gives on one further case I haven't mentioned, so I can check it against my own gut: a prediction-market price that everyone knows is dominated by traders who will not, or cannot, update on new information.
