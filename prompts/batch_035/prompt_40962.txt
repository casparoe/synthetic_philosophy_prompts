Draft the joint statement that would emerge from an adversarial collaboration between two philosophers who have argued for years: a deontologist who holds that the doing/allowing distinction has intrinsic moral significance — that killing is worse than letting die even when outcomes and motives are identical — and a consequentialist who holds that the distinction has at most rule-of-thuth status [fix: "rule-of-thumb status"], its intuitive pull explainable by status quo bias, omission bias, and conflation with ancillary factors (the effort and cost of intervening, responsibility for the victim's antecedent peril, the reliability of intervention). The document should contain: a numbered section of claims both can sign, including about the canonical cases where their intuitions converge despite their theories, about which ancillary factors genuinely matter to both sides, and about the difference between the broad doing/allowing distinction and the narrower killing/letting-die distinction, which may not stand or fall together; a disagreements table stating each residual disagreement in wording that the side holding that view would accept, flagging any portion of the dispute the parties discovered to be merely verbal (for example, whether "killing is worse" was being used to claim pro tanto wrongness or all-things-considered wrongness); and a statement of the cruxes — among them whether any non-question-begging analysis of the distinction survives the counterexamples each side has raised (initiating a new causal path versus redirecting an existing threat; harm that runs through the agent's own body versus through an external instrument; withdrawing a benefit one was already providing versus never conferring it; using up a victim's resources versus failing to add to them), and whether the intuitions favoring the distinction would survive the removal of the ancillary factors. For each crux, specify what both agree would bear on it — including novel probe cases constructed to cross-cut the candidate analyses (a case where the agent redirects a threat through her own body, a case where an omission removes something the agent's earlier action supplied) — with each side's prediction, recorded in advance, about how thoughtful reasoners with no prior stance on the debate would classify the cases and about which counterexamples will strike the other side as decisive.
