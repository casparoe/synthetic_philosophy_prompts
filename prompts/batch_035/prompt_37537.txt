So for the last two years my brother-in-law Dev (finishing a PhD in, roughly, origin-of-life chemistry) and my old college roommate Marta (paleontology background, professionally cranky about optimism) have been having the same fight every time we're all at a family thing: is the universe full of life or is Earth basically a one-off? Dev's side: life showed up here almost as soon as the surface stopped being molten and getting bombarded, extremophiles live in basically every horrible place we look, the building blocks are all over meteorites and comets, and there are something like a billion trillion stars, so even a ridiculously improbable chemistry event happens over and over. Marta's side: we have a sample size of one and no idea how many dice were actually being rolled, the jump from "interesting chemistry" to "cells with heredity" has never been shown to be anything but a fluke, it then took something like two billion years to get from boring little cells to anything complicated (and that may have needed a one-time merger event), and meanwhile the galaxy is silent, which at least *prima facie* smells like an absence of technological civilizations. Last month they finally got fustrated enough with talking past each other that they agreed to do a proper adversarial collaboration, and because I'm the only person they both trust not to slant it, I've been voluntold to draft the joint statement before our next dinner, and they'll each mark it up.

Here's what I need the draft to do. First, a numbered section of claims both could actually sign — about the organic chemistry, about what a sample of one can and can't support, about how much the Fermi silence really tells us given how hard interstellar detection is, and about what a biosignature detection on an exoplanet (say, oxygen plus methane in the same atmosphere) would and wouldn't settle, since Dev thinks abiotic false positives are a cop-out and Marta thinks they're half the story. Then a table of the remaining disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept — I'm thinking the big ones are whether the early appearance of life on Earth is evidence that the origin is easy or whether it's contaminated by selection effects (Marta keeps saying we'd find ourselves on the lucky fast planet no matter what the odds were, and Dev says that argument proves too much). Then for each crux, some concrete thing both agree would bear on it — Mars sample return, plume chemistry at Enceladus, the first decent survey of temperate rocky planet atmospheres, whatever — with each of their predictions written down in advance so nobody can retro-fit later. And if you think part of their fight is actually just verbal — like whether "the universe is full of life" means microbes vs. means technological civilizations, and they're each conveniently switching between the two whenever it helps — please call that out explicitly, because I suspect it's like a third of the argument honestly. I've seen examples of these collab writeups formatted as agreed-claims-then-table, so something like that, but the content is what matters, not the formatting. Keep it signed-by-both honest: no mushy "more research is needed" cop-outs in the agreed section, and no gotcha phrasings in the disagreements.
