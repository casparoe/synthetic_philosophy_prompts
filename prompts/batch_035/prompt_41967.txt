I want one essay question (90 minutes) comparing epistemicism and supervaluationism, but not the stock version where students recite "sharp cutoffs vs truth-value gaps" and call it a day. The crux I want to actually test is higher-order vagueness. The supervaluationist advertises that she has no sharp boundary between the definite cases and the borderline ones — that's her whole selling point — but once you ask about the boundary between "definitely P" and "definitely not definitely P", sharpness seems to sneak back in, and if you go vaguer at every order you end up with (arguably) infinitely many sharp boundaries of diminishing significance, which is somehow meant to be better than the epistemicist's single honest cutoff per predicate. Meanwhile the epistemicist, who never pretended otherwise, seems to take higher-order vagueness in stride: each "definitely" operator just gets its own unknowable cutoff. So the question should force students to judge which theory is really paying the higher-order bill, and whether Williamson-style margin-for-error principles about why the cutoffs are unknowable earn their keep or just restate the phenomenon in fancier vocabulary. Could you write the question, a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme totalling 20 marks that separates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain, plus a one-line note on how students typically botch each one?
