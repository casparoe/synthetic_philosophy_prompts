(3) There is no non-arbitrary fact of the matter about how to compare units of value or disvalue across moral theories: the magnitudes a utilitarian attaches to outcomes and the stringency a deontologist attaches to a constraint are not measured on a common scale, and any exchange rate between them is a convention we adopt rather than something we discover.

(4) I am roughly 85% confident in consequentialism; and I hold that when one's credence in a single theory is high enough, one may for practical purposes simply act on that theory, treating the residual credence as noise.

(5) A concrete verdict: a doctor who is 85% consequentialist, and who could discreetly kill one patient to save five, acts wrongly in doing so — a minority-credence theory's condemnation of a rights violation should not be washed out by majority-credence gains in aggregate welfare, however large those gains.
