I sit on the coverage committee of a regional health insurer, and our de facto policy is that we reimburse therapies and refuse enhancements — the difficulty being that after two years of votes, nobody on the committee can state the difference in a way that survives our own case files. Before we review another line-drawing proposal, I want the specification written down first. Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of the therapy/enhancement distinction has to deliver for the purpose of coverage decisions, with the motivating case behind each requirement. In particular, I want to see how the account must handle: vaccination and contraception, where we prevent a condition that has not yet occurred and yet everyone agrees we fund; growth hormone for a girl with Turner syndrome versus the same hormone for an equally short, healthy child; hearing aids and cochlear implants, where the treatment target is partly a mismatch between a body and a built environment; stimulants for a diagnosed ADHD patient versus the same pills taken off-label by a medical student the night before an exam; beta-blockers for angina versus beta-blockers for stage fright; and the cosmetic cases like LASIK and tooth alignment that our members write in about. The account also has to survive the generic slippery worry — that every intervention merely moves someone from one state to a better one, so therapy is just enhancement from a worse starting point — and it must not quietly rest on whatever is statistically common, since that would make the line shift with population health.

Two further things I most want to see. First, mark which desiderata are non-negotiable for a funding body as opposed to merely analytic preferences — for instance, whether the distinction genuinely has to do normative work (picking out interventions we owe people) or whether descriptive classification plus a separate fairness argument could suffice, and note if you suspect any pair of requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, so that the list itself is a result. Second, a very brief scoring of the obvious contenders against your list: a biostatistical account resting on species-typical functioning, a fair-opportunity account in the spirit of Norman Daniels, and the eliminativist view that the distinction does no justificatory work at all and coverage should be decided on other grounds. Tell me which single requirement knocks out the most candidates — my own guess is the vaccination cases are the graveyard, but I want to be shown rather than told. Keep the weight on the criteria and their cases; the scorecard can be terse, since the committee will do its own arguing.
