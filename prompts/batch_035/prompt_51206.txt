If you get to act after seeing a signal instead of before, your expected payoff weakly goes up. The proof is trivial: the informed you can always just ignore the signal and do what the uninformed you would have done, so having the option to react can only help. This is why rational agents should always gather more information before deciding, and why the expected value of running any experiment is non-negative. And since the theorem holds for any agent with any beliefs, as long as they act consistently, it's about as close to an a priori truth as decision theory gets.'
