I've been trying to figure out what my actual views on animals are by going with my gut on cases, and now I want to know if theres any principle behind them. Eating a hamburger: fine. Eating a dog: obviously not. Eating pork from a pig that lived outside with space to root around: fine, actually good, better than the factory farm stuff. Swatting a mosquito that might bite me: fine. Kicking a raccoon for fun: monstrous. Boiling a lobster alive: seems bad but I keep doing it at crawfish boils. Testing a new cancer drug on lab mice: fine, even good. Testing the same drug on chimps: not fine, use the mice. Feeding my cat meat: fine, cats need it. Feeding my cat meat vs feeding my dog meat when the dog could do fine on a vegetarian diet: no difference to me, both fine, though I guess the dog's meat is supporting the same industry I'm mostly ok with. Shooting a deer cleanly for food when I'm not even a good enough shot to guarantee a clean kill: fine, actually kind of admirable, better than the supermarket. A rich person who pays extra for pasture-raised beef but flies weekly: fine, no tension there as far as I'm concerned.

The one I keep circling: I'm told pigs are at least as smart and social as dogs, so if the principle is something like intelligence or capacity to suffer, my dog/pig verdicts have to go, and I really don't want to give up either. But if the principle is just "species membership," that feels gross, it's exactly the move I'd reject if someone used it about humans. Is there some third principle that saves all or most of my verdicts? If not, which verdicts would you tell me to drop, and what does your best principle say about a case I didn't list -- say, a lab that wants to breed dogs lacking functional pain receptors, so they can be experimented on without suffering?
