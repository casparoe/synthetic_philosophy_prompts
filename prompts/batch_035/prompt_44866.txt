There's a pattern I keep running into around my neighborhood and I can't find a word for it anywhere. My neighbor on the east side rerouted his downspout a few years back so the runoff heads for the low spot between our lots, which turns into a little pond on my side every spring. He knew it would, he just didn't care — he did it to keep water off his own foundation. Everyone on the street, including people who barely know him, talks about it like he floods my yard "on purpose." And nobody actually thinks he woke up wanting to flood me. But the same guy's sprinkler overshoots and waters my tomato patch for free all summer, and when I tryed to thank him, everybody laughed — oh no, he's not watering your tomatoes on purpose, that's just a happy accident, don't go thanking him. Same guy, same couldn't-care-less attitude, and we hand him totally opposite verdicts on "on purpose." Here's the part that really gets me, though: people still plan around the sprinkler water. The guy on the corner literally planed his fence repair around the morning spray, and the week my neighbor's timer broke and the tomatoes dried out, folks were annoyed at him like he'd broken a promise. So it seems like "on purpose" doesn't track what's in anyone's head at all — it tracks whether we get to be mad at you, or whether we get to count on you.
