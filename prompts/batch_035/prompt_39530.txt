Our Bayesian epistemology reading group worked through the Judy Benjamin problem a while back and it's still bugging me. Quick setup in case you only half-remember it: Judy is a soldier dropped somewhere in a region split into a Red-held half and a Blue-held half, and the Red half is itself divided into two sectors, say Second Company's sector and the HQ sector. Then the radio tells her they can't say where she is, but that if she's in Red-held territory, the odds are 3 to 1 that she's in Second Company's sector rather than the HQ sector. That's it — nothing about Red versus Blue. When we started arguing about what her posterior should look like, one of the older grad students shut the whole discussion down with just condition on your evidence: she learned a proposition, Bayes's rule tells you what to do with a learned proposition, next topic please. I'm pretty sure that one-liner is doing way more work than it appears to, but I can't untangle it myself.
