been hanging around the game-theory-adjacent corners of the rationalist internet long enough that I keep seeing the same fight: one person says rational players iterate dominance all the way down, so in the traveler's dilemma (two travelers claim compensation for identical lost souvenirs, the airline pays the lower of the two claims plus a small bonus for the lowballer and a matching penalty for the other, and iterated dominance unravels the whole thing to both claiming the minimum) they both play the bottom, and the other person says nobody actually plays that so the model is junk — and they just talk past each other. So heres the ask: write me a decision tree that sorts people's actual positions on iterated elimination of dominated strategies, including dominance by mixed strategies, as a solution concept. The branch nodes should be commitments you can state without knowing any position labels, stuff like: does rationality by itself ever force you to drop a pure strategy that's only dominated by a mixture; do a player's beliefs about the other players have to be independent of each other; does each extra round of deletion require one more layer of everyone-believing-everyone-is-rational, and is there some realistic depth at which actual people hit a wall. The leaves should include the iterate-to-the-end hardliners, the only-a-few-rounds-of-deletion bounded-rationality crowd, the epistemic-program people (rationalizability, common belief in rationality), and the outright skeptics about mixed-strategy dominance, with one sentence per leaf on its most awkward commitment. Flag any two leaves the tree honestly can't seperate, keep it to maybe 6-8 branch nodes, and use unraveling cases like the traveler's dilemma as diagnostic material wherever it helps.
