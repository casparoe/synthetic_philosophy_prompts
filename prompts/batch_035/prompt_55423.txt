I write historical novels — mostly set in the 1880s and 90s — and for the past two years I've been having the same argument with my friend who's an actual academic historian, and we've officially reached the "we just stop talking when it comes up" stage, which I hate, because I think there's something real underneath it and neither of us can say what.

The fight is always some version of presentism. If I have a character, say, a perfectly decent 1890s factory owner who genuinely believed he was doing right by his workers while paying them starvation wages and firing anyone who got injured — my historian friend says that when I judge him, or when I arrange the novel so the reader judges him, I'm smuggling our standards into a world where those standards barely existed as live options, and that this makes the fiction dishonest even if it's satisfying. My position is that there's no other place to judge from: I'm writing in 2026 for 2026 readers, his "past on its own terms" is a fantasy because the terms were themselves contested then (plenty of people in the 1890s, including his own workers, thought the arrangement was monstrous), and pretending to suspend judgment is itself a judgment. And the weird thing is we agree on all the facts of the case, every time. We've read the same diaries, the same factory records. It's purely about what either of us is supposed to be doing.

So what I want is a diagnosis, not a referee's decision. Is this a substantive disagreement about whether moral facts have a historical location, or a verbal one where "judging" means two different things (offering an assessment vs. holding someone culpable for not seeing what his time made nearly invisible)? Is he doing history and I'm doing something else — moral rhetoric aimed at the present — and we've just been slapping one word over both activities? Or is it bedrock: he has an intuition about humility toward the dead and I have one about the dead's victims, and neither intuition can be argued for from anywhere? It would help me a lot if you said what, if anything, would count as progress between us — a distinction, a case we could construct, one of us conceding something specific — or whether, given your diagnosis, we should just agree to write our different things and drop it. And if it matters to your answer, I'm also genuinely torn about whether the novelist's situation is the same as the historian's here or actually easier, since I never claimed to be neutral in the first place.
