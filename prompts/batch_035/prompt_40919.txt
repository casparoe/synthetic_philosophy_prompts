I sit on the five-person "sensitive research review board" at a mid-sized research institute. Our remit is work with obvious civilian benefit but a plausible misuse path. This month's case: a team trained an AI assistant that proposes wet-lab synthesis routes for novel small molecules; so far it's been benchmarked on ordinary medicinal-chemistry targets, and the lead PI wants it up as a preprint tomorrow. Our charter, though, is lopsided: any single board member can approve posting, but pulling something back requires all five of us to agree. And here's our own dirty math: each of us privately puts maybe a 70% chance on this preprint doing more harm than good (it's unusually useful to a careless or worse-than-careless actor, and nothing in the benchmarks bounds what the assistant will propose once it's out), yet if blocking takes unanimity, we'd need all five to hold that line — 0.7 to the fifth is about 17%, so under our own numbers we publish in roughly five out of six cases like this one even though every member thinks harm is the likelier outcome. The default rule publishes things a majority of the board thinks shouldn't go out, which is honestly embarassing for a review board. I have to circulate a recommendation memo covering both this case and the standing rule going forward. Please draft it as exactly five numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro and no conclusion: (1) what we do with this specific preprint, (2) the reasoning for that call, (3) the standing rule change I should propose to the director, (4) the strongest real objection to that rule change — from the open-science side, not a strawman — and (5) my answer to it. And please actually engage with the numbers above rather than just gesturing at caution.
