My friend and I argued for like two hours last night about anthropic reasoning, and it basicaly came down to him treating his own existence as evidence and me refusing to, and neither of us landed a clean blow. So now I want to see it done properly: write a dialogue between a defender of the self-sampling assumption and a defender of the self-indication assumption, set right inside the presumptuous philosopher case. The setup: some modest physics evidence gives a small likelihood ratio in favor of the theory on which the universe contains a trillion times more observers, and the SIA-defender concludes he should now be overwhelmingly confident in the big-universe theory, which the SSA-defender thinks is insane. Make them actually engage with each other rather than talk past each other: the SSA-defender should press on what it means to shift your credence a trillion-fold on barely any evidence, and the SIA-defender should press back on how she claims to treat her own existence as carrying no information at all while still quietly conditioning on being an observer in some reference class, and why the Doomsday argument is just the price she pays for her view. No strawmen on either side, and end with each of them naming what would actually move them off their position. A draw is fine — I mainly want to see where the real fight is.
