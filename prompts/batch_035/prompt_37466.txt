I've been going down a rabbit hole on philosophical zombies lately (the atom-for-atom identical but nobody-home kind, not the horror movie kind). One thing I keep noticing is that the zombie argument always asks you to imagine a full copy of a whole human. So I got to wondering what happens if you spread the "person" out. Here's what I want: build me a thought experiment where instead of one zombie twin, you have a swarm of tiny devices, none of which is individually smart or person-like at all, but which together behave exactly like a person -- they answer questions about thier inner life, get annoyed when you doubt them, write poetry, the works. Constraints: no mystical group-soul stuff, each device can be as dumb as a thermostat, and the swarm has to sincerely insist it's conscious. Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph. After that, walk me through three things: (1) is the swarm easier or harder to conceive of as completely unconscious than a classic zombie twin, and why -- does your intuition feel the same here or does it wobble? (2) given that, does the swarm version help the zombie argument or hurt it (i.e., if you can or can't clearly conceive of this thing as lacking conciousness, what does that say about the inference from "I can imagine it" to "it's possible")? and (3) be honest about which single feature of the setup is doing the work, and what a defender of the original zombie argument would say once you point that feature out. If you think the whole swarm thing just collapses back into the ordinary zombie case, tell me that straight up instead of pretending it's a new problem.
