So here's my situation: I've found myself pretty committed to the view that indicative conditionals are just propositions with truth conditions — that when I say "if the match gets struck, it lights," I've asserted something that is flat-out true or false, same as any other statement, and that the probability of that conditional equals the conditional probability of the consequent given the antecedent. I know David Lewis had these triviality results back in the mid-70s that are supposed to sink exactly this package, but I've never been fully convinced they hit the view itself rather than some idealisation of it — the assumption that the probability-of-conditional-equals-conditional-probability equation has to hold across every admissible belief revision, or that there's one single probability function doing all the work, or whatever. It feels like the results might be attacking a strawman version of the view.

So tell me straight: what would actually count against my position? I want the objections sorted from genuinely fatal to merely awkward, including ones that come from outside the probability stuff entirely — things like negating or embedding conditionals ("it's false that if p, then q", conditionals inside conditionals, conditionals as antecedents), the way indicative and subjunctive conditionals seem to come apart with the same antecedent and consequent, and whatever else occurs to you. Which of these would a committed truth-conditionalist about indicative conditionals themselves concede is real damage, and which would they shrug off as begging the question against them? And an honest assessment of the escape routes: if I say the probability equation only holds at the context of utterance, or that the conditional's truth value is relative to an assessment standard, have I saved the view or just qualified it into something unfalsifiable — and is that kind of insulation fatal for a semantic thesis, or is it just how semantics goes? I'd also like to hear what the view predicts that its rivals (the ones who say conditionals aren't truth-apt at all) don't, and whether any of those predictions could ever be checked against anything, even loosely.

Basically I want a map of where my view is actually exposed, not a survey of the literature. Rank the threats and tell me which one I should genuinely lose sleep over, if any. Oh, and write your answer in British English please (idealisation, not idealization, that sort of thing).
