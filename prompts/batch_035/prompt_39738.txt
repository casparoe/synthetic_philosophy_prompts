I'm presenting at a philosophy of mind workshop next month where I'm defending epiphenomenalism about phenomenal consciousness -- the view that qualia are real, non-physical, and generated by physical processes but with no causal power back over the physical. My paper runs roughly like this: the knowledge argument and the conceivability of zombies give us reason to think phenomenal properties don't supervene on physical properties; but the physical world looks causally closed -- physics never seems to need a non-physical extra to balance its books; so the least bad option is that qualia are real but causally idle, a kind of by-product.

The audience is mostly hard-nose physicalists (some type-B types who think consciousness is physical even though we can't see how), a couple of Russellian monists who think the answer is hidden in the intrinsic nature of physical properties, and one property dualist who agrees with me qualia are non-physical but thinks interactionism is obviously right. There's also a cognitive scientist in the room who will come at this from the reportability/memory angle rather than the metaphysics.

Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the eight-ish objections I'll actually get from this particular room, ordered by how much damage each could do to my position. Phrase them the way each person would actually phrase them -- the physicalists will come at the causal argument, the Russellian monists at my dismissal of their view as a cheap fix, the interactionist will be annoyed I've handed him causally idle qualia when he wanted efficacious ones. For each objection give me a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Pay special attention to the paradox of phenomenal judgement -- if qualia don't cause anything, then my current utterances about qualia aren't caused by qualia, including the utterances in my talk -- because I know that's coming and I want it handled properly rather than waved at. And tell me the question I'm hoping nobody asks.

Also: is there anything I should concede up front about how the view handles reports, memory and introspection before someone forces me to? I'd rather give ground early on the small stuff and hold the line on causal closure. If you think my paper's framing makes any of this worse for me -- e.g. whether leaning on zombie conceivability invites trouble the causal closure half of the paper can't pay for -- flag that too.
