Does the following configuration have a name? I'm the numbers person on a three-member grant panel for a small research foundation (my day job is clinical trial statistics, so excuse me if I'm using the wrong words for all this). Our rule is that a proposal gets funded only if it clears all three of our criteria -- significance, feasibility, and what we call "capacity to deliver". Last month: reviewer 1 (me) scored the proposal as clearing all three, so I voted to fund; reviewer 2 cleared significance and feasibility but not capacity, so following the rule she voted against; reviewer 3 cleared significance and capacity but not feasibility, so he voted against too. Final tally: don't fund. But if you tally each criterion separately, significance clears 3-0, feasibility clears 2-1, capacity clears 2-1 -- so criterion by criterion the panel thinks the proposal clears everything, and the panel also unanimously endorses the rule "fund only if all three are cleared", and yet the panel majority votes not to fund. Nobody made an arithmetic error and nobody was being sneaky; each of us is individually perfectly consistent. It's like the panel as a whole believes three things that can't all be true at once, even though no member does.

Two things I want to sort out. First: a colleague on the panel who took some econ says this is "Arrow's theorem, obviously", and another says it's the Condorcet cycling thing, but I don't see how it can be either of those -- there are no rankings and no cycle anywhere in sight, just yes/no judgments on questions that are logically connected to each other. So is there an actual established concept for exactly this, and how does it relate to (or differ from) those two, assuming I'm remembering them right? Second: someone proposed fixing it by rewording the rule as "reject if it fails any single criterion", claiming rejection would then follow the majority. I checked on paper and this changes literally nothing -- same votes, same outcome, it's just relabeling. But when I played with a genuinely different rule, "fund if it clears even one criterion", I could build the mirror image: every criterion fails by majority and the panel still votes unanimously to fund. So it doesn't feel like rewording can fix anything, only flip who benefits. Am I right about that, and is there any principled way to aggregate our judgments that avoids the problem altogether without collapsing into "let the chair decide" (which one member actually proposed, ha)?

To be clear, I'm not looking for a reading list or references -- just the concept, if it exists, what exactly its edges are (what counts as an instance, what doesn't, how it's different from the nearest neighbors), and whether the "no way out without giving something up" intuition is actually correct.
