Last week I asked him to actually explain the term to me and this is more or less what he said: free-floating rationales are the reasons behind evolution's designs, but they aren't reasons that anyone thought up, stated, or is aware of -- the heart's rationale is to pump blood, but no designer intended that and no organism knows it, and that's what makes the rationale "free-floating." So far so good I think, but then he went on: since the rationale exists only in the explanations we give after the fact, it's basically a projection of biologists onto nature -- it lives in our theories, not in the world. And from there he said that because of this there's no real fact of the matter about why anything evolved, just competing stories we tell, some of which happen to predict better than others, and that this is the lesson we should take over into other domains instead of assuming things have genuine functions. He finished by saying Dennett's big point is that reasons existed before reasoners did, and that once you grant that, you can watch Mother Nature gradually figuring things out over billions of years.

I'm inclined to like the picture because it sounds very deflationary and science-y, but something felt like it shifted halfway through his explanation -- the early part sounded like the reasons are really out there waiting to be discovered, and the later part sounded like the reasons are just something we invent. Those can't both be right, can they?
