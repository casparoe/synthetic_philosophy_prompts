I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and for the past few months I've been reporting a piece on griefbots — the chatbots companies now build out of a dead person's texts and emails so their relatives can keep texting them. While reporting it I finally got around to reading Hofstadter's I Am a Strange Loop, the one he wrote after his wife Carol died, where he argues that an "I" is a self-referential pattern and that a genuinely carved-off chunk of Carol keeps running, not metaphorically, in his brain and in the brains of everyone who knew her well. I found it moving and weirdly convincing, and somewhere between the griefbot interviews and that book I've ended up with a set of views that I can no longer tell apart from each other in terms of which ones fit together. So here's the package: (1) I'm a physicalist — the self is nothing over and above brain activity. (2) I've come to believe Hofstadter's line that people really do survive, partially, in the brains of those who knew them — my mother died last year, and when I told a friend that some of her lives on in me, I meant it as more than a metaphor. (3) I think a griefbot is categorically different: however good the training data and however much the survivor feels it, the bot is a tribute act, its output mimicry, and it will stay that way no matter how good it gets. (4) I also think that a sufficiently faithful whole-brain emulation of a living person — same structure, running on silicon — would continue that person; they wouldn't have died. (5) And yet I hold that death of the brain is simply the end for the person themselves: no soul, no further chapter, once the brain stops they're gone, full stop. (6) Finally, I'm suspicious of the move where personal identity becomes a matter of degrees or convention — deep down either it's the same person or it isn't, and there's a fact of the matter about which.

My editor keeps asking what my "angle" on the griefbot story is and I keep stalling because I'm honestly not sure my own position survives contact with itself. Go through the pairs and tell me which of these are genuinely in tension and how badly, which only look like thier in tension, and for each real conflict what the cheapest fix would cost me. Rank them by severity instead of flagging everything equally, and if there's one revision that would clean up the most mess, name it. I can't see where the problem is myself — that's the whole reason I'm asking. Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then give me the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my question back at me.
