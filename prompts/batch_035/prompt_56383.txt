so there's this thought experiment in philosophy of mind (the "extended mind" thing, from the 90s): a guy Otto has memory problems, so he writes everything he needs to remember in a notebook that he carries everywhere. when he wants to know where the museum is, he looks it up in the notebook. the claim in the literature is that the notebook is doing for Otto exactly what biological memory does for everyone else, so the notebook literally counts as part of his mind — his mind isn't stuck inside his skull. when I was explaining this to a coworker I came up with an analogy I can't stop thinking about, and I want you to audit it.

the analogy: a running process isn't just the CPU. a process's state is spread across registers, RAM, and pages swapped out to disk, and the OS counts the swapped pages as part of the process even though they're physically on a totally different device. nobody says "the process is only what's in the registers right now." so: Otto's brain is the CPU, his notebook is swap space, and saying the notebook isn't part of his mind is like saying a process is just its registers. the more I use it the more it makes extension sound obvious — which is exactly what makes me suspicious. analogies that make something sound obvious are usually smuggling in the conclusion.

here's what I think might be the load-bearing part: in the OS case, the swap access is transparent and automatic. the process doesn't decide anything — it just faults and the page shows up, as far as the process can tell it never left memory. but Otto deliberately pulls out the notebook, opens it, and reads. that's slow, effortful, and he might have left it at home. is that a real disanalogy or just a difference of degree — after all, when I "just remember" something, my brain still has to do a retrieval, it's not instant either. and there's another one bugging me: the OS mapping is designed, some engineer explicitly decided that swap pages count. the otto-brain-notebook system just grew that way, nobody declared anything. does designed vs. grown matter, or is that a red herring?

so, the audit: lay out what maps onto what (CPU, registers, RAM, swap, page fault, and where do I go — am I the process? the OS? that one feels weird), then where the mapping breaks, then which breaks actually matter for the extension conclusion versus which ones I shouldn't waste time on, and whether the conclusion survives once the real breaks are on the table. be honest about whether this analogy was carrying my belief the whole time or whether it tracks something genuine. and if the swap one fails, tell me whether there's a better analogy from systems land — or whether the fact that I need a computer analogy at all to make it intuitive should tell me something about the whole debate.
