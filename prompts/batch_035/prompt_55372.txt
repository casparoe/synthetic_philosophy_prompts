I run a reading group that's working through Marx with some non-specialists (a nurse, a software guy, and a retired accountant, all argumentative). We keep going in circles because people use "exploitation" and "alienation" to mean about five different things, and everyone assumes their meaning is the obvious one. What I want is a quiz, like a "which theory of exploitation do you actually hold?" thing, that sorts people by their answers to concrete cases rather than by which label they like. So: around ten questions, each one a short scenario with two or three answer options, phrased in plain language so nobody can tell which school of thought an answer is supposed to point at. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the views.

For the scenarios, I'd like you to draw on stuff like: a worker in a poor country who chose the factory over subsistence farming and is much better off than before, and says she wasn't cheated; a well-paid software engineer who loves her salary but says the standups and the ticket queue make her feel like a ghost; a freelancer who sets her own hours and rates but has no health insurance and one bad month from ruin; a worker cooperative where everyone votes on everything but pays are low; an artist who gives her labor away for free because she'd do it anyway; and a fully automated factory where nobody works but a shareholder class collects the output. The point of these is that the standard views come apart on them — the surplus-extraction view (you're exploited when someone pockets value you produced, roughly Marx's own story), the unfairness-in-distribution view (exploitation is about getting a worse deal than some baseline of fair exchange allows, the Roemer-ish reading), the domination view (it's about lacking control over your working life, not about the split of the proceeds), and the alienation view where even a fantastic deal can be rotten because of what the work does to you as a person. If the scenarios can also catch the "no coercion, no exploitation" libertarian position as a leaf, that'd be great, and if there's an "unnamed" cell in your logical space that nobody actually defends, say so and tell me whether you think it's empty because it's incoherent or just unexplored.

One more thing: please flag honestly any pair of views the quiz can't really separate — I'd rather the scoring key admit "if you answered this pattern you could be either X or Y, these questions don't split them" than have it pretend to more precision than the cases deliver. And keep the alienation questions distinct from the exploitation ones where possible, so people can come out as e.g. "exploited but not alienated" or vice versa; that combination is actually one of the things my group fights about most. Keep the whole thing at a level where a smart person who's never read a page of Marx could take the quiz and get sorted correctly.
