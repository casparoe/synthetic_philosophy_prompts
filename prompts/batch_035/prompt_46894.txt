I've spent roughly fifteen years in rationalist spaces, and a recurring fight — most recently in a thread about whether an AI trained to instill 'human values' would simply be exporting one culture's values at scale — keeps turning on the phrase 'Western values', which I have never once seen defined. Someone objected that there are no universal human values, only Western ones. Someone else replied that science, abolition, and the idea of human rights are Western values and the rest of the world was welcome to them. A third pointed out that 'Western' was doing opposite work in the two replies: a certificate of origin in one mouth, a demotion in the other.

I want a disambiguation I can argue from. What distinct precise claims can 'these are Western values' express? I can sketch some candidates: a genealogical claim (they happened to crystallize or be codified first in Europe and its diaspora); an exclusion claim (other civilizations actually reject them, rather than merely not having originated them); a contingency claim (they are local in the way a cuisine or a script is local, with nothing deeper to recommend them); a universality-undercutting claim (their global spread reflects who held power when they spread, so citing their widespread acceptance as evidence of anything is circular); an ownership claim (they belong to Westerners in some way that licenses their propagation, or perhaps obligates restitution for it); and a conversational claim (invoking them is a status move, not a description at all).

For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, substantive and defensible, substantive but false, or outright confused; then say which readings would actually support the original objection — that the alignment project of promoting 'human values' is incoherent or covertly parochial — and which would support the proud-export reply. A subsidiary question that keeps resurfacing in my circles: do these readings extend to epistemic norms? We treat 'form beliefs on evidence and update when you're wrong' as a fixed point, but it too has been called a Western value, and I'd like to know whether that is the same claim with the same force or a different one masquerading as it. Finally, tell me whether part of the thread was purely verbal — both parties agreeing on the genealogy, one reading 'Western' as 'contingent, so drop the universalist rhetoric' and the other as 'homegrown, so be glad it spread'. Lay it out as a menu with verdicts, ending with the reading the objector actually needed for their argument to work and whether it survives scrutiny.
