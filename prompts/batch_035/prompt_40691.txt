I'm on my city's algorithmic tools review panel, and we have to decide this month whether to sign off on a model that scores homes for lead-paint hazard and ranks them for inspection. Our own audit found the usual bind: because actual hazard rates differ a lot between neighborhoods, the model can't be calibrated by neighborhood and give each neighborhood the same rate of missed hazards at the same score cutoff — you have to pick one. Half the panel wants calibration (a "65" should mean 65% chance everywhere, or residents in the worse-off neighborhoods will just stop trusting the scores), and the other half wants equal missed-hazard rates (otherwise the tool quietly reproduces the disparity it was bought to fix). One member floated a compromise I can't stop thinking about: drop the ranking and run a weighted lottery among the top 300 homes. Can you draft the memo I'll circulate? It should recommend one concrete decision, give the losing side's strongest point a fair shake, take the lottery idea seriously rather than waving it off, and end with a line or two on what we'd tell a resident whose "68" home got passed over for a "61" home. Keep it under a page — and sorry for typos, I'm typing this between meetings.
