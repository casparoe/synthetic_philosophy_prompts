i do back-office admin at a public employment office (the place that pays unemployment benefits) and the longer I'm inside the system the more philosophically weird the whole thing looks, so maybe you can untangle it for me. here's the practice as I see it from the inside: to keep your benefit you have to be available for work, document a minimum number of job applications per week, show up to scheduled meetings and mandatory "employability" courses, and accept any reasonable offer that comes along. Miss things without good cause and your payment gets cut, sometimes a percentage, sometimes entirely for weeks. Meanwhile the same system (a) pays people deemed medically incapable of work with no strings like this attached, (b) pays carers and pensioners without demanding any job search, and (c) treats a claimant who hid a part-time job as basically a thief, while someone living comfortably off inherited wealth who never works a day gets a tax break on their savings and zero lectures from anybody. So my question: what does all this presuppose? Is it that unemployment is at bottom a motivational problem and the conditionality is just incentive design, or that benefit money has to be *earned* by conducting yourself as a worker-in-waiting — a desert thing? Those two readings seem to predict different policies in some of the cases I listed, but I can't tell whether that means the system presupposes something inconsistent or whether I'm missing a third reading that fits everything. And is the fraud-vs-rentier asymmetry a genuine contradiction in the presupposed theory, or does it just fall out of the thought that you can't attach conditions to money you weren't distributing in the first place?
