My friend's been pushing this on me after our ethics-of-belief seminar and I keep going back and forth on where it breaks. Maya's argument, roughly:

(1) James says that when the evidence can't decide between believing p and disbelieving p, and the option is live, forced, and momentous, we're entitled to go ahead and believe. His own examples: trusting a friend, religious faith.

(2) Whether the evidence decides a question is not a fact about the world out there -- it's a fact about my epistemic situation, which I access fallibly. From the inside, "the evidence doesn't decide" and "I can't see how the evidence decides" feel identical.

(3) So whenever I honestly can't see how the evidence settles p, I'm in James's territory, and entitled to believe whichever way I prefer.

(4) And what I can't see depends on how hard I look. If I'd rather believe p, I can decline to read the studies, not ask the follow-up question, stop thinking at the convenient point. Then the option is genuinely undecided *for me*, and per (3) I'm entitled to p.

(5) So James doesn't just permit faith in a friend or religious commitment -- he licenses motivated reasoning and evidence-avoidance wholesale. The rule for when it's okay to believe ahead of the evidence can be satisfied by manufacturing the very condition that triggers it.

(6) That can't be right. So something before it is wrong.

Maya thinks the argument shows James's whole permission is a scam, and that Clifford basically wins by default. I suspect there's an equivocation somewhere around (2) and (3) -- James seemed to mean the *nature of the case* leaves the evidence silent, not my current state of confusion -- but when I said that she came back with: how do you tell the difference between those without stepping outside your own evidence, which you just admitted you can't do? Which step would you reject, and what do you pay for rejecting it? (I'd honestly rather not give up (6), and I'd also rather not hand the whole thing to Clifford, so if there's a way to keep both tell me what it costs.)
