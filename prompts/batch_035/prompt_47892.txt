Write a corporate product recall notice, maybe 500-600 words, from a fictional company that manufactures higher-level properties — their catalog includes things like liquidity, market crashes, team morale, and the wetness of water. They're recalling their entire "emergence" product line after customer complaints that whenever you try to make something happen at the emergent level, the underlying microphysics has already done it. The document should be deadpan corporate throughout, but the jokes have to come from getting the actual debate right, so that a philosopher of science laughs instead of winces: the causal exclusion worry (if the physical realizers already fully cause the effect, what work is left for the macro property — is it just riding along?), multiple realizability showing up as the legal defense ("compatible with a wide range of underlying hardware"), Nagel-style bridge laws appearing in the compliance/appendix section as the promised-but-undelivered documentation, and weak vs strong emergence as different model numbers with different warranty terms. Maybe a FAQ section where a customer asks whether they can keep using their emergent property for everyday prediction and the company's answer quietly concedes that practically speaking yes, but the property is being offered "as a manner of speaking only." The satire should exaggerate what reductionists actually argue and what emergentists actually claim back, not invent positions, and by the end a reader should have felt the real question: do higher-level properties do causal work of their own, or are they just nice packaging around the parts? Funny to someone who knows the literature, readable to someone who doesn't.
