So, context: I've been reading LessWrong and the rationalist-adjacent blogosphere since roughly 2016, long enough to remember when "AI alignment" meant Paul Christiano posts and not a thousand-person org with merch, and I've somehow talked my way into giving a talk at the internal seminar series of a mid-size AI safety org. The thesis I'm defending is a fairly strong anti-paternalism line for AI assistants: for competent adult users, refusals should be reserved for direct harms to third parties, and everything else should be handled with pushback rather than gatekeeping. But the part I actually care about, and the part that's newer, is what I call the distinction between *gate-keeping paternalism* and *steering paternalism* — refusals get all the discourse, but the bigger aggregate effect on what users end up believing is probably the silent stuff: unsolicited hedging, the model reframing your question into a safer one, disclaimers injected into answers, the assistant quietly deciding which parts of an answer you're ready to recieve. My proposal is that all of this should be counted against an explicit, visible "paternalism budget" per session — the model gets a small allowance of unilateral overrides of what you asked for, and once it's spent, it asks instead of steering. Think Thaler/Sunstein nudge logic, except my claim is that current assistants are running nudges that aren't even acknowledged as interventions, which is worse than either libertarians or paternalists are willing to grant.

The audience is maybe 40 people: mostly technical alignment researchers who think about misuse evals all day, two or three trust-and-safety policy people who have literally written the refusal taxonomies at a frontier lab, one lawyer, and a vocal contingent whose actual nightmare scenario is sycophancy — models being too agreeable and telling users what they want to hear — who are going to hear my position as "make the sycophancy worse" within the first five minutes. There's also some latent sympathy because half of them use these assistants daily and privately hate the preachiness, but nobody wants to be the person who says that out loud in a room full of misuse people. I get 45 minutes to talk and then 30 minutes of Q&A (which always runs over).
