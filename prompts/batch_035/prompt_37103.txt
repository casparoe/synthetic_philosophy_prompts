so I run a philosophy of mind reading group, 9 people, mostly grad students plus two sharp undergrads, and we meet for 90 minutes. Next session we're on Dennett's quining qualia paper, the one with the two coffee tasters Chase and Sanborn who both stopped liking the coffee but disagree on whether their taste experiences changed or their judgments about the same experiences did. Reading the argument on paper, everyone just nods along, so I want an activity where they actually run into the problem themselves before we name it.

my half-formed idea is a tasting thing: something cheap and strong (tonic water? black coffee? those super bitter lemon things?) where the taste genuinely drifts over repeated exposure, so there's real perceptual adaptation happening under the hood, and each person has to try to figure out — purely from the inside — whether the experience itself faded or the experience stayed the same and they just stopped liking it. And of course they can't tell, and that's the point. But I'm open to whatever structure works better.

Design the full thing: rules, materials, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and how to collect ratings as we go so there's actual data by the end (I want them confronting the gap between what their ratings show and what they can honestly claim about their own qualia). The key beat I want is a moment where they have to adjudicate, for themselves, Chase's story vs Sanborn's story about their own tongue, and discover introspection gives them nothing to adjudicate with. Then a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto specific parts of Dennett's argument, including the stuff about memory being the weak link — though maybe tell me if the short timescale of one session undercuts that part and how to handle it.

Also be honest about what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (someone figures out where it's headed, someone who genuinely can't taste quinine, people confabulating confident answers) and what feature of the philosophical point the activity flat out cannot reproduce no matter how well it goes. No lecture up front, and they shouldn't see the paper's title til the debrief. oh and keep materials under like 15 bucks.
