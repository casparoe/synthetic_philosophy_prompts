i'm a data scientist at an insurance company and i've somehow ended up as the office interventionist. whenever causation comes up in a meeting, i'm the one saying forget Lewis-style possible-worlds counterfactuals, the actual content of a causal claim is what would happen if you intervened -- grab the variable, set it, hold everything else fixed, see what moves. that's the Pearl / Woodward picture and i really do believe it. it's what lets us do anything practical with our models, and i like that it ties causation to things you could actually do instead of metaphysics about nearby worlds.

my cousin, whos doing a philosophy PhD, needles me about this every thanksgiving. her three complaints: (1) interventionism secretly leans on the counterfactuals it claims to replace, since "hold everything else fixed" and "set the variable to a value it never had" are themselves counterfactual notions, so the replacement is more like a relabeling; (2) it can't handle causes that aren't manipulable even in principle -- the moon's pull on the tides, someone's genotype, being born in 1962 -- and stretching "intervention" to cover those makes the whole thing vacuous; (3) the modularity assumption, that you can wiggle one mechanism without disturbing the others, is just false for the systems i actually care about -- markets, bodies, ecologies, anything with feedback loops, which is to say basically everything except the toy models.
