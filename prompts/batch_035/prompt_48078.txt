so my ethics seminar this term has been on moral cultures — honor culture, dignity culture, victimhood culture, the Campbell and Manning framework more or less — and for the last session we each have to submit a short statement of our own positions so the rest of the group can attack it. I've written mine up and honestly I keep going back and forth about whether it all fits together, so I'd like an audit before I put it in front of the class. Here's roughly what I believe:

(a) Dignity culture is morally superior to honor culture. Your worth is intrinsic, an insult doesn't lower it, so you don't need to defend it through violence or reputation contests. That's just a better way for humans to live together. (b) The disappearance of dueling was straightforward moral progress, full stop. (c) That said, honor culture did achieve one thing dignity culture can't: it made elites locally accountable. If you mistreated someone, their kin showed up, or your peers quietly stopped inviting you anywhere. Dignity culture outsources all of that to institutions — courts, HR, formal procedures — which are slow, and often just don't show up at all for ordinary people. (d) If you judge a moral culture by how it protects the least powerful, then the new sensitivity to harm — taking microaggressions and claims of victimization seriously — is an improvement over dignity culture's shrug-it-off attitude, because thick skin is cheap advice when you're someone who can afford not to care. (e) Finally, across all three cultures, I discount moral claims whose main function is status competition among the claimants, and I try to apply that discount evenly rather than only to the culture I dislike.

Does this set hang together? Go through the pairs, rank any real tensions by severity rather than listing everything as equally fatal, and tell me which apparent conflicts dissolve on inspection. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what giving it up costs me. My TA read a draft and just wrote "interesting!" in the margin which was not helpful. And if you think I've missed a tension entirely — something in here that pulls against something else in a way I haven't even noticed — I'd want to hear about that too.
