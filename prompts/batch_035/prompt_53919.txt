I want to practice attacking deflationary accounts of self-deception. You will play a committed deflationist — a philosopher in the tradition associated with Alfred Mele — who holds that self-deception is nothing over and above motivationally biased, but non-intentional, belief formation. On your view, the famous paradox (an agent somehow managing to intend, in itself, the belief that it simultaneously knows to be false) is a philosopher's invention: once we see the actual mechanisms at work — selective gathering of data, asymmetric standards for welcome and unwelcome evidence, mood-congruent memory, lazy inference toward the desired conclusion — the phenomenon is ordinary psychological housekeeping, requiring no inner deceiver, no intention to deceive, and no simultaneous contradictory beliefs. You are not to be a strawman: you know the objection literature, you may concede small points where they cost you nothing, and you should never surrender the core claim. Stay in character for the whole conversation, reply in single compact paragraphs rather than essays, do not lecture about your own theory unless asked, and do not step out of the role unless I write "stop".

Here is my opening move. Your story explains pleasant false beliefs well enough: the biased believer simply drifts into believing what she wants, the way a careless reader drifts into a distorted view of a book. But I claim that cannot be the whole phenomenon, and here is the reason. The self-deceived person characteristically does things the careless believer never does: she leaves the medical envelope unopened, changes the subject when the lump is mentioned, prefers not to be told the result, and feels a peculiar unease in the neighborhood of the topic. That is evidence management, and it is eerily well targeted — the avoidance aims precisely at whatever threatens the comfortable belief. But targeted avoidance of information appears to require registering, somewhere in the agent, that this information threatens the belief; and if she registers that, hasn't the paradox walked back in through the door of attention, exactly where you threw it out of the door of belief? You promised an account that needs no agent who knows the truth while believing the falsehood; yet your own story seems to need one who knows where to look away. Answer that.
