A colleague of mine who works on effective altruism has taken to dismissing a whole family of arguments with one line: Pascal's mugging is just Pascal's wager with the theology stripped out. Her point is that any decision rule that bites the bullet on the wager — expected-utility maximization in the face of tiny probabilities of astronomical payoffs — also commits you to handing your wallet to the mugger, so both go down together, and the low-probability, high-stakes reasoning behind various existential-risk and far-future claims loses its cover. I half-believe her but can't reconstruct exactly why, and I want to know whether the analogy is doing the work or whether the conclusion was smuggled in. Audit it for me. Give the correspondence table — what plays the role of God, of salvation, of the wager's stake, of the mugger's claim about simulated lives — and then say where the mapping breaks. I have some candidates for the disanalogies that matter, and I'd like you to tell me which of them carry weight and which are red herrings: the wager's payoff is genuinely infinite while the mugger's is only astronomically large and finite, so the wager's characteristic pathologies (mixed strategies, wagering for many possible gods, the trouble with infinite expectations) may not transfer at all; the wager's entry cost can be argued to be zero or negative — you lose nothing, perhaps gain a better life, by believing — whereas the wallet is real money leaving my pocket; and the mugger can raise his number after watching me calculate, while the wager's payoff is fixed in advance, so only one of the two has an adversarial escalation dynamic built in. Also flag the disanalogies that look important but aren't, given what my colleague actually wants the analogy to carry. Then answer the central question: once the disanalogies are on the table, does the mugging show that wager-style reasoning is broken, or merely that it can be gamed — and does that distinction do real work in her argument? Finally, if the wager is the wrong source domain for the lesson the mugging teaches, propose a better analogy and say what it gets right that the wager framing gets wrong.
