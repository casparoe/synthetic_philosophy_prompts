My friend has an arguement that perfect forgeries should be valued exactly like the originals, and the worst part is I think the same reasoning proves things nobody would accept, but I can't find the broken step.

(1) Aesthetic value has to supervene on what an informed viewer could in principle pick out by examining the work -- otherwise art criticism, which only has looking (and reading, listening, etc.) to go on, could never be right or wrong about anything. (2) A perfect forgery matches the original in every pick-outable respect: same paint laid the same way, same composition, same everything you could point to. (3) So a perfect forgery has the same aesthetic value as the original. (4) Any remaining difference -- that Vermeer's actual hand moved over one canvas and van Meegeren's over the other -- is a historical fact, not an aesthetic one, so the extra reverence for the original is like paying extra for a sock because a celebrity wore it.

So far, so sorta plausible. But then he pushes it further, and this is where I get nervous: by the same reasoning, if a monkey at a typewriter (or some random process, whatever) produced text identical to Middlemarch, that would be just as great a novel, since everything pick-outable in the text is identical. And if a stranger rewrote by hand a letter from your dead mother, perfectly, so that every detectable property matched, it would be worth the same to you as the original. And a machine-exact copy of your signature on a contract would bind you just as much, since the ink pattern is the same. He takes these as further support for his view, which frankly alarms me -- but if I reject the conclusion I have to say where the chain breaks, and every premise still looks fine when I stare at it individually. Part of me wants to keep (3) and just insist the monkey case has nothing to do with forgery, but that smells like special pleading. So which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? And if you think the whole thing is confused in a way that picking a numbered step won't fix -- like "perfect forgery" or "pick-outable property" is doing sneaky double duty somewhere -- please just say that plainly instead of appeasing me with a premise. Also write your answer as continuous prose, no headings or bullet points, like an email.
