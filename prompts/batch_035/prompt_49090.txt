so heres something thats been bugging me for a while. my city (and i think most governments) handles people not having enough in like three completely different ways, and i cant figure out if theres one idea of fairness behind it all or if its just a mess.

way one: if you dont have money for food, you get money-ish things — food stamps, cash assistance, whatever. nobody asks what would make you personally happy, they just top up your resources and let you spend it.

way two: some stuff is given to everyone as an actual thing, not money. free public libraries, free schools, free transit in some places. rich or poor, you get the library. nobody hands out book vouchers instead.

way three: if you use a wheelchair, my employer cant just give you extra cash to deal with it, they have to rebuild the doorway and put in a ramp. we change the world, not the person's wallet. and yet the same system gives blind people a check sometimes? like sometimes its money, sometimes its the environment.

i keep going back and forth. maybe the system thinks equality means equal money-and-stuff in your hands, and the ramp stuff is just cheaper or more practical than cash. or maybe it secretly thinks equality means everyone can actually DO things — like actually being able to get into the building — and the cash programs are the weird part. or maybe its all just "whatever mix weve historically cobbled together" with no theory at all.

so what i want from you: take the three ways seriously as one system. what does the combination have to assume about what makes two peoples lives equal — is it what you have, what you can do, how good you feel? which parts of the system actually force one of those answers and which parts could go along with any of them? is there a real inconsistency (like giving money for one problem and ramps for a near-identical problem) or can some view make sense of all three at once? and if the government picked one of these ideas of equality and committed to it for real, not just on paper, which of the three ways would have to change, and in what direction?

im not looking for the "correct" policy, i want to know what the current mess presupposes, if anything.
