I'm on the review board for a state employment agency (unemployment benefits), and we have to sign off on a fraud-detection model that scores incoming claims for how likely they are to be fraudulent. High scorers get their benefits frozen pending manual review. The model is well calibrated overall, but because base rates of fraud differ across demographic groups, it apparently can't be calibrated within each group AND have equal false-positive rates across groups at the same time — a consultant waved the impossibility results at us and that part checks out, so we have to pick.

Half the board wants calibration as the hard constraint: a given score should mean the same thing for everyone, and group-level miscalibration feels like it poisons every downstream decision that touches the score. The other half wants to equalize false positives: the harm isn't the number, it's the frozen benefits, and its the false positives that cause that, and they land harder on one group than the others. Neither side has moved in three meetings.

Please draft the decision memo I'll circulate before Thursday: a concrete recommendation (one of the two constraints, or some third option like group-specific decision thresholds or capping how many claims get frozen at all), reasons the losing side can actually recognize themselves in, and an honest section on what we're giving up either way. I'd especially like the memo to get at the thing I suspect is under the whole fight: whether we're treating the score as a measurement of fraud-propensity or as just an input to a decision, and whether that difference alone settles which constraint we should care about.
