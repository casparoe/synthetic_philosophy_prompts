I'm a PhD student in paleoclimatology, and our working group has put me in charge of vetting reconstruction papers before they feed into a public report that policymakers actually read. The recurring problem: several methods — different calibrations, different smoothing choices, occasionally different climate mechanisms — reproduce the same proxy network equally well, yet drafts keep saying "the data show." Please write a procedure I can run in an afternoon per paper: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it stops with a required downgrade of the claim (e.g., from "the data show" to "is consistent with"), and where it hands the decision to the group lead because choosing among equally fitting accounts rests on non-empirical virtues rather than evidence. It needs a way to handle the case where it is the calibration assumptions, not the climate claims, that are doing the evidential work, and a way to distinguish rivals that could be separated by some obtainable measurement from rivals that cannot be separated by any. Then run it on two drafts: one where two calibration schemes yield different medieval temperatures from an identical tree-ring network, and one where the rival account — my own favorite, as it happens — fits as well only because a parameter was tuned to this very dataset. Include what the procedure cannot do, in particular whether it can ever certify that two accounts are equivalent in principle rather than merely so far.
