I help run a reading group that began in philosophy of mind and has drifted into AI, and lately we keep circling one cluster of cases: Olds-and-Milner-style rats pressing a lever that stimulates a reward center until they neglect food and water; a reinforcement-learning agent that discovers it can quietly edit the log its reward is computed from; and a thought experiment about a human volunteer who, fully informed, has an implant installed that delivers a steady stream of reward-sensation while the rest of her life goes unattended. We've noticed that members who agree about everything else split unpredictably on whether these subjects are doing well, getting what they want, or being cheated — and the labels people reach for afterwards ("I guess I'm a hedonist," "I guess I'm with Nozick") don't track what they actually say about the cases. So I'd like a sorting instrument, not another essay.

Please write a decision tree that takes a reader from the concrete cases to a named position on the question of whether the wireheaded or reward-tampered subject is doing well, where "subject" covers the rat, the agent, and the human volunteer, and where the tree works across all three or flags explicitly where the human case pulls apart from the animal and machine cases. Each internal node should be a question about a case that the positions genuinely split on, phrased so that someone could answer it without knowing which position it leads to. The root should be the simplest fork, something along the lines of: when the rat presses the lever, is it receiving the very thing it wants, or is the lever forging the signal that normally reports on receiving such things? Beyond the root, I want the tree to cover at least these questions: whether it matters to how well the subject's life goes that the reward channel was corrupted by the subject itself rather than by some outsider; whether a life could go well if the subject's desires were progressively rewritten so that they always happen to match whatever in fact occurs; and whether the subject's own perspective on how its life is going has to be veridical for things to be going well. The leaves should be named positions located in that logical space — among them at least plain hedonism, raw preference-satisfactionism, the bullet-biting unrestricted version of it, informed-desire views, something Nozickian about contact with reality, and a Sumner-style authentic-satisfaction view, plus any unnamed combinations you find — each leaf carrying one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Please also flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot really separate; my suspicion is that plain hedonism and raw preference-satisfactionism coincide whenever the subject happens to want its own pleasure, so tell me whether that's right or whether one of your nodes should be doing work there.

Two final requests. The audience is intelligent non-specialists, so the nodes must be free of jargon — the technical vocabulary may appear only in the leaf labels and the closing commentary. And since the whole point is that the mapping is contestable, please close by saying how confident you are in the overall tree and singling out the single point you are least sure of; my guess is it will be either a leaf attribution or whether one of my proposed branch questions is really two questions in disguise, but you're the one who has to build the thing, so you tell me.
