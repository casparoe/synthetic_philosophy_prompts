I sit on a volunteer advisory board for a regional health authority that has to decide which complementary and alternative practices may be offered alongside conventional medicine in public clinics, and what, if anything, we should say to patients about the evidential status of those practices. I have no academic background in philosophy of science -- I trained as a nurse -- but I have spent several years thinking about this on my own, and I keep running into what I understand is called the demarcation problem: the question of what separates science from non-science or pseudoscience.

The standard answer I get, falsifiability, seems to do almost no work in our actual decisions. Practitioners of the therapies we evaluate are often perfectly happy to make testable claims, and some of them have even run small trials that came out in their favor. Conversely, plenty of mainstream medicine has been pushed through on evidence that would not have survived strict falsificationist scrutiny. So the thing that bothers me is that the classic criterion seems to cut the wrong way at the level we operate on: we are not judging grand theories but individual practices, specific studies, and people's institutions, and I suspect demarcation might work differently at those levels than it does for whole theories. I have also read that some philosophers think the whole search for a single dividing line is a mistake and that pseudoscience is better thought of as a cluster of overlapping warning signs, none of them individually necessary or sufficient.

Given all that, here is what I want from you. Suppose someone like me has perhaps a year of spare-time reading and thinking to devote to this, with the practical aim of producing defensible guidance for our board and for the clinicians who have to implement it. Which of the open questions in this area should such a person prioritize, and in what order? For example: does it matter for institutional decision-making whether any single criterion of science exists at all, or can we proceed with a checklist of red flags without settling that? Does it matter whether certain historical cases (astrology, alchemy, phrenology) are classified as pseudoscience, as failed science, or as something else, or is that dispute academic in the bad sense for our purposes? Does the question of where fields like economics or string theory fall on the line have any genuine bearing on our health-related decisions, or is it a distraction? And are there questions I have not listed that should rank higher than any of these?

For each item in your ranking, please explain your criteria -- how much our practical decisions depend on it, whether it is tractable for a motivated non-specialist rather than a trained philosopher, and whether settling it would actually change what we advise. I am especially interested in which alleged implications of the demarcation debate are overrated for our kind of use case, and which are load-bearing even though people rarely mention them. Please do not recommend books or papers; I want the reasoning, not a syllabus.
