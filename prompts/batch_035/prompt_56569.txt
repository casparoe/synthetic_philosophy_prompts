I'm preparing a handout for an interdisciplinary workshop on accountability for autonomous systems, with an audience of engineers, lawyers, and philosophers. Please write a taxonomy of the positions in the debate over "responsibility gaps" as a decision tree. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of authors. The first split should be about what kind of gap is being claimed: a gap in the conditions for attributing responsibility at all (control, knowledge, or intention failing when a machine's behavior outruns what any human foresaw) versus a gap in our practical ability to hold anyone to account, given how causation and expertise are diffused across designers, operators, users, regulators, and the system itself. Later nodes should cover at least these questions: whether the gap is real or merely an artifact of insisting that machine conduct fit a human-shaped mold of agency; whether a real gap should be closed by design (for instance through traceability requirements or a demand for meaningful human control) rather than by legal doctrines like strict liability, vicarious liability, or mandatory insurance; and whether some gaps should simply be tolerated rather than closed at all, or responsibility redistributed to collectives such as firms or professional bodies rather than individuals. Each leaf should carry a position name (or "unnamed" if no one, to your knowledge, occupies it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Phrase every node as a question a workshop participant could answer without knowing which position it leads to, so the tree sorts people by their commitments rather than their loyalties to labels. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate, and aim for eight to twelve leaves.
