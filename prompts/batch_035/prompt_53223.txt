I'm giving a talk next month at a workshop on empirical moral psychology, and I need to get ready for the Q&A. My thesis is a bit awkward to place: I think the situationist critique of virtue ethics (you know, the Milgram / Good Samaritan stuff about character traits being swamped by trivial situational factors) never actually landed, mainly because the classic studies are shakier than philosophers pretended — but I still think virtue ethics has to change, because even if the effect sizes were tiny, the Aristotelian habit of thinking of character as a stable disposition that carries across situations is doing real work in the ethics and has to go, or at least get rebuilt. My rebuild: phronesis is mostly situation-management. The practically wise person isn't someone with a robust trait that shows up in all conditions, she's someone good at pre-structuring her environment so the situations that would corrupt her mostly never arrive (choosing her friends, avoiding certain bars, putting the biscuits on a high shelf, Ulysses-and-the-sirens style). So I end up closer to the situationists than most virtue ethicists will like, while refusing them the conclusion that character talk is empty.

So I can't lean on the classic studies at all, the psychologists in the room will demolish me if I try.
