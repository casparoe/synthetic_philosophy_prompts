**Exam question:** Anthropic reasoning offers two main rules for handling evidence obtained through one's own existence: the Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) and the Self-Indication Assumption (SIA). (1) State each assumption in your own words and explain the difference between them. (2) Explain the "presumptuous philosopher" scenario, say which of the two assumptions it is usually taken to be a problem for, and say why.

**Student's answer:** SSA tells you to reason as if you were a random sample drawn from the set of all observers who could possibly have existed — so when a theory says the world contains a certain population of observers, you treat yourself as a random one of them. SIA goes further: it says that the mere fact that you exist is evidence, and specifically evidence in favor of theories on which more observers exist, because the more observers there are, the more "chances" there were for someone like you to be around to notice anything at all.

The presumptuous philosopher case: suppose we have two cosmological theories, T1 and T2, which are identical in every observable respect and have equal prior probabilities, except that T1 says the universe contains a trillion observers while T2 says it contains a trillion trillion observers. Under SSA, you should consider it vastly more likely that you live in the T2 world, since your probability of finding yourself in a world, given that world, is proportional to how many observers it contains. The presumptuous philosopher then announces, without doing any physics at all, that T2 is almost certainly true — which seems absurd, since the two theories were evidentially on a par. This is generally regarded as a decisive objection to SSA, and it is the main reason many philosophers prefer SIA, which keeps the two theories at roughly equal odds. A related benefit of SIA is that it generates the Doomsday argument: since SIA favors worlds with many observers, it implies we should expect the total number of humans to be small, so that our relatively early birth rank doesn't need explaining. SSA, by contrast, cancels the Doomsday argument and leaves our birth rank probabilistically irrelevant.
