I want a thought experiment about moral particularism, built to a pretty specific spec. The background: particularists like Dancy claim that a feature which counts in favor of an action in one context can count against it in another — the feature's valence can flip, it's not just that other reasons outweigh it. Generalists have a standing reply: nothing flips; the feature stays a positive reason but some further fact defeats or undercuts it, and some *different* reason explains the overall verdict. That reply is basically always available, which makes it suspicious. So I need a case where the redescriptive move looks bad.

Construct a scenario, one paragraph, ordinary life (no trolleys, no ticking bombs, no demons), where a single feature plausibly reverses polarity between two situations, and design it so that the generalist's "defeater plus a different reason" story looks ad hoc rather than illuminating. The two situations should be as parallel as you can make them — same agent, same kind of act, comparable stakes — so that the only thing that changes is how that one feature contributes. The classic example people reach for is promising (normally a reason to keep it, but a promise extracted by threat seems to become a reason *against* keeping it, not just a reason that got outweighed) — feel free to use something fresher than that if you can pull it off.

After the scenario, walk through both readings: the particularist's valence-flip story and the generalist's defeater story, and say which handles the case better and why. Then tell me honestly which detail of the case is doing the work — if you stripped that detail out, would we be back to an ordinary case where nothing flips and the generalist has nothing to explain? And finally, give the generalist their best shot: what auxiliary machinery would they invoke (default reasons, enablers vs. reasons in the strict sense, conditionals like "if you promised, that's a reason, unless..."), and does taking on that machinery cost them anything elsewhere — e.g. does it quietly concede the holism the particularist wanted all along? One more constraint: no fair just stipulating inside the case that the feature flips valence — the flip has to emerge from the concrete details, not be announced. Watch out that you dont accidentally build in a second reason that's doing all the work.
