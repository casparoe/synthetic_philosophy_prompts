I'm the guest editor of a special journal issue in the philosophy of history on non-human causes as putative "historical actors" — germs, climate fluctuations, and volcanic eruptions doing explanatory work that historians once reserved for kings, classes, and ideas. I can fit about eight papers. My prospective contributors split into two camps: one wants to police the metaphors, holding that when we say smallpox "conquered" the Americas or that Tambora's 1815 eruption "reshaped" a hemisphere's politics, we have smuggled agency back into nature; the other camp replies that the real danger runs the other way — that centering non-human causes rehabilitates environmental determinism of the kind Jared Diamond is routinely accused of, in which nature does everything and people do nothing. Given the limited space, which open questions in this tangle most deserve the slots, and in what order? I care more about the reasoning than the list: for each question, say whether it is upstream of the others, whether it can be advanced by conceptual analysis and historical case studies rather than new empirical data, and what developments over the next few years would move it up or down the ranking. Please respond in American English.
