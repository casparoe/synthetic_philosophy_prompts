I'm doing a Masters in philosophy of mind and we've spent the last three weeks on Jaegwon Kim's causal exclusion argument, and honestly I keep flip-flopping on how much of a threat it really is to non-reductive physicalism. The issue as I understand it (tell me if I'm strawmanning anything): whenever a mental property M causes some behaviour, there's also a physical property P that realises M and, by the causal closure of the physical, is in itself causally sufficient for that behaviour. So either M's causing the behaviour just is the same causal process as P's (in which case mental causation seems to get absorbed rather than vindicated), or M is a genuinely seperate cause and we get systematic overdetermination of a sort nobody tolerates anywhere else, or M does no causal work at all. The standard non-reductive reply is that higher-level causes are fine because causal explanation can operate at different levels of grain — same as why it's okay to say the hurricane caused the damage even though some microphysical state did. But I can't tell whether that reply defeats the argument or just declines to play its game.
