quick context: i'm in a political philosophy seminar and next week we each have to defend a "position snapshot" on distributive justice while everyone else pokes at it, so I want to know if mine survives poking before I commit to it. here's roughly where I stand:

1. I think the leveling-down objection just kills telic egalitarianism. if you take from the better off and give to no one, nothing has gotten better in any respect whatsoever — so inequality as such can't be an intrinsic evil. that's decisive, not just a cost the egalitarian grudgingly pays.
2. so I'm a prioritarian: the moral value of an outcome is the weighted sum of individual wellbeing, with strictly concave weights, and what's bad is that people are badly off, full stop — not that they're worse off than others.
3. but I'm not a leximin person. absolute priority is crazy: a big enough benefit to a slightly better-off person beats a small benefit to a slightly worse-off person, and that's fine.
4. although — when someone is in genuinely desperate straits, destitution-level, I do think no benefit to the comfortably well off, however large, could outweigh even a small benefit to that person. within that extreme gap priority is absolute.
5. seperately, I think there can be fairness-based reasons to divide goods equally between people with equal claims (like two heirs splitting an estate) even when the weighted prioritarian value of an unequal split would be higher. I take this to be compatible with 1, since the fairness thing is about claims and how we may treat people, not about the value of outcomes.
6. and for a single person facing gambles that affect only her own life, all that matters is her expected benefit — the priority weighting obviously has no application there, since worse off than whom, exactly?

go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look like it, and rank the real tensions by severity. for each real one, say what would have to give and what the cheapest repair actually costs. don't just flag everything as equally broken — I need to know where the fault lines actually are before wednesday.
