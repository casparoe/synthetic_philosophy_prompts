Been reading the blogs for like a decade now and I remember when safe Pareto improvements first made the rounds — Caspar Oesterheld's stuff about delegating your bargaining power to conditional commitments, the whole "sure, we'll fight over the thing, but first we sign a deal so both of us are better off in expectation than if we'd just fought" trick. Here's what I've never been able to square: if two agents can always precommit to one of these schemes, doesn't that basically prove that conflict as such is just an irrationality? Like, any agent that ends up in an actual fight just failed to grab free money that was sitting on the table. Wars, lawsuits, price wars — all of them coordination failures rather than genuine conflicts of interest, since the SPI construction guarantees the Pareto improvement over the default no matter what.

And if that's right, I don't get why people still treat bargaining as this deep open problem. Once you've signed onto the SPI the surplus is locked in and the rest is just implementation details, right? My friend keeps saying I'm missing something — that the SPI somehow renames the bargaining problem instead of solving it — but I don't see it. The whole point of the construction is that it's safe, i.e. you get the improvement regardless of how the underlying dispute shakes out. So where's the flaw? Is it something about ex ante vs ex post, or is my friend just being contrarian?
