So I teach ethics and I'm putting together my position statement on supererogation for a faculty workshop thing, and before I embarrass myself I want to know if my commitments actually hang together. Here's roughly the package:

1. Supererogation is real: some acts are genuinely good to do while it remains permissible not to do them. Jumping on the grenade, donating a kidney to a stranger.

2. I reject any deep doing/allowing distinction — letting die and killing are morally on a par when the outcomes and costs to the agent are the same.

3. Duties of beneficence toward strangers are all imperfect duties: they're satisfied by some pattern of giving of the agent's own choosing, and no particular act of aid, at no particular time, is ever strictly required.

4. Morality is only moderately demanding — it's permissible to keep enough of your income for a decent life even when giving more would do more good.

5. My case judgments, which I'm fairly firm on: the passerby who won't ruin his expensive shoes to wade into the shallow pond and save the drowning child does something *wrong*; but the donor who gives enough to save exactly one child through an effective charity and then stops has done nothing wrong at all.

6. It's never apt to praise someone merely for doing what duty already required — praise is for the beyond-duty, that's kind of its whole job.

My worry is that when I line these up, 2 and 5 seem to collide (pond guy and distant donor look relevantly similar if the doing/allowing distinction does no work), and 3 seems to make the pond judgment impossible (a duty that never requires any specific act can't condemn one specific omission). But maybe I'm wrong about where the real pressure is, and maybe some of what looks like a collision isn't one. I honestly can't tell which of my six commitments is causing the trouble, or whether it's the same culprit twice.

So: audit the set. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection (I suspect there's at least one that only looks bad, maybe involving 1 and 3, or the latitude idea and supererogation generally, but don't let me steer you), rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and roughly what it costs me to give it up. If there's a single revision that cleans up the most, flag that too. Keep it under 600 words — I'd rather have the sharp version than the complete one.
