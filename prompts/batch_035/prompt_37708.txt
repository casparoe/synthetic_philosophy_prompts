I'm a third-year PhD student in political theory putting together a dissertation prospectus, and my committee is split on how much of it should engage social choice theory. My advisor treats Arrow's impossibility theorem as a settled result whose philosophical work is finished — something we cite and move past — while my second reader is convinced the interesting questions are all downstream of it and mostly unresolved. I have about two years before the dissertation needs to be defensible, a solid background in mathematical logic but not in mechanism design or economics proper, and a nagging sense that I can't yet distinguish the live problems in this area from the museum pieces.

Help me sort the open questions worth working on. Candidates I've been circling: whether Sen's move from ordinal rankings to interpersonal comparisons of well-being dissolves the impossibility or merely relocates it — the question of whether Arrow is a theorem about the informational parsimony of social choice rather than about democracy as such; how much the Gibbard–Satterthwaite result on strategic manipulation changes once deliberation before voting is taken seriously, or whether that just imports the aggregation problem into the deliberation itself; whether judgment aggregation and the discursive dilemma are a genuine generalization of Arrow or a formally similar but conceptually different problem that borrows its prestige; whether domain restrictions like single-peakedness are realistic structure or wishful thinking about the geometry of political disagreement; and whether mechanism design, by changing the game rather than the aggregation rule, is a real escape route or an evasion of the theorem's point. Rank these — or replace entries with something I've missed — and defend the ordering.

I care about the reasoning more than the list: which criteria are doing the work (importance for democratic theory, tractability for someone with logic rather than economics training, how neglected a question is, how many other questions depend on its answer), where the criteria pull against each other, and what evidence or developments in the next few years would move an item up or down. I'd also like your view on the meta-question my committee is really fighting over: is Arrow's theorem still generating research, or has it become mostly a citation and an excuse to sound rigorous?
