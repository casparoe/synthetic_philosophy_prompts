My friend is deep into the extended mind stuff (Clark and Chalmers, Otto and his notebook, all that) and over the years she's handed out verdicts on a whole bunch of cases about when something outside your skull counts as part of your mind. Her track record so far:

- the classic one: an older man whose memory is going, carries a notebook everywhere, writes down everything he needs to remember, consults it automatically without thinking, trusts it completely — she says the notebook entries literally are his beliefs, his mind extends into the notebook;
- the same man, but its the notes app on his phone instead of paper — also extended, same reasoning;
- a tourist who looks something up once in a guidebook she'll never open again — not extended, just a tool she used once;
- a woman who uses GPS for every drive but still keeps her own mental map and treats the app's suggested route as advice she'll sometimes override — not extended, because she doesnt automatically endorse it;
- and the one that surprised me: a bloke who's hopeless at remembering anything social, so his wife handles all of it — every appointment, every birthday, she reminds him and he never double-checks, treats her word as gospel. My friend says yes, his mind extends into her, or at least the remembering part of it does.

Now she's got new cases coming up and I want to predict her. The big one: a guy who's hopeless at arithmetic and uses his phone's calculator for literally every calculation in his life — shopping, splitting bills, work stuff — reflexively, never questions the result, and honestly couldnt do the sums in his head to save his life. What will she say about the calculator guy? And what about the fridge list — extended or not, and if so whose mind is it part of, hers, his, both, neither? It looks to me like more than one rule fits her track record equally well, and they come apart on at least one of these new cases.
