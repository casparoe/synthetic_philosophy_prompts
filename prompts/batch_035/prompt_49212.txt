You are a professional philosopher of biology with a strong background in the definitional debate about life, and I need your help building a diagnostic instrument rather than an explainer. I write a small blog read by a mixed audience of astrobiologists, origin-of-life chemists, and curious laypeople, and I want to run a "which definition of life do you actually hold?" quiz. My readers have all read the usual pop-science line that "biologists can't agree on what life is," but the quiz's whole point is to show that people who say "life is just chemistry that maintains itself" and people who say "life is anything that evolves" are not vaguely disagreeing — they come apart cleanly on specific cases, and so does everyone else in the debate.

Please write eight to ten questions, each presenting one concrete case with two or three answer options. The cases must be ones where the major candidate definitions genuinely diverge, and I'd like you to draw them from a spread that includes: a viable but dormant seed frozen for a decade; a worker ant (metabolizing but sterile); a mule; a virus; a prion; a self-replicating factory on the Moon assembled by robots from lunar regolith; a population of digital organisms in a simulation that mutate and undergo selection; and at least one case that clearly fails on every definition yet is engineered to tempt a "yes" answer (a candle flame or a hurricane is the classic bait for thermodynamic views, but feel free to do better). Phrase every question so a reader can answer it without knowing which school it is testing — the option labels should describe what the case is doing ("counts as alive, but only marginally," "alive the way a kidney cell is alive, not a free-living thing," etc.), never name the theory.

Then provide a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the positions: NASA-style evolutionary definitions ("a self-sustaining chemical system capable of Darwinian evolution"), Schrödinger-style thermodynamic or far-from-equilibrium accounts, autopoiesis and organism-first views, the gray-area or cluster-concept family in which "life" admits of degrees, and the deflationary position that "life" is a folk leftover that biology could discard as it discarded "monster" or "vital force." Before drafting the questions, please reason explicitly about the logical space: which pair of answers would jointly rule out every major position, which combinations no real theorist occupies, and whether that is because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored — and say so in the key. Please also flag honestly any pair of positions your instrument cannot separate with these cases (I suspect autopoiesis and some organism-first views will be hard to pry apart with any question short of a genuinely borderline one) rather than pretending the quiz distinguishes them.
