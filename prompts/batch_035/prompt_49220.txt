I design progression systems for live-service games — I'm the person who tunes the core loop, decides what counts as the grind versus actual play, worries about daily login streaks and why players churn the second the battle pass expires. Recently I stumbled onto Moritz Schlick's essay on the meaning of life (the Vienna Circle guy, wrote it in 1927) and his position seems to be roughly this: if you try to locate life's meaning in goals or purposes you get a regress — the purpose of the purpose of the purpose — so meaning can't be sitting out in front of you waiting to be achieved. It has to live in the activity itself, and the clearest case of that is play, the kind of absorbed, goal-indifferent engagement children have. Work, meanwhile, is at best a means, and the adult habit of treating everything instrumentally is exactly how meaning leaks out. He leans on Schiller here and also makes this whole thing about youth as a kind of template.

Restate his position in my actual vocabulary — core loops, intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation, reward schedules, win conditions, replayabilty, flow states, whatever else genuinely fits — as faithfully as you can, then give me the residue report: which parts translate cleanly, which parts I have to distort to shove into my terms, and which parts have no expression at all in game design language. The part I keep chewing on is that in my line of work the "intrinsic" pull is engineered. Somebody like me builds the loop precisely because players wouldn't keep logging in otherwise; we manufacture the absorption Schlick thinks just shows up when you're not instrumentalizing things. Does his view have anything to say about engineered play, or does the translation just break down there? And when something doesn't survive the translation — like the fact that a life has no designers, no patch notes, no next installment, and no option to reroll — should I read that as a gap in my vocabulary or as a sign his claim was never really about play in the first place and the game comparison is doing less work than it seems? I'd like a real answer on whether "life is play" survives contact with an industry whose whole job is faking it.
