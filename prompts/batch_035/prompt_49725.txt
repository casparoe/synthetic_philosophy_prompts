I've been defending something like Weber's protestant ethic thesis in a seminar and want to know what would actually beat it. To be clear I'm not holding the cartoon version (calvinism caused capitalism, full stop) but the middling one: that the anxiety induced by double predestination pushed certain people into a disciplined, this-worldly asceticism, and that this ethic was a real causal factor -- not just a reflection of emerging capitalism -- in where early capitalism took root and who flourished in it. My advisor keeps pushing the materialist stuff -- rational bookkeeping, capital accumulation, the fact that Catholic Italy and Flanders got a lot of it first -- and honestly some of that lands.

So: what would count against my version? Not the standard textbook objections, or at least not only those. What I want is a map of where the thesis is actually exposed versus where it's been insulated by qualifications, since I've noticed that whenever an objection comes up, some Weberian says "ah but Weber said elective affinity, not cause" and I can't tell if that's a rescue or a retreat. Specifically, help me with: (1) timing -- if Protestant regions were already more commerical before the Reformation (say because of trade routes or whatever), does that kill the causal claim or just move the mechanism somewhere else? (2) the literacy confound -- protestants read the Bible, literate people do better at commerce, how much of the correlation does this soak up and can anyone pry those apart? (3) Catholic industrializers -- Bavaria, France later, etc. -- awkward or fatal? (4) the psychological mechanism itself, salvation anxiety producing disciplined labor -- is there any way that's testable or is it just a story that fits after the fact? (5) the Huguenots and English dissenters being commercially dominant minorities -- this is the kind of case Weberians love, but does it do the work or could a materialist explain a persecuted market minority just as well? And (6) I've heard the take that the thesis really explains why capitalists felt *morally okay* about capitalism rather than why capitalism happened -- is that move a clarification of Weber's actual point or a concession dressed up as one?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which ones a committed Weberian would concede as counting against them versus wave off, and say whether the thing is ultimately falsifiable in a way that matters. I'd rather find out the thesis is sunk now than in my advisor's office.
