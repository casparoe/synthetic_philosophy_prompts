I'm on the ethics committee at our hospital, and a case coming to us Thursday has split the palliative team in two. A patient in his 70s has an LVAD — an implanted, battery-powered pump that has taken over almost all the work of his failing heart. Without it running, he'd die within minutes. He's decided he's done: he had a stroke last year, cant walk, needs total care, and says this isnt a life he wants. He's asked us to deactivate the device. Most of my committee treats this as routine: like withdrawing a ventilator, we're stopping a treatment the patient refuses, his underlying disease kills him, and that's letting die, not killing. But a minority, led by one of our cardiologists, says the ventilator analogy fails here: the pump isnt supporting his heart, it functionally IS his heart — the organ itself barely does anything — so deactivating it looks less like withdrawing a treatment and more like removing a working organ, which puts it on the killing side of the line, or at least somewhere murkier than our policy assumes. She isn't saying we must refuse; she's saying we should stop pretending it's exactly the same thing as extubation, and she wants the record to reflect that. On top of that, she wants the patient sedated before deactivation since death comes fast, while another member thinks sedating first is backwards — that we're then anesthetizing him solely in order to end him. Please draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the meeting: a concrete recommendation (say what we should do about deactivation, and about the sedation question, and note who should be in the room), the reasoning, and a genuinely fair treatment of the cardiologist's position — she has a real point about where the treatment/organ line sits, not just squeamishness. Two things: first, please mark which parts of the memo rest on settled practice in bioethics and which are genuinely contested, since the committee tends to treat anything printed as consensus; second, keep it under 500 words total — nobody on the committee reads more than that.
