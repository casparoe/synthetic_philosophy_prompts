Here's the case I want to open with, which is my own variant and the heart of the paper. Jones is deliberating about whether to lie under oath. Unbeknownst to him, a monitoring device is implanted that watches the neural buildup of his decision, driven by a statistical predictor: if the forecast says Jones is heading toward telling the truth, the device triggers stimulation that makes him lie anyway; if it says he's going to lie, it does nothing. In the actual sequence the device stays completely idle — Jones lies for his own selfish reasons, and nothing besides Jones plays any causal role. My move against the usual prior-sign objection (you know, the complaint that the intervener needs a reliable prior sign of how Jones will choose, and any sign reliable enough to trigger the intervention is already a cause that explains the action, so Frankfurt cases are covertly deterministic) is this: my predictor doesn't read a sign *in* Jones at all — it works from a population-level model that only makes probabilistic forecasts about people like him, and it's slow, so it could only kick in a moment before the action, after Jones's own deliberation has already done its work. So I claim the actual sequence is entirely Jones's own, and he couldn't have done otherwise, and he's still blameworthy. My opening challenge to you: the lie was Jones's from start to finish, the device never fired and nothing constrained him in the actual sequence — so what work is the alternative possibility you insist on supposed to be doing here? Where exactly does responsibility require it?
