I've been cataloguing my gut reactions about when something counts as having a disposition, and I'd like to know whether a single principle captures them. My grandmother's vase: it sits on a shelf with a sensor that fires an instant-hardening pulse the moment anything touches it, so it will never break, yet I still want to call it delicate. My uncle's old wiring: one wire is connected to nothing, but a safety system would connect it to the mains the instant anyone touched it, so it would shock you exactly like a live one, yet I still say it isn't live. My grandfather: any provocation and he would explode, but whenever someone needles him my aunt presses a whisky into his hand and he stays calm; I still call him bad-tempered. My phone's glass: it is the shattering kind, but a factory-bonded film spreads any blow so the glass itself never cracks; here I genuinely waver. A glassblower's ornament: when tapped it visibly starts to crack, but a polymer in the mix knits the crack shut before it can complete; I say it's fragile. Is there a principle that delivers all five? If not, tell me which of my verdicts to overturn and why -- and if the phone glass is where I should come off the fence, say which side. Then tell me what the principle predicts for three cases I haven't decided: the same vase but with a sensor fast enough only to beat light blows, so a hammer would shatter it before the pulse lands; the whisky replaced by a sedative that puts my grandfather to sleep before any anger begins; and the film replaced by a coating that only stops cracks that have already started from spreading.
