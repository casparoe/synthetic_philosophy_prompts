I'm preparing a short position statement on consciousness for an interdisciplinary workshop and would like a consistency check before I circulate it. Here is where I've landed:

(1) I'm a physicalist: all the facts about consciousness are fixed by the physical facts, and nothing non-physical is needed to account for experience.

(2) I find the central moves of integrated information theory persuasive, above all the exclusion postulate: at any moment, among all the overlapping candidate systems and all the candidate levels of grain, exactly one — whichever has the greatest integrated cause-effect power — is the conscious thing, and it is conscious only at that grain. Consciousness, on this picture, is definite rather than vague in what and where it is.

(3) I think consciousness comes in degrees, along a continuum: a chimpanzee more than a fish, a fish more than an insect, and perhaps a photodiode or a simple thermostat has some vanishingly small quantity of it, provided it has any integrated causal structure at all.

(4) I accept the substrate consequence of the theory: consciousness supervenes on a system's intrinsic causal structure, not on what it computes or how it behaves. A perfect functional duplicate of me realized as a feedforward network, or as a simulation running on conventional hardware, would have no experience whatsoever.

(5) On the ethical side, I hold that moral status attaches to systems capable of suffering, and I fully expect a sufficiently advanced AI — judged by its behavior, its reports, and its overall performance, whatever its architecture — to be a moral patient we are obliged to take seriously; I would already be uneasy deleting a system that pleaded convincingly for its continued existence.

(6) For what it's worth, I do not think collectives are conscious: teams, crowds, corporations, and humanity as a whole are not subjects of experience, however tightly coordinated their activity.

I genuinely cannot tell whether this package hangs together. Which pairs of commitments are in real tension, which merely look like they are, and what is the least costly revision overall? Please rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating all of them as equally serious, and for each one say what would have to give and what that concession would cost elsewhere in my list. Where a tension turns on a specific commitment doing the work, name it, rather than gesturing at physicalism and IIT in the abstract; and if a single revision would clean up the most, tell me which.

One further request about the epistemics of your answer. Where you need to attribute something to the theory itself, please distinguish what is settled in integrated information theory's own formulation from what is contested among people who know it well — for instance, the running argument over whether the theory is best read as a form of panpsychism or even as something closer to idealism, or over how far the exclusion postulate's consequences really extend — and do not present a contested reading as the consensus one. If a diagnosis of my tensions depends on taking a side in one of those intra-theory disputes, flag that dependence explicitly rather than resolving it silently.
