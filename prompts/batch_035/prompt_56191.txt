I'm compiling the case record of a structural realist I know well, and I'd like your read on how she'll rule on two new theory transitions. She is a defender of structural realism in the Worrall tradition — the view that what survives theory change is structure, not objects — and over many seminars I've gathered her verdicts on whether the predecessor theory's structure was "retained" in each of the following transitions:

1. Fresnel's ether-based wave theory of light to Maxwell's electrodynamics: retained. The diffraction equations carried over essentially intact; only the ether was discarded.
2. Phlogiston theory to Lavoisier's oxygen chemistry: not retained, but she insists this is no counterexample, because phlogiston theory's genuine predictive successes were too meager for anyone to have expected retention in the first place.
3. Caloric theory to thermodynamics and the kinetic theory: retained. Fourier's heat-conduction equation, developed within the caloric framework, survived essentially untouched; the caloric fluid did not.
4. Lorentz's ether electrodynamics to special relativity: retained. The transformation equations and the electrodynamics survive; the ether's ontological weight does not.
5. Newtonian gravity to general relativity: retained, on the strength of the Newtonian limit — the recovery of inverse-square behavior at low velocities and weak fields.

The new cases, on which I need predictions:

Case A: Kepler's three laws to Newtonian celestial mechanics. The ellipse survives only for the idealized two-body problem; the third law gets corrected for the finite masses of the planets; the area law is reinterpreted as a consequence of angular momentum conservation; and Kepler's own Pythagorean machinery — the nested regular solids — is of course abandoned. Retained or not?

Case B: Ptolemaic astronomy to the heliocentric succession running from Copernicus through Kepler. The geocentric models' confirmed predictions — apparent angular positions, retrograde loops, eclipse timings within the precision of the era — were preserved by the successors, and one can even argue the epicyclic machinery is formally reconstructible as something like a Fourier decomposition of apparent motion; yet deferents, epicycles, and equants were abandoned as representational apparatus. Retained or not? Note that her phlogiston escape hatch — the predecessor wasn't successful enough — seems unavailable here, since Ptolemaic astronomy was a predictively serious research program.
