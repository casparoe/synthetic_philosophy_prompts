ok so this has been bugging me since english class and I need to know if theres a word for it. we just finished a novel where the main character dies at the end and like half the class was crying, me included. afterwards I got to thinking about how weird my feelings about it actually are.

first off, the sadness was real, it messed up my whole evening. but I never for a second wanted to *do* anything about it. when my dog was sick last year I couldn't sit still, I kept checking on him, couldn't focus on anything. but when a character dies the sad feeling just kinda sits there. no urge to warn her, not even a thought like "I hope the actress is ok". so it's like real sadness with the "do something about it" part just missing?

but heres the part that really confuses me. our teacher asked if we'd change the ending if we could, and I realized I'd honestly be kind of offended if the author had let her live. I want her to die! well, not want-want, but a version where she survives would be a worse book and I'd feel cheated reading it. so I'm pitying this person and at the same time I'm glad about the exact thing I'm pitying her for. and it's not like the two feelings cancel out, they both feel completely real at the same time.

does philosophy have a name for this setup? my cousin mentioned something called the "paradox of fiction" but when I looked it up it seemed to be about whether you can even have real emotions for fake people, and that's not really my problem — I'm fine accepting the emotion is real. my problem is that it points in two directions at once, one at the character and one at the story itself. is there an established concept for the two-directions thing specifically, and if there is, how is it different from the general paradox of fiction? and if there isn't, can you coin a term for it with an actual definition, and tell me what counts as a borderline case (like, does it still apply when a villain suffers, since then I kind of want the suffering AND approve of it being in the story?). I don't want just a label, I want to understand what exactly the phenomenon is and where its edges are.
