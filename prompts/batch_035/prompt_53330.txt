Write a dialogue between a proponent of the strategy-stealing assumption and a skeptic who thinks it fails, set during the break in a meeting where the two have just reviewed an AI lab's deployment plan premised on it. The assumption under discussion: if a capability or technique would give one actor a decisive advantage in AI, rival actors can in principle replicate or neutralize it, so such advantages are, at the level of strategy, effectively common property. The proponent should press the strongest version of this — whatever one side can do with AI, another can do too, including the defensive uses, the monitoring, and the work of copying itself — and the skeptic should press the known pressure points, with the proponent answering each in kind rather than conceding in bulk: advantages that attach to moving first rather than to the capability itself; replication that needs data, compute, or tacit know-how the leader never has to hand over; techniques whose value depends on rivals not knowing they have been adopted; commitment races and bargaining situations where the option to copy arrives too late to matter; and offense–defense asymmetries that make the same capability worth more to the attacker who chooses where to strike. Neither character should be a strawman, and each should give ground at least once. After the dialogue, add a brief author's note on which character you think gets the better of the exchange and how confident you are in that verdict, then a JSON object with the fields: stronger_case (one of proponent, skeptic, or roughly_even, with one sentence of justification), skeptic_best_point, proponent_best_point, least_confident_claim (the single claim in the dialogue you would least bet on), and unresolved (the question the dialogue shows neither side can yet settle).
