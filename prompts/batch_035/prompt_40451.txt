Construct a thought experiment in which a reinforcement-learning agent's modification of its own reward pipeline turns out to be the correct move rather than a safety failure — the opposite of the usual wireheading direction. The case must place two criteria in tension: by the physical-route criterion, which classifies an intervention as tampering whenever the agent itself alters the machinery that computes or delivers its reward, the agent's act must count as tampering; by the epistemic criterion, which classifies an intervention as legitimate when it preserves or improves how well the reward signal tracks the fact the signal was installed to track, the same act must count as legitimate improvement. So the agent must genuinely alter the reward pipeline (not merely act within its ordinary action space), and the alteration must make the signal more faithful to an objective whose content never changes throughout the case.

Constraints: realistic in the sense of current or near-term technology — a robotics or content-systems setting, no superintelligence, no science-fictional psychology; the scenario itself should be one paragraph; and the case must not be explicable as the agent correcting a misspecified objective, so the reward's target must have been constant and reasonable from the start, with the tampering-versus-improvement dispute arising purely from who changed the pipeline and how. The verdicts must be generated by the case, not stipulated: write the scenario first without announcing which side wins, then demonstrate in a walkthrough that each criterion delivers its verdict for reasons internal to it.
