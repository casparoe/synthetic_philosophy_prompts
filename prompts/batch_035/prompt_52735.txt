I'm a machine-learning researcher by training and inclination: my working vocabulary consists of reward functions, policies, world models, embedding spaces, interpretability probes, and agents understood as optimisers. My philosophical training is close to nil. Until recently this caused me no trouble, but a situation has arisen that forces the question. A close relative has begun cognitive behavioural therapy, and as best I can tell the entire treatment rests on the idea that her distress is caused by her beliefs — that is, on the assumption that there are discrete, identifiable, revisable propositional attitudes inside her which a therapist can locate, challenge, and replace with better ones. Sitting in the waiting room last week, I realised I could not say whether my professional vocabulary commits me to regarding that picture as true, as false, or as a convenient fiction, and it has been bothering me since.

So here is my request. Take the philosophical debate between eliminative materialism about beliefs and desires and its opponents — the eliminativist claim that propositional attitudes are posits of a defective folk theory that a mature cognitive science will simply discard, and the realist replies that they are indispensable for prediction, explanation, and mutual understanding — and restate both sides as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use. Beliefs as learned representations inside a world model; desires as something in the vicinity of reward functions or learned objectives; therapy as targeted retraining or fine-tuning; misrepresentation as error in the learned model; irrationality as, perhaps, a mismatch between the objective actually being optimised and the objective the designer intended. Do this honestly: don't quietly import assumptions from the optimisation-and-representation vocabulary and present them as the philosophers' claims.

Then give me the residue report, and treat it as the main deliverable. Which parts of the debate translate cleanly? Which translate only with distortion, and what exactly gets bent out of shape? Which parts have no expression at all in my vocabulary?

I have three candidates for the residue, though please don't feel bound to them. First, the normative dimension: beliefs and desires seem subject to rules of rationality — they can be inconsistent, they ought to fit the evidence — in a way that parameters and loss surfaces are not; my vocabulary has no 'ought'. Second, discreteness and revisability: CBT appears to trade in attitudes as countable, individually addressable objects, whereas everything I know about learned representations suggests they are distributed, entangled, and not addressable one at a time — yet the therapy seems to work. Third, Dennett's instrumental stance, which looks as though it should translate into talk of models at different levels of abstraction, though I suspect something is lost in that rendering, possibly the question of whether the patterns are really there.

Finally, give me your verdict on the residue itself. Does it show that my vocabulary is missing something the folk picture correctly tracks, or that the folk picture was never as clear as it seemed, so that nothing of substance fails to survive the translation? And if it is the latter, what should I conclude is actually happening when the therapy works?
