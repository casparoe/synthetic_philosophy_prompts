I help run an online community for people interested in mind uploading, and every few months we get the same fight where someone posts "obviously the upload is just a copy" and someone else posts "obviously it's you, the pattern is what matters" and then 300 comments happen and nobody learns anything. I want to cut through this with a proper "which view do you actually hold?" decision tree, pinned at the top of the sub, so people can find their leaf first and argue second.

Here's what I want. Build the tree from the cases, not from the labels. Every branch node should be a concrete thought experiment someone can answer honestly without knowing which position it feeds into. Use at least these cases, in roughly this order: (1) your neurons get replaced one at a time with functionally identical silicon chips while you stay awake, a full biological-to-artificial conversion by degree; (2) a destructive scan where the brain is destroyed as it's read; (3) a nondestructive scan where you're alive and well and the upload already running somewhere else; (4) two simultaneous uploads made from the same scan, running in parallel; (5) a destructive scan where, purely for budget reasons, the upload won't be started until 40 years after your death. For at least a couple of the cases, ask the question twice in different forms — once as "is it the same person?" and once as "would it be rational for someone facing death to pay for this?" — because I've noticed people give different answers to those, and I suspect that gap is doing real work in the argument.

The leaves should include at least these: the pattern/functionalist view that the upload is you in the straightforward sense; the psychological-continuity view that only survives when there's no branching, so the single nondestructive upload is you but the parallel pair isn't; the animalist or biological-continuity view that you die in every destructive scenario, gradual replacement included; the "no fact of the matter" deflationary view; and the view that says identity is the wrong question entirely, so the upload may not be "you" but uploading is still rational because what you have reason to care about survives. If there are other genuinely distinct leaves in the logical space, add them. Also please mark which combinations of branch answers are unoccupied in the literature as far as you know, and say whether each empty cell is empty because the view is incoherent, because it's just unattractive, or because nobody's bothered. And be honest about pairs of positions the tree can't actually separate — like whether the gradual-replacement case really distinguishes animalism from a conservative psychological-continuity view, or whether it's principled to accept the gradual case but reject every scan.

One more thing: the audience is smart laypeople, not philosophers, so keep the language plain at the branch nodes even if the leaves carry technical names. And where something in this debate is genuinely unsettled — like whether the "identity doesn't matter" move actually works, or whether branching cases really do force the no-branching clause — flag that explicitly instead of just presenting one side as the answer, because if I pin something that presents a contested view as consensus, the same 300-comment fight will happen but now it's my fault. Around 12 to 15 branch nodes and however many leaves fall out of that is about the size I want.
