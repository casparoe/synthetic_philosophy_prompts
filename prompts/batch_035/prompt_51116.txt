ok so this might be a weird one but ive been turning it over for months. a while back i read this guy douglas hofstadter who has this idea (in one of his books, dont ask me which) that when you know someone really well you kind of build a rough copy of them inside your own head, so a persons "I" isn't all contained in their own skull — chunks of you are running in everyone who knows you. he even talks about his wife dying and continuing to exist in him, or two old friends being partly each other. i'm probably butchering it but thats the gist.

meanwhile the real world: facebook has these "memorialized accounts." when someone dies, the profile gets a "remembering [name]" banner, it stops showing up in ads and birthday reminders (and people-you-may-know i think), nobody can log in as them anymore, and before dying the person can name a "legacy contact" — someone who afterwards can pin a post to the profile and answer incoming friend requests, but can NOT log into the account or read their messages.

so heres whats bugging me: what does that setup actually presuppose about what a person IS? the rules make total sense if the account is basically a gravestone — you can tend a grave, you don't climb into it and speak from it. but they also kind of make sense if you think the dead person still has interests of their own (keeping the messages private is their interest, not the mourners'). and hofstadters version — the person being partly distributed across the survivors — seems to be almost nowhere in the rules, even though letting friends keep posting "remember when" comments on the memorial page is arguably feeding exactly that distribution. so is the implicit view coherent, or does it just flip between "the account is the deceased's body/property" and "the account is a mourning space for the living" whenever convenient? and what would the features look like if the engineers had taken the distributed-self thing seriously — some way for the version of you in your friends heads to keep updating each other, the comment section as you running on borrowed hardware? i realize how creepy that sounds and i'm honestly not sure i'd want it, so tell me if thats a me problem or a real tension in the idea itself.

give me the analysis in whatever structure works for the argument, and then, after the piece, a small JSON object with these fields: "implicit_theory" (one sentence: the view of the person the current rules make the most sense under), "ruled_out" (list: rival views of personal identity the rules quietly exclude), "underdetermined" (list: aspects of the practice that several different theories would all be fine with, so they can't count as evidence for any one of them), and "if_distributed_self_true" (2-3 sentences on what would have to change in the feature set).
