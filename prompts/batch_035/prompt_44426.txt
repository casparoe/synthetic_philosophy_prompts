I'm basically a hedonist about wellbeing: pleasure is the only thing that's good for you for it's own sake, pain the only bad. Not the crude "eat candy all day" version — I just think that at bottom, all that ultimately matters for how your life goes for you is how it feels from the inside.

The objection I keep hitting is the heterogeneity problem, and I want to actually know how exposed my view is, not just rehearse the standard exchange. The version I care about goes: the things we call pleasures — the taste of cold water after a long run, getting absorbed in a proof, the relief when the biopsy comes back clear, schadenfreude — seem to share no common felt quality. So either there's some hidden thread running through all of them, or hedonism lumps wildly different things under one word and calls them a single good. The reply I've come across is that the common element isn't a sensation at all: pleasure is an attitude, roughly being pleased that things are a certain way, and every case is an instance of that. But I suspect this buys uniformity at the cost of detaching the view from anything recognizably pleasant — a masochist in agony could count as doing great if he's "pleased that" he's suffering? And when the hedonist then adds that the attitude has to be appropriate or aimed at the right things, it starts to look like goodness is leaking in from somewhere other than pleasure.

So: tell me what would count against my view. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward. Which of them would a committed hedonist themself concede as genuinely counting against hedonism (I mean ones actual hedonists have conceded, not just what critics say), and which would they wave off? Also, does hedonism predict anything that its rivals don't, so that it's genuinely risking something rather than just sitting there? And last: has the view been protected by qualification after qualification to the point where nothing could ever count against it — and if so, is that disqualifying for a view of this kind, or is it fine?
