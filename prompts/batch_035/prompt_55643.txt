Explain what antirepresentationalism amounts to for Rorty, what role the Sellarsian and Quinean arguments play in getting him there, and then evaluate the strongest objection to his view that you can construct: if no belief represents the world, what becomes of the difference between getting things right and merely going along with what one's community accepts?"

The student's answer, transcribed verbatim:

Rorty's target in Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature is the picture of the mind as a great mirror that reflects reality, with knowledge understood as the accuracy of the reflection and philosophy as the inspector of the mirror's surface. He assembles his attack from two main arguments. From Sellars he takes the attack on the Myth of the Given: if there are no intrinsically compelling, non-conceptual givens that anchor empirical knowledge, then the idea of a belief standing in a purely causal-but-also-justificatory relation to the world loses its grip. From Quine he takes the attack on the analytic/synthetic distinction: once meaning and truth are holistic, there is no standpoint outside all theories from which to compare a theory with reality-as-it-is-in-itself. Put together, these dissolve the representationalist picture: there is no interface between scheme and content, so nothing for a belief to be accurate to. This is why Rorty calls his position a coherence view rather than a correspondence view: truth is warranted assertibility, i.e., what one's peers let one get away with saying. Davidson made the same point when he argued that the scheme/content dualism is the third dogma of empiricism and that we cannot make sense of a belief failing to fit the world, so truth is just coherence within a comprehensive web.

The strongest objection, I think, is that Rorty has no account of error. If truth is what my community accepts, then the pre-Copernican community was right to say the sun goes around the earth, since that was warranted by their lights, and Copernicus was wrong until enough people came around. This consequence seems absurd: the dinosaurs went extinct before there were any communities at all, so there were dinosaur-related truths before there was anyone to be warranted in asserting them. Rorty can bite the bullet here, since for him the question of whether the community is in touch with reality is just the representationalist question over again. But biting the bullet on this scale makes his view hard to distinguish from relativism, which he officially rejects, and it deprives him of the resources to say that science discovers rather than creates — a concession he cannot afford if his beloved Dewey is to be vindicated. So the objection stands: antirepresentationalism collapses into community relativism unless Rorty can restore a non-representational notion of the world pushing back on us, and it is not clear he can, since he also rejects causal theories of reference and of knowledge on the ground that causation is just another epistemic relation.
