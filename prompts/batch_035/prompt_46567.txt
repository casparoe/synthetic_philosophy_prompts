I am a novelist preparing a book whose narrator is a moderately reliable obsessive who apes Nietzsche's table, lifestyle, and self-diagnoses, and my agent has just learned I have joined a small private club of eleven members that publishes, annually, a pamphlet of "regimens for the improvement of body and spirit." The club's editor wants to include my narrator's defense of the Nietzschean rules — the insistent claim that German cooking caused his long misery and Piedmontese food his recovery; that alcohol and Christianity are the twin poisons of Europe; that strong coffee and tea spread darkness; that one should eat light and rarely, sleep little, and distrust every doctor — but three other members have threatened to resign, calling the regimen dangerous crankery being laundered through literary prestige. The editor asks each contributor to submit a written opinion for the pamphlet's editorial board, recommending whether to publish, and the stakes are genuine to the club: it prides itself on never printing anything its members cannot personally vouch for, and my narrator's contribution would break that tradition, since no one else proposes to eat as he does.

Draft the opinion I should submit. It should recommend a concrete course of action (publish in full, publish with a counterstatement, reject, or something better you devise), and it must engage the philosophical substance rather than only the etiquette: is Nietzsche's advice in the "Why I Am So Clever" vein best read as a serious proto-biohacking claim about the mind depending on the metabolism, as self-medicating testimony from a chronically ill man generalizing his symptoms to mankind, or as an extended performance whose point is not the diet at all but the demand that each person treat their own body as an experiment no physician can run for them? Address honestly the strongest consideration on the losing side — that if the regimen is performance all the way down, publishing it as a "regimen" precisely destroys its meaning, and that Nietzsche himself ties the advice to a doctrine of individual variation which any pamphlet's general publication betrays. I should add that my narrator, predictably, insists that any disclaimer be addressed to "sluggish bowels and not to the reader," which the board will not allow; your recommendation should tell me what to do with him too.
