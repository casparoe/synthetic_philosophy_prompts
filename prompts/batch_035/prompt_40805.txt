My thesis is that both halves share a mistake: they treat the alignment tax as a single quantity that is either competitively fatal or eventually zero, when in fact the costs of alignment decompose into fixed development costs (which amortize across deployments and may become selling points), per-unit runtime costs (which are genuinely taxed), and costs of forgone capabilities (which are what actually drive racing pressure, and which rarely get measured separately).
