I teach a graduate seminar that will devote its closing month to the question of why some nations are rich and others poor, and I want the final sessions to map where the live issues actually are, rather than rehashing the institutions-versus-geography-versus-culture debate. So frame the question for me as a problem of causal inference from history: we have a single, unrepeatable run of the process, no counterfactual populations, and candidate causes that are entangled with one another over centuries. Given that setup, which open questions in this area matter most, and why? I care less about the list than about the reasoning behind it: for each question you place near the top, say what makes it upstream of the others, whether it is tractable at all without controlled experiments, and what kind of evidence — natural experiments, within-country variation over time, comparisons across otherwise similar polities — could in principle settle or at least move it. Please also keep two registers distinct throughout: which claims are reasonably settled among economic historians and philosophers of science who work on these issues, and which remain genuinely contested, so that I do not end up presenting a live position to my students as if it were consensus.
