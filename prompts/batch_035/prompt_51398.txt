So a bit of background on me first: I'm a retired geochemist (37 years, mostly isotope work, so I'm used to arguing about measurement error and not much else), and since retiring I've fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole. The evening class I go to has two guys who've spent a year arguing about punishment and getting nowhere. One is a pretty hard retributivist — desert comes first, punishing the guilty is intrinsically required, not just useful. The other is a consequentialist — it's all forward-looking, deterrence and incapacitation, and he thinks talk of "desert" is either confused or secretly consequentialist. They've started repeating themselves, so I've been reading about adversarial collaborations and offered to draft the joint statement for them, on the condition that neither side gets to veto wording that isn't actually slanted.

Here's what I want, and please be strict about it:

First, a numbered section of claims both men could actually sign. No padding — I'll be cross if it includes stuff like "punishment is a serious matter requiring justification." I want substantive agreements: e.g. that the state intentionally harming a convict needs justification, and that "causing hardship" and "punishing" aren't the same act-type (the difference between a punishment and, say, quarantining someone, or withholding a privilege for administrative reasons); that at least some punishments actually inflicted in real systems are too harsh for either of their theories; that the question "what does this person deserve?" and the question "what effects will this sentence have?" are distinct questions that can come apart. If you can find more agreements like that — especially about what counts as a punishment being imposed *as* punishment — even better.

Then a disagreements table. Each row should be stated twice: once in wording the retributivist would sign, once in wording the consequentialist would sign. Same row, two phrasings, and neither phrasing may smuggle in a win for its own side. I want to be able to show each guy his column and have him nod.

Then the cruxes, and for each crux a test case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. I have a case I invented for one crux — tell me if you think it's unfair or loaded. Call it the sealed sentence: guilt is certain; the trial is in camera and the record sealed, so nobody who could be deterred will ever learn of the sentence; the offender has a degenerative condition guaranteeing he'll never offend again; no rehabilitation is possible or needed. So there's no deterrence channel, no incapacitation channel, no rehabilitation channel — the only effects of punishing are the suffering itself and the cost to the state. My prediction is the retributivist says the state should punish anyway (perhaps at reduced severity), and the consequentialist says punishing is all cost. Write down what each man would say the state should do, and also whether each side concedes that the case bears on the crux rather than just being a freak case that their theory can bracket.

I also want a crux about whether part of their dispute is verbal — whether saying "he deserves punishment" is supposed to state a reason the institution must respond to, or merely characterizes the wrong in a certain tone of voice — and one short paragraph at the end saying which parts of the dispute, if any, are about words and why. And if you can identify a genuinely empirical crux — say, how much sentencing severity actually deters, or how much the public's compliance depends on sentences tracking perceived desert — include it too, with each side's prediction and what evidence they'd each accept as telling against them.

One more thing: I've noticed both of them use "we punish because he deserves it" and "he deserves it because we punish" style arguments without flagging which direction they're arguing. If you see circularity like that in the standard debate, please point at it. And keep the whole thing tight — these guys will only read something that fits on a few pages. I'd rather have six sharp agreed claims than twenty mushy ones.
