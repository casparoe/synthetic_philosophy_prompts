Background so you know where I'm coming from: I'm a retired evolutionary biologist. Population genetics mostly, drosophila, some later work on virulence evolution, and I did my time on the grant treadmill so I've seen every fashionable analogy sweep through a department. Now that I'm retired I'm finally reading philosophy properly (Dennett mostly, don't judge me) and something keeps snagging that I want sorted out.

It's the meme business. Dawkins coins the word back in the 70s, fine, cute endpaper to the gene's eye view. But now everyone from my grandaughter to podcast hosts to serious people worried about misinformation uses it, and as far as I can tell there are two analogies getting blended into one mush. The gene one: a meme is a unit of culture that replicates with variation and gets selected, so culture is literally another Darwinian system. The virus one: ideas "infect" you and spread through social networks like an epidemic, and you're basically a passive host. And the people using it always want it to do the same job, which is to conclude that ideas don't win because they're true or argued well -- they win because they're catchy, same way a rhinovirus wins by being transmissible. So rational evaluation is epiphenomenal, bad ideas spread like outbreaks, end of story.

Here's my problem. Part of me says as a Darwinian I should love this -- selection applied outside biology, what's not to like. Another part of me remembers the 90s, when half my department was sure memetics was the next big thing, and then it just sort of fizzled, and I never understood exactly why it fizzled. Was it wrong or just unfashionable? And now the analogy is back and doing heavier lifting than before.

So audit it for me the way I'd audit a grad student's model. Give me the correspondence table: what plays the role of the gene, the phenotype, fitness, mutation, drift, the species barrier (does an idea have one? can it jump between "populations" of experts and laypeople?). Then show me where the mapping genuinely breaks -- I suspect fidelity of copying is a big one, and I want to know if there's a decent answer to the "what exactly is the replicator" question, since my gene version at least had a clean molecular answer. Then the part I care about most: which of the breakages actually matter for the conclusion people want from this (that beliefs spread purely parasitically, evaluation be damned), and which are just pedantic? And is there a better analogy? I keep half-suspecting the right one is less like a virus and more like a cultivar -- something we breed on purpose, with deliberate selection -- but I can't get that straight either, since plenty of beliefs really do seem to spread against our interests. Set me straight. Typos forgiven, mine included.
