I'm an undergraduate taking a seminar on social choice theory, and we just finished a unit on the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem. At a panel discussion on electoral reform that the department hosted this week, one of the political scientists on the panel dismissed the entire question of voting method design with the remark that "every voting system can be gamed," adding that since strategic misrepresentation is universal, arguing over ranked choice versus plurality versus approval voting is rearranging deck chairs. The moderator moved on and everyone nodded, but it struck me that this one sentence could be expressing several quite different claims, and that the theorem we studied licenses at most one of them.

Could you lay out the distinct precise readings the slogan could be carrying? Off the top of my head I can imagine at least these: (1) a bare existence claim that for any voting rule satisfying certain conditions — determinism, at least three alternatives, every alternative winnable, no dictator — there exists some preference profile and some voter who benefits from misreporting; (2) a frequency claim that such manipulation opportunities arise commonly in realistic electorates rather than at contrived profiles; (3) an in-principle claim that nothing whatsoever escapes, including mechanisms that use randomization, monetary transfers, or restricted preference domains like single-peakedness; (4) an equivalence claim that all manipulable methods are equally manipulable, so the choice among them is a wash; and (5) a normative claim that manipulability is disqualifying in the first place, so that no method survives. There may be readings I'm missing, and I suspect some of these are true, some are substantive but open, and some are straightforwardly false.

For each reading, please tell me: is it true as stated; is it actually what the Gibbard–Satterthwaite theorem, or any related result you'd want to invoke, establishes; and — crucially — does it support the dismissive use the slogan was put to at the panel? I'd also like you to flag explicitly what the theorem is silent about, since I want to say precisely at next week's seminar which parts of the panelist's remark were licensed and which were hand-waving. Please format the answer as a numbered list of readings, each with a verdict, that I can bring to class.
