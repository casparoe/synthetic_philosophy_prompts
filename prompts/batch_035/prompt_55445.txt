I run a two-hour evening workshop at an AI-safety bootcamp for engineers with no philosophy background, and my session is on Dennett's ladder of kinds of minds — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian creatures. The audience already thinks in terms of training loops, reward models, and agents that plan, so they'll be pattern-matching the whole time; I want a plan for the session, not the content itself.

Specifically, I want you to tell me the order the pieces must go in and why the sequence can't be shuffled — in particular, why the Darwinian level has to be established as a baseline of competence-without-learning before the Skinnerian level makes sense, since otherwise they'll hear "trial and error" as one thing rather than two loci of generate-and-test. Then the carrying example: I need a single scenario that can be escalated through all four rungs without changing the subject — foraging in a changing environment, predator evasion, whatever you judge best — and I want you to say what that example must contain and what it must be kept free of (I suspect it must not involve anything language-like too early, or the engineers will jump straight to thinking about tokens and ruin the ladder's dependence structure).

Then the misconception map. The ones I anticipate, roughly in the order they'll arrive: (1) that the ladder is a phylogenetic ranking of species rather than a taxonomy of design refinements that can be stacked or relocated onto non-living substrates — this one is fatal for this audience because their whole interest is where artificial systems sit; (2) that Skinnerian learning is just "Darwinian trial and error but faster," missing that what changes is the locus where variation-and-selection happens and hence what information accumulates over an individual lifetime; (3) that Popperian creatures are conscious deliberators rather than anything that can run an offline simulation of a candidate action and veto the bad ones before acting; and (4) that a large language model trained with RLHF is straightforwardly Gregorian, when the more uncomfortable suggestion is that it might be a Popperian or even Skinnerian system wearing Gregorian clothing — the cultural mind-tools in its training data doing the thinking rather than any competence of its own. For each, tell me at what point in the sequence it's cheapest to defuse it and what the defusing move is, and tell me honestly if you think (4) is too hot for hour one and should be seeded earlier as an open thread instead of resolved.

Finally, the check: one question I can pose at the end — something in the neighborhood of asking them to place a chess engine that runs deep lookahead search, or a honeybee colony, on the ladder and defend the placement — that would show whether they understood the ladder as a claim about where design information is stored and consumed rather than as a ranking of cleverness. And tell me what I should openly refuse to bring in at this level, and how to say so without making the audience feel fobbed off.
