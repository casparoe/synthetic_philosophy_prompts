For our next meeting I want to run a game in which the club splits into six independent "member states," each with its own budget of points and its own goals, and a rotating "Council" that can propose policies applying to the whole group. The catch is a ratchet: once a state accepts a policy, it cannot opt back out on its own — exit requires persuading everyone else, and pooled policies give everyone bonus points, so there's always a reason to accept the next centralization and never a cheap moment to reverse it. Design the complete game for me: the rules and turn structure, the roles (Council president, national representatives, and "domestic voters" each representative must periodically answer to), and a scoring scheme in which handing decisions to the center buys real efficiency gains while steadily increasing a separate "voter resentment" score that representatives can only discharge by public defiance or by losing office. I want the students to run into the trade-off themselves — that the very rules that make the union effective also make it feel distant and hard to hold anyone accountable for — before I name sovereignty pooling, the democratic deficit, or ever-closer union in the debrief. The debrief should map specific moments from the play onto those ideas, and I'd also like an honest note on what the game predictably gets wrong the first time I run it, and how to handle it.
