i've been reading about the future-like-ours argument -- the one that says what makes killing wrong, when it is wrong, is that it deprives the victim of a future like ours: experiences, projects, enjoyments, all cut off. and since a foetus has such a future ahead of it, killing it is seriously wrong. the classic objection everyone raises is the contraception problem: if depriving something of a future-like-ours is what makes killing wrong, then contraception -- or even just a couple deciding not to have sex on a given tuesday night -- deprives some *possible* future person of their future too, which seems obviously absurd. the standard reply is that in the contraception case there's no determinate victim, no particular individual who is deprived, so nothing wrong has happened.

ok, with that background: write a satirical piece, roughly 600-700 words, in the form of a deadpan government white paper / policy announcement from a fictional "Ministry of Possible Persons" (or invent a similar name) in a country that has decided to take the future-like-ours logic completely at face value and start protecting all futures of value, not just the ones that have gotten around to existing yet. Bureaucratic register, numbered sections, maybe a short foreward from the minister.

Programmes to include (or invent your own in the same spirit): a monthly "future protection quota" requiring couples of childbearing age to attempt conception, on pain of a small fine indexed to the estimated value of the future forgone; a bereavement allowance payable at each menstruation; a national day of mourning for the possible persons lost to abstinence; and crucially, assigning each merely possible future person a provisional tax reference number, so that the ministry has a determinate victim on file for every deprivation. that last one is them answering the contraception objection in the worst possible way, and the comedy should come from how proud they are of it.

The humour needs to come from actually pressing on the real philosophical pressure points -- the determinate victim issue, how on earth you individuate possible people (the ministry should have some handwavy procedure for deciding *which* possible person died when a given egg went unfertilised), whether the value lives in the future itself or in the person connected to it -- not just from making civil servants sound stuffy. I want someone who has taught this argument to come away thinking the satire is fair, that its target is the logic and not a strawman. Somewhere include a dissenting footnote from the ministry's own ethics board, which keeps insisting that you cant wrong someone who never exists, and is told to refile its concerns in triplicate. Also work in at least one reference to the trouble caused by citizens who keep bringing up the violinist (you know, the famous case of waking up surgically attached to a stranger who needs your body for nine months to survive) every time the ministry proposes compelling anyone to gestate for the sake of a future.

End with the minister noting, with quiet sadness, that the policy polls badly with literally everyone who currently exists, and directing staff to look into representation for the constituency that doesn't.

Please write the answer in British English (humour, colour, organise, etc.), and keep it deadpan throughout -- no winking at the reader. the comedy should just be the beauracracy taking the argument seriously and following it wherever it leads.
