The exam question was: "Bostrom's Presumptuous Philosopher case: two rival theories of everything, T1 and T2, get equal prior credence on all the non-anthropic evidence; T1 says the world contains a trillion trillion observers, T2 a trillion times more than that. Before the physicists run an expensive experiment that would settle the matter, a philosopher announces the experiment is pointless -- he can already tell you T2 is about a trillion times more likely than T1. Explain how the Self-Sampling Assumption and the Self-Indication Assumption each treat this case, what each costs you elsewhere, and state which assumption is standardly taken to underwrite the Doomsday Argument."

And the student wrote: "SIA says: treat your own existence as evidence. Finding that you exist is more likely in worlds with more observers, so each hypothesis gets weighted in proportion to how many observers it contains. With T1 and T2 starting even and T2 containing a trillion times more observers, the philosopher's trillion-to-one claim for T2 is exactly what SIA delivers. The cost: you become presumptuous -- you accept the philosopher's reasoning and skip the experiment, and the same move favors any theory that says the world is bigger, no experiment needed. That seems like philosophy bullying physics.

SSA says: reason as if you were a random sample from the observers in your theory. On SSA you condition on being one of the observers that exist, so you get no trillion-to-one boost here -- but note SSA doesn't quite leave you at the prior either: since you're guarenteed to exist under both theories, your existence still favors T2 a little, proportional to how much likelier the bigger world makes it that you'd be among its observers at all. So SSA is presumptuous too, just much less so, and in the same direction.

The Doomsday Argument is the SIA view at work: your existence is evidence, and SIA also takes your birth rank among humans as evidence, so finding yourself early in the sequence shifts you toward a shorter future for humanity. SIA's real cost is that it drags in Doomsday, and that's the bullet the SIA-er has to bite."
