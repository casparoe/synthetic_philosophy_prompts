I write about science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and for a piece on how people handle tradeoffs I've been badgering a source I've quoted for years — a decision researcher who is scary-consistent about this stuff — with little comparison cases over coffee. Her rulings so far: two otherwise identical cups, one with marginally better beans and marginally worse temperature, and she says flat out that the better-beans cup is better than the other one; then a chain of a thousand cups, each a hair better in beans and a hair colder, running from steaming-and-mediocre all the way to lukewarm-and-sublime, and she says the first cup is clearly better than the thousandth, no hesitation, she'd even pay real money to swap back to the first; and when I asked her point blank whether "better than" has to be transitive — if X beats Y and Y beats Z, does X beat Z — she looked at me like I was an idiot and said that's not a preference, that's just what the word means. I'll admitt I smiled, because as far as I can tell those three answers can't all be true, and she gave all three without flinching.

I've got one more session with her and I want to walk in with a prediction instead of just gawking. Next case: two apartments pulled out of a 400-step spectrum, each step slightly more space but a slightly noisier street, running from a spacious place on a highway to a tiny one on a dead-quiet lane. The two are #80 and #320 — far apart along the chain, nowhere near the endpoints. And here's the part I'm actually curious about: I'm going to run it twice. Once with just those two apartments described on their own, and once with the whole 400-step table printed out and spread in front of her while she picks. What's she going to say in each run? Honestly I care less about the verdicts than about the rules that could be generating her track record — the reading where all three of her answers stand as stated because "better than" just isn't transitive, the reading where her coffee verdict is really a tolerance for tiny tradeoffs rather than a strict ranking (so single steps are "improvements" but not "better"), and the reading where what's better depends on what else is on the table. Which of those fits her best, which of my two runs (if either) actually pries them apart, and if none of it settles anything, what case should I cook up next that would?
