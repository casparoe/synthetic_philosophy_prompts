We're doing a mini-unit on "ways of arguing that look like cheating but maybe aren't," and the text I got assigned is that ancient Chinese one, the white horse dialogue by Gongsun Long. My summary of it, in case it matters for designing this: he tries to convince someone that a white horse is not a horse, and the argument I can follow is that "horse" names the shape/animal and "white" names the color, so "white horse" is doing a different job than "horse" -- if you ask for a horse you'd accept a yellow or black one, but if you ask for a white horse those don't count, so the two terms can't be the same thing. There's also something about how saying you want a horse is compatible with white OR yellow OR black, but saying you want a white horse rules out the yellow and black ones, which is supposed to show the two requests aren't equal.

Here's my problem: when I explained this to my friend she said "that's just a dumb word trick, obviously a white horse is a horse" and I suspect half the club will do the same five-second eye roll and we'll be done in ten minutes. So I want an activity or game where people basically walk THEMSELVES into treating "white horse" and "horse" as seperable before they know that's the thing we're discussing. Like maybe they're border guards or running some kind of checkpoint where the rules quietly make the labels come apart, and they only realize at the end what position they've been defending. The teacher's one instruction was: don't just tell them it's a fallacy, make them feel why someone smart could've thought it was true.

So, design me the session.
