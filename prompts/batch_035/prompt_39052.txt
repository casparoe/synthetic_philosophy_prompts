I manage risk for a five-person sports-betting syndicate. We bet mostly moneylines and totals, a few hundred bets a year, and our bankroll is real but small relative to our members' incomes — losing it all would hurt, but it wouldn't end anyone. The same argument keeps breaking out: one of us insists we should bet full Kelly, since it provably maximizes the long-run growth rate of the bankroll and anything less leaves money on the table; another says full Kelly is reckless, since even a modest edge has you risking a large slice of the bankroll on a single game, and a normal cold streak would be unbearable. I want to stop litigating this bet by bet.

Write us a staking procedure we can actually run before each weekend's slate: how to go from our estimated edge and the posted odds to a stake; what fraction of full Kelly to bet and the reasoning behind that fraction; how to handle correlated bets on the same slate (two bets on the same match, several legs of the same parlay, five bets on the same tournament winner); a cap on aggregate exposure; and rules for when to pause and re-estimate after a drawdown. Be honest about where the procedure is judgment dressed up as arithmetic — in particular, how much of a fractional-Kelly choice is genuinely a response to our edge estimates being noisy, versus just not wanting to feel the variance. Mark the points where the procedure has to stop and hand the decision to a human, because the remaining question is one of values rather than probability. Then run it on two cases: a bet where our model claims an unusually large edge (say, 12% on an NFL moneyline in a market we normally price within a point or two of consensus), and a slate of eight bets of which five are on the same match. I suspect the procedure will sometimes tell us to bet less than our gut wants; that's fine, I'd rather know.

Finally, please separate what is settled mathematics in the Kelly framework from what is genuinely contested among people who know the topic — especially whether betting below full Kelly is a rational adjustment for estimation error or merely a psychological comfort — and don't present either side of that as the consensus.
