longtime lurker of the rationalist blogosphere here (lesswrong since the old days, ssc comment threads, the whole ecosystem) and there's a pattern I keep bumping into that I've never seen cleanly named, so I'm asking here.

the pattern: whenever two parties with genuinely different values have to share a decision, they almost always split the difference instead of randomizing over the extremes, even when the randomization looks better for everyone ex ante.

concrete instances, because that's where I keep noticing it:

- two friends deciding on a restaurant. they go to the middling place both feel lukewarm about rather than flipping a coin between the two favorites. if each of them genuinly prefers "50% chance of my favorite, 50% chance of yours" to "100% guaranteed mediocre" — and they almost always say they do, if you actually ask — then the compromise is dominated and they still pick it.
- an org with a fixed budget torn between two causes. the default is a 50/50 split. but if impact per dollar has convex-ish returns, or even just ordinary diminishing returns where the second tranche does less good than the first, the split can be strictly worse than coin-flipping the whole pot at one of the two. I remember people arguing about this in the hits-based giving debate — one side said diversify because risk aversion, the other side said risk aversion about impartially-valued outcomes is straight up irrational, and as far as I can tell it never got resolved.
- me internally. I'm maybe 60% consequentialist, 40% something deontish. the standard advice (moral parliament style setups, act on the mixture) means I'm a watered-down blend all the time. nobody ever suggests "flip a coin, commit to whichever side wins, for this decision." is that because it's a bad idea or because it feels bad?

so really two questions. one: does the preference for averaging over lotteries have a name? I half remember a "compromise effect" from some psych context but I think that's about consumers picking the middle-priced option on a shelf, which is related in spirit but not the same thing at all. two: is the coin flip actually better, or is refusing it tracking something real? like, I know that in vNM-land any mixture over outcomes just IS a lottery, so the distinction I'm drawing technically shouldn't exist, and yet it keeps feeling like it does, and I can't tell wether that feeling is a bias to be corrected or a clue that something in the vNM setup (risk neutrality? who counts as the agent?) is doing work I'm not seeing.

and to be clear I don't want just a term, I want the carving: what exactly is the phenomenon (choosing to average when lotteries are available), what is it NOT (ordinary pareto improvements, ordinary compromise), and where the edges are. if there's no established name, coin one and give me boundary cases.
