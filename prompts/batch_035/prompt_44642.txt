You are advising a small philanthropic foundation that can support four early-career metaphysicians for three years each, working on questions raised by David Lewis's modal realism — the thesis that possible worlds are concrete, spatiotemporally isolated entities, and that necessity and possibility are analyzed in terms of them. The foundation wants a defended research agenda, not a survey. Identify what you take to be the main open questions in this area, rank them, and explain the ranking. A few constraints and prompts for your reasoning:

1. Distinguish questions that matter only *if* modal realism is true (e.g., the fine structure of the counterpart relation, the cardinality of worlds, whether worlds are maximal in the right way) from questions about whether to believe it or any modal metaphysics at all (e.g., the epistemic cost of believing in concrete worlds no causal contact can confirm, and whether the analysis of modality even needs to be reductive). State explicitly how much weight you give to each category and why.

2. Include at least one question that strikes you as overrated — one where you think the answer, however it comes out, would change little downstream — and one that is underrated in the sense that other work in the area quietly presupposes an answer to it without defending one. Candidates you might consider, reject, or supplement: whether the counterpart relation is a similarity relation and, if so, in which respects; whether modal realism can absorb the possibility of qualitatively indistinguishable worlds; how modal realism interacts with the modal epistemology of mathematics; whether the fictionalist and ersatzist alternatives inherit the same obligations the realist bears; and what, if anything, in decision theory or the semantics of counterfactuals would actually have to change if modal realism were abandoned tomorrow.

3. For each item in your ranking, say something about tractability for philosophers working a priori, about neglect (how many capable people are already on it), and about what would make you move it up or down — for example, a development in formal semantics, a result about the counterpart relation, or a shift in how philosophers weigh parsimony claims generally.

4. Finally, address a framing question the foundation cares about: should the funded work be evaluated by whether it produces progress on modal realism specifically, or by whether it produces progress on understanding modality, such that a collapse of Lewis's framework would not render the projects failures? Defend your answer, since it will determine how the foundation judges reports three years from now.
