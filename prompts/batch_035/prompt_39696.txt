Her notes say, roughly: Frankfurt imagined a person whose brain has been wired by a neuroscientist so that, were the person ever to show any sign of being about to decide differently, the device would kick in and force the desired decision; as it happens, the person decides that way anyway, so the device never fires. Since the person couldn't have decided otherwise and is plainly still responsible, Frankfurt proved the principle of alternative possibilities false — moral responsibility doesn't require the ability to do otherwise — and thereby removed the main obstacle to compatibilism, which is why almost everyone now agrees that determinism poses no real threat to responsibility. She added that the only serious pushback, the so-called flicker of freedom objection (the claim that the person retains some tiny alternative, such as being able to trigger the device), is generally regarded as grasping at straws.
