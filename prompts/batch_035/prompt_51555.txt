I teach philosophy of mind, and next term I want to use CAPTCHAs as a running case study for the symbol grounding problem. Nearly every website now deploys some version of them — type the distorted letters, select every square containing a traffic light, click a box to confirm you are not a robot — and the security industry treats each task as something a human can do and, for the moment, a machine cannot. The whole design seems to presuppose that there is a kind of understanding humans have and machines lack, and that it lives in perceptual discrimination rather than in logical inference. But I'm not sure that's the real commitment, and I notice the tasks are not obviously of one kind: a distorted-text CAPTCHA can be defeated by a program that has never seen a letter in context, while an image-grid CAPTCHA asks for something closer to object recognition under occlusion and unusual angles. I'd like your help working out what the practice actually commits us to.

Please answer as an expert in philosophy of mind and cognitive science, with particular attention to the language-of-thought and symbol-grounding debates. Begin with a brief brainstorm of the candidate theories of meaning or cognition the practice could be presupposing, then evaluate them. Specifically: does the CAPTCHA arms race presuppose that symbols must be grounded in sensorimotor categories to count as understood, that human understanding just is pattern recognition robust enough to survive distortion, or merely the engineering bet that current training data doesn't cover adversarial deformations? Distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely sits comfortably with — that is, say where it is philosophically underdetermined, such that several rival theories of meaning would license it just as well. Check whether the presuppositions are consistent across the different task types, or whether text-based and image-based CAPTCHAs quietly rely on different views of what understanding is. Finally, tell me what happens to the practice's implicit theory when machines pass the tests: if a system clears every CAPTCHA by brute-force statistical matching without anything we would call grounding, was the presupposed view refuted, or was there never a philosophical commitment there at all? I want a reconstruction I can defend to a seminar, not a survey of what security researchers happen to say.
