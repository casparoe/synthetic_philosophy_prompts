novelist here, deep in the weeds of a book, and i need someone to audit my beliefs because I suspect they're quietly at war with each other. backstory first, then the beliefs.

I'm writing a three-generation novel set in Bohemia between roughly 1810 and 1890: grandfather is a Czech-speaking peasant, grandson ends up a nationalist journalist in Prague. Now, where I've landed intellectually: (1) I basically accept the modernist account of nations — nations in the full sense are a product of the last two centuries or so, print capitalism, mass literacy, schools, censuses, and a peasant in 1810 did not have national identity the way his grandson did; a lot of what feels ancient was consciously revived or flat-out invented in that window. (2) I also think constructed doesn't mean fake — national identity is constructed the way money and marriage are, which is to say it's as real as anything. (3) the emotional arc of the book depends on the grandfather already feeling part of something old — a Czech people with saints and kings and Hus, going back as far as anyone remembers — and I want the reader to feel that continuity as truth, not as something revivalists are manufacturing. (4) I'm a complete maniac about anachronism in other people's historical fiction — I threw a book across the room last month because a medieval peasant talked about the nation like a 19th-century pamphleteer, and I'd be humiliated to have done the samething. (5) I believe a novelist's loyalty is to the character's emotional truth, and stepping outside the character to wink at the reader about how invented it all is would be a smug betrayal. (6) I recently had a huge fight with my brother-in-law, who's gone full the-nation-is-a-thousand-years-old, blood-and-soil on facebook — I was the one lecturing him that nations are actually pretty new — and I really do not want my book passed around as ammunition by people like him.

So: act as a philosopher who knows the nationalism debates (Anderson, Hobsbawm, the whole modernism vs. perennialism fight) and also thinks seriously about how fiction works. Before you answer, take a moment to brainstorm and restate each of my six beliefs in your own words so we're sure we're talking about the same thing. Then figure out which of these actually conflict and which only look like they do — please don't just flag every pair as an interesting tension, that's useless to me. Rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one tell me what would have to give and what the fix costs me artistically. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Bonus points if you can say whether the fix lives in my plot, in my narration, or in my beliefs — because I genuinely don't know where the problem is.
