I'd like your actual credence, decomposed, on a question my thesis advisor and I have been circling: that a clay statue and the lump of clay that constitutes it are numerically distinct objects, co-located in the same region at the same time. My credence that they are distinct sits around 0.7; hers is around 0.2. Please give your own number, but what I really want is the derivation — the sub-questions the credence depends on, a rough probability for each, how they combine (be honest that they are not independent), and which one the final number is most sensitive to.

Here is how I see the terrain, though you may carve it differently. First, the indiscernibility argument: the lump can survive being squashed into a ball while the statue cannot, and things with different persistence conditions cannot be identical. Second, the replies: that survival is sortal-relative, so the alleged modal difference attaches to our descriptions rather than to the objects; that persistence conditions can be indeterminate; that maybe the statue does survive squashing, just barely. Third, the counting worry: if the statue and the lump are distinct, the world contains two things made of exactly the same parts, and it is obscure what could ground the difference — or whether that is a genuine cost rather than a prejudice about how many things a room should contain. Fourth, perdurance: on a four-dimensionalist picture, statue and lump share every temporal part and differ only in their complete careers, which suggests both that they are distinct and that there is nothing very deep about the difference.

Two further cases I'd like folded into your decomposition if you find them relevant. In the first, the sculptor finishes the statue and then fires it in a kiln: the lump of clay arguably goes out of existence when it becomes terracotta, while the statue survives. Does this vindicate the distinctness claim, or does it only show that we track sortals sloppily, lapsing between the clay and the statue mid-sentence? In the second, the sculptor kneads a lump for an hour before shaping it, and we ask when, if ever, the statue began to exist — with no answer seeming principled rather than stipulated. Does that indeterminacy cut for or against distinctness, or neither?

Finally, tell me where you think my advisor and I actually disagree: which single sub-question would most need to flip for her 0.2 to become my 0.7, and whether you think at bottom we are assigning probabilities to different propositions — she may suspect the entire dispute is verbal, while I suspect it is as determinate as any question in metaphysics. And say which argument or thought experiment, if it fully succeeded, would move your number the most.
