You are an expert in experimental philosophy and moral psychology, well versed in the literature on the side-effect effect, and skilled at reverse-engineering the implicit rules behind people's judgments. Please think carefully and start with a brief brainstorm of candidate rules before committing to a prediction.

In my experimental philosophy reading group, one member — call her Dana — has been delivering verdicts on intentionality vignettes all term, and I want to predict her verdict on next week's case before she gives it. Her record so far: (1) the classic case where a CEO, saying he cares only about profit and not at all about the environment, launches a program that foreseeably harms the environment: she says he harmed it intentionally, and she blames him; (2) the mirror-image case where the program foreseeably helps the environment: not intentional, and no credit; (3) a case where a chairperson under a standing rule against taking pens takes them anyway, foreseeing that colleagues will be left without pens: intentional; (4) the same depletion, caused by an assistant whose duties include taking pens: not intentional; (5) a professor who violates academic decorum through outrageous self-promotion, foreseeing that this will bring welcome publicity to his department, while caring only about his own fame: she says he brought the benefit about intentionally, and credits him.

Next week's vignette: a plant supervisor at a facility where releasing wastewater into the river is the universal, long-standing, and perfectly legal practice — his own included — foresees that this season's release will harm the fishery downstream, and says he doesn't care, he is just running standard operations.

Predict Dana's verdict on whether the supervisor harmed the fishery intentionally. Identify the candidate rules that fit her entire record — norm violation regardless of the outcome's valence; abnormality of the conduct relative to the agent's role and to what people in that position typically do; her own disposition to blame or credit the agent; and any others you can devise. Say which of these rules the new case separates and which it leaves tied, give your best guess with reasons, and then design the further vignette that would best discriminate among the candidates that remain indistinguishable after her answer.
