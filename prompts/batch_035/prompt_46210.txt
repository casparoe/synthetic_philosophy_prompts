I teach philosophy of language and logic, and this term I am drafting a paper on future contingents with a colleague in metaphysics. We have hit a wall and want to locate our disagreement precisely. Here is the proposition on the table. Suppose that on a particular day D it is settled that a naval engagement may occur, but whether it does is not yet determined; to sharpen the case, stipulate that the admiral's decision hinges on a coin he will flip at dawn, and grant that the flip is genuinely chancy — not predictable in principle by any agent inside the world. Uttered on the evening before D, does the sentence "There will be a sea battle on D" have a determinate truth value at that moment? My colleague's credence that it does is about 0.85; mine is about 0.45, and I suspect mine is really a weighted average of three different questions I have not disentangled. Give me your own credence on the proposition, but the deliverable I care about is the decomposition. Separate at least these terms, plus any others you think belong: (a) the probability that bivalence holds unrestrictedly for declarative sentences about the future even when the underlying facts are unsettled; (b) the probability that unsettledness of the facts is even the right thing to be tracking — that is, that whether a sentence has a truth value should depend on how things will go rather than on how things stand when it is uttered; (c) the probability that a genuinely open future is a coherent metaphysical position rather than merely epistemic openness misdescribed; and (d) the probability that the Ockhamist maneuver — the sentence is true or false, but it is not yet settled which — preserves enough of what we wanted from truth to count as vindicating bivalence rather than changing the subject. Assign a rough probability to each term with a line of justification, show me how they combine into your final number, and identify which term the number is most sensitive to. Then tell me, given your decomposition, whether the gap between my colleague's 0.85 and my 0.45 most likely tracks a semantic disagreement, a metaphysical one, or a merely verbal one about what "has a truth value" amounts to. Finally, if you think the coin-flip sharpening makes the case importantly unlike the classical sea battle — because the indeterminacy is physical rather than practical or deliberative — say so, and tell me which of your terms moves and in which direction.
