Someone at a psych-adjacent philosophy talk I went to kept dropping Hacking's line that we are "making up people" — as in, when psychiatrists coin a diagnosis or bureacrats start sorting a population, they're not just describing kinds of people, they're making up people. In the Q&A I asked if this just means labels influence behavior, and she looked a bit pained and said no, it's much stronger, something about kinds of people that didn't even exist before the classification. I left pretty confused honestly. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims "making up people" could be standing for — from the weak one everyone would grant, through medium-strength ones about looping effects and self-understanding, up to the full-on constructivist one — and for each say whether it's plausible, whether it's actually false, and whether the speaker could have needed it for her argument? A related thing I want cleared up: I've heard Hacking himself worried that looping effects can die out over time (some classification stops changing the people it names). Does that fading-away worry undermine any of the readings, or actually support some of them? A menu I could bring to the next talk would be perfect.
