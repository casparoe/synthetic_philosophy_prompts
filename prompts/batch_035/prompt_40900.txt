My grandfather spent forty years restoring an old wooden sailboat, and by the end literally every plank, rib, and fitting had been replaced at least once — he kept a few of the original planks in his garage. He left the boat to me in his will, and at the wake my mom and my uncle got into it over whether the thing at the marina is even the boat he meant. (1) My uncle said obviously not: every part is new, so it's a replica that happens to sit in the same slip, and a boat made of entirely new planks isn't my grandfather's boat. (2) My mom said that can't be right, because the changes happened gradually, one plank at a time over decades — by his logic your own cells turn over and nobody stays the same person long enough to inherit anything. (3) He said the analogy is fake, because the boat was completely disassembled during a refit in 2018 — for six months it existed only as labeled lumber in a barn, so whatever continuity her story needs was interrupted outright. (4) She said that's still continuous repair: the pieces were all there, waiting to go back together, like a patient on a heart-lung machine — it's not as if anyone built a new boat from scratch. (5) He said fine, then the design is what matters — but that proves too much, since the boatyard still has the original plans and jigs, and if they built a second hull from them, nobody would say there were suddenly two of grandpa's boat; and anyway, if I rebuilt a boat out of the original planks in the garage, we'd have two candidates and her view has no way to pick between them. (6) My mom's last word was that boat-identity is just bookkeeping: the registration, the name, the family all treat it as the same boat, so it is, and there's nothing deeper to find out. He replied that if it's all bookkeeping, then there's no fact for the will to be about either way, which can't be what grandpa intended. I'm the one whos supposed to sail it this summer and I'd honestly like to know. Can you go through their exchange and say who's right at each step — not just who wins overall — and whether there's anything both of them are missing?
