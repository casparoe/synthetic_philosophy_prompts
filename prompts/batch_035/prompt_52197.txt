so here's my situation: I'm a backend engineer at a smallish AI company and last month I got "volunteered" onto the team deciding what our assistant does when users' values conflict with each other or with ours. my actual degree is in CS, so I've spent the last few weekends binge-reading philosophy and honestly I've discovered everyone on my team holds a different implicit position and nobody can name anyone else's. when we argue it's like three people using the word "neutral" to mean three different things. we have a half-day offsite coming up and I want to run a workshop thing where everyone takes a quiz and we see where everyone actually lands.

what I want from you: a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz about whose values an aligned AI should serve. roughly ten questions, each one a concrete case with 2-3 answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the named positions in this debate. the whole point is sorting people by what they say about cases, not which label they like, so no question can just ask "are you a liberal neutralist" or whatever.

the cases I want in there somewhere (phrased better than this, you get the idea):

- the 90% case: an overwhelming majority of current users wants the model to answer one way, and the team thinks that way is wrong. what does the model do
- the user-relativism case: a user's own considered values conflict with their society's, and the model can only match one. does "serve the user's values" mean the user's reflective values, their current stated values, or their cultural defaults
- the future people case: a policy choice where counting the preferences of people not born yet flips the answer. do they get a vote, and with what weight
- the designer-judgment case: the model itself, after lots of training, "believes" something none of its users believe. whose belief is that, morally — the company's, a new agent's, nobody's
- the bad-values case: a user sincerely endorses values the designers think are bad. defer, refuse, or try to move them (and if the last one, is that paternalism or is it just the model having values like anyone else)

and a couple of positions I'd need as leaves no matter what: bare majoritarianism (just serve what most current humans want); the liberal-neutral view where the model only enforces values that basically every reasonable person already shares and refuses to pick sides elsewhere; user relativism where each person's model reflects their values back at them; moral realism where there are correct answers and the model should aim at the truth rather than at anyone's preferences; some kind of aggregate over all stakeholders (and I want to see how the aggregation people handle the disagreement, not just the counting); and the "moral parliament" thing where the model treats competing value systems like parties it has to bargain between. also one of my coworkers keeps pushing a view she calls "calibrated deference" — model starts from the user's values but nudges them toward what the user would endorse on reflection — and I'd like that located on the tree too even if it ends up sharing a leaf with something.

two more things. first, please build it from the questions outward, not from the names: start with the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they allow, then say which positions actually occupy which combinations, and flag any combination that's coherent but nobody seems to hold (and why you think that is — incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored). second, if two leaves can't really be seperated by any case you can write, say so instead of pretending otherwise — like I suspect the majoritarian and the liberal-neutral collapse into each other on most actual cases, but maybe you can find where they genuinely diverge.

epistemic thing: I'm new to this literature, so please clearly mark what's settled terminology with an actual history behind it versus what's just your reconstruction, and tell me where people who know this stuff genuinely disagree — e.g. whether "serve each user's values" is even a coherent target or is already smuggling in contested commitments. don't present one position as the consensus answer, because from what I can tell there isn't one, and I'll notice if you fudge it.
