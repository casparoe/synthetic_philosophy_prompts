My roommates and I have been fighting about electoral reform for like two years and we've finally agreed to do the whole adversarial-collaboration thing properly. One of us is all in on proportional representation — any system where a party's seat share roughly tracks its vote share, whether party-list, STV, MMP, whatever. The other defends single-member plurality districts, on the grounds that they produce clean alternation in power, a personal MP who answers to an actual place, and governments that can actually be thrown out. The PR side says FPTP is basically a rigged lottery where millions of voters get nothing and outcomes depend on how votes happen to be distributed geographically; the district side says PR fragments the party system, hands the balance of power to tiny kingmaker parties no one voted for as such, and severs the link between a voter and an identifiable representative.

Write the joint statement we should be able to produce. I want the claims both sides can sign — about what elections are for, about which parts of our dispute are empirical versus values, and about what the other side's system actually does as opposed to what its worst implementations do — then the disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording. Then the cruxes. I suspect one is whether a vote is primarily a contribution to a national party tally or a choice of a local representative; one is whether a party with 12% nationally deserves 12% of the seats or should have to win pluralities somewhere; and one is whether two-party competition really moderates policy or just suppresses preferences nobody in the two parties holds. For each crux, give a concrete case or piece of evidence both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

There's a hypothetical we keep circling that you should work in somewhere: a party that gets 34% in every single district, against two rivals who get 33% each, concentrated in different regions. The PR side says it obviously deserves about a third of the seats and anything else disenfranchises its voters; the district side says it lost head-to-head contests everywhere fair and square, and that's what contests are. Can that disagreement be kept exact, or does it bottom out in the word fair itself? If part of our whole fight is verbal — e.g. about what representation even means — say which part and why.

Format: numbered agreed claims first, then a disagreements table (disagreement | wording the PR side would sign | wording the district side would sign | what would move one or both sides).
