Quick background in case it helps: I'm in a college course on philosophy of physics and my reading group has been stuck for three weeks on the argument, usually credited to Rietdijk and Putnam back in the sixties, that special relativity basically forces you to accept the block universe. The way our professor set it up: you and I walk past each other on the street, moving at slightly different velocities relative to some distant event E (say, a supernova 100,000 light years away). Your plane of simultaneity slices through spacetime such that E has already happened; mine slices such that E hasn't happened yet. But relativity says no frame is privileged. So if "what is real for you" includes everything simultaneous with you in your frame, then E both has and hasn't happened, and by chaining observers you can make any future event of any observer "real" for someone, so nothing is genuinely futurity — the whole four-dimensional block just is, and the flowing present is a kind of illusion or at best a perspectival thing like "here" in space.

So here's my ask. My credence that special relativity, once you really accept it, commits you to eternalism about time (the block universe, no ontologically privileged present) is around 0.75. My friend Dara in the group says 0.3 — she thinks the argument smuggles in the conclusion by treating "simultaneous with you in your frame" as if it meant "definitely settled and real for you," which she says is a technical relation about clock readings, not an ontological one, and that presentists can just refuse the premise. Give me your own number, but more importantly decompose it: your probability that the argument is actually valid once spelled out (does transitivity of "determinate relative to" plus frame equivalence really get you to "real, period"?), your probability that presentism can be patched up to coexist with SR — say by positing a preferred foliation, or leaning on cosmic time from cosmology, or redefining the present as a single point — without the patch being an ugly ad hoc addition, your probability that whatever you concluded survives moving from special relativity to general relativity where things get messier, and any other terms you think belong in there. Give a rough probability and a line of justification for each, then show how they combine into your overall number.

Then two follow-ups, since those are the parts I actually care about: (1) which single term is your final number most sensitive to, and what specific argument or piece of physics would move that term the most — like, if someone showed a foliation-independent way to define a present, how much would that shift you, and if some QM result (relativistic Bell stuff keeps coming up in our discussions) ruled out preferred foliations, how much would it shift you the other way? and (2) where do you think the disagreement between me at 0.75 and Dara at 0.3 actually lives — is it about the metaphysics of what "real" means, about whether the physics constrains metaphysics at all, or about the costs of the patch-up options? I want to figure out whether we're disagreeing about physics or about something that no amount of physics could settle.
