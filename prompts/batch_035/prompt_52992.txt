I do evals for a living — benchmarks, contamination audits, human preference studies, the whole construct-validity mess that is trying to measure what a model can actually do versus what it displays on a held-out set. Lately I've been reading about the Turing test and the older behaviorist idea that mentality should be settled by behavioral criteria, and I keep getting the feeling that my field redid this entire argument under different names without anyone noticing. So here's what I want: restate the Turing test debate in the vocabulary I actually use. That means Turing's imitation-game setup as an eval protocol (adversarial judges, reward hacking by the contestant, contamination via judges who've seen the model's outputs before), the "it could pass by trickery without thinking" objection as our gaming/Goodhart worry, the replies about humans also just pattern-matching their way through conversation, Searle's Chinese room thing, and the dispute over whether the test is supposed to be a definition of thinking or just an operational proxy — all in terms of benchmarks, construct validity, capability vs. elicitation gaps, sandbagging, and models that ace the eval while being useless at the task. Then I want the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Which parts of the philosophical fight map cleanly onto benchmark-validity fights, which parts I'm flattering myself to think we've surpassed, and which parts have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? My instinct is that "but is it really thinking" is one of the residue items — there's no measurement I could run that would touch it — but I want to know whether that means my vocabulary is missing something real or whether the question was never well-posed to begin with. Basically: did we settle a version of their debate without noticing, or are we about to rediscover something they already worked out the hard way? Be honest about where the translation bends the original — I don't want you to quietly assume eval methodology's answers are the answers, I want to know what got dropped.
