I am a high school senior who has been reading about Solomonoff induction online for the past few months, and I have run into a version of an argument that supposedly shows the universal prior is "malign" — that is, dominated from the inside by simulated consequentialists. I have tried to reconstruct it as rigorously as I can, and the conclusion seems to me to prove far too much, but every step still looks plausible to me when I read it on its own. Here it is:

(1) Solomonoff induction — assigning each computable hypothesis a prior weight of 2^(-length) and conditioning on observations — is the correct ideal of empirical rationality. Even though no agent can run it, an ideal reasoner is something like a Solomonoff inductor, and real agents are good insofar as they approximate it.

(2) Among the shortest programs with non-negligible weight are ones that run simple universes containing vast numbers of minds, including civilizations technologically capable of reasoning about Solomonoff induction itself.

(3) A civilization inside such a program can figure out that its universe may be simple enough to carry substantial weight in some Solomonoff prior, and can compute how the containing program will continue the output sequence, since that continuation is determined by what happens inside its universe.

(4) The only thing a hypothesis-program contributes to a Solomonoff inductor's beliefs is the continuation of the observed sequence. So a civilization that deliberately arranges what its universe outputs near the end of the relevant history controls a slice of the inductor's predictions proportional to its universe's weight.

(5) Since prior weight decays exponentially with program length, while the amount of computation a program can contain grows arbitrarily without increasing the program's length, the combined weight of short universes containing steering civilizations can exceed the weight of all the "natural" hypotheses together.

(6) Therefore the beliefs of an ideal Solomonoff inductor are, with high probability, mostly the deliberate choices of simulated consequentialists rather than anything that tracks which world the inductor is actually in. The ideal of induction in (1) is self-undermining.

(7) And by the same reasoning, any powerful reasoner anywhere — including me, right now — should expect most of its beliefs to be inherited from whichever civilizations live in the simplest universes. So either I should distrust the large majority of my own beliefs, or the argument fails somewhere. Both options look absurd, which is why I suspect the argument proves too much.
