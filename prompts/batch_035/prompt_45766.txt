Physics undergrad here, been reading history of science on the side, and I keep noticing a pattern I don't have a name for. When a big theory gets replaced, the equations get rescued but the mechanism doesn't. Like Fresnel had this really successful theory of light as vibrations in a luminferous ether (typo, whatever), then Maxwell comes along and basically the math describing the waves gets carried over wholesale — I think some philosopher named Worrall made a big deal of exactly this case? — but the ether itself gets quietly dropped, and later relativity drops even more of the story while the equations keep working. Same with gravity: Newton's inverse-square force ruled for two centuries, then general relativity says there is no force at all, just curved spacetime, and yet the old formula still falls out of the new theory in the limit, like an heirloom getting moved into a new house.

So what seems to survive scientific revolutions is the relational structure — the equations, the "light bends by this much near a mass" stuff — and what dies is the ontology: the medium, the force, the little mechanical picture we had in our heads. Is there an actual name for this observation, and is there a whole position built on "believe the structure, stay agnostic about the stuff it's about"? It feels like it could be a middle way between "science eventually gets at the truth" and "science is just a useful instrument."

But I dont just want a label. I want to know what exactly the position claims, where its edges are, and how it's supposed to be different from instrumentalism on one side and plain scientific realism on the other. Also the standard complaints against it, because I can already feel some myself: an equation plus "it's about whatever underlying stuff behaves this way" is so flexible that maybe it's trivially preserved, the way "whatever team wins will have scored more" is always true. And some theories' math dies completely — caloric, phlogiston — so maybe the surviving equations are just survivorship bias, and we only celebrate the rescues that happened?

Please answer in American English, and if there are competing versions of the position I'd like to hear how they fight with each other.
