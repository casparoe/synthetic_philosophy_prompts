I've been reading about Kavka's toxin puzzle lately and I can't get it out of my head, so now I want to inflict it on fiction. In case you don't have the details fresh: a billionaire offers you a million dollars, deposited at noon today, if at noon you *intend* to drink a toxin tomorrow that will make you violently sick for a day but do no lasting damage. The billionaire pays purely on your intention, which he can somehow verify perfectly, and once the money is yours nothing forces you to follow through — drinking the toxin is pointless suffering. Kavka's point was that you seemingly can't get the money: the moment you understand the setup, you know you won't drink tomorrow, so you can't sincerely intend it now, even though you'd happily have paid for the ability to so intend.

Write me a short story about someone in this exact bind. My seeding suggestion: make her a philosophy PhD who washed out of academia and now does quant work, someone who knows all the literature — Kavka, the smoking lesion crowd, the two-boxer/one-boxer wars on the internet — and is convinced the puzzle is just a solved problem of insufficient cleverness. She tries a ladder of schemes to manufacture the intention: telling everyone she knows that she will drink (reputation as a binding device), drafting a contract with a friend who gets a huge penalty if she doesn't drink, even training herself with nausea-inducing candy in the days before. Let some of the schemes get genuinely close before they fray, and give her an interior life so the ending isn't just a puzzle recap. What I care about most is the ending — I don't want a cheat where she finds a loophole, and I don't want a lazy "she just did it, willpower!" either. Show me whatever honest resolution you think is actually available, whether that's getting the money, losing it, or something weirder.

Keep it under 900 words, and no epilogue or "years later she reflected..." coda — end at the last real moment of the story.
