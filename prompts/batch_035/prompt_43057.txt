my roommate and I have been going in circles for months about the boundary problem in democratic theory -- you know, the problem of constituting the demos: for any given collective decision, who counts as a member of the group entitled to make it? I'm basically team "all-affected interests": anyone who's interests are significantly affected by a decision should get a say in it. She's team "all-subjected": the demos is whoever is actually subject to the authority/coercive power of the decision-making body, not just whoever happens to be affected by the outcome. Over time we've accumulated a running list of test cases we fight about: a coastal county voting on whether to build a sea wall that inland taxpayers partially fund, seasonal migrant workers who can't vote where they live and work 9 months a year, foreign investors hit by a new tariff, future generations and climate policy, tourists passing through during election season, and whether any of this changes when a referendum is binding versus merely advisory.

Write us the document we should've produced instead of yelling across the kitchen: a numbered list of claims we could both genuinely sign off on (not mushy compromise fluff -- real points of agreement, including about what makes the boundary problem hard in the first place), then a table of our disagreements, where each side's position is stated in wording that that side would itself accept -- she should read the description of the all-subjected view and nod, and vice versa for me. Then lay out the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling them would actually move at least one of us. I suspect some candidates are: whether affectedness is ever measurable/stable enough to ground inclusion rights, whether being subject to coercion is just one species of being affected or something categorically different, and whether the demos can legitimately vary decision-by-decision or has to be fixed at some higher level (and if so, who fixes THAT, which feels like it just regresses the whole problem). For each crux, give a concrete case, argumnet, or thought experiment that both of us would agree bears on it, and record what each of us would predict about it in advance -- especially on our test cases like the sea wall and the migrant workers. And if part of our fight turns out to be just verbal -- like maybe we mean different things by "governed" vs "affected" -- please say which part and why, because honestly I've started to suspect a decent chunk of it is.
