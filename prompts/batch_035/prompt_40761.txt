The remark clearly impressed the room, but it did a lot of work in a very few words, and I want to understand exactly what I was being told before I write it up. Please lay out the distinct precise claims the "center of narrative gravity" slogan could be making -- from the comparatively modest (the self is an abstraction we use in prediction and explanation, like a center of mass in physics) to the most radical (the self is a fiction, and selves are no more real than fictional characters) -- and for each reading say whether it is plainly true, contentious, or arguably false. Pay particular attention to which readings would actually support my interlocutor's move: does any of them license concluding, from the analogy with centers of gravity, that the question of whether an AI has a self doesn't deserve investigation? Please write in American English, and present the readings as an ordered list I can consult while drafting the piece.
