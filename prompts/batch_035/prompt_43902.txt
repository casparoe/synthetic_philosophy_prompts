I'd like two things from you on the Sleeping Beauty problem, and I want numbers with visible reasoning, not a tour of the literature.

First, the setup, stated precisely so that nothing depends on memory of any particular paper. On Sunday, Beauty is told everything below and believes it. A fair coin is tossed. If it lands heads, she is awakened once, on Monday; she is interviewed, then given a drug that erases the interview from her memory, and the experiment ends. If it lands tails, she is awakened on Monday, interviewed, erased, awakened again on Tuesday, interviewed once more, and the experiment ends. On each interview she is asked for her credence, at that moment, that the coin landed heads. The two familiar answers are 1/2 and 1/3; call the reasoning styles behind them halfer-style and thirder-style. I don't need names, schools, or citations — just the arguments.

Question 1. Give me your credence, as a number, that 1/3 is the correct answer for Beauty at the moment of an interview in this standard setup. Then decompose that number: identify the three or four sub-questions it genuinely rests on — for example, whether an awakening gives Beauty any evidence about the coin at all; whether rational credence at an awakening should equal the expected fraction of interviews that are heads-interviews; whether the right test is the odds she should accept on bets placed once per interview, and whether per-interview or per-experiment betting is the relevant unit; and whether the dispute is fully substantive or partly verbal, dissolving once a scoring rule is fixed. Assign each sub-question its own probability with a one-line justification, show how they combine into your headline number, and say which sub-question the headline number is most sensitive to.

Question 2, the part that cannot be answered by reciting. Modify the setup as follows: everything as before, except that on tails the Tuesday awakening now occurs only with probability 1/2, determined by a second fair coin tossed out of Beauty's sight on Monday night after she is returned to sleep; on heads there is still exactly one awakening, on Monday, and never a Tuesday one. Beauty knows the full protocol on Sunday. Compute her credence in heads at the moment of an interview in this variant, first under halfer-style reasoning, then under thirder-style reasoning, showing the arithmetic in both cases. Then answer three things:

(a) Does the decomposition you gave in Question 1 survive the variant, or does the variant put pressure on one of your sub-questions in particular — and which one, and why?

(b) If your thirder-style arithmetic yields 2/5 in the variant, does accepting that commit you to 1/3 in the standard setup? If your halfer-style arithmetic yields 1/2 here, does that commit you to 1/2 there? Explain exactly what the variant does and does not settle about the original dispute.

(c) Name the single argument or consideration that would move your Question 1 headline number the most, and say in which direction and roughly how far.
