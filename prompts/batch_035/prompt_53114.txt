ok so I've been asked to contribute a position piece to a volume on assertion and ethics, and before I send it off I want a consistency check, because I have a nagging feeling the package doesn't hang together but I can't see where. here's the set of views I'm committed to in the draft:

(1) Lying is asserting to someone, with the intent that they believe it, something you believe false. Belief is what counts, not the truth of the sentence — so you can lie with a true sentence (if you sincerely think its false) and you can't lie with a false one you sincerely believe.

(2) Misleading — saying only things you believe true while engineering a false belief in the hearer via implicature — is pro tanto less wrong than lying.

(3) My explanation of (2): the liar offers his word as the reason for belief and bypasses the hearer's judgment entirely. The misleader leaves the last inference to the hearer — the hearer could always have asked a follow-up, pressed harder, checked the record. So the misleader corrupts the hearer's epistemic agency but doesn't bypass it, and thats supposed to be what makes lying worse.

(4) But I also hold, and want to keep holding, that the courtroom witness who answers a direct question with a carefully crafted literally-true reply — constructed so it suggests the opposite of what actually happened — is roughly as culpable as an out-and-out liar. And a courtroom answer to a direct question seems to be precisely the case where there was nothing left for the hearer to press or check: the question was already as direct as questions get.

(5) In the justified-deception cases (murderer at the door and the like), my line is: mislead, don't lie. Lying to the unjust is still lying; misleading can be permissible.

(6) And when deception is not justified, I hold that the lying/misleading distinction makes no difference to permissibility, only to degree of blame — the wrong-making feature is the intended false belief, and the distinction is a difference of degree, not of kind.

Go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and rank them by severity. If something has to give, say which commitment should go and what it costs me — I'd rather find out now than in the reviews.
