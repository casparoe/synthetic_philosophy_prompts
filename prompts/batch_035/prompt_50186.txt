I'm a philosophy PhD student teaching intro phil of math, and I've become weirdly convinced that The Phantom Tollbooth is secretly taking a side in the platonism vs nominalism fight. In Digitopolis the Mathemagician runs an actual mine where numbers are dug out of the earth as sparkling stones, and his whole pitch is that numbers are mined, not made — which sounds like platonism (numbers are just there, waiting to be discovered), except the stones also break, and the Mathemagician says not to worry because the fragments get used for fractions, which makes numbers physical objects you can chip and repurpose, about as anti-platonist as it gets. And then there's the .58 child from the average family, a walking talking statistical abstraction who insists he's just as real as a whole kid. I want to open a fist-year session with the book before we touch any Benacerraf. Pick exactly five scenes or characters from the book, and for each give a sentence or two on what it shows about the platonism/nominalism debate — or where the book is cheating to get a laugh. No intro or conclusion, just the five numbered points. Take the ontology as seriously as the book takes its wordplay, and flag honestly if any scene you'd want to use actually resists this reading.
