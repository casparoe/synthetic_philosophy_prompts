Give me your own credence, decomposed: (1) the probability that Lewis's proofs refute the truth-conditional view outright rather than merely forcing a retreat, e.g., to relativizing the Equation to a single fixed probability function; (2) the probability that the Equation holds unrestrictedly, once you set aside McGee-style counterexamples to modus ponens and the cases where a chain of individually assertible conditionals yields a plainly false one; (3) the probability that the best escape from triviality is the no-truth-value view, on which conditionals are not propositions at all, versus a trivalent or supervaluational semantics; and (4) the probability that the compounds problem — what to say about negated, conjoined, and iterated conditionals — sinks the no-truth-value view independently of any triviality result.
