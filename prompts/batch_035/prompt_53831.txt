My friend and I keep arguing about whether a coin toss is, at the moment the coin leaves the thumb, already decided. She holds that given the laws of physics the outcome of that particular toss is fixed at release, so speaking of two equally open futures is a fiction; the 50/50 is nothing but our ignorance. I suspect that even at release there is no fact of the matter that any agent short of a Laplacian demon could exploit, because whatever irreducible uncertainty exists in the launcher's hand gets amplified during flight and, above all, on landing. Please do not give us a lecture on determinism in general; we want your credence, with the derivation shown. Specifically: give a number for the probability that a physically ordinary coin toss is predictable from the state of the world at the moment of release by an agent with unlimited measurement accuracy, and decompose it. The terms we can see so far: whether the toss is even chaotic in the relevant sense, since the flight itself may be nearly linear in the launch parameters, with the bounce off the surface supplying whatever instability there is; whether quantum indeterminacies present at release could be amplified up to the scale of the whole coin, or whether the dynamics are too regular to act as such an amplifier; and whether a determinate outcome exists at all if the quantum state at release itself spreads over both results. Give a rough probability and one line of justification for each term, combine them, and then tell us which of the two of us is most likely to be wrong about what — that is, whether our disagreement is really about chaos, about quantum mechanics, or about what it means to call an outcome fixed. Please write your answer in British English.
