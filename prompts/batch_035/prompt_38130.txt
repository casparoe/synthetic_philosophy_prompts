hey, so I write for a tiny philosophy-of-mind humor zine and need a parody piece for the next issue. The concept: Dennett's idea that the self is a center of narrative gravity — basically an abstraction we posit, like the center of mass of a chair, nothing you could physically locate anywhere, not in some particular lobe, but the story-spinning brain can't do its predictive work without positing it, and the self is the main character of the autobiography it spins.

The piece I want is a deadpan service brochure from a fictional moving company that specializes in relocating centers of narrative gravity — full-service self-moving, packing, transport, reinstallation into your new life story. Format: a headline (the company name can carry a joke) plus exactly five numbered clauses, each a sentence or two long, with nothing before clause one and nothing after clause five — no intro, no sign-off, no conclusion.

Every joke has to be one that actually tracks the Dennett view, so someone who knows the literature nods instead of winces. Things I'd love to see worked in, though your own are fine as long as they're genuinely Dennett-adjacent rather than random: the crew opening you up and finding no self anywhere, and the contract treating that as completely normal rather than grounds for a refund; the awkward fact that a center of gravity can shift without anything physical moving, which makes the transport fee philosophically dubious; a clause about what happens if a client's narratives start orbiting more than one attractor and the center splits like a binary star; a guarantee written for a useful fiction — guaranteed the way fictions are guaranteed, whatever that means; something about how the character gets moved but the brain doing the storytelling stays put, so the client has to narrate themselves into the new place anyway.

The target is the temptation to treat the narrative self as literal cargo — something you could lose in a move, leave in storage, or have stolen. The brochure must never wink at this. The people who run the company are dead serious about hauling selves; they just keep quietly admitting, in the fine print, that it's abstractions all the way down. Keep it deadpan throughout — legible irony, no nudging the reader. American english pls, and please keep any typos out of the piece itself, those are my department.
