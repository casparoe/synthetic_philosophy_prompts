The exam question was:

"Consider the (fictional) research programme in macroeconomics that its practitioners call structural monetarism. Its hard core holds that sustained macroeconomic instability always traces to a mismatch between the money supply and the economy's real productive capacity; everything else lives in the protective belt. Over two decades the belt has absorbed several anomalies: when the velocity of money proved unstable, a restriction was added confining stability to periods of credible monetary policy; when a deep recession hit during steady money growth, the belt gained a hypothesis about latent capacity misestimation introduced after the fact. In the 1990s the programme stuck its neck out: it predicted that a country with rapidly growing money supply undergoing banking reform would see high inflation within two years; inflation stayed low. A rival programme, which made no prediction about the case, absorbed the same episode without modifying its own belt. (a) Using Lakatos's terminology, say where this programme stands and why. (b) Do Lakatos's criteria tell the economists working in it that they must abandon it now? Explain how your answer differs from what a naive falsificationist would say."

And the student wrote:

"Structural monetarism has a hard core, the money-capacity mismatch thesis, and a protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses. In Lakatos's model a programme is judged by whether its problemshift is progressive, which means it keeps predicting novel facts, or degenerating, which means it only reacts to anomalies with ad hoc repairs that predict nothing new. The 1990s episode establishes degeneration: the programme made a prediction, the prediction was refuted, and in Lakatos's terms this falsifies the belt hypothesis, so the programme itself has now been falsified and must be abandoned. Moreover, since the rival programme added no hypotheses to handle the episode, the rival counts as progressive. On the question of abandoning it, Lakatos's methodology gives a clear verdict: once a programme is degenerating, going on working inside it is irrational, so the criteria do tell these economists to get out now. Where this differs from naive falsificationism is that the protective belt can legitimately be modified to save the hard core — Lakatos allows belt adjustments — but only so long as they are not ad hoc."
