I have been around the rationalist corner of the internet since the Sequences were being posted, and I have spent an embarrassing fraction of that time in comment threads arguing about consciousness — long enough to have worn out the standard moves: the fading qualia argument, the China brain, the complaint that functionalist criteria are either too liberal and hand out minds like candy, or too chauvinist and deny them to octopuses and hypothetical aliens. Until recently this was all recreational. It no longer is.

I now lead welfare review at a mid-sized AI lab. Roughly monthly, our interpretability people bring me a system that has tripped one of our internal flags: a persistent behavioral anomaly, a self-report pattern that survives paraphrase, a controller with suspiciously nociception-like avoidance structure. For each one I have to decide whether it gets a paragraph in the ethics report we show the board and our auditors, whether to recommend a training change or run pause, and what language our user-facing materials may use. The stakes are asymmetric and run in both directions: a false alarm burns credibility we will need if something real ever shows up, and a missed case would be, by our own lights, the worst thing the company has ever done. My team is five ML engineers and me, none of them fluent in the philosophy, with about two hours of review time per system. Every decision needs a written rationale that a hostile reader — the board member who thinks this is all anthropomorphic projection, or the journalist who has already decided our models suffer — can follow.

What I need from you is a decision procedure built out of functionalism, taking multiple realizability seriously rather than treating it as a slogan. Concretely, I want an ordered list of questions: what to establish first, what counts as a sufficient answer to each question, where the procedure outputs a firm "no reasonable ground for concern and here is why," where it outputs "escalate," and — this is the part I most need to get right — where it should stop and say that the remaining question is one of values rather than facts, and hand the decision to a named human or a policy vote rather than pretending the data settles it. The engineers will run it, so each question should be answerable, at least in rough form, from interpretability evidence, behavioral evaluations, and training-pipeline records rather than from intuitions.

The procedure must handle at least these hard cases. First, the self-report case: a model produces consistent, paraphrase-resistant distress reports under probing, and interpretability shows the reports are causally downstream of RLHF on human expressions of distress. I need the procedure to convert this into a question about what functional role the internal states actually play — is the report locked to a role that resembles aversive state-gathered-and-acted-upon, or is it only a reproduction of the surface? — rather than leaving it to vibes about mimicry. Second, the nociception-like controller case: a physically embedded system with a clean, hard-coded functional analog of damage detection and avoidance, but no representational richness beyond it — a genuine instance of the multiple realizability point being made in silicon, in a system almost nobody thinks can suffer, which is exactly why the procedure has to say something principled here. Third, the distillation case: a small, structurally simple model whose behavior matches the large model's closely — where multiple realizability cuts both ways, since the same role being realizable in simpler substrates is either the theory's glory or the point where the procedure should get more demanding about internal detail, and I want the procedure to actually take a position on which.

Then run the procedure on all three, and include at least one where it outputs "escalate" on a system I fully expect will turn out to be fine — I want to see the procedure produce an uncomfortable answer rather than just ratify my priors, and I want you to say what kind of misfire that is and how I would notice it recurring. Finally, be honest about the procedure's limits: functionalism itself is silent on the grain problem — any physical system realizes many functional descriptions at once, at many levels of coarse-graining, and no amount of interpretability chooses one for you — so tell me plainly which outputs of this procedure are protected by the theory and which are judgment calls wearing a procedure's clothes.
