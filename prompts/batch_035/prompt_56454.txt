I am grading a written exam for an upper-level undergraduate course on rational choice and decision theory. The course covered causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and then updateless decision theory (UDT), including its treatment of Newcomb-style problems; students were told that UDT evaluates candidate policies from a standpoint prior to the agent's observations, rather than evaluating individual acts given everything the agent knows. Please grade the following script. Assign a letter grade (A–F, with +/− as appropriate) and justify it with point-by-point comments: I want comments specific enough to show the student which sentences are correct, which are mistaken, and why the mistaken ones are mistaken — including any errors that a hasty grader would let slide. Where a claim is defensible but loosely stated, please say so, and distinguish it from the outright errors. Close by indicating what a fully correct answer to part (c) would have required.

The exam question:

"Consider the following variant of Newcomb's problem. Box B is transparent. Earlier, a predictor — whose predictions are near-perfect — forecast the agent's choice and filled box B with $1,000,000 if and only if it predicted that the agent would take only box B. Box A, which is opaque, contains $1,000 throughout. The agent then looks into box B and observes that it does contain the million. (a) What does causal decision theory recommend at this point, and why? (b) What does updateless decision theory recommend, and why? (c) Explain why the recommendation in (b) is not simply an evidential argument, and say where, if anywhere, the agent's observation of the full box enters the UDT analysis."

The student's answer:

"(a) CDT says take both boxes. The predictor acted in the past, and the contents of box B are fixed now, so nothing the agent does can causally affect them. Taking both boxes therefore causally dominates taking only B: the agent gets the $1,000 in box A for sure, and the million either way. So a CDT agent two-boxes even after seeing the million.

(b) UDT says take only box B. The reasoning is that an agent who one-boxes in this kind of situation is an agent who tends to find the million in box B; one-boxing is good evidence that the predictor filled the box, so by conditioning on its own decision the agent can expect more money. In this respect UDT behaves like evidential decision theory: both recommend one-boxing, and for the same reason — one-boxers walk away rich.

(c) 'Updateless' means the agent does not update: it ignores what it has observed and acts as if it were still at the beginning, before looking into box B. That is why it can one-box even though it can see the million sitting there. The policy plays the role of a precommitment the agent signed at the dawn of time; since the agent never updates, the observation of the full box never enters the analysis at all."
