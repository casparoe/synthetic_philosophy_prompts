Two friends of mine have been at each other for like two years now. One is a committed fast-takeoff person: he thinks once AI systems can meaningfully speed up AI research itself, we get a recursive loop, the loop compounds, and some single project blows past everyone else in months or even weeks — a hard-to-reverse decisive strategic advantage. The other is a committed continuous/gradual-takeoff person: she thinks every input to AI progress (compute, data, algorithms, researcher-hours) scales smoothly, so AI improving AI research just adds a smoothly growing term to an already-smooth curve, and the world economy absorbs it the way it absorbed electrification — huge over decades, not a blink-and-you-missed-it discontinuity. They've finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration and want a draft joint statement to work from. Please write that draft.

Specifically I want:

1. A numbered section of claims both could sign. Cover at least: that AI has already sped up some parts of AI development (autocomplete, chip design, code assistance); that there is no known physical or mathematical barrier to AI that outperforms humans at AI R&D; that takeoff speed and what policies make sense are at least somewhat linked but not identical questions; and whatever else you think genuinely belongs in the overlap. Don't pad this with platitudes — every item should be something one of them might actually have resisted signing a year ago.

2. A disagreements section (table is fine) with each remaining dispute phrased so that its own side would accept the wording. E.g. the fast-takeoff person will sign "feedback loops, once real, tend to concentrate advantage because the leader's gains feed back into its own rate of gain" but not "takeoff will be gradual because all previous technology transitions were"; the continuous person will sign something like the reverse. Find those formulations — the sentence each side reads and says "yes, that's my view, stated fairly."

3. The cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them. My guesses for candidates, so you can push back on these: (a) whether there are diminishing returns to intelligence applied to AI research itself, or whether the returns structure has a region where output compounds faster than the effort put in; (b) whether progress historically has been roughly continuous in *aggregate* but can still be sharply discontinuous for *a single leading project*; (c) how much of the speedup so far attributed to "AI helping AI research" is real signal versus hype; (d) whether compute, not algorithmic cleverness, is the binding constraint, since compute doesn't get recursively cheaper fast. But find the real cruxes, not just mine.

4. For each crux, a concrete argument, historical case, or observable that both would agree bears on it — plus each side's prediction about it, written down in advance like a real preregistration. E.g. if the observable is "does the measured fraction of AI-research output that is itself AI-produced trend smoothly or with a jump over the next few years," say what each of them predicts and why. Historical analogies count as bearing on the crux only if both of them already accept the analogy as relevant at all — flag where an analogy is itself part of the dispute (I suspect the "software-only startup outcompeting incumbents" analogy is like this).

5. If any part of their fight turns out to be verbal — e.g. they mean different things by "takeoff," or one means "time from AGI to world-economy-doubling-in-four-years" and the other means "time from AGI to a single actor being able to dictate terms" — say which part, and propose the definitions that would make the residual disagreement empirical.

Keep the drafting fair: the statement shouldn't read like the fast-takeoff person ghostwrote it or vice versa. If one side genuinely has a stronger position on some crux, let that show up only in the predictions, not in slanted phrasing. And a couple of places you should note "both sides flagged this item as contested wording" — a real adversarial collaboration has a few of those and I want them in.
