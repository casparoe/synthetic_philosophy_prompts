My flatmate is a hardcore Lewisian and last night she talked me into an argument I can't get out of. (1) On modal realism, possible worlds aren't descriptions or fictions -- they're concrete universes, all equally real, just causally and spatiotemporally cut off from ours. (2) For every choice I ever face, there are worlds where my counterpart takes each of the available options, so every option I could take gets taken, and every outcome I could care about obtains somewhere. (3) If an outcome obtains no matter what I do, then choosing is pointless, because the whole point of choosing is to affect which outcomes obtain. (4) Under modal realism, every outcome obtains no matter what I do -- whichever way I go here, the full menu of outcomes is realized across the worlds, and the total inventory of everything that happens is fixed. (5) So under modal realism, deliberation is pointless: it makes no difference whether I give to charity, cheat on my partner, whatever -- reality as a whole tells exactly the same total story either way. (6) But deliberation obviously isn't pointless, and I'm not about to start treating my own choices as theater. (7) So modal realism is false.

She keeps saying I'm smuggling in a bad notion of "outcome" but refuses to say where, which is annoying because I can sort of feel a slide happening between (3) and (4) without being able to name it. And honestly the part I like least is (5) -- the world where I shrug and stop deliberating doesn't look like a world where I've learned something true. Which step should I give up, and what does giving it up cost me?
