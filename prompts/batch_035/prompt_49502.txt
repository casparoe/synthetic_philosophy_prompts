Longtime lurker here finally trying to write the post I keep promising people. I run a small rationalist-adjacent blog and I'm tired of the alignment discourse being a fight between vibes, so I want to build a decision-tree post titled "which take on prosaic alignment do you actually hold?" for readers who know the jargon but have mostly absorbed their position from whoever they read first.

The tree should start from the premise everyone in this fight at least pretends to accept: that transformative AI plausibly gets built out of systems recognizably descended from today's -- big neural nets, gradient descent, RLHF-style feedback on top -- without a paradigm shift coming to the rescue first. That's the prosaic premise, the shared trunk. From there I want the tree built from the logical space, not from a list of faction names: work out the questions first, then locate the real camps in the combinations of answers.

The distinctions that matter for my purposes, so use these (or close to them) as your branch nodes:

- whether today's feedback methods are transmitting human preferences at all, or just training a policy to score well on a proxy whose alignment is basically coincidental;
- whether the scary failure modes (deceptive alignment, whatever you want to call it) are already latent in current systems under optimization pressure or only show up past some capability threshold;
- whether interpretability work can ever substitute for an actual theory of alignment, or whether it's structurally forever post-hoc;
- whether a lab scaling up under competitive pressure can be trusted to run the safety experiments at all, or whether that question is downstream of the others.

Leaves that have to appear somewhere: the prosaic optimist ("it's an engineering discipline, we'll muddle through"), the full doom camp, the "we need new theory before anyone scales further" camp, the "we can bootstrap oversight tools using the systems themselves" camp, and the "the whole goal-directed framing is confused and that's why it's fine" camp. If some combination of your branch answers doesn't correspond to anyone real, say so at that leaf and diagnose why nobody's standing there -- is it incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Hard constraint: every node has to be answerable by someone who's never heard of any of the camps. No "do you basically agree with Eliezer?" nodes, no asking people which label they identify with. Wherever you can, make the node a concrete case instead of an abstract question -- e.g., "your model passes every eval your team can write; do you update toward safe, or toward 'the thing we care about isn't measurable'?" And keep it to at most ten branch nodes so it's actually postable as one diagram.

Two more asks. First, flag any pair of leaves your tree structurally cannot separate -- if two camps end up answering every node identically and only differ in mood, I want that admitted, not papered over. Second, since my blog is basically me arguing with myself, once the tree is laid out I want you to walk through it yourself, giving your honest answer at each node, and end the whole post with a final line in exactly this form:

Verdict: [leaf name]

so my readers can fight about whether you sorted yourself right in the comments. Becuase honestly, the tree is only useful if it can sort the person who built it.
