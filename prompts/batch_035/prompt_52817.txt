I should begin by saying who is asking. I spent just under four decades as a geophysicist, most of it concerned with inferring the structure of the mantle from seismic travel times, and since retiring I have been reading philosophy in a disciplined way, working from an introductory logic text into the philosophy of language. I have now read enough about truth to be puzzled, and what I want from you is a number rather than a survey.

The puzzle is this. In my working life, truth seemed to function in two different ways. In day-to-day inference — checking a velocity model against a seismogram — an attribution of truth appeared to do nothing beyond assertion: to say that a model was true for a given region was, in practice, just to assert the model. Yet the institution I worked in treated truth as an achievement with a nature worth investigating: grant proposals promised to uncover the true structure of the Earth, review panels asked whether we were getting closer to the truth, and we took the convergence of independent measurement methods — seismic, gravitational, geochemical — as evidence that we were closing in on something. As I understand it, deflationism says the first appearance is the whole story: the truth predicate is exhausted by the equivalence schema, whereby the proposition that p is true if and only if p, and there is no further substantive property of truth for a theory to analyse. Inflationary views — correspondence theories most prominently — say otherwise.

Here is the proposition I want your credence on, stated as precisely as I can manage: deflationism about truth is correct, in the sense that (a) truth is not a substantive property whose analysis is a genuine theoretical project, (b) the entire content of the truth concept is fixed by the equivalence schema and its instances, and (c) every legitimate explanatory or normative use of the word true can be discharged by that schema alone, without remainder.

Please give the number as a decomposition, not as a bare answer. At minimum, include terms for the following, each with a probability and a sentence or two of justification:

1. The probability that truth has no hidden nature — that there is no substantive property there to be analysed, so that the analytic question about truth is exhausted by the schema.
2. Conditional on the first term, the probability that the explanatory uses survive: when we explain a colleague's successful prediction by saying their model was true, or explain a theory's long-run success by appeal to its truth, can the schema's instances carry all of that weight, or does explanation of that kind require a substantive property?
3. The probability that the normative role of truth — its standing as the norm of assertion and perhaps of belief — is either capturable deflationarily or else no part of the truth concept at all, so that a failure there would not count against deflationism.
4. The probability that the semantic paradoxes, the Liar above all, do not discriminate between deflationism and its rivals — that a deflationist can absorb them without smuggling in a substantive notion of truth.

Please also tell me whether further terms belong in the decomposition, and combine the numbers into an overall credence. My own tentative credence sits around 0.6, though I confess it reflects indecision more than evidence; if yours differs materially, I would like to know where the disagreement most likely lives — which term, that is, we would be arguing about at dinner. Then answer two further questions. First, which term are you least confident in, and what argument or kind of evidence would move it most? Second, a question nearer to my old trade: does the practice I described — treating the convergence of independent methods as evidence that we are approaching the truth — presuppose an inflationary view, or can a deflationist make just as good sense of it? If the practice turns out to be genuinely compatible only with inflationism, that is a fact about my own profession that I would like to know, and I would count it as a strike against deflationism however the arithmetic comes out.
