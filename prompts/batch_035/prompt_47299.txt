Two of my colleagues in the epistomology reading group have been at each other for years about bootstrapping / easy knowledge. One thinks the reasoning is fine: a source that in fact delivers knowledge can just give you knowledge, full stop, and any view that forbids bootstrapping leads to scepticism or an infinite regress of reliability checks. The other is convinced the pattern is epistemically circular -- using a source to confirm its own reliability, like the person who checks their thermometer is working by reading the thermometer and comparing it to... the thermometer. They've finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration and asked me to draft the joint statement, but every draft I write gets rejected by one of them for slanting the wording.

Write the document for me: a numbered section of claims both could sign (about the bootstrapping pattern itself and why it strikes people as fishy); a disagreements section where each side's position is phrased so that that side would accept its own wording; the cruxes -- my guess is one is whether the circularity is a defect in the reasoning itself or only in the reasoner who can't antecedently know the source is reliable, but pull out others if you see them; and for each crux, some case, argument or thought experiment both would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If part of their dispute is verbal -- say, over whether "knowledge" or some weaker status is what the bootstrapper gets -- say which part and why. And be honest if there's a crux neither of them could settle with anything they'd both accept; I suspect the deepest one is like that, and I'd rather know now than after another decade of sniping.

Please write in British English, and keep it to a length the two of them could actually read and sign at a meeting.
