My brother in law and I have been having basically the same argument for two years and I've finally realized we're not disagreeing about any actual case, we just use different words. It started with the classic one-boat-two-islands thing (5 people on one island, 1 on the other, you can only reach one) — he says flip a coin, I say obviously row to the five — and its spiraled from there into every variation we can think of. So now I want to make one of those online quizzes, called something like "do the numbers actually count?", that sorts people by what they would DO in cases, not by which label they like. Alot of people who say "flip a coin" have never checked whether they'd still flip it with a million people on one side.

Write me 9 questions. Each one a concrete rescue-type scenario with 2 or 3 answer options, and a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions: numbers count straight up, the flip-a-coin / everybody-gets-an-equal-chance view, the "numbers only start counting past some threshold" view, and the view that no one's claim beats anyone else's so it just doesn't matter what you pick. These cases have to be in there somewhere: (1) 1 vs 5 strangers, (2) 1 vs 1,000,000, (3) 5 vs 5 where one side is a family and the other is strangers, (4) save 1 person for sure vs save all 5 but only with a 20% chance, (5) prevent 100 people from each breaking a leg vs prevent 1 death, and (6) a case where YOU are secretly one of the five and don't know it yet when you pick. For the remaining questions pick whatever cases split the positions apart best.

Two things I care about: no question can just straight up ask "do the numbers count?" — that's the whole point of the quiz, people should be answerable without knowing the labels. And after the scoring key, tell me where the quiz is likely to fail: which two positions end up impossible to separate no matter what you ask, which single question is most likely to sort someone into the wrong bucket, and what you'd change about it if you had to bet. Also flag what answer pattern would show up in the data that doesn't fit any leaf, and what that person probably actually believes.
