I'm a journalist — mostly science and ideas, long-form features for a British current affairs magazine. I'm writing a profile of my old editor Dilys, who for twenty-odd years was the person every story with a privacy question got run past before it went anywhere near print. She's retired now but still terrifyingly consistent, and the piece is basically about how she actually decided things, because on the surface her rulings look all over the shop.

Her track record, as best I've reconstructed it:

1. We named a councillor who was seeing a lobbyist whose firm had a planning application she'd voted on — affair and all. Dilys said run it. Their defence was they'd only ever met in public places, restaurants, parties etc., and she basically said if you conduct a relationship in public you can't whinge about privacy.

2. A presenter who'd done a documentary on organised crime was getting threats, and one of our writers had pieced together her home address from land registry records and old photos. Dilys spiked the whole section and was furious it had even been drafted.

3. We got senior council officers' exact salaries through an FOI request and named them alongside a story about the council's finances. Fine by her.

4. A teacher at a struggling school had a friends-only Instagram with photos of her drunk at a wedding, and a parent leaked them to us. She wouldn't touch it, even though it briefly looked like the school might act against the teacher, which arguably made it newsy.

5. The obituaries one. A well-known chef had told us on the record years ago about his addictions, so we could rerun all of that after he died. But when his ex-wife offered us details of a suicide attempt he'd never disclosed, she refused to run that bit even though he was dead and it arguably explained half his career.

Now the new case. A minister resigned citing family reasons, but he'd told an interviewer the year before that he was completely fit to serve, and there's a leak of pages from his psychotherapist's notes suggesting a relapse he was hiding. Everyone assumes he'll run for leader next year. Our current editor wants Dilys's read before anything runs.

I'd like to predict her verdict before I ask her — there's a bet riding on it. What rule do you think she's running, what other rules fit the track record just as well, what does each of them say about the therapy notes, and is there a next case that would actually seperate the rules? Also, does her refusal in the obituaries one give us any grip on how she'd treat the fact that the minister might not even be in a state to know what's been published about him if he deteriorates? I realise that's a weird angle but it feels like the sort of thing she'd have a view on.
