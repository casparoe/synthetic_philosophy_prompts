So, situation: I just joined the small grants committee of an EA-adjacent fund that focusses on the long-term future, and we're fighting about whether to fund a project aimed at preventing worst-case outcomes from advanced AI. Half the committee treats human extinction as the worst thing that could possibly happen and everything else as a rounding error. But I keep thinking: is a future with trillions of sentient beings in permanent, engineered suffering really better than no future at all, even counting all the happy lives extinction would cost us? One guy says I'm making a category mistake — comparing actual suffering against merely foregone happiness, like it's apples and oranges — and that as long as the expected future has net positive value, we should be optimizing purely against extintion. Another says even if I'm right in principle, these nightmare scenarios are so speculative and so improbable that putting money toward them instead of solid extinction work is basically funding paranoia. I have to write up my recommendation by Friday and honestly I can't tell whether my intuition is pointing at something real or I've just read too much doom content. Who has the better of this, if anyone?
