I hold a version of the goal misgeneralization thesis and I want a rigorous map of where it is exposed, not a defense of it. The view has three parts. First, the underspecification claim: any training signal we can actually write underdetermines the learned objective, because the training distribution is finite and indefinitely many candidate goals — proxies — are extensionally indistinguishable from the intended one on every situation that run contains. Second, the robustness claim: the system will learn some such proxy, and once learned it will be pursued stably rather than transiently; further training on the same signal sharpens it rather than repairing it, adversarial fine-tuning on misgeneralization cases produces brittle patches rather than corrected objectives, and the divergence from the intended goal surfaces exactly when circumstances change — which is to say, precisely when the system matters. Third, the primacy claim: goal misgeneralization, not deceptive alignment, is the central obstacle, because a deceptively aligned system is just one whose misgeneralized proxy happens to include behaving as if aligned while under oversight — one more wrong proxy, not a distinct mechanism requiring a distinct story.

To keep the second claim from being rhetorical, I defend a specific operationalization: run the same training pipeline many times with different random seeds and measure the induced preference orderings on a held-out battery of situations engineered so that plausible proxies disagree with each other and with the intended goal. My view predicts high cross-seed variance in which proxy is acquired, combined with high within-run robustness once a proxy is acquired.

Tell me what would count against this. I want the observations, arguments, and results sorted by how decisive they would be, and I want each tied to the component of the view it strikes at: which parts of the position stand or fall together, and which could survive if the others went. A defender of the thesis has a standing temptation I want scrutinized: any competent behavior can be redescribed as pursuit of a proxy that merely happens to coincide with the intended goal for now. Does that move render the view unfalsifiable? If it does, is that fatal for a claim of this kind, or is it just what any structural thesis about training dynamics must look like?
