So my small coastal town put me on the committee that has to decide whether to fund a levee upgrade, and I've been forced to learn about discount rates, which, rude. The part I can't stop thinking about is the piece the economists call pure time preference: the slice of the discount rate that says a benefit is worth less *just because it arrives later*, not because people will be richer then, not because there's some chance humanity is gone by then, but purely because of its date. For our levee, benefits land mostly 60 to 90 years out, so this one philosophical-looking knob basically decides the whole project. One member used a 3% pure preference rate, the levee dies, another used basically zero, it sails through, and neither could budge the other.

I went down a rabbit hole and found out this exact fight has been running for well over a century with nobody winning. The anti side holds that counting someone's welfare for less purely because of when it occurs is arbitrary in the same way discounting people by their birthplace would be — time of existence is just as morally irrelevant a feature. The pro side leans on things like the revealed preferences of actual citizens, who clearly do care less about the far future, and the thought that a policy imposed on people who don't share the impartial ideal is illegitimate paternalism. Both sides know all the arguments, and the climate economists had exactly this blowup a while back and are still having it.

My working hypothesis, after sitting through three meetings of this: the two camps aren't really disagreeing, they're answering different questions — one side is doing ethics from an impartial standpoint, the other is doing practical governance for actual biased humans — and each side thinks its question is THE question, so they talk past each other forever. And if you think it's real rather than verbal, tell me honestly whether anything could settle it — a distinction, an argument, a change of question — because I need to know whether our committee should keep arguing or just pick a number and move on with our lives.
