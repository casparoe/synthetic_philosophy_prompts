I teach a graduate seminar on personal identity, and for our unit on origin essentialism I want a thought experiment that does something I have not managed to do with the standard cases. The usual examples set origin essentialism against its rivals — psychological-continuity views, best-candidate accounts — but they leave the essentialist's own apparatus untested, and I have come to suspect that origin essentialism is not one thesis but a family, since it is never quite specified which event fixes a person's origin. So: construct a case in which three natural precisifications of "origin" — the gametes, the zygote, and whichever later developmental event one might instead fix on — deliver three different answers to one and the same question, where that question concerns who exists, when a given person began to exist, or whether some individual could have failed to exist while the relevant cells lived on.

The constraints are these. Nothing in the scenario may exceed biology that actually occurs: identical twinning by embryo splitting, blastomere biopsy, embryo freezing, and tetragametic chimerism are all fair game, but no cloning vats, no teleporters, and no convenient genetic coincidences that quietly guarantee identity or difference. The central question should be one a court, a fertility clinic, or a parent could in principle have to answer, not a merely technical puzzle. And the divergence must be generated by the structure of the case rather than stipulated — a case whose description already reads off the answer ("the person is the one who developed from the zygote") fails, because that is exactly what is at issue.

After presenting the scenario, run each of the three precisifications through it and show the reasoning from precisification to verdict, so that the case could be graded like an exam question. Then identify which structural feature of the scenario forces the split, say what happens to the case when that feature is removed, and give the best unified response available to the essentialist: either a principled selection of one origin-fixing event over the others, or a reformulation on which a person can have several origins at once. My seminar students should come away knowing whether origin essentialism is a determinate thesis or an ill-defined family of them.

Begin with the scenario itself; no preamble and no restatement of my request.
