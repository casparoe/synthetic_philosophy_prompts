for my counterfactual history course we just finished the path dependence unit — paul david's qwerty paper, brian arthur on increasing returns, gould's replay-the-tape idea, and then the skeptics who say even absent the lock-in, roughly the same outcome arrives anyway (someone else invents the lightbulb, etc). our last session is a kind of show and tell, so: write a sestina about this where the form does the arguing. the six end-words are fixed once the first stanza exists and come back in a rigid rotation no matter what the stanzas are about — that's the lock-in, and i want the poem to be aware of it. the stanzas themselves should tell one concrete lock-in story from the middle of it, not a history lecture — e.g. standard gauge railways, a keyboard layout, or the internal combustion engine beating out the electric car around 1900. pick one and make it about the moment the dye was cast, the small event that tips it, not the whole arc. then the envoi is the counterfactual: it either suggests the same poem would have gotten written with six different end-words (the convergence view) or that the end-words made the poem what it is (contingency) — commit to one and earn it, don't dodge. someone in my class who knows what a sestina is should be able to see the mapping between the form and path dependence without me explaining it.
