My friend is really into these big-history documentaries and after the latest one he gave me his whole summary of the "world is getting less violent" thing. Basically he said: violence has been dropping in a pretty straight line since we came down from the trees, the numbers prove it — your odds of dying at another human's hands were way higher in a tribal or medieval society than they are now, and that's per capita, so it's not just that there are more of us around. Wars between the big rich countries have basically stopped, so if the trend holds we're headed toward near zero. And anyone who disagrees is just being nostalgic for some fake peaceful past.

I'm honestly inclined to believe him, I like good news, but a few things bugged me and I couldn't put my finger on exactly why.

One: he kept sliding between rates and raw numbers of victims. If the rate keeps falling but the population keeps climbing, couldn't the total number of people dying violently keep going up while the "decline" story stays technically true? Which of those am I supposed to actually care about? And here's something that occured to me at 2am — if some plague wiped out 90% of humanity, the survivors would probably have beautiful violence stats. Does that mean the per-capita measure is broken, or is that a dumb objection?

Two: everything he counted was people getting killed directly. Does the story change if you count preventable starvation, or mass incarceration, or how we treat animals? Is "violence" something you can just measure, or does deciding what goes in the numerator already decide half the answer? He kept saying "the numbers don't lie" like it's a plain fact of the matter, but the whole thing seems to rest on which things got counted.

So — was his summary fair to what the people making this argument actually claim? Which parts would they stand behind, which parts are overstated or subtly off, and does his "we can basically stop worrying about big wars" conclusion actually follow from the accurate parts?
