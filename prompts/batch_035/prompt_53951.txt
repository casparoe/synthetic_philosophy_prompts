My uncle, a retired electrical engineer, was diagnosed a month ago with late-stage pancreatic cancer, and he has now decided to refuse all treatment — not out of fear or resignation, but because of an argument he takes to be airtight. For years he has been sold on the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics — the idea that whenever events could go different ways, the world splits and every way of going actually happens, each in its own branch. Recently he came across videos and forum threads that push this to a conclusion he now regards as settled: somewhere in the branching tree there is guaranteed to be a version of him whose tumor spontaneously regresses, or who is the one-in-a-million patient saved by an experimental drug, or who simply outlives the odds. And since the versions of him that die will have no experiences after dying, he says, every version of him that exists at any later moment will be one that survived. From the inside, he concludes, he can never experience his own death, so declining treatment changes nothing he will ever witness — the only future he can ever find himself in is one where he pulls through.

I've tried to argue, but everything I come up with seems beside the point. Telling him that the overwhelming majority of branches of him die doesn't faze him, because those branches contain nobody left to complain about it. Pointing out that many surviving versions of him would be in terrible shape or prolonging the dying rather than escaping it doesn't get far either, since he answers that some suffering is just the price of guaranteed survival, and that at least one branch of him gets the good outcome. Saying it's all a misreading of physics feels hollow when I can't say exactly where the misreading is. So: is his reasoning actually sound given the picture of the world he accepts, or is there a real flaw in it, and if so, where exactly does the chain break? I'd especially like to know whether the step from 'every branch in which I still exist is a branch in which I survived' to 'I should expect to survive' is legitimate, or whether it quietly assumes something about probability, or about what counts as 'me' across branches, that many-worlds doesn't hand him for free. And is there anything I can honestly say that would make refusing treatment look like a mistake even on his own picture of the world, rather than a mistake about the physics itself?
