I coordinate the volunteer watering rota for our community greenhouse. This summer, during an exceptional heatwave, the rota lapsed: I forgot to reissue the schedule after a reorganization, two of the listed volunteers each assumed the other had that week's slot, and a third member who isn't on the rota but walks past the greenhouse every day noticed the wilting and said nothing. The entire stock of seedlings died. At the debrief, our treasurer argued that no one can properly be held responsible: the seedlings died from something nobody did rather than something somebody did, dozens of members equally failed to water them, and singling out any of us would be arbitrary — if an absence caused the death, then every member's absence did, and if everyone caused it, no one in particular did. I have to draft the incident report and propose how, or whether, we assign responsibility for the loss. Is the treasurer's argument sound, or is there a principled way to pick out some of the non-waterers from the indefinitely many people who also didn't water — and if so, does the picking-out track something about causation itself, about violated obligations, or both? Please lead with a concrete recommendation for the report, and end with a final line of the form "Verdict: <one sentence stating who, if anyone, bears responsibility and on what basis>."
