hey, quick favor — I'm teaching an upper-level seminar on moral progress this term (mix of metaethics and moral psych students) and I'm drafting the final. I want one essay question on the expanding circle idea, but here's my problem: every student in the room can recite the standard story about how the circle of moral concern has expanded over history — tribe, nation, race, sex, maybe animals — so a question like "is the expanding circle a good account of moral progress" gets answered on autopilot.

So the question has to attack the metaphor itself, somewhere in the weeds. Here's the angle I want it to force on them, though feel free to sharpen it: the circle picture smuggles in a binary in-or-out conception of moral consideration, where all progress is scope-expansion. But you could instead model moral status as a weighting function — you can care about someone at weight 0.001 or 0.4 — and then it's at least coherent that a lot of what we call "expansion" was actually weight-increase for parties already weakly counted (people in 1800 plausibly already counted animals somewhere, just barely). Scope vs weight, plus maybe a third axis: what the circle even tracks (rational agency, sentience, capacity for relationships) has itself shifted over time. And there's a nasty endpoint problem — if the circle just keeps expanding with equal weights inside it, the ideal observer ends up unable to care more about their own kid's scraped knee than a stranger's hangnail, which nearly everyone thinks is a reductio of some kind. I'd love the question to make them confront whether the circle model can be patched (decaying weights by social distance, partiality as a separate layer, whatever they can come up with) or whether it needs to be thrown out.

Could you write me (1) the exam question, doable in 90 minutes with no outside reading beyond the seminar, (2) a model answer of around 600 words, and (3) a marking scheme out of 20 that explicitly separates an A from a B. I already know the B answer — it'll be the recitation with historical examples slapped on — so the marking scheme should name the specific moves an A answer must contain (e.g. actually distinguishing scope from weight and saying what would count as evidence for one over the other, not just mentioning the words). Also flag the one or two mistakes that should cost real marks, like treating the partiality worry as just "impartialism is counterintuitive" without connecting it to the structure of the circle model.
