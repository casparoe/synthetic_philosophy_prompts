I've just come off a very long argument with my closest friend, who is a physics graduate student, and I would like someone to adjudicate it in terms I actually command. He defends the block universe: the view that past, present, and future events all exist in exactly the same sense, so that the passage of time is not a feature of reality at all. He rested his case on the Rietdijk–Putnam argument (Rietdijk's 1966 paper in Philosophy of Science; Putnam's "Time and Physical Geometry" from 1967). As he presented it: in special relativity there is no observer-independent fact about which distant events are simultaneous with a given event; each inertial observer carves spacetime into past, present, and future along their own plane of simultaneity, and observers in relative motion carve it differently. If one observer's present contains events that lie in another observer's future, and we have no grounds for privileging either carving, then everything that anyone could ever call present, past, or future already exists — determinism, or at least the "already-thereness" of the future, is supposed to follow. He also invoked Penrose's Andromeda paradox, in which two people passing each other on the street disagree about whether, in Penrose's words, "according to one of the two people, an Andromedean space fleet has already set off on its journey, while to the other, the decision as to whether or not the journey will actually take place has not yet been made."

Here is my situation. I am a distributed systems engineer with no physics training beyond popular books, but I am fluent in my field's vocabulary: Lamport clocks and the happens-before relation, causal partial orders, vector clocks, consistent cuts and global snapshots, linearizability and the other consistency models, total order broadcast, consensus, replicated state machines, and event-sourced architectures with append-only logs. My instinct throughout the argument was that my friend is describing something I deal with every day and drawing a conclusion I would never draw from it: we have no global clock, concurrent events admit no privileged interleaving, and different observers can totally order the same partial order of events in different ways — and none of that makes me think the log entries that have not yet been written somehow already exist.

What I would like you to do is restate the Rietdijk–Putnam argument in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then report the residue. A few candidate mappings occurred to me during the argument — an inertial frame as a rule for extending the causal partial order to a total order; a plane of simultaneity as something like a cut through the event history; the Andromeda paradox as two observers linearizing concurrent events differently — but I want you to check whether these mappings are honest or whether they quietly change the subject, and to correct or discard them where they do. Then give me the residue report in three parts: which steps of the argument survive the translation cleanly; which survive only with distortion, and what the distortion is; and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all. I am especially interested in the step from "there is no privileged global ordering of spacelike-separated events" to "all events are equally real," because in my world the first observation is mundane — we resolve it with consensus, or tolerate it with eventual consistency, or simply note that the divergence is unobservable — while the second seems to be an ontological claim my vocabulary never has occasion to make. On the other hand, an event-sourced system's append-only log is a fixed, four-dimensional record of everything the system ever did, so perhaps the block universe is just my world's log, and my instinct is the one smuggling in an assumption.

So, in the end: does the residue show that my friend's conclusion concerns something real that distributed systems genuinely cannot express, or does it show that his conclusion is the mundane observation about order-relativity dressed up in metaphysical language? Please do not soften the physics to spare my intuitions, and please write your answer in British English.
