My PhD advisor in history has a consistent line on objectivity that I have never managed to pin down, and a new book has just been assigned for her seminar, so I would like a prediction before I walk in. Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it: she praised a dense archival reconstruction of a seventeenth-century village for its evidential care but complained that its refusal to state a thesis was itself a hidden thesis, since the world it quietly depicted was orderly and consensual; she respected an openly Marxist account of an industrial dispute despite rejecting its framework, because its commitments were declared up front, though she faulted it for moving too quickly past incidents that did not fit the class narrative; she dismissed a bestselling narrative of a revolution that presented its sense of doom as something the sources had forced on the author, remarking that the same letters could just as well have carried a farce; and she rejected a computational study's claim that digitized archives and systematic coding had removed interpretation, on the ground that the coding decisions were themselves interpretive judgments. The new book is an archival history of a slave rebellion. Its preface states that it is written from the rebels' standpoint, on the argument that the surviving sources were produced overwhelmingly by the victors, so that claimed neutrality would simply deliver the victors' view a second time; it flags, honestly, every point at which the evidence complicates its sympathies; but it tells its story as an arc from degradation to self-forged freedom, never remarking that this shape is a choice, and the jacket claims that the book finally shows the rebellion as it actually was. Predict her verdict, explain the rule she seems to be running, lay out the other rules that fit her track record at least as well — naming the tradition each is closest to — and identify the further book, describable in a single sentence, that would best separate those rules. I care less about the verdict itself than about whether the candidate rules genuinely diverge on this case rather than merely in the abstract, so tell me honestly if they do not.
