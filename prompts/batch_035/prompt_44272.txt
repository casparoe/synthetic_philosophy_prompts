Sooo for background: I've been doomscrolling about AI risk and various human-extinction scenarios, and I noticed something weird about my own reactions that I don't have a word for.

Here's the thought experiment I keep running on myself. Compare two futures. In both, I live to exactly 80 and die in my sleep on the same date. In one, humanity carries on afterwards like normal. In the other, some asteroid or whatever takes everyone out maybe 30 years after my death — after everyone currently alive is already gone, so nobody I will ever meet dies a single day earlier than they otherwise would. From the inside of my life the two futures are identical: same people, same experiences, same everything right up to my last breath. And yet the second one feels flatly worse, and worse in a way I can't locate. Nobody seems to be harmed by the difference — the people who don't get born in scenario two don't exist to be harmed, and everyone who does exist has the exact life they'd have had anyway. So where does the extra badness even live? Is it real, or is it some illusion my brain is running?

And here's the weirder part. When I actually sit with the no-after scenario, it doesn't just feel bad, it drains the point out of things I do now. I'm halfway through writing a local history of my town, and thinking "and after 2080 no one will ever read any book again" makes the whole project feel kind of silly. Same with the oak I planted, and sort of with saving for my niece's kid's education. But — and this is what confuses me — ice cream doesn't lose its appeal. Music doesn't. A good evening with friends doesn't. Only the things that feel like they point beyond me go dead. To be clear it's not fear of death, I was always going to be dead by then either way and I've made peace with that. And it's not that I'm sentimental about humanity in the abstract, like I'll miss the human race the way you'd miss a friend — I honestly don't spend alot of time being fond of the species. And it's not that I want to be remembered, since in the normal scenario nobody remembers anybody either after a few generations. It's something about there being *a* afterwards at all.

So, my actual question: does this configuration have a name, or a literature attached to it? Specifically (1) the thing where activities with a beyond-me structure seem to need a continuing world of other people to keep their point, even when those other people will never interact with me or my work — is that a recognized phenomenon, and does it have a more precise label than just "meaning"? (2) the puzzle about where the extra badness of a no-future scenario resides when it can't be located in any individual — I gather there's a famous problem about future people who don't exist not being harmed by our choices, but in my setup nobody who exists is worse off either, so I feel like I've got some different edge of that problem, or maybe something else entirely, and I can't tell which. And (3) is it significant that pleasures survive the thought experiment and projects don't — does that split track any real distinction philosophers use, or is that just a quirk of my psychology?

I don't want to just get handed a term btw. What would actually help is the nearest named concepts, and where exactly my description pulls away from each of them. Even just a good word for the "activities that point beyond me" class would be useful, because I keep needing it and "legacy-ish stuff" isn't cutting it.
