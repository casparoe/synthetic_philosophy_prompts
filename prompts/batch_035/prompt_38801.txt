I'm a software engineer in the US, and I've been puzzling over the patchwork of rules that govern how much we're allowed to work. I'm salaried and classified as an exempt computer professional, so I get no overtime pay however many hours I put in, while the hourly contractor one desk over doing roughly the same work legally has to be paid time-and-a-half past forty hours. If I want to work eighty hours a week at one job, nothing stops me, and in the US I could also take on a second full-time job and nobody counts the total. But if I tried to sign a five-year contract binding myself to one employer at a fixed wage with no exit, that would be void as indentured servitude. On a sabbatical in France I ran into the opposite setup: a statutory 35-hour week, and a "right to disconnect" law under which my manager could face penalties for expecting me to answer Slack after hours. And I keep reading about karoshi cases in Japan, where courts award damages to families when a company's overtime expectations literally kill someone — while still letting the overtime itself happen.

What does this bundle of practices presuppose about consent and freedom? France seems to be saying that an employee's "voluntary" agreement to endless hours isn't really voluntary, because of the power imbalance — so the state must step in to protect people from choices they'd otherwise make. The US exemption for people like me seems to say the opposite: that a well-paid professional's consent to open-ended hours is genuine and should be honored. Yet even the US won't honor consent to fixed-term servitude, so somewhere there's a line between hours I can sell and things I can't sell at all. Is there a principled difference — duration, revocability, alienability of the will — or is the whole patchwork just history and lobbying? Put differently: if you had to reconstruct the implicit theory of what a worker can legitimately consent to, what would it look like, would it actually support all of these rules at once, and which rule would have to change first if we took the theory seriously?
