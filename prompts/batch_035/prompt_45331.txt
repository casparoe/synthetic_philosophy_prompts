I am a retired physical chemist who came to philosophy of language late, and I have been running a series of Twin Earth thought experiments past my wife to see what rule she is implicitly following. Her verdicts so far: on Putnam's original Twin Earth, where the lakes and taps contain XYZ rather than H2O, she holds that the locals' word "water" refers to XYZ, and that it did so in 1750, before anyone knew any chemistry; in the Burge-style case, she holds that the man who complains his arthritis has moved into his thigh still refers to arthritis; she holds that a speaker who cannot tell an elm from a beech refers to elms and to beeches equally well when he uses those words; and — here my background intrudes — when I showed her the data on heavy water (D2O: roughly ten percent denser than ordinary water, boiling near 101.4 °C, faintly sweet to the taste), she ruled that a beaker of it on Earth is water, just water of a heavier sort, since isotopic substitution does not change the compound. Next month I intend to put to her: (a) a Twin Earth whose oceans, rain, and taps contain pure D2O — does their "water" refer to water? (b) a Twin Earth whose watery liquid is a genuinely polymeric form of water with the same empirical composition but a different molecular structure (think of the polywater affair from my own field's history, except that the substance turns out to be real) — does their "water" refer to water? and (c) a Twin Earth whose northern oceans are H2O and whose southern oceans are XYZ, the two indistinguishable to the inhabitants, with both drunk and used indifferently — does their "water" refer to both, to neither, or does the reference somehow split? Predict her verdicts on these, reconstruct the rule or rules she is implicitly running — I can see at least two candidates, something like reference fixed by the role the stuff plays in the speakers' environment, versus reference fixed by shared underlying chemical structure, with the heavy-water verdict telling us where she draws the boundary of a structure, though there may be better candidates besides — say which rule you would bet on and why, and tell me what further case would most cleanly separate the candidates that survive. Please answer in American English.
