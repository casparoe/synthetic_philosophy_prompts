i own four rental units in a college town, and over the past couple years most of the small landlords here (me included) started using the same pricing software the big property management firm uses — it looks at what everyone in the area is charging and tells you to hold a unit empty rather than cut the rent. Rents town-wide have jumped, theres alot of anger, and now people are showing up at city council meetings demanding rent control.

I keep thinking about just dropping the software and going back to setting prices myself, which honestly feels like the decent thing to do. But my brother-in-law, who also rents out a couple places, says that's pointless purity: my four units leaving the system doesn't lower anyone's rent, I just lose income, and eventually I sell out to the firm that caused all this anyway — so the "moral" move ends with the worst actor owning more of the town. Is he right? And even if he's right about the money, it bugs me that "everyone else is doing it so my refusing alone accomplishes nothing" is exactly the reasoning that got us all here in the first place. Am I allowed to count that as a reason, or is that just me being sentimental about a business decision?
