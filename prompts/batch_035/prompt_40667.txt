hey so i need a villanelle, and im hoping you can actually put in the work on this one. pretend you're both a published poet and someone with a philosophy phd — think through the structure before you write anything, brainstorm a few candidate pairs of refrains first and choose the best pair rather than going with your first idea.

the subject is the paradox of fiction. the two refrains should be the two horns: one line asserting that you know the person in the book was never real, and one asserting something like — the grief was real anyway. the way a villanelle's refrains keep coming back and finally sit next to each other in the closing couplet should do the actual philosophical work here: every attempt to dissolve the paradox in the middle tercets should get interrupted by whichever line returns, and the ending should have the two claims side by side rather than one beating the other. someone who knows the debate should be able to tell which refrain is which.

the speaker: someone who has just finished a long doorstop of a novel in which a character dies near the end, and can't shake it. make it specific-feeling without naming any actual book.

here's the part where i want you to commit: don't hedge between the standard responses. pick one and let the poem take its side — either the walton-style make-believe line (the tears are real, but what they're about is fictional, we mourn-fictionally — quasi-grief), or the radford line (the emotion is genuine and we're simply irrational about it, and that's fine, humans are like that). the poem should read as fully committed to whichever you choose, and then afterwards add a short prose note — a paragraph at most — saying which reading you went with, where the poem takes liberties with the ideas, and one honest sentence about what the poem glosses over.

one extra bit of texture if you can fit it without breaking the form: the speaker remembers once crying over a memoir that turned out to be a hoax, and remembers how differently that sting felt from the novel — even though both were just words on a page. only include if it fits naturally, dont force it.

british english throughout please, and dont rhyme "real" with "feel" every single time, thats lazy.
