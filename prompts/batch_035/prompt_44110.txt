My two friends (Dev and Maya) had a long, kind of exhausting argument at dinner last weekend about Kelly betting, and they finally turned to me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Dev has been deep in poker bankroll management content lately and is now convinced Kelly is the uniquely rational way to size any repeated bet. His core claim: if you repeatedly bet the same fixed fraction of your bankroll on the same favorable coin, the full-Kelly bettor ends up with more money than someone betting any other fixed fraction, with probability one, eventually, and the gap keeps growing — so betting Kelly isn't a preference, it's just correct. Maya pushed back that this is a theorem about infinitely many bets and nobody gets infinitely many bets: over any finite number of rounds there's a real chance the full-Kelly bettor finishes behind a half-Kelly bettor, so the theorem doesn't transfer to actual lives the way he's using it. Dev then switched gears and said the deeper point is time averages versus ensemble averages — expected value averages over a population of parallel bettors, but you live one trajectory, and Kelly is what maximizes growth along a single trajectory, which makes it objective rather than a taste. Maya said that just relabels the question: maximizing a growth rate is maximizing a function of wealth, and someone can coherently care about a different function; and she noted that full Kelly sizes bets so aggressively that a 50% drawdown comes around fast, so people who actually do this shade down to fractional Kelly, and "half Kelly because drawdowns feel bad" is just risk aversion, i.e., a preference, smuggled back in through the bet size. Dev said no, fractional Kelly is justified independently, because your edge estimate is noisy and betting full Kelly on an overestimated edge is how you blow up. Maya said fine, but then the case for Kelly rests either on an infinite-horizon theorem, or on noise in your estimates, or on feelings about drawdowns — pick one — and Dev said it's all three and that's a feature not a bug, and Maya said that's just equivocation, and they basically went in a circle til dessert.
