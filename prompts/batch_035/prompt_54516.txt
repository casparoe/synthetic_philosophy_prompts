My brother and his wife got into it at Thanksgiving dinner over Dennett's stuff about competence without comprehension -- the idea that Darwin and Turing each pulled off the same strange inversion of reasoning, where you get the competence without anyone ever doing the comprehending. My brother works in ML, his wife is an evo bio postdoc, so this was probably inevitable. I mostly watched, and honestly I couldn't tell who was winning.

(1) He opened by saying it's one move made twice. Darwin showed you can get all the competence of a well-designed organism out of blind variation plus selection, with no designer comprehending anything, and Turing showed you can get intellectual performance out of mechanical symbol manipulation, with no mind grasping any meanings along the way. Same inversion both times: the competence arrives and the comprehension never shows up at all.

(2) She said no, those two are different in a way that matters. In the Darwin case the comprehension is absent from the entire system -- nobody anywhere had to understand anything for the eye to get built. In the Turing case the comprehension is present, it's just earlier: engineers comprehended a whole lot about computation and search in order to build the thing. So the machine's competence is borrowed comprehension, like a chess book that can be full of wisdom the book itself doesn't possess. Calling that competence without comprehension, she said, is like calling a frozen dinner home cooking without a cook -- the cook isn't absent, he's just upstream.

(3) He came back with learning systems. Nobody hand-coded the policy of the engines that learn purely from self-play. It came out of exploration that gets kept when it wins and pruned when it loses, which he said is structurally selection -- she said its structurally selection, grinning, same thing. And the engineers didn't comprehend the resulting style of play; the DeepMind people were reportedly as surprised by that famous move 37 against Lee Sedol as anyone else. So for learned systems, he said, the parallel is back on: the competence on the board isn't comprehended by anybody, makers included.

(4) She wasn't having it. The training loop is comprehended in outline: they picked the objective, the update rule, and the architecture, knowing in advance roughly what kind of thing this process reliably produces. Natural selection's objective wasn't picked by anyone, and that's exactly why it counts as comprehension-free -- designing the fitness function is a huge amount of comprehension smuggled in at step zero. And she said his move 37 point confuses nobody currently comprehending this particular policy with nobody comprehending anything; the makers comprehended the process that spits out such policies, which is alot more than you can say for anything upstream of evolution.

(5) He closed by saying maybe the slogan just needs a timestamp: competence at runtime without comprehension at runtime. If the policy is inscrutable, if even the team that trained it can't say why it prefers this plan over that one, then at the moment the competence is actually being exercised it exists without anyone comprehending it -- and that, he said, is the only version of the claim worth caring about anyway. She rolled her eyes, he grinned, and they went off to get pie. They asked me to referee and I genuinely couldn't.
