My logic seminar is wrapping up with Carroll's tortoise dialogue — the one where the tortoise accepts A and B, then calmly lets Achilles add each hypothetical ("If A and B are true, Z must be true," then "If A and B and C are true, Z must be true," and on up) as just another premise in the notebook, and the inference still never happens. I want a poem to close the last session. Here's the constraint that matters: there should be a refrain that is the tortoise's rule, and every time it comes back it has *grown* — one more letter folded in, since that's the regress: the rule keeps being taken on board as a premise, and the inference to Z keeps not occurring. Then the final stanza should drop the rule entirely and just say Z, flat, no "if" anywhere, because the only way out of the regress is to finally do the modus ponens instead of writing it down as one more line for the tortoise's notebook. Someone who knows the dialogue should recognize the notebook and the mounting letters; someone who doesn't should still get a good poem out of it. Maybe 16-20 lines, no fixed meter. And dont let it become a lecture — I want the heaviness of the ever-longer refrain to do the arguing, not the vocabulary.
