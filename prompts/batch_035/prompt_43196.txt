I'm a science and ideas journalist, and next week I'm giving a talk at a public ideas festival arguing that the situationist critique of virtue ethics misses. My line, roughly: the Milgram stuff and the Good Samaritan seminary study show that most people lack well-developed practical wisdom, not that character traits are a myth. Virtue is like expertise -- nobody concludes from watching amateur chess players blunder that chess mastery is an illusion, but the situationist experiments were all one-shot tests run on character amateurs. The audience will be mostly social and personality psychologists who think the situationism wars were settled decades ago (some of them may actually run priming-adjacent work and be defensive about replication), plus a couple of philosophers in the back who think situationism was always a strawman because Aristotle never claimed traits are situation-invariant. Draft me a prep sheet with the six or so objections these people will actually raise, in their own vocabulary, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each with a two-minute reply I can give on stage and a note on where that reply is soft. I especially want the objection I'm hoping nobody asks. Keep the whole thing under 700 words -- its for reading on the train, not a dissertation.
