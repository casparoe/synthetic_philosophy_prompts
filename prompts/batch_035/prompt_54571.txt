Two people at the longevity nonprofit I volunteer at have been fighting for like three years and I've been asked to moderate a proper adversarial collaboration between them. Camp A, the gradualist: if you replace neurons one at a time with functionally identical hardware — no behavioral change at any step, the person stays conscious the whole way through, keeps going to work, notices nothing — then the end-state digital person just IS the original, and thinking otherwise is sneaky dualism about the wet stuff. Camp B, the copyist: the end-state person is at best a very good copy of a dead person, and the smoothness of the transition is a trick played on our intuitions, the same way a teleporter story makes a fatal event look like travel — a milllion tiny replacements looks nicer than one big one but it's the same outcome. B is happy to say the copy has full moral standing and might even rationally pre-commit to being uploaded; what they deny is survival.

Write the joint statement both of them could actually sign. I want: (1) the claims they can both put their names to — about what functional equivalence does and doesn't guarantee, about what each individual step of the procedure is, about the copy's moral status, whatever they genuinely share; (2) the disagreements stated precisely enough that each side accepts the wording of its own view — no strawmanning, and B's view has to be phrased so B signs it, not so it sounds like A softened it; (3) the cruxes, meaning the sub-questions where settling one would actually move somebody — I suspect one is whether identity over time requires any causal-physical continuity in a substrate or only psychological continuity, and another is what the branching case does to everyone's intuitions (biological brain kept running in parallel the whole time, so at the end there are two people each with an equal claim), but work out whatever you think the real cruxes are; and (4) for each crux, something both sides agree bears on it — a thought experiment, a prediction about how some further case comes out, maybe a real result from neuroscience or prosthetics research they'd both accept as relevant — with each side's prediction written down in advance so it can be scored later, which is the whole point of writting them down imo.

Also flag any part of the fight you think is purely verbal — e.g. maybe it's partly just a fight over the word "survive" — and say which part and why, because if we can get that out of the way the real disagrement might be smaller than it looks.

Format: numbered agreed-claims section, then a table for the disagreements and cruxes. And one practical note that should shape the document: our donor will fund either destructive whole-brain scanning research or gradual-prosthetics research, not both, and the choice rides on whether gradualness actually buys survival or just makes the copy look legitimate — so if there's a crux that decision turns on, make that explicit.
