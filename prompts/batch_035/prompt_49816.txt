I have been reading rationalist blogs and forums for the better part of fifteen years — the Sequences and most of what flowed downstream from them — so the vocabulary I actually think in is the Bayesian-causal one: credences, conditioning, screening off, causal graphs and d-separation, Pearl's ladder of association/intervention/counterfactual, do(·) as surgery on a structural model, and the evidential-versus-causal decision theory dispute, which my corner of the internet frames as the difference between conditioning on your action and intervening on it.

Here is the translation I currently run, and the one I want tested. When a philosopher utters an indicative conditional, I hear a conditional credence: what my probability of the consequent would be after conditioning on the antecedent. When she utters the matching counterfactual, I hear an intervention: sever the equation for the antecedent-variable, fix it to the counterfactual value, propagate through the actual causal structure, and read off the consequent. On this picture the indicative/counterfactual distinction just is the conditioning/intervention distinction; Stalnaker's and Lewis's closest-possible-worlds semantics is a hand-rolled approximation to structural causal models; and the EDT/CDT argument is the same distinction one rung up, applied to deliberation itself.

Restate the actual philosophical debate in this vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, claim by claim: the material conditional and its paradoxes, with the standard wedge being Adams's minimal pair — the indicative "If Oswald didn't kill Kennedy, someone else did", which most of us accept, against the subjunctive "If Oswald hadn't killed Kennedy, someone else would have", which most of us reject; Adams's claim that the assertibility of an indicative is its conditional probability, and Lewis's triviality results, which purport to show that no proposition can occupy that role; the closest-world semantics for counterfactuals and its similarity metric; Sobel sequences, where an antecedent chain that stays close to the actual world flips a counterfactual from bland to catastrophic as it lengthens; and backtracking readings, where the past is allowed to vary with the counterfactual antecedent.

Then give me the residue report, in three categories: which parts of the debate translate cleanly into conditioning-and-intervention talk; which translate only with distortion, and precisely what gets distorted; and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all. Then answer the substantive question: is the residue a defect in my vocabulary — something real that conditioning and intervention cannot capture, which the philosophers were tracking all along — or a sign that the original debate was less clear than it looked, and that my vocabulary merely exposes the muddiness?

I have suspicions I want checked rather than confirmed. First, Sobel sequences seem to walk steadily further from the actual world while holding fixed, at each step, what the previous counterfactual established — a kind of iterated counterfactual dependence that do(·), which holds the graph and every non-intervened variable fixed at actual values, does not obviously reproduce; I cannot tell whether that is a genuine limitation of interventionist semantics or merely a fact about how the antecedent is being enriched as the sequence progresses. Second, I cannot decide whether the triviality results say anything I should care about, given that I am content to treat conditional credences as primitive and to deny that indicative conditionals express propositions at all — or whether that stance costs me something once conditionals embed under negation, disjunction, or further conditionals. Third, I want your view on whether my translation silently assumes causal decision theory, since it maps counterfactuals onto interventions full stop, and whether that assumption is doing legitimate work or smuggling a resolution of the EDT/CDT dispute in under the heading of translation.

Please write the response in British English, structured as follows: the translation, claim by claim; the residue report in three categories; then the substantive verdict on where the residue belongs. Close with a single final line of the form "Residue: ..." containing a one-sentence summary of what, if anything, failed to survive the translation.
