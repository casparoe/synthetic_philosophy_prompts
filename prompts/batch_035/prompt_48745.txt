Is there a name for the following thing? When people get called out for a wrong assertion, the accusation is almost never "what you said was false," it's more like "you said that like you KNEW." Example: my flatmate told me the pharmacy closes at 9, it actually closed at 8, and when I complained, his defense wasn't "well, it might have been open," it was "I was pretty sure, my bad" — as if the offense was the tone of certainty rather than the falsity of the claim itself. And conversely, a friend of mine hedges absolutely everything ("I think", "don't quote me on this") and when she's wrong, which is honestly often, nobody is annoyed with her at all. Same false content, totally different blame. It seems like what you can actually be blamed for isn't matching your assertion to the facts, it's matching your assertion to your own epistemic state — speaking with more confidence than you're entitled to. Is there an established concept for this mismatch, the gap between expressed and warranted confidence?

I got onto this after arguments with my partner about directions (I flatly asserted a road was closed; it wasn't; she was livid; my defense was that I genuinely believed it, which everyone agreed was somehow irrelevant). I know philosophers have fought about what you need in order to assert something at all — I've run into the idea that you need knowledge versus the idea that justified belief is enough — but that debate seems to be about the binary case, assert or stay silent, whereas what I'm describing feels graded: the same flat-out assertion can be fine from one speaker and blameworthy from another depending on how much they had behind them, and the hedge seems to do real work rather than just being verbal throat-clearing. It's also clearly not lying, since the person believes what they're saying, and it's not plain error either, since we blame the overconfident wrong person more than the appropriately hedged wrong person.

So what I'm after: does this configuration have a name in the literature, and if so, how does it relate to whatever the standard positions on the norms of assertion are? If there isn't an established term, could you coin one and define it carefully, including where its edges are — for instance, does "I think the pharmacy closes at 9" assert the same thing as "the pharmacy closes at 9" with a discount, or does the hedge change what was asserted such that being wrong doesn't falsify anything you actually said? And is the one phenomenon or several? A bare label won't do me much good, I'm trying to work out whether I've noticed a single thing here or accidentally smooshed together two or three different ones.
