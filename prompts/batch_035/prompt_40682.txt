I've been trying to figure out whether generations are like biological species (real joints in nature) or like constellations (lines we draw between stars), and my gut verdicts are honestly kind of a mess. So: Millennials, Boomers, Gen Z — these feel like real groups to me, with real shared traits, not just arbitrary bins. But the cutoffs! The Boomer line is 1946–1964, and everyone knows those exact years are basically vibes — my uncle born Dec 1945 is somehow not a Boomer while his wife born Feb 1946 is. That doesn't bother me that much, actually — I'd say the kind is real even if its edges are fuzzy, like a mountain range doesn't stop being real because you can't say exactly where it ends. But here's where I wobble: "Generation Jones," the supposed micro-generation of late Boomers (1954–1965), who missed the Vietnam draft and hit the job market during stagflation — that one feels fake to me, marketing, even though the causal story behind it is *better* than the one behind the official Boomer dates. And meanwhile "people who were kids during the 2008 financial crisis" feels like a real cohort to me even though it cuts right across Millennial/Gen Z lines. Zodiac signs obviously aren't real. Years ending in 7, not real. But a cohort defined by sharing one sharp formative event (say, a whole country's kids living through the same year of lockdowns) feels real in a way "Gen Alpha" doesn't, even though the lockdown kids are mostly inside Gen Alpha. Is there a principle that saves all of this? My tentative one is something like "a cohort is a real kind iff its members were shaped by overlapping formative events in their youth" — but I suspect that principle actually vindicates Generation Jones and dissolves the named generations, which is the opposite of my gut. So: what principle (if any) captures my verdicts, which verdict would you tell me to give up, and what does your principle say about a case I haven't mentioned — say, two countries using the same generation labels for birth cohorts whose historical experience was completely different, do those labels even pick out the same kind?
