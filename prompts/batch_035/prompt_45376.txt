I'm writing a novel with a gambler character, Rosa, and to nail down her voice I've been running her through little betting scenarios — I write each one down and decide what she'd do before I draft the chapter. Her track record so far (each bet is $500 if she draws the color she names):

1. Urn K: 100 balls, 50 red, 50 black, and she watches me fill it. Urn C: filled by flipping a coin 100 times, heads = red, tails = black, but nobody wrote down how it came out. She took K over C, though she said either was honestly fine.
2. Same urn C vs a known urn with 30 red / 70 black. She took urn C pretty easily.
3. Offered urn C on its own — $500 if she names a color and draws it — she had zero preference between calling red or black.
4. Urn D: her poker buddy Denny filled an opaque jar with 100 balls, whatever mix he felt like, and I didn't peek. Vs a known 50/50 urn she took the known one; vs a known 40 red / 60 black urn she STILL took the known one.
5. For calibration: a known 30-red-urn bet at $500 vs $250 cash on the table, she took the bet. She's not a timid person, and she's happy to take plain known-odds risk all day.

The scene I have to draft next week: Denny's jar vs a known urn with 25 red / 75 black. What does she do? Honestly I care less about the answer than about the rule she's running, because I can see at least two that fit everything above. Maybe something like "she discounts any urn whose mix she can't verify, flat, whoever filled it" vs "unknown mixes are fine as long as no person chose them, but if a person did the filling she assumes they stacked it against her." Lay out the candidate rules, say what each one predicts for the Denny-vs-25% scene, give me your best guess with your reasons, and then tell me the extra scenario that would best split the rules apart — I can slip one more chapter in later if it'll settle her character. (The Denny-specific version of the second rule also interests me: what if a total stranger, or her sweet little niece, had filled the jar instead?)
