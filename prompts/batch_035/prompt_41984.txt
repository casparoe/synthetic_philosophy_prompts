I sit on the rules committee of the national agency that administers disaster relief for businesses. Our statute grants relief to firms that are "genuinely small," with no number attached, and our appeal panels have been deciding the penumbra inconsistently: last quarter one panel granted relief to a firm with 480 employees, while another denied a firm with 90 — the latter being the dominant provider in its local market — and both panels wrote their decisions as though they were discovering what "genuinely small" had meant all along. The committee must now settle the standard for future cases. Three options are on the table: (a) a bright-line rule, e.g. at most 200 employees; (b) a multi-factor test with a rebuttable presumption around a number; (c) retain the phrase, supplemented by nonbinding guidance listing relevant factors.

The committee's debate has split along lines familiar from the philosophy of vagueness. One member, an epistemicist, holds that the statute already has sharp boundaries: for every firm there is a fact of the matter whether it is genuinely small, our panels merely cannot reliably know where the line falls — so option (c) is correct in principle, and a legislated line under (a) would replace the law rather than interpret it. Others take a supervaluationist view: for firms in the penumbra the statute says nothing determinate; "genuinely small" is true or false only relative to a way of sharpening it, so consistency requires openly choosing one precisification — which is just option (a) pursued honestly, while option (b) merely relocates the penumbra.

Please act as a professional philosopher specializing in vagueness who also has experience drafting regulatory standards, and think through the case carefully before writing. Draft the short decision memo I will circulate before Thursday's meeting: recommend one of the three options (or a defensible variant), specify how it would resolve the two appeal cases above if reapplied to them, and — this is essential — engage the strongest consideration on the side you reject rather than a strawman version of it. Finally, make clear which philosophical claims about vagueness the recommendation genuinely depends on and which are dispensable window dressing.
