So here's my situation: I'm a professional sports bettor, eight years in, mostly soccer and tennis, and I've gotten by fine on a vocabulary I never really examined — odds and prices, expected value, closing line, sample size, variance, edges, steam moves, bad beats, "the market's number" vs "my number." What I want is for you to take the three big interpretations of probability — frequentism, subjectivism (the Bayesian kind), and propensity theories — and restate each one in that vocabulary, one at a time, as faithfully as you can. Like: is the frequentist's long-run relative frequency just my sample-size talk writ large? When I say a team is "truly" 60% to win a specific match tonight — a thing that happens exactly once — is that a propensity claim, a subjectivist claim, or just me being sloppy? When the closing line keeps beating me, am I collecting evidence about frequencies or about my own calibration?

For each interpretation I want the full accounting: what translates cleanly onto something I actually do, what translates only with distortion (be honest about where you're fudging), and what has no expression in my vocabulary at all. Then the residue report, which is the part I actually care about: is there something the philosophers are argueing about that my vocabulary simply cannot say, and if so, is that because my vocabulary is missing something, or because their disagreement is less substantive than it looks? A bettor can go a whole career never deciding whether probabilities live in the world, in his head, or in the frequency of outcomes — I want to know if that's a fact about betting, or a fact about me not thinking hard enough. And if one of the three interpretations turns out to be nearly useless in my terms, say that plainly rather than making it fit.
