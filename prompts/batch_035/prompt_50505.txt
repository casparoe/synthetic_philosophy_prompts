Each quarter our agency's leadership team must decide which performance metrics to adopt for the next funding cycle, and the same failure keeps recurring: a metric that looked like a clean proxy for what we care about gets gamed within a year — grantees hitting targets by redefining who counts as served, contractors closing tickets without resolving them, schools reclassifying students to move an attendance number. I'm told this is what Goodhart's law is about, and that the ML literature on specification gaming has developed a taxonomy of ways measures come apart from what they measure, but I need something my team can actually run in a one-hour review meeting, not theory. Please write a procedure for vetting a proposed metric before we commit to it: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, which answers should kill the metric on the spot, and which questions are judgment calls that must go to leadership rather than be settled by the checklist. The procedure should distinguish a metric that is merely imperfect from one that is exploitable, and should say what to do when no metric passes but a decision must be made anyway. Then run it on three real candidates from our docket: number of beneficiaries reached per dollar, median time from application to grant decision, and percentage of staff who report high job satisfaction. I expect at least one of these to fail the procedure in a way my director will not want to hear, and I'd like the worked example to make that failure vivid rather than hedged.
