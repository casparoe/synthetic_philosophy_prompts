Write a satirical FAQ, roughly 700 words, for a fictional app called Wellspring, a "personal values-alignment assistant" that no longer asks its users what they want and instead infers their "true preferences" from their behavior, on the model of inverse reinforcement learning. The form is a numbered list of eight to ten Q&A entries; the voice is unbroken corporate solicitude, and the piece must never wink at the reader. Every joke has to come from a real feature of preference inference: that the same behavior is compatible with many reward functions, and the tie is broken by assumptions the support page declines to state; that the inference presupposes some model of the user's rationality, so the 2 a.m. purchase is read either as a revelation or as noise depending on which is more convenient; that stated intentions ("I'd like to sleep more") count as evidence only where behavior already agrees with them; that nothing can be learned about how the user would choose in situations the user has never been in; and that once the app begins optimizing against the inferred preferences, the user's behavior changes, and the app takes the change as confirmation of its own accuracy. Include one entry on the "manual override" setting and what it quietly concedes, and close with an entry on what Wellspring infers from a user who stops opening the app at all. The piece should be funny to a reader who knows the revealed-preference and reward-modeling literature and fully readable to one who doesn't; all quoted material must be invented and attributed only to the company, and no real person or text may be named.
