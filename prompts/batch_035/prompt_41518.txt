Retired actuary here, reading decision theory books for fun in my old age. Something's been bugging me for weeks and I can't find the right word for it, so let me just describe the thing and hope it rings a bell.

You know how the fair price of a bet is supposed to be the probability-weighted sum of all the payouts. I was playing around with toy bets built on "flip a coin until the first heads comes up" — the payout depends on how many flips it took, with the amounts growing about as fast as the probabilities shrink, so each term of the sum stays roughly the same size. If all the payouts go in your favor the sum just blows up, and you'd say the bet is worth infinitely much — weird, but at least it's a determinate answer. But I stumbled on a variant where the payouts alternate in sign: you win on some flip numbers and pay on the others. Because of how I set the amounts, the terms of the sum shrink slowly, something like 1, 1/2, 1/3, ... with alternating signs, which I vaguely remember from school converges to a finite number — but only just barely, because if you drop the signs the whole thing runs off to infinity. And here's the part that really got me: since it converges only in that fragile, order-dependent way, if I add up the terms in a different order — taking all the winning outcomes first and saving the losing ones for later — I can make the "fair price" come out to practically any number I picked in advanced. Same bet, different bookkeeping, and the value wanders wherever I want it to go. It's not huge, it's not infinite, it's not any number at all.

Is there a name for a bet like that — one whose value isn't merely very large but genuinely undefined? I'm aware there's a classical paradox about a coin-flip game whose value is infinite, but this seems like a different animal: that one at least settles on "infinite," mine won't settle on anything. And to be clear, I'm not after just a label — I want to know what exactly the phenomenon is, how it differs from the infinite-value kind of case (and from an ordinary badly-priced bet), and whether the people who think about this for a living have a settled story about what a rational agent should do when offered one. Refuse to rank it against other options? Use some kind of cutoff after finitely many terms and accept that the answer depends on the cutoff? Treat two such bets as incomparable? If there's no established term for it, tell me that straight, and then maybe suggest what you'd call it and where you'd draw the edges.
