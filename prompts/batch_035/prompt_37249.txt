1. Consent only counts when the other person engages your will rather than going around it -- coercion and deception are both ways of going around it.
2. Deception about a fact vitiates consent whenever, had you known the truth, you would have refused (the would-have-refused test).
3. Courtship is saturated with misrepresentation: inflated job titles, curated photos, concealed exes, feigned enthusiasm for your vinyl collection -- and everyone knows couples where full disclosure up front would have meant no relationship at all.
4. So a large fraction of ordinary sexual consent is obtained by going around the will.
5. Consent obtained by going around the will is invalid.
6. Therefore a large fraction of ordinary sex is nonconsensual, morally and maybe legally.

Where exactly does the argument go wrong, or if it's valid, which premise is least costly to drop?
