Two guys on my team got into it at happy hour after one of them mentioned his wife is pregnant via IVF. (1) Dan said something like: every one of us could easily have come from a different sperm, since a guy produces millions of them, so it makes no sense to say this particular sperm is essential to you -- clearly a slightly different sperm and an extremely similar kid would've been born, and we'd just call that kid Dan. (2) Max disagreed: if a different sperm had fertilized the egg, the result would be someone else entirely, a brother-like sibling, and it wouldn't be Dan at all -- his whole genome would be different, so "a very similar Dan" is confused from the start. (3) Dan came back with the IVF point: embryos split sometimes, so suppose the very zygote that became you had split into identical twins instead -- same sperm, same egg, but now there are two people and neither is obviously "you," so it can't be the raw origin that makes you you either. (4) Max said that case just shows something weird about splitting, not about origins -- his claim was never that same origin guarantees you, only that a different origin couldn't be you. Dan said that's moving the goalposts, and that if "same origin" neither guarantees nor is even trackable across cases, the whole essentialism thing starts to look like a verbal game. Can you go through their four moves and say who's right at each step, plus what, if anything, both of them are missing?
