My roommate will not stop bringing up the Jim twins whenever nature vs nurture comes up, and it's made me realize I don't actually know what that story is supposed to prove. Recap in case you're fuzzy on the details: identical twins Jim Springer and Jim Lewis, born in Ohio in 1940, adopted into separate families when they were about a month old, reunited in 1979 at age 39. When they met, it turned out both had married a woman named Linda, divorced, and then married a woman named Betty; both had sons they'd named James Alan (spelled slightly differently, I think); both had childhood dogs named Toy; both smoked Salems, drove Chevys, did woodworking, and even took family vacations at the same beach in Florida without knowing it. A psychologist named Thomas Bouchard saw the press coverage, tracked them down, and the whole thing snowballed into the Minnesota Study of Twins Reared Apart, which ended up running for decades and finding things like moderate heritability for personality and even some behavioral habits.

So here's what I want: analyze this whole episode through the lens of heritability as a technical concept, not as a slogan. Walk me through what heritability actually is and isn't (population statistic, not an individual-level fact about genes determining anything, only defined relative to a particular environment distribution, etc.), and then run the mapping both directions. One direction: what does heritability say about the Jims? Which of those coincidences could plausibly be downstream of heritable traits, which ones are we just pattern-matching after the fact (is there really any meaningful sense of a gene for naming your dog Toy?), and where would a heritability estimate be totally silent here? I want you to be careful about stuff like the base rate problem -- how many pairs of twins who lived parallel lives without the fun coincidences never make the news -- and also the stuff people raise against the twin-study design, like twins reared apart often not being reared THAT far apart, or sharing a womb for nine months. The other direction: what does the episode do to the concept? Did the Jim twins PR bonanza end up helping heritability research or badly distorting what the public thinks a heritability of 0.5 for personality actually means? And be honest about where the theory just can't speak -- I'd rather hear "heritability has nothing to say about the dog's name" than have you force it. Please also tell me what I should say back to my roommate next time he brings it up.
