I'm writing a paper on whether historical materialism can be stated as a substantive, falsifiable thesis rather than an immunized slogan, and I need a stress-test case built to order. Construct a thought experiment meeting the following specifications; a case that stipulates the verdicts rather than generating them from the theories will be useless to me.

The scenario. A controlled pair: two societies (or one society that splits into two founding populations) with identical economic bases — same forces of production, same relations of production, same property forms, same class structure, same mode of extracting surplus. They diverge superstructurally: a doctrine, ritual, or legal innovation arises in one and not the other, and its appearance must not be traceable to any economic cause — no crisis, no class interest, no differential technology explains why it emerges there and not in the twin; a founder event (charisma, an accident of transmission) is the permitted exogenous trigger. Within two generations, the superstructural difference feeds back and alters the base: the affected society's property forms or class structure measurably diverge from its twin's. Then a third phase: the new base reshapes the doctrine — the surviving society's belief system drifts toward whatever its transformed economy needs, so that after another few generations the fit between base and superstructure looks, once again, seamless.

Constraints: a realistic preindustrial or early-modern setting; no aliens or exotic physics, though a twin-society stipulation is allowed; the doctrine's content must not mention economics or property at all; and it must not be the Protestant ethic in disguise — no salvation anxiety, no vocational asceticism, nothing Weber already used.

After the scenario, walk through the verdict each of three readings of historical materialism gives. First, strong economism, on which the superstructure is epiphenomenal: this reading must deny that the divergence can occur — tell me exactly what it has to deny, and what that denial costs it. Second, the conciliatory reading Engels took in his late correspondence, on which the superstructure has some autonomy and reacts back on the base while the base keeps explanatory priority in the long run: this reading should be able to absorb phases one and two — so tell me whether phase three is what lets it declare victory, and whether that victory is explanatory work or immunization by construction. Third, the functional-explanation reading associated with G. A. Cohen, on which superstructural items are explained by their effects — they persist because of what they do for the base: tell me whether your case gives this reading something real to explain, or whether its account of the doctrine's persistence collapses into the observation that it survived because it was useful, without saying useful to whom or how selected.

Then do three further things. Identify the single load-bearing feature of the case — I suspect it is the exogeneity of the founder event, but you tell me — and say what happens to the case if a defender of strong economism is allowed to reinterpret that event as itself economically caused after all. Give the best two replies available to the conciliatory reading that do not render the thesis unfalsifiable. Finally, give your own verdict on whether the case shows historical materialism to be falsifiable, vacuous, or something in between, and what the case is ultimately for.

Format your response in these sections, in this order: "The scenario" (one paragraph, up to 350 words), "The three verdicts" (up to 450 words), "The load-bearing feature" (up to 150 words), "The determinist's counterattack" (up to 200 words), "The conciliatory reading's replies" (up to 200 words), "My verdict" (up to 150 words).
