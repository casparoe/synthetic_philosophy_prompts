My advisor says that analogy is doing all the work and that it's badly disanalogous: the makers are dead and get nothing from my visit, while the living artist collects royalties and status every time I stream the movie. Can you audit it properly? Give me the mapping -- what plays the role of the cathedral, the worshipper, the serf, the benefit that flows back -- then say where the mapping breaks, and separate the disanalogies that matter for this use (dead vs living beneficiary seems to matter, distance in time maybe less so) from the ones that don't. Also want to know what each side quietly smuggles in about the difference between consuming something and endorsing its maker, and whether either conclusion -- "keep engaging" or "stop" -- actually survives once the disanalogies are on the table. And if the cathedral comparison can't carry it, is there a better one, like eating at a restaurant whose owner is awful, or is the whole game of settling this by analogy a mistake?
