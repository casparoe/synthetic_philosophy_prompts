she did the math on expected winnings, says its literally infinite, and now she's convinced she can charge basically whatever she wants per ticket and still call the game "fair" -- she's floating $20 with the proceeds going to the shelter. i told her that can't be right, because nobody sane would pay even $50 for a game where the single most likely outcome is winning $2. she just says "take it up with the math."

also the shelter's treasurer pointed out they only keep like $3,000 cash in the box that night, so realistically the payout caps there no matter what happens with the coin.

so now we're arguing about three seperate things and i'm hoping you can settle them:

1. is her "infinite expected value" thing actually correct, or is there some trick to it? like, where exactly does the reasoning go off the rails, if it does?

2. if the payout really is capped at the cash on hand, what would a fair (or at least defensible) ticket price be? she wants an actual number to put on the flyer.

3. she still wants to advertise the game with a line like "our expected payout is infinite" as a joke, and i feel like thats the kind of claim that gets a small nonprofit in trouble, even jokingly. am i being uptight?

for what its worth, most of the attendees are poker people and at least a couple of actuaries, so someone WILL check the math, probably before the second drink.
