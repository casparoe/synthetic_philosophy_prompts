I'm putting together a little position statement on health and disease for a bioethics reading group and want a consistancy check before they tear into it. Roughly where I stand: (1) whether a condition is a disease is an objective, value-free fact — cancer would be a disease even if nobody had any feelings about it. (2) the core of disease is biological dysfunction, some part of the organism failing at the job it's built/evolved to do, with health just being proper function. (3) homosexuality was never actually a disease — it got listed as one for much of the twentieth century and then dropped, and I think that change was us revising our values, not biology learning anything new. (4) infertility is a disorder only relative to wanting children — a happily childfree person with the same plumbing problem is not disordered. (5) ageing is not a disease, even though it involves decline in the functioning of pretty much every organ. (6) whatever else is true, a disease has to be bad for the person who has it — that's part of the concept, not a side effect of it. and (7) I don't buy the line that there are two senses of "disease", a technical one for the textbooks and a loose everyday one — I think it's a single concept doing all the work, in the clinic and in the theory.

Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and how badly? Rank the tensions by severity, give me the cheapest fix for each and what it costs, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Be blunt — I genuinely can't see where the problem is, so dont spare me.
