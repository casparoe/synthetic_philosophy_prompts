so a weird thing happened recently: a friend of mine went deep into hard-money/crypto circles and started citing Aristotle at me (money is just convention, it's the useless metal that becomes money, that whole genre of argument), and I went back to the Ethics and the Politics to argue back, and somewhere in there I realized I'm no longer sure my own reading even hangs together. here's roughly where I've landed:

1. in the book five discussion of justice in exchange, what grounds the equality is the reciprocal relation between the crafts — the builder and the shoemaker stand in a ratio, and exchange is an equalization of persons first, of the goods only secondarily.
2. Aristotle on money is basically conventionalist: nomisma is whatever the community agrees to use, revaluable at will. I take this pretty literally, and I think it makes him an early forerunner of the legal/chartalist view of money.
3. the natural/unnatural distinction in the Politics (getting wealth for the household's sake vs. getting money as an end in itself) is a real moral distinction, and it works because the household's need is a natural limit — need is finite, accumulation isn't.
4. Marx was too hard on him. the line that Aristotle failed to spot the common substance of value because greek society rested on slavery, so the equality of different labors was invisible to him — I don't buy it. the proportionality analysis doesn't need a common substance in the first place, so there was nothing for the slavery to be hiding from him.
5. the usury condemnation still holds up: charging interest is the most unnatural way of getting wealth because it makes money breed money, and money was introduced for exchange, not reproduction.
6. proportionality gives Aristotle a standard by which an actual price can be unjust — not just "tough luck" but a genuine violation of justice in exchange — so his theory is not neutral about market prices the way it's usually taught.

go through these and tell me which of them are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix would be for each. rank them by severity instead of handing me a flat list, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I honestly don't know where the problem is — for all I know it's item one and item six, or none of them — so don't go easy on any of them. also, if the crypto guy is right about something for the wrong reasons, I'd want to know that too.
