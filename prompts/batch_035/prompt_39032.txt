My friend keeps calling Cleon's speech the ultimate retributivist rant — Mytilene did the crime, so the city deserves total destruction, and muddying that with calculations about consequences is for soft people — while Diodotus is the guy who argues purely on deterrence grounds: if rebels know death is certain either way, they'll fight to the bitter end and you'll get more desperate resistance, not less, so sparing the many (executing just the ringleaders) is the smarter policy.

My question: can the Mytilenean debate be read as Dennett vs. Caruso happening 2400 years early?
