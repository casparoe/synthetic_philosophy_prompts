I teach an upper-level philosophy of science seminar and am writing the final exam. Please draft one essay question, answerable in about ninety minutes, on Popper's claim that the theory of natural selection is not a testable scientific theory but a metaphysical research programme, together with his later partial retraction. The question must not be answerable by reciting the falsifiability criterion and Popper's verdict on Darwinism; it should force students to take a stance on the tautology objection (that natural selection explains survival by citing fitness, where fitness is measured by survival), on whether that objection can be defused by interpreting fitness as a causal propensity rather than a retrospective description, and on what exactly Popper's retraction conceded — in particular, whether he came to see the theory as testable in restricted applications while keeping its overall status as metaphysical, and whether that is a stable position or an unstable compromise. It should also require students to say, at least briefly, whether the episode counts in favor of falsificationism as a demarcation criterion (Popper corrected himself) or against it (it took decades and outside pressure to catch a misclassification that the criterion arguably licensed), and to address the worry that Popper's treatment of Darwinism generalizes badly to other historical sciences, given his own appeal to situational logic elsewhere.

Then provide: a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 with five must-hit points, the marks each is worth, and a one-line note per point on how students typically lose the marks — for instance, by treating the propensity interpretation of fitness as settling the matter rather than as one move in a live controversy, or by reading Popper's retraction as a simple recantation of everything he had said. Students should get no credit for either direction of the tired debate about whether creationism is falsifiable and evolution is not.
