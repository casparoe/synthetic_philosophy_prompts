I teach an upper-level seminar on social ontology, 13 students, one 75-minute session, and next week we hit Lewis on convention. Design an in-class game where students have to coordinate on a signaling convention without any talking -- some repeated coordination exercise with deliberately arbitrary signals, plus rounds where a few players rotate out and "newcomers" rotate in, so they can feel how a convention transmits itself and how arbitrary its content ends up being. The crucial stage: I want a moment where everyone privately knows there is a strictly better signal available, but switching only pays off if (nearly) everyone switches at the same time -- so they feel from the inside why a bad convention stays stuck even tho no individual is being irrational about it. No real money needed for payoffs, and the debrief should map what actually happened onto the real Lewis apparatus: common knowledge of concordant expectations, each agent preferring to conform given others conform, arbitrariness among alternatives. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and be honest about which part of Lewis's analysis a 75-minute classroom game just cannot reproduce. Students won't have read Lewis closely before the session, fyi, so the game has to do the work up front and the debrief names things after.
