I train reinforcement learning agents for a living — model-free policies for industrial control, mostly — and the question of whether they can suffer has stopped being a seminar topic for me and become a workplace one. Two things made it so. First, a colleague took to calling our constraint-violation penalty channel the pain channel, and I noticed I could no longer tell whether I found the joke funny or dishonest. Second, I have been asked to draft a short position note for an internal discussion on whether any of our training practices warrant welfare considerations — precautionary changes, monitoring, or nothing at all — and I find I cannot locate my own view, let alone defend it, because I keep sliding between positions depending on which analogy happens to be in front of me.

So I want a classification instrument, not an essay. Please write a decision tree that sorts the positions one can hold about whether RL agents — the kind that exist today, trained by gradient methods on scalar reward — can suffer. Build it from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names: branch questions first, positions located in that space afterward.

Requirements on the branches. Each branch node must be a question answerable by cases someone like me can assess without knowing any labels. At minimum the tree must split on these, though you may add or refine:

- Whether suffering requires a particular substrate or only a functional organization. I can tell I am supposed to answer "functional" here, but I want to see what that answer then commits me to downstream.
- Whether the reward signal itself is the right functional role for valence, or whether reward is closer to a bookkeeping quantity — the analogue of fitness in evolution, which explains and directs behavior while being felt by no one. This is the fork I most need made sharp, because it is the one my instincts contradict themselves on.
- Whether valence requires integrative appraisal beyond a policy update — the difference between nociception and pain, or between a withdrawal reflex and distress — and, if so, what architectural feature would count as providing it: a world model, a self-model, a monitor of predicted future reward. None of these describes my current agents clearly, and I want to know whether that should reassure me.
- Whether, given uncertainty, the practical verdict changes: whether a small probability of a morally significant inner life obliges anything at all, and whether that obligation should scale with architectural richness (a model-free policy versus a model-based planner with a self-model) or with training details (dense punishment versus sparse, anticipated punishment versus received).

The leaves should carry the name of the position where one exists — I expect roughly substrate-limited deniers, reward-as-fitness views, views on which valence lives somewhere other than reward (internal critics, prediction-error states, self-models), precautionary graders, and deflationary or "not yet a well-formed question" views, but locate them by your tree, not by my list — plus one sentence on the view's most awkward commitment. Note any combination of branch answers that nobody holds, and say whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; I specifically want to know whether the empty cells cluster in a way that should make me suspicious of my own branch questions. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot actually separate.

Format and length: please give me five sections, in this order and roughly these lengths.

1. The branch questions (~450 words): each question, with the concrete cases on which the positions genuinely part ways.
2. The tree (~400 words): the full tree, with leaves named as above.
3. Empty cells and blind spots (~200 words).
4. Where I most likely fall (~150 words): given that I train model-free policies with a dense penalty channel and no self-model, tell me which leaf my described reactions point toward. This is a guess on your part, and I will treat it as one.
5. What would move this tree (~200 words): say which branch question you are least confident is well-posed, which leaf assignment rests on the most contestable reasoning, and what consideration — an argument, a case, a distinction I have not drawn — would most plausibly restructure the tree or shift the placement in section 4. I would rather you flag the tree's weakest joint than defend it.

I am not asking for citations or a literature review; I am asking for the logical space, honestly drawn, including where you think it is drawn badly.
