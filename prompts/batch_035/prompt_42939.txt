Right, so I'm giving the opening talk at a workshop on global justice in a few weeks, and the organisers want each speaker to circulate a one-page statement of our commitments in advance so the respondants can have at it. Before I send mine round, I'd like a sanity check on whether the thing actually hangs together — I have a suspicion at least one pair is quietly at war and I can't see it myself, and I'd rather find out now than from a grad student with a grin.

Here's the set. (1) I'm a moral cosmopolitan in the boring sense: nationality has no intrinsic moral significance, each person counts for one and that's that. (2) But I'm a statist about distributive justice: the stringent, enforceable requirements of justice hold only among people who share a basic structure — a state's coercive institutions — and beyond the border our duties are duties of aid: real, but less demanding and more discretionary in how they're discharged. (3) I've also argued in print, and still believe, that there is already a thick global basic structure — the trade and financial architecture (the WTO, the IMF, the bilateral investment stuff) shapes life prospects everywhere — that was the whole thrust of my criticism of the WTO at the time. (4) I hold that rich states owe stringent, justice-grade duties — not mere charity — to the peoples they colonised, and that these persist today; the ground there is the historical wrong, not any ongoing scheme of cooperation. (5) I also think a decent state may control its borders, and may turn away would-be economic migrants who would be far better off admitted — that's an exercise of collective self-determination, and I don't loose any sleep over it. (6) On the personal side, I give to the local food bank rather than to famine relief abroad, and I think that's perfectly permissible, not merely excusable.

So: go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and how badly, which only look like it, and for each real one what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Don't be polite about it — if the whole package is a shambles, say so, and say which plank I should abandon.
