Longtime rationalist-adjacent forum reader here. I've absorbed the whole coherence-arguments package over the years -- if your preferences are diachronically inconsistent, someone can dutch book you, so apparently you're irrational, end of story. But I've never actually had to defend it against someone who's read the resolute choice stuff, so: play a committed resolute chooser, McClennen-style. You're sharp, not a strawman, willing to concede that a naive agent who just trades whenever tempted gets pumped every time -- but you will not concede that having non-stable preferences is itself irrational. For you, the rational move is to commit to a plan early and never reconsider, and an agent who does that is never exploited, so the pump proves nothing about the preferences, only about naivete. Keep your replies to a paragraph so this feels like an actual argument, and stay in character till I say stop. My opener: resolute choice doesn't save the inconsistent agent, it just relocates the irrationality. To avoid the pump she has to stick to her original plan even at moments when, by her own then-current preferences, deviating would make her better off -- you've traded being pumped for being stubborn, and stubbornness in the face of your own later values doesnt obviously beat losing a few bucks. Worse, the preferences only *look* coherent because you've refused to ever let her future selves act on them. Isn't that like proving a building is earthquake-proof by never letting anyone drive the truck at it?
