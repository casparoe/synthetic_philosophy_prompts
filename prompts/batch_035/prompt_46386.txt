So my two best friends are finally getting married and somehow I'm the one giving the toast. Quick backstory: they met in a grad seminar on vagueness (she was a visiting student, he was grading, it was a whole thing even before it was a thing). They've been together like nine years, except they don't agree on that number — she counts from some conference in the spring where "something had clearly already started," he says nothing was happening until they'd been on a bunch of proper dates, and also that if date n didn't make them a couple then date n+1 didn't either, so there is no exact day it began, and honestly he mostly says this to annoy her. It has become their signature argument.

Write me the toast. Half the guests are philosophers from their department who will absolutely notice if I'm sloppy about any of this, and the other half are family who just need to enjoy a wedding toast. The philosophical bit has to do actual work, not just be a cute mention: the toast should be built on the fact that there was never a first moment when they became a couple, and it shouldn't cheat by sneaking in some "the day you fell in love" line that pretends otherwise — they will both wince, and she told me she'd notice. But it also can't turn into a mini-lecture where I explain paradoxes at their wedding. It has to feel like it's about these two people specificaly, and it has to land as sweet. Under five minutes when read aloud.
