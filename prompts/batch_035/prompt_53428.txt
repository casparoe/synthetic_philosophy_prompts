so I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy, half-way through my thesis on aggregation and rescue cases, and somewhere along the way I've collected a set of positions that each feel right on their own. my supervisor keeps saying "you can't hold all of that" but hasn't told me which one has to go, so I want somebody to actually stress-test the package. here's roughly where I stand:

(1) In the classic Taurek-style case — five people on one side, David on the other, I can save either the five or the one but not both — I don't think the bare fact that more would die on one side gives me any reason at all to prefer the five. if anything settles it, it's a coin: each side gets an equal chance, and I think that's permissable even if not required.

(2) I do think there's a real duty of easy rescue: if only one person is in peril and rescuing them costs me little, I must help — that's not a supererogatory nice-to-have.

(3) But when the victims are statistical — say I'm choosing which of two safety programmes to fund, one of which is forecast to save five lives and the other one — I'd go with the numbers without hesitation, and I think that's not just permitted but required.

(4) I'm committed to each person counting for one and nobody counting for more than one: everyone's claim to rescue is exactly as strong as everyone else's, no matter who they are.

(5) I don't believe in interpersonal sums: there's no fact about how well things go for the six of them collectively, welfare doesn't add up across lives, so "five deaths is worse than one" can't be grounded in some total.

(6) I also think the morality of a situation can't depend on how it's sliced up: whether the case is described as one choice between two groups, or as a sequence of choices I make one rescue at a time, shouldn't change what I'm allowed to do.

go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and for each real one how bad it is and what the cheapest fix would be. don't just flag everything you can find — I want a severity ranking, and then your view on which single revision would clean up the most. also, and this probably goes without saying given the topic, please write the whole answer in British English (organise, recognise, behaviour, that sort of thing).
