Bit of context about me: I write mid-list historical mysteries, self-published for the last six years, and I'm one of nine people on the board of a co-op — roughly 45 genre authors who share a newsletter, a promo site, and an ad budget. The whole point of the co-op is that none of us has to fight the algorithm alone. I'm the one who has to circulate a written opinion before Thursday's board meeting, and I'm dreading it, so I'd like you to draft it for me.

Here's the case. Three of our members have gone "rapid release": AI-assisted drafting, a novel every 45 days, everything exclusive to Kindle Unlimited, first-in-series permanently free. And it's working — for them. Their also-bought slots are crowding out everybody else's, our shared newsletter's open rates are sagging, and the slower writers in the co-op are becoming invisible on the storefront. So the board has to pick between basically three options: let everyone adopt the rapid-release model, adopt it with caps (a floor price, a cap on releases per author per year, enforced by expulsion), or refuse and keep the co-op as a slow-publishing island. One board member keeps calling this a "multipolar trap" and going on about Moloch — his point being that each author's individually rational move (feed the algorithm before somebody else does) quietly burns the commons we all write in, and that a cap only means anything if enforcement is real. The rapid-release three think that's naive: you can't cartel you're way out of an arms race you don't control, and if our co-op caps itself, someone outside it just takes the slots. I honestly can't tell who's right, which is exactly why I want the memo to do the work instead of my gut.

Draft the written opinion I'll circulate: a clear recommendation with concrete terms — actual numbers for any caps, what happens to violators, and a set date for reviewing whether the decision worked — plus the reasoning, and a serious engagement with the strongest version of the rapid-release camp's objection, not a strawman version of it. And please tell me whether the Moloch framing is actually doing argumentative work here or whether it's just a mood: if I circulate something that gestures at it, I want the gesture to be load-bearing.
