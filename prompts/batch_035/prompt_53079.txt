so I teach ethics, and after a seminar last week a grad student told me "you can't hold all of that" about my views on the separateness of persons, and when I asked which one should go she just smiled, which was annoying but also fair. The package, as best I can state it: (1) aggregation within a life is completely legitimate — prudence is basically utilitarianism for one person, and my Tuesday self has no standing to veto a benefit to my Friday self. (2) The seperateness of persons objection to utilitarianism is right: there's no morally significant entity that both suffers the loss and receives the gain when welfare shifts between people, so interpersonal agregation is a mistake, and people's claims against being harmed for others' benefit can't just be summed away. (3) I'm a reductionist about personal identity in Parfit's sense: identity consists in psychological continuity, there's no further fact beyond that, and what matters in survival doesn't include identity itself. (4) In rescue cases, numbers count: if you can save five strangers or one stranger at equal cost, with nobody used as a mere means, you're required to save the five — and I don't consider that a case of aggregation. (5) I support mandatory vaccination, which means deliberately imposing a small risk of serious harm on a few people who would otherwise be fine, so that many others are protected, and I'd defend that in principle, not just as a messy second-best. For what it's worth, I also think nobody's claim on me gets weaker with distance or lack of relationship.
