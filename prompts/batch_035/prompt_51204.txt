Longtime LW lurker here, mostly a consumer not producer of takes, finally posting something. I keep noticing that arguments about prosaic alignment are ships passing in the night — half the participants are actually disagreeing about something upstream, like whether an LLM-ish system even has values vs. is just imitating, or whether alignment properties survive distributional shift out of the training regime. So for a Discussion post I want a decision tree that takes a reader from the starting commitment "transformative AI gets built by roughly scaling current methods, no new paradigm needed" to a named position on what we should actually do about it. Branch nodes should be concrete questions a person can answer without knowing which position it leads them to — e.g. is mesa-optimization/deceptive alignment a load-bearing worry for you, does outer-loop optimization like RLHF buy real alignment or just a facade that could come apart under pressure, can human feedback scale with the system (debate, IDA-flavored stuff), do you need interpretability before deployment or are behavioral evals enough, is corrigibility a stable achievable property or does increasing capability eat it. Leaves should include at least: the naive evals-will-catch-it optimist, the corrigibility-first view, scalable oversight, interpretability-gated deployment, alignment-by-default, and the doom leaf, plus whatever else the logical space demands. Please note the combinations nobody actually occupies and diagnose why (incoherent? unattractive? just unexplored?), and flag any two leaves the tree fails to actually separate — I suspect the corrigibility and doom branches collapse together under certain answers, but check me on that. Keep it to ~8 internal questions max so it's postable, and phrasing of the nodes matters more than the leaf names.
