Compatibilists like Dennett say the free will worth wanting is just being able to act on your own reasons and desires without someone holding a gun to your head. But your reasons and desires were themselves caused by genes and upbringing you never chose, so hasn't he just moved the problem back a step instead of answering it? Surely the hard determinists win the argument here, and the "worth wanting" move is a dodge. Second thing: if he gets to pick which variety of free will counts as the one worth wanting, couldn't a theist insist that what they want is contra-causal soul power, and be entitlted to it by the same logic? Where exactly do these objections go wrong (if they do), and please keep apart what is actually settled in this debate from what compatibilists and incompatibilists still genuinely fight over — don't present one side's framing as the consensus.
