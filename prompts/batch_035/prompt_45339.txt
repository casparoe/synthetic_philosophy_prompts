I teach a final-year module in philosophy of science at a UK university, and I'm writing this year's finals paper. One section is on falsificationism and its discontents, and I want a single essay question for it on Malthus — specifically on whether a theory can be "right but early". My students have done Popper and Lakatos properly, plus a bit of Duhem-Quine, so they can all recite that falsification is never clean and that research programmes can stall or progress. What I actually want to test is whether they can apply that machinery to a hard case rather than name-dropping it.

Here's the setup I'd like. Malthus's mechanism — population tending to grow geometrically while subsistence grows arithmetically at best — famously didn't come true where he predicted it, thanks to the demographic transition and the like. Yet people keep insisting he was "right but early," that the preventive and positive checks were internal structure of the theory rather than bolt-ons, and that the live test cases are later reruns such as Ehrlich's Population Bomb or the Club of Rome's Limits to Growth, where the same "we merely postponed the crunch" move got made all over again. The question should force students to grapple with at least this much: (a) the difference between an unconditional point prediction and a conditional structural prediction, and where Malthus's actual claims fall on that spectrum; (b) when adding a condition to a failed prediction counts as legitimate refinement versus what I've been calling, in lectures, "falsification laundering" — re-describing after the fact a prediction that failed as having been conditional all along; and (c) whether "right but early" is even a coherent category for a prediction about a timescale the theorist never pinned down. To seed things a bit, in lectures I used a toy case of a doctor who predicts a patient will collapse within six months "unless the medication keeps working", and then, when the patient is fine at six months, declares vindication — I'd like the question or the model answer to deploy or at least address that analogy and say where it maps onto Malthus and where it breaks.
