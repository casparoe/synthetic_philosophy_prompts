In November 2018 the researcher He Jiankui announced, at an international genome-editing summit in Hong Kong, that twin girls had been born from embryos he had edited with CRISPR, targeting the CCR5 gene in the hope of conferring resistance to HIV. The work had been carried out with little institutional oversight, was condemned almost universally, and He was later sentenced to prison in China. I would like you to analyze this episode through the lens of the unilateralist's curse, and then to say where the theory stops explaining it.

For the first half, be disciplined about the mapping. Who exactly counts as an agent in the curse's sense — every lab with the technical capacity to edit viable embryos, or only those operating under He's particular combination of ambition and lax oversight? What is the act whose value is at stake? And what does the theory recommend each agent do? Walk through the mechanics the curse describes: as the number of capable actors grows, with each one privately judging the act to be on balance bad, the probability that someone does it anyway approaches one, and each actor's restraint does nothing to prevent the bad outcome once the pool is large enough. Then confront the twist the He case adds: nothing in the record suggests He believed his act was bad. Does the curse, which presupposes agents sharing a judgment of badness, actually explain anything here, or does it quietly reduce to a description of dispersed power with no normative bite? And what does the case do to the theory's remedies — coordination, moratoria, peer review, an agreed red line — given that the same biology community did once manage a self-imposed moratorium on recombinant DNA work in the 1970s, while in 2018 every relevant institution condemned He only after the fact?

Then run the analysis in the other direction: what does the He affair show about the unilateralist's curse itself? Is it a confirmation, an anomaly, or a case the theory never anticipated — one that turns not on a sincere agent whose restraint is undone by the size of the pool, but on evasion, ambition, and a world of jurisdictions that share no threshold at all?

Finally, tell me what would change your verdict on whether the curse genuinely applies here: what evidence about He's beliefs, or about the structure of the case, would flip you to a different reading, and at which step of your reasoning is a mistake most likely to have crept in.
