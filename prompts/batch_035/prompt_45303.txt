just got off the phone with Dana after about two hours going in circles on Parfit's hitchhiker and rational irrationality, and I genuinely can't tell anymore whether my position is coherent or whether I was just defending turf. so I want an audit, not a lecture. here's roughly what I believe:

(1) In the original setup — the driver only stops for hitchhikers she predicts will pay, she predicts I'll pay, so I get my ride into town — once I'm standing in town with the money, I should pay her. Full stop. At that moment, "should."

(2) We invented a variant where the driver isn't a predictor at all, she just picks people up on the off-chance they'll pay out of graditude. In that one I'd say stiffing her is unkind, maybe a bit shameful, but not irrational, and I wouldn't say I "should" pay in the same sense as (1). The difference between the two drivers feels real to me and I want to keep it.

(3) I'm on board with the Schelling point that it can be rational to make yourself irrational. Before the trip, if I could take a pill that makes me unable to renege, I'd take it, and taking it is clearly the rational move.

(4) But I also hold, and I argued for this hard tonight, that rationality is a fact about acts at a time. There's no separate fact about whether a disposition is "rational" over and above whether its acts are. I kept telling Dana that "my disposition to pay is rational even if this particular act of paying isn't" is double-counting, and if the acts are irrational the disposition doesn't get to be rational by association.

(5) And at heart I'm a causal decision theory person. What you have most reason to do is what gets you the best outcome causally. Which, standing there in town, is: keep the money, smile, wave, walk away.

(6) One more, which Dana called a weird hill to die on: I don't think it makes any difference to what I should do in town whether she made her prediction last week or as I was climbing in the car.

So where's the actual inconsistency? Dana says (5) just kills (1) and that's the end of it, but I have this feeling (3) is doing some work in the background — except (4) seems to block exactly the move (3) would let me make, and I can't get that straight.
