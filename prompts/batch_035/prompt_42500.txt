I'm preparing a position statement for a workshop on the metaphysics of mathematics and would like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My commitments, in no special order: (1) Platonism about mathematics — there are mind-independent abstract objects, and the theorems of arithmetic quantify over some of them. (2) A uniform semantics — mathematical statements are true or false in just the way ordinary empirical statements are, namely in virtue of how things stand with the objects their terms refer to; the truth of a claim about 17 is fixed by 17 itself, as a claim about Everest is fixed by Everest. (3) The causal theory of knowledge is false — knowledge does not require causal interaction with its object, so the epistemological horn of Benacerraf's dilemma, as originally posed, does not bite; I take this to be the standard and correct response to that horn. (4) Structuralism about arithmetic — numbers are positions in the natural-number structure; any progression of objects could realize that structure, and there is no substantive fact about which particular objects our numerals denote, hence no fact of the matter about whether the number 3 is identical to any particular set. Please tell me which pairs of these commitments are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be; rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally serious; and for each, say what would have to give, and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere. I have no settled view about which commitment I would abandon, so please do not read one into the order in which I have listed them.
