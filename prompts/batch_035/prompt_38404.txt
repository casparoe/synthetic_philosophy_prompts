So last night my friend and I got into a huge argument about panpsychism that ended at like 2am with both of us annoyed. His position was basically that the combination problem is the whole ballgame: if consciousness is fundamental and spread across all the little stuff, fine, but then you owe us a story about how a bunch of micro-experiences (if that's even the right word for whatever an electron has) add up to the unified stream that I'm having right now, typing this, mad at him. My counter was that "you owe us a story" is doing a lot of unexamined work — owe us a story that does WHAT, exactly? Like, physicalists also can't really explain how neurons produce experience, and we don't treat that as an automatic loss for them, so why does the panpsychist have to clear some bar the physicalist doesn't? He said that was cheating, that the panpsychist's whole sales pitch is explaining consciousness "from the inside" and if the combination story is allowed to be a brute mystery then the sales pitch is void. And around and around we went.

What I actually want, to prepare for the rematch, is a proper requirements list: what does a solution to the combination problem actually have to deliver to count as a solution? I can think of a bunch of candidate constraints but I don't trust myself to sort the real ones from camp prejudice — e.g., it has to explain subject-summing specifically (why N micro-subjects yield ONE macro-subject rather than just a crowd of co-located little minds), it has to say something about why the macro-experience's structure tracks brain structure (why a lesion changes my experience instead of just unplating some micro-experiences), it has to solve the boundary/decomposition issue (why does this brain sum and the room it's in not), and it arguably can't be allowed to just quietly reintroduce strong emergence at the level of subjects, since the whole point was that fundamental experience was supposed to make emergence unnecessary. There's also the "no new fundamental relations" instinct — if you need a special phenomenal bonding relation that only experiences enter into, that smells ad hoc — and the demand that the solution generalize to the rock/table cases that panpsychists themselves bring up as bullets they'll bite.

So here's the task: write me the desiderata, each with the case or consideration that motivates it, and then actually commit — tell me which ones you think are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are preferences of a particular camp, flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable (I half suspect "no subject-level emergence" plus "a macro-subject exists" might already be in tension, and I want you to take a side on that, not lay out how different people would answer), and tell me which single requirement does the most to kill the various combination solutions floating around, whether that's some kind of phenomenal bonding, subject-summing by shared structure, or just "the micro-level facts plus structural facts jointly fix the macro facts, no further relation needed." Don't hedge into "it depends on how we define" — pick, defend, and if you think my framing above smuggles in a bad assumption (like that "micro-experience" is coherent), say that straight out and say what the list looks like once you fix it. A table is fine for the list itself but the verdicts I want in prose. No reading lists or name-dropping a bunch of papers, just the argument. Also, fair warning, my friend will absolutely notice if a "requirement" is just the hard problem of consciousness wearing a fake mustache, so be explicit about which entries are doing that and whether that's fatal or fine.
