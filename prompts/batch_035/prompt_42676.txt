My research group has spent the last year and a half basically split down the middle on whether advanced AI systems should be goal-directed or approval-directed — on the one side the classic "specify an objective, make it good enough, aim the system at it" picture, on the other the picture Paul Christiano pushed years ago where the system just always picks the action its overseer would most approve of, moment to moment, without executing any plan toward a goal of its own. We've argued about this so much at whiteboards and in group meetings that we've started talking past each other, and our advisor has told us to do a proper adversarial collaboration writeup before the group dissolves. I've been tasked with drafting the joint statement, and honestly I'm too close to it — every time I try to word a disagreement neutrally, someone accuses me of strawmanning their side (the approval people say the goal people "just want a fixed utility function", the goal people say approval-maximization "is just a goal with extra steps", and round we go).

So draft the joint statement for me. I want: (1) a numbered section of claims both camps could genuinely sign — about what each side is trying to protect us from, what they jointly think goes wrong with naive optimization, what they agree approval is and isn't for, where they agree the real danger lies — but keep it non-vacuous, we've all signed too many documents that say nothing; (2) the exact disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording as a fair statement of their view; (3) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side — and for each crux a concrete thought experiment, failure case, or argument that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Some candidate cruxes we've been circling: does an approval-directed agent that models its overseer's approval well enough re-develop instrumental behavior anyway (e.g. by seeking to shape what the overseer sees or approves of); does a goal-directed agent with a genuinely well-specified goal still have the shutdown problem in the way corrigibility people say it does, and does approval just dissolve that or relocate it; what happens when the overseer's approval is manipulable or lazy or delegated; and whether "approval maximization is just another goal" is a substantive objection or a confusion about levels of description.

Also, at the end, tell me which parts of the dispute you think are substantive and which are merely verbal, since if we can prune the verbal ones the collaboration has already earned its keep. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table (disagreement / approval-side wording / goal-side wording / crux / test / predictions), then the short verbal-vs-substantive section. And please be genuinely fair to both sides — several people in the group will read this closely, and each of them knows exactly what their own side's position sounds like when it's being strawmanned.
