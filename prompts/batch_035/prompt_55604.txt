My friend and I have been going around about two-dimensional semantics for like six years now, and last night was the worst round of it yet — four hours, a whiteboard, a lot of "no look, that's just the diagonal." He's all in on the Chalmers version: primary and secondary intensions, water could have turned out to be XYZ, names and natural kind terms having these two routes from world to extension. I'm closer to the Soames line: the primary intension is just a description we happen to associate with the term, so the whole thing is descriptivism with better typography, and the "necessity a posteriori" data that 2D was supposed to illuminate was already handled by Kripke without it. And the thing that's starting to bug me is that neither of us has moved an inch in six years, and as far as I can tell neither has the literature since the mid-2000s — Soames wrote a whole book attacking it, Chalmers and Jackson replied, and everyone's just been in their lanes since.

So I want a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. What KIND of disagreement is this, actually? Some candidates I've been kicking around since last night:

(a) It's verbal. He's doing metasemantics (what fixes reference for speakers like us, given our epistemic situation) and I'm doing truth-conditional semantics (what the sentences mean once reference is fixed), and "intension" means different things in our mouths. If that's right we should be able to find some sentence where we genuinely talk past each other, and honestly sometimes it feels like we do.

(b) It's a proxy war. He wants 2D because of where it lands on conceivability arguments — his primary intensions are supposed to be the thing that makes the zombie reasoning go through, and I resist 2D for exactly the same reason in reverse. Neither of us has EVER argued about it on purely semantic grounds, now that I think about it, so maybe the semantics debate is downstream of a fight about the epistemology of modality that we've never had out loud.

(c) It's substantive but evidence-starved. A semantic theory should earn its keep on data — truth conditions for actual sentences people assert. Can anyone point to a sentence where 2D assigns different (and better) truth conditions than plain old Kaplan-style character/content, or plain direct reference? He says the necessary a posteriori and the "it's necessary that Hesperus is Phosphorus but you could've found out otherwise" stuff, but I keep saying direct reference gets all of that already and 2D only adds a story about WHY it feels weird, which maybe is psychology not semantics.

There's also this historical thing I noticed and cant tell if it matters: the framework had a modest job first — Stalnaker's diagonal for assertion and context-shifts, which nobody fights about — and the war only started when it got promoted into a general theory of meaning and modality. Stalnaker himself always seemed lukewarm about the inflated version. Does that matter for diagnosing the stall? Like is the fight really about how much work a semantic framework should be asked to do?

And give it to me straight at the end: is this stalled because the question is ill-posed, because the two camps are optimizing for different explanatory virtues and neither cares about the other's scoreboard, or because there's some actual thing — an application that clearly works, a formal result, a sharable datum — that would count as progress and just hasn't shown up? If it's the last one, what would it look like?
