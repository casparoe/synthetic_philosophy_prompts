I run a weekly reading group on philosophy of mind for undergrads, about 14 people, 90 minutes, and next session's topic is the symbol grounding problem (Harnad's old line about how symbols can't get their meaning just from other symbols). Instead of me lecturing, I want a game they play first where they run into the problem themselves before anyone names it. Can you design it?

Here's the shape I have in mind, feel free to improve on it: pairs, where one person is a "lookup system" that only knows a finite set of word-to-word mappings (like a dictionary that defines everything in terms of other entries in itself), and the other person is a "user" who has to actually do something with a target concept -- answer novel questions, sort new cases, pick between options. No pointing, no pictures, no gestures, no tone of voice; pure verbal symbols all the way down. Then some twist that makes them feel the difference between the pairs that stayed purely symbol-to-symbol and whatever the "grounded" condition is -- maybe a round where a couple pairs quietly get allowed one ostensive act, and then you compare performance on transfer questions neither side has seen. The users should discover that the purely definitional system cracks exactly when you step outside the material it was built from, and nobody should be told why some pairs are doing better.

What I need from you: full rules, materials (stuff I can print ahead of time, no software), a timeline that fits 90 minutes including the debrief, and how to handle the fact that my students WILL try to cheat the no-pointing rule and how that cheating can itself be used in the debrief rather than just policed. The debrief should map specific moments in the game onto the actual distinction (symbol-symbol vs. symbol-thing, why the circularity isn't automatically vicious, etc.) and should be honest about one thing a game like this cannot reproduce -- like, whatever my students feel, the whole system taken as a unit might still be doing something the inside person isn't, and a game with humans sitting inside it can't really stage thatSystems-reply point. Also tell me what predictably goes wrong the first time I run it. Don't give away "symbol grounding" in the setup at all -- they only hear the name in the debrief.
