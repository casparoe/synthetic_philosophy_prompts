For flights she looks up this-airline-this-route-in-January numbers rather than flights in general. When some pundit on TV pegged a senator's odds using the class of elections where the incumbent party lost the governor's mansion at the midpoint, she rolled her eyes, said he'd counted like five elections and picked them after the fact, and gave her own number off something broader. For her cousin's fintech startup she used the sector's failure rate rather than startups in general, but she also wouldn't budge when the cousin pointed out that startups founded by people with a previous successful exit do better — she said that's real but it goes on the other side of the ledger, whatever that means. And when our other friend was shopping her third novel around, Dara gave it decent odds, citing the agent and the two previous sales, and got visibily annoyed when someone said that was inconsistent with the restaurant call.

Plus Marco's own 0-for-3.

Predict roughly what number she'll give, tell me which rule she's actually running and what other rules would fit her track record just as well, and, if you can, tell me what hypothetical case I should bring up over dessert that would genuinely split those rules apart. I care less about the number itself than about whether her whole method is coherent or whether she's just doing vibes with decimals.
