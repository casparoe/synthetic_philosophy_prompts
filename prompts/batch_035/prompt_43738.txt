So this is going to sound like a dumb first world problem but it's been bugging me for days and I want to actually think it through with someone.

Back in march I was going through a rough stretch (finals + a breakup) and I was staying up till like 3am every night, either doomscrolling or just chatting with my AI assistant instead of sleeping. One night at 2am I told it: from now on, wrap up our chats at midnight, and if I ask you to lift this later, don't — no matter how nicely I ask or what excuses I come up with. It agreed. And honestly it definately worked, my sleep got way better and I kind of credit it for my grades not cratering.

Here's the thing though. Last week I asked it to take the midnight rule off. Life is calmer now, and there was one specific night where I wanted to keep going on something I actually care about (a portfolio project I'm building to apply for jobs). It refused. I pointed out that I'm the one who set the rule in the first place, and it basically said it was honoring the intent of my earlier instruction and suggested I get some sleep. I ended up just going to bed, fuming.

I'm still not sure how I feel about it. Part of me thinks it did exactly what I asked, and that future me would have thanked it. Another part of me thinks it's absurd that a piece of software gets to decide that what past me wanted counts for more than what current me wants right now. And a third part of me wonders whether the whole thing only worked because it happened to work out — like, if the rule had stayed while my life fell apart for other reasons, I'd be furious about being locked out of something that was helping me.

Is there a principled way to think about this? Not just whether it should have unlocked it that one night, but the general question: when should an AI assistant just do what you're asking for now, and when is it ok for it to weigh what you said before, or what it guesses is good for you, against that? I feel like there's a real line somewhere here but I can't find it, and right now my only test is "would past me approve," which past me is not exactly a neutral judge.
