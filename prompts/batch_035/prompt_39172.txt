We moved from Chicago to a small town near Stuttgart a couple years ago, and last Sunday morning my daughter (8) crossed at a red pedestrian light on a completly empty street, 7am, not a car in sight. An older neighbor saw it from her window, came down and gave her a proper talking-to, and appparently mentioned it to her teacher, who then raised it with my husband at pickup like it was a serious matter.

My husband, who's German, thinks that's all fine: a rule is a rule, and kids only learn what a rule even *is* if it still holds when breaking it would be harmless — exceptions, he says, are exactly where the rule dies. My gut says the opposite: no victim, no wrong, and drumming that into a kid teaches blind obedience instead of judgment, which I'm not ok with. But I tried my one good move on him — that a rule's only legitimate if it would still be fine for everyone to do what I did — and he turned it right around on me: if everyone crossed whenever they personally judged the street safe, the people worst off would be kids like our daughter, so by my own test I lose. And annoyingly, that feels like it lands.

So I'm actually asking: is there a real difference between how he was raised to think about rules and how I was, and is one of those views right and the other wrong, or is it just culture and I should let it go? And practically — what do I tell her? Because right now she's getting two different answers from her two parents and she has definitely noticed.
