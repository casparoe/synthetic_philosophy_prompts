I work on mechanistic interpretability — features, circuits, ablations, the usual — and I've just spent the better part of a train ride arguing with an old friend who did his PhD in medieval philosophy about whether "same mechanism" is a well-defined notion. He's now claiming the whole debate I thought we were having is just the problem of universals wearing a hoodie, and I want to see whether he's right by doing the translation properly.

The argument, briefly: I held that mechanistic sameness is an empirical question with a crisp criterion — two components in one model, or in two models, instantiate the same mechanism if they play the same causal role, which we test with interventions (ablate one, patch in the other, check the behavioral difference). He kept producing cases that made my criterion wobble: an induction head in a 2-layer model versus whatever the in-context-learning circuitry of a much larger model amounts to; a "French token" feature realized as a single direction in one checkpoint and as a distributed set of weakly aligned directions after a fine-tune that rotates the basis; the same circuit in two models trained from different seeds, where the differences are not just in the parameterization but in marginal edge cases each handles. In each case I found myself unable to say whether there was one mechanism, two, or an ill-posed question, and he said that's exactly the "one over many" his people have been fighting about since Plato wrote the Third Man argument.

So here's the request. Translate the main positions on the problem of universals — Platonic realism, Aristotelian realism, conceptualism, resemblance nominalism, and trope theory — into my working vocabulary: features as directions or subspaces in activation space, circuits as subgraphs with causal roles, weight bases and rotations, model organisms, patching and ablation experiments, superposition and distributed representations. Do each position faithfully, as it would be argued by someone fluent in both literatures. For instance, if I've understood him at all, the resemblance nominalist should say that the two French-token realizations are not two instances of a shared thing but merely resemble each other, and that resemblance itself comes in degrees with no fact of the matter about where sameness begins — I want to know if that's a coherent thing to say in my vocabulary or whether it collapses.

Then give me the residue report. Which parts of each position translate cleanly? Which parts require distortion to fit — e.g., does "causal role," which I can actually measure with interventions, quietly do the work that a realist universal was supposed to do while denying the ontology? And is there anything in the philosophers' dispute with no counterpart at all in interpretability talk — the Third Man worry, say, or the question of whether universals exist uninstantiated (is there such a thing as a mechanism that no network has ever implemented, and does anything in my practice ever commit me on that)? Finally, a verdict: does the residue show that my vocabulary is missing something real, or that the metaphysical dispute was less substantive than it looked all along? My friend and I have a rematch scheduled, and I'd like to arrive armed.
