my metaethics reading group is doing expressivism next week and i want to open the session with a villanelle on the Frege–Geach problem. heres the gimmick: pick one moral claim (lying is wrong, cruelty to animals is wrong, whatever). one refrain asserts it flat out; the other refrain embeds exactly that same claim as the antecedent of a conditional. the trick is that a villanelle's refrains come back word-for-word in new stanzas, with new lines around them each time, and they have to mean the same thing everytime — which is precisely what the expressivist has to explain and cant just stipulate away. so the form does the argument. and dont end on a shrug: i want the poem to commit to the sameness of meaning between the asserted use and the embedded use, and to show the expressivist paying the cost of holding onto that — maybe the final couplet, where the two refrains finally sit side by side, is where the sameness gets owned outright. someone in the group who knows the literature should be able to tell whats going on without a program note. after the poem, add one short section (3-4 sentences) titled "what the poem is doing", where you say where you took liberties with the ideas or leaned on a contested reading of the problem.
