hey! so I'm putting together a little anthology of philosophical-ish sci-fi shorts and I want one on how meaning gets into a signal in the first place.

quick setup (from Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach — I'll summarize so you dont need it): he splits any message into three layers. the frame message is just the fact that there IS a message at all — like noticing the marks in the sand are Japanese writing rather than random scratches, before you can read a word of it. the outer message is the decoding instructions — knowing it's Japanese doesn't mean you can read Japanese, and the text has to somehow bootstrap you into reading it. the inner message is the actual content, the thing finally being said.

here's the story I want: a radio astronomer detects a signal from a long-dead civilization, and after decades of collective effort humanity cracks the frame and outer layers... and the inner message turns out to be almost nothing. something like: we existed, and we hoped someone would notice. and I want the story to make the reader feel why that's not a letdown but the whole point — that being the kind of beings who can pick a message out of noise is itself where the meaning lives, maybe more than in whatever the message says.

some things I'd love you to work in (not all required, your call):

- a running argument with a colleague who insists the signal is natural — a weird pulsar, interstellar scattering, whatever — so the frame message itself is contested, not just the decoding
- the astronomer's personal life mirroring the layers somehow — like a relationship where two people know they're misunderstanding each other (frame recognized) but can never quite find the decoding key (outer), or an estranged parent whose last letter they still haven't opened
- if you want a twist, maybe the inner message, once decoded, is just instructions for decoding — the outer message all the way down — or a message that reads differently (or not at all) depending on which frame the reader brings to it

aim for maybe 800-1200 words, and dont feel like you need to explain Hofstadter's terminology inside the story itself — the three layers should do real plot work rather than get lectured about. feel free to take a different direction than my sketch if you see a better one, as long as the gap between recognizing-a-message and decoding-it stays at the center.
