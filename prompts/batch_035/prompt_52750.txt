If I can either cure one stranger's chronic migraines or give ten strangers the best month of their whole lives, I cure the migraines, no hesitation. If there's a pill that takes the edge off my worst days but also flattens my best days, I take it. Between two futures with the same number of people — one where everyone is mildly content and nothing ever goes badly wrong, and one where everyone is wildly happy most of the time but a few thousand people get tortured — I pick the mild one, even though the second future has vastly more total happiness in it.

But now the parts that don't fit. If I could flip a coin where heads gives a stranger a year of pure bliss and tails gives them a week of mild depression, I flip it without thinking — so a week of suffering trades off against happiness just fine, it's not sacred. And I think a universe with one genuinely happy child in it is better than an empty universe, even though nobody is made better off by the child existing. Yet I also can't shake the feeling that the worst suffering — like one person in permanent agony — can't be made up for by any amount of happiness anywhere, even billions of ecstatic lives. My brother says these definately can't all be true at once: if a week of depression trades off fine against bliss, why does permanent agony get special treatment, and if a happy life makes the world better, then it must be outweighing whatever suffering is inside it.
