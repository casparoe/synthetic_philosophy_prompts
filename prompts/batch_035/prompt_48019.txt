i've been chewing on the meta-problem of consciousness — chalmers's thing where instead of explaining consciousness itself you explain why we all go around insisting there's a hard problem in the first place — and i have an essay half-drafted that makes a specific claim, but i can't tell if it's a real point or just me being clever. tell me if it falls apart.

the thesis: the meta-problem literature treats our problem-reports as one kind of datum, but they're actually two different kinds that can come apart, and the standard framing quietly only captures one of them.

the two kinds, as i want to split them:

1. gap-reports: reports of an inability. i can't see how this physical story could add up to the feeling of pain. these are, on their face, about my own explanatory capacity — plausibly the output of some limitation or quirk of a mind trying to reductively explain itself, the cognitive equivalent of an optical illusion.

2. what-it's-like reports: avowals that there is something it is like to be me right now. these don't present as being about any inability of mine at all. they present as a datum, not a gap.

the move: the meta-problem, as usually posed (explain why people make the reports they make about consciousness), is essentially a research program about gap-reports. a completed physical explanation of gap-reports would be exactly what you'd expect a physicalist world to contain: minds like ours hit a wall when they run reductive explanations on themselves and report the wall. fine, easy-problem-shaped, everyone signs off.

but the what-it's-like reports aren't reports of a wall. if i give you the full causal history of why you say there's something it's like, i've explained the report — but the thing you were reporting wasn't a fact about your explanatory limits, so redescription of the datum as a datum-about-reports doesn't obviously touch it. my claim is that the problem-report framing works when the report is an expression of puzzlement and fails when it's a direct avowal, and that nobody has been careful about that difference. where this leaves me: the meta-problem is a genuine shared-data project between dualists and physicalists, but only for half the data, and the half it doesn't cover is the half dualists actually care about.

the objection i'm least sure how to handle: an illusionist would presumably just say that what-it's-like avowals are also products of limitations — introspective ones — and that the datum they report doesn't exist to be missed. which maybe just collapses my two kinds back into one. i don't have a reply yet.

anyway, please critique the draft. give me exactly five numbered points, each one or two sentences, no intro or conclusion — hit whatever's weakest: does the two-kinds split actually survive, have i misread how the meta-problem framing is standardly posed, is the illusionist reply fatal or answerable, whatever else you think breaks.
