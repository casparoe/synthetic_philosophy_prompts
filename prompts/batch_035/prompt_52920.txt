Two friends at a physics meetup got into it about whether relativity forces you to beleive in the block universe (the idea that past, present and future all exist equally, no objective flowing "now").

Marco opened with the classic Rietdijk-Putnam style argument: in special relativity simultaneity is relative to your frame, so an astronaut flying past Earth at high speed has a "now" that can include events that are in my distant future, say in Andromeda. If what's happening now for her is real, my future events are real — at least for her. But then chain observers together and you can reach any future event whatsoever, so the future is exactly as real as the present, and the flowing now is an illusion. The universe is a four-dimensional block, period.

Jules's first objection was that simultaneity in SR is just a convention for coordinating clocks, bookkeeping, not a fact about existence — like latitude lines, and you wouldn't say a city exists less because it sits at a different longitude. Marco replied that the argument doesn't need simultaneity to be deep, it just needs the premise that whatever is real for one observer is real, full stop, and since planes of simultaneity criss-cross into each other's futures, the block follows anyway. Jules said that premise is exactly what he denies: "real for an observer" is a confused notion — reality isn't indexed to frames, real is real. Marco came back with: fine, if reality is frame-invariant then either every frame's now-plane counts or none does, and "none" would make even the present unreal, so all of them count and you get the block that way instead.

Then Jules pivoted: relativity of simultaneity is a feature of flat spacetime, but in the actual universe cosmology hands you a preferred foliation, cosmic time, so you can perfectly well say the objectively real now is the cosmic now and the future just isn't there yet. Marco said that's resting your metaphysics on an accident of how the matter happens to be distributed — make the universe slightly lopsided and your precious foliation disappears. Jules shot back that everyone's metaphysics is hostage to physics, that's the whole game, and at least his story explains why the present feels so vivid while Marco's leaves becoming a total mystery.
