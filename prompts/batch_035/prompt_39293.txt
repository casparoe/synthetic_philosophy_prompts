I'm a backend engineer with no formal philosophy training; everything I know about this comes from a podcast and one essay I've read a few times now, Strawson's Freedom and Resentment. I'm putting together a short write-up of where I stand on moral responsibility for an internal discussion group at work (we're mostly engineers, so the bar for rigor is "sounds coherent over coffee"), and before I embarrass myself I'd like a consistency audit of my position. Here is roughly what I believe. (1) Determinism is probably true in the sense that matters: every choice I make is fully the product of prior causes, and given those causes I couldn't have done otherwise. (2) I think Strawson more or less settled the free will debate: since our reactive attitudes -- resentment, gratitude, indignation, hurt feelings when someone slights us -- are an inescapable feature of human interpersonal life and aren't going anywhere, the question of whether determinism makes blame "justified" is a kind of pseudo-problem, and anyone arguing that we should drop blame in light of determinism is fighting basic human psychology. (3) At the same time, I've been meditating seriously for about three years, largely following a Stoic-flavored practice, and my sincere goal is to eventually stop feeling resentment and indignation entirely -- to meet everyone, including the colleague who sabotaged me last year, from something like Strawson's objective standpoint, treating them as a system of causes to be managed rather than as a target of blame. (4) I believe resentment toward a person is appropriate if and only if they were genuinely responsible for the slight, which is why I don't resent my boss's behavior now that I've learned he has a brain tumor that's affecting his impulse control, but I do still resent the colleague from (3), even though I also believe his behavior is equally the product of causes he didn't choose. (5) On the practical side, I think blame is useless as a system for improving behavior -- clear feedback, consequences, and training do everything blame does without the collateral damage -- so in my day job I've largely replaced blame-talk with purely forward-looking performance management, and I think everyone should.

So: does this hang together? My suspicion is that my picture is fine, but a friend in the group claims that (2) and (3) flatly contradict each other -- you can't both think the reactive attitudes are inescapable and make shedding them your life goal -- and another says (5) amounts to abandoning the participant stance in exactly the way (2) declares impossible, so my "practical" view smuggles in the philosophical position I claimed to have refuted. I don't know if either of them is right, and I also can't tell whether (4) contains a real tension between my tumor-excuse instinct and my resentment of the colleague, or whether it's just a difference of degree. Please go through the commitments pairwise where it matters, tell me which tensions are genuine and which only look like tensions, rank the real ones by severity, and for each say what the cheapest revision would be and what that revision costs me. If one single change would clean up the most, tell me which one and why. Assume I'm willing to give something up if I have to, but I'd rather know exactly what I'm paying for it.
