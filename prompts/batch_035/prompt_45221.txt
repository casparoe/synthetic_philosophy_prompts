Write a parody tenure-evaluation letter, around 450 words, from a senior philosopher who accepts Galen Strawson's Basic Argument and is determined to write a letter consistent with it. The humor must come from getting the argument right, not from caricaturing it: he knows the argument doesn't rest on determinism, so he can't appeal to the causal closure of physics as his excuse, and he knows that random quantum nudges to the candidate's deliberations would not have made her more responsible either — the problem is that nobody can be ultimately responsible for how they are, since that would require being causa sui, and any appeal to the candidate's having shaped her own character just pushes the question back to whatever shaped the shaper. Every evaluative sentence must be one he can sincerely utter: praise recast as reliability, consequences, and inherited causes (training, upbringing, luck), never as desert; and the same constraint must apply to the criticism section, where a weak publication cannot be the candidate's fault any more than a strong one is her credit. He should catch and repair at least one lapse into ordinary merit-language mid-paragraph. The letter still has to function as a competent, specific, comparative tenure letter addressed to a committee that expects desert-talk, so a reader finishes genuinely unsure what, if anything, is left of praise once ultimate responsibility goes. Present it as exactly five numbered paragraphs of two or three sentences each, with no salutation, signature, or anything outside the numbered items.
