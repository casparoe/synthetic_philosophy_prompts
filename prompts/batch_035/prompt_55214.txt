Our philosophy of religion discussion group has been meeting for about two years and the divine hiddenness debate has basically calcified into a fixed script. Quick sketch so you know where everyone's at: one side says a perfectly loving God would make sure nobody ever ends up in nonresistant nonbelief — people who don't believe in God but aren't resisting, sincere seekers included — so the fact that such people clearly exist is evidence against God. The other side has a stable of replies: that there's no such thing as genuinely nonresistant nonbelief if you dig into it (people are more self-deceived than they admit), that hiddenness is actually respect for freedom since a real relationship needs room to be declined, that we're terrible judges of what a being like that would do, or that hiddenness is just the problem of suffering wearing a different hat. Nobody in the group has moved an inch in two years and I'm starting to wonder what kind of disagreement this even is. My pet hypothesis is it's not about evidence at all — it's that each side smuggles in a different picture of what love between persons requires. The believers think love involves distance and patience, the other side thinks love means communicative availability, and each reads thier picture into "perfectly loving" and then finds the other side's God cold or the other side's demand needy. But maybe I'm wrong: maybe it's really about burden of proof, or about which cases count as nonresistant (the person praying and hearing nothing vs. the person who never thinks about it), or it's the two sides answering different questions under one label. Give me an actual diagnosis using what each side claims, not a survey of the positions, and then be honest with me about whether anything — a sharper distinction, a better thought experiment, whatever — could count as progress here, or whether the argument is just a machine for converting priors into fancy words.
