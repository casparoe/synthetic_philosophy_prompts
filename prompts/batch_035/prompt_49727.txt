so, context: I'm a PhD student in political philosophy, and for the last two years I've also been doing grant evaluations for a small EA-adjacent fund, which means I actually have to produce cause rankings rather than just read about them. before our next funding round I want to check whether my working views are even jointly holdable, since I've never written them down side by side. Here they are, numbered:

1. The importance/tractability/neglectedness (ITN) framework is the right skeleton for cause prioritization, and the three factors should be combined into an overall score that you can literally rank causes by.
2. Ethically I hold a person-affecting view: there's no value in creating happy people for their own sake, and our obligations run only to people who do or will exist.
3. Despite (2), I rank reducing existential and global catastrophic risk at or near the top of my own priorities.
4. I take cluelessness seriously, in the sense philosophers use it: for most actions the indirect effects on the far future swamp the direct ones, and we can't even get the sign of the long-run expected value.
5. My escape from (4) is that cluelessness lives in the scale factor — the far future is the thing we can't see — while tractability and neglectedness are local, checkable facts about the present. So you can still rank causes on T and N plus rough scale, without pretending to know long-run EVs.
6. Neglectedness is a fact about the world's total attention, EA attention included, so a well-publicized ITN analysis partly eats the neglectedness it measures — which I take to mean the community sometimes shouldn't publish, or openly coordinate around, its rankings.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all the same. For each real one, say what would have to give and what giving it up costs. My suspicion is that (5) is doing more work than it can actually bear, but I might be wrong — maybe the problem is somewhere I haven't looked. Whatever your audit says, please end with a machine-readable final line of the form \verdict{N}, where N is the number of the single commitment whose revision would clean up the most, or the word "none" if you think the set is tenable as is.
