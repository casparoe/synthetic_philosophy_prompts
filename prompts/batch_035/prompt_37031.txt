Next month I'm giving a talk at an interpretability workshop -- audience is maybe 30 people, mostly ML researchers doing circuit-level work (the causal scrubbing / activation patching crowd), plus two philosophers of science who keep dragging in the New Mechanist literature, and one grumpy empiricist who thinks any talk of "mechanisms" at all is just a vibe. My thesis, roughly: when people in mechanistic anomaly detection say a model is "doing something via a new mechanism" off-distribution, they're implicitly relying on a notion of mechanism-individuation that nobody has made precise, and the right way to cash it out is functional -- two behaviors run on the same mechanism iff they degrade together under a shared battery of interventions (ablations, retraining under distribution shift, etc.), not iff they share weights or share circuit topology. Same-mechanism is a relation defined by counterfactual co-failure, and it's probably intransitive, and that's fine, we just have to stop pretending it carves nature at hidden joints.
