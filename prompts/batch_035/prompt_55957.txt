I'm a software engineer who builds telemetry and monitoring dashboards for a hospital network, so I spend a lot of time thinking about what makes a measurement meaningful, and the one clinical quantity I keep coming back to puzzles me: pain. Across medicine, pain is measured by self-report — a 0–10 number, or a row of cartoon faces — and that number goes on the chart alongside temperature and blood pressure, where it drives real decisions about analgesia. Pain has even been promoted as a fifth vital sign, despite there being no instrument that reads it. At the same time, clinicians routinely override a high self-report when vital signs and behaviour look calm, in cases they file as drug-seeking rather than pain. I've been reading around the philosophy of mind, and I gather illusionism holds that phenomenal properties — the subjective, ineffable what-it-is-like character of experience — simply don't exist; there are only physical and functional brain states, plus our habit of judging that there is something more.

What view of consciousness does pain measurement as an institutional practice actually presuppose? Does treating the patient's say-so as the primary data commit the hospital to there being a private quality that only the subject can access and report — exactly the sort of thing the illusionist says is an introspective illusion? Or could an illusionist keep the scales exactly as they are, on the grounds that reports reliably track real functional and representational states, and that a useful measurement doesn't need the property it purports to measure to be metaphysically respectable? And is the practice internally consistent: if the patient's report is authoritative because only the patient can access the sensation, what licenses the drug-seeking overrides — does the institution half-believe a behaviourist, third-person standard after all?

Please reconstruct the implicit theory, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits well with, say where it is philosophically underdetermined such that several views of consciousness would license it, and tell me what would actually have to change in the wards if illusionism were adopted as official doctrine.
