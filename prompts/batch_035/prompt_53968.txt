here's the pile of views I've landed on for my reading group's topic this month — could you have come from a different sperm and egg — and before I put it in front of the group I want to know whether it actually hangs together or whether I'm holding positions that secretly hate each other. the set:

(1) Kripke-ish about artifacts: this very desk couldn't have been made in a different workshop out of completely different planks and still been this desk. though I'm fine with a few planks being replaced over the years, its still the desk.

(2) origin essentialism about me: if a different sperm had reached the egg, or the same sperm a different egg, the result wouldn't have been me, just some never-existent sibling. that one feels rock solid when I say it fast.

(3) but the genes themselves aren't the point. if the zygote I came from had one gene edited at the single-cell stage — eye color, whatever — that's still me, just me with different eyes.

(4) over time, what makes me me is psychological continuity realized in the brain. cerebrum transplanted into another body, I go with the cerebrum. gradual replacement of everything else, fine, still me. on this view my origin is just the causal story of how my brain got built, not part of what I am. and I'll admit: when I actually picture the kid from the different sperm — same parents, near-identical brain, near-identical life — part of me wants to say that's me too, not sure what to do with that one.

(5) on the modality side I like a counterpart-style semantics: when I say I could have been a sailor, whats making that true is some counterpart of mine being a sailor. but I ALSO think theres a real, determinate fact about whether I could have had different origins — not something that depends on which similarity relation we happen to find natural, or on how we choose to talk.

(6) twinning: if that zygote had split into identical twins, I'm pulled both ways — toward saying neither twin would be me, and toward saying one of them would be me with no fact about which. and separately I hold that identity claims are always determinately true or false.

go through the pairs — which of these are genuinely in tension and how badly, which only look like it, and for each real one what the cheapest fix costs. then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. don't be diplomatic about it, this is a reading group, I can afford to give something up — I just genuinely don't know which one, and I'd rather find out from you than in front of everyone tuesday.
