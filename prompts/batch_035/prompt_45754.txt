Before I read any further, I want the specification these accounts should be judged against. Write the desiderata: what an adequate theory of creativity has to deliver — which phenomena it must explain (why the space of plausible variations is small enough to explore rather than astronomically large; how a successful variant can itself get reified and become a base-level concept that future twiddling takes for granted; why we credit some variations as creative and dismiss others as trivial rearrangements), which distinctions it must respect (between recombining known elements and generating genuinely new dimensions of variation; between novelty to an individual and novelty to a culture; between unconventionality and creativity proper), and which cases it cannot get wrong — for instance, a chess variant with one altered rule and general relativity both start from existing structures, but we are reluctant to call them creative in the same sense, and the theory should say why or bite the bullet. Give the motivating case behind each requirement, mark which are non-negotiable and which merely express the prejudices of one camp, and tell me whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect the requirement that creativity be genuinely new and the requirement that it be traceable to prior structures pull against each other, and that tension is what I most want to see worked out. Then, briefly, assess the knob-twiddling model and a brute-force search model against the list; keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard.
