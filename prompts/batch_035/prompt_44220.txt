I run a monthly philosophy evening at a bookshop, and next month's text is the butterfly passage from the Zhuangzi — Zhuang Zhou dreams he is a butterfly, flits about pleased with itself, wakes as a man, and then cannot work out whether he is a man who dreamt he was a butterfly or a butterfly now dreaming he is a man. The audience is curious adults with no philosophical training, and a fair number of them were drawn in by pop-science podcasts that bill the passage as an ancient forerunner of simulation-theory scepticism. I have one evening, ninety minutes, a flipchart, and no Chinese. Plan the session for me rather than explaining the passage yourself: the order the pieces must come in and what depends on what — in particular, whether the passage can mean anything to them before they have seen what kind of question Zhuangzi is not asking; whether to let the simulation framing carry the evening and then prise it apart, or to head it off at the door — and if not that, what concrete example should carry the whole thing, what it must have and what it must lack, given that the butterfly does not seem to be a randomly chosen dream-object and the closing remark about the transformation of things seems to be doing work that the Cartesian reading quietly deletes, so the question is where in the sequence that remark can safely land; the misconceptions I should expect, in the order they will actually arise — I can already hear that he is saying all perspectives are equally true, that this is just lucid dreaming, and that Descartes got there first and did it properly — and exactly where each one should be absorbed into the flow rather than argued down; what I should openly refuse to explain at this level and say so, whether that is the textual history, the wider metaphysics, or the scholarly argument over whether the passage is meant as an argument, a joke, or neither; and one question I could put to the room at the end that would tell me whether they have grasped what the passage is doing rather than merely been charmed by it.
