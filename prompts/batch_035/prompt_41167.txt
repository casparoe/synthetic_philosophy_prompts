Could you write a dialogue between two experimental philosophers who disagree about what the Knobe effect actually shows? You know the finding: when people hear about a chairman who adopts a program for profit, doesn't care at all about the environment, and the program happens to harm it, most say he intentionally harmed it; in the mirror case where the program happens to help it, most deny he intentionally helped it. One character should think the asymmetry reveals something about the concept of intentionality itself — that it's partly a normative concept, so the folk judgments are tracking something real and the philosophers who defined "intentional" purely cognitively got it wrong. The other should think it's an artifact — roughly that people hear "intentionally" in the harm case as a way of saying "blameworthily," or that blame contaminates the judgment, and that under the right conditions (right wording, right population) the asymmetry should shrink or vanish. To keep them honest, have them argue over a concrete new case rather than the chairman: a hospital administrator who adopts a cost-cutting scheduling system knowing it will add an hour of uncompensated paperwork to every nurse's shift, and says he doesn't care about that, only the budget — versus the mirror case where the system removes an hour of paperwork. Would the asymmetry even show up there, what should follow if it does, and does it matter that the nurses aren't the environment (the victim is internal to the organization, and the outcome is bad but arguably not wrong in the same way)? Real back-and-forth please — each one responding to the other's actual points, with the strongest objection to each side coming out of the other's mouth. No strawmen, and no referee swooping in at the end to declare a winner.
