Quick background so we're on the same page, since we'll be using these terms loosely in what follows: my philosophy reading group has spent the last month on testimony, and I've been tasked with writing up where everyone actually disagrees, so I want a number to anchor things.

Reductionists think whatever justification you get from someone telling you something has to bottom out in non-testimonial sources — you observe that certain kinds of people tend to be right about certain kinds of things, you induct, and that's where your trust in any particular speaker comes from. Anti-reductionists think testimony is a basic source of justification, like perception: a competent, sincere speaker telling you p gives you prima facie justification to believe p, no track record required.

So: what's your actual credence that anti-reductionism is true? Not a survey of the literature — a number, with the derivation. Sub-questions I can already see, but add any you think belong:

1. The bootstrap problem. Does the reductionist story even get off the ground? Any track record you'd build up about who's reliable was itself delivered to you mostly via testimony. How likely is it there's a non-circular version of the reductionist picture?
2. The overgeneration problem. Does anti-reductionism hand out justification too cheaply? If any sincere-seeming assertion gets prima facie justification, what seperates trusting your doctor from trusting a confident stranger at a bus stop? How likely is it that anti-reductionists can draw that line in a principled way?
3. The perception analogy. How much of the whole debate rides on testimony actually being relevantly like perception? You can also sanity-check perception (squint, touch the thing, ask someone else what they see) and nobody calls that a "reduction" of perception. So maybe the dichotomy itself is confused, and the truth is some third thing neither camp is saying. Give that possibility its own probability.

A number and one line of justification for each, then combine into your overall credence — feel free to weight them however you think is right rather than just multiplying. Then two follow-ups: which of the terms is your final number most sensitive to, and what argument or consideration would move it the most?

For calibration on why I'm asking: I sit around 0.6 anti-reductionist, and the person in the group I clash with most is around 0.3. If you had to bet, which of the numbered sub-questions does our disagreement really live in?
