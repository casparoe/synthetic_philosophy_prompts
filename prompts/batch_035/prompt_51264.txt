Something I keep noticing in "separate the art from the artist" arguments, and I want to know if the actual move has a name. When a musician or director turns out to have done something awful, people very quickly start re-reading the work through the vice — the lyrics were always creepy once you know, the paintings suddenly look sadistic, we just couldn't see it before. So the inference runs from the artist's biography into the work, and it runs retroactively, changing what people take the work to have been all along. But the channel seems to only go one way. Nobody reasons from the fact that a novel has a genuinely repellent narrator to the conclusion that the author shares those vices — if anything, portraying evil convincingly gets counted as a mark of moral imagination. Vice flows from the artist into the work, never out from the work to the artist, and I've never seen anyone flag this asymmetry as odd.

There's a second, related slippage: people use "immoral art" for two totally different situations. One is a work whose content is itself morally rotten (say, a poem whose whole point is a vicious slur) and the other is a perfectly fine work made by a rotten person — Caravaggio literally killed a man, but that doesn't make his canvases cruel in the way a sadistic poem is cruel. Yet in arguments both get lumped together, and I think the lumping is what lets the retroactive re-reading happen, because once the maker is bad, people go hunting for the badness in the work and then act like they've found an independent defect.

So: is there an established term for either (a) the one-way, retroactive transfer of vice from maker to work, or (b) the distinction between morally flawed works and morally flawed makers? If I remember right, the intentional fallacy blocks inferring the author's intended meaning from the poem, which feels like a cousin of the work-to-artist inference I'm claiming nobody makes — but it's not obviously the same thing, and ad hominem doesn't fit either since people aren't using the vice to dismiss an argument. I don't just want a label dropped on me, I want the carving: what the nearest concepts are, how each one misses, and if nothing fits, a coined term with a definition and a boundary case that shows where it stops working. Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion.
