been lurking (and occasionally posting badly) on rationalist-adjacent forums for over a decade now and the same fight keeps happening: someone says morality is relative to culture or framework, someone else says that can't be right because then you can't say slavery was wrong, first person says actually you can say it's wrong-for-us, thread reaches 400 comments, nobody changes their mind, repeat in eight months. I've watched this cycle maybe five or six times now and I've started to suspect the participants aren't even disagreeing about the same thing.

here's my half-formed working theory, offered as a theory and not a conclusion: the whole relativism vs. universalism fight is actually three distinct questions that keep getting run together in one word. (1) a descriptive/anthropological one about whether moral codes in fact vary, (2) a semantic one about what moral claims even mean or what makes them true, and (3) a first-person normative one about how I should hold my commitments given that I might have been born elsewhere. my guess is the "relativists" in these threads are mostly answering 3 in a way they find motivating, the realists are answering 2, and the 400 comments happen because each side thinks the other's answer is an answer to THEIR question. but I've also seen people argue the disagreement is genuinely substantive and can't be dissolved this way, so I genuinely don't know.

what I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict for team relativism or team realism. sketch what each side is actually doing when they make their moves — e.g. the standard self-refutation charge ("is 'all morality is relative' relative?"), the standard reply, the disagreement argument that cross-cultural dispute presupposes shared standards, the realist comeback that this proves too much — and tell me which of those moves is doing real work vs. shadowboxing. then tell me, given the diagnosis, what would even count as progress here: some sharpened distinction, some kind of thought experiment, a change in what question we ask? or is this just bedrock all the way down and the cycle is eternal?

one ask: at the end, say how confident you are in the diagnosis overall and explicitly flag the single point you're least sure about. I'd rather have an honest "here's where my diagnosis could be wrong" than a clean essay, and if you think my three-questions framing is itself part of the problem, say so and say why.
