I'm a PhD student working on well-being, putting together the commitments section of my prospectus, and I'd like to know whether the package below is jointly tenable before my committee picks it apart. I hold: (1) hedonism about well-being — how well a life goes for the person living it is fixed entirely by the balance of pleasure over pain; (2) the heterogeneity point as a claim about phenomenology — the pleasure of finishing a proof, the pleasure of a hot shower, and the pleasure of a ripe peach share no single felt quality, and no amount of introspection turns one into another; (3) the attitudinal analysis of pleasure is false — pleasure is not enjoyment of, or a pro-attitude toward, one's experience, since that analysis makes hedonism collapse into the desire-satisfaction theory of well-being I reject, and it would count a craved-but-hated experience as pleasant; (4) an anti-Mill position — when two pleasures differ in how good they are for you, that is always a difference in quantity (duration and intensity), never a difference in the kind or quality of their value; and (5) robust comparability — heterogeneous pleasures lie on a single cardinal scale, precise enough that a policymaker could in principle trade off an afternoon of concerts against a season of football matches on welfare grounds alone. Tell me which of these commitments clash, which only appear to, and what the cheapest repair to each genuine clash would cost; I honestly don't know which one I'd give up first. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning — no preamble and no restating of the list.
