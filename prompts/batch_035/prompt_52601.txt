My husband and I have been trying for a baby for about a year, and we just got hit with some annoying news: I need to start a medication for a chronic condition, and my doctor says I should be on solid contraception for six months first because the drug is strongly suspected of causing serious developmental problems in a fetus if you conceive while taking it. Fine, we can wait six months, that's not the end of the world. But the whole thing got me into a weird argument with my sister that I can't get out of my head.

She said it would be flat-out irresponsible of us to even consider conceiving during the waiting period, that we'd be knowingly bringing a child into the world who'd suffer, and that the child could rightly blame us later. Something about that felt off to me and I blurted out the following, and now I want to know if it actually holds up: if we conceived now, the resulting child would be a *different* child than the one we'd conceive six months from now — different egg, different sperm, a whole different person. So the kid born with problems isn't someone who "would otherwise have been healthy"; had we waited, that particular kid simply wouldn't exist at all. It's not like we'd be taking a healthy future kid and giving them a bad start. It seems like you can't wrong someone by bringing them into existence with a hard life, if the alternative for them is never existing? And yet my sister's intuition — that of course it would be wrong to roll those dice — also pulls at me, especially since "wait six months and then try" costs us so little.

So I guess my questions are: is my "different child, so no one is harmed" move actually sound, or is there a flaw in it somewhere? And if it is sound, does it really follow that conceiving during the window would be fine — or can something still be wrong with it even if no particular child is wronged? My sister and I would both appreciate a straight answer, though honestly I'm also curious how this plays out more generally, because I realize the same puzzle probably shows up in lots of decisions about when and under what conditions to have kids.
