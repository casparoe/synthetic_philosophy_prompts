So, background: I'm a retired physicist — semiconductors, mostly, forty years of it — and since I stopped going to the lab I've been catching up on philosophy like a man who ignored half the library his whole life. Just worked through Tolstoy's What Is Art? and honestly the guy is infuriating and brilliant at the same time. He throws basically all of western high culture under the bus and says real art is the sincere stuff that transmits a feeling from one person to another, that "infects" the audience, and that the best test is whether peasants understand it.

Here's my ask: I half-believe something but can't defend it properly, and I want you to defend it for me. The claim is that Tolstoy's own criteria, applied consistently and without special pleading, force him to count great scientific and mathematical writing as genuine art in his sense — arguably better art than most of the novels he condemns. Think of a really clean proof, or a physicist explaining a phenomenon so the audience suddenly *feels* the beauty of it. The emotion is sincere, it transmits to anyone regardless of class if you strip the jargon, and it's deeply individual to the person doing the explaining. Tolstoy obviously hated this conclusion (he had unkind things to say about science types), but that's the point.

So please make the argument properly: spell out which of his criteria do the work, where the weak points in my argument are (maybe he'd say transmitting the feeling of understanding isn't transmitting *his* kind of feeling?), and whether the position can survive those objections or has to retreat to something weaker. Dont be nice about it, I want it stress-tested. Also feel free to tell me if you think the whole thing rests on me misreading the infectiousness criterion — its been known to happen.
