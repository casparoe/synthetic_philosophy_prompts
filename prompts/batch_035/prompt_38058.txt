I'm a formal-methods engineer, and the vocabulary I actually think in comes from proof assistants and type theory: object languages and meta-languages, kernels and trust bases, definitional equality, tactics, reflection, proof terms checked by a small program. My best friend is a PhD student in epistemology, and last night, over a dinner that turned into a four-hour argument, she claimed that Lewis Carroll's 1895 dialogue "What the Tortoise Said to Achilles" exposes something my whole world quietly presupposes and never examines. I claimed, in return, that the regress the tortoise generates is a phenomenon we understand precisely and have engineered around deliberately, and that she was dressing up a solved engineering distinction as a mystery. Neither of us managed to state the other's point in the other's terms, which is why I'm writing to you.

Here's the task. Restate the tortoise's regress, and the broader debate it opened about whether deduction can be justified without vicious circularity, entirely in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. To seed the translation: treat Achilles' conditional as a proposition in the object logic; treat the tortoise's gambit — accept the new conditional as a premise, then demand yet another conditional to license applying it — as an attempt to promote an inference rule into an axiom; treat modus ponens as whatever it actually is in a proof assistant (kernel code? part of the trusted computing base? a definitional unfolding? decide and say); and treat the regress as what would happen if we tried to discharge a proof obligation by adding the discharging rule itself to the hypothesis stack. Then give me the residue report I care about most: which parts of the philosophical debate translate cleanly into this vocabulary, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression at all.

Two specific questions, since the argument came down to them. First: my friend's side has a standard reply to the tortoise, that inference rules are not premises and cannot be hoisted into the theory without regress — is that reply, once translated, just the observation that the kernel is not written in the object language, or is it making a further claim my vocabulary can't capture? Second: she insists that the interesting residue is the kernel itself, which my framework can express (we do talk about the trust base) but cannot justify — is that a genuine limit of my vocabulary, or does the worry dissolve once stated carefully? She says the residue shows her framework sees what mine cannot say; I suspect it shows her framework cannot say what mine says precisely. Tell me which of us the residue actually supports, and don't be diplomatic about it — we've agreed to let the translation settle the argument.
