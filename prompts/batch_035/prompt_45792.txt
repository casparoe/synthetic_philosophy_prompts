I sit on the technical standards board at an insurance analytics firm. Our flagship product lets claims adjusters type evidence into a claims system in plain language, and a back-end model converts it into probability updates on things like "this claim is fraudulent". There's a proposed new feature that would let adjusters enter conditional evidence — e.g. "if the fire started in the kitchen, the homeowner is four times more likely to be responsible than a faulty appliance was" — and have the system automatically turn that into an adjusted fraud score. Our lead Bayesian insists this is fine and the math is settled. But our other senior scientist keeps going on about the Judy Benjamin problem, something about how conditioning on a conditional statement isn't well-defined, and that depending on how you interpret the rule you can get different posteriors, some of them pretty absurd. The board has to decide next week whether to ship the feature as-is, ship it with mandatory disclaimers plus human review of the auto-updates, or block it. Draft the decision memo I'll circulate beforehand — clear recommendation and reasons the losing side can actually respond to, including a fair treatment of the lead Bayesian's position. Assume the readers are smart but have no background in philosophy of probability, so spell out the issue in plain terms without name-dropping papers.
