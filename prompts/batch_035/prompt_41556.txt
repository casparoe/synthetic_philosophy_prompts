Something I keep bumping into in the RL welfare debate and want a proper diagnosis of: the two sides seem to agree on basically all the technical facts and still end up miles apart. On one side you've got people who say if suffering is anything, it's a functional state -- something that carries negative valence, drives avoidance learning, reprioritizes behavior -- and negative reward in an RL agent does exactly that, so at some capability level you should take the "maybe it hurts" hypothesis seriously. On the other side you have people who say reward is just a number that the training process uses to shape the policy, it's not something the agent itself represents as good or bad, and comparing it to pain is like comparing a company's quarterly loss to grief. And the thing that makes me suspicious is that when they argue, they don't disagree about dopamine, about temporal difference learning, about whether the reward channel is even in the agent's observation -- they disagree about which of those facts is the relevant one.

So here's my working theory, tell me if it holds up: I think the disagreement is verbal in a sneaky way, because the two sides are using "the agent" to pick out different things. The reward-as-valence people are pointing at the trained system, the whole policy-plus-world-model that emerges, and asking whether there's something it's like to be that. The reward-as-chisel people are pointing at the learning algorithm from the engineer's point of view, where reward is an external input like the fitness function in evolution, and obviously evolution doesn't feel anything. Same system, two different "subjects," and neither side notices they've individuated the candidate sufferer differently. If that's right then the debate stalls because each side thinks the other is making an obvious category error, when actually they're just tracking different referents.

But I want you to push back if that diagnosis is too easy. A few things that complicate it for me. First, the functionalist side can't just be told "you picked the wrong subject," because their whole point is that function determines subjecthood -- if negative valence is realized in the network, then "who feels it" gets answered by the facts, not by how we chose to carve things up. Second, there's a disanalogy in the evolution comparison that people keep gesturing at but never spell out: evolution's "reward" isn't consumed by any token, it only ranks them, whereas an RL agent does integrate the reward signal into its next update, so maybe the chisel metaphor is doing more work than it should. Third, part of me thinks this is really a disguised epistemic question about how much weight to put on no-report evidence -- we've decided we can't ask the agent, so both sides retreat to their priors about when functional role is enough, and no argument moves anyone because the crux is evidential, not conceptual.

So: is the individuation-of-the-subject story right, or one of the other two, or some mix? Diagnose the structure of the disagreement using what the sides actually say, and tell me what would even count as progress here -- some new architecture, a better way of individuating the subject, an animal-cognition style behavioral test, or whether this is one of those cases where we should expect the standoff to last precisely because the question is less settled than either side admits. Not looking for a survey of who believes what, I want the diagnosis and an honest verdict on movability.
