I'm a political philosophy person who's spent twenty years not having to define anything, and now a comparative politics colleague has roped me into co-authoring the concept-formation section of her paper on democratic backsliding -- basically the question of whether "backsliding" picks out a real mechanism or is just the polite journal-article word for "people we dont like won the election." Before we write a single sentence about Hungary or Turkey or the US, I want the spec. What would an adequate account of democratic backsliding actually have to deliver -- and I mean adequate for the purposes the literature actually uses the concept for: deciding when the EU should open rule-of-law proceedings, when democracy indices code a country down, when observers say an election shouldn't be treated as fully free. Not adequate for the semiotics of it.

Some requirements I already suspect belong on the list, but I want each one pressure-tested, plus whatever I've missed:

1. It has to get the paradigms right (Hungary post-2010, Turkey, Venezuela) without swallowing everything a newly elected government does that the losers hate -- welfare cuts, immigration restriction, school reform. If the account classifies "governing conservatively" as backsliding, it's broken.

2. It has to be a concept of a trajectory, not a level -- two countries sitting at the same index score differ if one is stable and mediocre and the other is in free fall. But here's my worry: every actual measure is a level measure, and backsliding claims often ride on movements of a few tenths of a point that have to be inside coder noise. So the account either needs to tell us what trend-talk means operationally, or concede it can't be measured and explain why that's fine.

3. Symmetry: whatever marks court-reshaping as backsliding has to mark it when it's done by people we like, or done for ends we approve of. If refilling a court to lock in your majority is backsliding, then a hypothetical government that remakes the courts to entrench democracy itself (say, hard-to-repeal protections after a near-miss autocrat) has to come out the same way or the criterion is doing partisan work. Does the account have the resources to separate the mechanism from the direction?

4. The terminus problem: Orbán's Hungary has been described as "backsliding" for going on fifteen years. At some point it's just an autocracy -- a stable, elected-ish one. If "backslide" implies you could slide back, then entrenchment means the concept has a built-in expiry date, and nobody writes the expiry clause. What's the requirement here -- reversibility? a distinct phase after which the word should change?

5. The illiberal-but-genuinely-popular problem: suppose the median voter keeps endorsing the system. An account that says voters can legitimately consent away liberal protections risks making backsliding vanish exactly where it matters; an account that says they can't has smuggled in a substantive theory of what people aren't allowed to prefer. This feels like the Gallie "essentially contested concept" situation, but I don't want to just wave at that and go home.

So: write the full desiderata list -- the cases any adequate account must classify correctly, the distinctions it can't collapse (backsliding vs. mere ideological change vs. ordinary constitutional hardball vs. illiberal-but-stable), the results from the measurement world it can't contradict -- with the motivating case behind each requirement. Mark which are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps, because I'm sure some of mine are the latter. And flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- my own bet is that extensional fit on the contested cases (is the US one?) and symmetry/neutrality can't both be had, and if so that's a result about the concept, not a failure of the paper. Finish by telling me which single requirement does the most work excluding accounts people actually hold. You can spend a sentence each on how Nancy Bermeo's picture of gradual, legal state-led erosion and a bare elections-first minimalism do against the list, but keep it light -- the criteria are the part I need to get right before we can score anything. A table is fine but dont let it do the thinking for you.
