For anyone who doesn't know it: it's Dennett's case of a soft-drink machine that takes two quarters, and after people start feeding it slugs, it gets retrofitted with a coin tester that checks weight, diameter, edging etc. so it can discriminate genuine quarters from near-misses. Dennett's move is that once the machine is deployed in that new environment, its internal states get aboutness — they're about quarters, they misfire when a slug sneaks through, and this "real" intentionality is still derived from the designers, which he uses to destabilize Searle's original vs. derived distinction.

I suspect the order matters a lot for whether the group ends up thinking Dennett is making a modest point ("machines can kinda have beliefs") or a radical one ("original intentionality was never a coherent notion, Searle's brain states have it no more fundamentally than the vending machine does").
