Something my two friends argued about at dinner last weekend, and I honestly couldn't referee it, so I'm passing it on. My cousin spent the better part of a year grinding in a space MMO for a rare ship — I'm told there are only a handful on his server — and a few weeks ago a guildmate he'd flown with for month asked to borrow it for an event, then quietly moved it to his own account, blocked my cousin, and listed it for sale on a third-party site for real money. In-game support told him trades between players are final, nothing to recover. At dinner, Marcus said flatly that nothing was stolen: the ship is a row in a database, it still exists on the company's servers, my cousin can even go look at it; calling it theft is a metaphor, and the real content of the event is that a guy he trusted turned out to be a jerk. Lena thought that was way too quick. The exchange went like this. (1) Marcus: nothing physical changed hands, nobody is poorer in a way the world can audit, so no theft. (2) Lena: money in a bank account is also just a row in a database — if someone drained my cousin's account he'd have no trouble calling that theft, so "it's just data" can't be what does the work. (3) Marcus: the difference is legal — bank balances are claims the law recognizes, whereas the game's terms of service say you're licensing access and own nothing, and the company could delete the ship tomorrow; it's a lease on a server, not property. (4) Lena: but the exclusivity is real and enforced harder than most physical property — the server records exactly one holder and nobody else can use the ship, while car keys get copied and deeds get forged; and the "the company can revoke it" point proves too much anyway, since a landlord can end a lease and that doesn't make the apartment unreal while you're living in it. (5) Marcus: fine, but even granting the exclusivity, the harm is where the metaphor collapses — my cousin put no money in, the hundreds of hours were hours he was going to spend playing the game regardless, and playing is the whole point of playing; counting that as a loss is like counting a lost hand of cards as a loss. (6) Lena: that treats the hours as pure consumption, but he wasn't consuming them, he was producing something he wanted to have — people grind precisely in order to own the thing — and his grief over it is as real as grief gets; if the underlying argument is "only physical losses count," then say that out loud, but then be ready to say a burned diary is no loss either, since its value is also just what it meant to someone.

By the end they were mostly repeating themselves. Can you go through the six moves one at a time and say who's right at each step — where each person is at their strongest, where each overreaches — and tell me what, if anything, both of them are missing? I don't just want "it's complicated"; I want a verdict per move.
