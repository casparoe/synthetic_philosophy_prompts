Last night my friend Maya and I got into a huge argument that started over Maxwell's demon and ended somewhere in metaphysics, and now I want to sort out the wreckage. She's a physics grad student, I'm in CS. We were talking about the demon -- you know, the little guy who sorts fast and slow molecules and apparently breaks the second law -- and she kept repeating the line "information is physical" like it was a spell. Apparently it goes back to some IBM physicist in the sixties, and the idea is that the demon's real cost isn't in sorting the molecules but in the information it has to gather and then forget, so the books balance after all. Fine, I can buy that part. The problem is what she then used the slogan for. Because "information is physical," she now claimed: (a) that information is itself a physical quantity like energy or entropy, not an abstraction *about* physical systems; (b) that any computation anywhere must dissipate heat, no exceptions; and (c) that when I talk about "the same information" being in my hard drive and in my backup, I'm talking about something that doesn't really exist independent of its physical carrier -- so the idea of information as an abstract thing, the way I'd naturally describe it as a programmer, is somehow a mistake.

I pushed back by pointing out that the slogan could mean wildly different things at different strengths. Reading one: information always happens to be *instantiated* in some physical substrate, which is either trivially true or close to it. Reading two: manipulating information has unavoidable thermodynamic costs, which is where the demon story and Landauer's principle live. Reading three is her strong metaphysical claim, that information is a physical thing in its own right. My suspicion is that she proved the boring one and then acted as if she'd proven the radical one -- the whole conversation kept sliding between them, and by the end I couldn't tell which version was doing the work. She also got a bit smug about how "you software people" think computation is free, which honestly might've been about something else entirely.

So here's what I want: a menu I could actually have used in that argument. Lay out the distinct precise claims "information is physical" could stand for, ordered from weakest/trivial to strongest/most radical, and for each one say whether it's true, whether it's substantive or a platitude, and -- crucially -- which of the claims she made (a, b, and c above) it would actually support if true. I'd especially like it if you separated the thermodynamic readings from the metaphysical ones, since I think the demon and Landauer's principle at most get her something about costs, not about what information *is*, but I'm honestly not sure. And if one of the readings is straight-up false or in tension with how physicists elsewhere talk about information (they treat it as state, as correlation, as probability distributions -- pick your poison), flag that too. Also tell me which reading the person on the street is most likely to mean when they toss the phrase out, because if Maya meant the trivial one all along, I'll feel pretty dumb about how heated the last hour of that argument got.
