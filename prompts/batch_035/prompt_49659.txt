A friend of mine defends populist parties on the ground that only they respect the will of the people, and I set out to show him that the phrase can't do the work he wants. The argument I built now looks like it proves far too much, and I can't locate the bad step. (1) Democratic legitimacy rests on the will of the people: laws are legitimate because the people willed them, not merely because the people's agents were wise. (2) For the people to will anything, some procedure must register that will. (3) Every such procedure is fixed in advance by someone, who settles who counts as the people, which question is asked, which options are offered, and what threshold counts as deciding. (4) Whoever fixes these things shapes what the people can be recorded as willing: an agenda offering only A or B guarantees that the people will A or will B. (5) A will that is shaped by another is not the people's own; it is partly the agenda-setter's. (6) So no procedure ever registers the will of the people as such. (7) Therefore either no law is democratically legitimate, or democratic legitimacy does not rest on the people's will. My concern is that the same argument, run on individuals, would show that no one ever chooses freely, since every individual choice is also made from a menu someone else fixed — so something must be wrong. I suspect the trouble lies between (4) and (6), in the slide from "shaped" to "authored," but I can't make the distinction precise, and my friend answers that unilateral, unaccountable shaping simply is authorship; he also invokes Rousseau's distinction between the general will and the will of all to say procedures can track the people's real will better or worse without ever expressing it purely. Which premise should I give up, why did it look fine, and what does giving it up cost? I would most like to keep (1) and (3), and I want to know what exactly distinguishes the innocent forms of shaping — aggregation, deliberation, fair agenda-setting — from the kind that would make the output someone else's will.
