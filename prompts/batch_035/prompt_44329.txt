Something I keep running into when I read arguments about why some countries are rich and others aren't, and I can't figure out if it has a name. People compare countries (or regions, or historical city-states) as if each one were an independent test of the theory: this place had good institutions and got rich, that one didn't and stayed poor, so institutions must be the explanation. But countries aren't sealed off from each other. They trade, they conquer one another, they copy laws and technologies, and sometimes a single colonizer imposed basically the same setup on dozens of places at once. So the "tests" aren't independent — and it's worse than that, because the thing you're trying to measure (wealth, institutions) actually spread along the very channels connecting your cases. If Portugal extracts silver from Bolivia and Britain lifts textile techniques from India, then the histories of the "failed" cases are entangled with the histories of the "successful" ones, and the fact of entanglement itself is part of why some ended up rich and others poor. That seems like it should mess up the inference in a way that's not just ordinary noise, but I almost never see it addressed head-on when people do these big cross-country comparisons.

I vaguely remember that old-school comparative anthropologists had some complaint along these lines about comparing cultures statistically, but I don't know if it ever got a proper name, or whether whatever they called it actually carried over when economists and economic historians started doing the same kind of thing with countries. So my questions: is there an established term for this problem, both in whatever form the anthropologists had it and, if it's different, in the modern quantitative version? If the nearest established concepts don't quite capture it, can you coin a term and define it carefully? Either way I want the carving, not just a label: how is it different from the generic worry that your data points are correlated, from selection bias, and from the sweeping claim that you can never do causal inference on history at all because you can't rerun it? Where exactly do those neighboring ideas stop and this one begin — like, is a study of, say, ports vs. interior towns on one river affected by this in the same way a study of former colonies across continents is? Answer in American English please.
