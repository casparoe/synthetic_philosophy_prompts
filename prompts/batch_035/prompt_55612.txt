I'm a philosophy of mind grad student sitting in on a machine learning interpretability reading group, and a disagreement has crystallized there that I'd like a quantitative handle on. The proposition on the table: in current large language models, there exist individual features or directions in activation space that are genuine representations — states whose identity is fixed by their content (the code-review feature really is about code review), which can therefore misrepresent, and whose causal role in the model's computation is individuated by that content. The interpretability researchers treat this as nearly settled: they point to sparse-autoencoder features that seem to have single, nameable senses, to successful steering by adding a vector to the residual stream, and to interventions that succeed or fail depending on whether you edit the "right" feature. The philosophers in the room are split. One camp is happy with a deflationary reading on which being a representation just is playing a certain causal-computational role, so the question is close to trivial and the answer is yes; the other camp insists something more is needed — roughly, that the model's own downstream processes must consume the state in ways individuated by its content, not by ours — and doubts that any steering or ablation result speaks to that stronger condition at all.

Give me your credence in the proposition as stated, with the derivation. I'd expect the decomposition to include at least: (a) the probability that a deflationary, role-based account of representation is correct, so that no extra consumer-side condition is needed; (b) conditional on deflationism, the probability that the units interpretability actually works with — sparse-autoencoder features, steering directions, attention heads — are units of the model's own computation rather than artifacts of our analysis imposed on a messier underlying structure; (c) if deflationism is false, the probability that current models nonetheless satisfy the stronger consumer-side condition; and (d) whether "can misrepresent" even applies to the states of a system with no environment-relative norm of accuracy, or whether that requirement should be relaxed for artifacts like ours. Add any further terms you think belong. For each, give a number and one line of justification, then show how the numbers combine into the headline credence. Finally, tell me which single term the final number is most sensitive to, and what experiment or argument would move that term the most.
