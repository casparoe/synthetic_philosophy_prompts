Draft: "The Zero Point Problem"

Pearce's case usually gets attacked on three fronts: that suffering is somehow character-building, that predators matter too, that we'd be playing god. I find all of these weak and won't defend them. My worry is different and it's about the representational structure of valence itself.

1. The claim isn't just that we could delete suffering. It's that a motivational system running entirely on positive states — where every state is better than nothing, and differences between states are differences in how good they are — can carry all the information a mind needs. Pearce's answer to "wouldn't we become blissed-out vegetables" is that we'd be motivated by gradients of bliss: subtle differences in how good we feel would encode what matters, the way small differences in voltage encode sound in an analog signal.

2. My problem: an all-positive scale has no meaningful zero. On a bipolar scale, the sign of a state does real work — it separates "go" from "avoid" before you even look at magnitude. Once every state is appetitive, approach-vs-avoidance has to be read off magnitude alone, i.e., off slope. That's not impossible in principle — dimmer/brighter encodes information fine without darkness-as-negative — but motivation isn't just information transfer. Avoidance behavior has a functional signature: urgency, do-it-now-ness, override of competing drives. I doubt slope in bliss can fake that signature for a system that evolved, over hundreds of millions of years, to treat negative valence as the non-negotiable alarm channel.

3. Counterargument dana already gave me: engineering could just wire those urgency functions onto positive states directly — a "bright alarm" instead of a painful one. Sure, but then notice what happened: we reintroduced a functional asymmetry by hand. We kept everything the negative side was doing and just relabeled it positive. At that point the claim "suffering is abolished" starts sounding like a bookkeeping claim about phenomenology rather than a claim about the architecture of motivation. Which might be exactly right! But then the essay Pearce should write is "we will keep the alarm and change how it feels," and that's a much more modest and weirder claim than "gradients of bliss."

4. If I'm right that something like this zero-point worry is real, it doesn't follow that we shouldn't abolish suffering. It follows that abolishing it is a deeper redesign than the slogan suggests, and that some of the burden of proof lies on showing the redesigned minds still have anything like the values we started with.
