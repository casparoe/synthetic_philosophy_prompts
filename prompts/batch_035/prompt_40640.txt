My sister is getting married next month and I'm giving the toast. here's the thing -- her now-fiance spent like three years basically paralyzed about proposing, and he was pretty open about why: he'd gotten obsessed with the transformative experience stuff (L.A. Paul's idea) that you literally cannot know what being married is like before you're married, so how could anyone ever rationally choose it? Like the you who could evaluate the marriage doesn't exist yet, so the decision is made blind, that whole deal. My sister eventually wore him down, and the running joke in the family now is that he only proposed once he decided an unknowable outcome was the best he was ever going to get.

Half the guests are his old philosophy grad school friends who will know exactly what he was going on about for those three years, and the other half are relatives who think philosophy is a waste of a perfectly good brain. I want a toast where his whole epistemic crisis is the centerpiece -- ideally framing marrying anyway as the *answer* to the problem rather than a surrender to it, like maybe deciding to change without knowing who you'll become is what love is, not a failure of rationality -- but it absolutely cannot turn into a lecture. My grandma has to laugh at least once, and the groom is a good sport but nothing that makes him sound like a robot who can't feel things, more like a guy who overthinks for three years and then jumps. Maybe 4-5 minutes? Thanks!
