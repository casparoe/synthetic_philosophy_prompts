I am a retired condensed-matter physicist who has spent the last three years working through philosophy of language in the evenings, and I have reached a point where I would benefit from the judgment of someone who knows this literature more deeply than I do. Having absorbed the classical Fregean apparatus — sense versus reference, the compositional transmission of sense, and the puzzle cases involving the morning star and the evening star — I next worked through the Kripkean material that postdates Frege by half a century: rigid designation, the apparent necessity of identities like water being H2O, and the claim that such necessities are knowable only a posteriori. I am now reading about the two-dimensionalist response, in which an expression is assigned two intensions — one tracking how a speaker could take the world to be, one tracking how the actual world in fact is — precisely so that the Kripkean cases can be accommodated without surrendering the old idea that meaning is somehow epistemically constrained.

Here is my question, and I would ask you to answer it as a sustained piece of speculative intellectual history rather than a survey. Suppose Frege were resurrected, given a decade to absorb the intervening mathematics and logic as a quick study, and then confronted with the full two-dimensionalist program in something like its contemporary form. What would he make of it? In particular: (1) Would he recognize the distinction between an expression's epistemic and subjunctive profiles as a legitimate refinement of his own distinction between sense and reference, or would he regard the two-intension apparatus as a category error — as conflating what belongs to sense with facts about the psychology of the speakers who grasp it? (2) How would his commitment to sense as objective and shareable — the same sense for all competent speakers of a community — sit with the observation that speakers plainly disagree about, or are ignorant of, the actual-world profile of their natural-kind terms? (3) And would his long-standing aim of showing that arithmetic is analytic, pursued with the conceptual-rigorous standards of his own foundations program, leave him sympathetic to the contemporary uses of two-dimensional semantics in arguing for the epistemic scrutability of all truths, or suspicious of the thought that a modal apparatus could do epistemological work at all?

I confess my own tentative conclusion, which you should feel free to demolish: it seems to me that Frege's insistence that sense determines reference in a lawlike way already gestures at the kind of dependence the two-dimensionalists formalize, and that his reaction would be a guarded welcome, tempered by the worry that the epistemic intension is a psychological surrogate pretending to be a sense. I would like to know whether that reading survives contact with what you know of his actual commitments, particularly his late doctrines about thought and the third realm. Please be concrete: reason from specific Fregean doctrines and specific features of the two-dimensionalist machinery, and flag the points where you think honest speculation about a dead man's likely reaction simply runs out, so I can distinguish your evidential claims from your conjectures.
