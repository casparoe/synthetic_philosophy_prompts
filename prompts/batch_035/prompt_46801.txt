For some years now, entirely as a private pursuit, I have been trying to understand Gödel's incompleteness theorems. My formal education ended with school mathematics, so I rely on popular expositions, and there lies my problem: nearly everyone who invokes the theorems outside of pure logic seems to be using them as a licence for some larger conclusion, and I have gradually lost the ability to tell which of these uses are faithful and which are embellishment. Members of my small reading group, most of whom are in the same position as I am, keep asserting things about the theorems that sound momentous, and I would like an independent judgment before I embarrass myself by repeating them.

To make this concrete, I have written down six claims that I have actually come across, in books, in lectures online, and in conversation. They are these: first, that there are mathematical truths which can never be proved by any means whatsoever; second, that no consistent formal system can prove its own consistency; third, that Gödel showed mathematics is incomplete and therefore ultimately unreliable as a source of knowledge; fourth, that since arithmetic contains statements it cannot prove, whereas we can see the truth of those statements, the human mind must be capable of more than any machine; fifth, that the theorem applies to any sufficiently rich system of rules, so that even a legal code or a constitution must contain truths it cannot demonstrate; and sixth, that it follows from Gödel that there will always be questions science can never answer.

What I would like you to do is rank these six claims from most to least faithful to what the theorems actually establish, and assign each a score out of ten for accuracy, explaining in a few sentences why each lands where it does. I am especially interested in the claims that are almost right: where a claim holds only subject to some crucial qualification, such as one about the strength of the system or the meaning of provability, I would be grateful if you would say precisely what the qualification is and why it matters, since that is where I am most likely to mislead others. Please write your reply as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the manner of a considered letter; the ranking and scores can be conveyed within the flow of the paragraphs.
