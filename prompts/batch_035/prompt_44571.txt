I've been working through my notes on impact measures / low-impact AI and before I write this up as a blog post I want a consistency check, because I suspect I'm holding views that don't fit together. Here's roughly where I've landed:

1. You can define "avoid side effects" without a full specification of what we value. The trick is to penalize how much the agent's actions change its ability to satisfy a big, diverse pile of auxiliary goals -- the attainable-utility style approach. And crucially I think the auxiliary goals can be pretty arbitrary or even randomly chosen and the penalty still does its job.

2. That said, when we talk about "impact" I think what the auxiliary-goal penalty is really a *proxy* for is something agent-neutral: facts about how the actual state of the world diverges from how it would otherwise be. A meteor hitting a city is a massive impact, full stop, no agent or goal-set needed. Impact is out there in the world; the penalty term is just our estimate of it.

3. But the actual thing I lose sleep over is power-seeking -- agents accumulating resources, options, influence -- and I think at bottom impact is about the agent's *options*: what it can and can't do from here. The cancer-curing, utopia-building trajectories are low-risk even though they change everything, precisely because of what happens to the agent's position, not because of what the world looks like.

4. I also think that with the right choice of baseline -- something in the neighborhood of inaction rather than the initial state -- a strong impact penalty gets you corrigibility more or less for free. If the counterfactual is "the agent sits there doing nothing," then being shut down barely registers as impact, so there's no incentive to block the off switch.

5. Related: I think the baseline choice is basically an implementation detail. The interesting philosophical content of an impact measure lives in the penalty term -- *what* you measure change in (options, attainable utility, whatever) -- not in *what you compare it to*.

6. And separately: an ideal low-impact agent should still be able to cure cancer if we ask it to. Complying with the operator's request shouldn't count as impact; only the unrequested, unapproved extra stuff should. The whole point is "do what you were asked, don't break anything else."

So: does this hang together? My worry is that some of these quietly smuggle in a value specification through the back door, or that 2 and 3 are just incompatible and I haven't noticed. Go through the pairs, tell me which are in genuine tension and which only look like it, rank the tensions by severity, and say what the cheapest revision would be for each. I genuinely don't know which one I'd give up, so don't pull punches.
