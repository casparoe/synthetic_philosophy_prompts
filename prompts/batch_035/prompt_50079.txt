hey, so I'm a PhD student in phil of language and I run a study group for first-years who've done intro semantics but haven't actually read the externalism literature. I want to make a "which theory of reference do you actually hold?" quiz for them — the point being that most of them will say "externalist, obviously" without realizing they're internalists about half the cases, or that their answers commit them to positions they'd hate.

So: 8ish questions, each one a concrete case with 2-3 answer options, phrased so you can answer without knowing any of the position labels. Then a scoring key at the end mapping answer patterns onto: internalist descriptivism (Frege-ish), Putnam-style causal/externalism for natural kind terms, Burge-style social externalism, two-dimensionalism (narrow content as a primary intension, the a posteriori necessity move), and the deflationary "there may be no fact of the matter here" option. I'd rather the quiz sort them by what they say about cases than by which label they like, so no question should just ask "do you think meaning is in the head?" — Putnam already did that one to death.

Cases the quiz needs to cover (I'm fixing the angle here because this is where the positions really come apart):

1. Twin Earth, but don't just re-ask "is water H2O" — ask it about the *inhabitants*: a lifelong Twin Earther who is molecule-for-molecule like Oscar, does her word "water" mean water, and what did she *think* she meant?
2. A deference case, but NOT arthritis, it's too recognizable — invent a fresh one (someone using a technical-ish term slightly wrong while everyone around them uses it right, the question is whether their thoughts get the right content anyway).
3. Slow switching: Oscar moves to Twin Earth, never notices anything, ten years later he says "water is good for you." What is that thought about?
4. A failed-reference term like phlogiston, where the question is whether the term is empty or refers to combustion anyway.
5. This one's my advisor's hobby horse so it has to be in there: Twin Earth where the watery stuff turns out to be a stable emulsion of two distinct liquids — behaves like water in every ordinary respect, and their chemists discover it's a mixture but can't agree whether that makes "water" there empty, disjunctive, or still a real kind. I want a question where the options are "microstructure essentialism all the way down" vs "the superficial/explanatory role can make something a kind on its own" vs "no fact of the matter until the community's chemists settle it."
6. Something for the causal-chain vs. description split, e.g. a community still using a term whose reference was fixed by experts who are all long dead, while everyone alive has wildly false beliefs about the kind.

Also two things beyond the questions themselves:

- In the scoring key, flag any pair of leaves the quiz genuinely can't seperate (I suspect social externalism and causal externalism will blur on any natural-kind question, since the "expert's usage" is doing the causal work either way) rather than pretending it's clean.
- Note any answer patterns that come out unoccupied — combinations nobody actually holds — and say one sentence on whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

No reading lists, no citations, and don't quote anyone verbatim — the first-years can chase the papers later, I just want the sorting instrument. Keep each question under ~80 words so it works as a shared-notes handout. Thanks!
