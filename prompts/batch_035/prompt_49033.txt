"Brain death is death because the brain is the body's integrator. The whole-brain criterion says that when all functions of the brain, including consciousness, are lost, the body stops being a single unified organism. So although the patient looks alive — beating heart, warm skin — the patient is dead because integration is gone; wounds do not heal and infections are not fought in brain-dead bodies. The UDDA endorses this higher-brain standard: loss of consciousness is the key marker, because consciousness is what makes us persons and the person is what dies. The critic is wrong that brain death was invented for transplantation, though. The Harvard committee framed its work around the problem of ventilators keeping bodies alive indefinitely, and the dead donor rule independently requires consent before any organs are taken, so the transplant motive is checked. As for DCD, it uses the same standard of death. After the ventilator is withdrawn, doctors wait a few minutes after the heart stops, and since autoresuscitation after that window is essentially unheard of, the loss of circulation is irreversible, just as brain function loss is irreversible in brain death. Both criteria therefore converge on the same thing — the irreversible loss of the capacity for integration — and the DCD patient is dead in exactly the same sense as the brain-dead patient."
