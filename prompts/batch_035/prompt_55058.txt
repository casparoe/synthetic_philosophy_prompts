I keep going back and forth on whether beauty (or at least artistic merit) is objective, so I figured I'd just lay out my gut verdicts on cases and see if there's a principle behind them. Here's where I land: someone who says the Twilight books are greater novels than Middlemarch is *wrong*, not just different from me. Someone who's never read a poem longer than a limerick announcing that all poetry is boring isn't wrong so much as not entitled to a verdict yet. A seasoned critic who has read everything, loves lots of poetry, and still finds Milton dry -- I can't bring myself to call that person wrong, though I suspect Hume would. Sancho Panza's kinsmen who tasted leather and iron in the wine everyone else praised turned out to be right, and everyone else's palate just wasn't up to it, so popularity among drinkers isn't the standard. A person whose aesthetic tradition prizes imperfection and patina finding classical Greek statues too slick and lifeless -- not wrong. And my preferring a painting tipsy that I find merely fine when sober -- the tipsy verdict just doesn't count.

The obvious candidate principle is Hume's recipe of the true judge: practice, comparison, freedom from prejudice, good sense. But it seems to either (a) make the Milton critic wrong, since the joint verdict of qualified judges is itself the standard, or (b) deny that and end up with majority vote among the qualified as the standard -- which is exactly what the kinsmen case seemed to rule out. And I genuinely can't tell whether the cross-cultural case is supposed to come out as two standards, no standard, or one standard both sides only partially grasp.

So: is there one principle that captures all six verdicts? State it precisely if so, tell me which of my verdicts it has to sacrifice (I'd rather overturn a verdict than accept a gerrymandered principle, but you tell me which one is load-bearing), and then tell me what the principle predicts about a case I haven't listed -- say, a horror superfan who has seen everything ever made in the genre and sincerely declares some objectively shoddy slasher film a masterpiece. Keep your whole answer under 450 words.
