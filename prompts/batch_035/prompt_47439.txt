Please give an example of a pair of conditionals — same antecedent, same consequent, one indicative and one counterfactual — in which the counterfactual is true but the indicative is false. This is the hard direction: the famous stock examples mostly run the other way (indicative true, counterfactual false, as in the Kennedy assassination pair), and the reverse pattern is notoriously difficult to produce. Construct an original example of your own rather than borrowing one from the literature, explain precisely why the pair has the truth values you claim, and then say what your example reveals about the difference between the two moods — in particular, whether it fits better with a genuinely semantic distinction between indicative and counterfactual conditionals or with a pragmatic account on which the difference is one of presupposed context rather than truth-conditional content. If you think the reverse direction is impossible to satisfy, say so and defend that claim with a principled argument. Please answer in American English.
