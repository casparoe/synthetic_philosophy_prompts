My cousin's whole defense was that she's been dead over a hundred years, nobody's being harmed, the dead can't be harmed, it's not like we're hurting a person. And I kept sputtering that it's still wrong somehow but I couldn't get any traction on *why* it's wrong, and he kept winning on what seems like a technically solid point.

The thing is, when I cooled off I realized "the dead can't be harmed" is one of those one-liners that sounds like a single claim but is probably smuggling several. It has an Epicurean flavor (no subject, no harm), but it also gets used by people who absolutely agree you can harm the living by spreading false rumors about them, so it can't just be "false claims about a person don't matter." And there's something weird about the asymmetry too — the same people who say the dead can't be harmed are fine saying a biopic "restores her legacy" or "does justice to her memory," which sounds a lot like posthumous *benefit*, so what's the story there? Lay it out as a menu I could actually bring back to him, because right now I'm just gesturing.
