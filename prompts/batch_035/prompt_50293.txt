i'm a cog sci phd student working on human-robot teaming, and i've been reading the joint action literature on and off for a year now (searle, bratman, gilbert, tuomela, some tomasello) and honestly the field feels like it's arguing over cases without ever saying what the theory is even FOR. before I read any more of it I want the actual spec: what would an adequate theory of joint action / shared intention have to get right? write me the desiderata, with the motivating case behind each one. but don't just list options — I want definite calls on which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just one camp's taste. some things I specifically want you to take a stance on:

- implementability: does the theory have to state its conditions precisely enough that I could, in principle, program them? my stake here is that I need to know when my robot teammate is a participant in a joint action and when it's a fancy tool, and talk of interlocking intentions and meshing subplans doesn't compile. if the honest answer is that the theory is only meant to describe adult humans, say so and say exactly what breaks when you try to port it.
- the theory has to distinguish genuine joint action from mere coincidence (two strangers coincidentally painting the same house) and from strategic interdependence without shared intention (drivers in rush hour traffic). which of these case-pairs actually carve the distinction at its joints and which trade on irrelevant details?
- scale: does an adequate account have to cover temporally stretched, institution-mediated stuff — a firm shipping a product, generations of builders on one cathedral — or is it fine for the theory to cover only small-scale real-time interaction and declare the big stuff a different phenomenon? this is where I suspect people smuggle in their reductionist or anti-reductionist priors, so be explicit about yours.
- asymmetric and degraded cases: joint attention in preverbal infants, cooperation under coercion, one party mistaken about what the other is up to, one party only pretending to cooperate. does the theory owe us a unified verdict on these, or can it legitimately treat them as borderline by design? commit.
- is irreducibility of the collective attitude (the whole "we-mode" thing) a genuine requirement or a metaphysical preference? I want an actual answer, not both-sides.
