hey, could you write me a parody end-user license agreement? The product: TRUTH™, the truth predicate that comes pre-installed in every natural language, which the IP holder has suddenly decided needs a EULA. About 700 words, formatted as numbered clauses with a short corporate-voice preamble, in the deadpan legalese of actual software licenses — never breaking register.

The conceit is that the fine print quietly implements deflationism, and all the jokes have to come from getting the view right, not caricaturing it. So the content I want baked into the clauses:

- The core service is disquotation and nothing else. You may move, free of charge and unlimted times, between "snow is white" and "it is true that snow is white," in either direction. A clause should explicitly disclaim that this move involves any analysis of what truth consists in — the company doesn't know and, per clause 3, doesn't have to say.
- The one premium feature: generalization. The predicate exists so you can endorse "everything the auditor flagged is true" without conjoining a possibly infinite list of sentences one by one. The license should admit, somewhere in the middle, that this is the entire reason the product was ever developed.
- Liability: the company certifies nothing about the world. It is not responsible for whether your beliefs correspond to reality — correspondence is described as a third-party add-on, "sold separately, vendor unknown, not our problem."
- The Liar: self-referential input causes the service to return TRUE and FALSE simultaneously and hang. The company accepts no responsibilty and recommends, in a footnote, that customers refrain from applying the predicate within the language it's a predicate of. (Customers will ignore this footnote; that can be a clause too.)
- Pricing: $0.00 in perpetuity, framed as a generous promotional offer, though the clause should make clear the price reflects the amount of substantive content the product contains.
- A "features deprecated in v2.0" section: correspondence-to-facts, coherence-with-the-system, utility-in-the-long-run — all legacy modules, officially unsupported. The company notes these modules appear to have been doing work the predicate never did, which the company declines to comment on.
- One complaint letter appended at the end, from an invented correspondence theorist (don't name anyone real), grumbling that the product can't say what makes beliefs true or why truth matters — followed by the customer-service reply, which simply disquotes her complaint back at her and marks the ticket Resolved.

Don't wink at the reader — the company is completely sincere, and never lets on that anything might be missing from its product. It should be funny to someone who's read the Horwich-style minimalism or the old Ramsey redundancy stuff, but a reader who's never heard of deflationism should still come away with an accurate picture of the view: truth as a device of disquotation and infinite generalization, not a deep metaphysical property. And by the last clause, a reader should be left feeling the genuine question deflationism raises — whether the company has told us what truth is, or just made it very cheap.
