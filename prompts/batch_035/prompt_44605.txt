I'm on the dining services advisory committee at my school (it's mostly a rubber stamp thing but this year they actually gave us a real decision) and I need to write the recommendation memo before our meeting next week. The situation: our university signed some climate pledge, and dining wants to cut beef offerings by like 80% in the dining halls and replace a lot of it with chicken, plus expand the plant-based options. Straightforward sustainability move, right? Except one guy on the committee -- he's an ethics grad student and honestly kind of a lot -- argued at the last meeting that this is a disaster from an animal suffering perspective and we'd be voting for way MORE suffering, not less.

His argument, as best as I can reconstruct it: one cow produces a huge number of meals, while one chicken is basically one or two meals, so swapping beef for chicken multiplies the number of animals killed per meal by a ton. He also said chickens have it way worse on factory farms than cattle do, per animal. And then it got even weirder -- he said even the plant-based replacement isn't clean, because crop cultivation kills tons of animals too (pesticides, tilling, harvesting), including like insects and field mice, and if you count invertebrate suffering at all, a bowl of beans might carry a big suffering toll too. He mentioned someone's estimate that the number of animals directly killed by farming, especially if you count the small ones, could rival or exceed farmed animal deaths, though honestly he lost me there a bit and other committee members thought he was trolling.

Meanwhile the sustainability office people are pushing hard for the chicken swap because of emissions (cows are a methane disaster, everyone agrees on that), and they've got a student petition with like 900 signatures behind them. A few committee members just want to vote yes Thursday and go home.

So I need to write the memo that actually gets circulated at the meeting. It should make a concrete recommendation -- approve the swap, reject it, or approve with some modification (I honestly don't know which is right, that's kind of why I'm asking) -- and it has to do real math-ish reasoning about deaths per meal and suffering per animal, not just vibes. It also has to deal fairly with the grad student's argument instead of dismissing it as philosophical nerd stuff, but ALSO can't let the whole recommendation turn on heroic assumptions about insect suffering that half the committee won't buy. Keep it maybe 500-700 words, and remember these are students and dining administrators reading it, so no jargon without a plain-language gloss.

Also if it matters for how you weigh things: I eat meat, I'm in an environmental ethics course this semester which is honestly the only reason I know any of the philosophy words for this stuff, and the committee chair said she wants something we can actually vote on line by line.
