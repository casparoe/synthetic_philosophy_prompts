ground rules for the dialogue: both characters are good philosophers, no strawmen, and they have to actually engage with each other — no trading monologues that could have been delivered in any order. ideally at least one place where a character's response forces the other to retract or refine something they said earlier.

here's the thing im tired of: the stock exchange, where the perdurantist trots out temporary intrinsics, the endurantist mumbles adverbialism, and everyone goes home. so have them get through that early — the bent vs. straight version, the thought that shape is intrinsic but nothing can be both bent and straight simpliciter, the endurantist's options (understand "x is bent at t" as x standing in a relation, or as the having-of-bentness happening in an at-t way), and the perdurantist's comeback that these read like notational dodges. but then have them notice something real: they don't actually disagree about the truth of any sentence like "the wire is bent at noon" — the fight is over what makes it true. once they see that, move them onto the case i actually want them to fight about:

the blinker. suppose we find an exotic physical system that exists at t1, is completely annihilated at t2, and at t3 an exact duplicate of it — same matter, same arrangement — pops into being in the same place. did one thing persist, or did one thing die and a numerically different thing get born? the perdurantist wants to say it's a single four-dimensional object whose career just has a hole in it — there's no temporal part at t2 and there's no rule that temporal parts must be contiguous, only that they're hooked up the right way. the endurantist is in trouble in an interesting way: to say it persisted she seems to need the object to exist at t2, which is false by hypothesis, and to say it didn't persist she needs a story about why ordinary objects that don't blink count as persisting that doesn't just boil down to "no gaps happened." i made this case up mostly to annoy my advisor, so push on it however you want, including having a character attack the case itself (is it even coherent? is re-creation-with-same-matter enough like ordinary persistence that we should expect a theory to handle it?) — but someone should try each of the moves above and someone should push back hard.
