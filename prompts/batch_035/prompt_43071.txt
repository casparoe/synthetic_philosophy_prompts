I write novels for a living and somehow let myself get talked onto the jury for a mid-sized literary prize. Five of us, and we're down to three books before the final deliberations. One of our jurors, M., had to drop out for health reasons, and she's now telling us from her sickbed that we absolutely must not give the prize to one of the three — she says it's genuinely bad, not just not-the-best — and that another one is the clear winner, apparently by a mile. She's too unwell to write up any real notes, just these flat verdicts over text.

Here's where we've split. One juror wants to weight M.'s verdicts heavily, since she's the best reader any of us know and her track record has been vindicated over twenty years of prize juries and reviews — basically, if she says a book is bad, that's better evidence than most of us reading it ourselves. A third juror thinks that's completely backwards: judging a book isn't like reporting a temperature reading you took, it's like forming the response itself, so a juror who hasn't had the response can't really contribute a judgment at all, and borrowing hers makes our decision worse, not better. The fourth juror is agnostic but points out we're on a deadline.

Draft the short memo I can circulate before the final meeting saying what we should do about M.'s input — treat it as strong evidence, discount it, something in between like one of us speed-reading the disputed book before deciding — with reasoning the others could actually engage with rather than just restating positions. I want it to take seriously the idea that a jury verdict built partly on borrowed judgment might be a different and maybe shakier thing than one built on our own reading, even granting that M. is usually right, and also to take seriously that deadlines and limited readers are just real constraints, not excuses.

Since these people argue for sport: put a quick note at the end saying how confident you are in the recommendation and which single step in the reasoning you're least sure of — I'd rather get that flag than have the memo paper over it. And forgive any typos, I'm writing this from a hotel bar the night before.
