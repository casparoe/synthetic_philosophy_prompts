My roommate thinks the word "know" just tracks how confident you are, and I keep insisting it's messier than that, so I've been going through cases and just going with my gut, trying to see what pattern falls out. Here are my verdicts. I walk past a donut place every morning and it's always open by 7; if a coworker asks whether I know it'll be open when we swing by Saturday, I say yes, I know it will. Same donut place, same evidence, except now I'm picking up my mom's birthday cake there Saturday morning — it's the only bakery in town that does gluten-free, the party is that day or never — and suddenly I don't know, I feel like I have to call ahead, even though literally nothing about my evidence changed. But then the surgery case bugs me: if someone asks whether the anesthesiologist knows what dose is safe, the stakes could not be higher, and I actually have less direct evidence than in the donut case (I've never watched her work, I'm going on her being credentialed at this hospital), yet I'd say without blinking that she knows. And in the other direction, I've got a lottery ticket for tonight's drawing with astronomical odds against me, nothing important riding on it at all, and I still won't say I know I lost until I actually check the numbers.

There's also this thing where it seems to matter who's asking and how. My sister asked if I knew when our grandma's flight lands, I said yeah I know, I checked the itinerary twice. Then she goes "but do you KNOW know — if you're wrong she's standing at the airport alone for two hours," and honestly? in that moment I retreated to "pretty sure." In the space of ten seconds, no new evidence showed up, nothing about the stakes changed in any real way, just the tone of the question, and my willingness to say "know" shifted.

So is there a principle that gets all of this — stakes, error possibilities, conversational context, whatever else — or am I just being inconsistent? Ideally it would explain why high stakes blocks knowledge in the cake case but not the surgery one, and why low stakes doesn't guarantee knowledge in the lottery one. And whatever principle you land on, tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't thought through: I'm about to be questioned in small claims court (my word against a contractor's) about whether I knew he'd agreed to finish the work by the 1st.
