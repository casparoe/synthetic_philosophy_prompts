I'm in a reading group that just worked through the Ant Fugue dialogue from Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach (the one with the Anteater and his friend Aunt Hillary, the ant colony who's supposed to be smart even though her individual ants are dumb as rocks), and I want to make a decision-tree handout that sorts people by where they actually land on whether a colony can have a mind. The tree should start from the starting point everyone in the group accepts: ant colonies do flexible, adaptive stuff (reassigning workers, choosing between food sources, defending the nest) that's naturally described at the colony level, not the ant level. From there each branch node should be a question someone can answer without knowing where it leads. I'd want nodes at least touching on: whether there's a fact of the matter about minds or whether ascribing one is just a predictive tool; whether having a mind requires being a biological individual (i.e., does the superorganism view get to say the colony is one organism and therefore nothing spooky is happening?) or only the right kind of organization; whether a colony could count as a mind if it never learns anything within its own lifetime, with all the cleverness coming from the species' evolutionary history; whether it matters that the "substrate" here is made of animals that may have their own little minds, so whether levels of mind stack or exclude each other; and whether the sheer slowness of colony-level processes counts against it. Leaves should include Hofstadter's own level-jumping emergentism, a Dennett-style intentional stance instrumentalism, the superorganism/biological individuality position, plain functionalist multiple realizability, and a deflationary "it's just evolved coordination, no mind anywhere up there" view. Please note any leaf pairs the tree can't really seperate (I suspect Hofstadter's view and generic functionalism will be hard to pull apart), and point out combos of answers that no named position occupies, with a sentence on why nobody's there. Keep it usable as a one-page handout, maybe 8-10 nodes total.
