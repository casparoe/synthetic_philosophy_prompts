My flatmate finished a PhD on top-down effects in perception and has appointed herself arbiter of a running dispute: whenever a case comes up, she issues a ruling on whether it counts as genuine cognitive penetration — cognition reaching into and shaping perceptual processing itself, as opposed to merely biasing attention, memory, or later judgment. I have started keeping a record of her verdicts:

1. Knowing that the two lines in the Müller-Lyer figure are equal does nothing to the illusion: not penetration. Belief never gets near the lines.
2. A synthesized syllable acoustically halfway between "dent" and "tent," placed where only one of the two could begin a real English word, is heard decisively as whichever makes a word: penetration — "the dictionary reaches into the categorization."
3. Sine-wave speech, a sentence replaced by slow electronic whistles, sounds like chirps until you are told it is a distorted sentence, whereupon it snaps into speech and cannot be heard as chirps again: penetration.
4. The same wine labeled expensive is rated as tasting better, but tasters' ability to discriminate its actual flavors is unchanged: not penetration — "the label moves the verdict, not the tongue."
5. Subjects wearing a heavy backpack, or simply fatigued, overestimate the slant of a hill; but when asked to indicate the slant with a tilting disc rather than with a number, the effect largely evaporates: not penetration — "that is the body casting a vote on an estimate, not the eyes."
6. After a decade of reading films, a radiologist sees a hairline fracture that is pure noise to a novice, because "it just looks like a fracture": penetration — "expertise gets compiled into the look."
7. Searching a crowd for your friend in the red jacket: you find him faster, but nothing in the scene looks different; the crowd does not rearrange itself: not penetration — "selection is not construction."

Her lab is now testing a case on which she will have to rule, and I would like to predict the ruling before she delivers it. Subjects complete a hard workout and, still thirsty, view a water bottle across the room. Compared with rested controls, they report that it looks closer, and the effect persists when they are asked, blindfolded, to walk to the spot where the bottle had stood — a measure she tends to trust over any verbal report.

Please tell me: (a) the rules that could fit her entire record so far — I suspect more than one does, so give me the serious candidates, noting for each which verdicts it explains comfortably and which it strains to explain; (b) what each surviving rule predicts for the new case; (c) your best guess at her ruling, with your reasons for preferring it; and (d) in case several rules agree on this one, a further case she has not yet ruled on — one you would put to her — that would best divide them.
