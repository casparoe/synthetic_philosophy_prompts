i write novels for a living, and last week something strange happend to me while working on my current book. I was writing the scene where the main character dies — a woman I invented entirely, every detail of her life is fake because I made it up myself — and I caught myself actually crying at my desk. Real tears, skipped dinner, the works. And here's the thing: I'm the one who decided she dies! I could open the document tomorrow and let her survive instead, nobody could stop me. Yet I sat there grieving her like a real person I'd lost. My partner thinks I'm being silly and says if it upsets me that much I should just change the plot, and honestly I can't quite say why that feels beside the point. Is my reaction irrational? Something tells me there's a real question lurking here about whether you can genuinely feel emotions for someone you know full well doesn't exist — and maybe it's even weirder in my case, since I don't just know she's fictional, I'm actively making her up as I go. Curious what you make of it.
