What is your credence that accepting Bostrom's simulation argument should substantially reduce the expected value of orienting one's efforts toward the far future — enough to matter, say, by a factor of two or more — rather than leaving it roughly unchanged or raising it? By "orienting toward the far future" I mean the reasoning, often called fanatical by its critics, that because the future might contain vastly more moral patients than the present contains, influencing it dominates near-term alternatives in expectation. I want a number plus the decomposition, and I'd like you to consider — adding to, revising, or discarding as you see fit — the following terms: the probability that we are in an ancestor-simulation; conditional on that, the probability that the simulation runs long enough to contain an astronomical number of moral patients, rather than being truncated near our own technological level, on the thought that simulators may terminate runs precisely when those runs would begin spawning astronomical futures of their own; how much morally significant experiences inside a simulation should count, with reasoning rather than bare intuition, noting one asymmetry — if simulated experiences count fully, near-term work to help present beings is undiminished by our being simulated, while far-future work may not be; conditional on not being simulated, the probability that we ourselves reach an astronomical future; and whether actions inside a simulation can affect the base level at all. Then tell me which term drives your final number, what argument or observation would move it most, and whether the answer turns on which disjunct of Bostrom's trilemma one accepts. Finally, assess this proposed asymmetry: even a modest probability of living in a truncated simulation scales down the astronomical value typically assigned to the far future, so the simulation argument should dampen far-future fanaticism far more than it dampens any view that assigns the future merely ordinary importance. Is that asymmetry sound, or does it conflate how much value the future contains with how much we can influence it?
