I keep getting into it with my brother about torture and the ticking time bomb, and I realize I've never actually written down my own views, so here goes. My gut calls on cases:

Known nuke under the city, known guy who planted it, four hours to go, he's not talking: fine. Do it, and honestly I think I'd do it myself.

Same setup but we're only like 70% sure he's the guy: not fine anymore.

A police sniper taking a shot at a hostage taker when she's only 70% confident she can hit him without hitting the hostage: fine. This one bugs me because I can't see why 70% is enough when the trigger gets a lethal outcome but not when it's torture.

The state doing it completely off the books — no protocol, no legal cover, guy just grabs the pliers and accepts he might be prosecuted after, betting on a pardon: fine. Actually kind of admirable. The state passing a statute that authorizes torture in ticking bomb cases in advance, with safeguards: makes my skin crawl, and I'm pretty sure not fine, even though the statute is the one that would get due process right. But I'm also fine with the state having a standing policy for when snipers may shoot. So my rule can't just be "the state may never plan violence in advance."

Torturing him after the bomb is found and defused, as punishment for planting it: obviously not fine, even though he deserves it more than anyone. So it's not about what he deserves.

The one I keep flip-flopping on: the bomb is under a stadium, and the guy won't talk, but his wife — completely innocent, knew nothing about the plot — might know the disarm code without realizing what she knows. Torturing him feels permissible. Torturing her feels monstrous. And I cannot give a single good reason why, given that she's the only path to saving eighty thousand people, and he's already told us he won't break.

So: is there one principle that gets all or most of these verdicts, or am I just going to have to give one up? I'd want the principle stated precisely enough that it decides the wife case, and I'd want you to be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save — I suspect the sniper one and the statute one are where something has to break, but maybe not the ones I think. Keep it under 500 words and in american english please, and lead with the principle itself in a sentence or two before anything else.
