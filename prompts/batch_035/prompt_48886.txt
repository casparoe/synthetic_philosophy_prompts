I'm taking a philosophy of science course and writing my term paper on whether falsificationism survives contact with an actual physics controversy, and I've picked the 2011 OPERA neutrino episode because it's short, famous, and I actually remember the details. In case you don't: in September 2011 the OPERA collaboration at Gran Sasso reported that neutrinos beamed from CERN arrived about 60 nanoseconds earlier than light would have, something like 25 parts per million fast. Famously, they didn't claim a discovery -- they more or less said "our instruments say this, we can't find the error, please check us," other groups got ready to replicate, and by 2012 the anomaly was traced to mundane hardware problems (a loose fiber-optic cable and a clock oscillator issue) and the result was retracted. No physicist that I know of even briefly entertained abandoning special relativity. Those are the facts I want to work with; don't worry about anything more fine-grained than that.

Here's the paper I want you to outline, and please think carefully before commiting to a reading.
