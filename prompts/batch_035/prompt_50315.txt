I'm in a philosophy of physics reading group, and for next session we've each been asked to submit a short statement of our actual positions on the measurement problem so the group can interrogate them. I've drafted mine on Everett, and before I hand it over I want an outside check on whether what I've written is internally coherent. Here's where I stand. (1) I accept Everettian quantum mechanics: the universal wavefunction evolves unitarily, there is no collapse, and "outcomes" are just relative states of decohering branches. (2) I think the right way to understand probability in this picture is self-locating uncertainty — after a measurement with multiple outcomes, each future successor of mine should be unsure which branch they occupy, and that uncertainty is what quantum probabilities are. (3) I also endorse the decision-theoretic program: I take the Deutsch–Wallace line that a rational agent who cares equally about all their future successors must weight branches by squared amplitude, and that this suffices to recover the Born rule as a theorem of rationality rather than an extra postulate. (4) Separately, I hold a typicality principle: a rational agent should expect their experiences to be typical of the distribution of observer-moments, so if the overwhelming majority of my future selves see relative frequencies close to the Born weights, that's what I should anticipate seeing. (5) Finally, I reject branch-counting as a measure of anything, on the grounds that the number of branches is not a well-defined quantity — branching is a matter of decoherence and of how finely we choose to carve the wavefunction, so any rule that depends on counting branches is built on a convention.

These all seem individually defensible to me — they're each positions I've seen serious Everettians hold — but I've never laid them side by side before, and a couple of the group members are the type to pounce on any seam. So: audit the set. Tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which ones only look to be in tension but come apart on inspection, and for each real tension how severe it is and what would have to give. Then, if you had to recommend a single revision that cleans up the most while costing me the least, which would it be? Please be specific about which commitment is doing the damage in each case rather than giving me a generic list of "open problems in Everettian probability."
