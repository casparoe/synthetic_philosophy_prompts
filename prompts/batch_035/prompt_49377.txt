I'm on the council of a housing cooperative — about 400 units, plus a shared grocery store, a bike repair workshop, some guest rooms, and a decent sized communal garden. Ever since I took over the "resource allocation" seat we've been stuck in the same fight basically every month: some things we run through internal prices (guest rooms, groceries) and other things we decide by vote in the general assembly (garden plots, workshop budget, who gets the big storage lockers), and nobody can articulate why the line sits where it does. I took one econ history course in college where we read Hayek's "The Use of Knowledge in Society" and some of Lange's stuff about planners adjusting prices by trial and error till markets clear, and it struck me that this whole socialist calculation debate is actually way more useful for a co-op deciding between "set a price and let members sort themselves out" versus "we deliberate and decide together" than it ever was for cold war grandstanding. Also Mises's version of the argument, the bit about not being able to calculate rationally without genuine market prices.

What I want is a procedure the council can actually run in our monthly one-hour meeting, where most members have zero economics background and will roll their eyes at anything that sounds like a lecture. A checklist of questions, in order, for any resource we allocate: whether the relevant knowledge is dispersed and tacit (does some member on the ground know things the assembly could never collect?), whether we can realistically do Lange-style trial and error with an internal price, whether the good is even the kind of thing members think should go to whoever pays most — which I guess is where the procedure has to stop and hand it back to a values vote. Be honest about which steps are real tests and which are just judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and tell me where the procedure will misfire (like for goods where a price would be morally repugnant to members even if it'd be efficient).

Then run it on three fights we've actually had: (1) the guest rooms, which we price by the night and which always sell out in summer; (2) garden plots, which we allocate by waiting list, capped at one per household, and every spring someone proposes auctioning off the big sunny ones (that someone is usually me, for the record); (3) the bike workshop, where half the council wants per-repair fees and the other half wants a flat yearly fee with unlimited use. If the procedure gives an answer I won't like — and I suspect it will on atleast one of these — don't soften it, I'd rather know now than after I've proposed it again in the assembly.
