I run a small grantmaking program that can support about five researchers for three years on questions about the welfare of fish and decapod crustaceans in aquaculture — empirical and conceptual work on welfare assessment and sentience, not policy campaigning or supply-chain reform. Which open questions in this area matter most for that budget, and why? I'm less interested in the list than in the reasoning behind the ordering: for each question you'd fund, explain why it's upstream of other work (i.e., what else depends on it), whether it's more tractable for biologists or needs philosophers, and what findings over the next few years would move it up or down the ranking. Among the candidates I'm aware of: how to weigh brief but severe suffering at slaughter against low-grade suffering across a whole life, as in welfare-footprint approaches that estimate cumulative time spent in different pain states; whether the extreme mortality typical of shrimp larviculture makes early-life welfare the dominant term even if each larva suffers only briefly — and whether it even makes sense to count individuals that die before developing anything like a nervous system; the search for welfare indicators in animals that cannot give subjective reports; and how to treat species whose nociception is well documented but whose felt pain remains contested. Don't limit yourself to these — if I'm missing something more upstream, that's exactly what I want to hear. Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
