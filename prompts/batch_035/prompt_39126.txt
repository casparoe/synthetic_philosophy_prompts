Write a parody of an IT helpdesk knowledge-base article for the Abstract Objects Directorate, the customer-support body responsible for the Platonic realm. Users keep filing tickets complaining that they cannot causally interact with the numbers they need to know about, and the Directorate's standing position is that this is working as intended. The humour should come from getting the epistemology right: the tickets and canned replies should track genuine features of Benacerraf's dilemma — the causal isolation of abstract objects, the demand that whatever story we tell about mathematical knowledge be of a piece with the story we tell about knowledge of medium-sized physical objects, and the customer's creeping worry that any proposed channel of access will either be too thin to explain the apparent reliability of our mathematical beliefs or will quietly presuppose a causal connection the Platonist cannot afford. The piece should include: at least one escalation to a supervisor who follows Hartry Field in holding that the only acceptable resolution is a physicalist reduction, and who closes tickets accordingly; one customer who has given up and become an easy-road fictionalist, and is now paradoxically the most satisfied user on record; and a footnote in which the Directorate declines to adjudicate whether a customer's number two is the set {0, 1} or the set {1, 2}, on the grounds that the ticket requests an identification the service was never designed to provide. The piece should be funny to a philosopher of mathematics, legible to a general reader, and deadpan throughout — no winking. Around 500 words, formatted as numbered knowledge-base entries interleaved with sample tickets and replies.
