I'm a third-year undergraduate writing a term paper on Parfit's teletransporter, and I want a case that isolates something I think Reasons and Persons leaves underspecified. Parfit distinguishes psychological connectedness — direct psychological links, such as memories, intentions, and persisting character traits, holding between a person at one time and that person at a nearby time — from psychological continuity, which is just overlapping chains of such connectedness holding over long stretches, even where nothing links the two ends directly. He then argues that what matters in survival is Relation R, psychological connectedness and/or continuity, and that personal identity itself is not what matters. What I would like you to construct is a thought experiment in which these notions come apart in the uncomfortable direction: a case where the psychological continuity theorist is committed to saying the person clearly survives, and where personal identity clearly holds throughout — no fission, no duplication, no branching, one continuous living organism from start to finish — but where it is at least arguable that what matters has been lost, so that a Parfitian who insists that mere continuity without much connectedness is not enough for what matters must say that the person survives although nothing of what mattered to them does. Constraints on the scenario itself: it may be framed as a teletransporter variant or otherwise live in the world of Parfit's examples, but the mechanism of change must not be a philosopher's remote control or a spectrum of merely possible persons — it must be a single actual lifetime, and the psychological change must be wrought by something medically intelligible (a progressive neurological condition, a course of treatment, an implant), such that each step is a small change overlapping the one before it. Keep the scenario to one paragraph, no exotic physics, and make the end-state person awake, healthy, and leading a recognisable life rather than reduced to a shell. It must not simply restate Parfit's combined spectrum or his nineteenth-century Russian Socialist — those are the stock cases, and my supervisor will notice.

After the scenario, walk through the commitments. Say what the psychological continuity theorist must conclude and why; what Parfit's connectedness/continuity distinction commits him to here; whether Relation R obtains in your case, and which reading of the and/or you are relying on when you answer. Then be honest about the engineering: tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens to the verdicts if you remove it (for instance, if the steps are months rather than hours, or if exactly one strong thread of connectedness — say, one obsessive lifelong project — is deliberately preserved), and how a Parfitian would most plausibly attack the case — perhaps by arguing that overlapping chains of connectedness are themselves sufficient for what matters, or by accepting the conclusion that long-run survival is anyway mostly not what matters to us. Finally, say whether the case, if it works, threatens Parfit's negative thesis that identity is not what matters, or only his positive account of what is what matters, and whether the attack you sketched in the previous step would generalise to fission cases or is a special dispensation for your scenario. The point of the case, for my paper, is to test whether what matters can come apart from survival without invoking fission — so please generate the verdicts from Parfit's own definitions rather than stipulating them, and if you find the specifications cannot all be met at once, tell me which one has to give and why.
