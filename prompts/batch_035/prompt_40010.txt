My cousin, who was raised Hindu and is now a philosophy PhD student with a chip on her shoulder about it, walked me into this argument against karma as a theory of moral desert, and I can't get out — except the conclusion seems to prove way too much, so something must be off somewhere and I can't find it.

(1) Suffering is deserved only as repayment for a wrongdoing the sufferer was responsible for.
(2) You're responsible for a wrongdoing only if there's enough of a connection between the wrongdoer and you that it makes sense to call the debt yours — where the connection has to include some ability to recognize the deed as yours.
(3) Rebirth breaks that connection: the person reborn after the cheat dies has no memory of the cheating and no shared psychology worth mentioning, so the misfortune dumped on the reborn person is payment for a stranger's debt.
(4) So karmic desert is unjust — the universe bills people for lives they know nothing about.
(5) But the same holds inside one life: nobody being punished for a misdeed remembers it in the relevant way at sentencing time. The deed is always done by a past self, and the person in the dock is seperated from that self by the same kind of distance — memory faded, character shifted, sometimes dramatically.
(6) So if (2) really is required for desert, then no punishment is ever deserved: prison, karmic or human, always punishes someone who is merely causally downstream of the wrongdoer.

My cousin is fine with (6); she's a skeptic about desert altogether. I'm not.

Here's what confuses me. Part of me thinks the karmic theorist has an obvious reply I'm not seeing — something about the reborn person carrying the cheat's dispositions, so the connection isn't just causal but characterological, which the guy in the dock also has...? But the Buddhist version makes it worse, because they deny there's a persistent self in the first place, so who exactly is carrying the debt between lives? If the answer is something like a causal stream of mental events, doesn't that just collapse back into (5), where the prisoner is also a causal stream downstream of the crime?

Which step do I give up, what does giving it up cost, and is there a version where the karmic theorist gets to keep desert without me having to bite the "no punishment is ever deserved" bullet?
