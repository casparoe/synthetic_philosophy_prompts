I'm a machine learning engineer, ten years in the field, currently working on model evaluation — benchmarks, human annotation pipelines, "ground truth" datasets, inter-annotator agreement. Last year I read Rorty's Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature and then some of his later essays, and I came away with the uncomfortable sense that his antirepresentationalism was saying something about my daily work that I couldn't quite pin down. I want a translation, not a sales pitch.

Restate Rorty's neopragmatism in the vocabulary I actually use at work. That means: his claim that knowledge is not a matter of representations lining up with the world; his treatment of truth as a matter of justification to a community of inquirers rather than correspondence to how things are; his line that vocabularies are tools for coping rather than pictures of reality, so that adopting a new vocabulary is more like picking up a new instrument than getting a clearer view; and the book's attack on the idea that philosophy can adjudicate knowledge-claims from some neutral tribunal outside all vocabularies. Translate all of this into feature spaces and embeddings, learned representations, in-distribution versus out-of-distribution, benchmark contamination, inter-annotator agreement, eval harnesses, and the difference we gesture at between a model that has learned the world and a model that has merely learned the grader. Be as faithful as you can, and say where you're unsure a term maps.

Then give me the residue report, which is what I actually care about, in four parts:

1. Clean translations. Which of Rorty's claims come across with nothing lost? For instance, does his point about justification reduce to the observation that a "ground truth" label is just whatever an annotation team converges on — or does that translation quietly lose something he'd insist on?

2. Distortions. Which of his claims survive only in a shape he wouldn't accept? My suspicion is that my toolkit's built-in representationalism — we literally call the latent vectors "representations," we literally call the labels "ground truth" — re-imports the exact picture he spent a book dismantling, and I want to know whether that can be worked around with careful phrasing or whether it's fatal to the whole exercise.

3. What has no expression at all. His distinction between a private, ironist vocabulary for self-description and the shared public vocabulary of liberal solidarity; his hope that one vocabulary can be better than another without being truer; the strand in his later work where redescription is a moral achievement and not just a modeling choice. Does anything in my vocabulary so much as gesture at these?

4. Your verdict. Where the residue shows up, is it a defect of the ML vocabulary or of Rorty — is he saying something my field has no machinery to hear, or saying something that dissolves the moment you try to make it precise?

One constraint on the translation: don't smuggle in assumptions from my side and present them as his. If the honest result is that Rorty comes out trivially right, or trivially wrong, say which and why.
