i'm teaching a short unit on african philosophy and we're closing with ubuntu — the southern african idea that personhood isn't something you're just born with but something you achieve and keep achieving through your relations with others, usually summed up in the proverb about a person becoming a person through other persons. i want a villanelle to hand out at the last session.

the two refrains should be, roughly, the ubuntu claim (the self is constituted by its relations — no one is a person alone) and the individualist counterclaim (something in the neighborhood of the cogito: the self grounding itself, prior to and independent of what it owes anyone). heres the part i care about most: in a villanelle the refrains return unchanged while the tercets around them keep shifting, and i want that to do actual philosophical work. the same refrain should read differently depending on which tercet it follows — once as warm, once as almost threatening, say — so that by the time the two refrains finally sit side by side in the closing quatrain, the reader genuinely can't say anymore whether the relations make the self or the self makes the relations. and please don't resolve it in the last line with some tidy synthesis — our seminar ended up sitting right in that tension, and i want the poem to sit there too.

two more things. first, the students will know that ubuntu in the literature is both descriptive (a claim about what persons are) and normative (a claim about what we owe each other), and the poem should lean on that doubleness rather than ignore it — but without turning into a greeting card version of ubuntu, they will smell that a mile off. second, someone who actually knows this stuff should find the poem right, not just name-droppy, so get the idea itself correct even while the form is doing the arguing.
