so, full disclosure: I write novels for a living (mid-list thrillers, the kind with a ship or a body on the cover), and for my sins I've decided my next one opens with two sisters fighting over an inheritance. Their aunt, a retired maths teacher who liked leaving little puzzles in her will, has left them two sealed envelopes, one containing twice as much as the other, and a clause saying each sister picks one, looks inside, and may swap once before opening... well, before the other opens. The younger sister runs the classic argument on her sister: "whatever I see, the other envelope has either half or double, fifty-fifty, so switching gains me a quarter of my amount in expectation -- call it M, the other side has 2M or M/2, average (2M + M/2)/2 = 1.25M." The older sister just says: run the same argument from her side and you should both swap, then swap back, forever, which is absurd, so somewhere the younger one has smuggled in a probability she doesn't actually have. I want them to have this fight on the page, and I want it to be real -- I've read too many novels where the mathematician character talks like a fortune cookie.

here's the wrinkle I need built in, because the aunt was the type. A cousin of the sisters, a probabilist, shows up mid-scene and reveals the aunt told him exactly how she filled the envelopes: with probability 1/2 she put in 1 and 2 dollars, with probability 1/4 she put 2 and 4, with probability 1/8 she put 4 and 8, and so on, each pair half as likely as the one before. He claims (and I've checked the arithmetic, it's genuinely correct) that under this setup, whatever amount you find in your envelope, the expected gain from switching is strictly positive -- and yet by symmetry the swap-then-swap-back can't profit both sisters, and the total expected gain from a switch, summed over the pairs, comes out infinite. So now I have three positions on stage: the younger sister committed to her 5/4 argument, the older sister holding the symmetry line, and the cousin insisting that in *this* family, with *this* aunt, the younger one is sort of right in a way that should make everyone queasy.

Could you write the dialogue? Requirements: all three should be genuinely competent, nobody is a strawman, and they have to actually answer each other's points -- the older sister should engage the conditional-expectation computations rather than just repeating "symmetry!" and the younger one should feel the force of the infinite-expectation trouble rather than shrugging it off. I'd like it if the fight ends in a place a real probabilist could sign off on, even if it's an uneasy place -- my editor wants "human stakes," and frankly so do I, but I refuse to have a character win an argument by being likable. Maybe a page or two of dialogue, naturalistic speech rhythms, they can interrupt each other, and the aunt can be a presence in the room even though she's dead. Also if you can work in somewhere why the 1.25M move is treacherous in the abstract case -- what probability the younger sister is implicitly assigning, and why she's not entitled to it -- that would be great, because I want the reader to actually learn the thing, not just watch two people be clever at each other.
