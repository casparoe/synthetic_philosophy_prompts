My company went "flexible" about two years ago and I've been chewing on something ever since and can't quite settle it. On paper everything got freer: no fixed hours, no dress code, work from home whenever you want, my manager keeps saying he doesn't care how or when we get stuff done as long as it gets done. But at the same time they rolled out a dashboard where everyone's tasks and progress are visible to the whole team, a "wellbeing" app that tracks steps and sleep (officially voluntary, but our insurance premium gets cheaper if you opt in), quarterly peer reviews where everyone anonymously rates everyone, and these weekly check-ins where we each say what we did last week. Here's the thing though: nobody ever forbids me anything. There's no rule I'm breaking if I have a slow day. And yet I've never felt so watched, and honestly so controlled -- like I'm grading myself in my own head all the time. My brother works at a plant with a thick rulebook, punch cards and a union rep, and he at least knows exactly when he's off the clock. I feel like I'm performing 24/7.

So what I want to figure out is what this whole package quietly assumes about people. If you wanted to justify the dashboards and the rankings and the sleep tracking, what picture of human beings would you have to believe in first? Is it something like "people only behave when they can be seen"? Or "numbers are more trustworthy than a boss's judgment"? Or something stranger -- that the point was never to catch anyone slacking, but to get us to do the watching ourselves, so the surveillance keeps running even when nobody's actually looking at the dashboard? I keep going back and forth on whether the freedom part is a contradiction (they loosened up and tightened up at the exact same time) or whether the looseness is actually part of the machinery somehow. And the flip side: the peer reviews and wellbeing stuff are all framed as helping us grow and take care of ourselves, and I genuinely can't tell if that's a fig leaf or if the people running it really believe it, or whether those are even different things.

What I'd love from you is a proper reconstruction: what does this system presuppose about how control works, which rival pictures of it would NOT support these practices (like, would a straightforward "rules + punishment" view actually license dashboards and peer ratings?), and where is the setup vague enough that several different theories of power or human nature could each justify it fine. Also curious whether you think the "voluntary" part of the wellness app changes the philosophical story or just the legal one.
