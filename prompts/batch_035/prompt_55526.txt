He says calling it a "standard" or "diagnostic" smuggles in exactly the thing that's at issue, namely whether the idealization is normatively binding on us or just true of a fictional creature.

We tried to make it concrete a few months ago: we each wrote a credence on some prediction on the kitchen whiteboard, told each other the numbers, updated, and repeated — trying to literally iterate toward agreement the way the proof (or some extension of it, I vaguely recall reading that just announcing posteriors back and forth is enough to converge, if that's actually right) suggests. It sort of worked: we moved toward each other for a few rounds, then stalled, and then started arguing about whether the remaining gap was legitimate disagreement about priors or just the two of us being bad at updating. Weirdly, running the experiment made our meta-argument worse, not better, and I'd like to know why.

So here's what I want: a diagnosis grounded in what each of us is actually claiming.
