You are to act as a professional philosopher of mind, specialising in the metaphysics of consciousness, who is also comfortable talking to scientists on their own terms. Think carefully and methodically. Before composing your answer, brainstorm the possible mappings and discard the ones that cannot bear weight — I would rather have a short, honest translation than a long, flattering one.

Some background about me, so you can pitch things correctly. I am a retired experimental physicist. For forty years my working life consisted of building detectors, calibrating instruments, and reasoning about what their readings could and could not tell me about the systems under study. Since retiring I have read a good deal of philosophy of mind, and the higher-order theories of consciousness are the ones I feel I ought to be able to grasp: they seem, on the surface, to say that a mental state becomes conscious when something else registers it, which sounds like an instrument reading an instrument. But I keep suspecting that my vocabulary is either flattening the theory or flattering it, and I cannot tell which.

Here is the translation task. Restate the higher-order family of theories in the vocabulary of my trade — signals, detectors, readouts, gain, calibration, noise floors, the distinction between a reading and the quantity it purports to measure, observer effects and instrument loading. Translate, at minimum: (1) the basic higher-order thought theory, on which a first-order perceptual state is conscious only when there is a suitable higher-order representation of it — and note, by contrast, what the first-order rivals claim, namely that the signal's own properties suffice; (2) the higher-order perception theory, on which there is an inner monitoring faculty, something like a sense that senses the senses; (3) the same-order or self-representational variant, on which the state represents itself rather than being represented by a distinct state; and (4) a more recent refinement on which the higher-order representation is produced not by faithful observation of the first-order state but by a fast heuristic that guesses at what the first-order system is doing and tests the guess — attribution rather than readout, in effect.

I want the translation done honestly: do not let the instrumentation vocabulary quietly assume what the theory is trying to assert, and do not repair the theory in passing by choosing whichever physical reading makes it look best.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about:

- What translates cleanly, and why?
- What translates only with distortion? The core claim worries me most: that the higher-order state makes the first-order state conscious. In my trade, connecting a second meter to a first meter changes nothing about the first meter's reading; if anything, it loads the circuit. Is the higher-order theorist claiming something like loading — a real causal interference — or is that metaphor simply breaking under strain?
- What has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? Three candidates, though you may find others: (a) the possibility of misrepresentation — a higher-order state that registers a conscious perception where the underlying perception is absent or quite different. A meter can read high, but the theorist seems to want the faulty reading to constitute the conscious experience, whereas for me a faulty reading is just faulty, and nothing has been measured. (b) The question whether the higher-order representation is itself conscious, which threatens an unbounded regress of instruments unless something stops it, and the various devices the theorists offer for stopping it. (c) Phenomenal character — what it is like for the subject — which in my vocabulary is at best a label for something with no associated quantity.

Finally, answer the question I really came with: does the residue point to something the philosophical debate is missing — something real that my physical vocabulary cannot express — or does it point to something the higher-order theories cannot say, namely what they mean by a representation in the first place? Be candid about the possibility that 'representation' is simply less well defined than any quantity I have ever put a meter on, and about the mirror-image possibility: that the physicists' demand for an operational definition is itself an assumption being smuggled in as a standard.
