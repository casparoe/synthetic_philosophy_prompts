Is there an established name for this? I half remember Nietzsche, in one of the untimely essays, saying there are three ways history can serve life: monumental (great men and deeds as models to emulate), antiquarian (preserving and revering what actually happened), and critical (dragging the past to the bar and condemning it). Fine. But I keep noticing a way of dealing with the past that doesn't fit any of the three, and I can't find the word for it. I mean people who consume the past purely for its mood: the synthwave listener with zero interest in what 1984 was actually like, my friend who decorates her apartment mid-centruy but couldn't name a single designer, and another friend who watches space race documentaries not to learn engineering or admire astronauts but just because she likes the feeling of an era when people thought the future was going to be cool. Nobody is being emulated, no facts are being preserved with any piety (any generic 1980s will do as long as the vibe is right — she'll happily watch a documentary she knows is a bit wrong), and nobody is being judged. The past is basically aesthetic raw material, history as texture. It's not nostalgia exactly, since nostalgia is about your own past and most of these people weren't alive for any of it, and someone once told me about "hauntology" but that seems to be about mourning futures that never happened, which is a different thing. So: does this stance have a name, from Nietzsche or from anyone after him? Maybe he'd just file it under a decayed antiquarian mode? If nothing established fits, coin a term, define it, and draw the boundaries — what exactly the stance is, how it differs from antiquarian reverence, from nostalgia, and from commodification-of-the-past as a social critique. And I want you to actually take a side on whether it's a genuinely fourth mode of relating to history or just antiquarian history with the piety stripped out, and whether it can still be "for life" in Nietzsche's sense or whether it's precisely the thing he was warning about. Don't hand me a menu of possibilities and let me pick.
