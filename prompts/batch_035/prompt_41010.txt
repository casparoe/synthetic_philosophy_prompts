Quick background: I've been reading about pain asymbolia, the condition where patients report feeling pain -- they can rate its intensity, point to where it is, describe the quality -- but it doesn't bother them at all. They don't wince, don't withdraw, sometimes seem amused by it. I tried to build an argument on this and it seems to prove way too much:

(1) If pain is intrinsically bad, its badness depends only on how it feels, not on any relation to the persons other attitudes or states. (2) The asymbolic's pain feels the same as ordinary pain -- same quality, same intensity, same location. (3) So if pain is intrinsically bad, the asymbolic's pain is bad for the asymbolic. (4) Whatever is bad for you gives you a pro tanto reason to be rid of it, a reason you could in principle recognize. (5) The asymbolic has no reason whatsoever to be rid of their pain -- from the inside, nothing counts against it. (6) So the asymbolic's pain is not bad for them. (7) Therefore pain is not intrinsically bad: what's bad is the minding, the aversion or distress, not the sensation itself.

On its own I could almost live with (7). What I can't live with is the way it generalizes: if the badness of pain is relational in this way, then nothing is bad for you unless it hooks up with some attitude of yours, and goodbye to the badness of a painless death, deception you never discover, or your life going worse in ways you don't happen to care about. I'd much rather keep (1) -- the thought that pain is just bad, full stop, seems like the bedrock everything else in ethics sits on. But I also believe the asymbolia data. So which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me?
