Someone in my reading group walked me through this argument after coffee and I can't find the exit out of it. It's supposed to show that grounding theory collapses unless you allow brute facts, but as stated it seems to prove way too much — like, that there are no facts at all, which is clearly nuts. Here it is:

(1) Nothing is just brute: every fact must be grounded in more fundamental facts, since saying that's just how it is explains nothing. (2) So pick any fact F and follow its ground-chain downward: either the chain goes down forever, or it runs in a circle, or it bottoms out in ungrounded facts. (3) Circles are impossible, because grounding is asymmetric and irreflexive — nothing can figure among its own grounds, directly or indirectly. (4) Endless descent is impossible too: if the chain never ends, then no fact is finally responsible for F, and while each link gets explained by the next one, the whole infinite series itself is never explained — so we've smuggled a brute fact back in at the level of the series, violating (1). (5) Bottoming out is also impossible, since the terminal facts are ungrounded, i.e. brute, again violating (1). (6) So no fact F can obtain: nothing is true, reality is empty.

For what it's worth, I'd most like to keep (1) — ungrounded facts feel like a cop-out to me, and I suspect that's the one everyone ends up giving up anyway.
