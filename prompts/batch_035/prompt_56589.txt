here's roughly where I've landed. (1) I'm a compatibilist — determinism doesn't kill the kinds of control and agency worth wanting, I found Dennett's side convincing on that. (2) I also buy what Dennett says about everyday desert surviving: the runner who trained for years deserves the medal, the colleague who sandbagged the project deserves the blame, in some ordinary sense that doesn't require spooky libertarian free will. (3) BUT Caruso got to me on basic desert — I don't think anyone deserves to suffer *because* they knowingly did wrong, not in that ultimate, it-would-be-just-even-if-nothing-good-came-of-it sense. (5) At the same time I think excuses matter and matter for a *responsibility* reason — duress, coercion, serious manipulation should mitigate, and my instinct is that's because the person wasn't a full author of the act, not merely because excusing them has good consequences.
