So here's my situation: I'm on the editorial board of a mid-tier general-interest economics journal, and at next month's meeting we're voting on a proposal I honestly can't make up my mind about. The idea is that any paper whose core model leans on simplifying assumptions we all know are false -- perfect rationality, frictionless markets, a representative agent, instantaneous equilibration -- would have to include a short "assumptions appendix" where the authors justify, assumption by assumption, why the falsehood is harmless *for the question they're asking*, rather than just inherited from the literature. Reviewers would get a checkbox to flag papers where the appendix reads as boilerplate. The proposer's complaint is that half our submissions are Rube Goldberg machines where one unrealistic assumption quietly does all the work and nobody has to say which one.

The pushback is basically the Friedman line, which some of our board members state pretty forcefully: judging a theory by the realism of its assumptions gets everything backwards, since all interesting theories abstract drastically by design, and the test is whether the thing predicts, not whether its postulates read like a documentary. On that view the appendix is security theater -- it makes authors perform sincerity about assumptions whose only real defense is the model's track record, which we can already see from the results section. And a couple of board members add that it'll just incentivize strategically vague modeling, or push authors to journals that don't ask.

Could you draft the decision memo I'll circulate before the meeting? Give it a clear bottom line -- approve, reject, or approve with conditions (and if conditions, spell them out so an author could actually comply) -- plus the reasoning, and do take the Friedman objection seriously rather than waving it off; the people making it are the ones I most need to win over or lose gracefully to. If you think there's a version of the proposal that survives the objection, that's the memo I want. Nothing too fancy in terms of formatting, just something readable the board can act on, and please keep it under 800 words because two of our members will not read more than that.
