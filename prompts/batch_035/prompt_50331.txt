I am a professor of medieval philosophy who has spent the better part of thirty years teaching Aquinas, and I write as someone fluent in the scholastic vocabulary and largely innocent of the literature on machine consciousness. My university's AI ethics committee keeps returning to a question I find oddly shaped: not whether machines are conscious, but which computations matter morally — whether moral status attaches to a certain kind of information processing, whether it is substrate-independent, whether an exact functional duplicate of a mind running on different hardware would matter morally just as much, whether partial or degraded or briefly-instantiated computations matter partially. Restate the main positions in this debate for me in my own vocabulary: act and potency, form and matter, substantial versus accidental form, per se versus per accidens unity, the soul as the substantial form of the body, the distinction between sensitive and rational souls, and Boethius's definition of a person as an individual substance of a rational nature. Cover, at minimum, functionalism and substrate independence, the biological objections to them, the views that grade moral status by how unified a system's processes are, and the deflationary view that no computation, as such, could be a bearer of moral status at all. For each position, tell me what translates cleanly into hylomorphic terms, what must be bent or distorted to fit, and what has no expression in the framework whatsoever. Then give me the residue report: does the residue point to something the computational debate silently presupposes and my tradition would deny — for instance, that the unity of the moral subject can simply be read off from the unity of information processing — or does it point to something Aquinas cannot say? I am particularly interested in what my vocabulary does with the idea of duplication: two physically distinct systems running the same computation, which appears to be a single question on the committee's framing and threatens to come apart into two quite different questions on mine. Please resist the temptation to treat my framework as a museum piece; translate into it as a live option, and let the failures of translation be as instructive as the successes.
