Write me a dialogue between two philosophers, Sam and Ola. Sam is an epiphenomenalist about qualia; Ola is a type-identity theorist who thinks the paradox of phenomenal judgment sinks epiphenomenalism for good. Some constraints:

- Both should be genuinely good at this — no strawmen, and each should score at least one real point the other doesn't fully answer.
- Early on, Ola should press the core objection as a dilemma about her own utterance: when she says "I'm seeing red," the utterance is a physical event with physical causes, so the redness itself plays no role in producing it. How can the epiphenomenalist say she *knows* what she's talking about when she says that, rather than just guessing luckily?
- Sam's main line of defense should be the common-cause reply: the brain state causes both the quale and the report, so reports track qualia as reliably as anything tracks anything else. Ola should counter with the counterfactual problem: knowledge seems to require that if the qualia had been different, the report would have been different too, and epiphenomenalism flatly denies that. Sam gets a real reply here — maybe something about the nearest worlds where qualia differ being worlds where the physical base differs too, so the tracking never actually breaks.
- Somewhere in the middle, have them get into a concrete case I've been wondering about: the fridge hum. You stop noticing a constant hum after a while, and you only notice it when it stops. Ola should use this to push on absence-causation: is my judgment "there's no hum now" caused by the *absence* of the hum? And does the analogous thing happen with a constant pain, or a constant color tint — do I stop "noticing" my qualia too? What could noticing even be for an epiphenomenalist, given that noticing looks like a causal relation? Sam should struggle here but find something to say.
- Sam is NOT allowed to win by stipulation with zombies or conceivability arguments — those can come up but can't settle anything.
- At some point Ola should float the inverted-qualia-without-any-physical-difference possibility, and Sam should either bite the bullet or dodge in an interesting way.
- End unresolved: each concedes exactly one small thing to the other, and they should be real concessions, not diplomatic fluff.

Aim for 700–1000 words of dialogue, natural prose — they can interrupt, joke, get slightly annoyed. No lecturing at the reader from outside the conversation, and dont have them agree on terminology for more than a line or two.
