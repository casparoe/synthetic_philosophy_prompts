At a family argument about wealth and desert, my cousin dismissed the entire idea of earned success with the one-liner that no one is really self-made: no one is responsible for their own character, because character is handed to you by genes and upbringing. It sounded persuasive but also like several different claims stapled together — anywhere from the platitude that nobody accomplishes anything entirely alone, to something close to Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, on which ultimate moral responsibility is impossible because being responsible for what you do would require being responsible for how you are, which would require having made yourself that way, which launches a regress of prior shaping that nothing can terminate. Please lay out the distinct precise theses this slogan could be expressing, as a menu rather than one favored reading: for each, say whether it is a platitude, substantive and defensible, or straightforwardly false, and say which of the readings would actually support my cousin's conclusion that no one deserves their success, as opposed to merely the weaker conclusion that no one creates themselves from nothing. Include at least one reading a compatibilist could happily accept, and note where the inference from causal dependence to the denial of desert is doing unearned work. Finally, say which reading you find strongest, what a successful reply to the regress would have to look like for that verdict to change, and where in your own reasoning you are most likely to have gone wrong.
