I'm a third-year undergraduate taking a seminar on egalitarianism and distributive justice while also working through my university's econometrics sequence, so the vocabulary I actually use day-to-day is the one from my problem sets: structural equations, exogenous and endogenous regressors, error terms, omitted-variable bias, and counterfactual decompositions of an outcome. I'm fluent in OLS and basic structural models, shakier on identification.

I'd like the central luck egalitarian positions restated in that vocabulary, one by one. Take Dworkin's distinction between brute luck and option luck, together with his device of a hypothetical insurance market: is brute luck just the part of the outcome driven by omitted exogenous variables, and option luck endogenous, chosen exposure to risk — and what does the hypothetical insurance scheme look like as an econometric operation rather than a fiction? Then Cohen's complaint that Dworkin measures the wrong currency, access to advantage rather than advantage itself: is that a dispute about the choice of dependent variable? Then Anderson's claim that luck egalitarianism asks the wrong question entirely, that equality is about relations between people rather than a partition of anyone's outcome variable.

For each, give me a residue report: which claims translate cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which cannot be expressed at all in the language of a model with an outcome, a set of inputs, and a line drawn somewhere among them between "circumstance" and "effort." Here is my real question, and I want you to take it seriously rather than flatter me: from where I sit, the philosophers' argument about where to draw the responsibility cut looks like a fight over which regressors belong on the right-hand side of the equation — a specification choice, revisable in the light of better data, not a metaphysical one. Is that right? Or does the residue, especially whatever remains of Anderson, show that something is missing from my vocabulary rather than from theirs? I want an honest translation — don't smuggle in the assumption that everything about a person is either a variable or noise, and call that translation.
