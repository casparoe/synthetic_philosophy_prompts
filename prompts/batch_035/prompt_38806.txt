I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas — mostly neuroscience and artificial intelligence — and my editor has asked me to write a long-form piece laying out where I actually stand on the relationship between the mind and the brain, largely because I suspect that years of reporting on both wet labs and AI labs have left me with a private philosophy that doesn't cohere. Before I commit anything to print, I'd like a consistency audit of the views I find myself holding whenever I sit down to write about these topics. Here they are. First, I'm a functionalist about the mind: mental states are individuated by what they do rather than what they're made of, so a belief is a belief in virtue of its causal role in organizing perception, inference, and action, and the same state could in principle be realized in neurons or in silicon. Second, I take multiple realizability to be the central fact that keeps psychology and psychiatry autonomous sciences rather than footnotes to molecular biology. Third, I believe that a sufficiently faithful whole-brain emulation — a complete, fine-grained simulation of a particular person's brain running on computers — would be conscious, and would be the same person in every sense that matters for a headline or an obituary.

Fourth, I cannot make myself believe the old China-brain scenario: if the entire population of a large country were to implement a person's complete functional organization by passing notes according to the right rules, it seems obvious to me that there would be nothing it is like to be that arrangement, even granting that it functionally duplicates a conscious person. Fifth, my coverage of psychiatric research has pushed me toward thinking of conditions like depression as deeply neurochemical kinds — the papers I report on emphasize specific receptor systems and neurotransmitter pathways, and the experience has made me doubt that minds are anywhere near as substrate-flexible as my second commitment suggests.

So, do these five commitments hang together? I'd like to know which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way — in particular, whether my embrace of the whole-brain emulation and my refusal of the China-brain can both survive, and whether my functionalism is really compatible with the picture of mental illness as rooted in brain chemistry. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damning, and for each genuine one, tell me what would have to give and what that concession would cost me elsewhere in the set. If there's a single revision that would clean up most of the mess, identify it, since that's the one my editor will actually let me write about.
