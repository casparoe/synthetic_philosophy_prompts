Here's the exam question:

(25 pts) ClarityDesk, a customer support firm, replaces its review process with a single KPI: the percentage of tickets marked "closed" within 24 hours of being opened. Within a year, agents close tickets with one canned reply regardless of whether the problem got solved, an automated script closes tickets after 72 hours of customer inactivity, and follow-up surveys show the highest-scoring agents have the worst customer satisfaction. Separately, analysts looking at the very first month of data (before anyone gamed anything) find that the agents who happened to top the KPI were only modestly better than average on actual satisfaction. (a) Using the taxonomy from lecture, regressional, extremal, causal, and adversarial Goodhart, identify which failure modes are present here and explain what distinguishes each mode. (b) For each mode you identify, say whether a better-designed metric could have prevented it. (c) Could even a perfect proxy have failed here?

And here's the student's answer:

Goodhart's law says that when a measure becomes a target it ceases to be a good measure, which Goodhart stated in his 1975 paper on monetary policy. Campbell's law is the same idea restated for social indicators in education, so I'll use the terms interchangeably.

(a) The agents closing tickets with canned replies just to hit the KPI is adversarial Goodhart: the agents are strategic and are gaming the measure, and the measure is responding to the agents rather than to the goal. The auto-close script is causal Goodhart, since introducing the KPI causally changed the support environment itself (tickets now close without anyone resolving anything). The first-month finding is regressional Goodhart: when you select agents at the tail of a noisy proxy, the tail is enriched with agents who got lucky rather than agents who are genuinely good, so the top scorers' true quality regresses toward the mean. If the firm keeps optimizing on this proxy, the correlation between KPI and satisfaction will eventually flip sign, and the best KPI performers will become the worst agents, which is exactly what the follow-up surveys show.

(b) The adversarial gaming by agents could have been prevented with a better metric, e.g. verified resolution rate. Causal Goodhart could likewise be prevented by designing a metric that doesn't change the environment when targeted. Regressional Goodhart can be prevented by re-measuring the correlation between KPI and satisfaction after deployment and updating accordingly.

(c) A perfect proxy correlates 1.0 with customer satisfaction, so by definition it could not fail. Since no real proxy is perfect, ClarityDesk's real mistake was using a proxy at all, and the general lesson of Goodhart's law is that optimization should be avoided wherever possible.
