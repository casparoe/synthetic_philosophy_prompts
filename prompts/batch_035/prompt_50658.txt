You are an expert philosopher of economics, with a background in decision theory. Write an essay (roughly 800–1,200 words) on the following controversy: does the revealed-preference approach give "utility" a determinate meaning, or does it collapse into circularity, or into a purely tautological device for bookkeeping over observed choices?

In the course of the essay, weigh at least the following considerations:

1. The circularity worry: if preferences are simply defined as whatever choices reveal, then the explanation "she chose the soup because she preferred it" becomes a definition rather than an explanation, and utility can no longer do any genuine explanatory or predictive work.
2. The recovery problem for menu-dependent choice: consider a diner who reliably orders the second-cheapest wine on any wine list. Under a straightforward revealed-preference reading this shows a preference for that wine; but intuitively the diner's preference is not about wine at all — it is about not appearing stingy or ostentatious. If the real preference is defined over whole choice situations rather than outcomes, standard utility representations over outcomes miss it.
3. The self-binding case: a person buys a packet of cigarettes and simultaneously pays a friend a fee for every cigarette the friend catches them smoking. Both transactions are choices, and they appear to reveal contradictory preferences; consider whether this shows that revealed preference must distinguish first-order from second-order (or "meta") preferences, and whether that distinction can itself be recovered from choice data alone.
4. The operationalisation defence: perhaps revealed preference is only a way of assigning consistent numbers to choice data, which is scientifically respectable but useless for welfare analysis — for if "welfare as preference satisfaction" reduces to "welfare as getting what you chose", it cannot condemn even thoroughly adaptive, sour-grapes preferences.

Think through these cases carefully before settling on a thesis, and do not end on a non-committal note: defend a clear verdict about what, if anything, "utility" means on the revealed-preference programme.
