Something I've never gotten about Buridan's ass: if the two hay bales are genuinely identical in every respect the ass cares about, isn't its inability to choose just... correct? There's nothing to get wrong. People treat it like a paradox, but it looks more like a little proof that perfectly symmetric preferences plus no tie-breaking procedure equals starvation, which is fine as math but tells us nothing about real agents. Also, the standard "solution" of randomizing seems to just push the problem back a level — the ass now has to pick between coin-flipping and standing still, and if it's indifferent between those too, we're back where we started. So can you explain what the actual live philosophical issue is supposed to be? Like, what separates philosophers who think these cases reveal something about choice or value (indeterminate utilities, Ruth Chang-style parity, whatever) from people who just shrug? And does "irrational" even have a grip when the options are exactly tied, or does the concept sort of dissolve there?
