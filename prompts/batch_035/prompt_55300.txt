I help run a university reading group on long-term AI risk. For our final session I want a decision-tree handout that takes each participant from the starting claim "AI systems will eventually be able to do most of the cognitive work humans are now paid to do, including the research needed to build better AI" to a named position on takeoff speed and the resulting strategic situation. Constraints: the branch questions must be answerable by an interested non-specialist from intuition alone, not from knowing the literature — concrete questions such as whether capability gains historically arrive in sudden jumps or as smooth compounding; whether the binding constraint on a smarter system is compute or insights that must be found one at a time; whether an AI that accelerates AI research feeds that acceleration back into itself without steep diminishing returns; whether the gains would concentrate in a single project or diffuse across many competing labs; and whether outside actors could notice and react in time for it to matter. Use at most ten branch questions, and no question may simply ask which label the participant prefers. The leaves should be the actual named positions — hard takeoff ending in one project holding a decisive strategic advantage, continuous takeoff into a multipolar economy, and the deflationary view that this is just ordinary economic growth with a new input — plus any unnamed cells your tree forces into existence, each carrying one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Build the tree from the logical space first and place the positions in it afterward, rather than working backward from the labels. Flag explicitly any pair of positions the tree cannot cleanly separate (for example, a genuinely slow takeoff from a fast one that starts late), and include at least one branch that turns on something which looks like an empirical question but is really a value judgment, so the handout exposes that rather than papering over it.
