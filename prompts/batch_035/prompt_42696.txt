In my metaethics seminar we've spent the whole term on moral realism vs anti-realism, and I've started to suspect the two sides argue past eachother half the time. Write the joint statement a non-naturalist moral realist and an evolutionary-debunking anti-realist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the claims they agree on — e.g. that our moral faculties were selected for fitness rather than for tracking truth, and that this fact by itself doesn't yet settle anything; the exact remaining disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; and the cruxes. I suspect two of them: whether two agents who share all the non-moral facts, are fully informed and unbiased, could still rationally disagree about a moral claim, and whether such agents slowly converging on some moral claim over time would count as evidence of a mind-independent moral fact. For each crux, give the case or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Also say whether any part of their dispute is verbal rather than substantive, and which part. Last thing: when you list the agreed claims, please mark which are genuinely settled among people who know this area and which are actually contested — don't dress a contested view up as the consensus.
