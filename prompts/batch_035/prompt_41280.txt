My friend is a hardline Humean on miracle testimony — she's fond of the line about proportioning belief to evidence, though I don't think she's actually read the essay since undergrad. Her verdicts so far: she believes Caesar was assassinated on testimony alone, obviously. She believes her cousin's report that he won a decent lottery prize even though that was maybe one in a few million. She believes a colleague's report that he got caught in rain in a desert valley where it essentially never rains. She rolls her eyes at Fatima, at Lourdes, and at every faith-healing story she's ever been told. She believes the historical reports of the 1919 eclipse measurements even though that result overthrew Newton's physics and she wasn't alive for it. And when her reliable, sober friend swears he watched a coin land heads twenty times in a row, she believes him — but only because she's sure the tossing was somehow rigged.

The topic's coming up again at dinner and I want to be ready. Here are the new cases: (a) a different friend, disinterested and not prone to nonsense, reports that at a home poker game where she herself shuffled the deck between hands, one guest was dealt a royal flush three hands in a row — will my friend believe the event happened as described, believe the dealing happened but infer cheating, or just withhold? (b) The hypothetical: suppose there were a thousand independent, educated, cross-examined witnesses plus continuous video of a man levitating for a full hour — does she believe it happened, or does she say that's merely evidence the so-called law wasn't a law?

What I want from you: identify the rule she's actually running. My candidates are (1) never accept testimony to a violation of a law of nature, however strong the testimony; (2) weigh the antecedent improbability against the testimonial evidence, where no actual miracle report has ever cleared the bar but a hypothetical one could; (3) discount testimony wherever wishful thinking or religious bias could explain the report. Her record fits more than one of these — tell me which verdicts fit which rules, what each rule predicts about (a) and (b), your best guess at her answers with reasons, and the one further case I could put to her at the table that would best split the rules apart. And with (b), if her only escape route is redefining what counts as a law, flag that for me, because then I want to push her on whether her principle can ever lose.
