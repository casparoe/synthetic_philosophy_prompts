That sent me down a rabbit hole on moral testimony, and now I want a consistency check on the package I've ended up with:

(1) Testimony is how I know nearly everything anyway — history, what's actually in my food, the state of the war — so I'm an optimist about moral testimony too: if a trusted person who knows the situation tells me what I'd be doing is wrong, I can know it on their say-so. (2) But I also think the pessimists have a point that moral knowledge is a kind of understanding — you have to see why it's wrong — and understanding can't be picked up secondhand; you can inherit the belief, not the seeing. (3) I think ending the friendship on trust, without ever seeing it myself, was fully warranted, and I deserve the same credit for it as if I'd reasoned my way there — I don't feel like a worse agent because of the route I took. (4) I've always held that what makes an action genuinely admirable is doing it from your own appreciation of the reasons — its why I don't give much credit to people who only do right out of fear of getting caught.
