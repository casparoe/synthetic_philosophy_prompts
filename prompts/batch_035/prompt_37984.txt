I'm the sole program officer at a small regional foundation funding community environmental projects. We award roughly forty grants a year, I receive about a hundred applications per cycle, and I have two weeks per cycle to make recommendations to a board that must sign off on anything over $25,000. Rather than pretending to rank every application against every other — which I cannot do honestly in the time available — I want to rebuild my process explicitly on satisficing and bounded rationality: a procedure that fixes (a) how I search, in what order I review applications and what information I extract from each before deciding it's out; (b) how I set the aspiration level that counts as "good enough" to fund; (c) when I stop searching and recommend, rather than continuing to look for something better; and (d) how the aspiration level should move between cycles as I learn what the applicant pool actually contains, including the fact that a run of easy cycles can leave my threshold set for a pool I no longer have. Please write the procedure as concrete steps I can follow in order, state which parts of bounded-rationality theory you are keeping and which you are dropping for the sake of usability, and mark the points where the procedure cannot eliminate my judgment. Then run it on three cases I actually face: an application that is competent but unexciting on every criterion; an application that is brilliant on one dimension and mediocre on the rest, where I suspect a satisficer and an optimizer would part ways; and the case where nothing in the pool clears the current threshold — tell me what the procedure recommends, and be honest about whether that's a recommendation I will like, given that the board expects this year's money to be spent this year.
