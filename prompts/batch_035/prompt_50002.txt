I'm a philosophy graduate student working on the inefficacy objection — the complaint that an individual's contribution to a collective harm (or her abstention from a collective good) makes no difference, so she does nothing wrong by participating. In our seminar we've been working through the standard consequentialist replies: the expected-value move (tiny probability of being pivotal times enormous stakes), the nearest-threshold move, and views on which whether your contribution makes a difference can be genuinely indeterminate. What I want for the paper is a case study that isolates one variable cleanly, rather than another tour of carbon footprints and elections.

Here is the specification. Construct two scenarios that are matched on every parameter the inefficacy objection cares about: same number of contributors, same size of each individual contribution, same aggregate harm, same personal cost to the agent of abstaining, and — crucially — the agent's probability of being pivotal set equal in both cases, at effectively zero. You must explain, within each scenario, how the setup guarantees this near-zero pivotality (e.g., the agent has good reason to know she is nowhere near a decision point). The only permitted difference between the two cases is the causal structure by which the collective produces its harm: in one case the harm has a sharp threshold (nothing bad happens at all until some tipping point is crossed), while in the other the harm accumulates smoothly (every contribution adds a genuinely tiny amount of bad, with no threshold anywhere). The cases should be engineered so that the excuse "it makes no difference whether I join in" rings natural and sincere in one case and rings hollow and self-serving in the other — and you should decide and argue for which case is which, not stipulate it.

Constraints and deliverables: the settings must be mundane and realistic (a shared office kitchen, an apartment building, a community garden, a dorm — your choice), and I'd rather you avoid voting, climate change, and meat, since those have been done to death; likewise do not recycle Parfit's harmless torturers — no torture dials, no institutions of cruelty. Each scenario should be at most a paragraph. After presenting them, demonstrate that the cases actually meet the matching conditions (state each one and how the scenario satisfies it — including how you got the pivotality probabilities to be equal, which is the hardest part and where I most expect you to have fudged something; if you did fudge, say so and say which condition gave way). Then walk through, case by case, what each of three positions would say about the agent's participation: an expected-value consequentialist who counts small probabilities of being pivotal, a nearest-threshold view in the spirit of Kagan, and a view on which the difference-making facts can be indeterminate, in the spirit of Nefsky. Finally, tell me which structural feature of the pair is doing the work in generating the intuitive asymmetry, what happens to the pair if you remove that feature, and how a philosopher who insists the two excuses are exactly as good (or exactly as bad) as each other would attack your case.
