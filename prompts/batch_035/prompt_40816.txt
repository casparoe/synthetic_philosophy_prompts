For the free will unit of my undergraduate metaphysics seminar, I want a two-directional case analysis of Charles Whitman's 1966 tower shooting at the University of Texas at Austin, run through the compatibilism/incompatibilism debate. The salient facts, which my students will have in front of them: the night before the shooting, Whitman stabbed his mother and his wife to death; in a note he left behind, he said he could not understand his own mounting violent urges and asked that an autopsy be performed on his brain to look for a physical explanation; the next day he killed more than a dozen people and wounded dozens more from the tower before being shot dead by police. The autopsy revealed a glioblastoma pressing against his amygdala, though how much the tumor actually contributed to his behavior remains genuinely disputed -- and I want the analysis to run under both assumptions, that the tumor was behaviorally potent and that it was incidental. The deliverables: first, a disciplined mapping of what compatibilist analyses of responsibility (reasons-responsiveness, hierarchical identification, control-based accounts) say about whether Whitman acted freely, and what incompatibilist sourcehood views say; second, an honest accounting of where each camp does real explanatory work, where each is silent or misdescribes the case, and where they in fact converge rather than disagree. Third, I want the case run back against the theories: is it a confirmation, an anomaly, or something neither anticipated? Two specific issues should be addressed rather than dodged. One is the continuum problem: if this tumor removes responsibility, what of the lesser abnormalities and traumas on the way to it, and does either camp have a principled stopping point between full responsibility and none? The other is whether the incompatibilist is entitled to treat the tumor as determinism writ small -- as just the ordinary causal story of any action made suddenly visible -- and if so, whether that helps the incompatibilist or merely changes the subject from freedom to pathology.
