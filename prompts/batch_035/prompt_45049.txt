I'm taking a metaphysics course this semester, and our current unit is on presentism, the growing block, and the eternalist "block universe." The assignment was to interview someone with firm time-related intuitions, list their verdicts, and diagnose the view underneath. I interviewed my roommate Dana, and I've ended up with a record I can't diagnose on my own. Her verdicts so far: (1) before anything is decided, a statement like "there will be a sea battle in the harbor tomorrow" is neither true nor false, and only becomes one or the other once events settle it; (2) "Caesar crossed the Rubicon" is straightforwardly true, and not merely because of present-day records and traces -- she visibly rolled her eyes when I offered the "it's true in virtue of the footprints it left" analysis; (3) asked whether past events exist, she says the question is a trap: "there were such events" is fine, but "there are such events" doesn't say when, so it isn't anything; (4) she insists the past is fixed and the future genuinely open, and that this is a fact about the world itself rather than a fact about our ignorance; (5) she accepts special relativity as settled physics and doesn't seem to feel any tension between that and anything in (1) through (4); (6) she says that when a root canal is over, that is better news than if it had never happened, and not just because the procedure is earlier than right now.

The new case, which she hasn't ruled on yet, is the one our professor built out of relativity. Imagine an alien ship inbound toward Earth that passes a marker buoy a hundred light-years out, cruising at a substantial fraction of light speed. In the crew's frame of reference, events on Earth that we would date decades into our future are simultaneous with their flyby; and by chaining together a series of such observers, each passing the next, you can connect our "now" to any event we would call future, no matter how far off. So the questions for Dana are: in the crew's frame, are those Earth events real or not; is the crew's "now" on exactly the same footing as ours; and is it therefore already settled, from some legitimate standpoint, whether there is a sea battle in the harbor in 2050? Predict her answers, say which view she is implicitly running, and be careful here: at least two views fit her first six verdicts about equally well (someone in my section claimed you can even be a block-universe person and still deny that future contingents are true or false now). Identify the rival rules, give your best guess with reasons, and propose the further case -- perhaps something about a time traveler heading back to 44 BC, or about the root canal relief -- that would most cleanly tell those rules apart.

Please structure your response in four sections, in this order: "The candidate views" (roughly 200 words), "My prediction for the spaceship case" (roughly 150 words), "The best-fitting view and why" (roughly 150 words), and "The case that would settle it" (roughly 100 words).
