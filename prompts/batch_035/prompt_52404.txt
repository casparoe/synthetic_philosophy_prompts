I spent a career in physical chemistry before retiring, and I have lately been working through ethics as an interested amateur. One issue has lodged itself in my mind and I would like your calibrated view on it rather than an editorial. The question is the so-called paradox of deontology: the observation that if morality contains agent-relative constraints (for example, a prohibition on torturing the innocent, or on punishing the knowingly innocent, no matter what), then there will be situations in which honoring the constraint produces a strictly worse outcome than violating it — most starkly, situations where I could prevent five comparable violations by committing one myself. To a scientist this looks like a theory in trouble: in my field, a model that is systematically dominated in this way would be revised, not defended. But I am aware that consequentialists may be exploiting my training, since the paradox only bites if "worse" is evaluated purely in terms of total outcomes, and it is arguably question-begging to insist that this is the only admissible measure of a moral theory's performance.

So here is what I want from you. First, state a precise proposition to evaluate — I propose something like: "There exist genuine moral constraints such that it is impermissible to violate one constraint once in order to prevent several comparable violations of the same constraint by others," but tighten or amend it if you think it misstates the live issue. Then give me your credence in that proposition, decomposed into the sub-questions it depends on. The ones I can see are at least these: whether the doing/allowing distinction marks a morally fundamental difference; whether intending a harm differs morally from merely foreseeing it; whether a constraint can be grounded non-instrumentally without collapsing into either consequentialism or bare intuition; and whether the correct reading of the paradox already assumes a consequentialist metric of "worse." But I expect you to adjust the decomposition, and I want a number and a one-line justification for each term, then the combined figure, with a sentence on how sensitive the result is to each term.

I should tell you where I currently stand, so you can tell me whether my error is in the premises or in how I am combining them. My credence that the constraint holds in the torture-sheriff style of case is around 0.7 when I consider it as a moral question, but when I reflect as a scientist on how badly a theory behaves that generates such tradeoffs, my confidence sinks toward 0.4, and the oscillation itself bothers me — I suspect one of the two framings is illicitly importing its conclusion. Please identify which sub-question my two selves most likely disagree about, and tell me what argument or kind of case, if any, would most move your own number.
