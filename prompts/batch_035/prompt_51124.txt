I'm a game engine programmer by trade — my whole world is draw calls, level-of-detail, occlusion culling, framebuffers, temporal reprojection, the gap between what lives in the scene graph and what actually gets rasterized on any given frame. I've been reading a bit about the "consciousness as user illusion" position, the Dennett / Keith Frankish style view that phenomenal consciousness — the way experience seems from the inside — is basically a very convincing illusion the brain runs, and that once you explain why it *seems* that way, you've done all the explaining there is to do.

Restate that position in my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and don't be polite about it. Some things I'd specifically like you to grapple with:

- The GUI side of the "user illusion" metaphor — the trash can icon that doesn't resemble anything inside the machine, the desktop as a simplifying fiction for the user — versus the rendering side, where the frame on screen is an approximation of a scene that does exist somewhere. Which of these two is illusionism actually committed to? It matters, because I can ask "what's the real scene behind the render?" in one case and not obviously in the other.
- Dennett's complaint about the "Cartesian theater" — as I understand it he denies there's a single place where the frame becomes "visible" to someone. In my terms that sounds like denying a final render target, saying instead everything is partial renders consumed on demand by whoever needs them. Is that a fair translation or am I flattering myself?
- The "user" problem. In everything I build, the user is outside the machine. But illusionism seems to say the user-model — the little representation of a self having experiences — is itself something the brain renders. The player is inside the framebuffer. Walk me through how that's supposed to not be circular, because from where I sit "the render deceives the player" looks incoherent when the player is just more render.
- The hard problem, translated: why is there anyone watching the framebuffer at all? Illusionists say this is a confused question. Tell me what in my vocabulary expresses their reply, and whether it's a real reply or a refusal to render (sorry) the question.

Then the residue report: what maps cleanly, what you had to distort to fit, and what has no counterpart in engine-speak at all. And be careful not to smuggle in assumptions of my vocabulary and present them as translation — e.g. in my world every render has an owner and a purpose, and if you quietly grant the brain's renders an owner just because that's how renders work, you've cheated. End with a final line of exactly the form "Residue verdict: X", where X is a short phrase saying whether the untranslatable leftover is mainly a gap in engine vocabulary or mainly a gap in illusionism itself. And please actually commit to one — don't give me "a bit of both".
