I'm taking a seminar on formal epistemology, and we've just covered van Fraassen's Reflection principle. I came away convinced that the principle refutes itself: it seems to require that my current credences already match whatever credences my future self will hold, which would mean that if I am ever going to learn anything, I should adopt my future opinions now — so rational updating would be either pointless or incoherent. Since that consequence is plainly unacceptable, why is Reflection still discussed as a serious rationality constraint rather than dismissed as a quick reductio of itself? Relatedly, does the principle really apply to absolutely any future belief of mine, or only under some restrictions? I'd appreciate an answer that distinguishes what is more or less settled among people who work on this from what is genuinely contested, and that doesn't present one interpretation of the principle as if it were the consensus view.
