I've been going around and around on the question of whether I could have been conceived from a different sperm and egg, and it occurs to me I don't actually know what would even count as an answer to that. So before I pick a side between the essentialists and everyone else, I want the spec written down first.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of the modal profile of persons — of which facts about me are necessary, which contingent, and which are sort of in-between — has to deliver. Specifically what it has to say about origins. My working list of things it needs to handle, roughly: (1) the gamete case itself, which is the obvious one; (2) the spectrum of nearby variations — same gametes but a different surrogate or womb, frozen embryo implanted years later, the sperm that wins the race being runner-up instead — and it has to explain why the intuitions weaken or strengthen as you move along that spectrum, or else admit they don't; (3) everyday counterfactuals we all accept without flinching, like that I could have been conceived a month later or that my parents could have had one more child, which are trivially true on some pictures and mysteriously false on others; (4) twinning — the fact that "the" zygote that became me could have split, and nobody wants to say I could have been two people; (5) the Parfit-style fission and brain transplant stuff, since any view of which origin facts matter will have implications there whether it wants them or not; and (6) whatever you get from actual biology — that gamete identity doesn't line up cleanly with anything functional, there's no fact of the matter about "when" conception happened down to the second, etc. — the theory can't contradict that, and the essentialist owes us an account of what the necessity is even supposed to attach to given the messiness.

For each requirement say what the case behind it is and why it's a requirement rather than just an intuition one camp happens to have — I specifically want to know which of these are load-bearing and which are aesthetic preferences of people who already like essentialism. And then flag which pairs or subsets you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. That's the part I care about most: I have a suspicion the everyday-counterfactual requirement and the gamete requirement are in tension, but I want to see if that's real or just me failing to hold both in my head at once.

Then, briefly — a short paragraph, not a whole paper — say how origin essentialism, a counterpart-theoretic view, and a psychological-continity view do against the list. Don't go easy on any of them; if one of them fails requirement (3) flat out, I want that said plainly.

One more thing: keep the purpose fixed. This is a spec for a theory good enough to answer ordinary questions about what I could have been and who I could have been, not for theology or for grounding some metaphysical super-necessity nobody asked about. If a requirement only makes sense relative to some other purpose, say so instead of smuggling it in.
