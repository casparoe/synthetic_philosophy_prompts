I run a lunch seminar for the data science team at my company — smart people, no fairness background, and most of them quietly think "fair ML" is just a data cleaning problem. Next session I have 30 minutes and a whiteboard to teach them the impossibility result that when two groups have different base rates, you can't in general have both group-wise calibration and equal error rates across groups unless your predictor is close to perfect. I want to avoid the COMPAS angle entirely — they've all half-seen it and it just makes them defensive.

Don't write the lecture for me — plan it. What has to be in place before the tension is even visible (I suspect they need to see calibration and error rates as genuinely different claims about the same classifier first)? Should the carrying example be medical screening with two populations of different prevalence, or something about loans, and what does the right example need to have and not have? The misconceptions will come in some order — my guesses are "this is really about biased training data" and then "more data fixes it" — so where in the sequence should each one get killed, and how do I do it without derailing into a debate about the training data? What should I openly refuse to cover in half an hour and say so? And give me one question to ask at the end that seperates the people who actually got the impossibility from the people who just came away with "fairness is hard."
