okay so I'm writing this novel -- literary fiction, no wizards, no Borges pastiche -- and I've got a chapter where two characters stumble on a sentence that breaks truth. One is a retired logician, the other is a notary, and they argue about whether the notary can honestly certify a document. But I don't want the lame version where somebody just writes "this sentence is false" on a wall, the logician character would roll his eyes at that. So build me the scenario to spec. Constraints: (1) no sentence gets to refer to itself directly -- no "this sentence," no naming itself -- the paradox has to come out of a loop between at least two separate inscriptions, think A says B is true and B says A is not true; (2) it has to arise naturally in an ordinary setting, like a codicil to a will, a plaque, a footnote in a translation, that kind of thing, and the characters have to *discover* the loop rather than have it announced to them; (3) at least one of the inscriptions should say "not true" rather than "false," so the case isn't trivially handled. Realistic stakes, no oracles, no infinite libraries.

After the scenario, do the check. Walk through what a Tarski-style hierarchy of languages says about it -- can the loop even be sorted into levels, given that the inscriptions are ordinary sentences in one document? Then what a paracomplete/gap-theorist says, then what a dialetheist says. Tell me specifically where the three come apart: which inscriptions each view allows the notary to assert or sign off on, if any. Then tell me which feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens if I change one inscription so it quotes the other sentence verbatim instead of talking about it. And be honest if some constraint I've imposed makes the case impossible to pull off in a realistic setting -- I'd rather know than have you quietly smuggle in a demon.

The reason I need this to actually work is that it's dialogue material: the whole chapter is the two of them fighting about whether the notary can stamp the thing, so a scenario that just stipulates "and then truth broke" without the theories generating genuinely different verdicts is useless to me. If it helps the plot you can make one of the inscriptions be about sentences in a different language than the one it's written in, but only if that doesn't make the whole thing too cute.
