My brother and I have been arguing for years about whether people can trust what they find when they look inward. He says you can just notice what you're thinking and feeling, and why, the way you notice the color of a wall. I keep catching myself producing fluent reasons for things only after I've already done them, and I suspect everyone does this more than they admit. I want to settle this with something concrete instead of trading assertions, so I'd like a quiz for general readers — no philosophy background, no jargon in the questions themselves.

Please write an eight-to-ten-question quiz along the lines of: how much do you trust your own self-reports? Each question should be a short concrete case addressed to the reader — for instance, being asked why you preferred one job candidate over another and giving a confident answer, then wondering whether it matches what actually moved you; realizing you were angry at a friend only after noticing you'd been short with them all afternoon; being sure you'd notice if a passing mood had tilted your judgment about someone; explaining why you took a different route home than usual. Two or three answer options per question, each stating a stance on how much the person's own report can be trusted in that specific case — never a school name or a label.

Then give a scoring key. Build it from the distinctions first, not from a list of famous positions: whether introspection is direct and normally accurate about one's current mental states; whether that accuracy extends to the causes of those states, like motives and reasons; whether introspection is a kind of perception at all, or rather a story we construct that sometimes gets checked against our own behavior; and what follows about whether self-reports should be treated like testimony from a possibly biased witness. Locate the actual named positions in that space where you can, flag any combination the distinctions allow that no real position occupies, and say roughly where my brother's view and mine would land.

Finally, apply your scoring key to this answer pattern: on questions about giving reasons for choices, the respondent trusts their own report completely; on the question about noticing anger late, they say they'd trust a friend's observation over their own at the time; on questions about whether they'd notice a mood or a first impression influencing a judgment, they say yes, they would; on questions about explaining small everyday actions, they say their explanations are probably right. End your response with \answer{...} containing only the leaf label your key assigns to that pattern.
