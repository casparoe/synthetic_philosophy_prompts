I am a doctoral student in epistemology and I produce the interview series run by my department's graduate journal, in which a visiting philosopher is questioned for an hour before a small live audience. Our next guest is a defender of infinitism about epistemic justification — roughly, the view that since any finite chain of reasons leaves its final member unjustified, justification must take the form of an endless series of reasons, each supported by further reasons, with no privileged stopping point. She has argued in print that both foundationalism and coherentism are motivated by despair over our cognitive limitations rather than by the actual structure of justification. The subject has become awkwardly personal for me: the second chapter of my dissertation defends a modest coherentism, and my supervisor has spent the year pressing exactly the regress arguments this guest wields, so I want the interview to serve as genuine reconnaissance rather than a friendly restatement of her position.

Draft nine to twelve questions for this interview. They should press where the literature presses rather than merely ask for exposition, and each should anticipate her most likely reply with a keyed follow-up. At a minimum, I want one question each on: the objection from human finitude, that nobody can actually entertain infinitely many reasons, together with her apparent reliance on a distinction between reasons one possesses and reasons that are merely available to one; the distinction between linear and non-linear chains — if nothing forbids a reason from being supported by the very reasons it supports, why isn't an endlessly looping series just coherentism with extra steps, and where precisely does she draw the line between a non-linear infinite series and a vicious circle; the worry that longer chains dilute rather than transmit justification, so that infinitism buys its solution to the regress at the price of never crossing whatever threshold warrants believing anything; and the analogy she has drawn with non-well-founded set theory, where sets may be members of themselves and infinitely descending membership chains are permitted — one question should ask whether that analogy does any real dialectical work or is decoration, with a follow-up about what would happen to her position if the anti-foundation axioms had turned out to be inconsistent.

Please keep the questions formal enough to read aloud to an audience, avoid the generic sort about how she came to hold the view or who influenced her, and open the sequence with exactly one question that lets her state the position cleanly for listeners who have never heard of infinitism — but frame even that one so it ends by asking her to name, in her own terms, the single strongest objection to her view.
