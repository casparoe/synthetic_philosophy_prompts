My friend won't stop going on about Wigner's unreasonable effectiveness paper and after months of arguing with him I finally sat down and tried to write out what I actually think about the whole thing. Before I send this to him can somebody check whether it hangs together? Heres the package:

(1) I'm a platonist about math. Structures and theorems are out there, we discover them, we don't invent them.

(2) I think the Wigner "puzzle" is overrated as a puzzle. Given the enormous space of mathematical structures that exist (which I believe in because of (1)), it would be way weirder if the physical world didn't instantiate a whole bunch of them somewhere. So math applies to nature -- fine, nothing much to explain there.

(3) On the other hand I do think mathematical beauty -- elegance, symmetry, economy -- is a genuinely truth-tracking guide in fundamental physics. People like Dirac trusted elegance and it kept working out way too many times to be luck. When physicists pick the beautiful structure and it then predicts stuff nobody had measured, I think that's telling us something deep about the world.

(4) Despite that I'm a string theory skeptic. Forty years of gorgeous math and zero testable predictions means it shouldn't count as physics, and I get suspicious of anyone who treats its beauty as evidence it's true.

(5) Math itself is justified a priori. Applications confer zero confirmation on mathematics -- number theory wasn't sitting around waiting for cryptography to validate it, and the fact that quantum mechanics happens to use complex Hilbert spaces adds nothing, epistemically, to the math itself.

So, audit time: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they conflict, and where's the cheapest fix? I honestly can't tell if (2) and (3) can both be right, or if (4) is just me being grumpy in a way that contradicts (3), or whether any of this touches (1) or (5) at all. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each real one say what giving up the offending commitment would cost me elsewhere in the set. And if some of these tensions dissolve on closer inspection, say so -- I don't want to "fix" something that was never broken.
