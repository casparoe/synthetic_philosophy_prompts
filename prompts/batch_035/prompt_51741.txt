Write an essay on whether it can be rational to be irrational, taking Parfit's hitchhiker as the central case. Begin by laying out the standard setup in your own terms: a stranded hitchhiker promises to pay the driver once delivered to the city; the driver stops only because she predicts the promise will be kept; once in the city, paying is causally worse than keeping the money. Take a position on whether the hitchhiker should pay, and defend it against the strongest opposing argument rather than a strawman.

Your essay should then give sustained attention to a variant I'll call the diary hitchhiker. In this variant, the hitchhiker has kept a public, timestamped diary for years in which he records, sincerely and in detail, how he intends to act in each kind of situation he might face; motorists in the region are known to read such diaries before picking anyone up, and that fact is part of why he started keeping one; and the diary entry covering promises made under duress of being stranded says he would pay. The driver reads the diary before deciding to stop. He could not have foreseen this particular stranding when he wrote the entry, but he did foresee that some stranding of this general kind was possible.

Your essay must address three questions about this variant. First, is the diary hitchhiker's payment better captured by causal or by evidential decision theory, and does that distinction even cut at a real joint once diary-keeping is in place — or does the diary show that the two theories converge when agents can shape the evidence their future acts will constitute? Second, if keeping the diary is rational while paying after the driver has already stopped appears causally irrational, is rationality properly evaluated at the level of individual acts, standing dispositions, or policies chosen in advance — and which level is the right one, on what grounds? Third, does the variant support the thought that so-called rational irrationality is just rationality operating at a coarser temporal grain, or does it instead reveal that the notion of a single well-defined decision point is doing illegitimate work in the debate?

Reach a definite verdict on whether the hitchhiker in the original scenario — no diary, no advance machinery — should pay, and explain how much of your answer depends on the decision-theoretic framework you adopt versus how much survives framework-neutral. Do not end on a non-committal note.
