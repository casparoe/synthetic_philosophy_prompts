A philosophy podcast I caught explained Lewis's triviality results roughly like this: Lewis wanted to say that your degree of belief in a conditional 'if A then C' should just equal your conditional probablity of C given A, but then he proved this can't work — no proposition can have a conditional probability as its probability, and if you insist on the equation anyway, your entire probability function collapses into something trivial where there's basically nothing left to be uncertain about. The host then said that since conditionals include counterfactuals like 'if the match had been struck it would have lit,' the result also took down Lewis's own possible worlds semantics for those, which is why Lewis threw in the towel and admitted conditionals have no truth values at all. He finished by calling it one of the rare cases of a genuine proof in philosophy and said it basically settled the debate, since noone has a good reply. I'm inclined to believe the core is right (it's a proof after all), but the counterfactual part sounded off and the 'settled, no replies' bit felt too tidy.
