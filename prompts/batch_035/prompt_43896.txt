So I'm a professor who's been teaching the consequentialism seminar for years, and every fall some student raises the demandingness objection like it's a mic drop and every fall I mutter the same thing: it's not one objection, it's like three objections wearing a trenchcoat. And now I've finally got a PhD student who wants to write her dissertation chapter on it, but her current draft question is just "is consequentialism too demanding," which is unanswerable as stated. My half-formed angle, which I want to hand her as a workable proposal: the debate conflates how much sacrifice a theory demands (resources, welfare), how much it demands of your life structure (whether projects and relationships survive), and how much it demands of your agency (whether you're always required to defer your own moral judgment to a calculation). These can come apart — a theory could be modest on sacrifice but totalitarian about agency, or vice versa — and I suspect the standard replies to the demandingness objection (indirect consequentialism, satisficing, the appeal to disaster-prevention thresholds) each answer only one of the three while looking like they answer all of them. I don't want her to defend consequentialism or attack it, I want her to taxonomize and then test whether the taxonomy does any real work, i.e. whether classifying objections and replies this way actually predicts which pairs of objection and reply talk past each other in the literature.

Please write me the proposal I can hand to her — no bibliography, she'll slot references in herself, and don't invent any either. Since she processes documents better as short structured points, and honestly so do I at this point in my career, give it to me as a numbered list of exactly five items, each item only a sentence or two, no introduction and no conclusion. The five items should be: the question stated precisely enough to have a determinate answer, why the existing positions leave it open, the method she'd actually follow month by month, what would count as the project failing, and the realistic scope for what I gather will be one semester of her time before she has to defend the prospectus to the committee. If you think one of my three-way distinctions collapses into another under pressure, say so in the relevant item rather than flattering me — I'd rather find out now than in front of the committee.
