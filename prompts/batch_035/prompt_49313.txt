I got diagnosed with ADHD at 38 a few months ago, after a childhood of being "bright but doesn't apply herself," and since then I've been hanging out in alot of online ADHD communities. One phrase that gets thrown around there constantly comes from Ian Hacking: that psychiatric classification is a case of "making up people." Last week someone used it to brush off my question about whether I "really" had ADHD back in the 90s, when the adult diagnosis barely existed anywhere near my town and nobody would have known what to call me. Their answer was that the question is confused: the kind wasn't around yet, so I couldn't have been a member of it, end of story. That felt too quick but I couldn't put my finger on why. Can you lay out the different precise claims "making up people" could be standing for — the boring ones that are obviously true (the labels change how the people classified see themselves and act, the famous "looping effect"), the substantive ones (the kinds themselves only exist because of the classifying practices, or truths about people in the kind are somehow made true by those practices), and the genuinely radical ones — and for each reading say whether it's plausible and, more importantly, whether it actually supports dismissing my question? Maybe the honest answer to my question is something like "yes, but unnamed" or "you had the underlying condition but not the social role," and I'd love a menu of readings I can bring back to the group chat.
