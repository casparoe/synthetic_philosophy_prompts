so for my philosophy elective we got assigned weird ethical theories to argue against, and I ended up with this thing called lexical threshold negative utilitarianism. the way our teacher explained it: pains above some intensity threshold are lexically worse than everything below it, so preventing even one above-threshold pain matters more than preventing any amount of mild pain or creating any amount of pleasure, no matter how enormous. so one migraine is worse than a billion pinpricks, and no amount of bliss makes up for the migraine even a little.

my friend (who thinks he's a genius now) says the theory contradicts itself and he wrote up this argument to prove it:

(1) according to the theory, no amount of pleasure can ever compensate for an above-threshold pain. that's just what the threshold means.

(2) if no amount of pleasure can compensate for a pain, then pleasure and that pain are incommensurable — there's no exchange rate between them at all.

(3) if two things are incommensurable, then no comparison between them holds. it's not that one is bigger, it's that "bigger than" doesn't even apply.

(4) but the theory itself has to make comparisons — saying an above-threshold pain takes priority over every pleasure just IS saying the pain is worse than every pleasure.

(5) so the theory both asserts and denies that the comparison holds. contradiction, it refutes itself.

(6) and the kicker: the same argument works against anyone who thinks ANYTHING is infinitely more important than anything else. like people who say one death is worse than any number of broken fingernails. so if he's right, all lexical priority views are incoherent, not just this one.

he's clearly really pleased with himself but something feels off. like "compensate" seems to mean different things in different steps? compensating for a pain sounds like it could mean "trade off against it so the total comes out fine" which is maybe not the same as "compare to it at all". also why should incommensurability mean NO comparisons (3) instead of just no equal trades — some people say two things can be incomparable without being incomparable, ugh i dont even know if im using those words right. anyway I'd like to keep the threshold idea if possible bc the alternative (a billion pinpricks really being worse than one migraine) seems nuts too, and my grade sort of depends on finding a real objection and not a fake one. which step should i attack, and what do i end up paying if i do?
