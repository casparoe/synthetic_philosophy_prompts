My two friends have been arguing for over a year about why promises bind, and it goes nowhere because they keep talking past eachother. One is a Humean about it — she thinks promising is an artificial convention and the obligation is just the pressure of the practice plus mutual advantage, roughly on the model of Hume's two rowers keeping in time with each other. The other is sold on the normative-powers view — when you promise you exercise a power to change your own reasons, the way consent or commanding a subordinate does, and the binding force doesn't come from the practice being useful. Write the joint statement they should produce after a proper adversarial collaboration. I want: a numbered section of claims both could sign (I suspect they agree on more than they think — e.g. that a promise can bind even when breach would go completely unnoticed, and that the practice of promising is historically conventional); then a disagreements table where each row states one disagreement in wording both sides would accept — I'm guessing the big ones are the bootstrapping worry (if I can create a reason for myself just by willing it, why can't I give myself a reason to nap by saying so?) and what happens to the obligation when the mutual-advantage story fails, like a promise made to a dying man on a desert island where there's no ongoing practice to uphold. Then list the cruxes, and for each crux one concrete case or thought experiment both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Finally flag which of their disagreements you think are actually verbal. Keep it honest — don't let either side smuggle its assumptions into the agreed-claims section.
