Write a satirical job advertisement, roughly 650 words, from a fictional company — call it Pavlov Systems, a lab that trains reinforcement-learning agents for warehouse robotics — advertising the new position of "Machine Welfare Officer." The humor must come from getting the philosophy right rather than from invented absurdities: the ad should quietly run on the conflation of reward with valence, so that every "proof" the lab offers of its agents' wellbeing is just a description of its own training pipeline, delivered as though it were an ethical finding. Work in, without necessarily naming any of them: the treatment of negative reward as a mere credit-assignment signal rather than a candidate for pain; the lab's "positive-only environment," achieved by clipping all rewards at zero so that nothing is ever negatively reinforced (and hence, they conclude, nothing is ever suffered); the inference problem of distinguishing avoidance behavior from aversive experience, which the ad resolves by declaring the problem solved; wireheading repackaged as an "engagement" or "self-actualization" success story; the discount factor applied to the agent's future wellbeing, such that the further away the harm, the less it officially counts; and a nod to nociception without consciousness, phrased as reassurance. Use the standard furniture of the genre — an "About Us" section, Responsibilities, Required Qualifications, Nice to Haves, Compensation & Benefits — and keep it deadpan throughout: a skimming reader should find it a plausible corporate posting, while someone who knows the machine-welfare literature should laugh rather than wince. Include at least one testimonial quote from a fictional departing employee or concerned board member, whose objection must be stated fairly rather than strawmanned, and one parenthetical noting that questions about the inner lives of the agents should be routed to Legal. The satire's target is the lab's motivated reasoning — the practice of defining the question of machine suffering away by re-describing the engineering — not the seriousness of machine welfare itself, which the ad should ostentatiously affirm at every opportunity. End with the standard line about being an equal-opportunity employer, amended in some way that keeps the joke going.
