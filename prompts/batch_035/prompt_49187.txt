Construct a rescue scenario in which the view that the numbers count and the view that we should minimize aggregate harm come apart in a particular direction: a case where saving the larger group is what the numbers-counts view demands, while the smaller group faces by far the greater total loss. For instance, several people each at risk of a minor permanent injury against a single person at risk of death — though you are free to choose your own harms, provided the aggregate clearly favors the one. Constraints: the setting should be realistic (an emergency room, a rescue helicopter, a flooded town — no trolleys, no runaway trains), the scenario itself should be one paragraph, and the case's purpose should be kept in view, which is to test whether "the numbers count" can mean anything other than "sum the harms and pick the side with the larger total." After the scenario, walk through what each of three positions says about it: straightforward aggregation, the Taurek-style view that the numbers give no reason to prefer the many, and the intermediate view that numbers matter without anything being summed across persons. Then explain which feature of the case is doing the work; what happens to the verdicts if that feature is softened (say, if the many each faced death and the one faced permanent paralysis); and how a committed aggregator might resist the case — perhaps by denying that harms of very different magnitudes can simply be added together. Do not stipulate the verdicts: each must fall out of the theories themselves. Please answer in American English.
