I've been going down the Pascal's wager rabbit hole for a while now (no philosophy background, just arguing with people online), and I've noticed the debate never actually moves. Someone raises the many-gods objection — the wager proves too much, since Zeus or a god who rewards skeptics gets the same infinite payoff — and the reply is always to start assigning probabilities to the various gods, or ruling out the "wrong" kinds of gods somehow, and both sides end up exactly where they started. My hunch is the fight was never really about religion at all. It's about whether decision theory can say anything once you put an infinity in the table. The same stall happens with the St Petersburg lottery, where there's no theology involved: some people say you should be willing to pay any finite price to play, others say the setup just breaks expected utility and that's fine, math is allowed to have edge cases. And someone in a forum once pointed out to me that even if you grant the wager everything, you could mix strategies — bet on God only sometimes, with some tiny probability — and still get infinite expected value, which to me sounds like the argument destroying itself instead of guiding anyone. So what kind of disagreement is this, really? Substantive, or are the two camps answering different questions under one name — one asking "what should I do with my life," the other asking "is this even a valid piece of decision theory"? And given your diagnosis, is there anything — a fix to how we handle infinities, a distinction, whatever — that would count as actual progress, or is this one just permanently stuck?
