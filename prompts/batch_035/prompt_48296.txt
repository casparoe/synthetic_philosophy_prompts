From what I understand the idea is: if some external thing does for you exactly what biological memory does — some guy carries a notebook everywhere and looks stuff up in it the way you'd just remember it — then the notebook isn't a tool you're using, its literally part of your mind. There's a parity principle, plus some extra conditions, like the thing has to be reliably there and you have to actually consult it without thinking, or else it's just a tool. He says there's a famous thought experiment about a guy named Otto with Alzheimer's whose notebook plays the role his memory can't, versus a woman named Inga who just remembers. Whatever — he definately talks about it too much, but he did make me notice our whole industry is quietly betting on this being true.
