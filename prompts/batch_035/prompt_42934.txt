My roommate is a moral particularist and last night, over truly terrible noodles, he walked me into an argument I can't get out of. It goes: (1) a moral principle, to count as a principle at all, has to pick out some consideration -- lying, promise-keeping, causing pain -- and say it always makes the same contribution to whether an act is right, wherever it turns up. Call that the invariance requirement. (2) But for any candidate principle you can cook up a case where the consideration flips: a lie that saves a life, a promise whose keeping ruins someone, pain that's exactly what the patient asked for. (3) So there are no true moral principles, and moral judgment is just seeing the whole situation, case by case. That part I half buy, honestly. What bugs me is what he said when I pushed him: (4) the argument is completely general, so it applies to what to believe, not just what to do. "Trust your senses," "prefer the simpler theory," "accept testimony unless you have reason not to" -- each of those has cases where the consideration flips (illusions, misleadingly simple wrong theories, lying witnesses). (5) So by his own lights there are no epistemic principles either, and he just shrugged and said fine, good reasoning is also seeing the case as a whole.

Here's why I think this proves to much somewhere. If nothing general survives, I don't see how his own argument survives, because it runs on the maximally general claim that every principle has a flipping case -- which looks a lot like a principle about principles, with no exceptions allowed. But I can't locate the bad step. I'd like to keep (2), the cases all seem real to me. And I vaguely know generalists have things like W.D. Ross's prima facie duties -- does that dodge the invariance requirement, or does it just restate the problem one level up ("prima facie duty" starts to sound like a principle about how considerations normally contribute, which the flipping cases were supposed to forbid)? Tell me which premise or step to reject and what rejecting it costs, including whether the generalist escape routes are real escapes or just relabeling.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble and no restating my question back at me.
