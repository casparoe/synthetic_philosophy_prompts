writing up a position statement for a grad seminar on adaptationism and want someone to poke holes in it before I send it around. here's roughly where I stand:

(1) I'm an adaptationist — for any complex organismal trait, the default assumption should be that selection built it, and I think "panselectionist" is mostly just an insult people reach for when they've run out of arguments.

(2) I also think Gould and Lewontin's spandrels paper is one of the best things ever written in the field and basically right about byproducts and developmental constraints.

(3) some case verdicts: I think the human chin is a structural byproduct of jaw growth and needs no adaptive story at all, but I think the female orgasm is a genuine adaptation with a real selectionist account, and I find it annoying when people call the latter a byproduct of male biology — that always strikes me as question-begging.

(4) I'm fine with Kimura — most molecular variation really is drift, nearly neutral theory basically won that one.

(5) I think "just-so story" is a lazy criticism, and the right way to evaluate an adaptive hypothesis is to compare it against alternatives, not to sneer at it.

Do these hang together? Which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix for each? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as all equally bad, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Don't be polite about it.
