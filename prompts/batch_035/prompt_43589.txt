I was raised in a pretty devout evangelical home and walked away from all of it in my mid-twenties, and ever since I keep getting pulled into arguments from both directions: my brother-in-law is one of those "I only believe things there's evidence for" guys, and my best friend from my church days thinks faith can be legitimate even without proof. A few weeks ago I finally sat down and read Clifford's old essay (the one with the shipowner) and James's reply to it, and I realized I have no idea where I actually stand — my intuitions go different directions depending on the scenario. Clifford's bit about the shipowner who talks himself into believing his old boat is seaworthy and then collects the insurance when it sinks really got under my skin, especially the part that "he had no right to believe on such evidence as was before him" — the guy was completely sincere and it didn't help him at all. But James's comeback, that sometimes the evidence can't settle things and you're forced to choose anyway, so letting your needs tip the scale can be legitimate, also rings true for some things I've actually lived through.

So here's what I want: a "which ethics of belief do you actually hold?" quiz, the kind where all the questions are little concrete scenarios and the answers sort you into a position, not the kind where you just pick the label you like. Around ten questions, two or three answer options each. Some cases I'd want in there somewhere, because these are the ones that break my intuitions apart: a coach who gets better performance out of the team by genuinely beleiving they can win; telling a terminally ill relative to stay hopeful because optimistic patients sometimes do better; a friend trusting her husband isn't cheating when the evidence is genuinely 50/50; Pascal-style betting on God; a juror who thinks the defendant is probably guilty but not beyond reasonable doubt; my old pastor's line that belief in God is more like trusting a person than weighing evidence; and the free will question, whether you're better off believing you have it regardless of what the science says. The leaves should at least include Clifford-style evidentialism, James's view (with his live/forced/momentous conditions doing actual work — at least one question should test whether someone respects those limits or just thinks believing whatever helps is fine), the plain "you can choose to believe things on purpose" voluntarism, the "faith is a different thing from evidence-based belief" view my pastor had, and a leaf for people who think beliefs are never really chosen so the whole ethics question is confused from the start. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the leaves, plus two honest notes: any pair of leaves your quiz can't actually separate, and any answer combination that fits no leaf (say whether that's because nobody holds that view or because you couldn't reach it).

Once the quiz is built, run me through it: for each question give your best guess at how I'd answer based on everything I said above, then give your diagnosis. End with a single last line in exactly this form: \answer{position name} — the name of the leaf I land on, and if I'm a genuine hybrid, name the two leaves I'm straddling with an "or" between them. No extra text after that line.
