I'm a graduate student in computer science, and I've been in a running argument with a friend about whether large language models are intelligent. My side of it has been mostly pointing at behavior: they generalize to problems unlike anything in their training data, they catch their own mistakes mid-solution, they fail in ways that look like slips rather than gaps. Every time I try to press one of these points, he cuts me off with a line made famous by the psychologist Edwin Boring in 1923: "Intelligence is what the tests test." Since no language model sits an IQ test under standard conditions, he says, my question is settled by definition, and everything I've been calling evidence is just me misusing a word. He treats the line as a knockdown move, and my sense is that he's getting far more out of it than it contains, but I can't put my finger on where the extra content creeps in.

Please take the slogan apart for me. Lay out, as a menu I can use the next time this comes up, the distinct precise claims the sentence could be expressing — a harmless bookkeeping convention that reserves the term for test scores; a methodological proposal to define the construct operationally; an empirical thesis that performance on diverse mental tasks has a strong common factor and IQ tests track it; and any stronger metaphysical reading on which there is simply nothing to intelligence beyond test performance. For each reading, say whether it is trivial, defensible but contested, or plain false, and — this is the part I need most — whether it would actually license my friend's move of dismissing my question by definition, given that the systems under discussion aren't test-takers at all. One more thing I'd like folded in: I've read that Boring offered the definition as deliberately narrow and provisional, explicitly narrower than the ordinary concept, with the hope that further observation would let it be extended. If that's right, it would be a nice twist to close on, since it suggests the quip's author was doing almost the opposite with it of what my friend is doing.
