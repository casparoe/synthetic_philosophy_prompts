It went roughly like this: 'The verificationist says all truths are knowable. Fitch's trick is to take a truth of the form "p is true but nobody knows p." But it's impossible to know it, since knowing it would involve knowing p, and once you know p, "nobody knows p" is false — so knowledge would have something false as a consequence, which can't happen. Hence the knowability thesis forces the absurd conclusion that all truths are already known, and verificationism is hereby refuted, along with Dummett's anti-realism. The argument does lean on the KK principle — that if you know something, you know that you know it — but that principle is secure enough.'

Is this fair? Which parts would a logician nod along to, which parts are subtly off, and does the "verificationism is hereby refuted" punchline survive once you fix those parts?
