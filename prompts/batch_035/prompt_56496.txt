Prompt (for your dataset):

For years I've been a fairly orthodox Bayesian about updating, and lately I've been teaching myself the literature on the Judy Benjamin problem to check whether my package survives it. I want a consistency audit, because I suspect something in my list has to give, but I genuinely can't see which item is the odd one out.

First, the case, so we're on the same page: a soldier parachutes at night into enemy territory. From maps, the territory is split evenly between Blue and Red sides, and Blue territory in turn is split evenly into Blue-held and Red-held areas; with a uniform prior, her credence in Blue territory is 1/2, and conditional on Blue, 1/2 in Blue-held. Radio contact tells her: if you are in Blue territory, the odds are 3 to 1 that you are in the Blue-held rather than the Red-held portion. Nothing is said about the Red side.

Here are my commitments:

1. Update rules: when evidence makes you certain of a proposition, strict conditionalization; when evidence shifts probabilities without certainty, Jeffrey conditioning. I see no need for a third update rule beyond these.

2. Epistemic conservatism: rational updating should disturb prior credences as little as the evidence requires. I take this to be the deep justification for Jeffrey conditioning -- it is the minimal-change update for its kind of input.

3. The radio message is not a material conditional. Conditioning on the negation of Blue or Blue-held would absurdly raise her credence in the Red side, since Red territory verifies the material conditional wholesale. So the material-conditional formalization is out.

4. The verdict: after the message, her credence in being in Blue territory at all should remain exactly 1/2. Only the split within Blue should move, to 3:1. I hold this firmly and I think there is a fact of the matter about it.

5. Representation invariance: the rational posterior should not depend on how the agent happens to partition or describe the possibility space. An update rule whose output changes when you redescribe the same situation in different but equally natural terms is disqualified.

Where I suspect trouble: minimum relative entropy updating, which is the standard machinery people reach for when the evidence is a conditional, gives the wrong answer in this case -- it drags her credence in Blue territory below 1/2, which violates commitment 4. So I'm inclined to reject entropy-minimization as an illegitimate pseudo-Bayesian trick. But then I notice that Jeffrey conditioning, which I endorsed in commitment 1 and justified in commitment 2, is itself just relative-entropy minimization relative to a fixed partition. Am I rejecting a principle while keeping its special case? And if I keep Jeffrey conditioning but forbid the entropy machinery, do I have any way at all to handle evidence stated as a conditional probability rather than as a cell probability -- or does commitment 1 secretly contain a gap exactly the size of the Judy Benjamin problem? Finally, a friend claims that whether the entropy-style answer comes out as my favored 1/2 or as something below 1/2 depends on how richly you formalize the radio message as a constraint, which sounds to me like it threatens commitment 5, but I can't tell whether that's a real tension or him being slippery.

So: go through the five commitments, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and what the cheapest repair is and what it costs me elsewhere. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damning, and if you think one single revision would clean up the most, say which and why.
