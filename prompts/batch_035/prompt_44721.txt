I hold two epistemic positions jointly, and I've come to suspect I hold them because they're axioms of my subculture rather than because I've examined them. The first is the KK principle: if I know that p, then I am in a position to know that I know that p. The second, related, is a limited luminosity claim: for a decent range of my own mental states — believing rather than doubting, feeling confident rather than unsure, being cold rather than warm on a question — being in the state puts me in a position to know I'm in it. Together they underwrite a lot of how I and the people I argue with actually operate: calibrated introspection, the idea that knowing and merely thinking you know are states you can usually distinguish from the inside, the practice of treating "I know" as an input to further reasoning rather than as something requiring independent verification.

Tell me what would count against this package. I know the neighborhood contains Williamson-style margin-for-error reasoning — that nearby cases can't be perfectly discriminated, so a state of "seeming to be in the state" can't always co-occur with knowing you're in it — and I want to know how much weight that argument can really bear, whether it generalizes from the blurry perceptual cases it's usually run on to the introspective cases I care about, and whether the many-small-models version of the objection (your self-model is itself a model, and models are lossy) is a different point or the same one. But go beyond it: what empirical results, what possible cases, what theoretical considerations elsewhere in epistemology should move me? Which of these would a committed defender of KK or luminosity concede as counting against the view, and which would they dismiss as resting on a mistaken conception of knowledge? I'd also like a judgment on whether the views can be qualified into safety — retreating from all states to paradigmatic ones, from "always knowable" to "normally knowable" — without the retreat being a disguised abandonment of what made the view worth holding, and whether the anti-KK position carries its own costs, e.g. about whether an agent can use its own knowledge without an extra layer of self-trust that looks suspiciously like KK coming back in disguise. Rank the defeaters from fatal through serious to merely awkward, and be honest about how exposed my package actually is.
