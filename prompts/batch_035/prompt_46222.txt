I'm writing the methods section for a historiography seminar paper on explanations of the outbreak of the First World War, and my advisor told me to lay out my actual assumptions instead of smuggling them in. So I've written out this set of commitments I've been working with, and honestly I think it hangs together, but a couple of people in my cohort insist it can't. Here it is:

(1) When I evaluate decisions — the statesmen of July 1914, say — I only judge them relative to the information available at the time. The question is what a reasonable person, knowing only what they knew then, could have expected. What we know now about how things turned out is off the table as evidence about the quality of their judgment.

(2) Hindsight bias, properly speaking, is a memory phenomenon — Fischhoff's subjects, once told the outcome, misremember having predicted it. Since the historian sitting in the archive already knows the outcome and isn't misremembering anything, hindsight bias in that strict sense isn't really the historian's own problem; it's a problem for memoirists and witnesses whose testimony we have to discount.

(3) The historian's real danger is what Taleb calls the narrative fallacy: we compress the messy past into a coherent causal story, so the outcome ends up looking like it couldn't have gone any other way.

(4) Relatedly, I prefer structural explanations (alliance blocs, economic pressures, rail timetables, geography) over decision-centered ones, because structural facts don't depend on anyone's point of view — that makes them more objective, and less exposed to hindsight-style distortion, than reconstructions of what was going on in some minister's head.

(5) Whether something was obvious at the time should be tested empirically: look for contemporaries who actually predicted it. If nobody predicted it, it wasn't obvious, and later claims that it should have been seen coming are just retrospective inflation.

So: which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and how bad is each one? For each real tension tell me what would have to give and what it costs me, and rank them by severity — I'd rather know which one to fix first than get a flat list. I genuinely don't know where the bodies are buried, if any, so don't go easy on any of them.
