I am a retired experimental biologist — thirty-odd years of designing field trials, running randomizations, and partitioning variance — and I have lately been reading political philosophy in the evenings, which is how I have arrived at luck egalitarianism and at a suspicion I would like examined. My working vocabulary is that of designed experiments: treatments and controls, blocking, fixed and random effects, confounding, residual error, adjusting for covariates. My suspicion is that luck egalitarianism can be stated almost entirely in that vocabulary, and I want to know whether the "almost" is where everything interesting lives.

As I understand it, the position holds that a distribution is unjust insofar as the inequalities in it trace to brute luck — genetic endowments, childhood circumstances, calamities no one chose — and not unjust insofar as they trace to option luck, the outcomes of gambles one knowingly entered. The associated reform program looks to me like nothing more than adjustment: regress outcomes on chosen and unchosen variables, zero out the coefficients on the unchosen block, and call the resulting fitted values the just distribution. Where the assignment to circumstances is unobservable, one might do it Dworkin's way and run a hypothetical insurance market behind a thin veil of ignorance, which strikes me as eliciting a prior over latent variables by survey rather than by data. And the critics, as I gather, complain that this doctrine abandons the imprudent to their fate, or that it counts cultivated expensive tastes as chosen in a way nobody seriously believes.

Please restate luck egalitarianism in the vocabulary I have described, as faithfully as you can — choice as treatment, brute luck as confounded assignment, the insurance apparatus as a prior on latent variables, whatever else genuinely maps. Then give me the residue report I actually care about, with particular attention to these points. First: in my world nothing is exogenous by nature, only by design; the choice/circumstance cut is not given by the data but imposed by the experimenter. Does the luck egalitarian have anything better than an assumption of instrument validity — an article of faith that cannot be tested from within the framework? Second: my crude procedure — preserve choice coefficients, annul circumstance coefficients — must be distorting something. I would like to know what: the envy test, thresholds and sufficiency, the treatment of the severely disabled, whether the analogue of "holding people responsible" survives translation at all. Third: tell me whether the residue points to something my vocabulary cannot say (which would be my loss) or to something the position never said clearly in the first place (which would be the philosophers' problem). I would especially like your view on whether my sense that the whole debate is an endogeneity problem with a normative parameter — the choice/circumstance partition itself being the thing people are really fighting about — is a genuine insight or an artifact of translation. Finally, tell me plainly what would change your verdict on that last question, and where in your reasoning you think you are most likely to have gone wrong.
