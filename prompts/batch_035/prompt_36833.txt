For the past year my brother-in-law and I have been having a running argument at family dinners about whether "society" is a real thing or just a word for a lot of people standing near each other. He's a banker who talks about "the market" as though it were a person with moods, and I keep telling him that's superstition, but I've realized my own position is messier than my side of the argument suggests. I never studied any of this beyond a semester of economics, so I've just tried to work out what I actually believe. I've landed on something like the following five commitments, and I'd like someone to check whether they hang together.

Here they are. (a) There is no "society" over and above individual people: no group mind, no team that thinks — a company is just the people, the buildings, and the contracts, and "the nation" never wants anything, only people do. (b) At the same time, I'm quite sure things like money, borders, and laws are real in a way that isn't just in any one person's head — a ten-dollar bill really will buy lunch, and no single person, not even a whole government acting alone in a vacuum of everyone else's expectations, could make leaves legal tender by decree. (c) To explain a riot, a fad, or a stock panic, you have to explain why each particular person did what they did; "the crowd did it" is a description, not an explanation. (d) Yet it seems obviously true that many people join a riot partly because the crowd is already big — each individual's reason for acting refers to what the others are doing. (e) And I think the social sciences should stick to explanations that could in principle be restated in terms of individual people's beliefs, desires, and circumstances; when a historian writes that the nation wanted revenge, that's just sloppy writing.

I can't tell whether these five are a coherent package or whether I'm contradicting myself somewhere, and if I am, I don't know which of them should give. Please tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which ones only look like they're in tension, what the least costly fix would be for each real conflict, and which single revision would clean up the most. Give me your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
