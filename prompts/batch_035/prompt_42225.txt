Quick context: I teach a first-year epistemology elective at uni, 14 students, one 80-minute seminar slot, and next week we're on epistemic injustice. I want an activity where they run into hermeneutical injustice before anyone names it — so no lecture up front, and they shouldn't hear the term itself until the debrief.

Like getting them to discuss a scripted case of a manager repeatedly interrupting someone and passing their ideas off as his own, while banning "credit", "credit-stealing", "plagiarise", "undermine", "belittle", "harass", "diminish", "dismissive" and so on. But please actually work out the taboo list yourself — I want something like 12-15 banned words plus near-synonyms, and design the case so it can't be dodged with one clever synonym. The point is they should all *feel* that something is wrong in the case and keep fumbling for words that aren't there. Work out how the case gets presented (handout? short role-play with scripts you write? a 2-minute acted scene?), how groups are split, timing, and how taboo violations are policed.

Design it so the discounting happens for a prejudice-flavoured reason rather than a legitimate one. Again: mechanics, materials, timing, and how you'd keep it from being either obvious or too subtle.

Finish with a debrief that maps specific moments in each phase onto the Fricker-style distinction between hermeneutical and testimonial injustice — what was the "gap" in phase one, what was the "deflation" in phase two, and where they came apart.
