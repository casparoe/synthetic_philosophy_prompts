I am drafting a novel set in a declining New England mill town. One recurring character is Cornelia Marsh, seventy-one, who has edited the weekly paper for forty years, and her record of judgments is now fixed across earlier chapters: she crusaded against a councilman who steered paving contracts to an out-of-town firm at inflated prices; she condemned a regional chemical plant whose solvent had leaked into the creek; she opposed the county's plan to site a landfill half a mile from the elementary school; she wrote a warm column on the family that has run the town's Fourth of July fireworks for four generations; and when a young police clerk took proof of a beloved coach's padded expense account to the county paper, she demanded repayment yet, in the same column, scolded the girl for not raising the matter within the department first.

Two scenes remain. In the first, the selectmen vote to cut down the charter elm on the common — diseased, dropping limbs beside the playground, and pictured on the town seal — and I must decide whether Cornelia supports the cutting. In the second, a nurse at the county retirement home, her complaints about staffing ignored, sends photographs of neglected residents to a regional television station; conditions improve, but the home loses its chief benefactor and two aides are dismissed. What will her line be in each column?

I want reasoned prediction, not guesswork. State the two rules her record fits — one on which only harm and cheating move her; one on which loyalty, deference to proper channels, and attachment to the town's symbols do real work as well — say what each rule predicts for the two scenes, give your best guess with reasons, and name the further scene that would best decide between the rules. Then, since a friend tells me this divide mirrors the one between liberals and conservatives in moral foundations theory, tell me whether Cornelia's rule, if it is the second, would tell me anything about whether her judgments are correct, or only about how she arrives at them. Please keep your answer under 400 words.
