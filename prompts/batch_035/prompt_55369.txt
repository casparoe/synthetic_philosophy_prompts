I'm the managing editor at a small nonprofit that writes plain-language health and biology explainers for the general public — short pieces on how the body works, why we get fevers, what the appendix is for, that sort of thing. We keep fighting about function language. One writer claims the appendix's function is storing beneficial gut bacteria, another calls it a useless leftover, a third says vestigial organs have no functions full stop, and our medical reviewers are just as split. I gather philosophy of biology has a whole literature on what it even means to say a trait has a function — the selected-effect / etiological view where function depends on evolutionary history, versus the causal-role view where function is about what a part contributes to a system right now — and I want that turned into something my team can actually use. So act as a philosopher of biology who's used to translating for non-specialists, take a moment to brainstorm and think through the design before committing to it, and then write a decision procedure my editors can run on any sentence that attributes a function, a malfunction, or 'leftover' status to a trait: the questions to ask in order, what kind of answers license evolutionary claims versus merely causal-role ones, where it outputs 'cut the sentence or soften it to pure mechanism,' and where it hands the decision back to an editor because its a judgment call the theory can't settle. Then run it on three cases: the appendix and gut bacteria claim, fever and sickness behavior (is fever a function or a malfunction?), and a writer who wants to say depression 'exists for an evolutionary reason.' Make sure at least one case comes out in a way I won't like, be honest about where the two accounts of function simply disagree and no procedure can paper over that, and say what the procedure can't do when the underlying biology is itself contested. Write everything as continuous prose, no headings, bullets, bold or tables — like a long email — and keep it usable by people who've never read a word of philosophy.
