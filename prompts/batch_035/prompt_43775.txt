I write for the science and ideas desk at a mid-sized outlet, and I'm doing a short piece on whether math is discovered or invented. While poking around I found an explainer on another site that frames the whole debate around Benacerraf, and I want to know how much of it I can trust before I lean on any of it. It says, roughly, this: In his 1973 paper "Mathematical Truth," Paul Benacerraf set up a dilemma that has haunted platonism ever since. Horn one: if we treat mathematical statements the same way we treat ordinary ones, with a uniform semantics where "there are infinitely many primes" works like "there are infinitely many galaxies," then mathematical truth is easy to state but mathematical knowledge becomes a mystery, because numbers are causally inert abstract objects and Benacerraf insisted that knowledge requires causal contact with the thing known. Horn two: if we reinterpret mathematical talk so that it's about something we can actually get in touch with, we end up with a semantics so different from the rest of our language that we've quietly changed the subject, and math is no longer really about numbers at all. Benacerraf reinforced this with his famous argument that since numbers can equally be modeled as von Neumann sets or Zermelo sets, there's no fact of the matter what numbers are. He concluded that platonism is false and that if math is about abstract objects, we can never know it, which is why most philosophers of mathematics today have given up on mathematical objects and accept that numbers are useful fictions.

Something about that feels off to me -- like the causal contact thing is doing more work than it should, and the last sentence smells like a leap -- but I honestly can't tell where the real errors are. Can you go through it piece by piece? Tell me which parts a philosopher of math would accept, which parts are simplifications they'd sigh at, and which are just wrong (including any papers or arguments that got mixed together), and then tell me whether the final claim about what "most philosophers today" believe actually follows from anything correct in the summary.
