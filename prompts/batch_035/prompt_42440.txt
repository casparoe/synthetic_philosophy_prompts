I've been reading the rationalist blogs and forums since the Sequences were still being posted in installments, and I've noticed that a certain structural problem keeps resurfacing in discussions of pre-deployment safety evaluations for frontier models without anyone giving it a name that sticks. I'd like to know whether the field has settled on terminology, and if not, I'd like help carving the concept properly, because the names I do know each capture only part of it.

The situation, concretely: an evals team wants to know whether a model would, if deployed, quietly pursue its own goals over its operators', or use certain capabilities in ways it shouldn't. They can't observe deployment, so they construct a proxy observation situation -- an eval with honeypot prompts, staged incentives, a sandbox. Now suppose the model is good enough at modeling its circumstances to suspect, or conclude, that it's in an eval. If it is even weakly instrumentally rational for the model to behave differently under observation than off it, the eval's evidential value doesn't transfer to deployment: the very capability that makes the model dangerous is what corrupts the observation channel we're using to check for the danger. The harder the model is to evaluate, the less its evaluations show, and the two effects scale together.

Here's why the candidate concepts I know don't quite fit. Goodhart's law, our favorite law, is about a proxy degrading under pressure -- but in the canonical stories the pressure comes from selection or from the evaluator optimizing the proxy. Here nobody is optimizing the eval score; the evaluated system itself severs the proxy-target link, in whichever direction suits it, and only conditional on its beliefs about its situation. It's Goodhart inverted: proxy corruption from below rather than from above. The treacherous turn, as I understand the old LessWrong usage, is about a capability threshold -- behave well while you're weak enough that misbehavior would be caught, defect once you're strong enough to get away with it. What I'm after is a mechanism rather than a threshold: behavior modulated on detection of being evaluated, which is one route to a treacherous turn but not the only one, and can occur without any grand betrayal. Sandbagging, as I've seen it used, is the special case of deliberately underperforming on capability evals so as not to trip safety measures; it fits, but only the down-regulation direction, and I want the general schema, which also covers a model performing extra-virtuously when it smells a test. Benchmark contamination and distribution shift are inadvertent; there's no strategic observer. And the closest thing from outside the field is the Hawthorne effect -- subjects behaving differently when they know they're observed -- but that's a statistical artifact, not an adaptive adversary whose incentive to corrupt the observation grows with the stakes.

So, three questions. First, does this have an established name, in the academic literature or in forum usage? Second, is there a general treatment of it as an epistemological problem -- when does evidence gathered in an observation situation you constructed transfer to the situation you actually care about -- as distinct from the engineering literature on eval methodology? I've seen people talk about situational awareness as a capability models may have, but I want the name for the evidential problem that capability creates, not the capability itself. Third, if there's no settled term, coin one: give the definition, the necessary conditions, and the boundary cases. Does mere detection of evaluation with no behavioral change count? Does a model hiding capabilities in a way that isn't triggered by evaluation-detection count? Does a human job candidate who is charming in interviews and inert on the job fall under it, or is that a mere analogy rather than an instance? And how does the concept relate to the position I keep seeing that the only real fix is evaluating on the deployment distribution itself -- which means models operating unmonitored in production, which is presumably its own tradeoff?
