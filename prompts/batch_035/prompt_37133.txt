write me the desiderata: what any adequate explanation of complex, design-like phenomena — living things, minds, language, culture, whatever domain you think makes the test hardest — has to deliver in order to count as a genuine crane. for each requirement, give the case behind it. I'd expect things like at least one of these to show up (but you decide what belongs): the explanatory work at each step can't be done by something smarter or more designful than what it's supposed to explain; the process has to be something that could actually run with the time and resources the world had available; it has to be constructive rather than just consistent — lots of stories are compatible with the outcome without being able to grind one out; and the explanation of the explainer can't be indefinitely deferred (crane all the way down is fine, skyhooks smuggled into the foundations are not). but here's the part I actually care about: Dennett himself warns against greedy reductionism, jumping straight from physics to, like, love or inflation. so the desiderata have to do two jobs at once — exclude skyhooks AND exclude greedy reductionism, where the skimpier explanation pretends to be deeper than it is by skipping the intermediate machinery that does the actual lifting. those two jobs might conflict! a requirement strong enough to guarantee no skyhooks might be so demanding that only greedy-looking shortcuts can meet it, or so loose that greedy reductionism slides through. if some subset of your requirements can't be jointly satisfied, say so plainly, that's the most interesting result you could hand me. also flag which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just preferences of one camp (adaptationists, self-organization people, etc. will obviously weight these differently).
