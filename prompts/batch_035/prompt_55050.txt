I run a philosophy-of-mind seminar and want a decision-tree handout that sorts students by what they actually believe about the timing of experience, using the color phi phenomenon. The handout should first state the case plainly: two stationary spots are flashed in sequence, red then green, and subjects report a single moving spot that changes color midway — yet the color it changes to can only be determined after the second flash, so the change seems to have been present in experience only after its causes were complete. Every node should be a question a student could answer without knowing any of the labels; the central ones should probe whether a perfect memory-reader installed a second after the trial could in principle reveal a moment at which the unedited experience was already conscious, versus whether the brain could have revised the content before anything was conscious of it. Leaves must include the Stalinesque view (content edited before consciousness, on the model of fixing the script before the show trial), the Orwellian view (the raw experience occurred and the record was falsified afterward), Dennett's claim that the two do not differ because there is no determinate time at which it all comes together, and at least one leaf for the position that there is a fact of the matter but no possible experiment could settle it — with a sentence on whether anyone can really hold that view, and why. Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot separate, and include a node that catches students who think the report "it changed color midway" settles the timing on its own. Six to eight nodes; the audience is undergraduates who have read nothing on this topic.
