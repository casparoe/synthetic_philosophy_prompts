I've been trying to figure out my actual view on what to do when you're unsure about morality itself, and instead of reading more theory I decided to just go thorugh cases where I have a gut verdict and see what principle, if any, generates all of them:

- I'm about 70% confident eating meat is seriously wrong (animal suffering) and 30% confident it's fine. Gut: I should cut way down and avoid the worst stuff, but I'm not obligated to be a strict vegan, and I wouldn't blame a friend in the same epistemic position who keeps eating normally.
- I'm 60% deontologist about promises, 40% consequentialist. I promised my niece I'd take her to the park Saturday, and a volunteer thing came up that same afternoon where I could do a lot of good. Gut: keep the promise, and it's not close.
- I'm only 40% confident there's a duty to vote, but I vote in every election and do quietly think worse of people who can't be bothered. Feels inconsistent even to me.
- I'm maybe 30% confident that gossiping about friends is wrong, I gossip, feel basically fine about it, and yet I judge people who gossip maliciously. Thirty percent credence in wrongness seems like it should be too low for blame to come out — unless blame tracks something other than credence.
- I'm 90% confident death is bad for the person who dies (deprivation view) and 10% Epicurean, and that 10% gives me zero pause about anything. Small credence in an extreme view counts for nothing here.

So is there one principle that gets all five? The candidates seem to be the standard moral-uncertainty rules — maximizing expected choiceworthiness, majority vote among views, some hedging rule — but I don't think any of them delivers my verdicts uniformly (the promise case and the gossip case seem to pull in different directions, and I can't tell if that's because promises and gossip are different in kind or because one of the verdicts is just wrong). And there's the case I keep flip-flopping on: I have maybe a 1% credence that creating happy people is extremely good, and my gut says that 1% shouldn't dominate decisions about having kids — but if whatever principle saves the promise case can't say anything about the 1% case, I don't trust it. Tell me which of my verdicts have to go and what the surviving principle says about the kid question. Under 400 words please.
