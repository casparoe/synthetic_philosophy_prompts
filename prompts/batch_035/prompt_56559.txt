So here's roughly what I believe about the gender-equality paradox (the finding that in countries with more gender equality, men and womens field and career choices actually diverge MORE, not less, STEM being the famous case):

(1) The paradox is real. I know the Stoet and Geary 2018 study got that correction in 2019 and the follow-up found problems with the STEM version specifically, but the broader pattern shows up across other datasets too -- personality traits, values, occupational preferences -- so I dont think it's a statistical artifact.

(2) The best explanation is the liberation one: when you take away material barriers and economic desperation, average sex-linked interests get to express themselves, and the divergence is just what free expression looks like. In a poorer country you pick engineering because you need a secure paycheck; in Norway you can afford to follow your heart, and your heart is (on average) more gendered than your wallet.

(3) I accept that there are some average sex differences in interests, people-vs-things being the obvious one, while rejecting determinism -- nothing about biology fixes what any individual woman or man will or should do.

(4) I also believe the sociology side of this, honestly maybe more than I let on in arguments: gendered cultural meaning around fields of study is NOT weaker in rich egalitarian countries, it might be stronger. The stereotype associating math with men has been measured as stronger in more egalitarian and developed countries, and sociologists like Maria Charles argue that when a society loudly insists nothing is stopping you, self-stereotyping becomes easier rather than harder, because the only thing left steering you is the symbolic stuff.

(5) On the justice question I'm a level-playing-field person: what matters is genuinely open options, not proportional outcomes, and I've been using the paradox as ammunition against strict gender representation targets -- my line being that if the choices are free, the asymmetry isn't an injustice, so quotas are solving a non-problem.
