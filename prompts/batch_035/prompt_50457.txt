I spend a lot of time thinking about how much you can really squeeze out of old objects, and I've noticed I have pretty firm gut reactions about when an artifact licenses an inference to a belief or an institution. Here are my verdicts, for what they are. A burial where the dead person's flint blade was deliberately snapped in half, one half placed in the grave and the other half kept among the living: I'm comfortable saying those people held some real belief there, that the dead or the object needed that link. Hundreds of female figurines with exaggerated features from a settlement: I don't think we get too infer a fertility cult or goddess religion from that — could be toys, could be taste, and I wouldn't claim otherwise. A building module of the same length recurring in houses across villages that show no sign of ever having been in contact: yes, some shared canon or institution existed, even if we can't say what it meant to them. A region-wide switch from wildly varied handmade pots to uniform, machine-like ones: yes, I'd infer something like political unification or at least a production institution behind it. But decorative motifs shared across that same region: no — that could just be fashion, I wouldn't infer a shared identity or a 'people' from it. And a big mound built by a small community over generations: yes to an institution that could organize multi-year labor, but I balk at chiefdom or coercion from that alone.

So: is there a principle behind all this, or am I just tracking which stories I find appealing? The tension I feel most is the figurines vs. the snapped blade — both are "special treatment of objects," one gets a yes and one a no — and then the pots vs. motifs thing, where one kind of sameness across a region points to an institution and the other points to nothing at all. If there is a principle, I'd like it stated precisely, with the case that motivates each part, plus what it predicts for something I haven't mentioned: paintings in a pitch-black chamber you have to crawl a hundred meters through the rock to reach, in a spot nobody would ever see by accident.

And please be self-critical with me here: tell me which of my verdicts you think is just me being inconsistent rather than tracking anything real, and, if you do end up proposing a principle, what kind of case would most likely break it — what would have to turn up for you to amend it or drop it outright.
