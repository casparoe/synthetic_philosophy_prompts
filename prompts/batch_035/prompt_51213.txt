Last night a friend and I argued until well past midnight about imprecise probabilities, and I want to work through the residue of it in the only vocabulary I trust. She is a committed precise Bayesian: every rational agent has a single probability function, and where the evidence is thin you either choose a prior and start updating, or you simply are not yet in the business of rational choice. I kept pushing the imprecise alternative: that some epistemic states are better modeled by a whole set of probability functions — a credal set — with your credence in an event being the interval between the lowest and highest probabilities the set assigns, and with decisions governed by rules like Γ-maximin, where you evaluate each act by its worst expected utility across the set, rather than by maximizing a single expectation. She answered with the objections I now know by heart: that betting behavior will always force you back to a single number once you have to post odds; that Ellsberg-style preferences — preferring to bet on an urn of known composition over one of unknown composition — can be reinterpreted as ambiguity about your own utilities rather than your probabilities; and, the one that actually stuck, the dilation problem, where conditioning on evidence intuitively unrelated to an event can make your interval for that event wider rather than narrower, which she takes as a reductio of the whole framework.

By day I work on distributionally robust optimization and uncertainty quantification for machine-learning systems, and I am fluent in that vocabulary: ambiguity sets over probability measures, Wasserstein balls around an estimated distribution, minimax bounds, adversarial training, regularization understood as the dual of robustness, ensemble disagreement as an uncertainty proxy. What I want from you is a translation. Restate the imprecise-probability program — credal sets, lower and upper probabilities, the claim that a single precise prior misrepresents sparse or conflicting evidence, and the decision rules proposed for interval-valued credences — in my terms, as faithfully as you can. Where exactly does a credal set sit relative to an ambiguity set? Is a lower probability just the value of a robustness bound? Is Γ-maximin simply distributionally robust optimization with the decision variable ranging over acts instead of model parameters, and if so, what corresponds to the choice of ball radius? Then give me the residue report I care most about: which parts of the philosophers' debate survive the translation cleanly, which parts had to be distorted to fit, and which have no counterpart at all in my world — dilation, the question of whether uniqueness of the prior is required or merely permitted, anything else that genuinely does not live in the robustness literature. Finally, take up her dilation challenge through the translation rather than around it: if dilation looks pathological from where I stand, is that because my framework is missing something, or because her argument is weaker than it looks? I am not asking for a reading list or citations; I want the conceptual mapping worked out on its own terms.
