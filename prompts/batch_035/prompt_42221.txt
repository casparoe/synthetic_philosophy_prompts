Here is my opening move. In Marriage and Morals you treat jealousy the way your writing on education treats fear — as a passion to be handled gently in childhood so it never hardens into cruelty later. But your own principle as an educator is that you should never suppress a childs instinct, only redirect its energy, and that adults who've been conditioned out of a natural feeling end up hollow. Jealousy strikes me as exactly such a feeling — spontaneous, intense, and carrying real information: it tells you something you value is being taken away. If your programme works by rearing children until the pang simply never comes, haven't you built the liberated adult out of a child who was taught not to notice loss? That isn't freedom, it's selective anaesthesia, and I'd expect a man as suspicious of conditioning as you are everywhere else to smell the surgery here.
