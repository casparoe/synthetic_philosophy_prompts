My dad is retiring after forty-odd years as a typeface designer, and I'm giving the toast at his party. Half the room will be designers, the kind of people who can name a font off a menu from across the street, and the other half is just family who will not care about any of that. Dad's whole career came down to this one question he'd bring up at dinner way too often: what makes an 'a' an 'a'? It can't be the shape, since the 'a' in his fonts shares basically no ink with my terrible handwritten 'a' or the 'a' in a book, and I still read all of them instantly as the same letter. He also loved that you can show people a made-up glyph they've never seen, and if the rest of the alphabet is there around it, they just read it, no questions. But he would get genuinely annoyed whenever anyone said it's just family resemblance or whatever -- he thought that was a lazy answer, because an 'a' can share more actual ink with the 'o' beside it than with other a's, and you can still tell which font a letter belongs to even apart from knowing what letter it is, as if the identity lives somehow in the whole alphabet and not in the letter at all. I never fully followed him on that last part but I'd like to try. Write me a toast that works for both halves of the room, takes his question seriously enough that the designers won't wince (including why the easy answers annoyed him), and lands as an actual goodbye to his lifes work and not a lecture -- mom will tune out in about thirty seconds if it sounds like homework.
