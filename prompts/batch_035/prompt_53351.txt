I write about science and ideas for a living, and there's this fight in consciousness studies I keep circling back to that has basically not moved since the early 90s: Dennett's multiple drafts model versus, well, almost everyone who uses the language of things "becoming conscious" or "entering awareness" — with the global workspace people (Baars, Dehaene, that whole crowd) as the most interesting case. Dennett's line was that any theory which has contents arrive somewhere to be displayed is smuggling in a Cartesian theater with nicer furniture. The workspace crowd fires back that there's no observer and no stage in their model, just information getting broadcast widely across the brain, so the charge misses them entirely — and honestly, from the outside, the two camps sound weirdly similar sometimes. Both love the neuroscience, both are anti-dualist, and yet thirty years on I can't think of a single person who switched sides. My editor thinks the whole thing is verbal and the two camps just mean different things by "theater." My own hunch is it's actually about something deeper, like whether consciousness is even a natural kind, and the theater accusation is a proxy. Diagnose it for me: what kind of disagreement is this actually — verbal, or a real difference about explanation hiding under one label? And what, if anything, would count as progress here: a distinction, an experiment, a reformulated question, or nothing? I want a diagnosis with reasons, not another survey of the theories.
