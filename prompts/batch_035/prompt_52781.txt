At a recent meetup of a philosophy-and-physics discussion group I help run, a member — a graduate student in finance with an enthusiastic amateur interest in the many-worlds interpretation — closed the evening by announcing that he had stopped contributing to his retirement account and had instead put most of his savings into a handful of extremely high-variance investments. His reasoning, delivered as a settled fact, was the slogan in the title of my request: "There's always a branch where you survive, so you can never actually lose — you'll always find yourself in the world where it worked out." He gestured at the idea that any quantum gamble with a nonzero probability of a good outcome guarantees that "some version of him" wakes up rich, and that this is the version that will be doing the experiencing. When I raised the obvious worry — that the branches in which he survives the gamble might mostly be ones where he survives badly, maimed or bankrupt or both, or that "surviving in some branch" might not mean *he* survives — he waved it off as nitpicking about details of an essentially sound argument, and several people in the room seemed persuaded.

I want to walk into the next meeting with something more than my unease. What I'd like from you is a careful dissection of that one-liner into its distinct possible readings — laid out as a numbered list I can literally bring to the group, in American English. For each reading, tell me: (a) what it precisely claims, distinguishing for instance "some branch exists in which a person psychologically continuous with me exists" from "most of my future measure or amplitude lies in the surviving branches" from "the only experiences I will ever have are ones in which things worked out"; (b) whether it's trivially true, substantive but defensible, or outright false (including where it depends on contested views about personal identity or about how subjective probability relates to branch weights); (c) whether it's testable in principle or is shielded from test by construction; and (d) whether that reading, if true, would actually support my friend's investment and retirement strategy as opposed to merely describing a curious anthropic curiosity.

Two things I especially want handled carefully, since they're where my own intuitions outrun my arguments. First, the role of branch *weight* versus branch *existence*: if every outcome with nonzero amplitude is realized somewhere, does a course of action that makes the good outcome astronomically unlikely but not impossible come out "safe" for the subject, or does something like expected measure do the work that ordinary probability does? Second, the identity question: the slogan seems to slide between "there is a surviving successor" and "*I* survive," and I'd like the menu to make that slide explicit — including the version where the correct response is that if enough of my measure ends in annihilation, the surviving sliver shouldn't console me any more than an identical stranger's surviving should. For each reading, please also note the strongest point in its favor, so my friend can't say I brought him a stacked deck.
