I'm finishing up my profile for our upper-level metaphysics seminar, where each of us has to circulate a list of our commitments on persistence so the rest of the group can attack it, and I want a consistency check before I hand it out. Here is where I've landed. (1) I'm an endurantist about ordinary objects: a persisting table is wholly present at every time it exists, and persistence is strict numerical identity across times, not the succession of distinct temporal parts. (2) I'm an eternalist and a B-theorist: past and future objects and times all exist, and nothing in reality marks out a privileged moving present. (3) I think change is real and intrinsic: a single object can be straight at one time and bent at a later time, where bentness is a genuine intrinsic property and not shorthand for some relation it bears. (4) I think the standard endurantist escape from the problem of temporary intrinsics — saying the object has the property bent-at-t, or is bent t-wise, so that nothing has incompatible intrinsic properties simpliciter — changes the subject: being bent at a time is a relation to a time, and a relation to a time is not a way of being bent, any more than being popular with is a way of being popular. (5) On mereology, I hold unrestricted composition: whenever there are some things, there is something composed of them. (6) I also think coincident objects are possible: the statue and the clay it is made of are numerically distinct, sharing their exact spatial location and microphysical properties, differing in their persistence conditions and modal features. (7) And I hold that time travel into one's own past is metaphysically possible, at least where the history is self-consistent, without any metaphysical absurdity in the notion itself.

Since the seminar is run as a mutual audit, what I want from you is not a survey of the positions but a check on whether this set hangs together. Go through the commitments pairwise and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and how badly the real ones conflict. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what adopting the revision would cost me elsewhere on the list. Then rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally fatal, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most if I made it. Please don't try to guess which commitment I'm most attached to or nudge me toward the perdurantist side of the ledger — a classmate already accused me of smuggling in worm theory through the back door via (5), and I honestly don't know yet whether that accusation lands, which is part of why I'm asking. If some tension depends on a substantive metaphysical assumption I haven't stated (about, say, whether spatial and temporal location are relevantly alike), flag that dependence explicitly rather than resolving it silently one way.
