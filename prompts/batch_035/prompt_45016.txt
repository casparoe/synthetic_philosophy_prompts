In a discussion for my philosophy elective, a classmate who watches a lot of neuroscience videos summarized Daniel Dennett's position on consciousness as follows: "The Cartesian theater is Descartes' own theory — the idea that an immaterial soul sits somewhere in the brain and watches all of our experiences on a screen, like an audience watching a play. Since everything is edited after the fact, we're never conscious of anything in real time — experience is always a delayed story the brain tells. And because the story is all there is, Dennett's bottom line is that consciousness doesn't really exist; it's an illusion, so no one is actually aware of anything."
