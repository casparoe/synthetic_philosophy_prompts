ok so heres my situation. I'm a grad student whos been running a small reading group on metaphysics of identity for about a year, and the identity of indiscernibles debate has completely stalled us, in a way I find fascinating but also kind of exhausting. I want you to act as a professional metaphysician, someone who has taught this debate many times and has opinions about it, and help me diagnose WHY it hasnt gone anywhere rather than just re-litigating it.

quick setup of where we are, so you know the debate concretely. You know the deal: Leibniz thought that no two things can differ solo numero, that there cant be two substances that are qualitatively identical. Max Black pushed back in the early 50s with the famous thought experiment of a universe containing only two qualitatively identical iron spheres, two miles apart, no observer. If that universe is possible, the strong principle is false. Over the year our group has cycled through basically all the standard moves: the Leibnizian who bites the bullet and says God could create one sphere but not two; the person who retreats to the weak version and says only qualitative duplication *within a background* is banned; the possible worlds people who say a world with two spheres and a world with one are just... differently described, and primitive thisness does the individuating; the bundle theorist who says Black cheated because bare identity-to-a isnt a real property; and the folk who insist the principle was only ever meant as a methodological maxim, not a truth at all. Everyone knows everyone elses arguments, and nobody has budged an inch since roughly October.

So heres what I want, and please actually think carefully before answering, maybe start with a bit of brainstorming on the side about what kind of disagreement this even could be before you commit. My working hypothesis (presented as a hypothesis, tear into it if its wrong) is that the stall is because the two camps arent really answering the same question. The Black side takes itself to be doing modal epistemology: we can *conceive* of the two-sphere universe, and conceivability is (defeasible) evidence of possibility, so the anti-quantifier burden is on them. The Leibniz side takes itself to be doing ontology: they think a purely qualitative metaphysics is the only non-mysterious one, so spheres that are identical in every qualitative respect but numerically distinct would need some spooky thisness to hold apart, and theyd rather deny the possibility than accept the spooky thing. If thats right, then its not really a fight about spheres, its a fight about which discipline gets to set the rules — whether intuitions about possibility are data, or whether ontological parsimony gets a veto over them. And maybe its not even resolvable, just a standoff between two virtues neither side ranks the same way.

But maybe my diagnosis is too tidy! It could also be that the whole thing is verbal: maybe saying the universe has two spheres and saying it has one are just different descriptions with nothing at issue, and the parties are fighting over a sentence rather than a fact. Or maybe there IS a hidden substantive question — about whether identity facts are grounded in qualitative facts or brute — and the thought experiment is just a bad vehicle for it, which would explain why everyones wheels are spinning.

So: diagnose the disagreement for me. Substantive, verbal, different-questions-under-one-name, or a genuinely ill-posed issue? Use what each side actually says to make the case. And then be honest about what would even count as progress here — a sharper distinction between individuation and discernibility, some formal result, a cost-benefit comparison of thisness vs qualitative duplication, or just nothing, and we should all go read something else with our tuesdays. Im not asking for a survey of the literature or a bibliography, I want a diagnosis with a spine. My reading group thanks you in advance.
