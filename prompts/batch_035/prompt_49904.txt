My reading group is doing a series where each member circulates a short statement of their commitments on an assigned topic and the rest of the group spends the session trying to knock it down. I have been assigned diachronic rationality, and my draft keeps bothering me: the views in it feel individually right, but I have a nagging sense that some of them cannot all be held together, and I cannot for the life of me see where the problem is. So before I circulate it, I would like a proper audit. The relevant background is that we have been working through van Fraassen's Belief and the Will (1984) alongside some of the responses it provoked. Here is the package:

(1) I accept the Reflection principle in its full generality: whenever I am certain now what my credence in some proposition will be at a later time, my current credence in that proposition should equal that anticipated future credence, with no carve-outs for how I expect the change to come about. (2) At the same time, I am confident about the following case: suppose I know that a medication I must begin tomorrow will, as a side effect, make me flatly certain that a certain coin landed tails, whatever the truth of the matter, and that nothing I do once it takes effect can dislodge the belief. I think the rational thing today is to stay at 1/2 — anticipating the malfunction by shifting toward tails today would just be letting a foreseen corruption count as evidence. (3) I also hold that vulnerability to a Dutch book, including the diachronic kind, marks a genuine defect in an agent's opinions: if a bookie can place bets with me at different times, at odds I regard as fair at each time, in a way that guarantees me a net loss, then something is really wrong, and I do not accept the line that the bookie's cunning rather than my credences is doing the work. (4) Finally, in the ordinary run of cases I defer to my future self quite happily: if I am sure that after reading tomorrow's test results I will land at 0.7 in the merger going through — sure of the exact number, whichever way the results come out — then I should already be at 0.7 today.

What I would like from you: go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly, ranking the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be and what it would cost. I am especially unsure whether (2) and (4) can be held together without being arbitrary — one deference to my future self allowed, another refused — and I cannot tell whether that is a real inconsistency or only my failure to find the principle that separates them. Please also distinguish genuine tensions from ones that merely look like tensions, and say which single revision would clean up the most. I would rather learn now, from you, that something has to give than discover it during the session.
