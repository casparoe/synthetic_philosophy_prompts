Write a satirical press release, around 500-600 words, from a fictional institution (make up the name — something like the "Museum of World Civilizations" works) announcing a bold new framework for its holdings of Benin Bronzes and Parthenon Marbles. The framework should be called something like "Perpetual Shared Custody" or the "Open Horizon Dialogue Initiative" — the museum presents it as a historic act of decolonization that somehow involves nothing leaving the building.

The comedy has to come from real features of the restitution debate, so a specialist recognizes you know what you're satirizing. Ideas you can use (pick what fits):

- "digital repatriation": the source communities recieve unlimited access to high-resolution 3D scans, printable under a generous non-commercial license
- a loan-back scheme where objects return to their country of origin for six weeks every twenty years, framed as "reconnecting the collection with its living communities"
- the trustees' deep anguish that their hands are tied by the act of parliament governing deaccessioning — an act the museum has never once lobbied to change
- acquisition history described in the passive voice: the bronzes "entered the collection in 1897 following a British punitive expedition" — the word "looted" never appears
- the phrase "held in trust for all humanity," deployed where "kept" would do
- a new special exhibition titled something like "Who Owns the Past? A Conversation," admission £26, booking recommended

Include at least one quote from an invented museum director (grand, wounded, rhetorically generous) and at least one from an invented official in the source country who is somehow simultaneously described as a "valued dialogue partner" and completely ignored on every substantive point.

Keep it deadpan throughout — press-release register, no winking, the reader should be able to tell what's being ironized entirely from the gap between the language and the facts. And here's the hard part: don't let the museum say anything a real encyclopedic-museum advocate hasn't actually argued in public. Every sentence should be defensible within the terms of the actual debate — the satire should work as a reductio of the genre, not as a caricature nobody has produced. A reader who holds the universal-museum position should wince with recognition rather than feel strawmanned.

End with an invitation to a press preview, refreshments included.
