My grandmother died this past March, and sorting out her house has turned into an unexpected seminar on where meaning lives. Among her effects we found several decades of letters in a Judeo-Spanish dialect that no one in the family can read a word of, a locked digital journal she kept after her stroke (the passphrase apparently went with her), a shoebox of photographs with a hard drive containing what appear to be identical images as files, and, from a genetics kit she took for reasons none of us understood, a vial and report containing her DNA sequence. I had been rereading Hofstadter's chapter "The Location of Meaning" shortly before she died, with its distinction among the frame message, the outer message, and the inner message, and I now find myself applying it compulsively to these objects — with results I can't fully square.

Here are my verdicts, such as they are. The letters in the dialect are, I'm certain, saturated with meaning: burning them would destroy something real, even though not one of us could decode them, and even if no scholar ever got around to it. Her DNA, by contrast, encodes at least as much information about her as the letters do, but it strikes me as not a message at all — nothing in it is *for* a decoder. The Voyager Golden Record, sailing out with nobody to play it, I think of as carrying genuine meaning into the void; its meaning isn't hostage to being decoded. But her encrypted journal, which was certainly written *to* be read and encodes exactly as much as she put into it, I flip-flop on: half the time it seems as fully meaningful as the letters, half the time it seems it has become noise, since no possible finder could ever extract anything from it. And the photographs baffle me most: the bits on the drive are presumably identical to what the prints show, yet the prints feel like they *hold* her meaning while the files feel like a mere container that would *deliver* it to the right device.

So I'd like you to do the reflective-equilibrium work for me. Is there a single principle about the location of meaning — perhaps in terms of which of the three message levels survives in each object — that captures these verdicts, or close to it? Where it can't capture all of them, tell me which verdicts have to go and why, and be honest about whether saving one of them (I suspect my wavering on the encrypted journal is where the trouble is) costs the principle its simplicity. Then apply the result to cases I haven't listed: whether letters in her style generated by a model trained on the translatable ones would mean anything, and whether meaning that no possible decoder could in principle extract is still meaning. I'd like the analysis in prose, and then, as the very last line, a single sentence of no more than forty words beginning "Principle:" stating the best account you can defend.
