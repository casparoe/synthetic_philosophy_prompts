I'm a PhD student in philosophy of language, and in two weeks I'm giving the closing talk of our department's vagueness reading group. Each of us grad students presents there own "take" on the material at the end of term, and this is mine. I need a Q&A prep sheet, and I need it to be honest.

My thesis, roughly: the sorites paradox isn't really about vague predicates being somehow defective. It's about measurement. A vague predicate — "tall", "bald", "heap" — functions like a coarse measuring instrument laid over a quantity dimension (height, hair count, volume) whose actual granularity is much finer than the instrument's. Vagueness just IS the gap between the instrument's resolution and the dimension's. On this view the tolerance intuition ("one grain can't make a difference to heap-hood") is a true fact about the instrument — single-step changes fall below its resolution — and the paradox arises only when we treat an instrument-relative truth as a fact about the quantity and let it chain across the entire scale. So the sorites is, at bottom, a unit error: like concluding that a ruler can't measure anything because each millimeter of wood looks the same to the naked eye. The practical upshots I want to claim: no epistemicism, because there's no hidden sharp fact to be ignorant of — the instrument genuinely doesn't resolve it; and a deflationary take on supervaluationism, since precisifications aren't mysterious extra structure, they're just finer instruments you could swap in. And I get to say sorites reasoning is valid-ish reasoning from a false premis about what kind of thing a predicate is.

The room: about ten people. Mostly grad students who've drunk the supervaluationist kool-aid all semester, one logician who will care only about whether my "valid-ish" handwaving can be made formally precise, and our faculty mentor, a hardline epistemicist in the Williamson tradition who holds that every predicate has sharp boundaries and vagueness is just ignorance. There's also a first-year who asks one genuinely brutal question at every talk without seeming to realize it.
