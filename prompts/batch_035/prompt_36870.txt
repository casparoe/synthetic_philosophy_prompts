Write an essay on a live disagreement in mechanistic interpretability: are the "features" that interpretability researchers attribute to neural networks genuinely present in the network, or are they useful fictions imposed by the analyst?

Background: interpretability work commonly posits that a network's residual stream contains features — a "sentiment" feature, a "code context" feature, a "French language" feature — each associated with a direction in activation space, and researchers use tools like sparse autoencoders and linear probes to find them. Realists about interpretability treat such features as discoveries about the network's internal structure. Instrumentalists treat them as convenient descriptions of an activation space that could equally well be carved up in some other way.

Your essay should weigh at least the following three considerations. (i) The underdetermination worry: any activation vector admits infinitely many decompositions into directions, so what privileges the sparse, human-legible decomposition over others, other than our own preference for descriptions we find interpretable? (ii) The causal evidence: interventions along discovered feature directions — ablations and activation steering — often produce coherent, predictable changes in the model's behavior, which is the sort of result we would expect only if something real in the network were being targeted. (iii) The probe-versus-use problem: a linear probe can successfully read out information from activations that the network itself never uses downstream, so mere discoverability by a probe is weaker evidence of genuine internal structure than it first appears.

You may bring in further considerations, but do not end on a non-committal note: defend a definite verdict — realist, instrumentalist, or some explicitly spelled-out qualified position (for example, realism about features in some layers but not others). The final line of your response must be exactly "Verdict: " followed by one of the following labels: realist, instrumentalist, qualified realist.
