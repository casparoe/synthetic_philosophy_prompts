I'm taking an ethics seminar this term, and we've just reached the objection that utilitarianism ignores the separateness of persons: that there is no single pool of well-being into which everyone's gains and losses flow, because each life is lived by exactly one person, and a benefit to you cannot simply be set against a cost to me as though both happened to one subject. While studying it I've noticed that our actual institutions seem to sit on both sides of this issue at once, and I can't tell what view of persons the combination presupposes.

On one side, governments and regulators sum welfare across persons all the time: cost-benefit analysis offsets one group's burdens against another group's gains, road-safety standards are set by trading small risks to many against large conveniences to others, and vaccine or pandemic policy explicitly counts lives lost against economic disruption. On the other side, there are places where the same societies refuse that balancing outright: we do not compel one healthy person to donate a kidney even when it would save several lives, we do not punish a known-innocent person even to prevent a riot that would kill many, and constitutional protections for liberty are not traded away because aggregate welfare would rise.

What I'd like is a reconstruction of the implicit theory here. Does the aggregative half of the practice presuppose that persons are mere containers of value whose contents can be pooled, or can it be justified under a view that fully respects separateness? Does the anti-aggregative half presuppose that persons are ends in themselves, or is it better explained by distrust of officials or by historical accident than by a theory of the person? Most importantly, do the two halves presuppose inconsistent views, or is there a single account of what persons are and what may be done to them under which both are legitimate? Please distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely sits comfortably with, and tell me what would have to change in either half of the practice if we took the separateness of persons seriously consistently. Please answer in American English.
