I'm organizing the satire night for our grad conference (every year we do this, the theme for submissions is metaphysics of science, whoever gets the most laughs wins a plastic trophy shaped like Wittgenstein, long story) and I drew natural kinds as my topic. What I want is a mock FAQ from a fictional bureaucratic body — think like the IPCC or an ISO standards committee, but call it the International Commission on the Joints of Nature, ICJN. This body apparently has the official mandate to decide what counts as a genuine natural kind, and their FAQ answers questions from the public. Maybe 600-800 words, deadpan standards-body voice throughout, no winking at the reader ever.

The joke has to come from the actual philosophical content, not from wacky non-sequiturs. So the commission keeps trying to do essentialism's job — carving nature at its joints, necessary and sufficient conditions, the whole deal — and every single ruling they issue generates a new borderline case they then have to issue a follow-up ruling about in the next FAQ revision. Some things I'd like to see them wrangle with: biology being an absolute nightmare for them (ring species, asexual lineages, populations that interbreed only sometimes and only grudgingly), the fact that they're fine stamping water as H2O but keep getting hung up on tigers, jade turning out to be two different minerals wearing one name, whether extinct things still qualify, and whether there was ever a fact of the matter vs. them just declaring one because the paperwork was due. Also really want a sidebar where they respond to hate mail, ideally from both the lumpers and the splitters, who each think the commission is in the pocket of the other side.

The deeper joke, if you can land it, is the quiet tension between kinds that actually support induction and generalization versus kinds that are just convenient shelf-space we invented, and the commission is constitutionally unable to admit there might be a difference — or worse, suspects there is and keeps redefining its mandate to avoid finding out. And then end on something bleak, like their admissions officer noting that the joints of nature, upon closer inspection, seem to be less like joints and more like the seams in cheap upholstery, and the FAQ immediately moving on to the next question. One more thing: somewhere in there they should be visibly annoyed by five-year-olds, or by the question of whether five-year-olds are a natural kind, whichever is funnier to you. I've read enough real FAQ pages from standards bodies that I trust you on the formatting — just make it read like it was written by a committee that has never once been wrong and is starting to find that suspicious.
