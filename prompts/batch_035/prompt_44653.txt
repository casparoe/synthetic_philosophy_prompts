hey, so I'm a phil mind PhD student working on personal identity / consciousness stuff and I'm drafting a short essay on the gradual replacement argument (Chalmers style) and wanted to get your thoughts on it before I embarass myself in front of my supervisor lol.

The argument I'm defending, roughly: the standard gradual replacement thought experiment (swap neurons for functionally equivalent silicon ones, one at a time, nobody notices anything) is usually taken to show that functional organization is what matters for consciousness and identity. My claim in the essay is that it actually proves less than people think, because it only tests one direction — replacing bio with silicon — and it's quietly asymmetric in a second way too.

The second asymmetry is the semi-novel bit. The silicon prostheses in the thought experiment are always modeled as replicating the neuron's synaptic inputs and outputs. But real neurons aren't just nodes in a directed graph — they also influence their neighbors through volume transmission, ephaptic coupling (electric field effects on nearby cells), gap junctions, etc. So "functional equivalence" as it's normally defined in the setup is really just *synaptic* equivalence, and there's a whole background of non-synaptic influence that gets hand-waved away. In the essay I argue this doesn't actually sink the replacement argument — a prosthesis that also replicates the local field and chemical environment is still a possible prosthesis, so the thought experiment just needs to be stated more carefully — but it does mean the argument gives you a weaker conclusion than "function is all that matters". It gives you "synaptic function plus local environmental coupling is all that matters", which is a substantive carve-up, and it leaves open which side of that line the interesting stuff (qualia, identity) actually lives on.

And the direction asymmetry: nobody ever runs the thought experiment backwards. If bio -> silicon is identity preserving, then silicon -> bio should be too, and the backward direction is where intuitions get weird — most people's gut says that going back to squishy neurons can't *change* who you are, which is fine, but it also seems to imply that upload-and-come-back should be about as safe as a nap. Honestly I'm not sure about this paragraph, it might just be confusing.
