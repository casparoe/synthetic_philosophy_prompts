Write a set of customer reviews for the French Revolution, as if historical events were establishments you could rate on something like Yelp or TripAdvisor — star ratings, usernames, dates of visit, number of people who found each review helpful, the works. Around 700-900 words total: four or five reviews plus one management response.

The core joke should be the Hayden White point from Metahistory: the same body of facts can be emplotted in different generic modes, and the emplotment does all the evaluative work while everyone involved thinks they're just reporting what happened. So one reviewer reads the Revolution as romance — obstacles progressively overcome on the way to a promised land of rights and citizenship. One reads it as tragedy — a noble cause undone by its own hubris, the Terror as the protagonist's fatal flaw working itself out. One reads it as satire/irony — every high ideal quietly devouring itself, culminating in an emperor. And one reads it as comedy in the Northrop Frye sense that White borrows — social order disrupted and then reconciled and restored, with the Thermidorian settlement as the final act where everything gets patched up. Each review should be recognizably about the same events — the Bastille, the Terror, Napoleon marching everyone off at the end — but the storyline each reviewer chose should be what generates their star rating, and none of them should ever notice this. Maybe one of the reviewers should suspiciously echo a famous historian's actual reading of the Revolution without being named as such.

Then include a management response from the establishment's owner, a thoroughly Rankean nineteenth-century type, who patiently explains that the negative reviews are distortions caused by narrative embellishment and that his archive simply shows how things essentially were — and whose reply should be at least as shaped, rhetorical, and selective as any of the reviews, without him having any idea. If you can squeeze in one reviewer who read a pop paperback of E.H. Carr and keeps insisting that you should judge a review by first asking who the reviewer is and when they wrote it, all the better.

Deadpan throughout — the reviewers and the owner are all completely sincere. It should be funny to someone who knows this historiography (they should be able to tag each review with its emplotment mode) but still land for someone who just thinks history Yelp reviews are a funny premise. No citations or real quotations needed — don't have anyone actually quote Ranke or White, just channel them. And keep the history right: the facts cited in each review should be ones that really belong to the Revolution, since the whole joke is that the divergence comes from plot, not from anyone getting the events wrong.
