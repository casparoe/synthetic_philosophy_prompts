I'm taking a seminar this semester on historical memory (mostly built around Holocaust memory and how other genocides get compared to it), and I've talked myself into an argument I can't get out of. The professor keeps saying comparisions are dangerous, and I think I've reconstructed why, but the thing proves way too much. Here it is:

(1) To compare two atrocities is to place them on a common scale of severity.
(2) Anything placed on a common scale is thereby being treated as interchangeable with the other things on that scale -- the scale itself doesn't care whose suffering it is.
(3) Treating victims' suffering as interchangeable is a failure of respect: it registers each person's loss as a quantity instead of as something that happened to somebody.
(4) We have a duty not to treat victims' suffering that way, no matter who the victims are.
(5) Therefore comparing atrocities is always wrong -- not just clumsy or risky, actually wrong.

The problem is that (1) through (4) don't stop at atrocities. Triage in an ER, deciding which disaster gets the relief money, choosing which disease the research budget goes to -- all of that puts losses on a common scale too, so by my own reasoning all of it treats people as interchangeable quantities, so all of it is wrong. But we obviously have to triage, and I don't think the ER doctor is disrespecting anyone. Even worse: on this argument, a survivor who says "what happened to my family was worse than what happened to yours" would herself be doing something wrong, and that seems clearly wrong -- it's her family, and she's the one person entitled to say it.

So either one of the steps is bad, or the argument only *looks* like it generalizes and doesn't really. My suspicion is the trouble is somewhere around (2), but I can't put my finger on exactly what the slide is, assuming there is one. The professor mumbled something once about ordinal vs. cardinal scales but I didn't write it down lol. Which premise should I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? For what it's worth, I'd really like to keep (4) -- that one feels like the thing I actually belive -- but tell me honestly if that's the one that has to go.
