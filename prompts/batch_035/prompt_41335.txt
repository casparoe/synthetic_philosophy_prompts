I spent my career in control engineering, and since retiring I've been reading decision theory and philosophy of mind for pleasure. A pattern keeps bothering me, and I can't find a single name for it. In my old work, the controller is physically inside the plant — the same processes it regulates also constitute it — so we routinely dealt with systems that had to model an environment containing the modeler. The philosophy I read, by contrast, almost always draws the agent standing outside the world: it observes, deliberates, chooses, and the world updates accordingly. The moment you put the agent back inside, several things break at once, and I suspect they are one problem in different clothes. First, any self-model the agent can actually run must be coarser than the agent itself, so self-prediction is inherently lossy, and predicting one's own decision in full detail seems self-undermining — you cannot simulate your deliberation to completion and then still deliberate. Second, the agent's sensing and computing are physical events in the very system being modeled, so the line between gathering information about the world and acting on the world blurs. Third, there is no external vantage point from which to score policies: evaluating what the agent will do is itself something the agent does, from inside. Is there an established term, or a recognized literature, for this whole configuration — the agent as a physical subsystem of the system it models and optimizes over? I know the nearest pieces have names: Gödelian self-reference is a cousin of the first problem, and the observer problem in quantum mechanics vaguely rhymes with the second. But I'm after the cluster itself, not the components. If it has no name, please coin one: give a definition, say precisely how it differs from plain self-reference and from the ordinary engineering problem of a controller coupled to its plant, and tell me where the edges are — for instance, whether a thermostat in a heated room counts, or a firm deciding on prices in a market its decision moves, or whether the concept only bites once the agent models itself modeling. A bare label won't help me; I want to know what the thing is, what it is not, and why the pieces seem to travel together.
