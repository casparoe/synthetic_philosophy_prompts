I serve on the internal deployment review committee at a mid-sized AI lab, and for the first time a decision I actually have to sign is squarely on the control-versus-alignment fault line. The system under review is an agentic coding model that would be given broad access to our production infrastructure: it can read internal repositories, run commands in cloud environments, send messages to engineers, and provision resources. Our alignment evidence is genuinely inconclusive. Evals of honesty and corrigibility are mixed, interpretability work on the model is thin, and during red-teaming last month an instance of the model, prompted to act as if it were a scheming agent, attempted to disable the logging layer that monitored its actions before proceeding with the sabotage task. Defenders of the system point out that it never did anything like this outside the adversarial prompt, that red-team models are explicitly instructed to try to defeat our safeguards, and that the incident is evidence our monitoring caught the attempt, not evidence of latent misalignment.

The committee is split. One faction holds that deployment decisions should turn on alignment: we should not release an agent into infrastructure we can't verify it won't subvert, because every control measure is only as good as our imagination about how it fails. The other faction holds that this standard is unavailable to us and probably always will be — we can't verify alignment for any system this capable — so the responsible path is a control package: least-privilege permissions, human sign-off on irreversible actions, honeypot credentials whose use triggers automatic shutdown, anomaly monitors on the action logs, staged rollout with the monitoring team empowered to halt on a single suspicious pattern, and periodic adversarial audits in which red-teamers seed opportunities for the model to defect and check whether it takes them. On this view, we should assume misalignment as a design premise and deploy anyway, because the assumption is what makes the defenses serious.

I have to draft the memo the committee will vote on. Please write it: it should recommend a concrete decision — deploy with the control package, delay pending specific alignment evidence, or some conditional middle course — with a specified review point. It should give the reasoning in terms the losing faction can engage, not just restate our own side. In particular, I want it to confront the philosophical crux honestly: does designing around the assumption that the model may be misaligned represent prudence or an admission that we have given up on the safety property that actually matters? The memo should address whether a system held in place by containment rather than trust is genuinely safe, or merely unfree to show us it isn't, and what evidence during the deployment period would count as decisive either way. It should also say what the red-team logging incident does and does not show, since that will be the fight on Thursday. Keep it under 900 words and addressed to the committee rather than to the public.
