I run a small discussion group that's a mix of AI safety people and philosophy grad students, and next session is on wireheading and reward tampering. I want to make a handout that's a decision tree / taxonomy rather than another overview essay, and I need your help drafting it.

The starting point of the tree should be a fixed case: an agent (could be a rat with the pleasure centre electrode, could be a human with a wire, could be a policy network that's learned to tamper with its reward signal) that ends up successfully maximising whatever it was set up to maximise, and where everything downstream of that looks degenerate from the outside — bliss loops, reward hacking, fake strawberries that fool the classifier, whatever. The tree should then lead from that single starting point to a diagnosis of what exactly went wrong, or to the verdict that nothing did.

Please build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of famous names — work out the branch questions first, then locate the actual positions as leaves. I've thought about it a bit and I'd want the branching to at least cover: (1) whether the post-tampering state is genuinely good for the agent or not (this is where hedonism splits from desire-satisfactionism, objective list views, and agency-based views, and where the wirehead and the experience machine subject should end up separated if they can be); (2) whether the failure, if there is one, is a mistake about values (the reward was never the thing worth wanting) or a mistake about the world (the agent got the value right but gamed the measurement of it); (3) whether it matters morally that the agent tampered with its own reward versus just passively receiving it — the self-modification question; and (4) whether the whole problem is supposed to be an artefact of naive RL rather than something a sophisticated agent would also face.

Leaves: please get to somewhere around eight or nine, and make sure they include the pure hedonist leaf (where wireheading is simply fine and everyone else is confused), plus the AI-alignment diagnoses — reward misspecification, reward tampering as treacherous behaviour, goal misgeneralisation (the agent was never even chasing the reward), and whatever you'd call the view that says the real problem is we never fixed on a crisp notion of "the agent" whose reward it is in the first place. If a combination of branch answers has no natural occupant, say so and give me one sentence on whether that's because the view is incoherent or just nobody's bothered. And if two leaves can't actually be separated by the branch questions — I suspect the desire-satisfactionist and some versions of the objective list view might collapse together, but you tell me — flag that honestly rather than pretending the tree is cleaner than it is.

Audience is assumed to know basic RL and basic value theory but not the niche literature, so don't lean on specific papers or cite anyone. Every branch node needs to be answerable by someone who's never heard the terms "wireheading" or "reward tampering" before — phrase them as concrete questions about the case, not as "are you a hedonist?". Cheers!
