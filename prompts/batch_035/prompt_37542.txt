Been wondering for a while whether this puzzle I keep running into has a name, or whether anyone's actually worked on it, because it feels too basic to be new. Background on me: no university, I drive a delivery van, and my philosophy comes from long drives with podcasts and too much time on my own brain. I know about Bayes' rule at the "youtube explanation" level, meaning I can update on ordinary evidence fine, but I keep getting information of a different shape and I don't know what to do with it.

Here's the shape. A while back I was hiking in fog, unsure whether I was north or south of a river, and fairly sure that if I was north I was heading east and if I was south I was heading west (long story, the trail forked before the fog rolled in). Met another hiker who said: if you're on the north side, it's twice as likely you're on the eastern trail as the western one. Then she walked off. Notice what she didn't tell me: anything at all about the south side, and anything about how likely I was to be north in the first place. So my probability of "north" should stay exactly the same, right? The conditional is like a rule that only switches on in one case. But something still felt off -- it's information about a hypothetical version of me, and I genuinely can't tell whether learning things about a version of me that might not exist should ever leak back and change my beliefs about the actual world. The second time it happened I was waiting on a biopsy result and my sister (a nurse, being careful with me) would only say if it came back malignant, it's about twice as likely to be the slow-growing kind. It came back benign, and I sat there feeling like the past week of half-planning around the slow-growing kind had been based on nothing, and yet not nothing.

Here's the thing I actually did with it, which is probably where the real puzzle lives. I tried to make her sentence into a plain proposition so I could run Bayes on it -- treat "if A then B" as just "either not-A, or B". Turns out if you do that, her being right at the rate she claimed pins down a weird amount of stuff, and worse, if someone is consistently 90% reliable about conditionals like that, you basically can't be uncertain in the natural way about very many of them at once, the whole thing goes rigid. So that translation is wrong somehow, but every alternative I can think of either treats the conditional as an instruction ("IF north, THEN redistribute your east/west beliefs 2:1") that says nothing about how confident to be in the redistribution, or just shrugs. So my questions: does this general situation -- evidence that arrives as a conditional, and the question of what it does to your beliefs, including in the cases where the condition doesn't happen -- have an established name or literature? If there is a name, I'd like to know how people think you're supposed to update, and specifically whether there's a real difference between "the probability that if A then B" and "the probability of B given A", because they come apart in exactly these cases and I can't tell if that's a deep fact or me confusing myself. And if there's no settled answer, I'd still like to know what the candidate carvings are -- is the problem with conditionals, with probability, or with the idea that you should update at all on speech about worlds that never happened. Please don't just give me a one-word label if you can help it, I've been burned before by a term that turned out to cover only half of what I was asking about.
