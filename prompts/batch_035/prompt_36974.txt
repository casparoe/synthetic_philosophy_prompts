I've been chewing on this stuff for a couple years now, mostly on my lunch breaks, no philosophy background at all, so bear with me. I think I've got a coherent picture of fairness but every so often I notice two of my beliefs snapping at each other and I can't tell if it's a real problem or just me being tired. Here's the full set, numbered so you can point at them:

1. Nobody should end up badly off just because of luck they never controlled. Being born with a bad heart, or into a family with no money, shouldn't mean a worse life. That seems bedrock to me.
2. If things go badly because of choices you made with your eyes open, that's on you. The guy who quits a decent job to surf full time and ends up broke made his bed. Society isn't obliged to make him whole.
3. It's completely fair that someone born naturally brilliant at programming out-earns someone of average ability who works just as hard. Good for them, they hit the genetic jackpot and it's theirs to keep.
4. I don't believe in free will in the strong sense. Every choice anyone makes traces back to genes and upbringing and stuff that happened to them, all the way down. But I still think holding people responsible day to day is right and necessary.
5. All that said, I could never actually support letting the surfer from (2) starve in the street. Everyone, no exceptions, should get an unconditional floor: food, shelter, basic healthcare, whether or not they "deserve" it.
6. If some idiot goes backcountry skiing out of bounds and needs a helicopter rescue, you send the helicopter. You don't check the paperwork while they freeze. Though afterwards, sure, maybe they get the bill.
7. Equality of opportunity, not outcome. We should spend real money making sure kids from poor families get the same start (good schools, tutoring, whatever it takes) — but once you're an adult, your choices are yours.
