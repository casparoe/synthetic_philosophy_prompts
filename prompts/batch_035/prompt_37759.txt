I write a small pop-philosophy newsletter (few hundred readers, mostly not philosophers) and the next issue is on the repugnant conclusion and population ethics generally. I want you to build me a "which population ethics view do you actually hold?" quiz. Roughly 8-10 questions, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options, phrased so a reader with zero background can answer it — no jargon, and no question that just asks "do you think total welfare is what matters?" because that's testing labels, not views. The cases should be things like: a smaller world of very happy people versus a vastly larger world where everyone's life is barely worth living and which is better; whether adding extra happy people to a world, without touching anyone already there, can make it worse; the case where avoiding a smaller amount of suffering for existing people requires creating lots of new lives each with mild suffering; and a non-identity-flavoured case where a policy decision changes which people will exist at all, so you can't point to anyone it harms. I want the numbers kept small and imaginable (two planets, ten billion people, that sort of thing), not abstract set notation, and if you need truly astronomical numbers to make a case bite, say so rather than sneaking them in.

Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the positions: total utilitarianism, the average view, critical-level views, person-affecting views, and the procreation asymmetry as its own leaf if the cases can actually seperate it from plain person-affecting views — if they can't, flag that instead of pretending they can. Add anything else you think belongs as a leaf (variable value views, lexical thresholds, the "our intuitions here are just broken and no theory is right" option), but the leaves I listed must appear. Also point out answer combinations that no serious philosopher actually defends, and say for each whether that's because the combo is incoherent or just unexplored.

One more thing: if you think the whole exercise is doomed because people's judgements on these cases flip depending on framing, or because the cases are too unnatural for anyone to have stable intuitions about them, tell me that straight instead of cheerfully building the quiz anyway — I'd rather find out now than after it goes out. Please write in British English (wellbeing, behaviour, that sort of thing).
