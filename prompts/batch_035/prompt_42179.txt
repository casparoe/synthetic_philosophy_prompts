I'm the grad student rep on our department's colloquium committee, which means I take the minutes but don't get a vote. The actual committee is three professors — Halloran (chair), Beck, and Osei — and our written rule says a speaker can be invited only if (a) their work connects to at least one of the department's listed research strengths and (b) the total cost comes in under the semester's visitor budget. Both conditions are genuinely contested in practice, since the strengths list is vague ("practical ethics" is on there, but does ethics of technology count?), and there's a standing fight about whether an extra hotel night for a second talk hits this semester's budget or next semester's.

I went back through two years of minutes and reconstructed where everyone stood on the two criteria for each past candidate. Kessler (metaphysics of time, drives in from two hours away): all three said she fit and was affordable — invited. Duval (decision theory, business class from Zurich): everyone agreed he fit, but Halloran and Beck said the airfare blew the budget while Osei said it was fine — not invited. Narasimhan (Sanskrit literary theory): Halloran and Beck said it didn't fit the strengths list, Osei said it did, all agreed it was cheap — not invited. Bhatt (philosophy of biology): Halloran and Beck said fit, Osei said not really, all three said affordable — invited. Here's what I only noticed while typing this up: in every one of those cases, it didn't matter whether the chair called a vote on each criterion and then mechanically combined the results, or just called a single up-or-down vote on the whole question — both procedures gave the same answer every time. So the minutes don't actually tell me which procedure we've been running.

Next week we take up Ozdemir, and for the first time the two procedures split. Halloran thinks her work fits practical ethics but that flying her in for two talks busts the budget; Beck thinks her stuff is only adjacent to our strengths but that the cost is fine if she takes the Tuesday flight; Osei is yes on both counts. Criterion by criterion she passes both 2-1, but on a straight up-or-down vote she dies 2-1 (Halloran and Beck would each vote no for opposite reasons, Osei yes). So: what's actually going to happen — I'm fairly sure Halloran just calls whichever procedure gets her preferred outcome, and she has never once been on the losing side of a vote, though maybe I'm pattern-matching on noise — and what *should* the committee do if we want to be consistent with our own record? Which of the past cases is the real closest analog? Beck also has a standing thing about how anything touching the budget shouldn't be decided by a "bare majority" — would requiring unanimity on the budget criterion fit everything we've done so far too, and what would it predict here? And here's the thing I keep chewing on and can't quite settle: is there even a hypothetical candidate where the up-or-down procedure would invite someone that the criterion-by-criterion procedure rejects? I tried to construct one and couldn't, which feels like it can't be a coincidence. My friend who's into social choice theory says this is a famous thing that comes up with courts, but I'd rather work it out on our actual record before the meeting than go read a whole literature.
