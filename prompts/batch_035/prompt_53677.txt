I teach a small seminar on free will and agency (14 students, one 75-minute session), and next week is on sphexishness — the digger wasp thing where she drags her prey to the burrow, pops in to check it, and if you nudge the prey a few centimeters while she's inside she comes out and starts the whole dragging-and-checking routine over, apparently indefinitely. Design an in-class game where they run into their own version of this rather than just hearing about the wasp. My vague thought: everyone gets a written decision procedure with some kind of re-verification trigger built in, you run a round where following the procedure pays off, then you quietly change the context so the trigger starts firing on stuff that's irrelevant — but nobody is told the rules went stale, they just have to keep executing until someone notices, if anyone does. I'd like a spectator role too, so we can compare how the insiders vs the outsiders see the same round, and maybe the spectators get a scoring job so it's not just passive watching. Then a debrief that maps the loop onto the real wasp, onto Dennett's point that the wasp isn't just dumb but tuned to the wrong grain of environment, and onto the question of whether human habit and rule-following is just sphex behavior with a wider net — whether noticing is all free will needs to be. Include rules, materials, a timeline, and tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it. Also be honest about the feature of the idea the game can't reproduce: a wasp presumably can't notice ever, and my students always could in principle, so some of the sting has to be supplied by the facilitator rather than the activity — say how.
