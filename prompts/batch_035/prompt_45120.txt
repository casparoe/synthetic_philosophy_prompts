I lead a small team at an AI lab deciding where to spend the next two years of our research budget, and we're torn between two broad supervision paradigms: process-based supervision, where we grade the intermediate steps a model produces (for instance, its chain of thought or its decomposition of a problem), and outcome-based supervision, where we grade only the final product against a ground-truth or an evaluator's judgment. Before committing, I want a prioritization of the open conceptual and methodological questions that will determine which paradigm deserves the bulk of our effort — not a survey of existing work.

To make the stakes concrete: process supervision seems to promise better interpretability and more direct control over how a model arrives at its answers, but it risks selecting for reasoning that merely reads as careful — a graded reasoning trace can become a performance for the grader, and the grader's own biases then define what "good reasoning" means. Outcome supervision avoids grading artifacts of style, but it inherits the problems of outcome evaluation in domains where we cannot check results ourselves: math with verifiable answers is easy, but open-ended assistance, research help, or agentic tasks force us into the machinery of scalable oversight, debate, or weak-to-strong generalization. There is also a hybrid temptation — verifying process selectively where we can, verifying outcomes elsewhere — whose costs in training-signal inconsistency nobody on my team has thought through carefully.

Please give me a defended ranking of the four or five questions you would tackle first, where each entry addresses: (1) what exactly the question is, stated precisely enough that we could tell whether a project answered it; (2) why it sits upstream of the others — which subsequent choices depend on it; (3) whether it is tractable for a small team without frontier-scale compute, or whether it's a question only a large lab could settle; and (4) what evidence arriving in the next year or two would move it up or down your list. I'm particularly interested in whether you think the definitional question — what even counts as "the process" versus "the outcome" for a system that interleaves private computation with visible reasoning — is a prerequisite for everything else or a philosophical distraction we can reason around, and whether the honesty question (does process supervision produce models that actually use their stated reasoning, as opposed to post-hoc rationalization of answers computed another way) should anchor the whole agenda. Please also flag any question in this space that is genuinely overrated, in the sense that settling it would not change which paradigm we should invest in.
