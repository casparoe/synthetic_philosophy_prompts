I've just been appointed to a regional citizens' assembly that must recommend one of three options for cannabis: continued prohibition with minimal enforcement, Portuguese-style decriminalisation of use without a legal supply, or full legalisation through a tightly regulated state-supervised market. The public submissions all line up the same way — civil libertarians and harm-reductionists for legalisation, paternalists and moralists for prohibition — and I suspect these alignments are lazy rather than principled. To test that suspicion before we draft our report, I want a thought experiment built to make the alignments cross: a realistic one-paragraph scenario in which a consistent civil libertarian should argue for decriminalisation (or even light-touch prohibition) over legalisation, while a consistent paternalist should argue for legalisation — and crucially, each reversal must fall out of the side's own genuine principles, not from either party adopting a watered-down or strawmanned version of its view. The setting should stay within our assembly's remit and options; no invented super-drugs, no perfect enforcement. After the scenario, walk me through what each position recommends and why; then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to the two verdicts if that feature is removed. Finish with the strongest counter-move available to the paternalist who refuses to be conscripted into arguing for legalisation — does she have a principled way out, or must she bite a bullet?
