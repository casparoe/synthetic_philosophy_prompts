So for the past two years me and my coworker Dave have had this running lunch argument that started when he showed me an old dilemma from the rationalist corner of the internet: you have to pick between one person being tortured for 50 years, or a single tiny dust speck landing in the eye of every person in some insanely huge population, written as 3^^^3 (it's a power tower of threes, dave tried to get me to grasp the size of it once and we nearly missed a meeting). Dave is a total multiply-it-out guy — any harm at all, times a big enough number of people, beats the torture, and anyone who disagrees just can't picture big numbers. I'm on the "a speck is basically nothing, and no amount of nothings adds up to a something" side, but I'll admit what gets me is when he asks if I'd take the torture over just a billion specks, and I hesitate for a second, and he pounces on that hesitation like a hawk.

Anyway, I want to weaponize this into content for our group chat. Write me an Onion-style news article, headline plus 400-500 words, reporting that a fictional government agency (invent the name, something like a Bureau of Aggregate Harms or whatever sounds more bureaucratically plausible) has finally settled the debate by running the trade for real: they've collected one dust speck from every citizen in an enormous population, concentrated the whole thing into 50 years of torture for one paid volunteer, and the press release announces it as a smashing efficiency success with total suffering reduced to a single line item. The jokes need to come from getting the philosophy right, not from making either side stupid: the official defending the program should give the actual aggregationalist reasoning (any nonzero harm multiplied by enough people dominates the torture; people who shrug at a speck are being scope-insensitive, our intuitions just break somewhere past a few thousand), and the auditor refusing to sign off should get the real opposing argument (these harms aren't on one ledger; there's a kind-difference or threshold between an annoyance and an atrocity; you can't stack specks until they turn into a person, the same way no number of nickels becomes a marriage). Somewhere in there, work in the nasty continuity problem — the ladder of trades from speck to pinprick to papercut to broken finger on up to torture, where every single step seems obviously fine — maybe as an appendix buried in the agency's annual report that no one at the press conference wants to open. Give me at least two quotes, one from the multiply-it-out official and one from the dissenting auditor, and maybe one from a scope-insensitive member of the public. Keep it deadpan throughout, don't let the article declare a winner, and end with a kicker that lands on what actually unsettles people about this: whichever way you answer, something you believe has to give. Also dave WILL read this, so if you get the structure of the trade wrong he's going to leave a paragraph-long comment about it.
