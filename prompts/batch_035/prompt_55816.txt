I'm a retired analytical chemist — thirty-odd years in pharma QC and method validation, mostly chromatography. I got into philosophy after retiring (long story: a knee surgery, then a lot of waiting rooms), and my reading group has now arrived at the demarcation problem. Popper's falsificationism, plus the people who pushed back on him, especially the ones who argued you can almost always rescue a theory by blaming the apparatus or the auxiliary assumptions instead. Honestly, half the time I read these papers I keep thinking this is just an out-of-specification investigation with the serial numbers filed off, but I want to check whether that instinct is right or whether its just flattering my old job.

So heres the ask: restate the demarcation debate in the vocabulary I actually used at the bench. Do falsificationism as if it were a method-validation exercise — the validation requirement called specificity, where a method has to demonstrate it can distinguish the analyte from interferences, versus Popper's demand that a theory stick its neck out for a decisive failure. Take the Duhem-style objection ("maybe the instrument drifted, maybe the reference standard degraded, maybe the sample was mishandled") and translate it into what we called an OOS investigation: when a batch fails, you don't immediately reject the theory of your manufacturing process, you investigate whether it's the method, the instrument, the sampling — and the SOPs allow limited retesting under rules, which everyone at the bench knows can shade into testing until it passes, if management lets it. And take the critics' claim that real scientists shield their theories rather than dropping them, and render that as system suitability criteria and robustness studies: the pre-approved envelope inside which a result counts. Be faithful — don't quietly build Popper's conclusions into the translation and call it translation.

Then give me the residue report: which parts of the philosophical argument map cleanly onto lab life, which parts you had to distort to make them fit, and whether anything in the philosophers' debate has no counterpart in QC vocabulary at all. And then tell me straight: does the residue mean my lab vocabulary is missing something real, or that the demarcation debate is less substantive than it looks on the page? I have a sneaking suspicion Popper would be horrified by retest rules and Kuhn would find them entirely unsurprising, and if thats roughly right I'd like to know which one my working life actually supports. Write it in normal prose, no bullet-point skeleton, and at the very end give a single last line beginning "Verdict:" — a short phrase answering, honestly, "falsificationism, its critics, or neither, judged from the bench."
