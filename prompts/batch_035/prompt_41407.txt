I'm a grad student teaching assistant for a big intro-to-philosophy course, and our unit on personal identity is built around Daniel Dennett's piece where he narrates his own misadventures as a brain in a vat — the one from the late seventies, in his Brainstorms collection, where the government recruits him to defuse warheads and things escalate from there. You probably remember the setup: his brain (nicknamed Yorick after Hamlet's skull-jester) is extracted and kept in a vat in Houston, radio-linked to his original body, which he pilots around until it gets destroyed by vandals; the brain is then hooked to a new body, and late in the piece there's also a twin-brain, Hubert, whose story leaves him as a separate person, Fortinbras, corresponding with Dennett. The puzzle is where, if anywhere, Dennett himself is located through all this — at the brain, at the body, wherever he seems to be treated as being, or nowhere in particular.

Next week's seminar is on the late twist where Hubert-and-Fortinbras get their own life and the two start writing letters to each other. My section students keep splitting along two lines: half of them think the whole location question is just a verbal dispute, that "where am I" has no fact of the matter and Dennett is having fun at our expense; the other half want to nail down one true answer, usually "I am wherever my brain is." I've been asked to run a discussion about what the story is actually doing and to give the students a sense of which open questions about self-location are live ones, but I keep second-guessing what those even are.

So here's what I want from you: walk me through what you take the genuinely open questions about self-location or personal-location to be after this kind of case, and then tell me which of those questions are most worth an hour of discussion with intro students versus which are worth only a quick mention or none. To be concrete about the candidates as I see them: (1) whether the point-of-view intuition — that I go with whatever has the perspective, which in the original setup points to the brain, not the body — actually holds up, or whether Dennett is showing that it can't survive the later complications; (2) whether the physical-object answer, "I am my brain," is refuted by the story or quietly presupposed by it, since the story never lets the brain itself be replaced and some people read Dennett's closing line as admitting he can't shake the feeling that Yorick is the bedrock; (3) whether the right move is deflationary — that the question about where I am has no answer because there's no single thing "I" refers to in the first place, the way some people read the piece as a sell-job for a deflationary view of the self; and (4) something I suspect belongs on the list but can't articulate: whether the case shows that "where am I" is context-relative, shifting between the brain, the body, and the point of view depending on what we're trying to do with the pronoun. Rank these, argue for the ranking, and say what you'd cut for intro students and why. I'd also like your honest take on whether question 3 and question 4 are genuinely different or just two ways of saying the same deflationary thing — my section would smell blood if I presented them as distinct and then couldn't defend the distinction.
