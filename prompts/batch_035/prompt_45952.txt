I've been trying to work out what I actually believe about tolerating the intolerant, by going with my gut on a bunch of cases, and the results are kind of a mess. Here they are. A country banning a political party whose declared aim is abolishing elections: fine, obviously. A country refusing to ban a party with the same aim once it's polling at 40 percent: also fine?? I keep wanting to say you can't ban it *because it's popular*, which feels backwards — the more dangerous they get, the less I want to suppress them. My university disinviting a eugenics guy the philosophy department wanted to host: fine. The same guy being invited by a student club to debate a biologist: I actually think that's fine too, and I'm not sure I can defend the difference. A social platform banning someone for preaching medical misinformation: fine. The government fining that same person for the same posts: not fine. Popper's old line about tolerating the intolerant right up until they meet arguments with fists: I endorse it wholeheartedly, and then I notice I want to suppress the eugenics guy (who has never thrown a punch) while tolerating a street movement that literally brawls, because it's big and banning it would cause riots. So I can't tell whether my real principle is something like "only suppress the intolerant when they're too weak to fight back" (which sounds hideous when stated like that, and also I'm not sure it's wrong), or whether it's about who does the suppressing — private platforms and universities can, the state mostly can't except against fringe parties. Is there a principle here that saves most of my verdicts without being monstrous? Give me your best candidate (or two if they compete), say which verdict each clause is there to capture, and then tell me what it predicts for a case I haven't listed: a small town whose elected council votes to stop issuing permits for pride parades — is the council's intolerance something my principle says to tolerate, suppress, or fight some other way? And be honest about which of my gut verdicts you'd just throw out rather than contort the principle to keep.
