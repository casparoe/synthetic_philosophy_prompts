My sister and I are splitting up the stuff from our mom's house and it's gotten weirdly heated for two adults arguing about teacups. Her core argument: she should get first pick of more things because she "feels everything more." She's the sensitive one in the family — losing an object she cares about genuinely wrecks her for a week, whereas I'm the even-keeled one and I'm basically over it by dinner. She says splitting 50/50 is crazy because it treats us like two identical people, when in reality every item just matters more to her than it does to me, so an even split gives most of the total happiness to me and most of the total misery to her. And honestly, factually, she's right that she suffers more — I've watched it happen our whole lives.

But something about the whole framing bugs me and I can't pin down what. Partly it feels like she's being rewarded for having bigger reactions — if I'd somehow trained myself to care even less, would my claim shrink further? Partly it's that she "loses" harder but I don't see why her feelings should convert into a bigger pile of objects at the end. And there's a practical problem too: an uncle suggested we each get 100 points to spread across the items however we want, so the things we care intensely about cost more of our points. But obviously we'd both just exaggerate how much we care about everything, so we're back where we started — maybe worse.

Is there any principled way to think about who should get what here, or any real answer to her "it matters more to me" argument, or is the whole thing just feelings all the way down and we should coinflip the contested stuff? I'd especially like to know if there's some actual framework for this, because the teacups are proxies and we both know it.
