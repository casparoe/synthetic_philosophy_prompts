I've been teaching intro ethics for way too long, and every year we hit the is-ought gap and the room splits neatly in two: the kids who think Hume settled it in one paragraph so we can move on, and the kids who think it's obviously false because "torturing puppies is bad, look, I did it, I got an ought from an is." Neither side can construct a decent example. So that's the assignment for you.

Construct a thought experiment where a character derives a concrete moral obligation from premises that are all purely descriptive, and the derivation is deductively valid — i.e., a real, live-looking counterexample to the gap. Constraints: no definitional laundering (so nothing like redescribing the act as "the wronging of an innocent person" and reading the conclusion off the label), no euphemism smuggling a norm in through the back door, ordinary life setting (a landlord, a custody handoff, a group project gone bad — no sci-fi, no trolleys), and one paragraph for the scenario itself. Instrumental oughts don't count — "if you want to keep your job you ought to show up" was already conceded by Hume himself, and the conclusion has to be categorical, not conditional on the character's desires.

After the scenario, do the audit. List the premises, then flag any that turn out to be covertly normative on inspection — and be honest here, the interesting cases almost always have one hiding inside an innocent-looking word like "promise," "need," "harm," or even "someone." This is the part I actually care about for class. Then say what a Humean would say to block the inference, and separately, whether Moore's open question test adds anything in this case that the is-ought gap itself doesn't (they're related but not identical moves, and I want the students to see the difference concretely). Finally, tell me which single word in your own case is doing the smuggling, if one is, and what happens to the derivation if we're banned from using it.

If you genuinely think the gap can't be crossed, that's a legitimate answer — but then give me your best attempt anyway and show exactly where and why it breaks, and it better break for an interesting reason, not because you phoned it in with a strawman derivation.

End with a last line of exactly the form "Verdict: " followed by either "gap holds" or "gap crossed".
