After a career in condensed-matter physics, I have lately been reading philosophy of mind, and Dennett's thought experiment of the careenium — I believe it comes from his essay on "real patterns," though I would not swear to the source, so take my reconstruction of the setup as I give it — is the piece that has kept me up at night. Picture a frame in which steel balls, simmballs in Dennett's coinage if I remember it right, careen about, colliding and ricocheting, in strict obedience to Newtonian mechanics. Nothing else happens in the frame; the microdynamics is closed. Yet viewed at a coarser grain, the evolving patterns of ball motion implement a population of simulated agents — simms — whose beliefs and desires can be read off the goings-on, and whose deliberations issue in exactly the trajectories the balls were going to follow anyway. Dennett's question, and mine: do the symbols push the substrate around? When a simm acts on a belief, does that belief cause anything, or is the only genuine causation down at the level of steel?

My instinct as a physicist is that there is nothing here worth losing sleep over. In my old trade we happily assign causal powers to multiply realizable macro variables: temperature is nothing over and above molecular motion and is realized differently in a solid than in a gas, yet it is temperature differences that, in the effective equations, drive the heat flow, and nobody calls thermodynamics a bookkeeping fiction. Perhaps the intentional level stands to the neural level as thermodynamics stands to statistical mechanics, and the worry dissolves. But I gather the philosophers disagree, and the exclusion argument associated with Jaegwon Kim — that where the physical level fully suffices for an effect, there is no causal work left over for anything at a higher level to do — seems to have real teeth here. Unlike temperature, the contentful level appears to be picked out by a pattern in interpreter-relative description rather than by a macro variable with its own effective equations of motion, and the careenium is built precisely to rub that difference in.

Write an essay on whether content-level causation is real, using the careenium as the running test case. Weigh the exclusion argument and the overdetermination worry against pattern-realist and other replies, and tell me where my thermodynamic analogy helps and, more importantly, where it breaks. You may bring in whatever else from philosophy of mind bears on the question, provided you explain it rather than name it. Reach a definite conclusion — the symbols do causal work, or they are bookkeeping, or something in between with the difference made precise — and defend it against what you judge to be the strongest objection. Please do not end on a noncommittal note; a physicist can accept that the macro-level is causally real because it earns its keep in equations, and I want to know whether the intentional level can earn its keep the same way or must earn it differently.
