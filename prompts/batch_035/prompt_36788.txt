I've been sorting historical famines into two piles, natural disasters and political crimes, and I suspect my labels follow mood rather than principle. Here are my verdicts. The Great Leap Forward is a political crime, even though the aim was utopian rather than exterminatory and the scale of the deaths seems to have taken the leadership by surprise. The Irish famine of the 1840s is also a crime in my book — I blame the continuation of food exports and the doctrinal insistence that relief not interfere with the market far more than I blame the blight, even though the blight really was a plant disease and not a policy. Bengal 1943 I count as a crime too, a milder one: my impression is that food was not dramatically scarcer than usual, and the deaths came from wartime priorities, panic buying, and the army's needs. The Dutch hunger winter of 1944-45, by contrast, I refuse to call a crime, even though it was caused entirely by human action — a blockade plus a railway strike — and killed thousands. It feels to me like an act of war, closer to a bombardment: terrible, but a different category. And one hypothetical: a poor but well-governed country with a free press suffers a genuine, verified collapse of its harvest; the government does everything reasonably available to it — imports what it can afford, distributes what it has fairly — and some people still die. I want to call that a natural disaster, not a policy failure at all. Yet I'm also attached to a generalization I picked up somewhere: that in the modern era no famine with substantial deaths has occurred in a country with a free press and competitive elections, and that this fact is doing real explanatory work. Can you state a principle — perhaps about who could have prevented the deaths, or about what the deaths reveal about how a society normally distributes food — that fits these verdicts, and tell me honestly which of my labels it cannot reproduce? Then apply it to a case I haven't given: a closed dictatorship whose leadership knows people are starving, could feed them without giving up anything it cares about, and simply never gets around to it — is that the same kind of crime as the Great Leap, or a worse one, or a different one? And should my Netherlands verdict survive, or is it the one that has to go?
