So, bit of background on me: I write novels for a living, mid-career, the kind that gets called "quietly ambitious" in reviews, and for the last year and a half I've been working on a book whose narrator wakes up every section not knowing which day of her life it is — the conceit being that her memory resets each night, so she has to reconstruct her location in her own biography from fragmentary evidence. While researching this I stumbled into the actual philosophy literature on self-locating belief, and specifically the Sleeping Beauty problem, and now I can't stop thinking about it, which is a problem because I'm supposed to be writing prose, not probability theory.

Here's what I gather has happened, and please correct me if I'm mangling it. Beauty agrees beforehand to a fair coin flip; if heads she's woken once, if tails twice (Monday and Tuesday), with her memory of the first waking erased, and each time she's asked how confident she is that the coin landed heads. Thirders, following Adam Elga, say her credence on waking should be 1/3; halfers, following the reply David Lewis wrote, say it stays at 1/2 because when she wakes she learns nothing new — whatever she observes on waking, she knew beforehand she'd observe it sometime. And apparently this has now been going on for a quarter century, with Dutch book arguments on both sides, "double-halfers" who split the credence across days, betting-odds disputes, and at least one prominent philosopher (I read this somewhere and now can't find it, so treat with caution) saying the whole thing should be dissolved rather than decided. What I don't understand, as an outsider, is why it hasn't converged. Both sides agree on the setup, the coin is by stipulation fair, there's no dispute about any physical fact in the world — and yet from what I can tell nobody has changed camps in twenty years.

My novelist's hypothesis, offered as just that, a hypothesis: the two camps are answering different questions under one name. The halvers are answering "what should Beauty believe about the coin," and the thirders are answering something more like "what should Beauty believe about this awakening, about which moment of her life she's in" — and since my whole book is literally about a character who has to locate herself in her own timeline, I'd genuinely like to know whether that reading holds up. Is the impasse verbal, is it a substantive disagreement about what probability even is (a credence in a person vs. a frequency across awakenings), is it a hidden fight over whether indexical evidence counts as evidence? And the thing I most want an answer to: what, if anything, would count as progress here — a distinction, a formal result, a change of question — or should I expect my narrator, and the poor philosophers, to stay permanently unsure which day it is? A diagnosis with reasons please, not a survey of the literature — I can get summaries anywhere, what I need is someone to tell me what kind of disagreement this actually is and whether anything could end it.
