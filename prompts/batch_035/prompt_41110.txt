Been hanging around LessWrong, the EA Forum, and ACX comment threads for the better part of a decade, and there's a pattern I keep bumping into that I still don't have a clean label for. Figured I'd ask here instead of continuing to half-describe it in comment sections.

When people argue we should redirect huge resources toward preventing extinction or shaping AI, the argument almost always has this shape: the stakes are literally astronomical, AND we happen to live in the tiny sliver of time where the outcome is still up for grabs. The first half gets defended to death — wasted cosmic endowment, expected value, dwarfing all present-day causes, fine. But the second half, the "and it's *now*, this century, basically our generation" part, usually gets asserted in passing. Sometimes literally one sentence, something like: and crucially this is the window where it gets decided. Then the career advice and the funding priorities all fall out of it as if the premise had been established.

It's also more than a premise — it does psychological work. People who believe it talk like they're the generation that tilts the entire future, and you can see it change which jobs they take, how urgent everything feels, whether they think ordinary institutional caution even applies to them.

And here's the thing that really bugs me: the *critics* mostly do it too. When people attack this cluster of views, they go after the ethics (staring at a roulette wheel of infinite stakes, can't compare lives across time, etc.), while the timing claim sits there untouched, doing all the actual load-bearing work. Both sides fight over the consequences of the conditional and nobody argues about the antecedent.

So, two questions really. One: is there an established name for the thesis itself — the "we live at the uniquely influential moment in history" claim? I know it's adjacent to existential risk as a concept, but that's the stakes, not the timing. I want the name for the timing claim specifically, and whether it's an actual term people use or just a thing people gesture at.

Two: is there a name for the argumentative situation — the load-bearing premise being the undefended one while everyone argues about the parts that were never really in dispute? It's not motte-and-bailey, nobody is retreating anywhere, the premise just never gets engaged. It's not begging the question either, as far as I can tell, because they're not assuming their conclusion, they're assuming the thing that makes their conclusion matter. Or maybe it is circular in some subtle way, I genuinely don't know.

I want a real answer here, not vibes. Tell me what's settled terminology among people who actually work on this versus what's contested — my impression is the timing claim itself is VERY contested but maybe the name for it isn't? If there are competing labels floating around, give me all of them and tell me how they differ in what they claim, don't just hand me one author's branding as if it were the field's consensus term. Same for the meta-question: if there's no established name for the undefended-premise thing, just say so plainly, and if you want to coin something for it, flag clearly that it's your coinage and not something I can go find in a paper. And if you know of neighboring concepts that I'm apt to confuse these two things with (I already suspect a few), spell out where the borders are, since that's the part I actually care about.
