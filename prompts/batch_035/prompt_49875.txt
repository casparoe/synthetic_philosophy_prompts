Consider the following variant of the Sleeping Beauty experiment. The protocol is the usual one — Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is tossed, she is woken once on Monday and, if the coin lands tails, once again on Tuesday with her memory of the Monday waking erased and no indication of the day — with a single addition announced to her in advance: at every waking, before she is asked anything, the experimenter will truthfully state which day it is. The experiment is now underway; the experimenter has just said over the intercom that it is Monday, and Beauty has a few minutes to decide what credence to report in heads.

Write a dialogue between a halfer and a thirder, watching from the observation room, who disagree about what she should say — the halfer taking David Lewis's side of his exchange with Adam Elga, the thirder taking Elga's. Only these two should speak. The dialogue must work through two stages. First, reconstructing the moment of waking, they disagree about Beauty's credence before the announcement: the thirder says one third, the halfer one half, and each should present the other's reasoning in its strongest form rather than as a caricature. Second, and this is the real subject, they disagree about what the announcement does. The thirder holds that being told it is Monday returns Beauty to one half, the announcement exactly undoing the discount her view applied at the waking, and she should explain the mechanics of that. The halfer then faces a fork she must not dodge: if she updates on the announcement by ordinary conditionalization, she is committed to Beauty answering two thirds, so she must either argue that two thirds is not the absurdity it first appears or take the double-halfer escape and claim the answer stays at one half, in which case she owes an account of what is wrong with treating the statement that today is Monday as ordinary evidence about a fixed chance event. The thirder should press her on whether that escape is principled or ad hoc, and the halfer should press back on why an announcement about the calendar should move a credence about a coin at all. The exchange should be genuine back-and-forth, with each character responding to the other's actual arguments and at least one real concession along the way, and it must not end in a handshake compromise; close it by locating the precise point at which they still disagree, for instance whether the announcement is evidence about the coin or only about where in the experiment Beauty currently is.

Present the dialogue as continuous prose only, in the manner of a Platonic dialogue: attributions folded into the running text (the thirder replied, the halfer conceded), no script format with one line per speaker, and no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables of any kind.
