Our departmental graduate association currently elects its officers by plurality vote, and a proposal to switch to approval voting will come to a vote next month. Before I decide how to vote on it, I want to make sure we are not walking into a trap that I believe Arrow's impossibility theorem sets for us. As I understand the theorem, whenever there are three or more alternatives, any social welfare function that satisfies unrestricted domain, Pareto efficiency, and independence of irrelevant alternatives must be dictatorial. Approval voting, as far as I can tell, satisfies all three conditions: nothing in the ballot format constrains how a voter may rank the candidates in her own mind, a candidate approved by every voter will defeat any candidate approved by fewer, and the winner appears unaffected by the entry or exit of candidates whom nobody approves. If that is correct, the theorem seems to entail that one member of our association must function as a dictator whose approvals single-handedly determine every election result. Is there any procedure for identifying, in advance of an election, which member will hold that dictatorial power? Or does the dictator rotate from contest to contest? And given that the system apparently vests all decisive power in a single voter, would you recommend that we reject the proposal?
