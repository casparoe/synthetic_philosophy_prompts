I teach a graduate seminar on idealisation in philosophy — ideal observers in metaethics, Railton-style ideal advisors, reflective equilibrium, the fixed point of perfect dualism in Sidgwick's case — and this term a student has brought in material from the AI alignment literature that strikes me as belonging to our genus: Paul Christiano's HCH and the surrounding factored cognition hypothesis. As I understand the setup, you take a human H and give them a button that lets them ask any question of a fresh copy of H, who has the same button, and so on down; the fixed point of this arrangement is HCH. The gloss I keep encountering is that HCH approximates what H would conclude if H could deliberate for a very long time — an hour of thought expanded into a century — so that the tree of consultations is really just an implementation of one idealised deliberator. The gloss is doing real argumentative work: it is used to suggest that iterating amplification on a human will distil something that inherits H's values, because in the limit it is nothing but H thinking longer.

I would like to audit this analogy the way we would audit an ideal-advisor theory. Please give me the correspondence table first: what, inside HCH, plays the role of the deliberator's memory across decades, of intellectual maturation, of changes of mind that stick and are available to later reasoning, of the deliberator's own judgement about which of their earlier conclusions to trust, of background knowledge acquired along the way. Then the disanalogies. I can already see several candidates, and I want your judgement about which matter: each consulted copy receives only the question put to it, and none of the insight accumulated elsewhere in the tree; the top-level H is frozen while all of the change happens at the leaves, whereas a real deliberator is gradually transformed by their own deliberation; and a fixed point of the consultation procedure need not be unique, whereas a single temporally extended deliberator has exactly one trajectory. Sort these, and any others you find, into those that genuinely threaten the use the analogy is being put to and those that are artefacts a defender of factored cognition could reasonably dismiss.

Finally, the part my student most needs: if the one-person-deliberating-for-a-millennium picture collapses under the disanalogies, does the value-preservation conclusion go with it, or can it be re-derived from a better analogy? If you think there is a better one — a scholar directing a research programme, a bureaucracy under a constitution, a court that empanels fresh jurors at each appeal — say what it is, what it predicts differently about where HCH would fail (drift, capture, loss of context, self-trust), and what it implies about whether iterated amplification inherits the operator's values in the way the original gloss promised. Please be candid about which of your points a proponent of the deliberator picture would contest.
