So I TA for an intro ethics course, and this week we're covering supererogation — acts that are good to do but not wrong to skip. I want to build a handout where a bunch of acts get placed on a spectrum, with tiers running roughly from "you're kind of a jerk if you don't do this" up to "saintly, no reasonable person could demand this of you". Then in class the students have to argue about whether the acts landed in the right tier.

Here's the list I've got so far. Put each act in a tier (rename or add tiers if you want), give a one-sentence reason, and flag the ones you think are genuinely borderline rather than just quietly placing them:

- giving up your bus seat for someone who looks tired
- donating blood every couple of months for years on end
- giving 10% of your income to effective charities
- donating a kidney to a stranger while you're alive
- falling on a grenade to save five comrades
- forgiving someone who badly wronged you and never apologized
- staying late at work, unpaid, to help a coworker hit a deadline
- keeping a promise that has become very costly to keep
- calling out your boss over a racist joke at a work dinner

Two things I keep going back and forth on, would love your take: (1) a few of these seem to change tier depending on how long you've been doing them — the person who's donated blood for 30 years is a local hero, but the person who did it for 10 years and then quietly stopped gets side-eyed, as if the repeated supererogatory acts slowly turned into a duty. Is there any principled way to deal with acts that become expected over time, or do I just have to tell the students "it's messy"? (2) forgiveness — I honestly can't decide if it's an act at all (something you do) or more like a feeling you can't just decide to have. If it's the latter, can it even be supererogatory, or is it in the wrong category altogether? Maybe it deserves its own tier with a note that it's arguement for the students rather than an answer.

Keep it casual, this is a handout not a textbook — definately no footnotes. Thanks!
