I'm a high school junior who has never taken a philosophy class but who has spent the last three years building games — first in Unity, now in a custom engine in C++, with a lot of time in the modding scene. I've been reading about the philosophical zombie argument on my own, and I keep having the feeling that it's about something I deal with constantly in game architecture, but I can't tell whether that feeling is insight or a category error. So here is my request.

Restate the zombie conceivability argument against physicalism in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can. To be concrete about what that vocabulary contains: I think in terms of headless servers running the same deterministic simulation as the client build, with no rendering pipeline at all; save files that fully capture game state, so that any two machines deserializing the same save produce identical subsequent play; replays and input playback, where the whole run of a game is reconstructible from the initial state plus the input stream; entity-component-systems where behavior is data-driven; and the difference between the simulation tick and the presentation layer that merely samples it. When I try to do the translation myself, the zombie world comes out as something like "a headless build of our universe: same simulation, same save file, inputs identical, everything the physics and AI systems do is bit-for-bit the same, and the only thing missing is the rendering" — and the physicalist then seems to be claiming that the rendering just is the experience, while the anti-physicalist seems to be claiming that something could be missing even in a build where every system is present and running. But I'm not confident that's a fair mapping, and I suspect my rendering metaphor is quietly doing what the philosophers would call begging the question, because in my games the renderer is definitionally separable from the simulation in a way that may be exactly what's at issue.

So the deliverable I want is a careful translation followed by a residue report. First: which parts of the argument translate cleanly onto my architecture notions — the notion of physical duplicates, the conceivability step, the explanatory-gap intuition, the response that we're just bad at imagining what a full physical duplicate would entail. Which of these map onto things like desync, headless builds, or the fact that a replay is guaranteed identical? Second: which parts translate only with distortion — where you have to fudge the game metaphor to keep it running, and where the fudge is doing philosophical work. Third: which parts have no expression at all in my vocabulary. In particular, the first-person asymmetry — the fact that the argument turns on there being something it is like to be the player character from the inside, not just what the third-person simulation contains — is something I genuinely don't know how to state in engine terms, since in my world any "camera" is just another entity the simulation updates, and a build with no camera is not missing any gameplay.

Then answer the question I actually care about: is the residue a defect of my vocabulary or a defect of the argument? That is, when the zombie argument resists translation into simulation-and-presentation terms, is that because games are missing a concept that reality has, or because the argument was never as clear as it looked? I would especially like your view on whether my recurring intuition — that "consciousness" is like the question of whether anyone is actually playing the game versus merely simulating it, which the engine itself cannot register either way — is a genuine insight, a repackaging of something philosophers already have a name for, or a confusion I should be talked out of. Do not soften the translation to spare my metaphor, and do not smuggle in the game framework's assumption that presentation is always separable from simulation and then present that as a finding.
