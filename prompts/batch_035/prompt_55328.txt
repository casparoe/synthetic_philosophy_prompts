Been trying to figure out when it's actually okay to get your moral beliefs from other people, going by my gut on cases. Here's where I land, but I can't find the thread connecting them:

My closest friend has read basically everything on the ethics of abortion, thought about it for years, and eventually tells me her considered view. I more or less adopt it because I trust her. Feels fine, even smart — I can't become an expert on eveything. Basically the same thing but it's a famous philosopher's paper arguing factory farming is wrong, and I quit meat on his authority without being able to reconstruct the argument if pressed. This one feels off, like I've done something shallow, even if he's right. There's a panic situation — my sister's about to go into risky surgery and asks me a hard moral question I hadn't anticipated, I call a friend who's calm and good at this stuff, take her word, act on it. Totally fine. My uncle outsources all his moral views to his church: whatever the pastor says, that's his view, no reflection on his part ever. Feels bad, even though the pastor's track record is probably better than my uncle's own reasoning. Testimony as a pointer: a friend tells me her position on meat and it sends me off to actually read and think for a month, and I end up agreeing with her. Fine, obviously. Then a case where testimony gets me to belief directly: my doctor tells me the medical facts about my pregnancy complications, and a hospital ethics consultant tells me what's permissible. I'm fine deferring to the doctor on the facts but feel like the moral call has to be mine, even though the consultant has thought about this stuff way more than me. And the non-moral comparison: the same friend tells me which neighborhood has less crime and I just believe her, zero squeamishness.

My tentative principle was something like: moral testimony is okay as a pointer but not as a source — other people can tell you what to think about, but the belief has to be earned by working through the reasons yourself. But I notice "panic" seems to be doing all the work in the surgery case, and I wasn't busy or panicked in the friend-on-abortion case, I was just lazy, which the principle seems to condemn and I don't want it to. Also the principle doesn't obviously explain why my uncle bugs me — he gets pointed at moral questions every Sunday and never follows up, which is roughly what I'd be doing in the philosopher case, yet that one bugs me less.

So: is there one principle that gets all or most of these verdicts, or close? If there is, does the squeamishness in the philosopher and uncle cases track something real (like acting without understanding, or a kind of self-respect thing) or is it just a mood I should get over? Which of my verdicts would you overturn to get a cleaner principle? And I'd want to hear what your principle says about the case I keep chewing on: suppose my whole moral outlook was inherited from my parents and never once examined by me, and it happens to be a good one — have I done something wrong there, or nothing at all?
