I'm preparing a short position statement on Dennett's "Where Am I?" for a personal identity seminar, and I would like a consistency check before I present it. Here are the commitments I plan to defend:

(1) I am a straightforward physicalist about persons: a person is a wholly physical thing, and there is no nonphysical ingredient in being one.

(2) Where a person is, is where his body is, not where his brain is. In Dennett's split setup — Yorick in its vat in Houston, Hamlet tunneling under Tulsa — Dennett is with his body; the thing floating in the vat is an organ of his, no more "him" than his heart would be if it were on a bypass machine in the next room. When he stands in the lab looking through the glass at Yorick, what he sees is a remarkable object he owns, not himself.

(3) Persistence is psychological. When Dennett learns that the doctors flipped the master switch to Hubert, the bit-for-bit computer duplicate, at some unknown earlier time — and that he cannot say when, because the two run in lockstep — the right reaction is that he has survived without a hitch. And if Yorick were destroyed the day after he learns this, nothing of importance would have happened to him.

(4) Having a point of view is something the realizing system does. In the radio-link setup the body is an elaborate prosthesis, and the seeing that seems to go on under Tulsa is going on wherever the system that processes the signals is.

(5) A person can survive the destruction of his entire body and continue as the brain in the vat, fed artificial inputs — as nearly happened to Yorick in the year it lay dormant after Hamlet was destroyed. He would then be located in the vat.

(6) Identity is strictly one-to-one: a person cannot become two. Suppose that, years later, the government were to give Yorick a new body of its own while Hubert kept Fortinbras, so that two psychologically continuous individuals walked around, each with an equal claim on Dennett's memories. At most one of them would be Dennett, and there may be no fact of the matter which.

I would like to know whether this package hangs together. Which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, and how badly? Which apparent conflicts dissolve on closer inspection? For each genuine tension, what is the cheapest revision that would resolve it, and what would that revision cost me elsewhere in the set? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating everything as equally serious, and if a single revision would clean up most of the problem, tell me which one it is.
