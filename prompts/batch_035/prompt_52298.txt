We just finished the Ant Fugue in my philosophy of mind seminar (reading GEB), and for the last three weeks the class has been stuck on whether Aunt Hillary literally has a mind of her own or whether that's just a cute way of talking. The positions are fixed and nobody budges. The holist side says: individual neurons are as dumb as individual ants, intelligence is an organization effect, and if you accept the brain's own intelligence you've already conceded the principle — the colony's foraging and defense behaviour tracks features of its environment, is predictable and explainable by belief/desire ascriptions, and that's all there is to having a mind. The individualist side says: the only organisms with intentional states are the ants; colony-level talk is bookkeeping, exactly like calling a thermostat stubborn, and saying the "same principle" applies to brains just begs the question against minds that evolved as single organisms.

Two things happened that made me think the disagreement has a weird structure. First, someone proposed slowing ant-to-ant signal transmission down and speeding neurons up until the two systems ran at comparable timescales, and both sides instantly agreed this was irrelevant — but each seemed to think it was irrelevant for different reasons nobody articulated. Second, someone asked whether Aunt Hillary could suffer — whether the colony, not the ants, could be a moral patient — and a debate that had felt purely verbal suddenly felt like there had to be a fact of the matter, though nobody could say why the moral question changed things when the descriptive one hadn't.

My working hypothesis is that this is two groups answering different questions under one name: one asking what the intentional stance is licensed for, the other asking what grounds intentional states, with the moral status question smuggling in a third. But I might be wrong and it could be genuinely substantive all the way down. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, using what each side actually says, and tell me what if anything would count as progress — or whether the moral status move is just a confusion that should be untangled rather than resolved. And please be upfront about how confident you are in the diagnosis and which part of it you're least sure of.
