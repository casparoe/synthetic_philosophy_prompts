For years I've read, and argued in, the population-ethics threads that recur on the rationalist blogs and forums, and they keep collapsing in the same way: people who adopt the same label — 'I hold the procreation asymmetry', 'I'm a total utilitarian' — turn out to disagree about the actual cases, and the argument proceeds on labels rather than on anything that could resolve it. I'd like a decision tree that sorts commenters by what they say about cases.

The tree should start from a commitment nearly everyone in these threads accepts — that it is wrong to create a child you know will have a life of unrelieved agony — and branch only on concrete cases, each phrased so that a commenter can answer it without knowing which position it points towards. It should use at least these splits: (a) creating a child whose life will be just barely worth living, versus creating no one — better, worse, or neutral; (b) a non-identity case, where the child you create now is somewhat worse off than the different, better-off child you would have created had you waited a few years; (c) creating a child whose life will contain a fixed amount of suffering versus inflicting an identical amount of suffering on an existing person — is the first as bad as the second, less bad, or does it not admit of comparison?; and (d) whether adding happy lives to the world makes it better in a way that can trade off against suffering elsewhere, drawn separately for the same-number case and the different-number case.

The leaves should locate at least the total utilitarian, the standard procreation asymmetry on which creating miserable lives is bad while creating happy lives is not good, a person-affecting view that grounds the wrongness in wronging the child created (making explicit what the non-identity case does to it), a critical-level view, and any others you think the logical space requires. Flag any pair of leaves your cases cannot really separate, and note any combination of answers that no actual view occupies, with a sentence on whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. At most eight branch questions, and please write the answer in British English.
