longtime rationalist blog reader here (OB since the dialup days, permanent LW lurker). a friend teaches an upper-level ethics seminar and asked me to draft one question for her take-home final while she's on parental leave, and past-me of course said yes. topic: standing to blame and hypocrisy. the standard exam question here is just "does the hypocrite lose standing to blame?", and students answer it by reciting the usual cases and saying yes or no, which is basicaly useless. so I want a question with a twist that forces actual thinking. here's the twist I've been kicking around — build the question around it, or improve it if you can:

imagine a public registry, call it a blame ledger, where you can pre-declare your own violations of a norm before blaming anyone else for it. once you've listed your violations, the hypocrisy objection seems to evaporate — you're not a hypocrite anymore, just an honest sinner. but something still feels off: standing now looks like something you buy back with a checkbox ritual, more like disclosing a conflict of interest than a moral achievement. and if confession does NOT restore standing, then the standing loss can't really be about falsely signaling your own purity — which is what half the explanations of the hypocrisy condition seem to say it's about. either way something has to give.

so please write me (a) the essay question itself, for a 90-minute answer by upper-level students, built around this kind of case or a better version of it if you have one; (b) a model answer of about 600 words; and (c) a marking scheme out of 20 that names the specific moves an A answer must make vs. where a B answer stops — especially how students handle the confession case and the awkward implication that if only the blameless can blame, nobody can, and blame seems to do real work keeping norms sticky. one thing I really want: after the marking scheme, add a short note on which parts of your model answer you're actually least confident in, and what a sharp philosopher could say that would flip your marking scheme — I don't want to grade students against a take I only half believe in. and no padding the model answer with a reading list, she wants the reasoning not a bibliography.
