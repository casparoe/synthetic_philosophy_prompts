I'm a political theorist who mostly works on contemporary collective responsibility stuff (reparations, truth commissions, that kind of thing) and I've somehow agreed to give a talk at a German history institute defending a qualified Fischer thesis: that Germany's leadership in July 1914 knowingly accepted, and to a meaningful degree willed, a general European war, and that this grounds the further claim that Germany as a state bore distinctive responsibility for WWI -- not sole responsibility, and not the Article 231 war-guilt version, but something my talk leans on the notion of a continuing corporate agent to make sense of. My sketch: the blank cheque was an unusual move because Berlin paid to close an exit rather than take one; the September Programme shows war aims that read more like pre-existing ambitions than improvisations; and "holding Germany responsible" survives the fact that the individuals are dead because states, unlike people, persist.

The audience is the problem. There's an emeritus who was a student when Fischer's Germany's Aims in the First World War landed and still has scar tissue from that fight (he'll have views on the Riezler diaries). There's a cluster of younger global-history people formed by Clark's The Sleepwalkers who think the "who's to blame" question is itself a badly framed question and prefer structural accounts of 1912-14. There's a diplomatic historian who thinks everyone underweights Russian and British decision-making (she'll have McMeekin-ish sympathies). And the institute has invited two philosophers for me: a hard methodological individualist and someone who works on structural injustice.

Draft my Q&A prep sheet: around eight objections I'll actually get, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way that specific person would put it, with a one-line reply I can blurt out if interrupted mid-sentence plus a fuller two-minute version, and a note on where the reply is soft. Separate the objections that say Fischer got the history wrong from the ones that say even a fully vindicated Fischer thesis wouldn't establish national responsibility -- my replies are different and I keep muddling them. Tell me what to concede up front and include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. Don't assume I can read German.
