so I write longform pieces on science and ideas for a general-readership magazine, and I've run into an editor problem I can't quite articulate. I'm working on a piece pegged to a WWI anniversary about why the war broke out — July 1914, the assassination, the alliance commitments, the mobilization schedules, German anxiety about Russian rearmament, the whole mood of the era, etc. My editor keeps pushing me to name "the main cause." Her argument: readers need one story, and we do this kind of thing all the time in science writing anyway — we say the spark caused the fire even though the fire also needed oxygen and dry wood, and nobody calls that a lie.

Here's what's bugging me. Nearly every historian I've interviewed insists their favored factor was necessary — remove it and there's no general European war in 1914, or at least not then. But if five different things were each individually necessary, then none of them seems "main" in the way she wants — or does it? And then a couple of them argue the exact opposite: that the situation was so unstable that any two or three of the factors would have produced a war on their own, so picking out one cause is backwards in a different way. I can't tell if these two worries cancel out, reinforce each other, or point at different things.

So: do I push back on her and write the "many causes, no single main one" piece, or is she actually right that singling out a cause is a normal, defensible move that writers and even scientists make all the time? Don't give me both options and let me pick — take a side. If you think she's right, tell me what principle does the singling out (what makes something the cause rather than a cause), and then apply it: which cause does your principle actually select for 1914, and why that one over the closest runner-up? If you think she's wrong, don't just say "it's complicated" — give me the honest one-paragraph framing I could actually print, the one that doesnt pretend there's a main cause but also doesnt just dump a list on the reader. And if it matters where I come down: I want to be able to defend whatever I write to her, not just note that historians disagree.
