Right, so I've just gone back to work after taking a chunk of shared parental leave (baby's eight months old, I'm writing this during her afternoon nap, so bear with me). Since she was born I keep noticing something and I want to know whether its got a proper name in the literature, because "double standard" doesn't feel precise enough to be useful.

The pattern: the exact same bit of parenting, done by each of us, gets read completely differently depending on which of us did it. When I took six weeks off, my manager called me a good man and someone in HR put a congratulations in the team newsletter, as if I'd run a marathon. When my wife took the longer stretch, her manager's first question was whether she was sure she wanted to come back full time. My dad says I'm "babysitting" when I've got the baby on my own for an afternoon; nobody has ever said my wife is babysitting her own child — she's just looking after her, which is treated as the default state of affairs. When I do the night feed I get credit for it; when she does it, it's invisible. It's as if the baseline expectation is set at zero for me and at 100% for her, so the same effort lands above the line for me and at or below it for her.

I've half-heard of research showing mums take a pay hit after having kids while dads don't, or even do better — is that the same thing, or just the money-shaped version of it? And is there an established concept for the reading itself: the way an identical act gets described as going above and beyond for one parent and as merely doing your job (or even slacking off) for the other? If there is a proper term, I want to know how it's meant to differ from a generic double standard and from the plain fact that the leave policies are worded neutrally while the outcomes aren't. And if there isn't an established term, coin one — but define it properly, with the edges and a couple of boundary cases, because I definately don't just want a label I can slap on an argument with my dad.
