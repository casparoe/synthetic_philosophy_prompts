Retired physicist here, got into philosophy a few years after I stopped running experiments. Heres something I keep chewing on and can't sort out. All my working life I paid taxes and National Insurance in Britain, and now the state pays my pension and will treat me on the NHS. Foreigners who never paid in generally can't just move here and claim those things, and my government sends less than one percent of national income abroad as aid, most of it to people far worse off than the poorest Brit. And yet I spent half my career on international collaborations where borders genuinely didn't matter to who deserved credit for a result. So what does the welfare state actually presuppose about the claims compatriots have on each other? Is it that paying in creates a reciprocal debt, that being subject to the same coercive institutions makes us owe each other something special, or just shared identity and sentiment? And do the different bits of the arrangement presuppose the same thing -- does a small but nonzero aid budget cohere with a generous pension system, or are they quietly running on inconsistent theories? Finally, if you swapped in a properly cosmopolitan view, which part would have to change first?
