i teach a phil of art survey, and this week is the paradox of tragedy: why do we actively seek out in art — sad films, King Lear, sad songs on repeat — the exact states we spend our lives avoiding (grief, fear, pity)? my students always end up in two camps: the ones who say it's just not the same sadness once it's framed and at a distance, and the ones who think that answer is way too quick. i want to give them a pantoum, because a pantoum is basically the form of that first answer: lines come back verbatim but in new contexts, and their meaning shifts. so the repeated lines should read one way on first appearance and differently when they circle back — that's the "same but not the same" move doing its work in the form, not just stated in the content. but please don't let it land as a tidy solution: the final stanza should leave it genuinely open whether the reframing dissolves the paradox or only restates it prettier. four or five quatrains is plenty. and it should still work as a poem for someone who's never heard of hume's essay — no name-dropping, just the thing itself. one typo is fine in my notes above but the poem itself should be clean.
