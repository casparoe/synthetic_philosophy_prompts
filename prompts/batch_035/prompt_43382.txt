(2) Ben said all this proves is that she's being irrational or lazy: a rational agent always has *some* credence, and even if she won't state one, her betting behavior would reveal one anyway — so a sufficiently clever bookie could exploit her, exactly like any other incoherence. (4) Ben replied that sets of probabilities are useless as a decision theory: whenever one option is better on some functions and worse on others, the framework just goes silent, and the standard escape is to bolt on a rule like maximizing the minimum expectation over the set — which is just pessimism dressed up in notation, and secretly turns the set back into one precise ranking anyway.
