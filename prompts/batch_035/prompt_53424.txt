so I've been reading about the verisimilitude mess and I can't figure out whether the debate ended because people gave up on the question or because it dissolved into people talking past each other, and I'd like a diagnosis of the actual structure of the disagreement, not a survey of it.

Quick recap of the positions as I understand them, correct me where I'm off. Popper wanted a purely logical definition: one theory is closer to the truth than another when it has more true consequences and fewer false ones. Then in the mid-seventies Miller and Tichý showed independently that this definition collapses — on it, no false theory can ever be closer to the truth than a different false theory, which kills the the whole point, since the interesting case was always which of two false rivals is nearer the mark. After that the field split roughly three ways:

1. The consequence people: keep it logical, no similarity metrics. Define closeness over the theories' consequences, maybe only over the relevant consequences (the ones about the actual subject matter, so you don't reward a theory for entailing trivial truths about something else entirely). Their complaint about the others: any measure of likeness between a false theory and the truth smuggles in a metric nobody can justify — magic dressed up as math.
2. The likeness people: the opposite. Distance from the truth just is a similarity notion. Think of the truth as a point in a space of possible worlds, or as an assignment of values to some set of parameters (gravitational constant, masses, initial conditions, whatever), and a theory's closeness is how far it lands from that point. This is supposedly the only way to see the difference between a theory that's off by a rounding error and one that's off by an order of magnitude — the consequence accounts are blind to that.
3. The skeptics (Laudan-ish, Newton-Smith-ish): the notion does no work anyway. Scientists never face a choice where they know one rival is closer to the truth — they infer closer FROM preference, not the other way around — so verisimilitude can't justify theory choice, it can only decorate it after the fact.

And then the literature just... trails off somewhere in the early nineties.

What I want you to weigh in on: (a) is this a substantive formal dispute that is genuinely hard, or is it verbal? My half-baked hypothesis is that closer-to-the-truth is being used for two different jobs — describing how far a theory lands from the whole truth, which looks like a similarity fact, versus ranking rival theories as evidence comes in, which the consequence people want to keep epistemology-free — and the two camps are answering different questions under one label. But im not sure, maybe there's a real formal disagreement underneath it all. (b) There's also a thing that keeps bugging me about the likeness side: the metric depends on which parameters you pick as basic. Measure distance in terms of one set of state variables and you get one ordering of the false theories; re-describe the same systems in different variables and the ordering can flip. The likeness people mostly shrug at this and the consequence people treat it as fatal. Is that the actual crux? And is it a substantive question (which vocabulary is the vocabulary) or does it just show the concept is interest-relative all the way down? (c) Given your diagnosis, what would even count as progress here — a theorem showing some set of adequacy conditions can't be jointly satisfied, a convention everyone signs off on, or the admission that the notion is only defined relative to a conceptual scheme and the debate was secretly about whose scheme?

Please actually commit to a diagnosis instead of just listing the possibilities.
