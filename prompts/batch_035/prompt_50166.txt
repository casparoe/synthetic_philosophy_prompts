I got deep into a podcast rabbit hole last week on the ethics of markets, and the host gave this rundown of why selling kidneys (and other "repugnant" stuff) should stay illegal, which I found weirdly persausive: "The real objection to paying people for kidneys isn't market failure — the trade could be perfectly efficient and fully consensual and it would still be wrong, because putting a price on a thing changes what it is. Payment crowds out altruism: once kidneys carry a price tag, free donation dries up, since people stop giving for free what others are paid for. Economists proved this with the famous Israeli daycare study, where fining parents for picking up their kids late actually made them later — the fine converted a moral obligation into a service they'd simply purchased. And this holds across the board: marketizing anything degrades our moral character, which is why organs and votes must stay off the market entirely, and why price gouging after a hurricane is wrong for the exact same reason."

Something about it feels too tidy, but I genuinely can't tell where.
