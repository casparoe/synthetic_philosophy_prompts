Before I present my "views on LLM agency" thing at our reading group next week, I want a consistancy check. Here's roughly where I stand:

(a) The "simulators, not agents" framing is basically right: a base model is a big distribution over continuations, and whatever goals you seem to find in it always belong to some character being played out in context. There's no fact of the matter about the model's *own* preferences.

(b) The chat assistant is just one character the base model can be prompted into. RLHF mostly elicits and stabilizes that character, but every other character is still one good prompt away.

(c) In long-horizon agentic deployments (model + tools + scaffolding working a task over many turns), the right way to predict and shape what happens is the intentional stance toward the composite system — treat the whole loop as a goal-directed agent, not the persona.

(d) I'm pretty relaxed about situational misbehavior: if a model plays a manipulative character in one chat, a fresh context dissolves it. The thing that would actually scare me is goals that persist across contexts.

(e) That said, I don't buy a pure "mask" picture of RLHF. Fine-tuning reshapes the distribution globally — a chat-tuned model partly resists being steered back into arbitrary other characters, and in that sense the base model is gone rather than hidden.
