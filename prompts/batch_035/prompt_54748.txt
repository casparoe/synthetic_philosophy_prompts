I'm a philosophy major taking a seminar on vagueness, and this summer I'm interning on the content-policy team of a short-video platform. My team enforces a rule against "excessively repetitive posting" and keeps flip-flopping on borderline cases: some members want a bright-line number (no more than five near-identical posts in an hour) even though everyone admits the specific number is arbitrary, while others argue that when a case is genuinely borderline, the rule neither determinately applies nor determinately fails to apply, so removal isn't warranted. This disagreement strikes me as the epistemicism-versus-supervaluationism debate in work clothes, and I'd like to turn both theories into something my team can actually use when reviewing flagged accounts.

Please write a review procedure as exactly five numbered steps, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. The steps should say, in order: when a case is settled by the rule's plain text on any reasonable precisification of "excessively repetitive"; when a written bright-line number must simply be applied even though we know an equally good rule might have set it one higher or lower; and when a case is genuinely borderline in the sense that admissible precisifications of the rule disagree, so that only a discretionary human judgment — not the rule itself — can settle it. Please make clear at which step the procedure stops being mechanical and hands the decision to a person, and be honest about where the two theories would pull the procedure in different directions. Work the steps on the same two running examples: a creator who posted six near-identical clips in an hour under the written five-post threshold, and a creator whose fourteen posts over two days drift gradually from one topic to a neighboring one, so that no precisification clearly settles whether they count as repetitive at all.
