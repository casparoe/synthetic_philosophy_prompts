so i live in a small co-op building, four units, and last spring we passed this bylaw (everyone calls it the Own Your Domain rule) after a huge fight about who decides what. the idea: every owner is the sole and final authority over a list of "personal matters" that only touch their own unit — your pizza oven hours, your practice amp, whatever's on your personal list. we also kept this older standing clause that says if all owners unanimously prefer one building-wide option over another, the association has to go with what everyone wants. seemed bulletproof tbh.

well. here's how it shook out, i'll just give you the preferences, its all you need: every one of us enjoys running their own wood-fired pizza oven and would rather run it than not, holding everything else fixed — that's the own-your-domain part doing its work. but what we each actually care about is the neighbors' smoke. every single owner ranks "everyone runs their oven every night" near the bottom and "nobody runs an oven" way above it — literally all four of us would rather have a smoke-free courtyard than the current situation where all four ovens are going every single night. so we each chose to run our own oven (obviously — it's our own domain, and each of us likes our own oven), and now the courtyard is a smoke pit, and everyone hates it, and everyone knows everyone hates it.

at the last annual meeting we tried to fix it and it was a farce. motion for smoke-free nights: ruled out of order, infringes on domain sovereignty. motion asking people to voluntarily cut back: passes unanimously, nobody stops. motion to hand oven control to the board: basically dissolving the whole point of the bylaw. and through all of it the board kept insisting both standing rules are working perfectly. i keep feeling there's something deep here — like you genuinely can't have both "each person is the boss of their own domain" and "unanimous preferences win" at the same time, and it's not bad luck, it's structural. my cousin says some economist proved exactly this back in the 70s and i believe him.

anyway heres what i want: write the satirical minutes of that annual meeting. deadpan board-minutes voice, ~650 words, with roll call, treasurer's report, motions ruled out of order with reasons, at least one amendment that makes everything worse, and a "new business" section where someone almost says the thing and doesn't. the humor has to come from the real structure, not slapstick: the domain rule and the unamimity rule are both technically working, every individual choice was made correctly inside someone's own domain, the current outcome is worse for literally every owner than an available alternative, and every proposed fix requires breaking one of the two standing rules — and the board should keep citing whichever rule blocks the motion in front of it, with total sincerity. someone who knows social choice theory should recognize the two conditions as bylaws, watch the deadlock happen in real time, and feel the satire is fair; someone who doesn't should just read a very funny meeting where procedure eats everyone alive. nobody in the minutes is allowed to say "paradox" or name any economist — the residents don't know that stuff, they just know they're miserable and the rules say everything is fine. keep it deadpan, no winking at the reader.

btw use fake names for the owners and feel free to give them little personality quirks (the guy who keeps abstaining on principle, the treasurer who reports the smoke complaints as a line item), just don't make any of them the villain — the villain is the rules, that's kind of my whole point
