I'm a backend engineer and I've spent my whole career thinking in distributed-systems terms -- replication, consensus protocols, leader election, quorums, shared state, two-phase commit, merge conflicts in version control, eventual consistency. I dont have any philosophy background at all. Lately I've been poking at the literature on collective intentionality and joint action, because it seems like the philosophers are circling the same question I deal with constantly: when is a bunch of separate nodes doing things "together" rather than each doing its own thing that happens to line up?

From what I can tell there are a few main positions here. Searle says there's a genuine we-intention that can't be reduced to individual intentions plus mutual beliefs between the members. Bratman treats shared activity as interlocking individual intentions -- each person intends to do their part partly because the others intend theirs, plus commitments to mutual support and meshing subplans. Gilbert says two people form a joint commitment, and that this creates something like an obligation between the parties, such that one member is entitled to rebuke the other for defecting without notice. And Tuomela distinguishes acting in the I-mode, where each person privately reasons "this helps the group, so I'll do it," from acting in the we-mode, where the group itself has a point of view and its reasons are somehow the members' reasons. Hopefully I've got those roughly right.

Anyway, here's my ask: restate each of these positions in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Think replicas, single source of truth, consensus rounds, distributed transactions, quorum reads/writes, whatever fits. So e.g. I'm tempted to hear Bratman as basically describing a system with no coordinator, where consistency emerges from nodes continuously gossiping their intentions and adjusting, versus Gilbert as something closer to a protocol everyone explicitly signed onto, where dropping out mid-transaction triggers rollback or penalty. And Searle's irreducible we-intention sounds a lot like the observation that the behavior of a distributed system is not the behavior of any single node -- but I suspect that's me smuggling my framework's assumptions in and calling it translation.
