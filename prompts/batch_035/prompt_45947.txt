My classmate Dana keeps a notebook of verdicts on what exactly the cogito establishes in different circumstances, and I want to anticipate her next page. Her verdicts so far, in her compressed style:

1. The original meditator, doubting everything under the demon hypothesis, sincerely entertaining the thought: the inference works. Whatever else the demon fakes, this cannot be faked.
2. Uttered mid-dream: still works. Dreaming that one is thinking is still thinking.
3. A thinker whose every belief is false, including her beliefs about what she is currently thinking — the demon has her wrong even about her own occurrent thought: still works. The occurrence of the deceived thought is not up for faking.
4. A recording of me sincerely saying it, played back years later: fails. A replay of an event is not a fresh occurrence of it.
5. An amnesiac with no memories and no self-narrative, but with occurrent thoughts: the cogito proves he exists now, and nothing about who he was, whether he existed earlier, or what he is.
6. Descartes's further claim that the cogito establishes a thinking substance persisting through time: rejected. The proof is strictly momentary.
7. When someone raised the old Lichtenbergian point that Descartes was entitled only to the conclusion that thinking is going on — as one says it is raining — without importing an I, Dana's verdict was that the austere reading is compatible with the cogito but not forced by it.

Her new page, for next week: (a) a patient with thought insertion, who reports sincerely and correctly that thinking is going on in her head while insisting the thoughts are not hers — can she run the cogito on the inserted thoughts, and if so, on whose existence does it deliver? (b) the gap case: the meditator in the instant between two thoughts, when no thought is occurring — does anything prove he exists then? (c) a large language model that outputs the cogito sentence in the course of a discussion of Descartes; (d) a convinced no-self theorist who sincerely avows that he is thinking and that no thinker exists — is he refuted by his own avowal, on Dana's account?

Predict her verdict on each of the four. Then reconstruct the rule her track record is running: at least two rules should fit her seven verdicts equally well, and they should diverge on at least some of the new cases. Give your best guess with reasons, and specify the further case that would best separate the candidate rules.
