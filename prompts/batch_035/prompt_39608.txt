I run the website for my university's philosophy society, and I want a "which view of the identity of indiscernibles do you actually hold?" quiz — the point is to sort readers by what they say about cases, not which label they like, so no question can just ask "can two things share all their properties?". About ten questions, each a concrete possible case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto: strict PII, the weak only-distinguishable-by-space-and-time version, haecceitism, and the quietist "this is a pseudo-question" option if you think anyone actually holds it. The case list has to include: (a) Black's two iron spheres in an otherwise empty, perfectly symmetric universe; (b) a variant where the same universe is described as containing just one sphere, with the description given twice — I want the quiz to catch which readers think that's the same world, since that redescription move is the reply to Black I find slipperiest; (c) at least one quantum case, like two electrons in an atom where there's no fact about which is which, since I suspect intuitions and physics pull apart there; and (d) at least one case where what does the work is whether "being identical to a" counts as a real property — that feels like the load-bearing move and I want it surfacing as a diagnostic, not getting assumed. Flag any two positions the quiz cant seperate, and tell me honestly if some answer combination lands nowhere on the key.
