I'm a third-year PhD student writing the persistence chapter of my dissertation, and before I commit several thousand words to a position I want an honest audit of whether my starting package actually coheres. Here is the package.

(1) Endurantism: ordinary objects are wholly present at every moment at which they exist; persistence is not parthood spread out through time. (2) Persistence is strict identity — the thing sitting in this chair now is numerically identical with the thing that occupied a chair last Tuesday — and not some iterated chain of continuity and resemblance. (3) Objects have their intrinsic properties simpliciter: a wire that is bent is just bent, and I regard the move to instantiation-at-a-time as a change of subject rather than a solution, since it seems to make bentness a relation to a time and so not intrinsic. (4) I am a B-theoretic eternalist: tense belongs to our representation of the world, not to the world, and past and future things are no less real than present ones. (5) I take Minkowski spacetime seriously as a metaphysical datum: there is no privileged foliation, no physically or metaphysically distinguished global present slicing spacetime into layers. (6) Identity is transitive, full stop; and I am confident that I existed ten years ago despite sharing virtually none of my matter with the current occupant of my chair, while any analysis on which what exists now is merely a later temporal stage of something that also contains the ten-years-ago thing has, in my view, abandoned persistence rather than analyzed it.

My working assumption is that the package hangs together — it is roughly what my committee holds — but a student in my cohort insists that (3) and (4) are jointly fatal, that (1) and (5) cannot both be true, and that (2), (6), and material turnover generate the old ship contradiction outright. I would like a proper audit rather than the usual checklist. Tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly; which alleged tensions dissolve on inspection rather than merely being famous; for each real tension, what the cheapest repair is and what that repair costs elsewhere in the package; and, finally, which single revision, if I made it, would clean up the most. I am deliberately not telling you which commitment I would prefer to give up — I want to know what I am actually committed to before I decide.
