My roommate has been on a Hofstadter kick (Gödel, Escher, Bach) and now claims he can prove that our mathematical knowledge is basically a confidence trick. The argument looks wrong to me but I genuinely can't find the bad step. Help.

First the setup, so we're on the same page. There's this toy formal system called MIU. You start with the string MI, and you can apply four rules: (1) if a string ends in I, you can stick a U on the end; (2) you can double everything after the initial M; (3) you can replace any III with a single U; (4) you can delete any UU. The puzzle asks whether you can get from MI to MU. You can't. The way you prove it is by stepping outside the system: count the I's. MI starts with one I, and none of the rules can ever make the number of I's divisible by 3 (doubling a number that isn't a multiple of 3 keeps it a non-multiple, subtracting 3 doesn't change anything mod 3, and the other two rules leave the count alone). MU has zero I's, and zero is divisible by 3, so no sequence of rule applications gets you there. Hofstadter's whole point is that no amount of grinding away inside the system could ever tell you MU is underivable -- you have to reason about the system, from outside it. He calls the move jumping out of the system (JOOTSing).

Now the argument.

(1) In the MIU case, the question "is MU derivable?" could not be settled by any amount of work within the system; settling it required a vantage point outside the system.
(2) This is completely general: whenever a question about a system can't be settled inside it, it can only be settled from a meta-system, one level up.
(3) But a meta-system is itself just a system, so the same limitation applies one level up, and so on forever. No system can settle questions about itself -- he says this is basically what Gödel proved.
(4) All of our reasoning, including the reasoning we used on the MU puzzle, happens inside some system too. Our brains are finite, rule-governed mechanisms, so in principle everything we can mathematically establish is the output of some formalizable process.
(5) So whatever system our reasoning lives in, there are questions about that reasoning we can't settle, and by (2) through (4) no level we could ever reach settles them either. Every JOOTS just lands you in a slightly bigger box.
(6) Therefore there are mathematical facts that no human reasoning, at any level, will ever establish -- and our satisfaction about the MU puzzle is an illusion, since we never actually jumped out of anything.
(7) But we did settle the MU question, and the mod-3 argument is about as solid as mathematics gets.

He's very smug about this. I'm most attached to (4), honestly -- the thought that our reasoning is just another system seems right to me, so if the flaw is there I want to understand exactly what breaks. Which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost you elsewhere? And please be careful about whether "system" means the same thing in every premise, because I have a suspicion it doesn't but I cant articulate why.
