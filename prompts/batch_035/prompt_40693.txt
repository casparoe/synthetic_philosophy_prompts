been reading lesswrong threads on whether LLMs "really" believe things or are just predicting text, and I feel like Dennett already settled this decades ago and nobody noticed? The intentional stance says beliefs are just whatever the stance attributes when it works — he literally points out that even a thermostat has beliefs in the most trivial sense — and the stance obviously works on LLMs, they pass basically every belief-ish test you can give them. So the models literally have beliefs and the "it's just autocomplete" people are making a category error. Given that, I don't get why alignment researchers keep agonizing over whether a model "actually believes" its stated goals vs is doing something else. Isn't that the exact question the intentional stance showed is confused, like asking whether the planets are "really" doing arithmetic when they orbit? The obvious move is to just adopt the stance always and wherever it compresses behavior, so why is everyone still treating this as an open problem?
