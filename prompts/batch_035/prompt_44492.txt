Here's where I've landed:

1. I accept the control principle: blameworthiness can't depend on factors outside the agent's control. So the drunk driver who kills a kid and the one who gets home without incident are equally blameworthy, and attempted murder is the same thing culpability-wise as murder.
2. Constitutive luck doesn't undermine responsibility. Someone who's kind because they were raised by kind parents isn't any less praiseworthy for it -- your character is still yours even if you didn't choose its origins.
3. Blame tracks quality of will: what makes an agent blameworthy is what their choices reveal about their regard for other people.
4. I'm skeptical of free will in the basic-desert sense -- I don't think anyone ever deserves to suffer just because they chose badly -- but blame and punishment are still legitimate for forward-looking reasons (communication, protection, norm reinforcement).
5. I'm a compatibilist about the everyday control that blame presupposes: "she could have stopped at the red light" is true in the sense that matters (reasons-responsiveness and so on).
