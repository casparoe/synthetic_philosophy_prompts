I do data governance at a mid-size HR/insurance SaaS company, which in practice means I decide what categories go into our schemas. Every few weeks a team shows up wanting a new one: adding nonbinary as a gender option, a "high-value customer" tier, whether to keep collecting race, ICD-style diagnosis codes for our wellness product, "gig worker" vs "contractor". Right now these calls get made on vibes and it shows. I've read a bit of the social construction literature — Searle on institutional facts, the X counts as Y formula, Hacking on interactive kinds and looping effects, i.e. the way classifying people changes the people and hence the kind's own statistics — and it seems like there's an actual procedure buried in there about when a category tracks something real vs. when it manufactures the thing it names vs. when its just a filing convenience that we shouldn't pretend is discovering anything. Write me that procedure so my team can run it on a proposed category in an afternoon: the questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it terminates in a "don't add this," where it correctly punts to the execs because the question is values not data, and where it can't be eliminated as a judgment call no matter how much we dress it up. It has to handle the looping problem head on — the categories we ship change the behavior of the people classified, so our own telemetry can't be treated as ground truth about whether the category carves anything. Then run it on three: adding nonbinary as a gender option, creating a high-value customer tier, and continuing to collect race for fairness auditing. Include at least one of the three where the procedure gives an answer I'm not going to like, and say plainly where the procedure will misfire.
