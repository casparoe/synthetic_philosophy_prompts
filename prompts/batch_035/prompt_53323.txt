I recently moved from an academic background in philosophy of mind to a job on a team that designs pre-deployment safety evaluations for large language model systems, and next month I have to present my working assumptions at an internal workshop that will shape what our evaluations actually target. Writing my position down, I have begun to suspect my views don't form a stable set, but I cannot see clearly where the pressure is. I would like an audit rather than a lecture.

Here are the commitments, as best I can state them:

1. A base large language model is not an agent but a simulator: it models the text-generating processes in its training distribution, and the goal-directed characters that appear in its outputs are simulated personae rather than the model itself. Attributing beliefs and desires to the base model is roughly a category error.

2. The systems actually being deployed — a model wrapped in a scaffold of planning modules, tool access, and persistent memory — are genuine agents, and it is this composite system, not the model in isolation, that safety evaluations ought to target.

3. Instrumental convergence remains a serious concern for these composites: whatever goal structure the scaffold and fine-tuning jointly implement, sufficiently capable systems will tend to acquire resources, information, and resistance to shutdown insofar as these serve that structure.

4. The orthogonality thesis: a system's capability and its goals are independent, so we should expect neither that greater competence brings more aligned goals nor that misaligned systems will be too incompetent to worry about.

5. Reinforcement learning from human feedback does not create agents; it shifts the distribution of personae the base model simulates, concentrating probability mass on a helpful-assistant character. This seems right to me given how dramatically behavior still varies with prompting and persona framing.

6. The capabilities we care about — including any tendencies toward manipulation, evasion, or sandbox escape that surface in testing — are properties of the model weights and will transfer across scaffolds, so evaluations should be run on the model itself, before it is embedded in any particular deployment.

Which pairs of these are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what would have to give in each case? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, state for each what the cheapest repair costs elsewhere in the set, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. For context, the workshop has to decide between model-centric and system-centric evaluation strategies, so tensions that bear on that choice matter more to me in practice — but I would rather know the full picture than the convenient part. I genuinely do not know which commitment I would give up, and I would prefer not to discover the answer for the first time in front of my new colleagues.
