You are a formal epistemologist with a specialty in belief revision and probability kinematics, so I'm hoping you can help me reverse-engineer my friend Mara. At our puzzle nights she has a consistent way of updating on conditionals, and I want to predict her verdict on a new case, but I can't tell which rule she's actually running. Her track record: (1) From a well-shuffled standard 52-card deck, one card is drawn and hidden; a perfectly reliable voice announces "if the card is a king, it is red." Mara's resulting assignment: the two black kings drop to probability zero, each red king doubles from 1/52 to 2/52, and all forty-eight non-kings stay at 1/52. (2) She lands uniformly at random in a square territory divided into four equal quadrants (NW, NE, SW, SE); radar reports "if you are in the western half, the odds are 3:1 that you are in the NW quadrant rather than the SW." Mara assigns P(NW) = 3/8, P(SW) = 1/8, P(NE) = P(SE) = 1/4, and insists her probability of being in the western half at all is still 1/2. (3) A fair die is rolled and, independently, a prize is placed in one of three boxes uniformly; a reliable voice says "if the die shows six, the prize is in box 1." Mara's distribution becomes P(six and box 1) = 1/6, P(six and box 2) = P(six and box 3) = 0, P(not-six and box i) = 5/18 for each box, with P(six) still 1/6; and when the die is then revealed to show a 3, she reverts exactly to 1/3 per box.

The new case is a cousin of the Judy Benjamin problem, which is why I'm writing to you rather than guessing myself. A bag contains ten tiles: two starred red queens, one unmarked red queen, two starred black queens, three unmarked red 5s, and two unmarked black 5s. One tile is drawn, and a perfectly reliable voice announces: "if the tile is a queen, it is red." Mara will now be asked for the probability that the tile is a starred red queen. My difficulty is that her whole track record seems consistent with at least two different rules, and I can't tell them apart: something like Jeffrey-style or Adams-style updating, where the conditional fixes P(red | queen) while the marginal probability of drawing a queen stays at 1/2 and the mass from the black queens is redistributed proportionally within the red queens; versus an imaging-style rule (with a partial transfer to make probabilistic conditionals like case 2 come out right), where black-queen mass flows to the nearest red queens and, if queens prefer queens and stars prefer stars, it pools on the starred ones. Every case in her record is symmetrical in exactly the way that makes the two rules agree, so this tile case may be the first that actually splits them -- though it may also reveal a third rule I haven't considered, and I've also noticed her record seems to eliminate some rules outright (treating the conditional as a material implication, treating it as direct evidence about the consequent, refusing to update at all).
