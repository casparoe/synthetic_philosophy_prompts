I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and for years my beat has brushed against bioethics — euthanasia laws, pandemic triage, organ allocation — which has forced me to hold views on killing and letting die in public, usually on deadline. Writing a long-form essay on where I actually stand, I want a consistency audit before I commit anything to print. My working position: (1) the distinction between doing and allowing has no intrinsic moral weight — Rachels's pair of cases about the man who drowns his cousin for the inheritance and the man who merely lets him drown has always struck me as showing that killing and letting die are on a par when everything else is held fixed; (2) the trolley driver ought to divert the trolley away from the five onto the one, but a bystander may not push the large man off the footbridge, even to save the same five; (3) when those two verdicts need defending, what does the work is the difference between harm intended as a means and harm merely foreseen — the footbridge death is a means, the diverted trolley death is a side effect — and not anything about doing versus allowing; (4) I am broadly consequentialist about how lives should be weighed, and I suspect the remaining deontological constraints are evolutionary heuristics that made sense on the savanna and don't now; (5) I have never for a moment doubted that a surgeon may not cut up one healthy visitor to save five dying patients; (6) during the pandemic I wrote columns defending the withdrawal of a ventilator from one patient with a poor prognosis so that another with a better prognosis could have it, and I still think that was permissible — closer to diverting the trolley than to pushing the man; and (7) I have argued that voluntary active euthanasia is morally on a par with withdrawing life-sustaining treatment, and that both should be legal. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and what exactly would have to give in each case? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging, and identify the single cheapest revision that would clean up the most — but do not assume you know which one I would choose to abandon.
