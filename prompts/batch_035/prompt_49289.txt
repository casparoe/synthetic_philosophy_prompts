I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, but I've thought about decision theory enough to have settled on a position I take quite seriously: Hofstadter's superrationality. Where a group of agents reason the same way about the same symmetric problem, they will converge on one shared strategy, so the right move is to choose the strategy you'd want that shared strategy to be — cooperate in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma against a sufficiently similar reasoner, send a telegram with probability 1/20 in Hofstadter's Platonia setup, and so on. I like that it's not philanthropy: against a known conventional defector, you defect. I'm aware people file it somewhere between evidential decision theory and functional decision theory, though I've never fully sorted out the family relations. Tell me what would count against this view. I want the defeaters ranked by how damaging they'd be, from ones that would sink it to ones a committed superrationalist could shrug off, and including things that have arguably already happened: Hofstadter ran a real test, the Luring Lottery in Scientific American in 1983, where the prize was a million dollars divided by the total number of entries, the superrational prescription was for each reader to submit a single entry (or, on the mixed-strategy version, to randomize so that about one entry would arrive in total), and in fact enough readers submitted astronomically large numbers — several a googolplex — that the prize shrank to a fraction of a cent and no winner could be determined. Was that a failed prediction, mere evidence that Scientific American readers weren't superrational thinkers, or out of scope because the game was asymmetric in practice? Relatedly, I want an honest verdict on whether the clause about "sufficiently similar reasoners" has quietly insulated the view from any possible refutation, and whether that matters for a normative decision principle the way it would for an empirical theory. And if the strongest surviving form of the idea really is functional decision theory, tell me what that successor's best-known embarrassments are and whether they drag my view down with it.
