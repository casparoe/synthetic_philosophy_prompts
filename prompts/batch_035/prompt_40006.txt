Long-time reader, first asker: I run a small leftish politics blog with a mostly non-academic audience, and every few months the comment section erupts into a fight about whether "real equality" means equal money, equal happiness, or equal chances to live the life you want. People talk past each other for 200 comments, and I suspect half of them are defending views they'd actually hate if they saw the concrete cases. So I want to build a "which egalitarian are you, actually?" quiz for the blog, and I need help designing it.

The quiz should be 8–10 questions, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options, and then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions. The cases I definitely want in, because they seem to do the real sorting work: (1) someone born with a condition who needs way more money than average just to leave the house and get around town — does fairness mean covering that, or giving everyone the same amount and calling it even; (2) a guy who trained himself into a taste for obscenely expensive wine and now claims he's worse off than the rest of us on the same income; (3) a devout person whose religion imposes real costs — special food, no working certain days, all embraced willingly — is that a need like the disability case or a choice like the wine guy; (4) a survey where an oppressed group reports the same life satisfaction as everyone else, and a commenter says "see, no inequality here" — what should the reader say back. Feel free to swap or add cases if you think some do more sorting work than mine, but I want the quiz to be cases all the way down.

The leaves of the scoring key should be named positions: hedonic welfare, preference-satisfaction, resource egalitarianism (with the envy-test flavor flagged if it comes apart from plain resource-splitting), capabilities, a sufficientarian "equality is the wrong currency, what matters is a floor" leaf, and a prioritarian one if it fits naturally. Hard rule: no question may just ask "do you believe in equality of resources?" — people need to be sorted by what they say about cases, not by which label they like the sound of. And at the end, flag honestly which pairs of positions the quiz can't separate no matter what (I have a suspicion the wine case doesn't split preference people from resource people the way everyone assumes), and note any answer combination that turns out coherent but unoccupied — with a sentence on whether thats because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Aim it at smart readers with zero philosophy background, so every question needs to be answerable without knowing any of the terminology.
