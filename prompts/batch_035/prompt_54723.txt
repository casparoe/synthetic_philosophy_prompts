I've been circling around strong illusionism about consciousness for a while now — the view that phenomenal properties, in the special sense that makes the hard problem hard, simply don't exist, and that what needs explaining is why we're so disposed to judge that they do. The usual story is that this judgment is generated by some quirk of how introspection models our own processing, and once you see that the quirk is a quirk, the hard problem dissolves rather than gets solved.

Here's my problem though. Whenever I try to articulate what would actually change my mind about this, I come up empty, and that bothers me more than any of the standard objections (the "seems to me" regress, the obviousness of experience, etc.). Take a specific case: I hold that the "phenomenality illusion" has a particular structure — it's not just that we lack access to the machinery, but that our introspective model actively mislabels certain states as having an intrinsic, private, ineffable character, roughly the way the attention schema model claims we build a simplified model of attention. Suppose that's roughly right. What would a world look like where that story is false and phenomenal properties are real? Would anything be different? If not, am I holding a distinction without a difference — is my illusionism actually collapsing into a deflationism about phenomenal talk that a property dualist could happily sign? That would be embarrassing and I'd want to know.

So: give me the honest map of what would count against my view. Rank the defeaters — from things that would genuinely force a rethink, through awkward-but-survivable, to things illusionists just shrug off. Include empirical stuff (anything from psychophysics, blindsight, anaesthesia research that should worry me), philosophical stuff (cases where the illusionist story seems unable to explain the specific flavor of the illusion, e.g. why the illusion has the privacy and ineffability markers it does rather than some other markers), and meta-level stuff about whether a view insulated to this degree is doing any work. And tell me straight: which of these would a committed Frankish-style illusionist concede as real pressure, and which would they wave off with a slogan? If your conclusion is that the view is basically unfalsifiable and that's fine for a metaphysical thesis, say that — but then explain what the thesis is ruling out that anyone actually denies.
