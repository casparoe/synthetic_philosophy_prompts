**Half Your Child's Intelligence Is Already Decided**

Twin studies have been telling us the same thing for decades. Identical twins reared apart, who never met untill adulthood, nonetheless end up with similar IQ scores, similar personalities, even similar tastes in spouses and hobbies. Fraternal twins raised together — sharing a home, parents, and schools — are barely more alike. When psychologists do the arithmetic, intelligence in modern Western populations comes out around 50-80% heritable, and personality traits like conscientiousness settle near 50% as well. Behavior geneticists also find that the shared environment, meaning everything parents deliberately do, contributes almost nothing to how adult personality turns out, so parenting styles appear to be running on rails.

What follows for policy? First, for any given child, roughly half of their intelligence is fixed at conception. Tutoring, enrichment programs, and the rest can fight over the remainder, but they cannot do better than the leftover half — a program can at most move a child within the share the environment controls, and no further. Second, heritability climbs as environments become more uniform: the better-off and more equal a society gets, the more of the remaining variation is genetic. We have spent decades flattening environmental differences, which is precisely why the genetic differences now stand out. The returns on further spending are therefore shrinking. Third, if a trait is highly heritable within a group, then differences between groups on that trait are mostly genetic too. Average score differences between groups thus won't be closed by anything we do to the environment; they will have to be accepted, or at most nudged at the margins.

The moral for policymakers is plain. Interventions should be judged against a ceiling that genetic variance has already set. A program promising to raise intelligence across the board is promising what the twin data say cannot be done at scale. The honest position: admit the environment matters at the individual margin, while acknowledging it has largely done its work already.
