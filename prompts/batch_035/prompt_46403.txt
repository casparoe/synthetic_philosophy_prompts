My closest friend and I spent an entire evening arguing about the Judy Benjamin problem, and we ended it exactly where we started. I would like an expert adjudication, and because the dispute is partly numerical, I want it in the form of an actual probability.

First, the case, so we are arguing about the same thing. Judy Benjamin parachutes at night into a war-games area divided between Blue Army territory and Red Army territory, each of which is itself divided into a Headquarters Company area and a Second Company area. Before any message arrives, she splits her credence evenly: one half on Blue, and within Red territory, half and half between the two subdivisions. Her radio then comes alive and tells her only this: on the assumption that she has landed in Red territory, the odds are three to one that she is in the Red Second Company area rather than the Red Headquarters area. Nothing else is conveyed — no assertion about whether she is in Red territory at all.

I maintain that the rational response is to leave her probability of being in Red territory untouched at one half, and merely redistribute within it, so that her final probability of being in the Red Second Company area is 3/8 (and the Red Headquarters area gets 1/8). My friend, following the line of thought van Fraassen developed in his original discussion, insists on minimizing relative information subject to the constraint that the conditional odds within Red territory are three to one, which yields 3/7 for the Red Second Company area — and, uncomfortably for me, also raises her probability of being in Red territory at all from 1/2 to 4/7. He thinks that rise is a cost worth paying for a principled rule; I think a message that is explicitly conditional should not confirm its own antecedent, and that any rule which says otherwise has misfired.

Here is what I want from you. Please take the role of a formal epistemologist who works on belief revision and is used to giving numbers rather than shrugging. Before committing, brainstorm briefly: what exactly is the message evidence of, what readings of it are even available, and what update rules are candidates (plain conditioning on some construal of the message, Jeffrey-style redistribution, constraint-satisfaction methods like relative information minimization, and the thought that the problem is simply underdetermined). Then give me your credence that my answer — the one that keeps the antecedent's probability fixed and yields 3/8 — is the uniquely rational one, and decompose that credence into the sub-questions it rests on, with a rough probability and one line of justification for each. I would expect terms at least for the following, though you may replace or refine them: (a) the probability that the message is correctly modeled as a constraint solely on the conditional probability of Second Company given Red territory, carrying no independent information about Red territory itself; (b) the probability that, given such a constraint, there exists a unique rational update at all, as opposed to a family of admissible ones between which probability theory is silent; (c) the probability that a partition-relative method like information minimization, rather than something like the fixed-antecedent answer, is the right tiebreaker if a tiebreaker is needed. Combine the terms into your overall number, tell me which term the final credence is most sensitive to, and what argument or kind of case would move it most.

Finally, since the evening ended badly: given my position and my friend's, say where you think the disagreement between us most likely lives — is it a semantic dispute about what the radio operator's message asserts, a methodological dispute about which update rule to trust when they conflict, or a substantive disagreement about whether there is a fact of the matter here at all? Be candid rather than diplomatic; if you think one of us is simply wrong, say so and say why.
