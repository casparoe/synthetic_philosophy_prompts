I'm a retired condensed matter physicist (semiconductors, mostly — thirty-some years split between industry and a national lab), and since retiring I've finally started reading philosophy instead of just making fun of it over coffee like we used to in the department. And I've landed were the famous physicists did: Hawking declaring in a popular book that philosophy is dead, the line usually pinned on Feynman about ornithology being no use to birds, Weinberg devoting a chunk of one of his older books to arguing that philosophy of science has been of no use to science. My version of the view is a bit more specific: from roughly the 1920s on, whenever philosophy has tried to legislate for physics it's been wrong (positivism grumbling about unobservables, operationalism warping how quantum mechanics got talked about), and whenever physics needed conceptual work done, physicists and mathematicians just did it themselves and philosophers wrote commentary afterward. At best philosophy of science is scorekeeping.

But now that I've actually read some of the stuff — Popper, Lakatos, a bit of Duhem, some of the newer measurement-theory literature — I want to stress-test my own position rather than just enjoy it. So: what would count against it? And I don't mean "philosophy is worthwhile for other reasons" — that's a dodge. I mean concrete evidence and arguments that philosophy has been, or would be, genuinely useful to working science. Please deal honestly with the standard candidate cases: did Mach actually matter to Einstein, or is that philosophers claiming credit? When Bell took hidden variables seriously and respectable physicists laughed, and quantum information eventually grew out of that foundations talk — is that philosophy being useful, or just physics thinking out loud? Are the replication crisis and the mess around measurement validity in psychology places where philosophy would have earned its keep? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which ones a committed version of my view can absorb by adding qualifications, and be honest about whether those qualifications shade it into something unfalsifiable — and if they do, whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind, or just the nature of big-picture claims. If you think my view is just wrong, say so straight. Under 600 words please.
