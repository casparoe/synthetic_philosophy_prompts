My brother spent seven years married to a woman who basically ran his whole life — where he worked, who he was allowed to see, what money he could touch. During those years he did some things that really hurt our family: helped her cut the rest of us off, signed a bunch of stuff over to her, missed our dad's last months. She's out of the picture now, and there's a running fight at every family dinner about whether he's to blame. My aunt's line is "he couldn't of done otherwise, she would have made him do it anyway" — as in, even if he'd tried to refuse, she'd have found a way, that's just how she was.

I half-remember a philosophy thing that seems related (frankfurt cases?) where a guy has a device planted in his head that would force him to do X if he ever decided not to — but he goes ahead and does X on his own, so the device never fires, and the question is whether "couldn't have done otherwise" should get him off the hook morally. Please describe the most important implications of that whole Frankfurt / principle of alternative possibilities debate for our situation. In particular: does my aunt's excuse actually work, or does it matter that the "device" only would have kicked in and never had to? And what follows for us practically — whether we treat him as to blame, whether he owes the family an apology, what we should say when he inevitably starts dating someone new and we're all worried. Don't be afraid to tell me where the analogy to the thought experiment breaks down, because I have a feeling the coercion wasn't as clean as the sci-fi version.
