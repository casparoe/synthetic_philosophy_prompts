I'm a software developer with no academic background, but I've spent a couple of years reading whatever I could follow about the idea that the brain is a prediction machine — that perception, action, and perhaps all of biology come down to minimizing some quantity called free energy. I've come to believe this is more than a useful metaphor: I think it's literally true of organisms like us, and I've even grown attached to the stronger claim that it applies to any self-organizing system, since anything that persists has to keep its internal states within bounds against what's outside it. But a worry gnaws at me: whenever I try to spell out what the principle rules out, I can't. A bad habit? Free energy minimization. A hallucination? Free energy minimization. A thermostat? Arguably free energy minimization too. If everything from a bacterium to a thermostat can be redescribed this way, I can't tell whether I believe something deep or something empty. Tell me what would actually count against the view — which experimental results, which conceptual worries about whether the quantities involved are well-defined and measurable rather than just assumed, and which failures its own defenders would concede as failures rather than absorb by redefining terms. And be direct about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability, and whether that would be fatal to a principle of this kind or simply the price of working at this level of abstraction.
