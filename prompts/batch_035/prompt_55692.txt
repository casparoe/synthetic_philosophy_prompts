Taking a course on the ethics of future generations and we just covered Parfit's nonidentity problem, and it's making me see Never Let Me Go completely differently. I have to write an essay reading the novel through the problem and honestly I keep going back and forth, so I'd love your take.

Here's the tension as I see it. The clones in Ishiguro's novel exist only because of the donation programme — no programme, no Kathy, no Tommy. And their lives, at least in Kathy's flat, retrospective telling, look like ones they'd rather have than not: she treasures her memories of Hailsham, the friendships, the tape. So a Parfitian could argue the programme did them no wrong — they weren't harmed, since the alternative for them was never existing at all. But the novel clearly wants us to feel something monstrous was done to them, and Miss Emily's big speech at the end about how Hailsham existed to prove clones had souls seems to concede that society needed some story to feel ok about what it was doing.

So my question: does the novel dramatize the nonidentity problem, or does it sidestep it? Is it actually true that on a person-affecting view the guardians and the state did the clones no wrong, and if so what is the horror the reader is feeling tracking — is Ishiguro pointing at something the argument can't capture, like the wrong of being created purely as a means, or the way Kathy and Tommy's hope for a deferral gets crushed by an argument (their love simply doesn't matter to the system) rather than by a villain they could confront? And I can't decide wether Kathy's composure at the very end, imagining Tommy appearing across the fence in that field, is meant as evidence that her life was worth living or as a symptom of the very conditioning the novel condemns.

If you could give me a reading that takes both the novel and the actual Parfit debate seriously (person-affecting vs impersonal principles, the wrongful-life stuff, whether an impersonal principle that condemns the programme also commits you to saying a world of extra happy people is better) and say what the novel adds that the papers don't, that would be amazing.
