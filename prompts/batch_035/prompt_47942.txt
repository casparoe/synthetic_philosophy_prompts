A friend of mine who works in quantum information keeps dismissing the whole "is the wavefunction real" debate with an analogy: the quantum state is like a weather forecaster's probability map. It's a summary of our information about a system; measurement is just updating the map when new data arrives; "collapse" is nothing but the forecaster crossing out her old forecast. On her telling, nothing in the lab is ever really in superposition, the weirdness lives entirely in our bookkeeping, and people who reify the wavefunction are confusing the map with the territory. She uses this to argue the underlying world is basically ordinary and QM is pure epistemics.

Something about this bugs me but I can't pin it down. Audit the analogy for me. What exactly is supposed to map onto what -- preparation, measurement, collapse, interference -- and where does the forecaster picture actually break? Two things I half-remember and want woven in somewhere: (1) if two preparations really were the same underlying situation (just represented by different forecaster maps), you'd expect some sufficiently clever observation to eventually reveal that fact, whereas nonorthogonal quantum states can never be pooled like that no matter what you do; (2) I recall some argument from around 2012 showing that if you treat the quantum state as mere information about deeper facts, and assume independently prepared systems have independent deeper facts, you get predictions contradicting QM itself -- though I forget how that's supposed to go and how much it actually targets her view vs. a strawman. Tell me which disanalogies matter for her conclusion and which are just noise, what the analogy quietly smuggles in (e.g. is there a forecaster-side counterpart of being unable to observe without disturbing?), and whether her "the world underneath is ordinary" conclusion survives once the breaks are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole way. If there's a better analogy for the epistemic view, one that doesn't break where hers does, say so.
