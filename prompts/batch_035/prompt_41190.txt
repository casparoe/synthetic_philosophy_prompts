I got into an argument with my brother about whether the chess app on my phone "really believes" it is winning, or merely calculates as if it believed. He ended it by saying that belief is just a pattern: if the system reliably behaves in every way a believer would, then the belief is there, and asking whether it's "really" in there somewhere is like asking whether a centre of gravity is "really" inside the wheel. He also sketched something he'd picked up from a podcast about three ways of predicting a thing's behaviour: working out the physics, working out the design, and just treating it as a rational agent with beliefs and desires that wants things. On that last way, he said, even a thermostat counts as believing the room is too cold, so there's nothing left to argue about with the chess app.

I felt he'd won the argument and lost the question. As far as I can tell, "belief is just a pattern" could mean several quite different things, and I can't sort them out on my own. It might be the trivial claim that belief-talk is a convenient shorthand for describing behaviour; it might be the strong claim that there's a further fact about "real" belief that science will one day settle; it might be the claim that patterns themselves are fully real things, no less real than chairs, so that nothing is missing; or it might even be the claim that in borderline cases there simply is no fact of the matter, which would mean some questions deserve no answer rather than a hasty one. And it matters which one he meant, because only some of these support waving away my question about the chess app. I also wonder where the line is meant to fall: a thermostat, a sunflower turning towards the window, a door that only opens with the right key — presumably he'd say the pattern fades out somewhere, and I'd like to know whether his one-liner can tell us where, or whether it goes silent exactly when it becomes interesting.

Could you set out, in a numbered list I could read back to him, the distinct precise claims that "belief is just a pattern" might stand for, including the ones drawing on those three prediction stances? For each reading, tell me whether it is a platitude everyone accepts, a substantive and defensible thesis, or something false or confused, and then say plainly which of the readings, if any, would actually justify ending our argument the way he did. If some readings would instead reopen the question, or leave it with no answer either way, flag that too. Please write your answer in British English.
