I came out as trans a bit under a year ago and it's suddenly made the whole "social construction" thing feel a lot less like seminar material. My uncle keeps saying gender is "just a social construct" as if that settles it, and I keep finding that I agree with the construct part and totally reject the "just." So I've been trying to figure out what I actually think, case by case, and I'd love a principle that captures it. Here are my verdicts:

Money is constructed all the way down — if everyone tomorrow stopped treating banknotes as valuable, they'd stop being money, full stop. Race is also constructed, but I think it's different from money somehow: if everyone stopped believing in race tomorrow, the category would go, but the health and wealth disparities tracking it would persist for generations. Chairs, though, I don't want to call constructed in either of those senses — there's no natural boundary around chair vs. stool vs. bench, the boundary is drawn by us, but the objects are there regardless and they'd be there if we all forgot the word.

Then the two I care most about. Gender roles — the stuff about who wears what, who does what, what counts as flirting — constructed like race, I'm confident. But my own dysphoria, the thing that was there since before I had words for it and got dramatically better on hormones, feels like it was NOT constructed — though what it meant, and whether transition was even a thinkable response, obviously was. And then there's ADHD, which I got diagnosed with at 30: the category's boundary is clearly drawn by committees (where does subclinical end), there's presumably some real underlying distribution, and the diagnosis itself changed me — I started rereading my whole childhood through it. That looping thing worries me about "trans woman" too: the category exists, people get sorted into it, the sorting changes who they are and how they're treated, and it's not clear to me whether that makes the kind more real or less.

So: is there one principle that gets money, race, chairs, gender roles, dysphoria, and ADHD right? The sticking point is that money and chairs both lack natural boundaries, but only money vanishes when belief vanishes — so "constructed" seems to mean at least two different things, and then I can't say which one applies to "woman" as a category, which is the thing my uncle and I are actually fighting about. Maybe I need three kinds of constructedness, or maybe one of my verdicts is just confused. Please also tell me what your principle says about a case I keep flip-flopping on: my endocrinologist now treats "trans women on HRT" as its own population with its own reference ranges — is that discovering a real medical kind, or creating one, and does it matter which?

Also, since I'm going to use whatever you say in actual arguments with family: tell me what would change your mind. What case or argument would make you abandon the principle you land on, and where do you think your own reasoning here is weakest — I'd rather know that up front than find out from my uncle.
