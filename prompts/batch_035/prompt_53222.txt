write me a pantoum about chaos, predictability, and determinism. the form has to do the philosophical work: in a pantoum every line comes back verbatim, so the exact same words keep recurring while their meaning shifts as the surrounding lines change — thats sensitive dependence on initial conditions in miniature, fixed rules and the same "initial" line, but divergent readings. somewhere in the middle the poem should concede that Laplace's demon isn't refuted by any of this (determinism survives untouched, it's only prediction that fails), and toward the end it should gesture at how chaos kills point forecasts but not statistics — two-week weather is hopeless, climate isn't. the last stanza should circle back toward the opening couplet without landing exactly on it, like a trajectory on a strange attractor: never quite repeating, never escaping. 6 or 7 stanzas, and it should be a good poem first — someone who knows dynamical systems should nod, and someone who doesn't should still enjoy it
