two researchers at our org have been arguing about AI existential risk for something like two years and finally agreed to do a proper adversarial collaboration, and I've been asked to draft the joint statement for them to sign. Call one D (thinks misaligned AI plausibly kills everyone this century — puts it around 20% for extinction or permanent global disempowerment) and one S (thinks the framing is basically confused — under 1%, and tired of doomer rhetoric). Draft the document they should end up with.

What I want in it: first, the claims both could actually sign — about what current models demonstrably can and can't do, about what "alignment" even means for systems smarter than their overseers, and about which parts of the dispute are empirical versus which are basically about how you use words. Then a disagreements section, where each disagreement is phrased so that its own side would accept the wording as fair to them (this is the hard part — D won't sign "AI might escape our control," S won't sign "AI wants things"). Then the cruxes. My guess is three matter most: (1) whether goal-directedness comes for free once you push optimization far enough, or whether it has to be deliberately engineered in; (2) whether alignment gets easier or harder as models scale — D thinks steering gets harder as systems get more capable, S thinks the opposite, that more capable models are more legible and more steerable; (3) whether competing labs could actually coordinate a slowdown if risk became obvious to everyone. For each crux, say what evidence or argument both would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's concrete prediction in advance — including on things like situational awareness or deceptive behavior in current models, where they interpret the same results oppositely.

Also, and this is important: don't make it a mushy both-sides document. Where you have to choose how to phrase a disagreement, pick the phrasing you'd actually defend. If you think one camp's framing of some issue is question-begging — I suspect S's "it's all anthropomorphism" line partly is, and D's "optimization pressure" talk might be too — flag that in the statement itself. They've both agreed whoever drafts it gets to make those calls, so take them. And if you conclude some chunk of the dispute is purely verbal, say which chunk and why.
