I teach a mid-level metaphysics course, and every year when we hit causation by omission the students just dig in their heels — "not doing something can't cause anything, that's just weird." I want to hand them a short story this year instead of my usual lecture attempt, something that makes the issue vivid before we get anywhere near counterfactual machinery.

Write me a short story, maybe 700-900 words, where the central action is an omission: somebody fails to do something, and that failure is what makes everything go wrong. Some ideas for setting (feel free to pick or invent your own): a greenhouse where someone forgot to reset the automatic watering system before leaving, a night watchman who didn't make his rounds, an heir who never got around to telling anyone about the clause in the will. Melancholy is fine, even good.

Here's the part I actually care about, so don't skip it: somewhere in the story I want the "problem of many absences" to be felt rather than stated. There should be a moment where it's clear that lots of other people also didn't do the relevant thing — the neighbor who also never checked on the plants, the dozens of people who could have walked past and noticed — and only one of those non-doers comes out as the cause. The reader should be left asking themselves what exactly picks that one out, since the bare counterfactual ("if they had acted, the bad thing wouldn't have happened") is true of the others too.

And if you can work it in: I'd love for the story to suggest, without ever saying it, that whether a non-event counts as a cause seems to track whether there was some norm or expectation in place that the non-doer was supposed to live up to — the hired gardener's dead plants are his fault in a way the mailman's identical non-watering isn't. That's the thing I want them chewing on before we read the real stuff.

No character should deliver a philosophy lecture, noone should explain the plot. Just the story, with the puzzle doing its work underneath.

One more thing — keep it under 900 words, I have to photocopy it and I'm not made of toner.
