I teach a graduate seminar on moral responsibility, and I want to stress-test the compatibilist side against free will skepticism before putting it in front of my students. Please play a committed free will skeptic in the mold of Caruso's half of the Dennett–Caruso exchange: no one ever merits punishment in the basic desert sense, retributivism is therefore unjustified root and branch, and the criminal justice system should be rebuilt on your forward-looking, public-health–quarantine model, in which incapacitation and reformation do the work that desert was supposed to do. Play the position as sharply as it can be played — concede genuine points to me where the argument allows, but never the core denial of basic desert. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to roughly a paragraph so it feels like a real exchange. Here is my opening move: your quarantine model confines offenders on grounds of dangerousness alone, because you deny they ever deserve anything for their crimes. But the retributivist at least addresses people as agents whose deeds matter — she punishes the guilty for what they have done, in a currency that presupposes their responsibility, and thereby honors them in a way your system cannot. Your model, by contrast, sweeps people up on a prediction of what they might do, like carriers of a disease to be isolated. You insist your approach is more humane and more respectful of persons than just deserts. It looks to me like it treats wrongdoers as weather to be managed rather than as authors of their acts. Convince me otherwise.
