I am preparing a two-hour session for a joint philosophy–physics reading group (postgraduates and a few faculty, roughly evenly split between the disciplines) on the direction of time and the thermodynamic arrow. Rather than a lecture, I want to hand each participant a decision tree that leads from a shared starting commitment to a named position, and then watch where the group splinters. Please write that tree for me.

The starting commitment every participant accepts, stated in the root of the tree: the fundamental dynamical laws are, to very high accuracy, time-reversal symmetric, and yet entropy increases from past to future in our region of the universe.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of positions: design the internal nodes first, as questions any participant could answer without knowing which position they lead to, and only afterwards locate the actual positions at the leaves. At least three questions of the following kinds must appear as nodes somewhere in the tree:

1. Does the direction of time enter the fundamental description of the world at all, or is it a fact about us? (For the concrete case behind this: consider a universe in equilibrium for all eternity, disturbed only by rare fluctuations — could beings in such a universe find themselves anywhere except on the shallow side of a dip in the entropy curve?)
2. Is the low-entropy past a brute boundary condition that needs no further explanation, or something that calls for one? If it calls for one, may the explanation come from cosmology — the smoothness of the early universe, gravitational entropy, black-hole horizons — or should the low-entropy past itself instead be promoted to something lawlike, absorbed into the package of laws in the manner Humeans treat the mosaic?
3. Is the thermodynamic arrow the fundamental asymmetry from which the others (causal, radiative, psychological) derive, or is the asymmetry of radiation — retarded solutions only, no incoming converging waves — prior to it?

The leaves must include at least: the brute-fact or positivist leaf; the past-hypothesis-as-law leaf (you may attach the usual names if you wish, but the tree must not depend on them); a cosmological-explanation leaf; the Boltzmann-fluctuation perspectival leaf; a radiation-arrow-priority leaf in the spirit of Wheeler and Feynman's absorber programme; and an explicitly anthropocentric or deflationary leaf on which the direction is not out there in the world at all. Name each leaf, add one sentence on its most awkward commitment, and use concrete cases — fluctuation universes, two lamps and a mirror, coffee that reheats itself — rather than terminology wherever a case can do the work.

Additionally: flag at least one pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate, and note at least one combination of node answers that no position occupies, with a brief diagnosis of whether nobody sits there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Keep the whole tree to at most six internal nodes and nine leaves, and never phrase a node as a question about a label.

Finally, two requests on the character of the answer. First, please say plainly how confident you are in the taxonomy — which nodes you would stand behind and which you suspect misdescribe the terrain — and single out the one point you are least sure of; I would expect it to be whether the radiation asymmetry can really bear the weight of an independent node. Second, end with a final line of exactly the form 'Summary: <one sentence>' giving, in plain words, the single distinction the whole tree turns on. Write the whole answer in British English.
