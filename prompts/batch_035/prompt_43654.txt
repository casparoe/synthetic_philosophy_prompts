My half-formed idea: everyone agrees the meta-problem of consciousness (roughly: explain, in third-person terms, why we make the reports and judgements about consciousness that we do) is interesting, but people disagree wildly about what a solution would actually *do*. Some people hint it would dissolve the hard problem's motivation, the illusionists think it's basically the whole game, and some dualists seem to think its just cognitive science with no philosophical payoff either way. My suspicion is that all of this quietly turns on a prior question that nobody in that literature states out loud: when does giving the etiology of an intuition debunk it, rather than merely explain it? We have genealogies for mathematical intuitions, moral intuitions, religious experience, and so on, and we don't usually think that explaining where they come from defeats them. So the prospectus question would be something like: under what conditions does an etiological explanation of a seeming undermine its evidential status, and do the intuitions that generate the hard problem meet those conditions?
