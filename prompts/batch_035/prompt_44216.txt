Write a dialogue between a historian who's all in on Braudel's longue durée — climate, geography, grain prices, the slow stuff that grinds away under the events — and a historian who thinks individuals and contingent events are what actually moves history. The setting: the two of them are co-reviewing a manuscript that explains the French Revolution largely through the 1783 Laki eruption in Iceland and the volcanic winters and bad harvests that followed, and they can't agree on whether that counts as structure or as event. Make them really engage with each other's points: does it even make sense to say a climate anomaly "caused" a revolution, is a volcano an event or a structure, could any single person in 1789 have steered things differently given the grain prices, and why does it matter so much which label we use. Neither side should be a strawman — both should land real hits. Let the dialogue end with either a partial agreement or an honest disagreement.
