Two of my flatmates got stuck into this at dinner after our gender seminar and I couldn't referee it, so I'm passing it to you. Context: that big 2018 study by Stoet and Geary found that in countries with more gender equality (Scandinavia etc.), men and women's field-of-study choices actually diverge MORE, not less — fewer women in STEM relative to less egalitarian places. (1) Rob said the upshot is obvious: if you strip away discrimination and unequal opportunity, and the gap gets bigger rather than smaller, then what's left must be something like innate differences in interests — what else could be doing the work? (5) Rob said in that case the finding doesn't discriminate between nature and culture at all, so nobody should cite it as evidence for anything, and he wanted to know why it was framed as a paradox in the first place.
