So I've been around LessWrong since maybe 2013, read the sequences the way people used to read scripture, and I'm fully fluent in the house vocabulary: map and territory, words as pointers, the cluster structure of thingspace, mind projection fallacy, ontology identification, natural abstractions (John Wentworth enjoyer), Goodhart, disguised queries, and the whole instrumental-vs-epistemic rationality split. Here's my situation. A friend of mine is a philosophy grad student, and after a few drinks she told me the entire Human's Guide to Words sequence is just later Wittgenstein with the serial numbers filed off, and that if I actually sat down with the Philosophical Investigations instead of secondhand blog summaries I'd find the LW version is a caricature. I've since skimmed enough secondary stuff on language games and family resemblance to suspect she might be onto something, but I can't tell whether that feeling is because my vocabulary is genuinely missing something or because Wittgenstein is just doing philosophy of language badly and the LW reframing actually improved it.

So here's what I want. Take the core ideas of the later Wittgenstein -- language games, meaning as use, family resemblance concepts, forms of life, the idea that philosophical confusions come from language idling instead of working -- and restate each one as faithfully as you can in the vocabulary I actually use. For each one, tell me (a) does it translate cleanly, (b) does it only translate if you distort one side or the other, and (c) is there a residue that won't go in at all? For example, family resemblance looks like it should just be clusters in thingspace with no necessary-and-sufficient definition, game being the paradigm case. But I suspect Wittgenstein would push back and say the cluster itself is downstream of the shared purposes of a particular language game -- that there's no stable thing the word carves regardless of the games it's used in -- and I honestly can't tell whether natural abstractions already answers that or just changes the subject. Same with language games vs. the map being optimized for winning rather than accuracy: does that capture meaning-as-use, or does it quietly replace it with something more decision-theoretic than Wittgenstein ever intended? And forms of life -- is there anything in the LW corpus that's even trying to be that, or does my toolkit only have a slot for individual agents with maps?

Then give me the honest residue report: which parts of Wittgenstein is my vocabulary genuinely blind to, and what would I have to add to fix that; which parts are just less clear than the LW restatement and deserve to be dropped. Don't be charitable to either side by default -- if you think my friend is basically right that the sequence absorbed everything worth taking, say so, but if you think Wittgenstein has a point that no amount of cluster-talk can express, show me exactly where the translation breaks. I want to know whether I've been missing something for a decade or whether she's just gotten to philosophy-of-language-hippie status where everything is a language game.
