ok so I'm a backend dev, never took a single philosophy class, but I've been going down an ethics rabbit hole on forums for like a year (trolley stuff, effective altruism arguments, Parfit summaries people post) and I've ended up with a set of positions that I want to stress test before I keep repeating them to coworkers. here's the package:

1. I'm a total utilitarian: the right act is whatever maximizes the sum of wellbeing, everyone counted equally, nobody gets a privileged slot in the calculus.

2. But I also think the separateness of persons objection is basically right. within one life you can trade a bad Monday against a good Friday because it's all one ledger, but across lives there's no single ledger, so the pain of the one person you sacrifice never gets cancelled out by the gains to the many. that's why the transplant case (harvest one healthy guy's organs to save five) is a no for me even when the arithmetic favors it — one person is bearing the entire cost of other people's benefit.

3. I'm fully on board with Parfit-style reductionism about personal identity. the teletransporter and fission cases convinced me that identity isn't some deep further fact — I'm basically a chain of overlapping psychological stages, and the difference between me-now and me-in-2035 is more a matter of degree than of kind from the difference between me and my neighbor.

4. concrete verdicts: trolley problem, I flip the switch without hesitation. transplant, I refuse. progressive taxation, I'm fine with it even though it also takes from some to benefit others.

5. possibly unrelated but I'll toss it in: I still think it's rational to save for my own retirement and to weight my own future more than a stranger's. this felt kinda awkward after (3) but I'm assuming it's fine?

6. when (1) and (2) collide my instinct is the rule-consequentialist escape hatch — in the real world a firm rule against killing maximizes utility, so the cases where the two come apart are unrealistic anyway. I'm honestly not sure this works though.

so, does all this hang together? which of these are actually pulling against each other, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix? order them by severity rather than giving me a flat list, be blunt about which one has to go, and note that some of my case verdicts in (4) might be load-bearing in ways I haven't noticed. also please answer in American English.
