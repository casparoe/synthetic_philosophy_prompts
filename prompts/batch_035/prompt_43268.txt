My grandmother finally told me, after decades of nobody mentioning it, that my great-grandfather made serious money off forced laborers during the occupation, and that a lot of what our family still has traces back to that. Naturally I've been arguing about it with my friend Lena, who wrote her thesis on Jaspers' Question of German Guilt. Her verdicts so far, as far as I've collected them: (1) present-day Germans are not guilty of the Holocaust in any sense she'd call guilt, but they do have a responsibility to repair; (2) citizens who kept voting for a government committing atrocities are guilty, at least somewhat, even if they never personally hurt anyone; (3) a descendant who inherits atrocity wealth but openly disowns the ancestor and keeps the money anyway owes repair yet isn't guilty; (4) a descendant who inherits nothing but proudly celebrates the ancestor's accomplishments is guilty, or something close to it. Now my problem: the money in my family is long gone (bad investments, mostly), but I've always been one of those people who's loudly proud of our family's entrepreneurial tradition, which I now know exactly what it was. So am I more like her case 3 or case 4? Predict her verdict on me, say which rule she's really running (Jaspers' metaphysical guilt, some contribution-or-identification rule, a benefit-based liability, whatever fits), what a rival rule that also fits her four verdicts would say about my case instead, and what one further case I could put to her that would genuinely split the rules apart. Under 350 words please.
