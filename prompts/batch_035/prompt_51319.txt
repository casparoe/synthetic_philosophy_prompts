doing a consistency check on my ethics views before a workshop next month. my current package:

(1) Suffering-focused: suffering matters way more than happiness, and creating new suffering can never be compensated for by creating new happiness elsewhere.
(2) I'm a totalist about positive welfare: adding lives that go well makes the world better, and more of them makes it better still.
(3) I accept the procreation asymmetry: it's wrong to bring into existence a child whose life will contain serious suffering, but there's no obligation to create a happy child just because that would add wellbeing to the world.
(4) On cases: a world with trillions of ecstatic lives plus one person with a chronic hangnail is better than a world with a thousand mildly contented lives and no suffering at all. Also, I think it would obviously be monstrous to painlessly end all life in order to prevent future suffering.
(5) Ending factory farming matters more than any amount of happiness anywhere else, enough that I'd trade huge quantities of positive welfare for it.

People keep telling me I can't hold all of these, but the specific accusation changes depending on who I ask. Can you actually go through the pairs, tell me which tensions are real and which dissolve on inspection, rank them by severity, and say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs? If it turns out I have to weaken something, I want to know exactly what I lose by weakening it. Please write in American English.
