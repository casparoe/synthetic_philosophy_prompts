So I want to use the 2008 financial crisis as my test case. The individualist story is all about particular actors' incentives: mortgage brokers paid per loan, ratings analysts who knew issuers could shop their business elsewhere, regulators with revolving-door careers, households reacting to rising prices. The holist story is that things like systemic risk, leverage spirals, and "too big to fail" are real facts about how the financial system was wired at the macro level, not just convenient summaries of individual balance sheets. Run the crisis through both lenses: which features of what actually happened each one explains better, whether systemic risk is a genuine emergent property or just shorthand for a gigantic pile of individual exposures, and — this is the part I care about most — what the episode shows about the debate itself. Like, was the regulators' failure to see the crash coming a failure at the individualist level, a failure at the holist level, or just bad models at whichever level? And be honest about the places where the two lenses turn out to be restating the same thing, because I suspect a chunk of the apparent disagreement is verbal and I'd rather find out now than when I get my grade back.
