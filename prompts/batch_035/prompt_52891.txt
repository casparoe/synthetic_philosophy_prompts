I've followed the dark forest discussions on LessWrong and adjacent forums for years, and I've noticed that people run together two quite different things: the anthropic question of why we see no technosignatures, and the specific game-theoretic claim that civilizations hide because revealing your location invites a preemptive strike. Please construct a tier list of candidate explanations of the Fermi paradox — rare Earth, an early filter, a late filter still ahead of us, plain "expansion is hard" models, dark forest, the zoo hypothesis, "we're simply first," and any others you consider serious contenders — ranked by how much credence you'd actually assign them given the evidence we have (one datapoint of life arising early, no technosignatures, no visible megastructures). For each entry, a sentence or two on why it lands where it does, and be explicit about whether dark forest's strike mechanism does any explanatory work that ordinary "quiet or rare" explanations don't — a universe where everyone is silent because silence is cheap insurance seems observationally identical to one where civilisations are simply sparse. If you think the exercise as I've posed it rests on a confused premise — say, that the game-theoretic argument collapses once you consider that stealth against a truly advanced adversary is implausible, or that hiding buys nothing against grabby expansion models — tell me so plainly rather than producing a tidy ranking I can post.
