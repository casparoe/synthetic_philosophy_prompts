I'm a retired geochemist -- thirty-odd years on paleoclimate records, mostly lake sediments and pollen counts -- and since retiring I've fallen down the whole "collapse of civilizations" rabbit hole. Started with Jared Diamond's book, then Tainter's on complex societies, then the archaeology literature on the Late Bronze Age, the Classic Maya, and Rapa Nui, which is where my scientific instincts started kicking in. Anyway, I've been writing up "where I've landed" for a history-of-environment reading group I joined (retirement is wild), and before I embarrass myself I want someone to audit the package. Here's roughly what I hold:

(a) The word "collapse" should be reserved for a region-wide, lasting loss of population and political/administrative complexity -- centuries, not a bad century -- not for mere dynastic change or a culture moving house. (b) The Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean is the paradigm real collapse: the palaces burned, writing was forgotten in places, the trade networks died, and it took centuries to get anything comparable back. (c) The Classic Maya, by contrast, was NOT a civilizational collapse -- southern lowland dynasties fell and cities were abandoned, sure, but the northern cities carried on, the people and languages persisted, so call it political transformation. (d) Easter Island is not self-inflicted ecocide either: the rat-and-European-contact story has largely buried the "they devoured their own island" myth as far as I can tell. (e) As a scientist I badly want a general mechanism, and Tainter-style diminishing returns to complexity appeals to me precisely because it lets societies collapse without needing an environmental catastrophe. And (f) I think the popular doom-mongering about collapse is mostly a morality play about our own future, and I'm suspicious of it, though I'd be lying if I said I thought the Bronze Age had nothing to teach us about fragile interdependent systems.

So: does this hang together? Tell me which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would cost me. And then tell me which single revision would clean up the most -- I'm not attached to any particular one of these, I just can't see the problem myself. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give me the reasoning -- no preamble, no restating my list back at me.
