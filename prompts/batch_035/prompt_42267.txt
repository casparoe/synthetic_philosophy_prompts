I'm putting together a "persistence profile" for a metaphysics reading group — each of us writes down our commitments about time and persistence, and the others get to poke at them. I'd like you to act as a professional metaphysician who specializes in persistence, identity over time, and the philosophy of time, and audit my set of commitments for consistency. Please think carefully and work through this systematically: before giving any verdicts, briefly brainstorm and sort my commitments into clusters that bear on one another, and be explicit about your reasoning at each step. The deliverable I want is an audit, not a survey or lecture — assume I know the standard positions and arguments.

Here is my profile:

1. Eternalism. Past and future objects and events exist tenselessly; there is no objective coming-into-being or passing-away, and tense is not a feature of reality but of our perspective on it.
2. Endurantism. Ordinary persisting things are wholly present at every time at which they exist; persistence is strict numerical identity; there are no temporal parts of tables, rivers, or people.
3. Truthmaker maximalism. Every truth has a truthmaker among the things that exist.
4. Sparse physicalism about the fundamental. The fundamental properties are exhausted by those of completed physics; there are no fundamental temporally indexed properties and no fundamental merely Cambridge properties.
5. On the problem of temporary intrinsics, I much prefer adverbialism to temporal relativization. The poker at noon is not a thin stage that instantiates hotness; rather, the poker itself instantiates hotness noon-ly. I regard the relativization strategy — treating the poker-at-noon as the bearer — as smuggling stages back in under another name.
6. Special relativity is true, and there is no metaphysically privileged foliation of spacetime; no frame or simultaneity relation is distinguished by the structure of the world.
7. Persons persist by non-branching psychological continuity, and this criterion delivers strict identity over time. Against Parfit, I think identity itself is what matters in survival, not mere psychological connectedness. In fission cases, where psychological continuity branches to two equally good continuers, my considered judgment is that the original person survives as both.

Go through these and tell me: which pairs are genuinely in tension, and which only look like they are? Order the genuine tensions by severity rather than flagging everything equally. For each, say exactly what would have to give and what the cheapest fix costs me elsewhere in the profile. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most if I had to make one. I don't know where the real pressure is, so don't pull punches — but don't manufacture conflicts where a bit of care dissolves them either.
