I'm advising a small research group — two philosophers, a comparative psychologist, and one machine-learning researcher — that has roughly three years of funding for conceptual work on Dennett's hierarchy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures. Which open questions in and around that taxonomy are most worth their effort, and why? Among the candidates I can think of: whether the four kinds are genuinely nested rungs of a single ladder or partly independent capacities that co-occur for contingent reasons; where to place creatures that satisfy the Popperian criterion only by offloading hypothesis-testing onto conspecifics, tool use, or niche construction; and whether a system that fluently deploys humanity's accumulated thinking tools, as large language models do, should count as Gregorian by proxy despite arguably failing to be Popperian in Dennett's own sense. For each question you prioritize, state what makes it upstream or downstream of the others, how tractable it is for conceptual rather than empirical methods, and what findings in animal cognition or machine learning over the next few years would move it up or down the list. Throughout, please distinguish clearly between points about the taxonomy that are settled background and those that are genuinely contested among people who work on this, and do not present a contested reading as the consensus one.
