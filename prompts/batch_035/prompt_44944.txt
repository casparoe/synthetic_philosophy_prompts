I hold that special relativity supports the block universe: the argument that runs through Rietdijk and Putnam -- observers in relative motion carve spacetime into different simultaneity hyperplanes; the events on mine are real; your hyperplane intersects mine, so your present, however distant, is real too; chaining observers sweeps in everything past and future -- seems to me a genuine constraint of physics on metaphysics, not merely a consistency proof. I find the Penrose dramatization hardest to shrug off: two pedestrians pass on the street, and for one of them the Andromedean fleet's departure has already been decided while for the other it has not, prompting Penrose to ask whether the future of both people was already fixed. Tell me what would count against my position. I want the defeaters sorted from fatal to merely awkward, and distinguished by target, because I hold three separable claims: that the argument is valid, that its conclusion -- eternalism -- is true, and that physics positively supports rather than merely tolerates that conclusion. Which of these would a committed defender concede as genuine trouble -- Stein's point that presentness in relativity is local and that "real for observer O" is not a physical relation? A discovered violation of Lorentz invariance supplying a privileged foliation? The fact that relativistic cosmology already hands us a cosmic time and hence a natural foliation? Gödel spacetime? For each, tell me whether it cuts against the argument, the conclusion, or only the strongest reading of the physics-to-metaphysics inference, and whether it is live or merely logically possible. Finally: has the position been quietly requalified from "relativity implies eternalism" to "relativity is consistent with eternalism," and if so, is that insulation a problem for a view of this kind? Personally I would be most moved by a principled reason to deny that "real for O" can chain across observers, but tell me honestly whether any such reason is available, or whether the only exit is paying in a different coin.
