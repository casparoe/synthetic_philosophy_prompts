So I write novels — mostly 20th century political stuff, and my current one has a foreign-policy-brain character who's supposed to come across as genuinely wise, not just confident. Trouble is, I've realized the views I was planning to give him are basically my own views, and now I want to know if they actually hang together before I put them in anyone's mouth. Here's the package:

(1) Munich is the deepest lesson of the last century: accommodate an aggressor early and you just get a bigger war later, so leaders who don't see this have failed history class.

(2) Vietnam is also a real lesson: getting into somebody else's civil war, far away, against people who will fight forever, is a quagmire regardless of intentions.

(3) When (1) and (2) point in opposite directions during some new crisis, the way to resolve it is to ask which past situation the new one really resembles. So better pattern-matching is basically the cure — the leader with the deeper historical knowledge makes the better call.

(4) I find the Thucydides trap framing pretty persuasive: when a rising power confronts a ruling one, war is close to structurally determined, whatever any individual leader intends or decides.

(5) As a novelist I genuinely believe events turn on particular people. Put a different character in the room and Munich goes differently, the July crisis goes differently. Contingency, temperament, who's sleep-deprived that week — this stuff moves history.

(6) The fix for bad foreign policy is more historical literacy. Policymakers keep botching things because they dont know enough history.

I have a nagging feeling (4) and (5) can't both be true, and that maybe (3) is quietly assuming something it shouldn't — that the analogy method itself works if you're just knowledgeable enough — but I can't tell which of my worries are real tensions and which would dissolve if I thought about them for five minutes. Go through the set, tell me which commitments actually fight, which only look like they do, and rank them by how bad the tension is. For the real ones, say what would have to give and what it costs me. And if the whole thing collapses in some way I haven't even noticed, please say that plainly rather than rescuing it for me.
