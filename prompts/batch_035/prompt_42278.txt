A friend of mine who reads a lot of popular philosophy books has taken to telling me that all my "doing your bit" habits are pointless, and last week over dinner he set out his reasoning at some length. His summary went roughly like this:

"Morality in large collective problems works like this: what makes an act wrong is the harm it actually causes, and in a system with millions of participants, one person's contribution causes no harm at all. Your individual emissions don't raise global temperature by any amount — the harm comes from the total, and the total doesn't change because of you. It's the same with meat: the supermarket has already placed its orders for the year, so the number of animals reared and slaughtered is fixed whatever you put in your trolley, and a fixed amount of suffering with your purchases subtracted is still that same fixed amount of suffering. Since your act makes no difference, it makes no moral difference either — even consequentialism, which holds that acts are right or wrong solely by their consequences, tells you the act is not wrong. Philosophers have known this forever under the name of the problem of collective harm, and the only real reply anyone has is 'but what if everyone did that?', which is a straightforward logical fallacy, since what everyone else does is entirely independent of what you do. So in a modern economy, individual choices about climate, meat, or voting are morally weightless, and the only thing with moral force is systemic change — which, of course, no individual can bring about either."

I cycle to work and gave up flying three years ago, so perhaps I'm just motivated to disagree, but my honest reaction was that this is too tidy, and I have noticed my own objections pulling in different directions: sometimes I want to say he's wrong about the facts (surely my emissions do add something to the total, however small), and sometimes I want to say he's wrong about the moral step even granting the facts (that an act changes nothing needn't make it innocent). I don't have the training to sort out which of these instincts is doing the work, and I would rather not just take his word for it that the only reply available is the 'what if everyone did that' line he dismisses as a fallacy. It also seemed suspicious that he threw voting in at the end as though it were exactly the same case as meat and climate, when elections do occasionally come down to a handful of votes.
