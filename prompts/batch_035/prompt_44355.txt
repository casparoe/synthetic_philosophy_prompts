Every explainer of the frame problem uses blocks-world robots, but my sense is it's really about something I deal with weekly: when one thing changes (I switch jobs, a flatmate moves out), most of my standing assumptions stay true and a handful suddenly matter, and I somehow figure out which is which without re-deriving everything from scratch. Assume a theory of the frame problem is meant to explain exactly that ability in any agent, natural or artificial. Write the desiderata: what an adequate theory of this has to deliver -- which cases it must get right, which distinctions it has to respect (like facts persisting by default vs. facts that were never relevant at all, or updating beliefs about a change vs. updating while acting), which constraints from real agents (they're fast, fallible, and can't re-check everything) it cant contradict -- with the motivating case behind each, and say which requirements are non-negotiable and which are just prejudices of one camp (say, the demand that the whole solution be purely logical/syntactic). Also flag any two requirements you suspect cant be jointly satisfied -- that part interests me most. Exactly five desiderata, numbered list, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion.
