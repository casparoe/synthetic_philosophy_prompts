I am a third-year philosophy undergraduate taking a module in bioethics, and I have agreed to run a fifty-minute session for the university's Science and Society discussion group, an audience of sharp non-specialists, mostly engineers and medics with a few literature students, on the question of whether brain size is morally relevant. I do not want you to write the explanation itself; I want a plan for how I should deliver it. Please advise on the order in which the components must be introduced and why, given the dependencies between them: presumably the distinction between mere brain mass and cortical neuron count has to be separated early from intelligence itself, but you should tell me what has to come before what, and what falls apart if the sequence is rearranged. For the carrying example I am tempted to use the comparison between human beings and sperm whales, whose brains are roughly six times the size of ours, but I suspect that is the wrong choice for reasons I half understand and would like you to articulate; consider instead a fictionalised clinical trial of an experimental growth factor that measurably increases cortical neuron count in adult volunteers, and tell me what that example must contain and what it must be stripped of to do its job. I would also like a map of the misconceptions the audience will predictably arrive with or form mid-session: the conflation of size with intelligence and intelligence with moral status, the assumption that degree collapses into a threshold, the worry, inherited from the history of craniometry, that the entire topic is a respectable front for ranking human beings against one another, and anything else you foresee. For each misconception, tell me where in the sequence it should be headed off and how, rather than waiting for it to surface in questions. Suggest one question I can put to the group at the very end whose answers would show me whether they have genuinely grasped the issue or merely enjoyed the ride, and tell me honestly what I should openly decline to cover at this level, naming it as omitted rather than pretending it does not exist. Please write your response as continuous prose only, in the manner of a careful email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no tables of any kind. And please use British English throughout.
