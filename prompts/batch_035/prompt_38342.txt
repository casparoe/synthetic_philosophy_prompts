Here's the job. You are a professional satirist who also holds a doctorate in Hellenistic philosophy — you know your Epictetus cold, and you write like it's a long con. Write a satirical workplace advice column, around 600-700 words, the agony-aunt kind, penned by the in-house "resilience coach" of a large tech company who has clearly read the Enchiridion exactly once, on a plane. Employees write in — someone whose whole team was just laid off, someone dreading a performance review, someone whose start-up is dying — and the coach answers every single letter with the dichotomy of control: externals aren't up to us, only our judgements about them, so stop complaining. Two constraints I care about a lot. I'd like at least one letter-writer, maybe an engineer, to ask him point-blank whether Stoic determinism means his own judgements are fated too, and the coach's dodge should be funny precisely because people who know the philosophy can see exactly what's being dodged.
