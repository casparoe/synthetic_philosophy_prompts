Retired biochemist here, got into philosophy a few years after I stopped working and now I can't stop seeing philosophy everywhere. Something I ran into constantly in science and now keep meeting again in philosophy reading, and I've never found a word for it: categories that everyone uses without trouble but that fall apart the moment you try to say what all their members have in common. Species is the famous case — every criterion anyone proposes, whether interbreeding or shared ancestry or occupying a niche, either has known exceptions or turns out to be several criteria that pick out overlapping but different groups. "Planet" was like that until the astronomers just voted a definition into existence, and "gene" arguably still is. But what I want a name for is more specific than "hard to define". It's not ordinary fuzziness at the edges, a clear core with borderline cases the way "bald" or "heap" are. It's more that the members seem held together by a chain of overlapping similiarities — neighbors share a lot, members at opposite ends share almost nothing, and there may be no single feature that all of them have — and yet experts still classify new cases with high agreement, so the category is doing something more than convention. Does this kind of category, or the move of defining by overlapping similarities instead of a common feature, have an established name and literature? And a bare label won't satisfy me: I want to know how exactly it differs from ordinary vagueness, whether it's different from just a bunch of properties that happen to be correlated (a biologist friend mentioned something along those lines for natural kinds, I didn't quite follow), and whether the lesson is that the category is defective or that our insistence on strict definitions is the mistake.
