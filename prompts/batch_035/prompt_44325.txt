My Nomic group has accumulated rulings on self-reference disputes, and I now have to break a tie over a new one, so I want both a prediction of what we will decide and a recommendation of what we should decide for consistency with ourselves. The setup is standard: rules are divided into immutable and mutable ones, and rule changes require a two-thirds majority unless a rule says otherwise. Our precedents: (1) a proposal to replace the tie-breaking rule was adopted on a tie broken by that very rule, and we held the adoption valid — a rule may govern the act that repeals it; (2) we allowed repeal of a mutable rule carrying a rider declaring itself unamendable — self-inscribed perpetuity grants nothing; (3) we voided a proposal that would have lowered the two-thirds majority to the proposer alone, even though it had 60% support; (4) we voided a single bundled proposal that both transmuted an immutable rule to mutable and repealed it, holding that these must be sequential. The new case: a proposal to lower the majority for rule changes to a simple majority, explicitly worded to apply to its own adoption, which received 55%. Two readings fit our record — one on which no rule change ever affects its own adoption, so the old threshold governs and this fails, and one on which benign self-application is permitted and only power-concentrating changes like (3) are barred. Lead with your prediction and recommendation in the first sentence, then give the reasoning: which reading best fits the precedents and why, which precedent is the closest analog, and what case, if it arose next, would most cleanly test the reading you favor against the alternative.
