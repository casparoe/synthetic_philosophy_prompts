I'm a PhD student in political theory, and I'm prepping a session for our comparativist-heavy department's grad seminar on democratic erosion. The recurring problem in our discussions is that people talk past each other: when someone says "Poland under PiS was backsliding but the US wasn't" or "Turkey stopped being a democracy around 2013," it's never clear whether they disagree about facts, about the threshold for the concept, or about who gets to apply it. I want a decision tree that forces the disagreement out into the open.

Please write me a decision tree that starts from a concrete case -- say, an incumbent that packs the constitutional court, then uses friendly rulings to weaken media regulators and election administration, but keeps holding elections it could in principle lose -- and leads to a named position on what democratic backsliding is. The branch points should be questions a person can answer without knowing which position they'll land in, and they should be questions about cases and tests, not about labels. I'd want the tree to at least separate: (a) the view where democracy is basically competitive elections, so backsliding means closing the arena; (b) the view where it's executive aggrandizement in increments, none individually disqualifying, so the diagnosis is about trajectory rather than any single act; (c) the view where the real criterion is equality or non-domination, so a country can backslide without touching election rules at all; and (d) the deflationist view that the term is mostly a partisan weapon and we should retire it in favor of more specific complaints. If your tree can't cleanly separate any two of these, say so instead of fudging it. Also somewhere in there I want a branch about the partisan symmetry test -- i.e., whether you'd apply the same diagnosis if the other side did it -- since that's where my seminar always derails. Flag any leaf that's basically unoccupied in the actual literature, and say whether you think that's because the view is incoherent or just unattractive or merely unexplored.

Keep the whole thing under 800 words including the tree itself, since I'll put it on one handout. And at the end, give me a short note -- 3 or 4 sentences max -- on where the tree is most likely to mislead: which branch placement you're least confident in, and what a critic would point to first to argue the whole diagnostic approach is wrong. If someone brought a real-world case that broke your tree, I want to know in advance where it would break. No citations please, I'll dig those out myself after.
