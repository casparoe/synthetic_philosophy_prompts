hey, so I'm a science-and-ideas writer at a mid-size magazine and my editor let me have the back page for a "comedy issue" thing we're doing in the spring. I want to commission you for the piece, here's the brief.

Write a parody of a consumer-product review column, the kind of breathless "we tested 47 standing desks" Wirecutter thing, but the editorial team behind it are strict lexical threshold negative utilitarians. Quick refresher so we're on the same page: their view is that suffering beyond a certain severity threshold matters lexically -- no amount of happiness, however large, can outweigh even one instance of severe pain -- while below the threshold, pleasures and mild pains trade off more or less normally. The column should be about something mundane, like mattresses or vacation packages or kitchen knives, roughly 600-700 words, and the humor has to come from the actual structure of the view, not from making them seem dumb:

- every product review quietly becomes a risk assessment, since expected pleasure above the threshold literally counts for zero in the final score, so a mildly uncomfortable chair can never be "worth it" for the view
- the "best pick" should be whatever minimizes tail-risk of bad outcomes (the boring, over-engineered, safety-first option), with the "also great" and "budget pick" following the same logic
- somewhere they should run into the two-currencies problem head-on: pleasures and severe pains just don't commensurate, and you can play that like a finance desk trying to find an exchange rate between two currencies where one of them has been delisted
- include at least one dissenting tester, a classical hedonistic utilitarian who keeps trying to sneak aggregate pleasure back into the scoring rubric and gets voted down
- and end with them noticing the awkward endpoint of their own methodology, deadpan, without breaking character

So a specialist reading it should go "ah, yes, this is exactly what happens when you take the threshold seriously" rather than feeling like their view got strawmanned. Readers who know nothing about utilitarianism should still find it a funny review column, just one with weird priorities. No need to cite anyone, keep it clean of names and references. Deadpan throughout -- no winking at the reader, the comedy is that they're perfectly reasonable people being perfectly reasonable about this.
