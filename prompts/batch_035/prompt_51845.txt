Compose a satirical job advertisement for the post of Moral Saint at a fictional organisation. The advertisement must consist of exactly five numbered essential criteria and nothing else — no introductory paragraph about the organisation, no salary details, no closing invitation to apply. Each criterion should run to a sentence or two. The humour should come from getting the philosophy right rather than from simple exaggeration: the criteria should quietly take positions in the debate over whether maximal moral goodness is desirable, so that a reader familiar with that literature can see the author understands it and finds the satire fair. Among the criteria, include ones that do the following: one that quietly excludes every distinctively non-moral excellence — wit, culinary discernment, a love of tennis or of particular persons — as incompatible with the post; one that distinguishes between candidates who are good out of love and candidates who merely do their duty, and hints at which the panel prefers; one that concedes, deadpan, that the successful applicant will be rather dull at dinner parties and that this is by design; and one that reveals, without ever stating it, that the organisation is not at all sure it wants the successful candidate to exist. Keep the register of a genuine recruitment advertisement throughout — person-specification jargon, equal-opportunities boilerplate — and remain deadpan: the position being satirised is the ideal of moral perfection itself, not morally serious people.
