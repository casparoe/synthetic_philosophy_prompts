You remember the ice bucket challenge from 2014, right? Everyone dumping buckets of ice water on their heads on social media to raise money for ALS research, and it genuinely worked -- something like a hundred million plus dollars flooding into ALS charities in a few weeks, for a disease most of the donors had barely heard of a month earlier. Here's what I want: take that whole episode and run it through the importance / tractability / neglectedness framework that effective altruists use for picking causes. Roughly how does ALS score on each of the three axes, and what would the framework have said about where all that money *should* have gone instead (malaria nets or whatever)? And then the harder direction, because it seems like the framework kind of gets embarassed here. On the numbers, ALS is the definition of low importance -- rare disease, small patient population -- so an ITN purist presumably wishes that money had gone to bednets. Yet the windfall plausibly sped up ALS research by years (my impression is that some of it fed into work that eventually connected to an approved drug, though don't quote me on the details), and it's not like the donors would have given the same money to a more "optimal" cause -- they gave because of a viral stunt, and absent the stunt the counterfactual was probably zero. So what gives? Is this just a case where ITN is a tool for allocating a *fixed* budget and has literally nothing to say about how to generate donations in the first place, since the challenge was a marketing phenomenon and in the viral moment ALS's neglectedness collapsed to zero? Or does it show something worse, like the axes quietly stop making sense when a cause goes viral -- does "neglectedness" even mean anything if it can evaporate in six weeks based on nothing about the disease itself? I'd also like you to actually take a stance on whether a consistent ITN-er should wish the ice bucket challenge never happened, and whether saying yes reflects badly on the framework or on the person saying it. And please be honest about where the framework is silent instead of pretending it has opinions about everything.
