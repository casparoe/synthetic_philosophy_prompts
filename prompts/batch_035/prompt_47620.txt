I've been reading about coherent extrapolated volition -- the idea that instead of asking what people actually want now, you ask what they *would* want if they knew more, thought faster, were more the people they wished they were, grown up together etc. It comes from the AI alignment world, as an answer to what a superintelligent AI should do for humanity, but my situation is much smaller and I keep feeling like the two are connected somehow.

My grandmother has advancing dementia. About eight years ago, while sharp as a tack, she wrote an advance directive refusing certain interventions, with a paragraph about not wanting to "linger as a shell." Now she's genuinely content most days -- cat, garden shows, pudding -- and doesn't recognize the person who wrote that document. The family has split into camps: the ones who say the directive is her real voice because it was written by the version of her that could actually deliberate, and the ones who say the person in front of us enjoying her afternoon is the one whose day we're actually deciding about.

Please describe the most important implications of CEV-style idealized-preference thinking for a concrete case like ours. Not the AI stuff for its own sake -- I want to know which parts of the CEV framework actually transfer to surrogate decisions about a family member, where they break down, and what they'd say about which camp is (if either) reasoning correctly. Along the way, please say how confident you are in each of the main claims, and flag the single point you're least sure about.
