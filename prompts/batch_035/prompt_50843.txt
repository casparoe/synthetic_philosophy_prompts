My brother-in-law and I have been arguing about quantum immortality for going on two years now, mostly over text during family holidays, and we've finally decided to actually write down where we stand instead of lobbing the same three points at each other. For context on the two camps: he's a committed Everettian who thinks the quantum suicide / quantum immortality argument basically works -- that in a genuinely quantum-random fatal event, there will always be some branch where the gun misfires, and so "you" should expect to keep finding yourself on a surviving branch, in a way that doesn't hold for classical randomness. I'm also fine with many-worlds as an interpretation, but I think quantum immortality is a misapplication of it, roughly an anthropic sleight of hand where you condition on surviving and then act surprised that you survived. So note that we're NOT arguing about Copenhagen vs. Everett -- we both accept the branching structure -- the fight is entirely about what, if anything, an observer embedded in that structure should expect and how they should reason.
