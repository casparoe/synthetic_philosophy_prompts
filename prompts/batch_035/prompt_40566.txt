I'm a PhD student in epistemology, currently TA-ing a course that has just reached the post-Gettier literature. For my discussion sections I want a decision-tree handout that sorts students into a position on the analysis of knowledge — but I'm tired of quizzes that sort people by which label they happen to have heard of. So please build the tree from the cases upward: first the diagnostic cases and the answer combinations they permit, and only then the named positions located at the leaves.

The tree should start from the original phenomenon — justified true belief that is true only by luck — and branch on how a student treats the following fixed set of cases: (i) the canonical case, in which a justified but false belief entails a disjunction that turns out true through its other disjunct; (ii) fake-barn country, where perception is functioning perfectly but the environment is saturated with convincing fakes; (iii) a lottery, where the belief that your ticket will lose is overwhelmingly well supported and true, yet seems unknowable in advance of the draw; (iv) a case where the method is globally unreliable but the particular belief could not easily have been false — a thermometer stuck at a value that happens to be right; and (v) a deviant-chain case, where the fact that makes the belief true is causally downstream of the believing, but in the wrong way. Each branch node should be a question about a case that a student with no theory in mind could answer from intuition alone.

The leaves should include, at minimum: JTB patched with a no-false-lemmas clause; causal theories; simple reliabilism; sensitivity; safety; virtue-theoretic or anti-luck approaches; knowledge-first; and the deflationary line that the Gettier literature shows no informative analysis of knowledge is to be had. Please be explicit about any pair of leaves this case-set cannot prise apart — my suspicion is that safety and anti-luck virtue views will come out entangled, and I would rather know which further node would be needed to separate them than have the tree paper over the overlap. Finally, mark any answer-combination in the logical space that no position occupies, with a sentence on whether that is because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
