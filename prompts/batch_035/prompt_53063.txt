My friend Dara has been ruling on personal identity cases for years, basically since we argued about a Star Trek episode in college, and she's honestly pretty consistent about it. Her track record, as best I can reconstruct it:

- The classic teletransporter — destructive scan on Earth, exact replica steps out on Mars a second later — that's survival. The person got to Mars.
- Brain transplant: brain removed, kept alive in a support vat for a week while a new body is grown, then transplanted in. Obviously the person survives, she says, no hesitation.
- Cryostasis: frozen solid for a decade, brain completely intact, then revived. Survival.
- Gradual replacement: over about a year each neuron is swapped for a silicon duplicate one at a time, the body never shuts down. Survival, and not even a close call for her.
- The overlap case: scan sent to Mars, the original kept alive on Earth, both live in parallel for a week, then the original dies in an accident. Here she flips: the original was the person the whole time, the replica never was him, and when the original died the person died.
- The fission case: one scan, two replicas, original destroyed. Neither replica is him — he just died and two new people with his memories started existing.

3. The quick fission fix: two replicas are created but one is destroyed within a minute, before it ever wakes up. Does the surviving replica count as him?
