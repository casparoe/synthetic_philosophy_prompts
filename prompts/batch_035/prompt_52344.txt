"Disjunctivism is just the claim that veridical perception and hallucination have nothing whatsoever in common. When you actually see a table, your mental state is a completely different kind of thing from when you merely hallucinate one — there's no shared 'experience' underlying both cases. The motivation is the classic argument from illusion: when a stick looks bent in water, you're having an hallucination of a bent stick, and since that hallucination could in principle be subjectively identical to a veridical seeing of a bent stick, the good case and the bad case must involve the very same inner state. That's what the sense-datum people concluded, and the disjunctivists — some Oxford crowd originally, I think — deny it. But here's the problem: hallucinations genuinely do fool us, and if two states are subjectively indistinguishable they must share something in common, so disjunctivism is simply refuted. The only consolation is that in the good case you're directly acquainted with the table itself, so the disjunctivist at least escapes external-world scepticism completely — the evil demon worry never even gets going."
