I'm on the safety team at a company that trains large language models, and leadership is considering a proposal for evaluating a new system before deployment: two instances of a stronger model would debate whether the new system's deployment plan is safe — one assigned to argue that it's dangerous, the other that it's safe — with human reviewers judging the transcripts. A colleague objects that this can't give us real evidence: a sufficiently good debater can produce a persuasive-sounding counterargument to anything, and can even mirror its opponent's style of reasoning, so which side looks better will track rhetorical skill and framing rather than truth. And the failure mode we're most worried about is precisely that the new system might produce arguments that look good to humans while being wrong. Is the debate protocol worth running at all? If some version of it is, what conditions would need to hold — about the debaters, the judges, and how the question is posed — for a judged win to count as evidence about safety rather than evidence about persuasiveness?
