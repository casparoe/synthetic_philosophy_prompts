My roommate went down a quantum physics rabbit hole a few months ago and came out a full-on many-worlds guy — any time anything chancy happens he likes to announce that every outcome is literally happening to copies of him in other branches. It used to just be a bit. Lately it's started affecting how he actually behaves, and I want to know if there's a principled response or if I just have to live with this.

Two things in particular. At our weekly no-limit game he's been making calls nobody would ever make, chasing the thinnest draws for his whole stack, and when I asked him about it his justification was basically: "if every outcome happens, then some version of me wins a massive pot tonight, and that guy is just as much me as the one who busts out. Why would I fold? Folding protects one version of me and cheats the other. I'm playing for the one who gets lucky." He said it like it was obviously the rational move once you accept many-worlds.

Then last week he told me he's not renewing his travel insurance, because "the branch where my luggage gets lost exists no matter what, and the branch where it's fine exists too, so the best I can do is flip a coin between them, and 50/50 coverage isn't worth the premium." So in one case he acts like he should maximize the best branch, in the other like all branches are equal, and he doesn't seem to notice these pull in opposite directions.

Something in both arguments seems off to me, but every time I push back I end up just waving my hands about "probability" and he politely steamrolls me, since he's genuinely read more about this than I have. So: where DO the odds come from if all the branches are real? Is it just that there are more copies of him in the lucky branches (which sounds suspiciously like branch-counting and I suspect he'd object to it), or is there some better story many-worlds people tell? And if he's wrong — which I keep assuming — which of his two arguments is the more wrong one? They feel different in kind to me, like one is about how much to care about the other versions of him and the other is about how to weight them, but I can't articulate the distinction well enough to land it on him. Please give me something I can actually say at the table on thursday.
