I'm a PhD student in philosophy of physics preparing a handout for a seminar on the ontological status of the quantum state. Please write a decision tree that starts from the question of what the quantum state represents and terminates in named positions on psi-ontology. Construct it from the substantive questions rather than from interpretation labels — for instance: whether the quantum state purports to describe physical systems themselves or only an agent's information about them; whether, if it does describe systems, two distinct quantum states could be compatible with the same underlying physical situation (the issue the Pusey--Barrett--Rudolph theorem bears on); and whether accepting a real wavefunction commits one to any particular story about outcomes. The leaves should include Bohmian mechanics, Everett, objective-collapse theories, QBism, and a Copenhagen-style instrumentalism, and any combination of answers that turns out to be unoccupied should be included as an 'unnamed' leaf with a note on whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Please also flag honestly where the tree fails to separate positions — my worry is that Bohmian mechanics and Everett land on the same branch, since both treat the state as ontic, so if that happens, say what further question, if any, would split them. Finally, throughout the handout, keep separate what is settled among people who know this literature (e.g. the experimental status of Bell inequality violations) from what is genuinely contested — for example, what the PBR theorem actually establishes once its preparation-independence assumption is on the table — and do not present one contested reading as the consensus view.
