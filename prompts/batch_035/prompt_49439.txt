Longtime lurker here, been reading the decision theory threads since the old Overcoming Bias days, and I finally have a ranking request of my own re: Kavka's toxin puzzle. Quick setup in case: billionaire pays you a million tonight if at midnight you intend to drink a toxin tomorrow, one that makes you thoroughly sick for a day but does no lasting harm. Once midnight passes the money is yours no matter what, and you know all of this in advance. The puzzle is that intuitively you should somehow get yourself to intend to drink, but once the money's in the bank you'll have zero reason to actually drink, and knowing that in advance seems to make forming the intention impossible.

I've read a bunch of proposed resolutions over the years and I want them ranked, tier-list style, by how much you'd trust them to actually dissolve the puzzle rather than just redescribe it. The candidates as I understand them:

1. The CDT-ish line: the intention is genuinely irrational, or maybe impossible, for a fully rational agent, intuition be damned, and the puzzle dissolves by debunking the intuition.
2. The one-boxer line: agents with dispositions to form and follow through on such intentions do better overall, so rationality is about policy rather than act, and the "irrational" agent walks away with the million.
3. The Gauthier-ish line: intention isn't merely a prediction of your own future behavior but a real commitment state with some causal grip on your future deliberation, and once you see intention that way the puzzle evaporates.
4. The deflationary line: there's just nothing that "intend to drink" could be in this scenario, since intending entails being disposed to act when the time comes and you know you won't be, so the whole question rests on a confused folk-psychological concept.
5. My own half-baked addition: treat intending as a bet on yourself. Maybe intention is functionally like pricing a contract on your own future drinking, and the puzzle is really a coordination problem between today-you and tomorrow-you that collapses once you price the contract right. I suspect this is just 3 in worse clothes but I honestly can't tell.

Rank these (and slot in a candidate I'm obviously missing if there is one), and score each on two dimensions separately: (a) how well it explains why the intuition is so dang persistent, and (b) whether it gives an actual agent practical guidance before the midnight deadline. I care a lot more about (b) than (a). Also flag which candidates are secretly the same view.

Format-wise, please use these sections in this order: "Tier list" (just the ranking, one short line per tier), "Reasons" (the bulk of it, a paragraph-ish per candidate covering both dimensions), "Collapses" (which candidates are the same view in different hats, a few sentences), and "Your addition" (whether candidate 5 is worth anything and what you'd fix, short). If my bet-on-yourself framing is incoherent, say so plainly, I can take it.
