hey so im TA-ing a phil of mind course and next week we're doing the Hofstadter ant colony stuff, the Aunt Hillary dialogue from GEB. i want to close the session with a short poem, maybe 3-4 quatrains, no fixed rhyme scheme necesarily, and here's the trick i want it to pull off: every individual line has to describe one ant doing something completely mindless — following a pheromone trail, hauling a crumb, grooming a nestmate, whatever. like, entomologically mundane, line by line. but each stanza, read as a whole, has to come out as something a genuine thinker might say — a plan, a worry, an observation about the weather. the reader should get the levels-of-description point from the form itself: dumb parts, minded whole, without you having to editorialize about emergence anywhere in the poem. then the last stanza should turn on the realization that the person reading the poem is built out of cells doing basically the same thing, but you're not allowed to use the word "neuron" (or brain) anywhere until that final turn, and honestly maybe not even then. don't have the poem settle whether Aunt Hillary "really" has a mind — leave that dangling, thats what our discussion is for. keep it light though, a little charmed by the whole thing, not a lecture in verse
