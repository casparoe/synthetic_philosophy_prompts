Quick background, because the situation is genuinely odd: I teach philosophy of law, and for about six years I've been in a long-running Nomic game with a handful of colleagues and grad students -- the self-amending rules game where changing the rules is itself a move. Our ruleset has drifted pretty far from Suber's original, but we kept the skeleton: rules are either immutable (numbered in the 100s) or mutable (200s), immutable rules take precedence, and a mutable rule has to be transmuted into an immutable one before it can be entrenched, which under our house rules takes unanimous consent of the full roster. Disputes about validity go to a three-person panel of senior players we call the rules council, and I chair it this session. Last week a player (a cunning legal positivist, for what it's worth) proposed amending mutable Rule 232, which says the council interprets disputes, by appending this sentence: "Rule 232 may not be amended, repealed, or transmuted, and no rule enacted later shall have any effect insofar as it conflicts with this sentence." The amendment itself passed our ordinary two-thirds threshold for mutable rule changes, and now it's on our plate, because two players have already announced they'll propose repealing it next session and a third has threatened to quit over the whole mess.

The council has to circulate a written ruling before Friday's session, and I'd like you to draft the memo I'll send around. It needs a concrete bottom line -- uphold the amendment as enacted including the entrenchment sentence, strike just the entrenchment sentence as void, void the whole amendment, or something else you think fits better -- plus the reasoning in terms the players can actually act on. The obvious tension the memo has to confront: if the power to change rules is, in our game, unlimited, then the entrenchment sentence seems void the moment it's enacted (a rule can't limit the very power that made it); but if the entrenchment sentence is valid, then the power wasn't unlimited after all, and the player found a shortcut around the unanimity requirement for transmutation, which feels like cheating even though nobody can quite say why. I'd also like the memo to say what should happen when someone inevitably proposes the repeal anyway -- rejected as invalid?
