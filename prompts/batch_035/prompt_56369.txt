Write me a dialogue between two AI safety researchers, Dara and Felix, about whether their lab's agent should be allowed to rewrite its own source code. The setup: the agent reasons formally, and before swapping itself out for a modified version it wants a proof that the successor will only take actions it currently endorses. The catch is whats called the Löbian obstacle: by Löb's theorem, a system that proves enough arithmetic can't also prove that its own proofs are reliable — if it could establish "everything I prove is true", it could prove anything at all, including that its fine to hand control over to a garbage successor. Dara keeps proposing workarounds, like having each generation reason in a stronger logic than the last one used. Felix thinks this just pushes the trust problem up a level, since every system still has to assume something it can't establish from the inside. Make both of them genuinely sharp — Dara should have real counter-moves, not strawman optimism, and Felix should engage with her actual proposals instead of just repeating Löb at her. By the end they should land somewhere specific, even if they still disagree.
