Okay, so quick background: I've been in the same rationalist meetup for something like seven years now (long enough that we've done the repugnant conclusion argument at least four separate times, and once a guy nearly quit over it). Last week we had maybe the longest argument I've seen there yet. It started because Maya and Jonas announced they're trying for a kid. They're both carriers of the same recessive condition, so any child they conceive has a 25% shot at a painful lifelong disorder — the kind of life most people who have it still say is worth living, but only barely, and with a lot of rough going. IVF with screening is available to them but expensive and they're ambivilent, and that set off a two-hour fight between Maya's sister Priya and our friend Daniel. I mostly just watched. It went roughly like this.

(1) Priya said she holds what she called the procreation asymmetry: creating a person whose life will be miserable is a serious wrong done to that person, but declining to create a person whose life would be good is not a wrong at all — there's no one there to be deprived of anything. So Maya and Jonas should screen or not conceive, because a 1-in-4 chance of a bad life is a big deal. But she admitted she would never say a couple has a *duty* to have a kid even if the kid would be wonderfully happy. Absent happy people aren't victims; created miserable ones are. (2) Daniel said the asymmetry can't survive the non-identity point. The possible child with the disorder is *that* child; the alternative to conceiving her isn't a healthy version of her, it's no child at all. And nobody is harmed by being brought into a life that, however hard, they'd still endorse — so "the wrong done to that child" has no referent, since Priya is comparing an actual life against a baseline that never existed. Either existence can be compared to nonexistence, in which case every life worth living counts as a benefit and the asymmetry collapses back into symmetry, or it can't, in which case she can't call the miserable child a victim either. (3) Priya answered that Daniel is assuming harm has to be counterfactual — some other possible version of you that fares better — and she thinks that's just wrong as an account of harm. A person born into a life far below a decent threshold has been wronged whether or not a better-off counterpart exists anywhere in logical space; the wrong is landing below the floor, not losing to a rival possible person. She also said the endorsement test proves nothing, since people famously adjust their standards downward and tell themselves a hard life is worth living — self-reports from the only people alive to ask are biased exactly the way that matters. (4) Daniel said fine, if harm is threshold-based instead of comparative, then she owes us the threshold, and every candidate line looks ad hoc: "worth living by the person's own lights" lets the endorsement point back in, and an objective floor just means someone's intuitions got promoted to a principle. He said the asymmetry is an intuition shopping for a theory, and population ethics intuitions are notoriously frame-dependent — people flip on non-identity cases depending on whether the person is standing in the room. He also said he'd accept a weak asymmetry — creating miserable lives is bad in a way creating happy lives isn't correspondingly good — but claimed that's just the act/omission distinction wearing fancy clothes, and we've all been taught to distrust that one. (5) Priya closed by conceding her intuitions might be biased, but said every alternative is worse: total views ride straight into the repugnant conclusion, average views imply even creepier things, and at least her view never tells anyone to create suffering for the sake of maximizing numbers. Then they both turned to me and I honestly had nothing.

Can you referee this for me move by move? For each of the five steps, say who's right about the specific claim actually being made there, and where each of them is overreaching or sliding between two different things. Then tell me: does Priya's asymmetry survive in any form, and if so which formulation is the defensible one? Is Daniel right that the whole thing bottoms out in act vs. omission? And is there anything both of them are missing that would've gotten them unstuck — I'd like to be able to say something smarter than "huh" if this comes up again at the next meetup.
