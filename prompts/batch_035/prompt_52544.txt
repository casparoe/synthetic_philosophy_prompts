My doctoral supervisor has spent her career on the question of societal collapse, and she has a settled ruling on nearly every celebrated case. The Mycenaean palatial world around 1200 BCE: a genuine collapse — the palaces burned, the administrative script was forgotten, and nothing recognizable carried the system forward. The Indus cities around 1900 BCE: collapse, full stop — the urbanism and the script simply end. The Terminal Classic southern Maya lowlands: not a collapse but a reorganization — the population didn't vanish, it moved, and northern centers carried Maya kingship and writing forward. The western Roman Empire: not a collapse — successor kingdoms, the church, Roman law, and the population carried on. Easter Island: a genuine collapse — the statue system and the ecology that sustained it were terminally lost, whatever one thinks of the fashionable ecological narratives. The end of the Old Kingdom in Egypt: not a collapse — an interregnum, after which the state reconstituted itself.

Maddeningly, she refuses to state her definition; she says the cases come first and the definition is whatever survives them. We have a standing bet on Angkor. On one hand, the enormous low-density city and its waterworks were abandoned in the fifteenth century, the Sanskrit temple inscriptions ceased, and Theravada Buddhism displaced the older royal cults. On the other hand, the Khmer kingdom persisted in reduced form, the Khmer script and literary tradition continued, and the population remained recognizably the same people.

Predict her verdict on Angkor. More importantly, reconstruct the rule, or candidate rules, that best rationalize her six rulings — I want at least two rules that fit her record equally well, what each of them predicts for Angkor, and your best guess with reasons. Then propose the single further case whose verdict would most cleanly discriminate between those rules, and say what verdict each rule would demand for it. Finally, tell me whether her track record, once rationalized, amounts to a substantive notion of collapse at all, or only to a label for changes she happens to find interesting.
