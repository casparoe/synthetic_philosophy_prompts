So here's my situation. I'm a journalist — I cover science and ideas, mostly long-form features — and I've just gotten a commision for a series on what quantum mechanics actually *means* for a general readership. Budget is roughly 10,000 words total across three pieces. My editor thinks interpretations are the sexiest thing in physics right now and wants me to lead with the many-worlds stuff ("there are infinite copies of you!" vibes). My physicist friend, meanwhile, says the whole interpretive debate is metaphysics until someone proposes a distinguishable experiment, and that I should basically shut up and calculate. And I have a guy who keeps emailing me insisting superdeterminism is the real answer and everyone else is in denial.

I want you to play the role of a philosopher of physics who's spent twenty years in the foundations debate and also advises journalists, so you know how to be honest about what's hype. Please think carefully and actually brainstorm before you commit to anything — list the candidates first, then settle on a ranking.

Here are the disputes I could spend my words on, roughly:

- Everett vs Copenhagen vs Bohm — is this a substantive fight or mostly taste?
- The measurement problem itself, and where the classical world comes from
- Whether the Born rule is a postulate or something that needs deriving (I gather the Everett people have a hard time with probability?)
- Wigner's-friend-style extended Bell arguments and whether they force a choice between interpretations or just confuse everybody
- Bell/nonlocality — does Bohmian mechanics wearing its nonlocality on its sleeve count for it or against it?
- Whether quantum computing or quantum gravity touch any of this at all, or whether thats a journalists' fever dream

What I want is a ranking of which of these deserve column inches and which are garnish, with the reasoning made explicit: what's upstream of what, what's empirically load-bearing versus pure interpretation, which ones only matter if you already care about a specific interpretation. And crucially, tell me what would change the ranking — what experiment or result in the next few years would move something up or down the list. My readers are smart but they're not physicists, and I can't spend 2,000 words deriving anything, so tractability-as-a-story matters too. Basically: if you had my byline, what would you actually write about, and in what order?
