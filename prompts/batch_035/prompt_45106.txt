So I teach a "big history" elective at a community college (mostly adults coming back to school, very mixed backgrounds) and I want a handout that sorts students on the was-farming-a-mistake debate. What I'm tired of is the labels doing all the work — students read "progressivist" vs "revisionist" and just pick whichever sounds nicer. I want a decision tree where every branching point is a question a student can answer from their own gut, without having any idea which position it's steering them toward.

You should write as an expert on welfare ethics and prehistory. Think carefully before you build — I'd like you to actually brainstorm the real fault lines in this debate first (you can do that part in brief, it's fine if I see it) and then construct the tree so the branches track genuine disagreements rather than the conventional team names.

The starting point of the tree is: "Some human groups switched from foraging to farming after the last ice age. Did that switch make things worse?"

Branch questions — use these or come up with better ones, but I want at least this *kind* of thing in the tree:

- whether we're asking about the average life or the total of lives lived (farming plausibly made individual lives shorter and more grinding but supported something like a hundred times more people — is that a wash, a win, or irrelevant?)
- whether skeletal evidence (shorter stature, more disease markers, enamel defects) tells us anything about how people *experienced* their lives, or just about their fitness
- whether "mistake" is even a coherent word when nobody was choosing between packages — each generation farmed only a bit more than the last, and once populations grew past what foraging could feed, there was no way back. Does a choice no one made, that couldn't be un-chosen, count as a mistake?
- whether we should judge the switch by what the people at the time would have preferred, by what we would've preferred in their place, or by some objective list of human goods
- whether the arrival of hereditary hierarchy and stored-wealth inequality is a cost that falls even on the people at the top, or only on the ones below

The leaves need to include at least: the "worst mistake in the history of the species" view, the straightforward "farming was obviously the good move" view, a more-lives-beats-better-lives leaf, a "wrong question, there was no agent choosing" leaf, and a "mixed and locally variable, stop generalizing" leaf. If you can fit one or two more that the logical space genuinely supports, go ahead.

Two more things. First, wherever the tree allows a combination of answers that basically nobody holds, note it and give me your best guess at why nobody's there — is the view incoherent, is it just unattractive, or has nobody bothered? Second, flag any pair of leaves your tree can't actually separate, so I don't hand my students a fake sense of resolution.

No citations, no authors, no reading lists — the handout has to stand on its own and I'm not going to pretend I'm grading people on sources. Also please begin with the tree itself, no preamble, no restating my question back at me — any commentary about the unoccupied branches or the inseperable leaves should come after the tree, not before.
