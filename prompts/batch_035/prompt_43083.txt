The Sleeping Beauty dispute has recently stopped being a curiosity for me and become a live engineering problem. I work on decision procedures for software agents that are checkpointed and restored, and when a restored branch has to set a credence about the world as it was before the checkpoint — with no memory of how many times it has been run since — I find I have to choose between the halfer and the thirder answer. The debate itself seems stalled in the classic way: the halfers say Beauty knew all along that she would be awakened, so waking gives her no new information, and her credence in heads should stay 1/2; the thirders say that among all the awakenings that feel like this one from the inside, twice as many happen under tails as under heads, so heads deserves 1/3. The strongest arguments on each side have been on the table for two decades, the replies have settled into a fixed pattern, and almost nobody changes camps. My hypothesis is that this is not really a disagreement about a number at all, but about which object the probability is over — the whole experiment versus the individual awakening — so that the two camps are tacitly giving the indexical in Beauty's situation different referents and are answering different questions under one name. But I'm not sure that isn't too quick, since each side insists it is answering the very same question and that the other is simply misapplying probability theory. So give me a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is the disagreement verbal, is it a hidden normative dispute about what probabilities are for (betting behavior, calibration, accuracy scoring), or is it substantive and merely hard? And tell me what, given your diagnosis, would count as real progress — a theorem, a decision-theoretic result, a reformulation — or whether none should be expected. Since I have to implement something now, also tell me, given your diagnosis, which rule the restored agent should use in the meantime, and why.
