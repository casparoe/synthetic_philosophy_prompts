Set up an adversarial collaboration between the two camps in the 1920s Harlem Renaissance fight over what Black art is for. Camp one is the Du Bois line: in his 1926 speech at the NAACP conference in Chicago, published that October in The Crisis as "Criteria of Negro Art," he argued that art is part of the race's great fight, that Black artists who turn away from the struggle are being used against their own people, and that, despite the wailing of the aesthetes, all art is propaganda and always has been. Camp two is the aesthetic autonomists: Alain Locke, whose 1925 anthology The New Negro had argued that the new Black art should be judged as self-expression and beauty rather than as uplift, and the younger writers he championed, like Wallace Thurman and the FIRE!! magazine crowd, who went further and said that being ordered to produce race propaganda was just a new set of chains. Write the joint statement both camps could sign after a good-faith collaboration: a numbered section of claims both accept (about the condition of Black America in the 1920s and about what art can and cannot do for a despised group); a disagreements table with each side's position phrased so that side would accept the wording; then the cruxes — I suspect one is whether beauty in art is a route to truth about a race or just pleasure, and another is who gets to decide what counts as good propaganda — and for each crux a concrete case, work, or argument both would agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction written down in advance. If part of their dispute strikes you as verbal, e.g. over the word propaganda itself, say which part and why. Please answer in British English.
