At my school's philosophy club we're doing a session where each member submits a written position statement on one paradox and the rest of the group critiques it. Mine is on the surprise exam paradox (the teacher announces that there will be an exam next week, on some day, and that on the morning of the exam the students will not know it is that day). Before I hand it over to be picked apart, I'd like an honest audit of whether my positions actually fit together, because I've written them down one at a time over the term and I suspect some of them were adopted under pressure.

Here is the list:

(1) The first step of the students' reasoning is sound: if the exam has not occurred by the end of the fourth day, then an exam on the fifth day could not be a surprise, so the announcement would not be fulfilled.

(2) The backward induction from there is logically valid — I don't think there's a fallacy hiding in the iteration itself.

(3) Nonetheless I think the teacher's announcement can be true, and that when the exam lands on, say, Tuesday the students are genuinely surprised: on Tuesday morning they did not know the exam would be that day.

(4) I think the students know the announcement is true. The teacher is perfectly reliable, the students know she is reliable, and it's common knowledge in the classroom.

(5) I accept that knowledge is closed under known entailment, and I don't want to carve out an exception to closure just to escape this paradox.

(6) I think surprise is a matter of knowledge rather than belief: the exam is a surprise if and only if the students did not know beforehand which day it would be, regardless of what they believed or expected.

(7) In the one-day version of the case — the exam will be tomorrow, and it will be a surprise — I think the announcement simply cannot be true, and there's no clever way for the teacher to give a surprise exam on the only possible day.

(8) I don't believe there are any unknowable truths: every truth is, in principle, knowable by someone at some time.

Please tell me which of these are in genuine tension with each other and which only appear to be, ranking the tensions by severity rather than listing them flatly. For each real tension, say what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair would cost me elsewhere on the list. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, since I suspect I can't keep all eight.
