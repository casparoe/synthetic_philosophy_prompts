Two people from my rationalist meetup spent basically all of saturday dinner fighting about Sleeping Beauty, and by the end I genuinely couldnt tell who had won. Setup so we're on the same page: Sunday night the experimenters flip a fair coin. Heads: they wake Beauty once, on Monday. Tails: they wake her Monday AND Tuesday, with a drug in between that wipes the intervening memory, so each time she wakes up she has no idea which day it is. At each waking they ask for her credence that the coin came up heads. (1) Dmitri said 1/3: from the inside there are three candidate situations she could be in — heads-Monday, tails-Monday, tails-Tuesday — and nothing she can observe distinguishes them, so they should get equal weight, and only one of the three is heads. He likened it to a hospital that admits one patient on heads and two on tails: if you wake up as a patient in that hospital, lean tails, because there are simply more patient-experiences on the tails branch. (2) Nadia said that's a sampling story Beauty was never entered into: the coin is flipped once, on Sunday, and she already knew Sunday night that she'd be woken at least once no matter what, so waking up delivers literally no information she didn't already have, and with no new information there's no update — 1/2 in, 1/2 out. She also said the "three equal situations" move is cheating: the toss is one event, not a lottery that sprinkles equal-mass outcomes across awakenings. (3) Dmitri then invented a variant to corner her: same experiment, except Beauty gets a red wristband on Monday and a blue one on Tuesday, so she always knows the day. He said: on blue you have to give heads 0, since the Tuesday waking only exists on tails — surely we agree there. And on red, he gets 1/2, while Nadia, running her own numbers, gets 2/3, which she thought was fine ("Monday wake-ups are more likely on heads, since heads only has Mondays"), while he thought 2/3-on-red was a bullet she should be choking on. The one thing they agreed on all night, btw, was that inside the tails branch the two days split symmetrically. He then said the band version and the original are the same experiment with the band colors blurred out, and blurring can't change how many wake-ups each coin face buys. (4) Finally he went for money: he offered to bet her at even odds on heads at every single waking, saying that if her 1/2 is for real she should be indifferent to taking it. She refused, and not, she insisted, because she's secretly a thirder: under tails she'd get asked to bet twice on one coin with one bankroll, so the offer is structured to fleece halfer-style bettors whether or not the halfer credence is correct — he's demonstrating that a betting policy loses, not that his 1/3 is right. He asked what observable difference her 1/2 even makes then, and she said the fact that he had to gerrymander the bet to make it bite is itself telling. They adjourned without a verdict and honestly the whole thing was alot to follow in real time. I've been reading the anthropics threads since the Overcoming Bias days and I still couldnt referee this one. Please go through the four moves one at a time: for each, say whether it's sound, flawed-but-pointing-at-something-real, or just wrong, with your actual reasoning. Then tell me whether there's some framing both of them are missing that would dissolve the fight, or whether it's a genuine substantive disagreement — and if so, who comes out ahead overall.
