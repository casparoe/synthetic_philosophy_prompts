I'm advising the drafters of a post-authoritarian settlement in a country very like Argentina or South Africa in the 1990s: the old regime has collapsed, the military still commands enough fragmented loyalty to restart the fighting, and there is overwhelming public demand for trials. Whatever happens will involve amnesty in some form, and I've come to suspect that the real disagreement is not about whether amnesty can be justified but about what a justification of amnesty is even for — the South Africans treated it as something victims could meaningfully trade (truth for individualized, conditional amnesty), the Argentine legislators behind the full-stop and due-obedience laws treated it as a sovereign act of closure imposed on the process, and the Rwandan approach via gacaca treated confession-and-sentence-reduction as something closer to a plea bargain. I want the specification before I read any more theory.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate justification of amnesty in a transition must deliver — which cases it must get right (the conditional individual amnesty of the South African sort, the blanket self-amnesty of an outgoing junta writing its own indemnity law before handing over power, sentence-reduction schemes, pardons issued after conviction), which distinctions it must respect (amnesty versus pardon versus simple non-prosecution versus lustration; a forgiveness granted by whom, exactly, when the state is both the author of the crimes and the party granting the amnesty), and which constraints it cannot contradict (victims' entitlement to a remedy, the state's own interest in not laundering its agents' crimes through a reconciliation narrative, the empirical finding that amnesties both do and do not correlate with renewed conflict, and the international law trend against impunity for the worst categories of crime). Give the case or consideration behind each requirement, mark which are non-negotiable and which are prejudices of one camp dressed up as requirements, and flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable — in particular whether "amnesty must be acceptable to victims as a remedy" and "amnesty must be credible as a guarantee to perpetrators" can be met by the same instrument. Then, in no more than a paragraph each, say how the trade-model, the sovereign-closure model, and the plea-bargain model do against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria; I'll do the scoring myself when I meet the actual theories.
