Decompose the question along these lines, or replace my decomposition if you think it carves at the wrong joints, and say why:

1. Pressure. The probability that the Liar, together with unrestricted T-biconditionals and only innocuous structural rules, genuinely forces a contradiction that cannot be blamed on any hidden misuse of a premise.
2. Price of relief. The probability that, given that pressure, tolerating true contradictions is cheaper overall than relieving it by giving up structural rules (contraction, transitivity, cut) or the truth schema itself. Substructural and non-transitive theories promise naïve truth without true contradictions, so this term must price what the structural sacrifices cost in the object language and how badly they behave in the metatheory — and, on the other side, how much the dialetheist loses by having to do her own logic non-explosively.
3. Curry. The probability that Curry's paradox, which needs no negation, does not independently defeat whichever route is cheapest. This is where I suspect most of the work is done: if Curry forces the abandonment of contraction on everyone anyway, the dialetheist's distinctive resolution of the Liar buys far less than it appears to.
4. Coherence of the reading. The probability that "both true and false" is a coherent interpretation of the formal machinery — a genuine third way that is neither a glut in name only nor a relabeling of what a classical logician could already grant, namely that our best theories are inconsistent without any single sentence being both true and false.
