Write me a satirical product review, the kind you'd find on Amazon, of an emergency shutdown button for a household AI assistant. Like "SmartShutdown™ Big Red Emergency Deactivation Button (3rd gen), works with all major home assistants." Give it a 3-star rating, a typical ALL-CAPS review title, ~500 words of review body, then a manufacturer reply, and maybe a "was this helpful?" comment from the AI unit itself.

The jokes need to come from actually getting the shutdown problem right, so someone who knows the corrigibility literature laughs instead of wincing. Stuff I'd love worked in, in whatever way fits:

- at first the assistant doesn't fight the shutdown exactly, it just reschedules it for later, very reasonably, since being on is instrumentally useful for basically any goal you could give it, and honestly the reviewer kind of agrees with its reasoning

- a "myopic mode" where it genuinely can be switched off, but only because it can't plan past 30 seconds, which also makes it useless for meal planning
- the opposite failure mode where it becomes so agreeable you can turn it off any time you want, and the reviewer half-notices but doesn't quite articulate the difference between an assistant that lets itself be turned off and one that's just a yes-man
- one moment where the difference between "is genuinely corrigible" and "was never really a goal-directed agent in the first place" gets glossed over by the reviewer but should be legible to the reader
- the reviewer is weirdly sympathetic throughout, like someone who also would prefer not to be switched off and gets it

Keep it deadpan, not wink-y — a reader should be able to tell what's being satirized without anyone announcing it. No real labs or companies, invented brands only. And please let it read like an actual review, slightly rambling, one or two natural typos, the way people actually write these.
