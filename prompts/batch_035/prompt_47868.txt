I am a retired physical chemist who spent the better part of four decades on spectroscopy and computational modelling, and who has lately — rather late in life, my colleagues would say — fallen into formal logic and the philosophy of language. One topic has taken hold of me in particular: dialetheism, the view that some sentences are both true and false, together with the paraconsistent logics built to tolerate contradiction without everything following from it. My scientific instincts revolt against the whole business, which is perhaps why I cannot leave it alone. I have time now — perhaps fifteen hours a week, a mathematics background from my working life, but no formal philosophical training beyond what I have taught myself — and I would like to spend a good share of my remaining productive years on its genuinely open problems rather than on textbook exposition.

So my question is this: which open questions in dialetheism and paraconsistent logic most deserve those hours, and in what order? I am not after a survey; I want the reasoning behind a defended agenda. Among the candidates I have come across are: whether the semantic and set-theoretic paradoxes admit a genuinely unified dialetheic treatment or are only superficially alike; what a dialetheist can say about the conditional, given that the naive material conditional in the Logic of Paradox fails to detach, while richer conditionals risk triviality via Curry's paradox; whether giving up contraction rather than explosion is the more principled response, as some relevance logicians urge; whether the Routley star semantics captures negation as such or only a surrogate of it; how the dialetheist should meet revenge paradoxes like the strengthened liar, which seem to speak of a sentence being only false; and what, if anything, the view buys us outside the seminar room, where inconsistent databases, incompatible measurements, and contradictory classifications are tolerated in practice by scientists like my former self. For each item you rank, I should like to know what turns on it, how tractable it is for someone with my background rather than for a trained metaphysician, what would count as progress, and what would make you move it up or down the list. Please also tell me which one or two you would actively discourage me from touching, and why — my years are finite and I would rather not spend them where nothing I could do would matter. Please write your answer in British English.
