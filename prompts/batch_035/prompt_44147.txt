A longtime member of my rationality meetup — call her Dana — has settled opinions on personal survival, and I'd like help predicting her next verdict. Dana and I have followed the same arguments for years (Chalmers's fading qualia, Parfit's teleporter, the usual upload debates on the forums), but she has never written down an actual criterion. Her rulings so far: (1) the classic neuron-by-neuron replacement, one neuron at a time over roughly a decade, with the subject awake and lucid throughout: the subject survives and remains the same person at every stage; (2) destructive scan-and-upload, in which the brain is scanned, destroyed, and the scan run as a simulation: the subject does not survive — something new begins, however similar; (3) non-destructive scan, in which the original keeps her body while a simulation is also run: the one in the flesh is her, and the upload is a new person who merely remembers being her; (4) fifty years of cryonic suspension followed by revival of the original tissue: she survives. Next month's hypothetical, I'm told, is a fission variant: the neuron-by-neuron replacement proceeds as in (1), but at the halfway point the surgeons split the work — the remaining biological half stays in the original skull and is completed in silicon over the following years, while the already-replaced silicon half is transferred to a new body and completed to match the biological half it lost. At the end two people exist, each psychologically continuous with the pre-op patient through half a brain. Predict Dana's verdict: does the patient survive, and if so as one of them, as both, or as neither? Identify the candidate rules that fit her first four rulings (I can see at least a Parfit-style rule requiring an unbroken chain of overlapping psychological stages and a stricter rule requiring the same continuing object, but there may be others), say what each predicts for the fission case, give your best guess with your reasons, and propose the further hypothetical that would best separate any rules that still agree here.
