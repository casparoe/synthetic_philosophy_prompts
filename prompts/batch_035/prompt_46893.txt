Here's where I've landed:

(1) The Twin Earth case convinced me completely: if I have a molecule-for-molecule double on a planet where the lakes and taps are full of XYZ (the watery stuff that isn't H2O), then she and I mean different things by "water," and it follows that she doesn't believe what I believe — when she thinks there's water in the glass, her belief is about XYZ, not about water.

(3) I think in 1750, before anyone knew any chemistry, Earthians' word "water" already referred to H2O — reference is fixed by what's actually in their environment, not by their beliefs about the stuff.

(4) I think "water is H2O" is necessary, can only be known empirically, and isn't analytic — knowing what "water" means doesn't settle it.

(5) Gradual switching: if someone moved to Twin Earth and lived there for years, at some point their "water" tokens come to refer to XYZ, and this happens with zero internal change and nothing they could notice from the inside. I think that's just the standard view.

(6) I can't tell elms from beeches to save my life, but my word "elm" refers to elms and not beeches — the linguistic community carries the reference for me, and I'm fine with that.
