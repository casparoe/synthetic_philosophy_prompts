At a departmental dinner, two colleagues of mine got into an extended argument about Dennett's notion of sphexishness — the inflexible, procedurally rigid behavior exemplified by the digger wasp that, upon finding her prey moved while she inspects her burrow, repeats the entire inspection routine dozens of times rather than adjusting. The exchange had four moves. (1) Anna said the sphex is a mirror, not a contrast: if human behavior is produced by mechanisms, however layered, then the difference between us and the wasp is one of complexity, and the kind of free will Dennett says is worth wanting amounts to a compliment paid to especially elaborate sphexishness — flexibility that is itself mechanically triggered is not the freedom anyone wanted. (2) Marcus replied that this smuggles in an all-or-nothing conception: the wasp cannot notice that her routine is failing, cannot learn, cannot entertain the counterfactual that moving the prey again would trigger another check — and an agent that can notice and revise its own routines has exactly the sort of control that grounds responsibility, whatever the mechanism's ultimate provenance. (3) Anna countered that error-detection is itself just another routine — a second-order sphex program — so the regress either terminates in some unresponsive mechanism, leaving sphexishness at the bottom, or continues forever, which is incoherent; Marcus's ability to notice is a label for a mechanism, not an escape from mechanism. (4) Marcus said that demanding an escape from mechanism is demanding the libertarian free will Dennett explicitly set aside as incoherent: by Anna's standard, even a being that deliberated flawlessly and would have acted differently had its reasons differed would still count as sphexish, which shows the standard proves too much. Neither would budge, and they eventually asked me to referee, though I couldn't. Please adjudicate each of the four moves, saying who is right about what, and identify what, if anything, both of them are missing — in particular, whether the gradualist picture Anna dismisses in her third move actually leaves her position intact, or whether her regress argument succeeds against it.
