I'm a PhD student in philosophy of religion, and my dissertation chapter on the fine-tuning argument has stalled because I can't make my own credences precise. What I want from you is a number with its derivation, not a survey of the literature.

The proposition I want your credence in, call it FT: for at least one fundamental physical parameter — the cosmological constant, the ratio of the strong force to electromagnetism, take your pick — the life-permitting range is vanishingly small compared to the range of values we'd regard as physically possible, and this fact is evidence favoring design over single-universe naturalism, where that's the view that there is exactly one universe and its basic parameters have no further explanation.

The obstacle that keeps me from a stable number is the measure problem. The claim that a range is small presupposes some measure of comparison over the parameter space, but physics supplies no canonical volume element there, and the ratios one computes depend on how one parameterizes the space. My supervisor thinks this is fatal to the whole evidential case; a postdoc in my group thinks it's a technicality, since anthropic conditioning within a multiverse makes the objection idle; and my own suspicion is that the argument requires objective rather than merely subjective likelihoods, which I'm not sure I can make sense of.

So decompose FT into terms you actually endorse. Candidates, not prescriptions: the probability that the smallness claims are even well-defined given the measure problem; the probability that measure-relative smallness nonetheless evidentially favors design over a single unexplained universe; the probability that a multiverse plus observer-selection effects explains the data at least as well, so that fine-tuning tells us nothing about design. You may add, delete, or restructure terms, but assign each one a probability with a single line of justification, show the combination, and tell me which single term your final number is most sensitive to. Also tell me, given that I sit somewhere around 0.3 on FT while my supervisor sits near 0.1 and the postdoc near 0.7, which of those pairwise disagreements most plausibly tracks disagreement over which term. And if you think the entire decomposition strategy is confused — that fine-tuning evidence doesn't aggregate into a credence at all — say that instead, but then say what the disagreement between the three of us actually reduces to.
