My brother and I have been stuck in the same arguement for like two years now and I'm starting to wonder whether it's even a disagreement about physics.

Background so this makes sense: he's a physicist, I did philosophy. The fight is about the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. You know the setup — observers moving relative to each other disagree about which distant events are simultaneous, so if you accept that everything some observer presently registers as real is just real, period, and you chain that through different observers, every event ends up real for me right now, future ones included. Block universe, no objective becoming, the whole deal. There's a famous version of it where whether an invasion fleet in Andromeda has already launched depends on which direction you happen to be walking. My brother thinks this is basically airtight: relativity killed the privileged present, only a block universe respects that, so anyone resisting is just refusing to accept what their own physics says. I landed on the other side after reading Howard Stein, whose response — a short one in the late sixties and a much longer one in the early nineties — was that the argument smuggles in a transitivity assumption about the "real for" relation, and that you can define a coherent relation of causal availability that doesn't transit and doesn't get ruled out by the geometry, leaving room for objective becoming. We've gone maybe six rounds and neither of us has moved an inch.

Here's the thing though. When I actually try to write down what we disagree ABOUT, I struggle. We agree on all the physics — every coordinate transformation, every spacetime diagram. He agrees Stein's relation is well-defined; I agree that if you want an objective present it can't be a global simultaneity slice. So what's even left? My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the fight is really about what a derivation from physics to metaphysics is allowed to look like. He thinks a valid argument with true physical premises settles it; I keep feeling the chaining premise is doing metaphysical work while wearing a physics costume, and neither of us can name a neutral standard the other would accept. Or maybe it's worse than that and we're just using "real for an observer" differently, so the whole thing is verbal. Or maybe it's fully substantive and we're both waiting on something that isn't coming.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Substantive, verbal, two different questions under one name (he's answering "what does the theory imply," I'm answering "what must the world be like for the theory to be true")? Or bedrock intuitions about becoming that neither of us can argue for? Diagnose it using what each of us is actually committed to — not a survey of the literature, we've both read it — and then tell me honestly whether anything could break the deadlock: a conceptual distinction, some theorem about what relativity requires if becoming is objective, or whether two people who agree on every physical fact should just expect to stay stuck, and why. For what it's worth neither of us knows general relativity well enough to say whether it changes the picture, which might be its own separate problem, feel free to comment on that too.
