I write novels for a living and I've slowly realized my craft instincts have backed me into a set of claims about what emotions are, and I can't tell if the set actually hangs together. Here it is. (1) Emotions are about things: my terror is about the unleashed rottweiler, not just a free-floating blob of awfulness, and it can be about the dog wrongly — the rottweiler turns out to be a sweetheart, and my fear was just mistaken, the way a misreading is mistaken. (2) The best overall picture of them is perceptual: fear presents the dog as dangerous the way vision presents the couch as brown — it comes at me involuntarily, I don't infer my way into it, and when I'm writing deep POV the character's fear tints everything they notice, which is basically how free indirect discourse works, so I'm inclined to trust that intuition. (3) But emotions are also responsive to judgment in a way perception isn't: once I see the "snake" is a garden hose, the fear drains out over the next few seconds, and you can't do that with the Müller-Lyer lines no matter how many times you measure them. (4) The bodily felt part is constitutive rather than decoration — a character who registers danger with total bodily calm isn't frightened, he's just informed, and if I write him as "afraid" I'm cheating. And (5) whether an emotion fits its object is independent of whether the subject thinks it does — characters get their own fear wrong all the time, that's half of dramatic irony, and there seems to be a fact of the matter even when nobody in the book (or the room) has it right.

I genuinely can't tell which of these are load-bearing and which are vibes. So: audit the set for me. Which pairs are in real tension, which only look like they conflict but dissolve once you look closely, and what's the cheapest repair in each case? I want the tensions ranked by severity rather than a flat list where everything is equally bad, and for the worst one I want you to be concrete about which commitment has to bend and what I'd lose by bending it. I have no attachment to any particular item — I just want to know where the floor gives out. Please end with a final line in the form \answer{N}, where N is the number of the single commitment from (1)–(5) that I should give up or radically revise first.
