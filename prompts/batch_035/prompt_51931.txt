Background: I'm a retired condensed matter physicist, spent 35 years doing low-temperature experiments, and since retiring I've gotten deep into philosophy of religion and metaphysics, mostly via a reading group of other retired academics. Currently wading into the "why is there something rather than nothing" literature and enjoying it a lot, though some of the moves people make leave me cold.

The one that keeps coming up, in the reading group and in the pop stuff too, is the old slogan "nothing comes from nothing" (ex nihilo nihil fit, as our resident classicist likes to remind us). One member, a retired Anglican priest, uses it to argue that since the universe exists, something outside it must have produced it, i.e. God. On the other side, I've watched documentaries where physicists basically say modern physics has refuted the slogan: quantum fluctuations, vacuum energy, particle pairs popping out of the vacuum, therefore something CAN come from nothing, therefore no creator needed. Both sides seem to treat the sentence as if it obviously means one specific thing, and as a scientist my instinct is that it's doing very different work depending on who says it. To me, "nothing" in the physics usage is obviously a something — a quantum vacuum is a state of a field obeying equations, with structure and properties, and calling it "nothing" strikes me as rhetorical sleight of hand. But maybe the priest's use has symmetrical problems I'm not seeing, since "nothing comes from nothing" applied to God seems to stop applying the moment God shows up.

What I'd like from you: take the slogan apart. What are the distinct precise claims "nothing comes from nothing" could be expressing — a conservation principle, a causal principle, a Parmenidean thesis about the impossibility of a state of nothing at all, a principle about explanation, whatever else you can think of? For each reading, tell me whether it's a platitude, a substantive and maybe defensible metaphysical claim, or just false, and — this is the part I actually care about — whether THAT reading does what the priest needs it to do, or what the physicist needs it to do. Does any reading of the slogan, if true, actually get you to a creator, or does refuting it (if refutable) actually get you to a godless universe? I want to bring a sorted list to the next meeting so we can stop talking past eachother. I'm not looking for you to adjudicate whether God exists, just to map what work this one sentence can and can't do.
