So I'm giving a talk at an AI safety workshop next month defending a pretty narrow take: that "the alignment problem" should be defined as intent alignment — getting a system to reliably do what the people directing it actually intend, rather than misreading instructions, satisfying the letter while wrecking the point, or drifting off whatever the operators meant — and that "value alignment" as a target is, at least right now, underspecified enough to be a bad research goal. My line is basically: you can't align a system to values until you say whose values, aggregated how, and once you've answered that you've reinvented goverance, which is a real problem but a different problem than the engineering one.

The audience: mostly interpretability and evals researchers who think intent alignment is just "the model follows the prompt" writ large, and that the narrow definition quietly assumes the people giving instructions are benign; one moral philosopher who works on moral uncertainty and will say the narrow definition lets lab leadership off the hook entirely; and a couple of policy/governance people who think both framings are parochial anyway. nobody from pure capabilities, if that changes anything.
