So I went back to Dennett's old Where Am I? piece -- the one where the government needs his brain out of the way of radiation, so its parked in a vat in Houston while he wanders around getting remote signals through a radio link -- and instead of actually thinking about the question he asks, I started making up variants and noticing my gut reactions to each. Now I want to know what principle, if any, my reactions add up to, because I'm pretty sure they dont.

The cases, with my verdicts:

1. The original setup: brain in the vat, my body across the country, seeing through the cameras. Asked where I am, I want to say I'm where the brain is. But if you ask me where I'm *experiencing things from*, I want to say out west, with the body. I think these might be two different questions, but I cant quite say what the second one is about if not the brain.

2. Whole brain transplanted into a fresh body, old body destroyed: I go with the brain. Feels like the cleanest case in the whole list.

3. Only my cerebellum transplanted into someone else, the rest of my brain (memories, personality, everything) staying in my skull: I stay put, obviously. And this is where 'follow the brain' from case 2 starts looking bad, because apparently I only ever meant *part* of the brain.

4. One hemisphere transplanted into a donor body, the other hemisphere destroyed during surgery: the transplant recipient is me. Half a psychology in a new body somehow counts as enough.

5. Both hemispheres transplanted into two different donor bodies, both wake up, both have chunks of my memories and both think theyre me: and here I refuse to say both are me, refuse to say neither is me, and refuse to pick one, like the question is broken somehow. This one keeps me up at night.

6. Surgeons replace my neurons one at a time, over months, with silicon chips that mimic each neuron exactly. I'm awake and chatting through the whole thing: still me at every step, no question.

7. My brain is physically untouched and running fine, but a procedure wipes my memories and overwrites my personality with someone else's, completely: that's the end of me, even though every neuron is exactly where it always was. So apparently I don't really follow the brain after all, I follow something the brain is *carrying*.

8. Perfect scan of my brain, the original painlessly destroyed in the process, an exact copy wakes up elsewhere: I keep flip-flopping, but my current lean is that I died. Which is weird, because the copy has everything the hemisphere in case 4 had, and more.

So is there one principle that gets all of these? My half-baked candidate is something like: I go wherever the thing that carries my memory and character goes, *as long as* it never gets copied, only moved. But that last clause feels bolted on to save case 8, and I'm not sure it doesnt just restate my verdict instead of explaining it. It also leaves case 5 a mess: branching is exactly what that stuff seems able to do.

Please do the whole reflective-equilibrium thing on me. State the principle (or a couple of candidates) precisely, tell me which verdict each clause is there to capture, and then run it on some cases I havent listed, especially: (a) a hemisphere split where one body gets my autobiographical memories but none of my skills or temperament, and the other body gets the skills and temperament but thinks it woke up this morning with amnesia -- which one am I, or neither, or what; (b) my brain emulated and running at 1/100 speed on hardware spread across a datacenter, with no single place you could point to; (c) the two people from case 5 meeting a year later and having become pretty different from each other and from me.

And be honest about which of my verdicts has to go. My guess is its case 5 or case 8, probably 8, but I want you to actually pick rather than hedge. Also, separately, tell me whether the where-am-I bit in case 1 is a real metaphysical question or just me sliding between bodily location and something like point of view -- Dennett himself said he felt located out with the body, and I half think he was just changing the subject, but I half think the subject-changing is the interesting part.
