I write about science and ideas for a living — the kind of journalist who's done pieces on the organ shortage and the yearly outrage cycle about hotels charging $400 a night during hurricane evacuations — and somewhere along the way I've collected a set of opinions about what should and shouldn't be for sale. I want someone to audit whether they actually fit together, because I suspect they don't, and I honestly can't tell where the crack is.

Here's the package:

1. Paid kidney donation should be legal. People die waiting on transplant lists, dialysis is miserble and expensive for everyone including the state, and telling a poor person they can't sell a kidney for their own good has always struck me as the state protecting its conscience at their expense. I believe the Iranians run a paid system and it basically cleared their waiting list, though don't hold me to the details.

2. Disaster price gouging should be illegal, and I'm not soft on this. The $400-a-night hotel during an evacuation, the $99 case of water during the Texas freeze — that's straightforwardly wrong and should be prosecuted, not defended with a smug econ-101 lecture about price signals rationing supply.

3. Votes shouldn't be sellable. Even if a voter genuinely, freely wants to sell their vote and isn't coerced in any way, that market stays closed. Something about it corrodes the whole point of political equality.

4. My overall instict is anti-anti-commodification. Sandel-style arguments about money degrading the meaning of things mostly read to me as upper-class moralizing — there's nothing sacred being protected, it's just discomfort at watching poor people make choices the comfortable would never have to make.

5. My one real worry about markets like these is desperation. The problem isn't money touching sacred things, it's that poverty puts a thumb on the scale. So the fix is the background — a real safety net — not banning the sale.

6. And then there's surrogacy, where I'm much queasier than I am about kidneys and can't fully say why, which bugs me.

Some of these have to be fighting each other. My grounds for being furious at gougers look a lot like my grounds for legalizing kidney sales, just pointed the other direction, and I can't tell whether my vote-selling line survives my dismissal of commodification arguments or whether (6) is a genuinely held position or just a mood. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are in actual tension and which only look that way, rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and roughly what the fix costs. Then commit: which single revision cleans up the most? Don't hand me a menu of options and let me pick — tell me what you'd actually give up first and why, or say plainly that you think the whole package is defensible as is and why the tensions dissolve.
