Analyze the Han van Meegeren Vermeer forgeries (1936–1945) as a test case for two rival theories of what makes a forgery bad: Nelson Goodman's autographic/allographic distinction from Languages of Art, and Denis Dutton's account of artworks as performances whose value depends on the achievement they record. For convenience, the salient facts, which I take as given and which you should not go beyond: van Meegeren, a competent but critically dismissed Dutch painter, produced a series of fake Vermeers in the late 1930s and during the occupation, of which The Supper at Emmaus was authenticated in 1937 by the leading Vermeer authority Abraham Bredius, acquired by a Dutch museum, and celebrated as perhaps Vermeer's finest work; a further fake, Christ and the Adulteress, was sold to Hermann Göring for a very large sum; after the war van Meegeren was arrested as a collaborator, confessed to forgery, and demonstrated his claim by painting a fresh Vermeer before witnesses; he was convicted in 1947. Upon exposure, the earlier critical praise for Emmaus evaporated and the painting was redescribed as kitsch.

The mapping I want should be disciplined and run in both directions. First, theory to case: what exactly does each theory say was lost or corrupted when the paintings were revealed — for Goodman, say precisely what an autographic work is and why, on his account, undetectable forgery remains in principle relevant to painting but not to, say, a musical score; for Dutton, what the object is taken to record and why the revelation of a different performance defeats the earlier appreciation. Say where each theory is silent, for instance on the Göring sale, on van Meegeren's motive, or on the fact that the praise fell without any physical change in the canvas — and resist forcing either theory to speak there. Second, case to theory: does the Emmaus episode confirm the theory it is usually marshaled for, or expose a gap? In particular, address whether the collapse of critical opinion on revelation is evidence that we value paintings causally and historically rather than formally, or rather evidence of conformism among critics who were fooled; the two explanations make different predictions about how someone who never learns of the forgery should respond, and I want you to say which theory handles that case better and which is embarrassed by it. Conclude with a verdict: which theory survives the episode in better shape, and one respect in which both are tested beyond what their authors anticipated. Do not bring in further named theories, episodes, or authorities beyond the two given and the facts stated above; if you need an auxiliary distinction, construct it yourself. Keep the whole thing under 800 words.
