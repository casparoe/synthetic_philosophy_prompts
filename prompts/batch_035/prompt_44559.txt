ok background first: longtime rationalist-adjacent internet person here, ~15 years of lesswrong, the forum itself, the tumblr diaspora, discord servers, the whole ecosystem. over those years the proverb 'your freedom to swing your arms ends where my nose begins' has done an enormous amount of load-bearing work in arguments i've been in, and last month it finally broke for me and i want help taking it apart.

the breaking point: a thread about platform moderation of health misinformation. one side said deplatforming anti-vax content is just the proverb in action — the misinformation is the swing, public health is the nose, so removing it doesn't violate anyone's freedom, QED. the other side said no, the proverb is exactly why you DON'T deplatform: speech isn't a fist, there's no nose, and if second-order harms routed through belief count as swings then literally everything anyone says becomes a swing and nobody's nose ever stops begining. both sides quoted the same proverb at each other. both thought it was obvious. neither engaged with the other at all, it was painfull.

my problem is i can hear several different things packed into that one sentence and i can't tell which one anybody means, including me. is 'nose' physical harm only, or any setback to interests (economic, epistemic, psychological)? is the proverb a normative claim about when coercion is justified, or just a description of where legal systems tend to draw lines, or a stipulative definition of 'harm' as opposed to 'offense'? does 'ends where my nose begins' mean the nose-bearer gets to stop the swing unilaterally, or only that they now have a legitimate grievance — that's a big difference for who gets to decide. and is it about individually attributable harms only, or does it stretch to statistical noses, population-level harms that no single person wears on their own face? i've seen the proverb attributed to half a dozen different people over the years but i don't care about the archaeology, treat it as folk wisdom that floated loose from whoever said it first, that's how everyone deploys it anyway.

what i want, in this order:

1. 'The readings' — a menu of the distinct precise claims the proverb could be making, each in 2-3 sentences, roughly ordered from weakest-platitude to strongest-most-contentious
2. 'Verdicts' — for each reading, whether it's trivial, substantive-but-defensible, or flatly false, and — the part i actually care about — whether it supports the deplatform side, the leave-it-up side, both, or neither
3. 'What happened on the thread' — one paragraph diagnosing which reading each side was secretly invoking, and why they could both swear the proverb was on their side
4. 'The moral' — 3-4 sentences on whether the proverb earns its keep as an argument-ender or whether it should be retired to being a decoration people say at each other

keep it aimed at the proverb itself, no mill scholarship, no history of first amendment law. i want to be able to bring the menu back to the thread and go 'which of these six did you mean, pick one'.
