Been reading rationalist blogs and philosophy of mind threads since ~2014 and I've ended up with a package of views that maybe doesn't hang together, so audit it for me. Here's roughly where I stand:

1. Physics is causally closed, no exceptions — no non-physical event ever shoves a neuron around.
2. The explanatory gap is real. I'm not an illusionist, that stuff always struck me as changing the subject — qualia exist and they're not reducible to physical facts. Mary learns something new when she walks out of that room.
3. Given 1 and 2, I guess that makes me an epiphenomenalist: physical stuff causes qualia, qualia cause nothing back.
4. I still call myself some kind of materialist — no souls, everything starts with physics. Not actually sure that label survives 2, but it's the one I reach for.
5. My belief that I'm conscious right now is the most justified belief I have. More certain then anything in physics, honestly.
6. When I say "this is what red looks like", I'm reporting the actual qualia, not confabulating — introspection is basically reliable about phenomenology.

Does this hang together? Go through the pairs: which are genuinely in tension, which only look like it at first, and rank them by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. For each real tension tell me what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs me elsewhere in the list. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I honestly don't know which one I'd give up, so don't assume — I want the audit, not a lecture on why dualism is dumb.
