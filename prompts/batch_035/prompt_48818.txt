My friend and I spent like four hours last night arguing about whether there's some place in the mind where it all comes together -- the Cartesian theater thing, she's a big Dennett fan -- and I went to bed with a pile of gut verdicts that I suspect don't fit together. So here they are, and I want to know if there's a principle that captures them or which ones have to go.

In color phi -- red dot flashes on the left, green dot flashes on the right, quickly, and people report one dot moving across and changing color somewhere in the middle -- my gut says the person really did experience that intermediate color, even though nothing ever flashed there. But in the cutaneous rabbit -- wrist tapped twice, then elbow, and you feel phantom taps marching up your forearm in between -- my gut says the middle taps were never actually felt, the brain just backfills a story after the elbow tap arrives. I cannot for the life of me see why those two should come out different, but they do, for me.

Metacontrast masking, where a flash is followed so fast by a surrounding shape that you never see the flash: fine, it just never became conscious, no mystery. But Libet's readiness-potential stuff, where the brain activity precedes the felt decision by a chunk of time: there my gut says the decision-experience was there when it seemed to be, and anyone telling me the "real" moment was earlier feels like they're changing the subject.

The fridge one is where I keep losing my footing. You're reading, the fridge hums, it shuts off, and the sudden silence makes you realize you must have been hearing it the whole time. I go back and forth on whether I was conscious of the hum before it stopped. My friend says the question is broken, like asking whether a manuscript was final before the editor signed off on it.

My own attempt at a principle was something like: conscious = whatever I could have reported at the time if someone had asked. But that seems to make the phantom taps conscious too, which I really don't want, and it maybe doesn't even help with the fridge. Her line is basically Dennett's multiple drafts -- that there's no fact of the matter about when content "becomes" conscious, just a winning draft that gets established somewhere in the process of discrimination and report. So: is there a principle that gets all my verdicts, or close? If her drafts thing is the answer, tell me honestly which of my verdicts it overturns, and why the color phi one (which I feel strongest about) is the one that has to go rather than the rabbit one. And I'd like to hear what the winning principle says about a case we never got to: someone at a loud party half-hears their own name at the edge of the music, and only a beat later it lands and they turn around -- did they consciously hear their name before it landed, or no?
