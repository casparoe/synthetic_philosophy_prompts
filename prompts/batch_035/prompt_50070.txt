Construct a thought experiment for me in the domain of costly signaling and the handicap principle, built to the following specifications. The scenario must satisfy four constraints at once:

1. Honest signaling is maintained at equilibrium without any wasteful expenditure: every cost the signal imposes on the sender is received by someone as a benefit (a transfer, not a burn). No resources are destroyed merely to prove a point.
2. The signal's reliability must not rest on a physical constraint or index: it cannot be that only a genuinely strong, fast, or healthy sender is physically capable of producing the signal in the first place. Any sender, high-quality or low-quality, must be able to send it.
3. The scenario must be realistic and human-scale: think deposits, warranties, posted bonds, escrow arrangements, contractual penalties — not animal displays, and not exotic institutional machinery.
4. It must not rely on repeated interaction or reputation: the signal must do its work in a one-shot exchange between strangers.

After the scenario itself, which should be a single paragraph, provide the walkthrough:

(a) Explain what the verbal form of the handicap principle — the idea that signals are honest only because they are costly to send — seems to predict about your case, and why that prediction fails here.

(b) Show what a formal equilibrium treatment of signaling says instead: where the honesty comes from, and why the cost that matters is differential and contingent — the expected cost of the signal differs by sender type, while at the honest equilibrium little or nothing is actually paid.

(c) Identify the single structural feature of the case that is doing the work.

(d) Say what happens to the case if that feature is removed.

(e) Give the most natural Zahavian rejoinder — the claim that the case does involve real costs somewhere, merely hidden in pricing or risk — and assess whether that rejoinder saves the principle in its original form or quietly changes its subject from costly to differentially costly in expectation.

The purpose of the case is to test whether the honesty of a signal requires waste, or only that cheating be expensive for the sort of agent who would cheat. This fixes what counts as cheating in the construction: do not stipulate the verdicts; the case must generate them from the structure you build. If you find the four constraints cannot be jointly satisfied, say which one you had to relax and why, rather than smuggling in a stipulation — and in that event, be explicit about whether the relaxed constraint was the wastefulness requirement, the no-index requirement, or the one-shot requirement, since these fail for importantly different reasons.
