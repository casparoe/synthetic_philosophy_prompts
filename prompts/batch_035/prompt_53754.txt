got into a huge argument with my friend Maya last night, like three hours past when everyone else left the dinner party, about karma. she's not a full-on believer but she uses it constantly, and the version she was defending goes like this: karma is basically a moral bank account. every good deed is a deposit, every bad deed is a withdrawal, the universe is the bank, your rebirth is the account rolling over into a new one (with the balance carried forward, she was very clear on that), and suffering in this life is just the bank calling in debts you ran up before. she was using the analogy for two conclusions: (1) that being moral is really just long-range prudence, since the deposits pay you back eventually, with interest, and (2) that human justice systems are kind of redundant, or at least presumptuous, because the ledger settles itself eventually anyway, so who are we to add punishments on top.

I pushed back but mostly vibes tbh. the one thing I noticed is that every time I pressed her on a hard part — like who exactly is the account holder across lives, or what sets the exchange rate between one specific deed and one specific bit of suffering — she'd just re-apply the analogy with more confidence, as if the bank framing answered the question instead of illustrating it. which makes me think the analogy is doing way more work than anyone admits.

so here's what I want: a real audit of the bank account / ledger picture of karma. give me the mapping laid out explicitly — what's a deposit, what's a withdrawal, what's the interest rate, what's the bank teller, what's foreclosure, what's opening a new account — and then go through where the mapping breaks. then tell me straight: once the disanalogies are on the table, do her two conclusions survive, or was the analogy carrying them the whole time? and if the bank account is a bad picture but karma-as-desert-theory isn't dead, is there a better analogy for what it's actually claiming — law of motion, ledger without a keeper, something else? if you know the actual traditions (she'd be annoyed if I let the pop version stand in for them, and honestly so would I), flag where the pop "universe keeps score" version drifts from the more serious versions, but don't turn it into a bibliography, I don't need names and citations, just the positions.
