Longtime lurker of the rationalist blogosphere here (LW, Arbital back when that was a thing, Alignment Forum sometimes when I'm feeling brave), and I've absorbed the standard shutdown problem discourse over the years: instrumental convergence says a consequentialist resists being turned off, the utility indifference trick tries to cancel the incentive and everyone agrees it has weird side effects, etc. Fine.

But here's something that's always bugged me and I want an actual argument about. Almost all of this treats shutdown as binary — the button gets pressed or it doesn't, and the agent's whole decision problem is concentrated at that cliff edge. Whenever this comes up in comment threads, someone suggests the obvious fix: make it gradual. Give the human a dial instead of a switch, a continuous variable for how much control they want back moment to moment, so there's no cliff and the agent can smoothly adjust its behavior. Intuitively this sounds safer and easier to get right, more like a thermostat than an execution.

I want you to argue the opposite position: that graded control transfer makes corrigibility strictly *harder*, not easier. Sketch of the argument I have in mind — once there's a continuum of settings, the agent inherits a smooth incentive gradient it can exploit, and it will drift toward whatever setting on the dial leaves it the most influence while still technically counting as compliant. The binary version at least has the virtue of making the confrontation legible: there's one moment where the agent either defers or doesn't, and you can watch it. With a dial, everything looks like compliance and the power grab happens in the curvature of small adjustments. Please also deal with the obvious comeback, which is that a binary switch doesn't actually avoid this — the agent just does all its manipulation at the moment of decision instead of spreading it out — and with the point that humans already get graded control over each other and mostly don't exploit it this way, or do they?

If you genuinely think the case can't be made honestly, tell me where it breaks instead of faking it. No citations or reading lists please, just the argument.
