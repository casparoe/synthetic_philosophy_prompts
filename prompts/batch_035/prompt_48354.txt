so a few weeks ago i got in a car crash — someone ran a red light — and i had the cliché slow-motion moment, glass sort of crawling through the air. ever since, i keep trying to work out whether i actually *experienced* it in slow motion at the time, or whether i experienced it at normal speed and my memory rewrote it within seconds. i've been reading around this stuff and ive landed on the following set of views, and now i want an honest audit of whether they hang together:

(1) i'm a physicalist — experiences are just brain events.
(2) for any content there is always a determinate fact about exactly *when* it first became conscious. if you could have stopped the tape at any instant and asked "has he experienced X yet?", there was a true yes or no answer.
(3) in cases like this, and like the color phi setup where a dot seems to move and change color, the stalinesque story is right: consciousness holds off until the whole picture is in, and the imputed content — the smooth motion, the slow-mo — is what i actually experienced. nothing was experienced first and then secretly overwritten in memory.
(4) i reject the cartesian theater. there is no single place in the brain where it all comes together for an audience.
(5) i dont accept that whatever i seem to remember is what i experienced. memory is a readout that can be wrong, not the definition of what happened.
(6) the events of my experience happen in the same order, and at more or less the same times, as the neural events that constitute them — and that mirroring is part of what separates experiencing something from the unconscious processing churning away underneath.

something in here probably doesnt fit but i genuinely cant tell which, and i suspect a couple of these only look like they clash. rank the tensions by severity, tell me which are real and which dissolve on inspection, and for each real one say what would have to give. dont soften it — if my whole framing of the crash question rests on a mistake, i'd rather know.
