I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background — my exposure amounts to long-form blog posts and one abandoned MOOC — and I'd like an audit of my views on charitable giving before I write them up for a lunchtime discussion group at work. Here is the set:

1. I'm a consequentialist and a maximiser: the right act is the one with the highest expected value, and I believe you should act on expected value even when the probability is minuscule, so long as the payoff is large enough that the product dominates. If the arithmetic says so, you do it.
2. In my own financial life I'm risk-averse: I wouldn't stake a year's salary on a fair coin flip for double or nothing, and I don't think this undermines anything in point 1.
3. I give 10% of my salary to GiveWell's Top Charities Fund, on the grounds that its recommendations carry the most robust cost-effectiveness evidence available.
4. I find the long-termist argument persuasive: if humanity survives, the far future could contain an astronomically large number of lives, so reducing existential risk should dominate bednets on any plausible accounting. I have never redirected my giving accordingly.
5. I would not pay in Pascal's mugging. If a stranger tells me they will torture an unimaginably vast number of people unless I hand over my wallet, I keep the wallet, however impressive the arithmetic looks.
6. I'm suspicious of expected-value estimates for speculative causes, such as AI safety research: the error bars swamp the point estimates, and when estimates are that soft, one should prefer options that perform well across a range of reasonable assumptions rather than whatever tops the expected-value calculation.

Please tell me which pairs of these are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and for each genuine tension what would have to give and what it would cost me to give it up. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and identify the single revision that would clean up the most. I genuinely don't know where the problem lies, and I'm not angling for a particular verdict — I'd simply like to know whether this package can be held at once and, if not, which part of it is load-bearing in the wrong way.
