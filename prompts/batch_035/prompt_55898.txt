longtime reader of the rationalist blogs and forums here (lurking since way back), and something's been bugging me that I can't untangle on my own, so I'm throwing it to you.

In open-world games, killing adult human NPCs is basically the texture of the medium. Pedestrians get ragdolled for laughs, enemies respawn, kill counts are literally the score. Nobody inside the game or outside of it treats any of that as a moral event. But the exact same industry, often in the exact same games, fences off certain lives: child NPCs are unkillable or just don't exist (theres no kids in GTA at all), and where there's a beloved animal companion it tends to be flagged invincible while human strangers are fair game. Studios that let you mow down a crowd apparently consider some depicted lives off-limits.

So what's the implicit theory of moral status in that pattern? The obvious candidate — some NPCs matter, others don't — can't be it, because the morally relevant property can't live in the NPC: a child NPC is exactly as sentient (i.e., not) as an adult one, and a pet NPC less than either. So either the design presupposes that the wrongness is in the player, that harming certain fictional beings corrupts your dispositions, while harming others doesn't, which would need some account of why the line sits where it sits; or it presupposes nothing and is just tracking audience squeamishness, in which case the practice is still quietly claiming that our reactions to depicted lives are a fit thing to design around, which is itself a commitment.

Help me reconstruct what the pattern actualy presupposes, which rival views it rules out (would a strict no-fictional-being-matters view have to condemn the mod scenes that remove the fences?), whether the parts are consistent with each other or just fit the same mood, where it's underdetermined enough that several theories would license the same design, and what the games would look like if we swapped in a different implicit view entirely. Genuinely curious whether there's a coherent theory under there or if it's vibes all the way down.
