So our decision theory reading group has a blog that I basically run (mostly grad students, a couple of incurable Bayesians, plus some policy people who wander in from the MPA program), and I want to build a "which theory of risky choice do you actually hold?" quiz around the Allais mess. The goal is to sort readers by how they answer concrete cases, not by which label they like — so no question can just ask "do you accept the independence axiom?", every question has to be a choice between actual gambles with actual numbers and prizes, answerable by someone who has never heard of any of this jargon.

The cases I want in there, roughly: the original Allais pair with the millions (the standard 1A/1B vs 2A/2B setup everyone uses), a common-consequence variant where the shared 89% chunk gets swapped from a big prize to nothing, and at least one pair constructed so that a pure rank-dependent probability weighter and a regret theorist have to give different answers — that's the split I actually care about and it's the part I'm least sure how to engineer, so tell me if it can't be done cleanly and flag it instead of papering over it. I also want the Diamond-style fairness case in there somewhere: two people, one indivisible prize, coin flip versus deterministic assignment, and the reader has to say whether the flip is morally better or just equivalent. Plus a couple of second-order questions: after someone gives the Allais pattern, ask whether they'd want to be debiased or whether they'd stand by it as a rational preference — that descriptive/normative split should do a lot of the sorting work.

For the scoring key, the leaves should be something like: orthodox expected utility (your Allais choices are just mistakes), Allais' own position — cardinal utility defined over the whole prospect, where the preference for security near certainty is rational and which is NOT the same thing as prospect theory — regret theory (transitive, anticipated regret/rejoicing), rank-dependent weighting treated as a bias to correct for, and the same weighting endorsed normatively. Please flag where the quiz genuinely can't separate two leaves — I suspect Allais' composite-outcome view and regret theory will overlap a lot on my cases — and note any combination of answers that maps onto no leaf, with a sentence on whether nobody holds it because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Aim for 8-10 questions with two or three options each. Keep Ellsberg-style ambiguity stuff out of it, I don't want the quiz accidentally sorting on ambiguity attitudes instead of the independence stuff — though if you think one ambiguity question is needed as a control, say so and mark it clearly.
