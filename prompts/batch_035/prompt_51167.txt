Writing a novel here and I've hit a snag you might be able to untangle, since it's more a conceptual thing than a craft thing.

My narrator, a retired cognitive scientist who's a bit of a windbag, keeps sorting the people around her using this scheme she half-remembers from Dennett: Darwinian creatures at the bottom, who just get selected by the world and can't learn at all; then Skinnerian ones, who learn by trial and error in real time; then Popperian creatures, who can rehearse an action in their heads before doing it, so the bad trials happen in imagination instead of on their skin; and I think there's a fourth level on top, the Gregorian ones, who borrow other people's models of the world. (I may be garbling that last one, she's an unreliable narrator on purpose, which is partly why I'm asking you instead of her.)

Here's my problem. I want my protagonist, a forger, to be a full-blooded Popperian creature: a guy who runs the whole con in his head before he ever approaches the mark, revising and revising until the imagined version goes clean. But if all his testing happens in imagination — if the Popperian trick is precisely that the environment never gets to punish you — then by the time he touches reality he's done learning, the world can't surprise him anymore, and I've written myself out of act two. My narrator's line of thinking seems to imply that Popperian creatures don't learn from the environment at all, which would also make the scheme weird, since on her telling everyone above Skinner is basically sealed off from experience.

So, two things I need you to settle, and please don't hand me back a menu of options — make the call and defend it, I've got a deadline. First: is that actually how the hierarchy works, that a Popperian creature stops learning from the world, or does feedback sneak back in somehow (and if so, where — that's where my plot gets its second act)? Second: am I right to treat the four kinds like seperate species, where a character is either Skinnerian or Popperian but not both, or did Dennett mean it more like layers a single mind can have running at once? If it's layers, tell me straight whether my forger can plausibly be both the hyper-planner and the guy the world keeps slapping anyway — I've built a whole sequence around him getting it wrong, so if that's incoherent under the real scheme, say so and tell me which way to lean instead.
