Is there an established name for the following configuration? I am a semi-retired logistics analyst who plays a great deal of bridge and, lately, online poker. Over the years I have kept noticing a kind of situation, and I have never found a word for it. You are up against an opponent who predicts you specifically, and quite reliably — think of the older cousin who always seems to know which room you will hide in, or the regular opponent at the card table who has your tendencies down cold. The feature I am trying to name is not simply that he is better than you. It is that any plan you settle on is undermined by the very fact that you have settled on it. If I reason, "he will expect me to bluff, so I shall play it straight," I can hear him saying, "that is precisely what someone who thinks I expect a bluff would do." Whatever I conclude, I can picture him one level up, and the reasoning never comes to rest on anything. It is not an infinite ladder of "I think that he thinks that I think" for its own sake, either — with an ordinary opponent I can eventually pick a level and stop. The trouble here is that my own act seems to carry information about what he foresaw, so the option that looked best becomes the one I have the strongest grounds to believe he anticipated.

I know this is somewhere near Newcomb's problem and the one-boxing debate, and I would be glad to be told how exactly the two are related. But it seems different in at least one respect: in Newcomb there is a clean clash between two principles, yet each side of that clash still issues a definite recommendation. In my situation, the recommendation itself seems to flip the moment you try to follow it, as though there were no stable answer at all — or as though the advice is always "be the sort of person who ends up elsewhere," which is no advice to the person deliberating. And there is a second respect in which my situation may be its own thing: at the card table, the opponent is not a perfect predictor, just very much better than chance. If the pure, idealised version of this configuration has a name, is there also a name or a literature for the bounded version, where the predictor is merely good and the question is what it is rational to do given that your act is partially self-revealing?

To be clear, a bare label will not satisfy me. What I want is the carving: what exactly the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where its edges lie — how it differs from an ordinary prediction race, from iterated reasoning about an opponent's reasoning, and from whatever the nearest named concept turns out to be. If nothing established captures it, please say so plainly, and then define and delimit the phenomenon yourself, coin a term if one is needed, and explain how your definition handles the boundary cases I would want it to handle — the cousin who merely guesses well, the opponent who is good but not that good, and the imagined perfect predictor who anticipated your every dodge, including the dodges you considered and rejected.
