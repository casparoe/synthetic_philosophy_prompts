I've been teaching the Malcolm–Dennett exchange on whether dreams are experiences — Malcolm's claim in *Dreaming* that dream reports can't be reports of experiences had while asleep, because there's no way for anything to count as showing the dreamer dreamt what he says he dreamt, and Dennett's reply that the dream might be composed only at the moment of waking, spliced together from material in the brain's memory stores, with the whole sense of a temporally extended dreamscape a kind of illusion. I want to work out where I actually stand by going through cases and seeing what principle, if any, explains my verdicts. Here they are.

1. The ordinary case: I wake and report a long, vivid dream of being late for an exam in a building that keeps rearranging itself. Verdict: this was an experience, occurring while I slept.

2. My daughter has a night terror: she screams, sits up, eyes open, then falls back asleep and remembers nothing in the morning. Verdict: harder — something was happening to her, but I'm hesitant to say she *experienced* anything, precisely because of the missing report.

3. A sleep-talker answers, coherently, a question someone asks aloud in the room, and the next morning reports having dreamed of a conversation in which that question came up. Verdict: this is the strongest case I know of for dreams as real-time experience — the dream seems to have been tracking the room as it went along.

4. A patient under general anesthesia, who shows no signs of consciousness and remembers nothing afterward, later tells of a dream under anesthesia because it's suggested to her by a counselor. Verdict: no experience — I take this as confabulation plain and simple. But note I seem to be treating the *absence of a spontaneous report* as doing the work here, which is uncomfortably close to Malcolm's own reasoning.

5. A false awakening: I dream that I woke, walked to the kitchen, and started coffee, then really woke. Verdict: the dreamt waking was an experience — but was the coffee-brewing an experience *of* coffee-brewing, or an experience of falsely seeming to myself to brew coffee? Here my intuitions collapse.

6. Dennett's construction scenario, stipulated to be true: a dream is spliced together at the instant of waking, and the sleeper is in no conscious state at all before that. Verdict: no experience occurred. But this stipulated case is, from the inside, supposed to be indistinguishable from case 1 — so this verdict seems to force me to say I *can't know* that case 1 was an experience, which I'm not prepared to say.

7. A lucid dreamer, flagged to researchers with prearranged eye movements, reports having counted slowly to thirty in the dream, and the eye-signal timestamps match about thirty seconds of REM. Verdict: this feels like experience if anything does, and better evidence than ordinary reports — but if I let external, real-time correlation settle it, haven't I conceded to Dennett that the report alone (case 1) was never enough, which was Malcolm's point all along?

Is there a principle that captures all seven verdicts — something like "an experience occurred iff there was a conscious episode whose content matches the report, where the match is verifiable by [something]" — or does the attempt force me to choose between saving case 1 (dreams as experiences, full stop) and saving case 6 (the construction scenario as experienceless)? I suspect the crux is what work, if any, *verifiability* is doing in my verdicts: cases 2, 3, and 7 seem to turn on it, while cases 1, 5, and 6 seem to turn on it not mattering. State the principle or principles you'd propose as precisely as you can, say which case motivates each clause, tell me what it predicts about a case I haven't listed (say: a dream, remembered in fragments on waking but reconsolidated into a coherent narrative only when I tell it at breakfast — experience during sleep, experience at telling, or neither?), and be explicit about which of my verdicts you'd overturn and why the loss is worth it. I'd especially like to know whether my verdicts on 3 and 7 can be kept while still denying Malcolm's conclusion, or whether those verdicts quietly smuggle his criterion back in.
