So I sit on the board of a mid-sized private research foundation, and at next month's meeting we have to vote on the biggest commitment in the foundation's history: a roughly 12-year, $180M program to develop and run a regional soil-carbon monitoring network — hundreds of sensing sites, a shared data platform, farmer partnerships, the works. The vote is basically greenlight / kill / greenlight with conditions, and I'm supposed to circulate a written opinion before the meeting that actually recommends one of these.

Here's the fight we keep having, and it's basically the inside view vs the outside view dressed up in grant-committee clothing. Our internal technical committee — genuinely excellent people, two of whom have run large field programs before, and who have lived inside this proposal for two years — says the plan is solid: risk register is thorough, the pilot sites performed, and they put success odds around 70%. Our external review panel did a base-rate style analysis instead: they looked at how big multi-site environmental monitoring and large ag-tech partnerships have historically gone (cost overruns, sites dropping out, data platforms that never become interoperable, farmer participation collapsing after year three) and put our odds closer to 20%. Then it gets recursive, because of course it does: the technical committee's rebuttal is that the reference class is wrong — our program is bottom-up, the pilot de-risked the hard part, the sensing tech is mature now. And the panel counters that this is exactly what every insider group says, and the fact that they say it every time is itself part of the base rate. There's also a modesty problem layered on top: half our board thinks the whole point of paying for an external panel is that we should basically defer to it whenever it disagrees with insiders (we're not smarter than the aggregate of comparable programs), and the other half thinks that policy is self-defeating — if the inside view never counts for anything, we'd never have funded anything, including the two programs that are now the foundation's crown jewels.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate. It needs a concrete recommendation (including, if you go for "greenlight with conditions," conditions that a board could actually vote on — staged funding gates, kill criteria, whatever), not a survey of the epistemology. But I want the epistemology done for real, because people will smell it if I hand-wave: when, if ever, should the inside view be allowed to move you off the base rate, and what does "our case is different" have to prove before it counts? Is deferring to the panel epistemic modesty or just an excuse for not thinking? And be fair to the losing side of whatever you recommend — the strongest version of the argument you're voting against, not a strawman. Keep it to something the board will actually read. Also if you think our whole way of framing it (two views, pick a weighting) is confused, say so and tell us what the framing should be instead — but you still have to tell us how to vote.
