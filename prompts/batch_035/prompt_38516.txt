My roomate has gotten really into this view that what he calls the "movie in your head" doesn't actually exist — like, when you look at a red tomato, you think there's this raw red feel that you're directly acquainted with, but he says that's just a kind of user-interface the brain builds, and there's nothing phenomenal underneath. He likes the car dashboard analogy: the fuel gauge looks like it's telling you about the gas tank directly, but it's just a display, and consciousness is like that all the way down. He also says the funny thing is that we're being fooled into thinking we have an inner feel in the first place, and apparently that's supposed to be fine because being fooled is a real brain process even if the thing you think you see isn't there.

Logically I can sort of follow it, but two things bug me. First, if my experience of tasting coffee is an illusion, what exactly is being deceived — doesn't fooling someone presuppose someone having an experience of being fooled? Second, it feels like he's explaining my inner life away rather than explaining it, like a magician who insists the trick never happened. Is my unease pointing at a genuine hole in his view, or am I just clinging to the illusion he's talking about? I don't want reading recommendations, just want to figure out wether I should push back and how.
