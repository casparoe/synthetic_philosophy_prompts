I'm taking a philosophy of physics course this term, and the unit on the metaphysics of special relativity has left me more convinced than I expected to be by the Rietdijk–Putnam argument. The way I understand it, if simultaneity is frame-relative, and each observer treats everything simultaneous with their here-and-now as fixed and determinate, then by chaining observers together every event — including ones in my future — ends up determinate for someone, and the transitivity of determinateness collapses the distinction between the fixed future and the settled past. I've come out the other end committed to something like the block universe: no objective becoming, no world-wide flowing present. My instructor keeps hinting that students in my position are being set up to discover the view is far less secure than it feels in the week after you first really get the relativity of simultaneity, and I'd like to do that discovering now rather than on the exam.

So treat my position as the object: what would actually count against it? I can gesture at some candidate defeaters myself — insisting that a cosmological or neo-Lorentzian privileged foliation restores a global present even while respecting the empirical content of special relativity; arguing that the argument illicitly slides from "simultaneous with an observer" to "real, full stop"; denying that determinateness transfers between observers at all; or noting that in general relativity things get murkier, with Gödel's rotating universes allegedly showing that lapse and becoming can fail in ways the block eternalist can't cleanly accommodate. But I don't trust my own sense of which of these are serious and which are presentist whistling in the dark, and I'm sure I'm missing others, including empirical ones — I gather that if a Lorentz-violing collapse in quantum mechanics were ever confirmed, that would matter here, though I don't know how much.

Please give me the defeaters ranked from what would genuinely sink the view to what is merely awkward, distinguishing what a committed block eternalist would concede as counting against them from what they would shrug off, and say explicitly where you think the insulation-by-qualification risk lies — whether the position can always be patched by retreating to some weaker claim about the structure of spacetime, and whether that would be a vice in a metaphysical thesis of this kind. And do commit: don't leave me with a menu of live options. Tell me whether, all things considered, the argument from special relativity to the block universe should be believed, and which single development — conceptual or empirical — would most shake your confidence in that verdict if it materialized.
