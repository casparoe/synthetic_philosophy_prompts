I'm on the steering committee of a mid-sized AI evaluation org. Right now we put out a per-model "safety report": a dozen proxy benchmarks (refusal rates on harmful queries, sycophancy probes, jailbreak robustness, etc.), each with its own threshold. Our biggest funder wants us to collapse all of that into a single pass/fail "safety index" that downstream companies can gate deployment on, and a vendor has pitched exactly that — a weighted composite calibrated on our historical data, which they'd keep updating as new model scores come in. Half the committee loves it (one number, decisions actually get made), half thinks any single index is doomed to get Goodharted the moment companies start optimizing against it. Draft the memo I'll circulate before Friday: recommend adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions.

Two things I want the memo to actually do. First, engage with the fact that not all Goodhart-ing is the same thing — selecting on a noisy composite, a proxy breaking when you push to the extreme of the distribution, and strategic gaming are different failure modes that need different fixes (and some fixes, like keeping component weights secret or rotating the benchmark suite, have real costs of their own). Second, deal honestly with the strongest point on the single-index side: our current per-benchmark thresholds already get gamed piecemeal, so "no composite" isn't a no-Goodhart option either.

After the memo, give me a JSON summary with this schema:

{
  "recommendation": "adopt" | "reject" | "adopt_with_conditions",
  "dominant_failure_modes": [strings naming which variants of Goodhart's law drove the decision],
  "conditions": [strings, empty if none],
  "strongest_loosing_side_point": string,
  "review_trigger": string describing an observable that would force us to revisit this decision
}
