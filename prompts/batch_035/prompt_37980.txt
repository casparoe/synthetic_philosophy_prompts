I've gone and actually convinced myself of Lewis-style modal realism -- not as a picture I find useful, but for real: possible worlds are concrete, spatiotemporally isolated universes of exactly the same kind as ours, no modal metaphysics needed over and above them, modality just falls out of quantifying over worlds. I got there because the alternatives all seemed worse -- ersatz worlds need a prior modal ideology to build the worlds out of, fictionalism needs a story about why pretending gives truth, and so on. But here's what's starting to bother me: I genuinely can't articulate what would change my mind, and I'm not sure whether that's because the view is really that well-placed or because it's been qualified into being untouchable. So give me the full exposure map. What would count against it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward? I'm not interested in the incredulous stare -- "it's weird" isn't a defeater and I already know Lewis's reputation for shrugging at it. I want things like: the objection that counterpart theory can't do everything he needed it to do (I have some worries about transworld identity of duplicates or about counting counterpart relations), the asymmetry problem -- the actual world is supposed to be special only because we're in it, and that's allegedly a feature, but is there a case where that commitment becomes intolerable? Also the "expansion" moves, where the view just absorbs whatever the modal realist wants by positing enough worlds -- at some point does it collapse into a rival (fictionalism? a combinatorial view?) rather than being a distinctive theory with content? Which of these would committed Lewisians themselves grant as counting against them, versus waving off? And the meta-question: modal realism predicts nothing observationally, so it can't be empirically threatened -- is that just what metaphysics is like, or is a view that can't lose anything not really saying anything? I'd honestly find a clean demonstration that some case can't be described at all (the way some people think haecceitist cases or certain cross-world coincidences can't be handled) more persuasive than any amount of "it's extravagantly ontologically bloated," but tell me if you think I've got that backwards.
