Quick bit of context: I'm presenting at a workshop on competition dynamics next month, and the audience is mostly evolutionary game theorists who think of selection as not optimizing anything, plus a couple of antitrust economists. My thesis: the whole "Moloch / multipolar trap" framing — the one from the Scott Alexander essay that gets passed around — conflates two different situations. Type one is cases where every actor would rather not race (arms races, ad-spending wars, the classic PD structure): these are really commitment problems, and the fix is mechanisms that let actors credibly bind themselves. Type two is cases where the actors genuinely like racing and the "race to the bottom" is only a bottom from outside the system, so there's nothing to fix at the level of the actors at all. My claim is that most examples people file under Moloch are secretly type one mislabeled as type two, and that this conflation is exactly what makes the Moloch rhetoric so gripping and so useless for policy. Give me exactly five objections I should expect from this crowd, no intro or conclusion, just the list — each item one or two sentences: first the objection phrased the way they'd actually put it, then my best one-line reply and where that reply is soft. Make sure one of the five is the objection Im hoping nobody raises, and don't go easy on me on it.
