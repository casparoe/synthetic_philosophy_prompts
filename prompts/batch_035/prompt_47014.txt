Four months into parenthood and running on a fraction of my usual sleep, I have noticed something about my own behavior that I would like help identifying. When a routine of mine is interrupted partway through, I do not resume at the point of interruption; I restart the sequence from the beginning, and I complete steps whose purpose has already evaporated. If I am halfway through the bottle-feeding routine — sterilize, prepare, warm, feed — and my partner tells me she has just fed the baby, I nevertheless find myself sterilizing and preparing a full bottle before I catch myself. Interrupted between locking the front door and pocketing my keys, keys already in hand, I go back and re-lock the door. Filling the weekly pill organizer, interrupted on Wednesday, I have tipped everything back into the bottle and started again from Monday.

Three features make this feel like one phenomenon rather than several: the sequence, once triggered, runs to completion regardless of whether its goal still stands; interruption does not pause it but resets it to its start; and it is not unconscious — I am attending throughout and can say, correctly, in the abstract, what I am doing, just not why I am doing it given the circumstances. I half-remember an old anecdote, source forgotten, about a wasp that drags prey to its burrow, pops in to inspect, and, if you keep nudging the prey a few inches away, will drag it back and inspect again indefinitely; on four hours of sleep I suspect the difference between the wasp and me is smaller than I would like.

Does this configuration have an established name? The nearest things I can name do not fit. Habit or autopilot suggests attention is elsewhere, and mine is not. Compulsion suggests the behavior is unwanted, and mine is not until I notice it. Perseveration, as I understand it, is being stuck on a single response, not re-running a structured procedure from its start. So: is there a term for trigger-bound, goal-blind sequence completion, with the agent's knowledge intact but causally idle? I am not after a bare label — I want to know what the phenomenon's edges are, and in particular whether restarting from the beginning is a defect or a design feature, a cheap way of making a procedure robust to interruption that is worth the wasted steps.

Then the part that genuinely keeps me up, insofar as the baby allows anything else to: the reason-responsive, flexible version of me seems to be a thin layer that fatigue peels off to reveal a stack of procedures underneath. If most of daily behavior is like that, what remains of the idea that our actions are free? Is there a literature connecting this kind of mechanical rigidity to free will, and does it tend to conclude that the rigidity undermines freedom, or that freedom is precisely the evolved capacity to climb out of it?
