(1) If a cosmological theory implies that the overwhelming majority of observers having experiences like mine are Boltzmann brains -- random fluctuations in the far future of a universe dominated by dark energy -- then by ordinary self-locating reasoning I should conclude I'm probably a Boltzmann brain. (3) So if the theory is true, my evidence gives me no justification for believing it.
(4) A theory that destroys any justification for believing it cannot rationally be believed (he called this cognitive instability).
(5) Our best cosmology -- accelerating expansion, eventual de Sitter phase, thermodynamic fluctuations over unimaginable timescales -- does imply Boltzmann brains vastly outnumber ordinary observers.
(6) So our best cosmology cannot rationally be believed, and (7) we should revise or drop the cosmology.

And it also seems like ANY theory on which people like me are a tiny minority of all observers -- huge universe full of aliens, say -- would trigger the exact same reasoning, which can't be right.
