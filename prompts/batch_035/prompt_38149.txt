quick setup so we're on the same page: the paradox of fiction is three claims that each seem fine on their own but can't all be true — (1) readers genuinely feel emotions (pity, fear, grief) toward fictional characters; (2) you can only genuinely feel an emotion toward something you believe exists; (3) readers don't believe fictional characters exist.

I write fiction as a hobby, and I've run into the weirdest version of this in myself. Last year I killed off a side character I'd been writing for maybe five years, and I was honestly sad about it for days. Authors crying over their own characters is apparently a whole genre of interview anecdote. And my case seems stranger than the usual reader case, because I'm the one who decided she dies. There's no uncertanty, no "what will happen to her" — I hold all the cards, I have literally maximal knowledge that she's made up, since I made her up. The usual "suspension of disbelief" story looks weakest exactly here.

So here's what I want from you. First, your credence that the emotions readers feel toward fictional characters are genuine emotions in the same sense as emotions toward real people and events — an actual number, and "it depends on what you mean by genuine" doesn't count as one. Then decompose it: roughly how much of that number rides on whether genuine emotion requires belief in existence at all, how much on whether there's some in-between state (imagination, quasi-belief, alief-ish thing) that can do the belief's job, how much on the thought that these emotions are directed at props in a game of make-believe rather than at anything, and any other terms you think belong. One line of justification for each, then show me how they combine into your final number.

After that, use my author case as a stress test: which of the standard ways out of the paradox (reject each of the three premises in turn) does it fit worst? And does the fact that authors grieve their own creations move your credence up, down, or leave it alone? Last thing — I'd put my own credence around 0.8, and a friend of mine who studied philosophy puts hers at 0.15. If you had to guess, is the gap between us about one of the premises, or are we probably just using "genuine" to mean different things?
