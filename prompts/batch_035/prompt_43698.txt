I'm a third-year PhD student in philosophy finishing a paper on precommitment and self-locating evidence, and my reading group on updateless-style decision theories has been stuck for three weeks on an argument one of the cognitive science students brought to our last session. He calls it the Collapse Argument, because if it works, updatelessness collapses into full-strength evidential reasoning and is then refuted by its own examples. Here it is as he gave it to us. (1) Updatelessness says: evaluate policies from a perspective before conditioning on some of your evidence, and choose the policy with the highest expected utility from there; the theory is notoriously quiet about which evidence to un-observe, so the natural reading is the broadest one — un-observe everything whose evidential content is relevant to the comparison. (2) In counterfactual mugging, that means computing value before learning the coin came up tails, which makes paying rational, because my choice at this node is logically linked to the predictor's earlier simulation of my decision procedure. (3) But the arithmetic of ex ante expectation doesn't distinguish between correlations that run through a simulation of my decision procedure and correlations that run through a common cause or shared statistical profile: in both cases the abstain-policy or the pay-policy simply comes out ahead when you compute expectation from far enough back. (4) So updatelessness must also tell me to abstain in the smoking-lesion setup, and, more generally, to manage the news about anything my policies correlate with, including my own past. (5) To test this: I should decline a free vacation in my hometown, because suppose a demographic study shows that people who decline such vacations overwhelmingly grew up in stable, loving households, and having had a stable childhood is a large component of my lifetime utility. Refusing cannot causally touch my childhood, but on the broad ex ante perspective, a better childhood is part of what I'm buying when I select the refusal-policy. (6) A theory that tells me to forgo pleasures in order to have had, evidentially, better childhoods is not a theory of rational choice; so updatelessness, read broadly, refutes itself.

I can see two escape routes and dislike both. Denying (3) seems to require saying that logical dependence and mere evidential correlation come apart in a way the ex ante calculation must respect — but I had thought that refusing to care about the difference between causal and merely statistical structure was the entire point of the updateless turn; it's why it pays in counterfactual mugging. Denying the broad reading in (1) seems to leave the theory without a principled account of which evidence may be conditioned on, and I can't see how that isn't just patching the leaks case by case. My own instinct is that something goes wrong in (3), maybe that the "ex ante perspective" stops being a well-defined standpoint once you've thrown away all of your evidence rather than just the observation whose logical entanglement with your decision procedure is what made un-observing it valuable in the first place — but I can't make that precise, and I notice that I can't actually say what the reference class of "my decision procedure" is supposed to be in step (5) either, or whether the vacation-refusal is even an output of the same computation the demographic study is tracking. Which step would you reject, what does rejecting it cost, and why did the step look fine in the first place? One further request: please be explicit about what is settled among people who actually work on updateless and functional decision theories and what is genuinely contested — my sense is that the "which evidence do you un-observe?" question is a live dispute rather than a settled one, and I'd rather not walk into my advisor's office confidently repeating one side of it as if it were the consensus.
