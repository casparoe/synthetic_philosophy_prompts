I run post-incident reviews for a payments platform. We average one significant incident a week; each review gets ninety minutes with a rotating cast of engineers, and afterwards I sign a report that names a single root cause and a corrective action, because our auditors — and occasionally a regulator — will not accept anything less. The philosophy of science I've half-remembered says that inference to the best explanation is comparative: you infer to the best of the alternatives actually considered, which means a good report depends on which alternatives got onto the table, not just on how you score them. It also says there's a standing worry that the best of a bad lot isn't the same thing as the true one. Please turn all of this into a procedure my team can actually run inside the time and expertise constraints we have: how to force rival hypotheses into consideration instead of scoring whatever the first responder fixated on; what the scoring criteria should be and how to handle it when the usual virtues — simplicity, scope, mechanism — pull against each other; what makes an amendment count as an ad hoc patch to a dying hypothesis rather than a legitimate refinement; and, hardest for me, a stopping rule for when the evidence leaves two hypotheses live and the procedure should return no defensible root cause rather than pick the current leader, with the gaps where an engineer's judgment cannot be replaced by the checklist marked as such. I'd also like you to be honest about where the procedure will misfire — where it will certify a story because the room is tired, or refuse one because the honest hypothesis is boring. Then run it on two incidents: one where the leading explanation fits all the evidence but only by positing a condition nothing in the logs requires, and one where two subsystems failed in the same hour and each team has a clean explanation of its own half that cannot touch the other's. Finish by drafting the paragraph I can send to an auditor who has rejected a report on the grounds that, quote, root cause analysis must terminate in a root cause.
