quick bit of background: I help run a philosophy of religion discussion group, and we keep circling the stone paradox without ever getting anywhere because nobody agrees what omnipotence is supposed to mean in the first place. So before we go another round, I want the spec first.

Write me the desiderata — what any adequate theory of omnipotence has to deliver. Obviously it has to dissolve the stone paradox, but that's the easy part. It also has to handle the necessary-truths problem (can an omnipotent being make 2+2=5, or is "power over logic" a confused demand?), the changing-the-past question (Aquinas said no, Descartes-style voluntarism arguably says yes — your theory should say something about which camp is right), sinning (does the ability to do evil count for or against omnipotence?), self-limitation, and a wrinkle I want to add myself: can an omnipotent being create another omnipotent being? I suspect two such beings is incoherent since each could thwart the other, which would mean omnipotence is unshareable in a weird way, but I've never seen anyone argue it out.

For each requirement, give the case behind it, and mark which ones you think are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are just prejudices of particular theological camps (I'm guessing the logic one is a camp prejudice, but tell me if I'm wrong). Then flag any subset you think is jointly unsatisfiable — I have a suspicion the stone paradox and the two-beings case pull in opposite directions, and if the desiderata can't all be met at once I want to know that, since that would itself be the interesting result. Don't bother scoring Aquinas vs the maximal-power accounts vs the essential-consistency accounts against the list at length, one line each is plenty — the criteria are what I actually want.

And please push back if the project itself is confused — if omnipotence just isn't a coherent single property and the paradox is a symptom rather than a puzzle, say that plainly instead of helping me tidy up a broken concept. British English please.
