I'm putting together a "which species concept do you actually use?" quiz for the grad students in our biodiversity lab. Half of them say "biological species concept" when asked point blank, but then go sort everything by appearance in their day-to-day work, so I want the quiz to sort them by what they'd say about cases, not by which label they like.

Please write eight questions. Each question is a concrete case with two or three answer options that point somewhere in the logical space without naming any position — e.g. one case could be a putative ring species where the two ends of the ring meet and no longer interbreed, another could be an ancient all-female asexual lineage that's been on its own for millions of years. The set should include at least: a case where interbreeding and morphology disagree, the asexual case, a case about splitting a lineage in the fossil record where no breeding information is even in principle available, and a case about two populations that exchange genes occassionally but look and act totally differently (swap in better cases if you have them). No question should just ask "do you think species are defined by reproductive isolation" — that's the label, not the test.

Then give me a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto: the interbreeding/isolation view, diagnosability-based views (morphological or phylogenetic — if the quiz can't separate these two, say so rather than pretending), the ecological view if the case set can even catch it, a pluralist leaf, and an eliminativist/skeptic leaf along the lines of "there's no species rank, just lineages." If some answer combination fits no leaf, list it under "unnamed" with one sentence on why nobody's there (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).

Format: sections in this order with rough lengths — "The quiz" (~250 words), "Scoring key" (~200 words), "What the quiz can't sort" (~100 words).
