I've been trying to figure out what I actually believe about whether machines understand things, and instead of arguing about LLMs in the abstract I decided to just go with my gut on a bunch of concrete cases. Here's where I landed:

- A thermostat doesn't understand temperature. Obviously.
- A calculator doesn't understand arithmetic, even though it never gets a sum wrong.
- A chess engine that beats grandmasters does understand chess — at least chess. This one I feel pretty strongly about.
- My dog understands that the leash coming off the hook means a walk. It's dumb understanding, but it's understanding.
- Clever Hans the horse didn't understand arithmetic, but arguably understood something about reading his audience, so not a total loss.
- Someone with blindsight who can post a letter through a slot while insisting they can't see anything: understanding of the slot's location is somehow present, but I can't say where it lives.
- An old GPS unit that reroutes me around traffic doesn't understand the city, even though it knows the streets better than I do.
- Now the LLM ones. An LLM that passes the bar exam: I want to say it doesn't understand law. But that verdict bites me on the chess engine, because as far as I know the engine is also just doing symbol-crunching, and I gave IT the thumbs up.
- Same LLM, when I made up an analogy about my own job, got it and extended it in a direction I hadn't thought of. That felt like understanding in a way the bar exam didn't. These two can't both be right, or can they?

So my question: is there a principle that captures all these verdicts, or most of them? The chess-engine-yes / calculator-no pair feels like the load-bearing one for me — both are pure computation, both never make mistakes, so whatever separates them, it isn't "symbols vs. not symbols." And I'm aware of the whole stochastic parrots argument, but I don't want a take on that debate, I want to know whether my own pattern of verdicts adds up to anything coherent. If some verdict has to go, tell me which one and why it's that one and not another. Then tell me what the resulting principle says about two cases I haven't listed: a system that translates poetry between languages really well (does it understand the poems?), and a friend's kid who can read sentences aloud fluently but clearly has no idea what they mean.
