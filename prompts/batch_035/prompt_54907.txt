I sit on the editorial board of a small literary magazine (I write novels myself, this is my unpaid penance) and we have a mess with our annual short-story prize. A shortlisted entry, submitted under a pen name, may have been written partly by an AI — someone ran it through detection software, but those tools seem about as reliable as a polygraph at a family reunion. Our rules only say entries must be the entrant's own unaided work, and we never defined unaided or said what would count as evidence. Half the board wants to interview the author and decide based on that; the author has agreed to answer questions but only in writing, no video, which makes it even more like a Turing setup. The other half says that's exactly the wrong kind of test: we're not trying to establish whether a mind exists, we're trying to figure out what happened at the keyboard, and how someone talks about a story afterward doesn't settle how it got written. I have to draft the board's decision memo before our announcement date in two weeks. Please write it: a concrete recommendation we can act on in that window (e.g. accept, disqualify, accept with conditions, or something else), criteria the board members can actually apply, and a serious treatment of the written-interview option — don't just assume the Turing-style evidence is irrelevant, argue it out. It can be a page, plain language, we're not philosophers.
