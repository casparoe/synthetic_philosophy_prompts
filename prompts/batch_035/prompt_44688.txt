I've been collecting the various demands philosophers make of evolutionary debunking arguments in metaethics, and before I write anything further on the topic I want the full specification. Fix the purpose this way: an adequate argument is one that could rationally move a reflective moral realist — someone who already accepts the standard evolutionary account of how our capacity for moral judgment took shape and remains a realist anyway — to lower her confidence in realism. Not an argument that merely persuades people who were already suspicious of it.

Write the desiderata: what an argument meeting that purpose must deliver. At minimum, I want the list to cover: (a) whether the argument owes us a story about the level at which selection acted — on belief content, on behavior, or on the underlying psychological dispositions — and why the level it picks matters to the argument's force; (b) the overgeneration constraint: whether the argument must avoid carrying over to mathematics, logic, or ordinary perceptual belief, all of which have genealogies that were not selected for truth-tracking, or else locate a principled disanalogy; (c) what it must do about the third-factor reply, in which the realist claims evolution favored tracking independent facts that happen to overlap with moral truths; (d) what it owes us on scope — whether it debunks realism wholesale or only particular first-order judgments, and whether the debunker may choose the scope strategically without the choice becoming ad hoc; and (e) whether the argument must be reflectively stable — whether the debunker's own normative commitments, including the epistemic principle that beliefs with genealogies of a certain kind should be abandoned, survive the argument's own machinery.

For each requirement, give the motivating case: a scenario in which an argument satisfies the other requirements but fails that one, and visibly misfires because of it. Mark which requirements you take to be non-negotiable given the fixed purpose, and which are demands peculiar to one camp — error theorists and quietist realists will weight scope differently, for instance. Then flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable; my own suspicion is that (b) and (e) pull against each other, and I want to know whether you agree or can show the tension is merely apparent. Finally, one short paragraph at most on how a Sharon Street–style Darwinian dilemma and a Richard Joyce–style error-theoretic argument fare against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard — the criteria are where the thinking is, and the scorecard is something I'll do on my own.
