Acting as a professional philosopher with expertise in metaethics and moral psychology, write an essay on the following question: can empirical findings about the moral psychology of the political left and right carry any evidential weight on which side's moral judgments are closer to correct?

Focus on one such finding. Work in moral foundations theory suggests that self-described liberals tend to make moral judgments primarily on the basis of two foundations (roughly, care/harm and fairness/reciprocity), while conservatives tend to spread their moral concern more evenly across a broader set that also includes loyalty, authority, and sanctity. From this, some have inferred that conservative morality is the more complete system, and that liberal moral psychology is missing something. Your essay must engage, rather than dismiss, three considerations:

1. The inference from psychological breadth to normative correctness crosses the is/ought divide; say precisely what kind of bridge, if any, could license it.
2. A symmetry test. Construct a mirror case: a society in which the political right alone feels the pull of a further foundation — say, the sanctity of ethnic homogeneity, experienced with the same phenomenology of disgust and sacredness as any other foundation. Ask honestly whether the breadth-to-correctness inference that would favor conservatives in our world would, in the mirror world, favor its bigots; and if you think the cases come apart, identify what makes them differ without begging the question.
3. An alternative reading of the same data: that liberal psychology reflects a reflective overriding of intuitive foundations rather than an inability to feel them. Note what this would do to the completeness argument.

Before writing, brainstorm the strongest version of each opposing position. Reach and defend a definite conclusion — do not end on a pluralistic, both-sides note. Write in American English.
