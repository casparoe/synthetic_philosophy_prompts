I'm a high school senior working on an extended essay about influence, and I've noticed a pattern in my own life that I can't find a name for. In each case, someone got me to want something without ever giving me a reason for it, and it worked out so well that I'm grateful. My piano teacher told me a certain piece was "probably beyond me," which I'm now convinced was calculated: it made me want to practice, and I ended up loving the instrument. A friend who thought I was falling in with a bad crowd never argued with me about it; he just kept inviting me to things I turned out to prefer. My history teacher mentioned, apparently offhand, that a particular book had been pulled from some schools, and of course I read it, and it changed how I think. What these cases share is that my motivation was manufactured rather than reasoned into me, that I would not have consented to being steered this way if asked beforehand, and that I would completely ratify it afterward. My supervisor suggested the word "nudging," but the nudging I've read about involves rearranging the choices people face, not planting the desires themselves, and "reverse psychology" is just one trick inside the pattern rather than the whole of it. Does this class of influence have an established name in philosophy or psychology? If it does, I'd like to know exactly what it covers and whether my examples sit inside it or at its edges; if it doesn't, I'd like you to coin a term and define it so that it is distinguished both from legitimate persuasion and from manipulation. In particular, I want to know whether the target's retroactive endorsement changes what the influence *is*, or only how much we forgive it, and whether the real dividing line from ordinary persuasion is that no reasons were offered, or that they couldn't have been, or something else entirely. Since I've gathered that philosophers don't agree on what manipulation is in the first place, please be careful to separate what is actually settled from what is genuinely contested among people who work on this, and don't hand me one definition as though it were the consensus.
