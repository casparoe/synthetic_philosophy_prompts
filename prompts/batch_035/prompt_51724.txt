I'm preparing a one-page handout for a graduate seminar on speech act theory: a decision tree that sorts each student, by their own verdicts on five diagnostic cases, to the account of what makes an utterance the illocutionary act it is that they actually hold — Austin's felicity-conditions view, Searle's constitutive-rules account, a Gricean intention-based account, and at least one further position the logical space contains even though the seminar's reading list doesn't name it. Please build the tree from the cases upward rather than from the history of the literature, and keep Austin's stock examples — baptizing the ship, placing the bet, the bigamous marriage — out of it entirely. The five cases: (1) a corporation's mass-mailed, pre-drafted apology for a data breach, sent under the CEO's signature with no sincere intention behind it; (2) a customer-service chatbot that, lacking intentions altogether, outputs a promise that the refund will arrive within five business days; (3) a spokesperson who announces that the minister apologizes unreservedly to the affected families, and the question of whether anyone has thereby apologized; (4) a sarcastic promise made to a friend where everyone present understands that no commitment is being undertaken; and (5) a judge who utters a sentence of imprisonment off the bench, in a courthouse corridor, to a defendant over whom she does in fact have authority. Each branch node should be a single question about one case — whether the chatbot actually promised, whether anyone apologized in case 3 — phrased so a student can answer it without knowing which school the answer commits them to, and the nodes between them should isolate three issues: whether an illocution can occur in the absence of any pre-existing convention; whether satisfying the convention is sufficient, or whether speaker intention or audience uptake is additionally required; and whether some acts depend on institutional standing that neither convention nor intention by itself supplies. Cap the tree at six leaves. For each leaf, give one sentence on which of the five cases that position handles worst. Flag explicitly any pair of leaves your tree cannot separate given only these cases, and specify the additional case that would pull them apart. Finally, note any permitted combination of branch answers that no recognizable position occupies, and hazard a sentence on whether that's because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
