At our philosophy of mind reading group someone used the old Dennett line about how there are no skyhooks, only cranes to shut down a discussion about whether beliefs and reasons do any real explanatory work, or whether everything ultimately has to bottom out in physics/neurochemistry. His gloss was roughly: any explanation that appeals to something "higher level" is smuggling in a skyhook, so stop talking about propositional attitudes and start talking about neurons. The thing is, I half remember that Dennett himself coined the phrase "greedy reductionism" as an insult — for people who try to skip levels and explain complicated things directly in terms of their smallest parts, and that he thought that was just as much a mistake as believing in skyhooks. So I suspect the guy was using a slogan that, on one natural reading, actually rules out his own move.

Can you help me lay this out? What I'd like is a list of the distinct precise claims the no-skyhooks-only-cranes slogan could be making, from the trivial to the radical — e.g. the methodological point that you can't invoke miracles or unexplained explainers, the point that all design and complexity has to be built up by iterated, mindless processes with a history, a stronger global thesis that everything is in principle reducible, and maybe something in between. For each reading: is it trivially true, substantive and defensible, or false, and would it actually support what he wanted it to do in our discussion (i.e. dismiss beliefs and reasons as explanations)? And where does greedy reductionism fit in — is the crane picture supposed to *forbid* higher-level explanations or just *ground* them? I want something I can bring back to the group, so please be honest if some readings would backfire on him, since he'd basically be hoisted on Dennett's own petard. Sorry in advance, I know Dennett is dead and can't referee this one.
