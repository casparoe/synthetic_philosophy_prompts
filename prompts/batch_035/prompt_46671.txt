I have no background in philosophy or statistics; I've simply spent a long time thinking on my own about what probability actually is, and I've ended up with a set of positions I like individually but have begun to worry about as a package. Here is roughly where I've landed:

1. I'm drawn to the idea that probability is, at bottom, long-run frequency: the probability of a coin landing heads is nothing over and above the proportion of heads you would get in a very long series of similar flips.
2. I also think there is a fact of the matter, right now, about the chance of rain at my house next Tuesday — a number that exists out there in the world, independent of what any forecaster believes.
3. I accept that rational levels of confidence have to obey the standard rules of probability, because if they don't, someone can construct a series of bets that each look acceptable to you individually but together guarantee you lose money. I cannot find a flaw in that argument, and I don't expect to.
4. When you have no information that distinguishes the possible outcomes, I think you should spread your confidence evenly across them: with a fair-looking die and no other knowledge, one sixth on each face. And I can't see how the right assignment could depend on how you happen to describe or group the possibilities.
5. I take quantum mechanics to show that chance is a genuine physical feature of systems — that a radioactive atom has a real propensity to decay within the next hour — and not just a confession of our ignorance.
6. I think the question of which other events count as "similar flips" or the right comparison class for a given event is a practical nuisance, not a genuine objection to the frequency idea.

What I'd like to know is whether these six fit together. If some of them pull against each other, tell me which ones, how serious each conflict is, and what the least costly adjustment would be — I'm prepared to give something up, but I'd like to give up as little as possible. I'd also like you to identify any pair that only looks like a conflict but isn't, so I don't start revising things that were fine. Finally, since I'd rather not rebuild my whole picture on someone's word, please tell me where your own assessment is most likely to go wrong, and what argument or example, if it succeeded, would change your verdict about which of my commitments has to go.
