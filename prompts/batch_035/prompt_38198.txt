So I've been going down a rabbit hole on whether you can know you're in a given mental state — the whole liminosity thing, Williamson's cold hands case where you go from feeling cold to not feeling cold in tiny increments and there's gotta be a point where you're cold but can't know it, and the KK principle fights around it. I'm a longtime reader of the rationalist blogosphere, I've absorbed the classic takes on introspection-as-an-instrument, the meditators who claim they can just directly see their own mental states, the whole "your introspection is a lossy model of a model" shtick. And the thing that's bugging me is that everyone jumps straight to arguing about whether Williamson won, without anyone first laying out what a theory in this space is even supposed to deliver. It's the same failure mode as arguing interpretations of QM before agreeing on what counts as solving the measurement problem. So I want the spec first.

Purpose, so you know what to calibrate to: a theory good enough to tell us when a person's own report of their current mental state counts as knowledge rather than a true-ish guess, and what kind of access (if any) is special about the first-person case. Write me exactly five desiderata, numbered, each one a sentence or two, no introduction and no conclusion — the interesting claims should all live inside the items. Each desideratum should say what the theory has to get right and the case or consideration behind it, and if you suspect some pair of the five can't be jointly satisfied (say, reliability plus margin-for-error reasoning pushing toward anti-luminosity, or preserving any privileged access while handling self-deception and confabulation), fold that tension into the relevant item rather than adding extra paragraphs. Also flag, inside the item, if something is less a genuine requirement and more a prejudice of one camp — something only an infallibilist or a diehard internalist would demand — because I honestly can't tell anymore which parts of the usual demands are load-bearing and which are vibes. And somewhere in there, tell me which single desideratum does the most to knock out otherwise-attractive theories.

Two constraints so it doesn't come out generic. At least one desideratum has to explicitly involve the boundary case — state present but below the detection threshold, the feeling-cold-at-the-margin scenario — because that's where the whole debate actually lives and I'm tired of theories that only handle the easy middle of the distribution. And at least one has to address whether the theory generalizes to artificial minds doing introspection on their own internal states, since if first-person self-knowledge has a special structure, I want to know whether it's special to humans or to any agent with a self-model — and yes, I know the interpretability people say model self-reports are unreliable, but that's sort of my point: a decent theory here should explain WHY they're unreliable and whether a better-designed introspector could do better, not just shrug. No survey of the literature, no scorecard of existing views, just the five-item spec — I can read the rest elsewhere and honestly I've already read too much of it without a list to hang it on.
