Quick bit of background on me: I'm a retired materials scientist, thirty-some years in a polymer lab, and since retiring I've been reading a lot of political philosophy I never had time for back when I was arguing about melt flow indices. Now the actual request.

My brother-in-law is a fairly doctrinaire libertarian who's all in on a universal basic income — big enough cash payment to replace most of the existing welfare state, on the grounds that it respects people's own choices about their lives and starves the bureaucracy instead of feeding it. My daughter is a social democrat who thinks UBI is a trojan horse: a flat payment that sounds generous but would give the right an excuse to gut the programs poor people actually depend on, would subsidize employers who pay unlivable wages, and would give the same check to a surgeon and a single mom as if their situations were remotely comparable. They went at it for two hours over thanksgiving and mostly talked past each other the whole time.

Back in the lab, when two of us disagreed about what a batch of data meant, we'd sometimes write down beforehand what each of us predicted, so nobody could retrofit the story after the fact. That's basically what I want here. Write the joint statement these two could both sign after a genuine adversarial collaboration, with these parts:

- a numbered section of claims both would accept — about what cash can and can't do, about what existing welfare programs in rich countries actually accomplish, about labor markets, whatever they really share. Don't pad it with platitudes either of them would sign while rolling their eyes.
- the precise disagreements, each phrased so the person holding that view would accept the wording as fair to their side.
- the cruxes: the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them. My own guess is that one crux is whether universal programs are politically more durable than means-tested ones (the social security precedent comes up a lot in this fight) and another is whether giving people cash instead of in-kind services is respect or neglect — but work out what you think the cruxes really are, don't just take mine.
- for each crux, the argument or kind of evidence both would agree bears on it, with each sides prediction written down in advance. Natural experiments like the Alaska dividend, the Finnish pilot, or the old negative income tax trials are fair game if you can say what each side would expect them to show, but for some cruxes all there'll be is structural arguments about political incentives, and that's fine too — just be clear which kind of crux is which.

One more thing: if part of their fight turns out to be about words — for instance whether the welfare state even names the same thing for both of them — say which part and why. And if you conclude honestly that these two couldn't sign anything nontrivial together, say that instead of manufacturing a consensus. I'd rather know the disagreement is that deep than get a fake agreement dressed up as a collaboration.
