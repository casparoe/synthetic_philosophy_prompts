Two of my coworkers argued the whole lunch break about which historical thing AI should actually be compared to, and I couldn't follow who was winning. Dan said nuclear weapons are the right comparison because AI, like nukes, is one technology where a small number of actors hold something with runaway potential, and the fact that nuclear arms control treaties (mostly) worked tells us the goal should be an international treaty regime for AI. Maya said the printing press is the better analogy because the real effects of AI won't come from a single catastrophic event but from a slow reshuffling of who has power — like how the press didn't "go off," it just broke the Church's monopoly on information over a century and everything downstream of that got rearranged — and you can't treaty your way out of something like that. Dan said that if AI is like the printing press then there's literally nothing to do, since nobody could have sensibly regulated the press in 1500, so that analogy is just a fancy excuse for inaction, whereas at least the nuclear one tells you what the intervention looks like. Maya countered that saying the press "turned out fine" is survivorship bias — we're the descendents of the societies that absorbed it, and the people who lived through the wars of religion probably didn't feel it was fine. Dan then said the real test of any analogy is whether it points to a variable you can actually control: nuclear pointed at fissile material, which is countable and seizable, and asked what the printing-press version even is — licensing presses? — which got circumvented whenever it was tried. Maya's last word was that compute and model weights ARE countable like fissile material, so you could do treaties on those, but that in her analogy the harm doesn't live in the artifacts, it lives in what millions of ordinary people do with the new capabilities once they spread — so the nuclear comparison smuggles in the conclusion that control is the answer, instead of earning it. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right at each step, who's more right overall, and what (if anything) both of them are missing about how these analogies are even supposed to work?
