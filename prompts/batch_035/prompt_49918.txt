My friend and I argued for like three hours last night about the Historikerstreit and I realised at some point neither of us could say what the fight is actually *about*. My line was that the uniqueness claim (the whole Nolte/Habermas thing in 80s Germany) is basically unfalsifiable — if every comparison to the Gulag or colonial genocides gets treated as a diminishment, then "unique" just means "don't compare", which is a norm about commemoration, not a historical thesis, so historians arguing about it are talking past each other. He pushed Bauman on me: Bauman seems to dissolve the whole debate by arguing the Holocaust was a product of modernity itself (bureaucracy, instrumental rationality, the gardening state), which makes it comparable to other modern atrocities almost by definition, and the uniqueness people never really answer that, they just get angry. So my question: is this debate stalled because its partly verbal — people meaning different things by "unique" (morally vs causally vs in sheer scale vs in some essence) — or is there a genuine substantive disagreement buried in there, maybe about what the function of historical comparison even is (understanding vs normalization)? And realistically, would anything count as progress here — some conceptual distinction, or a better agreed question — or is it the kind of fight that's really about collective identity and can only end when people stop caring, not when anyone wins?
