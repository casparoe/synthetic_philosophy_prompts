After years of intermittent arguing about consciousness with my housemate — she works on higher-order theories and never misses a chance to tell me my views are a patchwork — I've finally written out my overall position to send her, and I'd like a consistency check before I do. The position, as I've drafted it, is this: (1) I've come to hold that the qualitative character of experience is a kind of user illusion: introspection is a generative model of the mind's own states, and like any interface it systematically misrepresents what lies beneath it, so the familiar phenomenal properties — the redness of red, the felt quality of pain — are not real features of experience at all. (2) At the same time, I am more certain that I am conscious right now than I am of any scientific claim whatsoever; no empirical finding, however strange, could make it reasonable for me to doubt that I am experiencing, and any theory of consciousness has to answer to that certainty rather than explain it away. (3) On moral status, I hold that what matters is the inner life itself, not the information processing: an exact functional duplicate of me that lacked inner experience would have no moral standing whatsoever, and I take such a duplicate to be possible in principle, even if it never occurs — a being's moral standing is fixed by whether there is something going on inside it, not by how it behaves or what it can report. (4) I think there is a permanent epistemic gap: no third-person description of a brain, however complete, will ever convey to someone who has never seen red what it is like to see red — and yet I remain a physicalist, holding that all the facts there are, are physical facts. (5) Finally, it seems to me that an illusion can exist only if something experiences it: an illusion of red requires a subject for whom it seems red, so even if the surface qualities are illusory, at most one layer gets peeled away — the underlying fact that there is seeming at all must be real, and no theory that denies this is acceptable to me.

Please go through the set and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look to be. I confess I cannot tell: on some days parts of this feel flatly incompatible (1 and 2, for instance — how can the qualitative character be an illusion if my certainty that I am experiencing outranks everything else?), and on other days the same pair seems to sit together fine. Order the genuine tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally bad; for each, say exactly what would have to give, and what that revision would then cost me elsewhere in the set. If there is a single revision that would clean up the most, name it and say which of the five has to go or be weakened — my housemate claims the whole thing unravels if you pull any thread, and I would like to know whether she is right or just enjoying herself.
