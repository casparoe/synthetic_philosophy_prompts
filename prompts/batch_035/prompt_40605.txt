Context: we were talking about process-based vs outcome-based supervision when training models to reason — do you reward just the final answer (check it's right, done), or do you grade the chain of thought step by step? His comeback was that outcome-based supervision has the mirror-image problem: with a sparse correct/incorrect signal you get credit assignment nightmares, and a model can learn to jump to right answers via some shortcut you'd never notice, whereas at least graded steps are auditable.

So is the process/outcome distinction really about what you reward, or about whether the model's process is legible enough to be graded at all? 3. My friend claims process supervision is inherently safer because you're not rewarding a model for reaching a right answer by bad means.
