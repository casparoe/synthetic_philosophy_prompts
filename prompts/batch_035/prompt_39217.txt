I serve on the safety review board at the small bottling plant where I work as a line mechanic, and we're stuck on an incident report I'd appreciate your help with. A hydraulic hose on filler #3 ruptured and burned a worker's forearm; the hose was well past its rated service life and had been flagged for replacement, but the replacement was on the shared checklist of whichever of two maintenance technicians was on shift, and neither of them did it that month. Our incident form has a single "root cause" field, and the board is split: half say the technician on duty "didn't do anything" and so can't have caused anything, and that the worn hose is the cause; half say skipping the inspection is exactly what caused the burn, and we should say so and recommend formal discipline. For what it's worth, I've been turning this over on my own and have a half-formed view that a failure to act only counts as a cause when the duty was someone's alone and not shared, but I can't tell whether that lets the technicians off too easily, since a duty divided can still be a duty failed. Please draft the short written opinion I'll circulate before Tuesday's meeting: it should recommend what we should write in the root cause field and whether discipline is warranted, and it should take seriously the strongest version of the view that a person who merely omitted an action cannot be a cause of anything.
