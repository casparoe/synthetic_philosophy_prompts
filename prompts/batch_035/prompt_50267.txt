Been trying to work out what exactly makes human extinction bad, beyond just the fact that alot of people die. Going by my gut on scenarios: a painless asteroid that kills everyone instantly seems worse than the sum of those deaths — the ending of humanity itself seems to add badness on top. A plague where nobody dies early but everyone goes sterile, so the last generation just lives out normal lives and humanity quietly stops, still seems terrible even though no one dies before their time. And in that scenario what bothers me almost as much as the ending is picturing the last generation knowing there's no future — them stoping the long projects, not funding basic science, not planting trees that take a century to grow. But then here's where I get stuck: I don't think it's bad at all that a merely possible future person never gets born. I'm not one of those "more happy people existing is better" people, that seems crazy to me. So possible people don't matter, but humanity having a future matters enormously? That feels like a contradiction but I can't see my way out of either half. One more data point: extinction with, say, 8 billion deaths feels much worse to me than the same 8 billion deaths spread over a few centuries with a healthy growing population the whole time, so it can't just be lost lives I'm tracking. Is there a principle that captures all of these, or do I have to give something up — and which verdict should go first?
