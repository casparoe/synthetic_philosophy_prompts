My friend and I have been arguing for years about what counts as a game, and I've kept a list of her calls. So far: chess, yes; children's ring-around-the-rosie, yes, even though nobody wins; our Sunday football match in the park, yes; professional footballers playing a league match, yes, even though it's their livelihood; a poker night played for matchsticks, yes; war, no; playing the stock market, no; the lottery, no; betting your car on a coin flip, no. The newest case we can't settle: soldiers on a training exercise, using laser-tag gear, under orders from their superiors, deadly serious about it, where everything is governed by rules that exist only because the participants treat them as binding, and nothing outside the exercise depends on who wins. Do you think she'll call it a game? What rule is she implicitly applying that fits all her past calls, and what other rules fit them just as well? Which of those rules disagree about the training exercise, and what further case would best tell them apart? I'm less interested in the answer than in whether her judgments track anything, or whether the things she calls games are simply similar to one another in different, overlapping ways, like members of a family.
