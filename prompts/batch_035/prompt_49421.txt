I'm a grad student who got roped into running a reading group that has now spent an entire semester fighting about laws of nature, and honestly I think it's time someone forced an output. One half of the room are best-system Humeans: laws just are the axioms of whatever systematization best balances simplicity and strength against the total mosaic of local facts, nothing governs anything, there is just the pattern and our summaries of it. The other half are anti-Humeans of the governing-laws persuasion: they think the regularities cry out for explanation, that the best-system story at best tracks the laws without being them, and that something like necessitation — or at least something with more metaphysical weight than elegance — has to be doing real work. Nobody has budged in months, and I've started to suspect a good chunk of the argument is people talking past each other, e.g. I'm genuinely not sure everyone in the room means the same thing by "governs" or by "explains."

So heres my ask: write the joint statement these two camps should have produced if they'd run a proper adversarial collaboration instead of just re-litigating the same seminar exchange every week. I want (1) a numbered section of claims both camps could actually sign — and be strict here, things they'd really endorse, not vague pablum — including claims about the mosaic, about what our best scientific theories do and don't commit us to, about what the best-system machinery gets right even if you're not a Humean about it, and about what any account of lawhood owes us. Then (2) a disagreements table where each remaining disagreement is stated twice: once in wording the Humeans would accept as a fair statement of their own view, once in wording the anti-Humeans would accept as a fair statement of theirs. Then (3) the cruxes — my guesses are the circularity worry (do best-system laws presuppose the very regularities they were supposed to explain?), the inference problem (if there are primitive necessitation facts, what explains why they generate the particular regular patterns we actually observe, rather than other ones?), and whether a primitive governing relation is even coherent rather than a metaphor — but you may find better ones. For each crux, name the argument or consideration both camps would agree bears on it, and write down in advance what each side predicts that argument would show, so the crux could actually be moved rather than just admired.

Two requests on how the document should handle itself. First, I want to be able to tell which parts of the joint statement are actually settled among people who know this literature and which are genuinely open — don't let the agreed-claims section quietly smuggle in something only one camp treats as obvious, and don't present either side as a monolith (the anti-Humeans in my group are split between necessitation-types and people who put the modal weight in properties or dispositions instead, and it's fine — useful, even — if the statement notes where those sub-camps' interests come apart). Second, if any part of the dispute turns out to be verbal rather than real, flag which part and say why, since "you're using 'governs' to do bookkeeping, they're using it to do metaphysics" would explain a lot of our worst meetings.
