Two of my friends have been arguing for weeks about this thing one of them read on a forum, and they finally asked me to referee, which is a joke because I barely follow it. The setup, as they explained it to me: suppose there could someday be something — a superintelligent AI, whatever — that can predict how you'll act and that sometimes extorts people. It presumably only bothers with people who'd cave and pay. So the forum's proposed move is to publicly take on a "surrogate goal": you commit to treating some small harm done to a third party as if it were a huge harm done to you, so that anyone out there weighing whether to extort you can see that threatening you never pays off, no matter what they threaten you *with*.

Dana thinks everyone should do something like this. Marcus thinks its self-defeating. Their argument, as best I can reconstruct it:

(1) Dana: it's just deterrence, like a country declaring it'll retaliate even when retaliation costs more than giving in would. You don't wait until you're attacked to build the habit — by then it's too late.

(2) Marcus: countries can make that threat stick because institutions carry it forward and everyone knows it. You're one person. At the moment the extortion actually happens, following through on the surrogate goal makes things strictly worse for you than paying would, so any predictor worth its salt predicts you'd cave or quietly drop the goal, and the whole scheme is written in sand.

(3) Dana: that's exactly why you practice it now, in ordinary cases — you make yourself genuinely the kind of person who follows through on small commitments, so the disposition is real and not theater. And people do have dispositions like this; we keep promises even when breaking them would be more convenient.

(4) Marcus: keeping promises has a point — it keeps you trustworthy to actual people, and that pays off in the actual world. Your surrogate goal pays off only in a hypothetical world, but it charges you in this one: once people learn you'll massively retaliate over small slights against others, some of them will game it, and you'll be burning real relationships for fake reasons.

(5) Dana: fine, calibrate it — make the surrogate goal small enough that the everyday cost is tiny but the deterrent signal is still there. The forum people talk about this too.

(6) Marcus: then you're back to my problem — the signal has to be big enough that extortion never pays, or it deters nobody, and small enough that it doesn't wreck your actual life, and there's no guarantee any size does both. At that point it's just a weird promise with extra steps.

Then they both looked at me. I truly could not tell who was winning, and honestly some of it went over my head. Can you go through their moves and say who's right at each step, not just who wins overall?
