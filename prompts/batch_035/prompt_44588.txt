I've got no philosophy training, just something I keep turning over on my own, so bear with me. The question is basically: when does a bunch of things count as ONE thing with parts, rather than just... a bunch of things? My desk is obviously one thing. The pile of papers on it seems more like a pile than a thing. A crowd of people, is that one thing? A wave in the sea? And when the thing gets rebuilt plank by plank (the old ship thing) at no point do I stop and say right, NOW it's two things, so whatever answer we give has to cope with that somehow.

Before I go and read what actual philosophers reckon, I want the spec first: what would any decent answer to this have to deliver? Please write out the requirements. I'm imagining things like:

- which cases it has to get right or at least have an opinion on (a person, a heap of sand, a car that's had every part swapped, a crowd, a molecule), and why that case is a requirement rather than just an example
- the distinctions it can't blur -- like the difference between "these things make up one thing" and "these things are all in one place", or between "it's one thing" and "I call it one thing out of habit"
- the bits of ordinary life and science it can't contradict. I'm thinking: if physics says everything is just particles in fields, and there's no fact of the matter about where a table "ends" at the atomic level, that's fine, but an answer that said there are no things with parts at all (no desks, just "desk-shaped particles") seems like it's paying too much for the theory. Is that a fair requirement though? Maybe that's just my prejudice, I honestly can't tell, which is sort of the point of asking.

Then mark which requirements you reckon are non-negotiable and which are really just tastes of one camp dressed up as requirements. And the bit I most want to see: can some of the requirements not be satisfied together? Like if one says the answer must always be sharp (never vague whether some stuff makes up one thing) and another says it must match how we actually talk, which is vague all over the place, that's trouble -- is there a subset that's jointly impossible, and if so which would you drop?

Also, at the end, briefly: which single requirement does the most work knocking out candidate answers? Like is there one requirement that most answers people naturally give (everything that's stuck together counts as one; or only living things count as one; or every collection counts as one, so there's a thing that's my left shoe plus the moon) fail on? I don't need a full survey of everyone's views, just which requirement is the biggest filter.

A table is fine if it helps. Plain english please, I've read a bit of the jargon online but don't assume I know what any of the technical words for this stuff mean.
