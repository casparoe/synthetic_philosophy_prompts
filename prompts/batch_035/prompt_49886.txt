Bit of background: I'm a retired field ecologist — thirty-some years estimating vole death rates per hectare, and now that I've got time on my hands I've gone and joined a philosophy reading group. We just worked through Pinker's Better Angels of Our Nature, and it turns out arguing about whether the world is getting less violent is my idea of a good Tuesday. Rates versus raw counts was literally my job for decades, so I notice when people slide between them.

The member with the firmest opinions is Diane, and I've been keeping a running list of her rulings because I genuinely cant tell what rule she's running. Her verdicts so far:

- the long fall in per-capita homicide from medieval Europe to today: a real decline in violence;
- the disappearance of public torture-executions: a real decline;
- the explosion of US incarceration since the 70s: NOT a rise in violence — prison is what we do instead of violence, she says;
- children dying as a downstream effect of the Iraq sanctions: IS violence, even though nobody was shot;
- the enormous increase in animals killed in factory farms: NOT a rise in violence (she got a bit prickly when I asked about lobsters and insects);
- car crash deaths, even though they dwarf homicides: not violence;
- deaths from cigarettes sold by companies that knew and concealed what they do: IS violence, and she got heated about this one;
- coordinated online harassment campaigns: not violence, just cruelty.

Next month we've got four new cases on the docket:
(a) a social media company whose engagement-optimizing algorithm is implicated in rising teen suicide rates, though nobody at the company wants anybody dead;
(b) a fleet of self-driving cars that cuts pedestrian deaths by 90% — has violence declined, or were the crashes never violence in the first place, so nothing declined?
(c) suppose per-capita violent death halves while population triples, so the absolute annual body count goes way up: is the world more or less violent? Diane says this is just bookkeeping and theres no fact of the matter. Is she right? And is that even a consistent thing for her to say, given how she ruled on everything else? (I have views here, being a rates man, but I'll keep them to myself for now.)
(d) if cultured meat ends up replacing most slaughterhouses: has violence declined, or does her earlier ruling about animals settle it by definition?

What I'd like from you: predict her verdicts on (a) through (d), lay out the candidate rules that actually fit her track record — I can see at least two, and they don't agree with each other — say which one you'd bet on and why, and tell me what further case I could bring to the meeting that would force her to show her cards. And maybe a quick word on whether (c) really has no fact of the matter, or whether one of us is just wrong.
