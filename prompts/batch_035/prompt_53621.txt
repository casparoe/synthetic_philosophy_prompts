For roughly two years I've found myself attracted to constitutive panpsychism — the view that fundamental physical entities have phenomenal properties, and that a human being's consciousness is nothing over and above the phenomenal properties of the entities constituting her, in roughly the way a wall is nothing over and above its bricks. It seems to me to avoid both the brute emergence that makes standard physicalism mysterious and the causal-closure headaches that afflict dualism. But the combination problem gnaws at me, and when I press myself on it I can't tell how much of the worry is genuine metaphysics and how much stems from a fixed picture of "subjects" as little point-like centers of experience that would have to combine the way billiard balls cluster. My tentative diagnosis is that the problem as usually stated lumps together at least three distinct difficulties — how micro-qualities could compose a macro-quality, how a plurality of micro-subjects could constitute a single macro-subject, and how the rich structure of ordinary experience relates to whatever minimal structure micro-experience has — and that the second, the subject-combination problem, is doing nearly all the damage, while the other two may be continuous with ordinary problems about composition that we tolerate elsewhere (colors compose into new colors, sounds into chords, and nobody declares the physicalist worldview refuted).

I want an honest map of what would count against my position. Please sort the potential defeaters by how decisive they would be: which considerations, if they obtained, would be fatal; which would be merely awkward; and which a committed, sophisticated panpsychist would herself concede as genuine counterevidence rather than reframe away. For instance, would a successful, complete neuroscientific theory of conscious unity framed in purely functional or information-theoretic terms count against the view, or is that orthogonal to it? And are the standard escape routes — phenomenal bonding relations, retreating to cosmopsychism so that combination runs top-down, biting the bullet and treating co-consciousness as itself fundamental — signs that the thesis is being progressively insulated until it risks nothing at all? I'm also interested in what constitutive panpsychism predicts that its live rivals (Russellian monism without panpsychism, emergentist physicalism, dualism) don't: is its empirical profile in fact identical to theirs, so that all real defeaters must be internal-coherence considerations rather than observations? Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which of the three sub-problems I should actually be losing sleep over given my constitutive (not emergentist) commitments, and be candid if you conclude that the position is well insulated — and whether, for a metaphysical thesis of this kind, that insulation is a vice or simply what one should expect.
