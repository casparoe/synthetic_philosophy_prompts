I teach a master's-level seminar on archaeological method (16 students, one 120-minute session). Design an in-class activity that makes students feel how radically underdetermined the reconstruction of beliefs and institutions is by material remains alone. The activity should have teams work separately from a fabricated assemblage of small finds that you invent for the purpose; include at least one object deliberately built to be readable either as an economic artifact or a ritual one, and at least one whose most tempting reading just projects a familiar modern institution onto the past. Each team should commit to a confident reconstruction before any team sees another's, so that the divergence itself — several internally coherent but mutually incompatible accounts of the very same objects — does the teaching, with no lecture up front. The debrief should then map specific inferences the students made onto the standing problems: analogical reasoning from better-documented societies, the functional ambiguity of object types, and the quiet assumption that spatial association implies institutional connection. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong on a first run, and be explicit about which part of real archaeological inference no classroom version can reproduce and I must supply in discussion. Present the entire design as a single JSON object with the fields "assemblage", "materials", "rules", "timeline", "team_roles", "debrief", "predictable_failures", "unreproducible_feature", and "confidence"; in the last field, state how confident you are in your design choices overall and name the single choice you are least sure of, with a sentence on why.
