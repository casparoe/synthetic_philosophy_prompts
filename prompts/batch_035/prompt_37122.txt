Here is one student's answer, copied as-is:

"The grandfather paradox goes like this: suppose Tim travels back to 1920 with a rifle and intends to kill his grandfather before his father is conceived. If he succeeds, Tim is never born, so no one goes back to kill the grandfather, so Tim is born after all and does go back and kill him. That's a contradiction, so backward time travel is impossible. Lewis disagrees. He argues that the time traveler cannot change the past, because the past already includes everything the traveler did when he arrived — Tim was in 1920 all along, as far as 1920 is concerned. So there is just one consistent timeline and the supposed 'change' is an illusion. This means the traveler cannot affect the past at all, only fit into it. Lewis's solution is essentially the Novikov self-consistency principle from physics — the idea that only globally self-consistent histories are physically allowed — just restated in philosopher language. On this view Tim can't kill Grandfather: something is guaranteed to go wrong (the gun jams, he misses, he slips), and that's enforced by the consistency of the timeline itself. So the paradox is dissolved, and Lewis has shown that backward time travel is logically possible."
