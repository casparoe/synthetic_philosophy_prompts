Quick backstory so you know who you're talking to: I'm a philosophy professor, mostly mind and cognitive science these days, but I did my undergrad in control engineering and worked a couple years in process control before grad school. So state-space models, observers, setpoints, disturbance rejection, Lyapunov functions -- that's vocabulary I actually think in, and I find it increasingly annoying that the computationalism literature seems to go out of its way not to use any of it.

Here's what I want. Take the standard debate over whether the brain is a computer -- by which the literature means, roughly: the old-school view that cognition is symbol manipulation over syntactically structured representations, the worry (Searle-style) that syntax alone can't get you semantics, the connectionist pushback that maybe what matters is the dynamics of a big network of simple units rather than rule-governed symbol shuffling, the multiple realizability point that what makes something a computer is abstracted away from its physical stuff, and the pancomputationalism worry that if you characterize computation loosely enough then thermostats, hurricanes, and bucket brigades all compute and the thesis goes trivial. Now restat all of that in the vocabulary I came up in: plants and controllers, feedback and feedforward, observers and estimators, reference tracking, internal models, stability margins, etc. As faithful a translation as you can manage, position by position.

Then -- and this is the part I actually care about -- give me the residue report. What translates cleanly? Does the software/hardware distinction map onto anything like the controller/plant distinction, or does that analogy quietly break (since in a brain, unlike a refinery, the "plant" and the "controller" are made of the same stuff and there's no engineer who picked the setpoint)? Does the internal model principle -- the observation that a good controller has to model the thing it's controlling in some sense -- vindicate the computationalists, or does their notion of a "model of the world" do more work than a control theorist's internal model does? Can the connectionist/symbolicist fight be restated as model-free versus model-based control, or does that flatten it? And what has no expression at all in control vocabulary -- I suspect the semantics question is a candidate, since "what does this loop compute" only ever gets an answer relative to somebody's purposes in building it, but I want you to press on whether that's a real gap or whether control theory actually has resources (optimal control objectives? cost functions?) that I'm underrating.

Finally, tell me which way the residue cuts: is control vocabulary missing something the philosophy debate needs, or does the philosophy debate look less substantive once you try to say it in engineering terms? I've got my own suspicions but I'd genuinely like to know where you come down, and don't be polite about it -- I'd rather have a sharp answer I disagree with than a diplomatic one that hedges.
