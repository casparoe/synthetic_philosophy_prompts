Maya, for the record, is on the side of restricting the knowability principle to the so-called cartesian propositions, and she spent the last hour of the argument telling me my view is just the relativized reading — every truth is knowable by someone at some time — in fancy dress, which stung, because I'm genuinely not sure she's wrong.

My thesis, roughly: the standard escape hatches all quietly give up what the anti-realist actually cared about, since the point was never which propositions some arbitrarily located agent might someday come to know, it was whose practice fixes what our statements mean. My own line is that Fitch's proof only goes through because it treats the knower as a single idealized verifier who can know a whole conjunction at once — the step from possibly-K(p and not-Kp) to the contradiction needs one knower who simultanously knows p and knows that p is not known, and once you take knowers seriously as bounded, located, temporal agents (different people, different times, different faculties), the distribution of knowability over conjunction fails and the proof stalls. The catch, and the thing Maya and I could not settle at all: on my reading, group knowability stops being closed under conjunction, and maybe stops being factive?? And I can't tell whether that's a feature, a bug, or just me dodging the modal logic.
