My sister-in-law Renate is on the steering committee of a local voters' group, and next month they have to take a position on a state ballot measure about sex work law. The options on the table are basically the three you always hear about: the Nordic model (selling stays legal, buying gets criminalized), German-style legalization (licensed brothels, registration, mandatory health checks), and New Zealand-style decriminalization (treat it as ordinary work under normal labor and zoning law).

Renate has never said a word about sex work itself, but she has strong opinions about every market even slightly adjacent to it, and I've been quietly keeping notes. Her track record:

- Paid plasma donation: totally fine, she does it herself, thinks it's almost a civic duty.
- Paid participation in drug trials: fine, admirable even.
- Selling a kidney: against. Should be banned outright, "nobody should have to make that choice."
- Commercial surrogacy: against, but with a twist that I thought was interesting -- she said if anyone belongs in legal trouble it's the agencies and arguably the intended parents, never the surrogate. Altruistic surrogacy (expenses covered, no fee): fine.
- Paid egg donation: against, same reasoning as the kidney more or less.
- Porn performing: fine, the industry should exist, she just wants performers to have unions and workplace safety rules like anyone else.
- Camming: fine, and she actually gets annoyed when platforms impose extra rules beyond normal employment law.
- Rent-a-friend / paid companionship services: fine, she thinks they're kind of sweet.

The one that keeps bugging me is the plasma one -- lots of people think paying poor people for plasma is exactly the kind of exploitative market she objects to elsewhere, but she's completely relaxed about it, even donates herself. So I don't trust my first guess at what her principle is.

I've got a bet riding on predicting her vote before the meeting. Can you help me think it through? What I want is:

1. What rule (or rules) actually fits her verdicts? I can see at least a body-parts-versus-services story, a regulate-the-risky-venues story, and something about going after the demand side while never blaming the seller, but I can't tell which is really her, or which verdicts each one has to strain to explain.
2. What does each of those rules predict for the three ballot options?
3. Your best guess at which she picks, and why that rule beats the others on her record.
4. The single further question or case I could spring on her at dinner that would best split the remaining rules -- like, something adjacent she hasn't been asked about yet, where the candidate rules give different answers.

No fair just telling me "it's complicated and depends on her values," I know that, that's why I need the bet settled.
