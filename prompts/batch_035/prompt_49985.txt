I am preparing a position statement on material constitution for a metaphysics workshop, and before I circulate it I would like a consistency check on the package of views I have ended up holding. Here is the set:

1. Goliath (the statue) and Lumpl (the piece of clay it is made of) are numerically distinct. My reason is the modal argument: Lumpl could survive being squashed into a ball, Goliath could not, so by Leibniz's Law they cannot be identical.
2. Their distinctness is not rooted in any spatial or material difference. Throughout their period of coexistence they occupy exactly the same region and are composed of exactly the same atoms.
3. Nothing has a modal profile that floats free of the categorical facts. Which persistence conditions and essential properties a thing has is fixed by the actual, non-modal facts, together with at most the sortal under which the thing is counted.
4. I am a realist about de re modality. Objects genuinely have persistence conditions and essences; these are features of the world, not projections of our classificatory practices.
5. I deny unrestricted composition. There is no object composed of my left shoe and the Eiffel Tower, and I take the lesson of this to be that ontology is not cheap: an object must earn its place.
6. On the case itself: when the sculptor later crushes the clay and reworks it into a different statue, I want to say that Lumpl survives and Goliath does not, and I want this to be an objective fact about what happened, not a bookkeeping decision we make after the fact.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a survey. Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which pairs only look to be in tension, and what the cheapest repair would be for each real tension, including what the repair costs me elsewhere in the set. I would particularly like to know whether any of my case verdicts in (6) is something my other commitments simply cannot deliver. At the end, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, if revision is needed at all. Please order the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally serious.
