Quick context: I'm a second-year PhD student, and a paper of mine just got accepted at a workshop on consciousness and cognitive science (UK, so this whole thing needs to be in British English, by the way). The paper is a kind of deflationary line on the access/phenomenal consciousness split. My claim is basically that the distinction, as Block draws it, does no real explanatory work: once you grant that GWT-style global availability explains reportability, integration, flexible control, and all the things we can actually test, the leftover "phenomenal consciousness" isn't a phenomenon but an artefact of how the concepts work — something like what happens when you ask for all the facts about a system and then demand one more fact that isn't about anything. So I'm not just saying access and phenomenal happen to coincide in us; I'm saying the further thing was never a target for explanation in the first place. I lean on the usual suspects — Sperling-style partial report doesn't show overflow, it shows fragile nonconscious buffers; change blindness and the postdictive stuff show the "richness" is an confabulated reconstruction; even Block's super-blindsight patient, if you build them carefully, is just someone with degraded but real access.

And one theorist in the middle who has published on the distinction between the workspace and the broadcast stage, which I honestly don't fully understand yet and probably should before I open my mouth.
