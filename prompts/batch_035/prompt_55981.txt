a bit of background: i'm a grad student and i've been asked to write a short "where i stand" piece for a seminar on formal epistemology, on dutch book arguments specifically. before i send it in i want a sanity check, because each thing i believe seems fine to me on its own, but i have a nagging feeling some subset of them is fighting and i genuinely can't tell which. here's the set. (1) i think the dutch book argument for the probability axioms is sound: an agent whose credences violate the axioms is thereby irrational, and the theorem establishes this. (2) that said, i read the argument as a consistency display rather than a point about money — rationality can't require caring about money, so what bookability really reveals is that the agent's degrees of belief can't be jointly realized, the way you can't simultaneously treat three options as mutually exclusive and exhaustive and yet rule none of them out. (3) in a million-ticket lottery i think it's perfectly rational to be fully confident, credence 1, that ticket #487,231 loses — that's just how anyone treats lotteries, nobody hedges on a specific ticket — while of course knowing full well that exactly one ticket wins. (4) when the evidence is sparse and pulls in different directions, i think the right response is sometimes an imprecise credence, a set of distributions or a range rather than a sharp number, and forcing a single number in that situation is epistemic theater. (5) i reject the diachronic dutch book argument for condtionalization: to place the middle bet the bookie has to know your future credences, and once you allow that you can book the conditionalizer in reverse just as easily, so the argument proves too much. (6) and i take the old ramsey idea seriuosly, that betting odds are basically a scale for measuring degree of belief, which is part of why i think of credences as genuine psychological states and not just bookkeeping.

what i want from you: go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension, which only look like they are, and rank the problems by severity rather than dumping them on me as all equally bad. for each real one, say what would have to give and roughly what that costs me. then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. be specific about which commitments are actually doing the fighting — don't just tell me generally that bayesianism has problems, i've sat through that lecture already. and please no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables, just continuous prose like an email.
