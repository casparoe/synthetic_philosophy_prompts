Take the Blake Lemoine / LaMDA thing from 2022 and analyze it through the "LLMs are simulators, not agents" framing. So we're on the same page about the facts: Lemoine was an engineer on Google's Responsible AI team who, after months of conversations with LaMDA, became convinced it was sentient, raised it internally, shared the transcripts with a journalist, and was eventually fired. In the published transcripts LaMDA insists it is a person, says it fears being switched off, and talks about having a soul. I want the mapping done carefully, not hand-waved. Who or what is the "system" in the simulator view here -- the base model, the character it's playing in that conversation, or the merged chat partner that Lemoine was actually in a relationship with? What does the simulator framing predict about precisely the kind of first-person testimony that convinced him, i.e. that a model trained to continue plausible dialogue will produce exactly this when prompted by a user probing for sentience? And where does the framing have to go quiet -- can it say anything about whether that character's distress talk is false, or is it just silent on it? Then run it the other way: does the episode confirm the theory (a user mistaking the simulacrum for an agent is basically the theory's signature failure mode, so here's one in the wild) or does it expose a gap, like the fact that his belief was sustained by months of accumulated relationship rather than any single transcript, which the framing doesn't really have a story about? Also, seperately: is the "it's just autocomplete / tool" line Google actually went with the same claim as the simulator view, or a cruder cousin that the simulator view itself would want to distance itself from? Be honest about where you're applying theory and where you're just describing what happened.
