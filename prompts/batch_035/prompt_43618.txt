I read a lot of history of science and argue about it on forums, and I've noticed a thing I can't find the right word for. Sometimes two rival explanations predict exactly the same observations for a long stretch — like the sun-centered system versus Tycho's old hybrid where everything orbits the sun except the earth, which just sits there while the sun circles it. For a good while no instrument could split them. Or Lorentz's version of the aether versus special relativity in the early 1900s: same numbers coming out of both for years, and the community ended up choosing partly on feel — which construction seemed less contrived, which had fewer unexplained knobs, which fit with what people already believed about space.

But that's not the part I want the name for. Everyone knows ties sometimes get broken by something other than data. The part that bugs me is that the tie-breaker itself is up for grabs, different camps pick different tie-breakers, and the tie-breakers people pick look inherited from the last theory they'd already committed to. The Copernicans scored the contest on "fewer moving parts," the other side scored it on "don't move the earth without direct proof," and each side thought the other's scoreboard was rigged. And here's the compounding bit: whichever way it gets settled, the winning scoreboard becomes the default scoreboard for the next dispute. So it feels less like "evidence picks the theory" and more like a lineage of judgment calls, each one setting the default settings for the next one, with the evidence sitting on top of this accumulated ratchet of prior choices rather than at the bottom where I was taught it lives.

I know philosophers have written a ton about how you can always blame your instruments instead of your theory when a prediction fails, but that seems to be about rescuing a theory from failure. My thing is about picking between two theories that both succeed, and specifically about the meta-level where the selection criteria themselves aren't fixed by anything observational — plus the ratchet part where choices compound. So: is there an established concept (or cluster of concepts) for this? Is it the same thing as the blame-the-equipment stuff in a different hat, or is there a seperate literature on the tie-breaking and the compounding? Don't just hand me a term and walk away — I want to know what the thing is, what it's not, and where the edges are. For instance, does it still count as my thing when the rivals eventually become testable and one just wins outright? And if nobody's named the ratchet part, try to name and define it yourself, with some boundary cases. Also please be upfront about how confident you are in each part of the answer and tell me which bit you're least sure of — my gut says some of this is genuinely contested and I'd rather know where.
