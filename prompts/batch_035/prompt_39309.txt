I'm on the board of a small neighborhood association — like 60 houses, we share a community garden and a tool shed and put on a couple events a year. There's this rule that goes back to when the place was founded in the late 70s: the garden plots get reassigned by lottery every spring instead of going to whoever wants them or to the people who've had them longest. Everyone complains about it. People who put three years of work into improving a soil can lose their plot to a random draw, and there's a waitlist anyway so it's not like the lottery is keeping anyone out. At the last meeting I moved to switch to a simple system: returning gardeners keep their plots, new folks get whatever's left. It lost, mostly because the older members voted against it as a bloc, and when I asked why, the best answer I got was "it's worked so far," plus one guy telling me to ask him again in ten years. The actual founders are dead or moved away, so nobody really knows why the lottery exists.

I still think I'm right on the merits. But it bugged me that the only thing I could say back was "that's not a reason." My brother-in-law says the fact that I can't state what the rule is FOR is exactly why I should go slow — his line was that reformers are the worst judges of the customs they want to reform, and that if the reason for a practice were obvious it wouldn't have needed to be a practice in the first place. That felt a little convenient, but also not nothing?

So my questions. Is there a principled way to think this through — some point at which I've earned the right to say "we've looked, there's no reason, change it," versus the fact that a practice survived for 50 years and the people living under it resist changing it counting as real evidence about something I can't see? Is that kind of resistance ever carrying information, or is it just loss aversion plus inertia dressed up as wisdom? And is there anything concrete I can do — some trial, some question I should put to the old guard, some failure mode I should watch for over a season or two — that would settle it, or at least tell me whether I'm the clear-eyed one here or the bull in the china shop? Honestly the part I want most is a way to tell the difference between the two in myself, because I suspect everyone in my position thinks they're the clear-eyed one.
