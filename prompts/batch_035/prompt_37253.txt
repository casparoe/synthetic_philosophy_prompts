I'm writing a term paper for a philosophy of mind course in which we've just covered Dennett's taxonomy of kinds of minds: Darwinian creatures, whose behavior is fixed by genetic variation and environmental selection; Skinnerian creatures, which condition on overt trial and error; Popperian creatures, which preview candidate behaviors internally and let bad options perish offline rather than in the world; and Gregorian creatures, which import cultural mind-tools like words, diagrams, and notation to restructure their own thinking. I'd like to run AlphaGo's 2016 match against Lee Sedol through this taxonomy, focusing on Move 37 in game 2 (the " shoulder hit" that human commentators initially scored as a mistake because it looked like something no professional would play) and Lee's Move 78 in game 4 (the wedge that turned the game and that AlphaGo reportedly assigned a vanishingly small probability until it appeared on the board).

Please write the analysis as a two-way case study: theory illuminating case, and case testing theory. Some things I specifically want you to be disciplined about rather than hand-waving: what exactly counts as AlphaGo's "behavior" and its "selection environment" at each stage (training on human games, self-play, and live play with tree search); whether self-play makes the training process itself Popperian even before the system ever previews moves in a match; whether AlphaGo is Gregorian in any nontrivial sense, given that its training data is the accumulated culture of human Go and that the DeepMind engineers who built it are plainly Gregorian creatures themselves; and what the taxonomy implies about Lee Sedol, if anything, when his winning move was precisely one that AlphaGo had not taken seriously in its previewing. Where the taxonomy is silent or misdescribes the case — for instance, if it was only ever meant for evolved or learning organisms and stretches too far to cover a system whose "hypotheses" are value networks and search trees — say so plainly instead of forcing the fit. Conclude with a verdict: does the AlphaGo episode vindicate Dennett's hierarchy as a graded story about sophistication, expose a missing category, or show that the taxonomy tracks the *provenance* of a mind rather than what it can do in play?
