I want a thought experiment for a paper on whether interpretability research discovers genuine representations in neural networks or merely projects a convenient vocabulary onto them. Construct a case in which two networks are alike in every respect an interpretability researcher could measure, yet differ in training history, such that a causal-role account of representation says they have the same contents while a selected-functions (teleosemantic) account says only one of them represents anything. My sketch: Network A is trained by gradient descent on natural images and develops an edge-detection circuit that its training history selected for; Network B is a distillation of A pushed until it matches A's input-output behavior — including, as it happens, an internal circuit structurally identical to A's — but nothing in B's history selected that circuit for detecting edges; it is a copy, the way a forged painting is a copy. Requirements: the scenario must be realistic (model zoos, distillation, and checkpoint recovery make something like it happen routinely) and stated in a single paragraph, and it must not stipulate which network really represents — the verdicts have to fall out of the theories, not be built into the story. After the scenario, please do four things: (1) show what probing, causal interventions, and behavioral tests would each find in the two networks, and why every such test is blind to the difference; (2) identify which feature of the case is doing the work, and say how the verdicts change if that feature is removed; (3) give the best realist reply — for instance that content is fixed by deployment history, that B's training is itself a selection process of a kind, or that fine-tuning B on real images would eventually give its circuits content — and assess how strong it is; and (4) tell me honestly whether this is just Swampman with GPUs, and if so whether the interpretability framing adds anything the classic debate could not — for example, whether the practical predictive and interventional success of probing gives us grounds to prefer causal-role over teleosemantic accounts that the original Swampman case could never supply.
