I got into Indian philosophy thru some youtube lectures and then fell down a wikipedia rabbit hole on the pramāṇas, so I've been working this out on my own and now I want to know if my views actually hang together. Heres roughly where I've landed:

1. Perception, inference, and testimony are the genuine ways of knowing, and that list should be exhaustive — anything that looks like a distinct way of knowing should turn out to be one of these three wearing a costume.
2. Upamāna (the one classical Nyāya counted as a fourth pramāṇa — being told a gavaya is like a cow, then later seeing a gavaya and knowing what it is) is bogus as a separate pramāṇa. When you see the gavaya you're remembering cows and inferring from the trusted instruction. It's inference plus memory, nothing new.
3. But arthāpatti, postulation, IS genuinely its own pramāṇa. Classic example: the guy who's fat but doesn't eat during the day, so you conclude he must eat at night. That conclusion kind of forces itself on you in a way ordinary inference doesn't.
4. Tarka — reductio reasoning, showing a view is false because it leads to absurdity — actually gives you knowledge. You can come to KNOW something is false this way, not just get pointed toward looking for a real pramāṇa.
5. You should trust your cognitions by default. Doubt needs a specific ground — something looks off, conflicting reports, whatever — before it's rational to withhold assent.
6. At the same time, you only really know a cognition is TRUE once it's been checked, once later experience pans out or you get corroboration. Before that it's more like a strong candidate.

I honestly can't tell whether there's a real problem hiding in this set or whether it all sits together fine, and if there are problems I don't know which one is the bad one. So go through it and tell me which commitments are actually in tension and how badly. Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, say what would have to give for each and what it costs, and flag anything that only LOOKS like a tension but dissolves once you think about it. Don't just say "it's all fine" or "it's all contradictory" — discriminate. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my list back at me.
