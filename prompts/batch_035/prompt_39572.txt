So my friend and I had one of those arguements last night that starts over coffee and somehow ends at midnight. The topic was the regress of justification. My position, which I defended way worse than I should have: a kind of foundationalism, but not the old-school infallibilist introspection stuff — instead, fallibilist foundationalism, where some perceptual beliefs get a sort of default justification straight from experience. That justification can be defeated, and it can be used to support other beliefs, but the basic beliefs themselves don't get their status from further beliefs behind them. She's a coherentist and kept hammering one move all night: if my basic beliefs have enough content to justify anything else, then they can be challenged, and once challenged they need support of their own — so the regress is back. And if they can't be challenged, they're too thin to justify anything. We went in circles on this and I honestly don't think I handled it well.
