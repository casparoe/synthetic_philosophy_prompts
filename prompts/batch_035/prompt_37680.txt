Can you analyze the Silicon Valley Bank run of March 2023 as a coordination game where the whole thing turns on common knowledge of rationality? The facts as I understand them, correct me if I've got them wrong: almost all deposits were above the $250k FDIC cap, mostly tech payroll and operating accounts; the bank announced a loss on its bond portfolio and a failed capital raise; a handful of well-known venture capitalists then said publicly, on Twitter, that founders should pull their money out immediately; depositors attempted something like $42 billion in withdrawals in a single day; regulators seized it that night and eventually guaranteed all deposits, including the uninsured ones. Map it carefully: who the players actually are, why the payoffs have the structure where everyone stays in if everyone else stays in and runs if others run, and what exactly the VC tweets did -- did they convert mere mutual or probabilistic knowledge into common knowledge, or does Rubinstein's email-game point (that even very high-order mutual knowledge short of common knowledge can fail to coordinate) not really bite here since the signal was a few named people speaking publicly, in the open, for anyone to see? Also worth asking whether the VCs were themselves players choosing an equilibrium rather than just messengers. Then be honest about where the model breaks: does a theory built for repeated play among patient agents have anything to say about a run that completes in hours over Twiter, and does the ex post guarantee of uninsured deposits show the game was mis-specified (the payoffs were never what depositors took them to be) or just that an outside player with rule-changing power stepped in and moved the equilibrium? Flag anything the theory is just silent on rather than forcing an answer. Give me your confidence in the main claims and single out the step in the analysis you're least sure of.
