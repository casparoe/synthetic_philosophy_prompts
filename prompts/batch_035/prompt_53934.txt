I've recently gotten involved in a tenants' association here in Manchester, and it's forced me to actually think about organisations for the first time instead of just grumbling about them, and that led me to Simone Weil's 1943 essay on abolishing political parties (I read it over the holidays after a friend recommended it). I've drafted the outline of an essay I want to write about it, partly for our association's internal newsletter but partly just to get my own head straight, and I'd really like feedback on it. Please respond in British English, by the way -- I know I slip sometimes and I'd like the reply consistent.

The core of my draft is this: Weil's claim isn't just that parties are corrupt or badly run, it's that they're structurally a kind of machine for whipping up and channelling collective passion, so that even a party founded on perfectly good ideas ends up serving the party rather than the ideas. Her reasoning, as I understood it, is that once you join a collective body, you feel the group's opinions as if they were your own, with a force and certainty you'd never feel alone -- and no one around you notices the lies you tell, because they're telling the same ones. So the party member ends up sincerely believing things he'd see through in a second on his own. My essay then takes the step Weil herself doesn't: I argue that this logic doesn't stop at political parties, it applies to any association where loyalty is the glue -- unions, churches, my tenants' association, and I suspect also the informal consensus of a research field or a newsroom. I claim the deciding factor is whether the body's stated purpose can, in principle, be used to dissolve the body -- a union could vote itself unnecessary if workers were protected some other way, but a party by her definition can never declare its work finished, because its purpose is its own growth.

What I'd like from you, roughly in order of importance: (1) have I got Weil right, or am I smuggling in things she didn't say? In particular, I'm hazy on her distinction between parties and other collective bodies -- she seemed to have some criterion about legitimate collective bodies that I'm glossing over, and if flattening it ruins her argument I want to know; (2) is my extension step actually valid, or is there a disanalogy between parties and looser associations that saves the latter from her critique? Our association has maybe 40 members and no whips, so part of me wants to believe we're safe; (3) honestly, does any of this seem worth publishing beyond the newsletter, or is it just warmed-over Weil? Don't hold back on that last one.
