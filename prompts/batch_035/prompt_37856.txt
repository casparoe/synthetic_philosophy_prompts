My best friend and I had one of those arguments last weekend that runs for four hours and ends nowhere. I don't even fully disagree with her bottom line, but something about using the slogan as a conversation-ender bugged me, especially because an hour later she was defending mandatory vaccination on the grounds that what you do with your body stops being only your business when other people's lives are at stake. And for that matter she also thinks selling your own kidney should stay illegal, even though that's your body too, apparently.

So now I'm trying to figure out what "my body, my choice" even is as a claim. Is it saying the fetus is literally part of her body, which seems like it just asserts the thing the entire debate is about? Or is it saying that even if the fetus is a separate person with a right to life, nobody can be forced to let another being use their body — the violinist-style point that you're allowed to refuse to be a Good Samaritan even when refusing costs a life? Those are wildly different claims and I suspect only one of them does any real work. And then there's the question of which versions of the slogan, if any, are consistent with her views on vaccines and kidney sales — is she just being inconsistent, or is the slogan on most readings way narrower than people think, like it's really an anti-compulsory-pregnancy claim and not a general body-ownership principle at all? I'd honestly never noticed it being used both as a total answer to abortion and as obviously inapplicable everywhere else.

Can you lay out the different things "my body, my choice" could actually be claiming, from the uncontroversial readings to the genuinely radical ones, and for each one say whether it's plausible, and whether it would actually justify using the slogan to shut down the conversation the way she did?
