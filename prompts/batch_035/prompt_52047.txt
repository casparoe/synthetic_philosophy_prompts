I'm part of a small reading group that spent this term on the foundations of thermodynamics, and last week's meeting ended in a standoff between two members, Dana and Elias, over Maxwell's demon. The demon — the tiny being that operates a frictionless door between the two halves of a gas container, letting fast molecules through one way and slow ones the other, and so apparently lowering the gas's entropy without doing any work — had come up in our discussion of the second law. Their exchange went roughly as follows, and none of the rest of us could settle it.

(1) Dana said the demon is harmless once you take its memory seriously. To sort, the demon must measure molecular speeds, record the results, and — since its memory is finite — eventually erase earlier records to make room, and erasing one bit dissipates at least kT ln 2 of energy as heat into the surroundings. That dissipation, she said, exactly cancels the work the demon's sorting can extract, so the second law is safe and the paradox is resolved.

(2) Elias replied that this is circular. Landauer's principle — the claim that erasure necessarily costs kT ln 2 per bit — is itself derived within thermodynamics or statistical mechanics that already presupposes the second law. So the argument has the shape "the demon cannot violate the second law because erasure is costly, and erasure is costly because of the second law," and that, he said, is not a resolution of the paradox but a restatement of it with extra machinery.

(3) Dana answered that the derivation of the erasure cost needs no thermodynamic input at all. Erasure is a many-to-one operation on the demon's memory: many distinct physical states of the recording device are driven onto a single standard state. But the dynamics governing the demon and its memory is Hamiltonian, and Hamiltonian dynamics preserves phase-space volume, so a many-to-one compression of the memory's states is impossible unless the displaced phase volume is exported somewhere — and it is exported as heated surroundings. That, she insisted, is a theorem of mechanics, obtained with no appeal to the second law, so there is no circle.

(4) Elias was unmoved. The phase-volume argument, he said, only tracks fine-grained entropy, and fine-grained entropy is constant for every closed Hamiltonian system, demon or no demon — so on its own it cannot distinguish a demon that successfully sorts molecules from one that bungles the job. The impossibility of sorting shows up only when you add a coarse-graining assumption about which macrostates count, and that assumption, he claimed, is precisely where the second law is smuggled back in under another name. He closed with a parting shot: in any case, nothing forces the demon to erase at all — give it unbounded memory and it can keep sorting indefinitely, one bit of record per operation, forever.

They asked me to referee, and I honestly couldn't. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move.
