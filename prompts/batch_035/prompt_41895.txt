My brother is in the army and got deployed last year, and it's been eating at me ever since. Quick background so you get the situation: our country went to war over a border region that's been disputed for decades. There was no attack on us — the government just decided the time was right to take the region by force, some mix of nationalism and wanting the resources there. I've read enough about it too think the war is plainly unjust, a war of choice started by our side.

Here's what I can't sort out. My brother insists that as a soldier his hands are basically clean either way: soldiers fight, that's the job, and soldiers on both sides are equally legitimate targets for each other no matter which side's cause is just. He says this like it's the obvious common-sense view that everyone held forever. But a friend of mine who studies philosophy told me something that stuck: if the war is unjust, then everyone my brother helps kill dies for the sake of something that should never have happened at all — so how could his fighting not be wrong? And by the same logic, the soldiers on the other side shooting at him are doing something closer to ordinary self-defense than he is, even though nobody likes to say that out loud.

So I want your read on the actual situation, not just abstractions. Is my brother doing something wrong by fighting in this war at all? Does it matter that he enlisted years before this particular war was on anyone's radar, and that he'd face prison if he refused to deploy now? And is the soldier on the other side who shoots at him in a genuinely different moral position from my brother when he shoots back — or are they, as my brother says, just two soldiers morally equal to each other? My friend says that can't possibly be right if the war itself is a crime, and honestly I don't know who to believe. It's not academic for me: it's about whether I should be proud of my brother or be trying to talk him into refusing to go.
