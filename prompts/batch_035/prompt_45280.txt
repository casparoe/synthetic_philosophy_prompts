I spent thirty-five years running condensed-matter experiments before retiring, and I have spent the past two years reading philosophy of physics more carefully than I ever did as a working scientist — Bell's papers, the interpretations literature, a little general philosophy of science. I have now written down what I actually believe about quantum mechanics, and I would like an audit of whether the set hangs together. Tell me which pairs of my commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and, for each genuine one, what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most. Here is the set:

1. I am a scientific realist about the unobservable posits of successful theories: electrons, the electromagnetic field, spacetime curvature. The empirical success of those posits would be miraculous if they were merely predictive bookkeeping, and I do not believe in miracles.

2. The quantum state, however, is not in that category. It is a representation of our information about a system, so that collapse is nothing more than updating on new data, the way a Bayesian updates on the result of a measurement. The wavefunction is not a physical field sitting in configuration space.

3. Bell's theorem, together with the loophole-closing experiments, settles that nature itself is nonlocal: no theory of purely local physical influences can reproduce the observed statistics for entangled pairs. I take this to be a fact about the world, not a fact about our description of it.

4. Systems prepared independently in different laboratories have independent underlying physical states — no conspiratorial holistic correlations in the underlying stuff between two labs that were not put there by the preparations themselves. This is part of why I regard the residual loopholes in the Bell experiments as closed in principle.

5. Every measurement has a single, definite outcome, and which outcome occurs is genuinely chancy. I regard both many-worlds branching and Bohmian hidden variables as surplus metaphysical machinery, and spontaneous-collapse dynamics as an ad hoc repair.

One thing that nags me: I have read that there is a theorem — I believe it goes by the initials PBR — that is supposed to put pressure on views like my (2). I do not want to be spared; if that theorem, or anything else, makes the package untenable, tell me where. My own tentative thought is that the soft spot is (3): if the quantum state is only information, then perhaps the nonlocality of the state is not nonlocality of the world, and my statement of (3) overreaches. But I am not confident that this thought does any real work rather than merely relocating the problem, and I would like to know which it is.

A request about the form of your answer: as you go, indicate how confident you are in each claim you make, and at the end single out the point in your own audit that you are least sure of, with a word about what further argument or evidence would settle it.
