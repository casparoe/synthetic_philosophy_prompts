I've been arguing with myself about how we should judge historical figures, and I've written down where I've landed, but I suspect the pieces don't fit together and I can't see where. Here is my position:

1. Morality is not relative to eras: slavery was just as wrong in 1750 as it is now, no matter what nearly everyone believed in 1750.
2. Even so, the "man of his time" defence usually works. It is unfair to blame someone for failing to see a moral truth that almost nobody in their society could see, and that applies to Jefferson on slavery as much as to anyone else.
3. When we judge people by the standards of their era, we should use the best standards available in that era, not the average ones — abolitionists existed in Jefferson's own world, so he had the position available to him and can be faulted for declining it.
4. Moral progress is real: we did not merely change our preferences about slavery; through argument and struggle we came to recognize something that had been true all along.
5. I fully expect people in 2300 to condemn my generation for some grave wrong we do not recognize, and I think that condemnation will be deserved.

Points 2 and 3 feel like they pull against each other, though I can't tell whether 2 defeats 3 or the other way around. But I also have a nagging worry that something else in this set is broken — for instance, I don't want point 2 to end up excusing my own generation from the judgment in point 5, yet I can't see how to stop it from doing that. And separately: Columbus feels worse to me than Jefferson, and I've noticed that Columbus was denounced by his own countrymen within a generation of his voyages, which on my view would leave him less of an excuse — but I'm not sure my principles actually deliver that verdict or whether I'm just rationalizing a gut feeling.

Please go through the set, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and say what the cheapest fix would be for each and what it costs. Then end with a final line of the form "Verdict: " naming the single commitment I should revise or drop first.
