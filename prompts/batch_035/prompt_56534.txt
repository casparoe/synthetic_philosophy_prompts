i'm writing about the heterogeneity problem for hedonism (why does a warm bath, solving a proof, and malicious glee all count as pleasure when they seem to share nothing?) and before I commit to my own answer I want the spec first: what would an adequate theory of what pleasures have in common actually have to deliver? write me the desiderata with the case or consideration behind each, and tell me which ones are negotiable.

some things I want on the list, feel free to add or push back on them: it has to cover the sensory/attitudinal divide (the smell of coffee vs. pleasure at the thought that your rival failed), it has to say something about the masochist and about anticipated pleasures, it can't just be "whatever we're motivated to continue" because that looks circular once the utilitarian tries to use it for aggregation, and it can't collapse into one giant disjunction that just restates the data (butlers old complaint about that, and moore's jab that a definition which says pleasure is the good is only interesting if it's not a bare list). also it should respect that pleasure isn't the same as absence of pain, and that we routinely take pleasure in false things — does truth matter or not?

flag what I most want to see: which pairs of desiderata are jointly unsatisfiable, e.g. the demand that the account be explanatorily deep enough to do work in ethics vs. the demand that it stay ontologically innocent enough for a physicalist to accept. and say which single requirement knocks out the most popular existing answers (the hedonic tone view, feldman-style attitudinal accounts, family resemblance stuff) — but keep the scoring to a paragraph or two, the criteria are the part I care about. purpose to fix adequacy against: whatever else it does, it has to let a utilitarian actually aggregate welfare with it, so bare conceptual bookkeeping isnt enough.
