As a journalist covering science and ideas, I want a satirical piece for our comment pages, and the target is a real weakness in the emerging trade in aquatic-animal welfare: the gap between what a certification scheme promises on paper and what a shrimp or a farmed fish actually experiences. Concretely, please write a parody corporate press release, around 550 words, announcing the launch of a fictitious company's premium welfare scheme for farmed whiteleg shrimp — call the company something bland and plausible, invented rather than real. The voice should be earnest trade-press PR: headline, dateline, three or four paragraphs of body copy, a bullet list of scheme features, and a closing quote from a fictional "Head of Crustacean Compassion".

The humour must come from real content rather than invention, so the piece should quietly exaggerate features that actually characterise this space. To that end, work in the following, each bent into a selling point: the now-familiar finding that decapod crustaceans satisfy standard evidence criteria for sentience, which the 2022 UK animal sentience legislation finally acknowledged — here recast as an exciting market opportunity; eyestalk ablation of breeding females, presented as a "selective enhancement" while the release never says what it is; in-water electrical stunning marketed as a premium add-on that farms can opt into at a higher tier, with basic-tier animals still going onto ice; stocking density and water quality expressed entirely as throughput and yield metrics; and an audit regime that visits a sample of ponds, the size of the sample left unstated. Somewhere the release should note, with pride, that the scheme covers more individual animals than any welfare scheme in history — a nod to the fact that farmed shrimp are counted in the hundreds of billions, so any per-animal improvement is dwarfed by the number of animals — without the writer appearing to notice what he has just said. If you can do it without breaking the deadpan, include one clause where the copywriter's enthusiasm briefly outruns the biology, e.g. promising shrimp a chance to express natural behaviours that the company has yet to identify.

Keep the irony legible but never wink: no sly asides, no exclamation marks signalling the joke, and no explicit editorialising. A reader who knows the sector should find it fair — each exaggerated claim should be recognisably a softened version of something schemes actually do — while a general reader should find it believable enough to be unsettling. British English throughout, please.
