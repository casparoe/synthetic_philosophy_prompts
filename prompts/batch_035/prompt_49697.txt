I've been asked to write a short position statement on objective chance for a metaphysics workshop, in a format where the other participants get to attack whatever we submit. Before I send mine, I'd like a consistency check on the package itself. Here are the five things I plan to commit to:

1. Deference, strongly. I accept the Principal Principle in a strong form: conditional on the true hypothesis about the chances, together with any other admissible information, one's credence in an outcome should equal its chance. And I read admissibility broadly: any information that concerns only the past, or the laws, or the chances themselves, counts as admissible.

2. Humean chance. There are no primitive propensities. Chances are fixed by whichever system of regularities best balances simplicity, strength, and fit against the total pattern of actual events.

3. Testimony about the future. If a source I know to be perfectly reliable tells me how some future events will in fact turn out — that the next hundred tosses of a certain coin will include exactly seventy heads, say — I think that testimony is admissible. It is not information about chances or about laws; it is information about plain matters of fact, and I can see no principled reason to bar it. And obviously I should believe it.

4. Time-relativity. Chances attach to event-and-time pairs: the chance-at-t of an outcome. Whenever I speak of the chance of something, I must mean its chance as of some time; there is no time-independent fact of the matter.

5. The covered coin. A coin was tossed last Tuesday and immediately covered, and nobody has looked. I hold that the chance, now, that it landed heads is one half — not zero, not one — and I would like my deference principle to be what explains the credence of one half that all of us in fact have.

I have a feeling that something in here doesn't cohere, but I can't tell whether I have one deep problem, several shallow ones, or nothing at all. Please go through the commitments, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what it would cost me to give up which of the implicated commitments. If a single revision would clean up more of the mess than the others, say so — but do the analysis first, since I would rather be pushed by the arguments than by my own suspicions.
