I sit on the internal ethics review panel at a mid-size ML research org (we mostly do RL for robotics and games, nothing frontier-scale). A team has come to us with a proposal for a long-horizon RL project where agents get dense negative rewards -- not just failure signals at the end of an episode, but continuous "pain" channels that penalize things like collision, battery depletion, and (this is the part that got people talking) a shaping term that punishes the agent for violating a learned model of its own physical integrity during training. One of our members, who did her PhD on animal welfare science before switching fields, is pushing for what she calls a negative-reward budget: a hard cap on cumulative negative reward per training run, plus mandatory logging of "aversive load," modeled on humane endpoints in animal research. She says we don't need to settle whether the agents suffer, we just need the policy to be defensible if they someday might -- she compares it to how the precautionary attitude toward invertebrate welfare got ahead of the science. Another member thinks this is category confusion dressed up as caution: reward is a number in a loss calculation, negative reward isn't the negation of anything it's *like* to be something, and a budget would just degrade research quality to signal virtue toward an entity with the interiority of a spreadsheet. A third guy keeps landing somewhere in between -- he says the interesting question is whether the *temporal structure* matters, i.e., whether a policy that acts to avoid negative reward over long horizons, with a world model that represents that avoidance, is already a minimal valence system even if nothing else about it is mind-like -- but he won't say what follows from that for our policy, which isn't helping.

For context if it's useful: the agents are model-based, trained for millions of episodes, and the designers deliberately built asymmetries in -- negative rewards are faster and more temporally precise than positive ones (they said this speeds learning, which is... honestly a little on the nose). Nobody on the panel disputes the engineering facts, only what they add up to. We also can't lean on "no persistent agent across episodes" as a clean out, because in the current proposal a single policy is preserved across a continuous simulated lifespan within each run, even though weights reset between runs.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate before our Thursday meeting. It needs to recommend a concrete course of action -- adopt the budget as proposed, adopt some modified version, reject it, or something else the team could actually implement and verify -- with the reasoning written so both factions could recognize their best point in it, not just the weakest version of the side that loses. Please take an actual position rather than listing considerations, and be upfront about where the argument depends on contested assumptions (e.g., functionalist views about valence) so we know which leg the recommendation stands on if those assumptions turn out wrong. No reading lists needed -- I want the reasoning, not the bibliography. And if you think the whole framing smuggles something in -- like assuming the animal-research analogy is the right template at all -- say so and say what you'd substitute.
