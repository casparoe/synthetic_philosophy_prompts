For three years I have sat on my city's commemorative review panel, and I have kept a private list of my verdicts in the hope of finding the principle behind them. The cases, with my gut answers:

1. A Confederate general's statue erected in the 1920s, decades after the war, at a time and place where such monuments were partly a statement about the racial order — remove entirely, and I don't think relegating it to a museum rotunda cures anything, since that just continues the honoring under another roof.
2. A statue of a founding father who owned slaves, erected for his political achievement — keep, with an added plaque putting the slaveholding in full view.
3. A university science building named for a man whose work in that very field was important but who was also a prominent eugenicist — rename, and here the plaque solution that satisfied me in case 2 strikes me as whitewashing, though I cannot articulate why.
4. A statue of the town founder, a slave trader whose fortune built half the city, erected by grateful citizens in the 1870s — remove.
5. A statue of someone morally comparable to the man in case 4, but carved by a sculptor of the first rank, so that it is genuinely a work of art — I keep going back and forth; my inclination is to move it indoors as art, but I distrust this, since whether honoring a person is wrong shouldn't depend on how well he was sculpted.
6. Renaming a street that carries the name of a long-dead mayor no living person can identify — fine, but I also think it accomplishes nothing morally; there is no honor left in the name to withdraw.
7. A mediocre statue of a man roughly comparable in virtue and vice to the founder in case 2 — I notice I want it gone, partly on the grounds that it is ugly anyway, which I recognize as no reason at all.

My tentative principle is that a commemoration should be removed when the bad is what is being celebrated, and retained with context when the bad is incidental to what is being celebrated. It fits cases 1, 2, and 4, but it fails on 3, and it is no help at all on 5 through 7. Can any single principle capture most of my verdicts? I would like it stated precisely enough to generate verdicts on new cases — in particular, what it implies about eponymous species names in taxonomy, where a bird carries a man's name that almost no birdwatcher connects to the man, and about a stained-glass window that is at once a depiction of a slaveholding bishop and a memorial donated by his grieving family. And if some of my verdicts have to go, tell me which ones and why those rather than the others.
