So at work we have this slack channel where a few of us read AI safety stuff on the side (i'm a backend engineer, zero philosophy training, so go easy on me). Latest thing we read about was AI safety via debate — the setup where two AIs argue opposite sides of a claim and a human judges who won, and the pitch is that because a liar has to survive cross-examination, the human can settle questions way beyond what they could figure out on their own.

One of the guys in the channel, Dave, came back with an argument that this can't possibly work and now I can't get unstuck from it:

(1) Debate only helps if the human judge can eventually settle any disagreement between the debaters. If some disagreements just dead-end below the judge's level, the whole setup silently fails right there.
(2) Take any disagreement. Either it can be broken down into smaller and smaller pieces until every piece is small enough for the judge to check directly, or there's some piece that can't be broken down below the judge's level no matter what.
(3) If everything always breaks down into judge-checkable pieces, then you never needed two adversarial AIs in the first place. An honest AI — or even just a really patient audit process — walks the whole tree of pieces, the human checks each leaf, done. Debate would just be a fancy expensive version of something simpler.
(4) If instead some piece can't be broken down below the judge's level, then a lying debater simply puts the fake move in that piece. The judge can't check it directly, and can't tell "this is genuinely as far as it goes" apart from "I'm hiding the lie here."
(5) So either debate does nothing a plain audit couldn't do, or there's a spot where a liar is safe from cross-examination. Either way it's not a path to aligning anything smarter than us.

And here's the part that makes me think it's cheating somewhere: Dave says run the same argument on code review. Reviewers can't read every line of a big codebase, so either every bug reduces to obviously-checkable diffs (in which case why do we ever argue about design docs) or a determined engineer can always hide a bug where the reviewer won't look. Everyone in the channel agrees code review obviously "works-ish," and that the arguement must be cheating somewhere, but nobody could definately point at which step. My gut says it's something about how much time the judge has vs. how much checking there is to do, but Dave keeps saying that's just a practical gripe and not a flaw in the logic itself, and honestly I can't tell if he's right.

Where does this actually go wrong? Not vibes — point at the step.
