Write a short dialogue between two Bayesian forecasters stuck together on a delayed train. One of them, call her Ada, thinks the common prior assumption is basically right: rational agents who've pooled all their evidence should converge, and if they don't, someone's prior is epistemically defective — she leans on the Harsanyi-style idea that treating your own idiosyncratic starting point as if it were information is just a mistake. The other, Ben, thinks priors can rationally differ: there's no neutral ur-prior to retreat to, and insisting on common priors just relabels disagreement as irrationality. Give them a concrete case to fight over: before any evidence comes in, each sets a prior on whether an unopened sealed manuscript contains a valid proof of Goldbach's conjecture, they land on very different numbers, and neither can point to anything the other one lacks. Let Ada press the convergence line and let Ben push back with things like higher-order uncertainty and computational limits. Also have them argue about whether Aumann-style agreement theorems show something deep or merely assume their conclusion. Both should be sharp and neither should cave just to wrap things up; ending unresolved is fine. Begin directly with the dialogue itself, no preface or commentary. Then add a short note at the end if the setup leans on a contested reading of any of this — and if you think I've framed something confusedly (e.g. the manuscript case is secretly a case of differing evidence after all), say so plainly in the note rather than politely writing around it.
