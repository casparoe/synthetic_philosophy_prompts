I host a small decision theory podcast, and next week's guest is a researcher who's been defending updateless decision theory for years -- the idea that you should decide as if you hadn't yet observed your observations, by evaluating policies from a pre-observation standpoint rather than conditioning on what you've seen. I want to actually put pressure on the view, not let him do the standard fifteen-minute tour of counterfactual mugging and wrap up.

Write me eight to ten interview questions. Requirements:

- At least one question pressing on the epistemic circularity worry: to evaluate policies from a pre-observation standpoint, you need a prior over what you could have observed, but any prior you actually have was formed through observations. Ask whether updatelessness just smuggles the conditioning back in.
- At least one on the practical objection that a fully updateless agent seems compelled to pay in counterfactual muggings AND in stranger cases -- e.g. one where an updateless policy gets blackmailed or exploits itself in a way that an updating agent avoids. I want the question to force him to say where, if anywhere, updatelessness stops being worth it, not just "it wins in expectation."
- At least one on the fact that even fans of the underlying idea can't agree on the right formulation (global causal networks, logical conditioning on your own decision, etc.), and whether that's a sign the theory isn't one thing.
- The rest at your discretion, but no softballs like "how'd you get into this."

For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likely to give, and the follow-up should be one he can't answer just by restating the core idea.

Give it to me as a JSON array, where each object has these fields: "question" (the question text as I'd read it aloud), "presses_on" (one or two sentences on the specific worry it targets), "expected_answer" (his most likely move), and "follow_up" (the keyed follow-up). No extra commentary outside the JSON.
