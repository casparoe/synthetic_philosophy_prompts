Our metaphysics reading group has spent basically the whole semester on the counterpart theory vs transworld identity thing and we've hit a wall and I'm starting to wonder if the wall is load-bearing. On one side you've got the Lewis people: no transworld identity, everything is in one world only, I could have been a baker in the sense that some guy very like me in another world is a baker. On the other side the Kripke-ish people: of course *I* could have been the baker, talking about a copy of me is missing the whole point, de re modality is about me myself, and if you tell Humphrey he almost won the election by pointing at a lookalike you have, as the objection goes, robbed him of the victory rather than told him how close he came.

Here's the thing though. Everyone in the room can recite both sides and nobody has moved an inch since like week three. My working hypothesis is that the actual disagreement is not where it looks like it is. Lewis himself admitted counterpart relations are just similarity relations and there's no single privileged one -- so how many counterparts I have depends on which respects you care about, which smells interest-relative. And if that's right, maybe the fight isn't "is there transworld identity" at all, but something like: is de re modality objective and world-independent, or is it somehow indexed to a way of individuating people -- and the Humphrey stuff is a rhetorical framing of that, not the real bone of contention. There's also the lurking formal thing: counterpart theorists can reconstruct the same modal sentences with varying counterpart relations that identity theorists get straight off, which makes me wonder if a chunk of this is notational variantism with metaphysical makeup on.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Substantive, verbal, or two questions smuggled under one heading? Is there a hidden formal question doing the work (what the semantics for quantified modal logic is really committing you to), or is it bedrock intuitions about what makes *me* me across possibilities? And given your diagnosis, tell me honestly whether anything -- a distinction, a formal result, some case -- could end it, or whether our reading group is just doomed to keep doing this forever.
