Two friends of mine — Dan, a physics grad student, and Maya, who did her PhD in philosophy of physics — had a long argument at a party about Bell's theorem, and I lost the thread halfway through. Can you adjudicate it move by move?

(1) Dan said Bell's theorem only rules out hidden-variable theories, so standard quantum mechanics is untouched by it. (2) Maya said no — Bell's derivation needs only locality, not hidden variables; the experiments violate the inequality, so any theory that's local in Bell's sense is dead, and since collapse in standard QM acts instantaneously at a distance, standard QM is nonlocal anyway, which means Bell's result agrees with it rather than threatening it. (3) Dan said the collapse "happening" at a distance is just an update of information, like a detective revising who she thinks committed the crime when a new clue arrives — no spooky action anywhere. (4) Maya said that comparison only works for correlations you can explain by a shared common cause, and Bell's theorem is precisely the proof that the quantum correlations can't be explained that way, so the information-update story is doing work it can't actually do. (5) Dan then said fine, but if you go Everett — many worlds, no collapse — the whole theory is perfectly local, so locality isn't dead either. (6) Maya said many-worlds people *claim* locality but there's a serious ongoing fight about whether they've earned it, and in any case the theory has to pay for it elsewhere, with the probabilities.

At some point Dan also muttered that if you hate all of these options you can just deny the experimenters' settings choices were free — superdeterminism, he called it — and Maya waved it off as unfalsifiable cheating, and neither elaborated.

Please rate each of the six moves from clearly mistaken to clearly right, with breif reasons, and say a bit about the superdeterminism exchange too. One request: where the experts themselves still genuinely disagree — about whether the Everett interpretation is really local, or about what exactly locality should mean after Bell — please flag it as contested instead of presenting one side as the consensus.
