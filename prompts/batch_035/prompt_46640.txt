Descartes holds that the proposition that I exist is indubitable: whenever I think, I cannot coherently doubt that I exist, since the doubting itself is a thinking. Some readers therefore reconstruct the cogito as a syllogism with a suppressed premise (whatever thinks exists), while others say it is not an inference at all but something I make true by performing it. The student wrote:

Descartes' cogito shows that my own existence is the one thing I can't be wrong about. The evil demon can deceive me about the external world and even about mathematics, but he can't deceive me into thinking when nothing exists, because thinking is an activity and every activity needs something that performs it. So the argument works like this: whatever thinks exists; I think; therefore I exist. Descartes himself says in the Second Meditation that the cogito is a valid syllogism with the major premise left implicit, and this makes sense, because otherwise the inference would be invalid.

The conclusion is that I exist, and moreover that I exist as a thinking thing. This proves more than the skeptics allow: it establishes that I am essentially an immaterial substance — my essence is thinking, as he goes on to argue with the piece of wax — and since substances can't be created or destroyed by natural means, this is already a proof that the mind survives the body.

Also, because the cogito is self-verifying, I exist comes out as a necessary truth: it's not just that I happen to exist, but that I couldn't have failed to exist, since in every possible world in which I think, I exist. This gives Descartes the firm foundation he was after, an archimedean point that no doubt can shake.

What it leaves open is whether anything exists outside my mind — the demon might still be deceiving me about bodies, including my own body. And it doesn't tell me how long I've existed: it's only a proof about the present moment, each time I perform it. That much Descartes concedes. So the cogito establishes that I am, and what I am, a thinking thing; what remains is to climb back out to the external world through God.
