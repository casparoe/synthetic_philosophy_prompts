hey, so I need some help writing an actual document, not just vibes. Here's my situation: I farm in a rural county and I'm one of five elected board members of our irrigation district — about 200 farms, all of us pumping from the same aquifer. For years the board mostly argued about ditch maintenance, but this summer two of my neighbors' wells went dry and my own irrigation well started sucking air in August, so this stopped being an agenda item for me and became about whether my kids keep the farm. Meanwhile our board is split and I've been asked to circulate a written recommendation before next month's meeting, and honestly I keep going back and forth, which is part of why I'm asking you.

here's the setup. The aquifer is clearly declining and everyone on the board agrees pumping is the cause, though nobody agrees on what follows from that. Two proposals are on the table:

1. Mandatory metering on every well plus annual pumping caps, with fines for going over. The pro side says voluntary restraint is a joke when rice and alfalfa prices are what they are — every one of us has an incentive to pump fast before the neighbors do, and we all know it.

2. A voluntary pledge to cut pumping 20%, with the district publishing who signed. The pro side here says forced caps will tear the community apart, get us sued, and just get cheated on anyway — half the wells are old and unmetered, and nobody trusts the district to enforce fairly. They point out that districts in the next county over tried caps and it turned into years of litigation and clandestine pumping.

And there's a third thing tangled into it. Our district pays to maintain an old canal and recharge pond that puts water back into the aquifer — it's a big chunk of our budget. One board member wants to cut that maintenance and use the money for meters, on the theory that we're subsidizing our own problem. The catch: hydrologically, a lot of that recharge ends up under farms that are OUTSIDE our district boundary and pay nothing toward it, so the people voting to fund it are also, in effect, paying for a benefit that leaks away to free riders. My brother-in-law (also a board member, also a pumper, and definitely not a neutral party on any of this) is the loudest voice for cutting it.

So what I need from you: I'd like you to take the role of a political economist with serious background in commons governance — Elinor Ostrom's territory, common-pool resources, collective action — who's also read enough philosophy to know the difference between arguing about what's fair and arguing about what works. Please think carefully before you write, maybe brainstorm the options and the failure modes of each first, then draft the memo itself. The memo should:

- recommend a concrete decision (or package of decisions) the board can actually vote on, with specifics — not "the board should study this further"
- deal with the metering-vs-voluntary question honestly, including why people who live here don't trust enforcement, and what design features might actually make caps stick versus what killed them elsewhere
- take a position on the canal/recharge cut, and grapple with the free-rider problem instead of just calling it unfair
- engage seriously with the strongest argument for the side you recommend against — if you say caps, give the anti-cap people their full due, and vice versa
- say something about my conflict of interest (I pump from the same aquifer as everyone else, and my own well trouble is hardly a neutral data point), since the other board members will read this knowing that

Keep it to something I can actually circulate — a page and a half, two max. Plain language, no jargon you don't unpack, and no citation list since nobody on this board would read one. I'd rather have a defensible recommendation I can stand behind at the meeting than something that hedges everything into mush.
