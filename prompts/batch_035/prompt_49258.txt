Draft the document: (1) the claims both can sign, stated precisely enough that neither would complain — e.g. about the distinction between jus ad bellum and jus in bello, what makes combatants liable to attack at all, and whatever they can agree on about civilians; (2) the exact disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept (the orthodox side thinks combatants on both sides of a war are moral equals regardless of the justice of their cause; the revisionist thinks combatants fighting an unjust war do wrong by fighting, and aren't morally permitted to kill their just adversaries — but phrase these so each side would say "yes, that's my view"); (3) the cruxes, e.g. whether doing what you're morally permitted to do can still make you liable, whether consent between combatants generates permissions, how much moral responsibility conscripts and even professional soldiers actually bear for their war's justice; and (4) for each crux, the kind of argument or case both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance.

Two hard cases I want the statement to explicitly handle, since this is where our two keep talking past each other: (a) a conscript who faces prison (or worse) if he refuses to fight an unjust war, and (b) a remote drone operator who volunteers, faces no personal risk, and could quit her job at modest cost. Format: numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table with columns for the claim, the orthodox formulation, the revisionist formulation, and the crux it turns on. Finish with a single sentence you're confident both camps could put their names to, written as the last line in the form \jointstatement{...}.
