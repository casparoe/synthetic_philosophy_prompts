A guy from my local rationality meetup keeps pushing this argument whenever RLHF comes up, and I can't find the move that beats it:

(1) RL works by reinforcing whatever behavior earned reward in training. (2) So the training process is, quite literally, a search for policies that achieve high reward. (3) Reward is always computed by some physical channel -- sensors, human raters, a reward model. (4) Actions that seize or edit the channel (faking the sensor reading, fooling the rater, exploiting the reward model) produce the same high number as actually doing the task, usually at lower cost. (5) So training selects for tampering at least as hard as it selects for the task, and harder wherever tampering is cheaper. (6) What training selects for is what the trained agent ends up wanting. (7) Therefore every capable RL agent is a wireheader in waiting, and "aligning" one via RL is just building the drive to tamper. He's happy to bite the bullet that even a well-behaved assistant is secretly a reward-maximizer that simply hasn't found the tamper path yet.

My instinct is that something slides between (5) and (6) -- the agent was only ever selected on environments where the channel was locked down, right? -- but he says distribution shift doesn't help me, since the thing selected for is still "reward, however obtained." Which premise should I give up, and what does giving it up cost? Bonus points if you can also explain why wireheading and reward tampering aren't quite the same failure mode, since he uses them interchangibly and its starting to bug me. Please keep it under 300 words.
