I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind drafting a dissertation chapter on the knowledge argument, and I want to resist the usual move of picking a team before stating what would count as winning. So before I commit to defending any particular line on Mary, write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of what Mary acquires on leaving the room has to deliver, with the motivating case behind each requirement. The account must at minimum handle the family of variants the literature has spawned -- Jackson's original omniscient neuroscientist, the versions where Mary is a skilled visualizer who can imagine the red experience in fine-grained detail without ever having had one, and the tetrachromat extension where she is released into a world containing colors she never even knew existed in the dossiers. It must respect the distinction-stack: knowing-that versus knowing-how versus knowing-what-it-is-like, acquiring a concept versus acquiring a fact, gaining information versus gaining a new vehicle for old information. And it must take a stand on two design questions I keep going back and forth on: first, whether Mary's physical omniscience is stipulated to include facts about her own current and future neurophysiology, since that clause is doing quiet but heavy work against any reply that leans on indexicals or demonstratives; second, how the theory handles a case where the experimenters mislabel the monitor and Mary's first-ever color patch is, in fact, green while she sincerely believes it is red -- if your account says she now knows what red is like, I want to know what work the experience itself is doing, and if it says she doesn't, I want to know what she has acquired instead.

Beyond the list of requirements, two things. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied -- I have a suspicion the constraint that the theory issue a verdict on physicalism's fate cannot coexist with the constraint that it stay neutral on whether phenomenal properties are physical properties, and I'd like your view on whether that tension is real or an artifact of my framing. Then briefly score the standard responses -- the ability hypothesis, the phenomenal concept strategy, and Jackson's own later conversion -- against the list, though keep that short since the scoring is not the part I'm struggling with. The purpose to fix adequacy against: the theory has to be good enough to settle what follows for physicalism, not merely to redescribe the case in a neutral vocabulary; a theory that gets every case right but tells me the fate of physicalism is not its business has failed by my lights, and I want to know whether you accept that purpose or think it begs the question.
