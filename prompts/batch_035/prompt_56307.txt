Elite sport bans a long list of perfomance enhancers — steroids, EPO, blood transfusions — but is fine with the rest of the toolbox for getting faster and stronger: altitude tents, cryotherapy, optimized diets, surgery. A shooter can apparently get eye surgery leaving them with better than 20/20 vision, no problem. And here's the wrinkle I keep snagging on: an asthmatic athlete can get a therapeutic use exemption to inhale a substance that would get a healthy competitor banned. Which seems to quietly commit the whole system to a rule like "you may restore your own baseline, but not exceed it." So what theory of the athlete does that edifice presuppose? That natural gifts are somehow yours in a way that acquired advantages aren't? That enhancement is cheating while training isn't, and if so what actually marks the line — and why does lasik to superhuman vision fall on the legal side while a fraction of a dose of something falls on the other? Reconstruct the implicit view for me, then tell me whether the practice strictly needs that view or whether it's underdetermined — compatible with a fairness story, an authenticity story, a "don't overwhelm the testing regime" pragmatism — and what anti-doping would have to look like if we just dropped the natural/artificial line altogether.
