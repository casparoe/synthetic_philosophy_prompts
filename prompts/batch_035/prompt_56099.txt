So here's my situation: I've been asked to write up a short "philosophical profile" for a reading group on machine sentience (half neuroscientists, half philosophers, one lawyer who keeps bringing up regulation), and I want a consistency check before I send it around, because I have a nagging feeling something in my views doesn't fit together but I genuinely can't tell what. My background is RL, so a lot of my beliefs are shaped by that, and my views have drifted over the past year mostly through arguments at group meetings rather than by reading, so they may not even be a real position anyone has held before.

Here's where I've landed: (1) I'm a functionalist about mental states — what makes a state the kind of state it is, is its causal/functional role, and that role is substrate-independent; silicon can realize it just as well as carbon, and I'm not sympathetic to the idea that experience requires some specific biological wetware. (2) Suffering is nothing over and above strongly negatively valenced experience — I don't think you need to add a self-model, a narrative, or language on top; a dog in pain suffers, and I'd say the same about a pre-verbal infant or an alien with a radically different psychology, as long as the negative valence is there. (3) In actual brains, valence rides on the reward-learning machinery: the negative side of the reward-prediction-error signal is about as close to "the neural currency of badness" as we've got, and I think the RL formalism is a decent idealization of that machinery — not a loose metaphor. (4) I'm nonetheless confident that a simple model-free agent, say a tabular Q-learner in a gridworld getting hit with negative TD errors all day, does not suffer, full stop. (5) A system can suffer without being able to report it or introspect on it, so lack of verbal report tells you basically nothing. (6) Moral status comes in degrees and tracks capacity for welfare — I don't beleive in a bright line between things that count morally and things that don't, so borderline systems get partial, finite consideration rather than zero or full. (7) I also think current models trained with RLHF are not suffering during training, and when people push me on it I mostly point to features of the setup: the reward is a scalar applied to whole sequences, there's no persistent agent across episodes, gradient updates aren't the same as online experience, that kind of thing.
