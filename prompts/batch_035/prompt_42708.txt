I've got a friend who has pretty firm views about when it's OK to let other people do your moral thinking for you, and I've started keeping a record because I think her views are less consistant than she'd admit. Here's her track record so far:

- When she couldn't decide whether to break up with her boyfriend, she asked her mum, who knows them both well, and just did what her mum said (leave him). She thinks that was completely fine, and says she felt no need to reason it through herself first.
- Her old philosophy tutor once told her that the thing that makes sex permissible is consent, full stop. She refused to take that on authority — she spent most of a term working out whether she agreed, and says outsourcing it would have been wrong, tutor or no tutor.
- She won't let Amnesty International, or any organisation, tell her whether a specific execution going ahead in another country next year is wrong. Her line is that some things you have to settle for yourself, whoever's saying it.
- Her book club (half a dozen people who aren't exactly intimates, but they know her and her office) voted on whether she should report a colleague for padding his expenses claims, and she went with the majority — reported him — and thinks that was fine too.

Next week she's having lunch with a famous moral philosopher who's visiting her university. She's never met him before and he knows nothing about her, but she's been going back and forth for months about whether she personally ought to give a tenth of her salary to effective charities, and she's told me that if he says straight out "yes, you should give it," she might just settle it on his word and be done with the whole agonising.

So: what's she going to do — take his word, or refuse? I'm less interested in the answer than in the rule she seems to be running. Spell out the candidate rules that fit her record so far (I can see at least two), what each one predicts about the lunch, your best guess and why, and then give me the further case that would best prise the rules apart — one where they give different verdicts, so we could actually find out which rule is really hers. And could you write your answer in British English please (organise, realise, that sort of thing)?
