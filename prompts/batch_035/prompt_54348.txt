I read about Douglas Hofstadter's old AI research program, Letter Spirit, which was meant to capture the "essence" of each letter well enough to recognize any 'a' ever drawn and even generate new 'a's in styles it had never seen -- and now I can't stop noticing that I have strong gut opinions about what counts as an 'a' and no idea what's behind them. So here's the exercise: I'll dump my verdicts on a bunch of glyphs, and you try to find the principle that explains the pattern, because I definately can't.

The verdicts. The double-story serif 'a' in Times New Roman: yes, obviously. The single-story 'a' I actually write by hand: yes, even though those two barely resemble each other. But Greek lowercase alpha in some ancient Greek text: NOT an 'a', it's a different letter in a different alphabet, even though it looks almost identical to my handwritten one. A fraktur 'a': yes, but only because I know it's playing the a-role in that font; shown alone I'd probably call it some weird o-g hybrid, and yet it feels way more like an 'a' than alpha does?? The upside-down A in a math paper, ∀: not an A anymore, it's a quantifier meaning "for all", even though geometrically it's literally an A rotated 180 degrees -- but that same shape in my nephew's wobbly alphabet drawing is definately an A, crossbar sticking out and all. Captcha 'a's warped to hell and half-dissolved: still 'a's, that's the whole point of captchas. A mirrored 'A': instantly not an A, which is strange because mirroring distorts the shape less than any captcha does. '@': not an 'a', even though squinting makes it look like an 'a' wrapped around itself, and I've heard people say it grew out of the letter historically (not sure that's true, correct me if I'm making it up). My handwriting on a bad day, where the loop doesn't close and it's geometrically closer to a 'u': still an 'a' within the sentence, and I'd read it back as one a week later.

My tentative guess is that it's about the *role* a glyph plays in some writing system rather than its shape, but that runs into trouble fast. The case I keep going back and fourth on: novelty fonts where each letter is a little scene -- say the 'a' is built out of two anchors and some rope, or letters made of flames or candy canes. If the anchor-monstrosity occupies the a-role when you type with that font, is it an 'a', or just a picture standing in for one? And relatedly, an 'a' traced in the air with a finger or written in water -- is that even a glyph, or a performance of one? Can you give me a principle, or a couple of rival candidates, that captures my verdicts, says what it predicts for the anchor-font case, the finger-writing case, and one or two cases I haven't thought of, and is honest about which of my verdicts it simply can't accommodate and would have to overturn? Please also flag how confident you are in all of this and point at the part you trust least -- I'm aware this wanders into deep theory territory (categories, style, essence, whatever else is going on there) and I have zero philosophy background, so I'd much rather know where the ground is soft than get a slick answer.
