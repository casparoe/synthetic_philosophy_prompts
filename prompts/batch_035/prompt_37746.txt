In a seminar on the French Revolution, our tutor closed the discussion of the Terror by remarking that revolutions devour their children, and moved on as though that settled why Danton and Hébert ended up following Louis XVI to the guillotine. It has nagged at me since that the sentence could be advancing several quite different claims, some trivial, some substantive, some probably false. Please lay out, as a list I can bring back to seminar, the distinct precise theses the slogan might stand for — for instance, that purges are structurally forced once legitimacy rests on revolutionary purity alone; that the skills needed to seize power select against the temperament needed to hold it; that the original coalition's commitments are inherently unstable; that this is only an observation about what happened in 1793–94; or that it is near-circular, since a takeover that spares its founders gets reclassified as something other than a revolution. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a defensible generalization, or false, whether it is backed by anything in the French case specifically, and — most importantly — which readings would actually support using the line as an explanation of the Terror, as opposed to a rhetorical flourish pinned onto it.
