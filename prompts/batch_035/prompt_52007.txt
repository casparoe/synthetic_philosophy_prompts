I'm in a reading group that started out about the ethics side of panpsychism -- like, if experience goes all the way down, does that create some kind of moral uncertainty about how we treat matter, wild speculation about suffering in fundamental physics, that whole vibe -- but honestly we've spent two years stuck on the metaphysics and never gotten to the ethics at all. The fight is always the same: the panpsychists in the group say consciousness can't just emerge from wholly non-phenomenal stuff, because that would be brute emergence of a categorically new property, and pushing experience down into the fundamental level is the more parsimonious move; the emergentists say sure, but then you get the combination problem -- how do trillions of micro-experiences at the level of, say, electrons combine into one unified subject like me, and how do they fail to combine in a rock? Both sides seem to concede that they're each holding an unexplained residue, and neither side has moved an inch. Everyone's smart, everyone's read the same papers, and it's gotten a bit tedious.

Here's my working theory, tell me if I'm onto something or just being cynical: the disagreement isn't really about the structure of reality at all, it's about standards for what counts as an acceptable explanation. The panpsychists treat the combination problem as an ordinary "we don't understand the mechanism yet" problem, like heredity before DNA, while treating radical emergence as flatly incoherent; the emergentists treat strong synchronic determination between levels as totally normal (liquidity from molecules, life from chemistry) and see the panpsychists as demanding a standard of explanation for consciousness that they'd never demand for anything else. If that's right, then both sides share all the facts and it's basically a disagreement about epistemic tolerances -- which would explain why no argument ever lands, since each side classifies the other's central phenomenon as the "mystery" and its own as the "work in progress." But maybe I'm wrong and it's actually verbal, a fight about the word 'fundamental' or about how liberally to use 'emergence.' There's also a third option one guy keeps pushing, that the whole question is ill-posed because we lack any grip on what phenomenal properties are supposed to *do* causally, so we can't even state what would distinguish the two pictures.

So what I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right: what kind of disagreement is this really, based on what each side actually says? Substantive, verbal, different questions under one name, clashing bedrock intuitions about explanation? And then, given your diagnosis, tell me honestly whether anything could count as progress here -- a formal account of synchronic emergence, progress on the neural correlates of consciousness, a principled decomposition of the combination problem into separable sub-problems, or is this just a permanent standoff we should expect? If it IS a permanent standoff, I want to know whether that's a reason for our group to just skip to the ethics, or whether the ethics itself depends on how this resolves (like whether moral circles could even in principle extend below animals if the combination problem has no answer).
