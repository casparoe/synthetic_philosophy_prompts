My grandmother passed away a couple weeks ago and she grew up speaking Czech, so I'm translating her favorite poem into English for the back of the funeral program. The problem is the poem itself: it's about a river drying up, and every stanza drops one syllable per line, so by the last stanza theres almost nothing left, just like the river. I keep flip-flopping between two instincts and can't tell which is right — keep the shrinking meter and loose a lot of the sense, or keep the sense and let the shrinking go. Someone told me to read Hofstadter's big book about translating one tiny French poem and honestly it just gave me more ways to feel torn. So help me think it through: write me a short dialogue between two translators arguing about what I should do. One is convinced the disappearing meter IS the poem, so anything without it is just a paraphrase with line breaks. The other thinks reproducing the constraint in English produces a gimmick that reads as a stunt instead of grief, and wants to press the point that forcing lines into a syllable count will make me invent filler words anyway — i.e., that "preserving the form" secretly means writing new content the poet never chose. Neither should be a pushover, I want them to actually land some blows on each other. And please write it as plain continuous prose, no headings or bullet points or bold or anything — just like a page out of a novel.
