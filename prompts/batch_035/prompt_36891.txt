writing a piece on personal identity and could use help untangling a conceptual fight for it. Dennett's line is that the self is a center of narrative gravity -- not a little person inside your head but an abstract posit we use to organize the story of a person, roughly like physicists use a center of mass for an object. Galen Strawson objected that plenty of people, whom he calls Episodics, dont experience their life as a narrative at all, and they seem fine -- so narrativity can't be essential to being a self.

Here's the question I keep circling: imagine someone with advanced dementia whose biography is kept running by her family -- they tell her who she is, remind her what she loved, keep the story going. Is she still the center of that narrative gravity, or have the relatives become co-authors, sort of like how a random scatter of stars only becomes a constellation once several stargazers agree on the lines? And on that picture, does Strawson's objection actually land a hit on Dennett, or does Dennett's view never need the story to be experienced as a story from the inside in the first place? Would love a clear walkthrough of where the two views really make contact.
