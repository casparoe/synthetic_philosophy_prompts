the piece is about guilt versus shame. the standard framing I keep running into: guilt is the good moral emotion (it targets the act, makes you want to repair, empathize with the victim, confess, make it right) and shame is the bad one (it targets the whole self, makes you hide, blame others, sometimes lash out, and doesn't even track whether you did anything wrong -- you can be ashamed of a limp or an accent). the psychologists I interview basically treat this as settled, and the journalists have taken it and run.

3. there's a philosophical counter-current I find compelling but can't evaluate: that guilt is just "shame with a disaggregated self," i.e., guilt only works because you've already bought into a norm so deeply that you carry your own audience around with you -- so guilt isn't the alternative to the external gaze, it IS the external gaze, internalized and disguised. on that view the guilt-is-good consensus is a kind of propaganda for a culture that wants to pretend it has no audience.
