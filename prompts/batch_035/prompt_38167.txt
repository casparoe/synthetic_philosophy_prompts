In my sociology of punishment seminar, the instructor keeps invoking "knowledge is power" — usually credited to Francis Bacon, but clearly meant in Foucault's sense — and yesterday used it to brush off a classmate's question about whether a statistical recidivism-risk instrument used in sentencing could be a neutral tool. Her reasoning seemed to be: since, on Foucault's view, knowledge is never anything but an effect and instrument of power, asking whether the instrument is neutral is like asking whether a whip is neutral. Something about that inference feels too quick to me, but I can't pin down why. Please lay out the distinct precise theses that "knowledge is power" could be expressing, from the near-trivial to the genuinely radical, and say for each whether it is a plausible reading of Bacon, something close to what Foucault actually argued about power/knowledge, or neither. In particular, distinguish any strong identity claim (knowledge just is power) from weaker claims about production, dependence, or mutual circulation between the two, and note for each reading whether it would actually support my instructor's move — treating neutrality as a confused question rather than a difficult achievement — or only the more modest claim that the tool is not in fact neutral. Present the readings as a numbered list I can bring to seminar, and please write your answer in American English.
