Quick one before i dive into comparing theories of vagueness: i want the spec first. Write me the requirements list — what any adequate theory of vagueness has to deliver. The cases it must handle: the conditional sorites vs the mathematical-induction version (and whether a theory can accept one and reject the other), penumbral connections like "tall and not tall" coming out false rather than neither-true-nor-false, higher-order vagueness (borderline cases of borderline cases), and comparison classes — a "small" elephant is still enormous next to a mouse, so vagueness seems somehow indexed. Plus one requirement i actually care about that people rarely state: the theory should be usable when a line has to be drawn in practice — say a content moderation team picking a cutoff for when a post counts as "harassing" — so it should say something about what changes when you move the cutoff, not just that its vague all the way down and shrug. For each requirement say why it's a requirement, then mark which are non-negotiable and which are really just the prejudices of one camp (epistemicism, supervaluationism, that lot). Tell me which single requirement excludes the most contenders, and if any pair of the desiderata can't be jointly satisfied — i suspect the practical-usability one clashes with something — that's the bit i most want to see.
