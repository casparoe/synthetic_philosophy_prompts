So I'm writing up my views on objective chance for a metaphysics of science seminar and before I commit I want a consistency check, because I have a sneaking suspicion something in here doesn't hang together. Here's the package. (1) Chances are real, single-case propensities: the chance that this particular radium atom decays in the next hour is a mind-independent fact about the atom and its physical situation, like its mass — not a fact about anyone's ignorance, and not some abstract summary of patterns in the mosaic (I am not a best-system person, that part is settled for me). (2) When I learn that the chance of an outcome is p and I have no inadmissible evidence, my credence in that outcome should be p — deference to chance is basically constitutive of chance, the Principal Principle feels nearly analytic to me. (3) Chances are indexed to times and shift as the world unfolds — the chance of the coin landing heads was 1/2 while it spun, and is 1 or 0 now that it's landed, even though it's hidden under a cup and nobody knows which. (4) Chances are genuine probabilities — they obey the usual axioms just like credences do. (5) And the one my cohort keeps hassling me about: I think fundamental physics is deterministic, and that quantum "randomness" will eventually go the way of dice randomness, i.e. bookkeeping for ignorance of exact initial conditions — while still insisting the coin flip above has an objective 1/2 chance, since a trial set up like that has a genuine disposition to come up heads about half the time across physically similar setups.

My best-system friend says stop being precious about propensities, go Humean, and everything smooths out. My propensity-or-nothing friend says determinism and single-case propensities are flatly incompatible so one of (1) or (5) has to die, and he also mutters that if propensities are dispositions of complete physical situations, nothing in the nature of a disposition guarantees they behave like probabilities at all — which I admit I haven't fully digested. I don't want to pre-judge anything, but I'd flag that the coin under the cup feels fine to me and I'd be surprised if that's the real problem. So: which of these commitments actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, what's the cheapest repair for each genuine tension and what does that repair cost me, and please rank the problems by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad. If the whole thing is unfixable without dropping something I called settled, say that too, but be specific about what has to give and why.
