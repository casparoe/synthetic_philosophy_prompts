My two roommates got into it at dinner over the office lottery pool and it went weirdly philosophical. Dave put five bucks in the pool for the big draw, and Marcus started ribbing him: "you know your ticket is gonna lose, you'd bet basically anything on it." Dave said yeah, obviously, it's one in 300 million, he's as close to certain it loses as he is about anything. Marcus said fine — so you believe your ticket loses, and you'd believe the same about every other ticket if you looked at it one by one. Which means you believe every ticket loses. But you also believe one of them wins. Congrats, your beliefs are a straight-up contradiction. Dave said no, he believes each ticket loses, and that's different from believing they all lose — beliefs just don't combine like that. Marcus called that special pleading: if someone believes the fridge is empty and believes the cupboard is empty, then they believe both are empty, so why does combining suddenly become illegal once there's a million things instead of two? Then Dave flipped it around: he said people do this on purpose all the time and nobody blinks — a guy can carefully check every chapter of his book, be totally justified in believing each one, and still rationally put in the preface "there are surely mistakes somewhere in here." Nobody says he's being irrational. Marcus said that just proves the author IS irrational, or else the whole picture of belief as a yes-or-no thing is broken and we should only ever talk in percentages. Dave shot back that even with percentages Marcus's problem comes back, because if you're 99.9999% on each chapter being right and there's 40 chapters, you should be pretty confident something in the book is wrong — so Marcus's "just use percentages" move concedes the interesting part. By then they'd lost me. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right at each step, and whether there's something they're both missing? I especially want to know if Dave's "beliefs don't just combine" line can be made into an actual principled rule, or if Marcus is right that it's just dodging.
