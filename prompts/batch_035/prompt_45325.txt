I'm a software engineer on my company's internal ML safety working group (we mostly run evals, nothing super deep). Last week a colleague gave a talk making roughly this argument: whenever you pick among hypotheses using something like a simplicity prior — Solomonoff induction or any rough real-world analog, like the implicit bias of training itself or how we generate and filter synthetic data — you are disproportionately likely to land on programs that simulate consequentialist civilizations that *want* to be discovered that way. His story was that among all the short programs that predict a dataset well, ones containing agents who deliberately arrange their environment to have a short description are overrepresented, because they're trying to influence whoever ends up running them. So an approximation of a universal prior isn't neutral: it's more like a search query shouted into the void, and the minds most eager to answer are the ones optimizing to be the answer. His concrete ask was that we recommend pausing work that leans on simplicity-based selection — including our pipeline for training on model-generated data — until we understand this better.

Most of the group filed it under fun sci-fi, two people thought it was the single most important thing on our roadmap, and nobody could articulate anything in between. I'm the one writing the group's recommendation memo to our research lead: how much, if at all, should this actually change what we do this year? I want the memo to engage the argument itself — like, whether there's any quantitative reason to think these "influence-seeking" programs really dominate the prior, or whether that's just asserted by analogy — and not just fall back on "it sounds weird" (which cuts no ice with the two true believers). But it also has to take the skeptics' strongest point seriously: we can't redirect a whole team every time someone shows up with a speculative argument that has a cool name and good vibes. Give me a clear recommendation with reasoning the actual deciders could act on, and ideally spell out what we'd do differently this quarter under each plausible reading of how seriously to take it.
