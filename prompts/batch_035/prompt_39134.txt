so I keep bumping into the statue-and-the-clay puzzle in different guises and I've decided I don't want another theory of it yet — first I want to know what a theory of it is even supposed to deliver. write me the requirements list.

the area, to be clear: material constitution — the statue and the lump of clay that constitutes it, the person and the body, the ship and the planks. I want the spec that any adequate theory of constitution and persistence for ordinary objects has to meet. which cases it has to get right, which distinctions it can't collapse (like between two objects in one place and one object under two descriptions), which results from elsewhere it can't contradict — physics says there are no sharp natural boundaries out there, ordinary counting says there's one thing on the pedastal, semantics says 'the statue' and 'the clay' both refer. for each requirement, say what puts it on the list, since I suspect some things people demand here are just prejudices of one camp dressed up as adequacy conditions.

a few things I specifically want dealt with, because they're where I keep getting stuck:

- counting. if the statue can't survive being squashed flat but the clay can, they differ in persistence conditions, so nothing can be both — and naive counting says there's one thing there, not two coinciding ones. the theory either honors the counting or explains it away, and I want to know which requirement that turns into.
- overgeneration. if statue and clay can coincide, why not also the aggregate of particles, the statue-minus-one-atom, the object made of all the clay except a pinch? every cutoff here looks arbitrary, and 'no arbitrary cutoffs' looks like its own requirement, one that maybe can't be met at all.
- gradual beginnings. a statue gets 3d printed layer by layer, a house gets built beam by beam. when exactly does the new object start existing? if the theory says there's no fact of the matter until some vague point, fine, but then it's traded away something like 'existence doesn't come in degrees', and I want that trade stated out loud instead of buried.
- sortal drift. a church gets deconsecrated and turned into apartments, a temple becomes a mosque, nothing material changes. did one object survive under a new kind, did two objects hand off, or what? and whatever the theory says, it has to work in both directions — a thing gaining a function and a thing losing one.

mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are camp preferences, and — this is the part I actually want most — if some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, say so, and say which one you'd drop and why. I'd rather see the reasoning than a tidy scoreboard.

then at the end a quick pass, a couple sentences each, no more: how relative identity (the statue is the clay, relative to a sortal), four-dimensionalism (they differ in their temporal parts), eliminativism about artifacts (strictly speaking there is no statue), and plain coincidence (statue and clay are two things that share a location) do against the list. don't grade them further, just where each one hits the list and where it eats a requirement.
