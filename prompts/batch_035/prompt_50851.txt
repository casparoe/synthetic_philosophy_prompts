In discussions of Molyneux's problem, the 2011 Project Prakash study is often reported as having finally answered the question: five children and adolescents in India, blind from birth because of dense bilateral cataracts, were tested shortly after sight-restoring surgery on their ability to match a shape they had just felt to one of two shapes placed before their eyes. Their performance on within-modality matching was strong — touch to touch, sight to sight — but on the cross-modal task they scored at or barely above chance, improving to near-ceiling within days once given feedback. The experimenters took this to support a negative answer to Molyneux's question. I would like you to explain why a number of philosophers regard that verdict as premature, covering at least three concerns. First, the capacity confound: Molyneux seemingly imagined a fully functional visual system merely awaiting input, whereas these subjects had suffered years of form deprivation during visual development, so a failure of cross-modal transfer might reflect degraded visual processing rather than an absence of pre-established sight-touch correspondence. Second, the distinction between discriminating and identifying: Molyneux's original wording asks whether the newly sighted man could "distinguish and tell which is the globe, which the cube" — so explain why a subject might manage to tell the two visible objects apart, which is a purely visual achievement, without being able to identify which object is the one he touched, and which of the two achievements the experiment's matching task actually measures. Third, the role of inference: Leibniz held that sight and touch share the notion of extension, and Reid described a blind geometer reasoning from visible to tactile shape, so the experiment's measure of immediate perceptual matching may not probe what these rationalist answers to Molyneux claim. For each of the three concerns, state precisely which premise of the optimistic interpretation it attacks, and then say which you take to be the most damaging and why.
