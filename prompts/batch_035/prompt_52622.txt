So, background: I've been hanging around the rationalist-adjacent corners of the internet since the old Sequences days, and one debate that never dies there is whether phenomenal consciousness is just a very persistent bug in our self-model. The serious academic version of this is illusionism, and the analogy I keep running into when people argue for it is the phlogiston one. The story goes: back in the day chemists thought burning was the release of a fire-stuff called phlogiston; Lavoisier showed that combustion is really combining with oxygen, and phlogiston wasn't reduced or reidentified with anything — it just got deleted from science, and nothing of value was lost. The illusionists say the same should happen with qualia: once neuroscience explains discrimination, report, and behavior, the leftover phenomenal glow shouldn't be reduced to brain stuff, it should be dropped entirely, and the only thing left to explain is why it seems like there's a glow. People deploy this to argue that the hard problem is a fake problem and that anyone trying to identify qualia with some physical property is basically looking for the vault where the phlogiston is stored.

My friend (a physicalist but not an illusionist) thinks the phlogiston comparison smuggles in its conclusion. His counter-analogy is élan vital: when molecular biology came along we did delete vital spirit, but we kept life as a perfectly respectable concept, and nobody thinks biologists are studying an illusion. On his version consciousness is like life — the spooky extras get eliminated, but consciousness itself survives deflation. What bugs me is that both camps seem to be mining the same stretch of history of science for whatever parts happen to fit their side, and I can't tell which of them, if either, is actually tracking the structure of the case. Also I vaguely remember reading somewhere that phlogiston theory wasnt actually the mess its reputation suggests — it explained a fair bit and its defenders had stories for the awkward bits — but I have no idea how much weight that should carry in this fight.

So heres what I want from you. First the mappings, layed out explicitly: in the phlogiston version, what plays phlogiston, what plays oxygen, what plays Lavoisier, and what plays the moment of deletion. Same for the élan vital version — what plays vital spirit, what plays molecular biology, what plays the thing that survives. Then where each analogy breaks, and importantly, separate the disanalogies that actually matter for the conclusion being carried (that we should eliminate the phenomenal, versus merely deflate it) from the ones that are just noise. Does the eliminativist conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy doing the arguing the whole time? And is there a better analogy available — some people on the forums also push a user-interface thing, consciousness as the brain's desktop, where the icons aren't the machinery — or does that one come with its own baggage?
