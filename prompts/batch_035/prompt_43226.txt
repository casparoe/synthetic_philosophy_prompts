so I've been volunteered to write the "commitments profile" for a little philosophy of science reading group I'm in (we take turns and everyone else gets to poke holes). I picked dispositions as my topic because it's what I actually care about, and here's roughly what I wrote down, but before I post it I want a sanity check on whether the whole package even hangs together:

1. At bottom I'm with the conditional analysis: to be fragile just is (roughly) to be the sort of thing that would break if dropped or struck, to be watersoluble to be the sort of thing that would dissolve in water. Whatever patches it needs, I don't want a story where a disposition completely floats free of what would actually happen. If a thing would under no circumstances ever shatter, I don't see how calling it fragile means anything.
2. That said, I take finks and masks seriously as counterexamples to the naive version. I don't like the sorcerer example everyone trots out, so heres my own: my printer has firmware that, the instant a paper jam starts to develop, detects it and clears it before any error can show up on screen. Over the whole life of the printer, nothing ever goes visibly wrong. I still want to say the paper-feed mechanism stays jam-prone the whole time, the jam-tendency is just intercepted before it can manifest. Same deal with a glass in bubble wrap: still fragile, doesn't break when you strike it, the wrapping just prevents the breaking.
3. Dispositions are intrinsic. Two objects that are atom-for-atom intrinsic duplicates cannot differ in what dispositions they have.
4. A disposition can go its whole life unmanifested and still be real, and I'm also fine with dispositions being gained or lost through pure re-arrangement of relations to surroundings, without the object's own makeup changing at all.
5. When we say a substance is toxic or a person is irritable, we're not secretly talking about categorical microstructure — the disposition talk is the primary content, and whatever machinery underlies it is a grounding story, not the meaning.
6. Related to 3: whether a glass counts as fragile shouldn't turn on what's happening in a building down the street.

Honestly they all feel right to me individually but the thread will be full of people way more read than me, so before I embarrass myself: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and if something has to give, what's the cheapest revision and what does it cost me? Rank the tensions by how bad they are rather than just listing everything as equally fatal. And if some pair looks like it conflicts but actually doesn't, please show the dissolve explicitly, because otherwise I'll fold on it in the thread for no reason.
