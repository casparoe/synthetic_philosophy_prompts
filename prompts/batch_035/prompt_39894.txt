I am preparing a short position statement on the metaphysics of consciousness for a graduate seminar, and I would like a consistency audit of my settled views before I circulate it. As far as I can tell, these are my considered commitments:

(a) I am a functionalist: what makes a state a mental state of a given kind is the role it plays in the system's economy of inputs, outputs, and other states, and there are no further facts about mentality over and above such functional facts.

(b) I have come to accept illusionism of the Dennett/Frankish variety: phenomenal consciousness as traditionally conceived — qualia understood as intrinsic, private, ineffable properties presented to an inner observer — is a user illusion that the brain constructs about itself. Nothing in the world has the properties so conceived, including us.

(c) I hold that consciousness in the minimal sense — that there is something it is like to be me right now — is more certain than anything cognitive science could ever tell me, and that any theory which denies this, or explains it away as mere confusion in our concepts, is refuted by that fact alone.

(d) I take introspection to be a genuine faculty of observation, on a par with vision: its deliverances, such as that I am currently experiencing the redness of the apple, are data that any adequate theory must accommodate, and the existence of illusions and occasional errors in perception is no reason to distrust a sense as such.

(e) I hold that the moral badness of pain is grounded in how pain feels rather than in its functional role. A system that had the full functional profile of pain — withdrawal, reports, stress, avoidance learning — but for which there was nothing it is like would have nothing bad happening in it, and harming such a system would not wrong it.

(f) I think zombies are conceivable, but that this conceivability is itself a product of the user illusion, and therefore provides no evidence against physicalism.

I genuinely do not know where, if anywhere, this package comes apart, and I have no attachment to any particular member of it surviving the audit. Please go through the commitments pairwise, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which apparent difficulties dissolve on closer inspection, and for each genuine tension what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair would cost elsewhere in the package. Rank the tensions by severity rather than flagging everything equally, and conclude by telling me which single revision, if any, would clean up the most.
