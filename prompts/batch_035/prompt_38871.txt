I'm a high school student writing my extended essay on pain asymbolia, the condition in which patients report that they feel pain but are not bothered by it, sometimes smiling or laughing while saying that a stimulus hurts. Most discussions I've read take the condition to show that pain has two separable parts: a neutral sensory quality, and a distinct affective or evaluative component that carries all the badness. My thesis is that this inference is too quick, and I would like an argument supporting it. Please argue that asymbolia does not establish that the badness of pain is detachable from how pain feels. Along the way, please address whether the asymbolic patient's report that something "hurts" can carry its usual meaning once aversiveness is absent; whether the disturbed affect in asymbolia might instead have changed the sensation itself, so that what is preserved is not pure pain but something already diluted; and a thought experiment I want to use: whether a hypothetical drug that induced lifelong asymbolia should count as a perfect painkiller, or as something closer to a lobotomy of the will to avoid harm. Argue for the thesis in at most 400 words.
