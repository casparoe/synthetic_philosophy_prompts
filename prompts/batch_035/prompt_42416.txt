You are a professional philosopher with expertise in nineteenth-century German philosophy and political thought, and you also write sharp, deadpan satire.

Write a short satirical op-ed, roughly 650–850 words, published under the byline of a fictional think tank that lobbies, straight-faced, for the revival of Hegel's views on war. The think tank can have an invented name (something bland and consultant-like, e.g., "Institute for Civilizational Renewal Through Occasional Conflict" — you may devise your own, preferably duller and more bureaucratic than that). The piece argues, in good Hegelian spirit, that the modern world has become too comfortable, that a well-timed war would do us the world of good, and that a new generation of leaders should be trained to feel grateful for the contingency and sacrifice war provides.

The satire must be internally faithful to what Hegel actually holds. In particular, ground the jokes in these real features of the view, stated in your own words (do not use quotation marks for any of this):

- In the Philosophy of Right (especially the sections around §324), Hegel argues that war, in which the state and its members face contingency and the threat of death, prevents the stagnation that would set in under a condition of permanent peace; he compares lasting peace to a dead calm in which the winds no longer blow, and suggests that this exposure to contingency preserves what he calls the ethical health of a people. The health analogy is essential: the op-ed should exploit it (prescriptions, dosages, wellness industry), and the stagnation/standing-water image is fair game.
- Hegel denies that war is an absolute evil or a mere accident; the view rests on his account of the state as an ethical whole whose members find their actuality and honor in serving it, and on his claim that in war the community takes priority over the private pursuits of civil society.
- Hegel also holds that in war the enemy must not be treated as a criminal and that, historically, nations emerge from wars with renewed vigor and inner cohesion.
- The view is explicitly not cosmopolitan: Hegel thinks there is no world state or universal authority above states, so international relations are governed by treaties that bind only contingently, with war the final arbiter when states' claims collide. The think tank can find this prospect bracing.

The target of the satire is the argument itself — its consequences and its characteristic tone, not a strawman. Exaggerate real features; do not invent new ones. Every claim the think tank makes should be one the position can actually accommodate. The humor should come from getting Hegel right: a reader who knows the Philosophy of Right should laugh in recognition and judge the satire fair, while a general reader should still find it funny and legible. Keep the voice deadpan throughout — no winking, no authorial asides, no "of course this is monstrous." The reductio must emerge from the reader's own recoil, not from any signal in the text that the writer shares it. End with a flourish that is simultaneously a genuine statement of the view and, to a normal reader, quietly horrifying.

One structural requirement: at some point the think tank should concede that modern war is much too destructive to serve the purpose, and should therefore recommend, in keeping with its mission, the regular cultivation of the *dispositions* that war would instill — a curriculum of grateful acceptance of contingency, suspension of private interest, and occasional sacrifice of members — while continuing to hope, as a matter of principle, that real wars do eventually resume. The comedy here should come from the tension the view can never quite resolve: whether the virtue is in the thing or in the attitude, and how a think tank monetizes the difference.

Do not quote anything from Hegel verbatim; paraphrase his claims in plain language. Do not cite or name any living scholar, and do not include a bibliography or references. Before writing, think carefully about the structure of the piece: what the through-line is, where each of the Hegelian commitments above gets its moment, and how the closing flourish lands. You may brainstorm briefly first, but deliver only the finished op-ed, under a headline, in the think tank's voice.
