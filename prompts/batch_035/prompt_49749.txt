This is for a reading group on predictive processing and I keep going in circels.

My labmate thinks the free energy principle refutes itself, and I half-believe him. His argument:

(1) Things that persist must, on average, occupy a limited set of states — otherwise they'd dissolve into their surroundings.
(2) Surprise, in the technical sense, is just negative log probability, so a persistent thing is, on average, in low-surprise states.
(3) The FEP says organisms minimize surprise two ways: by updating their generative models to better predict sensory input, and by acting on the world so their predictions come true.
(4) But the low-surprise-ness of a persistent thing's states doesn't have to be something the thing does. Natural selection keeps lineages in their characteristic states by killing off the ones that drift. A candle flame stays a candle flame by riding a steady energy gradient. A rock stays a rock because gravity and chemistry keep it where it is.
(5) Whatever the mechanism, the result is the same: a thing whose states are unsurprising, given that thing. And per (3), that's all minimizing free energy amounts to.
(6) So the FEP applies to rocks, flames, hurricanes, and whole lineages just as much as to brains — things that plainly don't perceive, learn, or infer anything.
(7) The FEP is supposed to be a theory of perception, action, and cognition. A principle satisfied by a rock is a theory of nothing. So the FEP is empty.

I offered the obvious reply — that only systems with generative models should count — and he said that just guts (5): I'd be adding a condition the bare math never contained, and at that point the principle isn't explaining anything, it's a fancy redescription of the class of systems I already cared about. He also claims some FEP people are happy to bite the bullet and say sure, even a snowflake or a candle flame is doing it, in some minimal sense, since all you need is the right kind of boundary between thing and environment.

So: where does the arguement go wrong, if it does? Which premise is the weakest, why did it look fine at first, and what does rejecting it cost? Is there a third way that's neither "rocks need not apply" by fiat nor "everything does it, isn't that elegant"? I'd obviously like the FEP to come out nontrivial, but I don't want to win that by stipulation. And please don't just give me the textbook gloss on what variational free energy is — I've read it, it doesn't settle this.
