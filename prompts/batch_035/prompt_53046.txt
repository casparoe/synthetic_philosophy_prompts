I'm a philosophy professor who chairs the Q&A portion of my department's colloquium series. Next week's speaker is a philosopher known for defending an Orwellian account of the color phi phenomenon: her view is that in the apparent-motion case, subjects are conscious of the two flashed spots exactly when the flashes occur, and it is only a very rapid post-experiential rewrite of memory that makes the subsequent report include an illusory interpolated color sweep. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the position. At least one should press on the speed the rewrite would require — the report changes within a fraction of a second, far faster than any ordinary memory revision we know of — and one should take up her response to Dennett's argument that if the revision is fast enough, there is no longer any fact of the matter about when the experience "actually" happened, since every record, internal and external, then agrees. Include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give for each question, and close with a question about what empirical result would count in favor of her view over the Stalinesque alternative. Keep the questions on the timing of experience rather than on her career or general method.
