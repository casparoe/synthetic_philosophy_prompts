I've been thinking about the old heap puzzle on my own: take a heap of sand and remove grains one at a time; at some point it's no longer a heap, yet no single removed grain seems to make the difference. My conclusion is that there must be an exact boundary after all — for any collection of grains there is a fact of the matter whether it is a heap, perhaps even an exact number of grains where it tips over — it's just a fact we could never know, since nothing in our experience marks it. I assume the same holds for "tall", "bald", and "rich". I find I can live with this, but I want to know what would count against it. Please give me the arguments, cases, and observations that should move me, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and say for each whether a committed defender of my view would concede it as a genuine problem or dismiss it as a confusion. I'd be especially interested in anything showing that my view, once qualified to handle objections (for instance by letting the boundary shift between speakers or occasions), has become insulated from any possible counterexample — and if it has, whether that's a defect in a view of this kind or just the price of the position. For what it's worth, I think I'd be most moved by a demonstration that my view forces some consequence that even I couldn't swallow.
