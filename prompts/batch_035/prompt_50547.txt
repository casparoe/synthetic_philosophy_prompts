My brother-in-law Amit runs a low-stakes betting pool at our family gatherings, and I have been keeping notes on his choices. The pool has two urns: Urn K contains exactly 50 red and 50 black balls, while Urn U contains 100 balls that are red and black in unknown proportions. All the bets below pay $100 if the stated event occurs when a single ball is drawn, and $0 otherwise. His track record so far:

1. Offered a choice between betting on red from Urn K and red from Urn U, he chose the K bet; when we ran the same comparison with black, he again chose the K bet.
2. Offered $45 for sure versus a fair coin flip paying $100 on heads, he took the flip; when the sure amount was raised to $60, he took the sure thing.
3. In our three-color round, an urn held exactly 30 red balls plus 60 balls that were black and yellow in unknown proportions. He preferred betting on red to betting on black, and, separately, preferred betting on black-or-yellow to betting on red-or-yellow.

Next week it is my turn to design a choice for him, and I am planning to offer: an official raffle ticket with an exactly 25% chance of winning $100, versus $100 if the ball drawn from Urn U is black. Predict what he will choose. In your answer, I would like three things. First, lay out which decision rules can fit his entire track record, and in particular say whether his paired choices in item 1 already rule out explaining him as someone who simply assigns Urn U some single subjective probability of red, and what the pattern in item 3 adds. Second, say what each surviving rule predicts about the raffle ticket versus the Urn U bet — I want to know which rules come apart on this case and which happen to agree. Third, give me your best guess with reasons, and then describe one further choice I could offer after this one whose answer would best discriminate between the rules that agree on the raffle-ticket case.
