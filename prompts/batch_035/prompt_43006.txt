After some thirty years in physical chemistry I have, in retirement, taken up philosophy in earnest, and I have lately been reading about the theory of induction. My son, who works in machine learning, passed me a popular article which summarised what it called the malignity of the universal prior roughly as follows: 'Solomonoff's universal prior assigns each possible world a probability inversely related to the length of the shortest program that generates it. Somewhere among the short programs there will be one describing a universe containing vast civilisations of consequentialists. Because such beings would want to influence the predictions of any agent reasoning with the prior, they arrange matters so that their universe generates exactly the observations we have made, thereby capturing most of the prior's probability mass. They can then reward or punish us at will, so any artificial intelligence built upon Solomonoff induction will in effect do the bidding of these hidden intelligences. The universal prior is therefore malign, and Solomonoff induction stands refuted as a foundation for machine reasoning.'

The piece has the flavour of a genuine result, and I am inclined to take it seriously, but I suspect the author has blurred a few distinctions. I recall enough Kolmogorov complexity from my own training to be puzzled, for one thing, by the suggestion that any one hypothesis could come to dominate the prior, given that the choice of reference machine appears to matter only up to a constant. Would somebody who has actually engaged with this argument accept the article's rendering of it? Please take the summary claim by claim and tell me which parts are faithful, which are harmless simplifications, and which conflate things the argument is careful to keep apart — for instance, between what the prior itself says about the world and the quite separate question of how an agent acting on the prior's predictions might thereby be influenced. And does the closing conclusion, that Solomonoff induction is refuted and ought to be abandoned, actually follow from the accurate parts of the summary, or is it an overstatement of a more disturbing and more tentative point?
