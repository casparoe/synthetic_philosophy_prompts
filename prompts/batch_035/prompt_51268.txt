I've been arguing with a classmate about whether "Why is there something rather than nothing?" is a real question, and I want an outside estimate of where our disagreement lives. My view is that it is a substantive demand for contrastive explanation: we ask why there is anything at all in the same way we ask why the sample turned blue rather than red, and contrastive explanation requires that the losing alternative be genuinely possible — so the question is live only if absolute nothingness is a metaphysically possible state. Her view is that the demand never gets a grip: any explanation must cite something that exists, so every answer either appeals to something whose existence is itself unexplained (begging the question) or terminates in a brute fact, and once you see that, the feeling that an answer is still owed should evaporate rather than persist as an itch.

My credence that the question is substantive and has a non-trivial answer is about 0.45; hers is about 0.1. Please give me your own number, but more important than the number is the decomposition. Suggested terms, to which you should add any you think belong: (a) the probability that absolute nothingness — no concrete things, no laws, no abstracta — is metaphysically possible rather than incoherent or impossible; (b) the probability that, given its possibility, a contrastive explanation of the totality of concrete things is even coherent, as opposed to explanations that always explain some things by appeal to other things; (c) the probability that, given coherence, some candidate actually obtains — a necessary existent, explanation by abstract facts, "nothing is unstable," or a brute fact accepted without the demand dissolving; and (d) the probability that my classmate is right that the residual sense of an unanswered question, after brute fact is admitted, is a cognitive illusion rather than evidence of incompleteness. Assign a rough probability and one line of justification to each term, show how they combine into your final credence, and identify the term your number is most sensitive to. Then say which of the two of us your figure suggests is wrong, and what exactly we are most likely disagreeing about. Please keep the entire reply under 350 words.
