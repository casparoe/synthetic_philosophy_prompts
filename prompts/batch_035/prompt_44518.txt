I'm preparing a review essay on whether famines are best classified as natural disasters or political crimes, and before I adjudicate anyone's answer I want the specification fixed in advance. Write me the desiderata: what an adequate theory of famine causation must deliver, with the case or consideration behind each. It must get right Bengal in 1943, where aggregate foodgrain availability was not dramatically below that of non-famine years yet several million died while the wartime government rationed and protected Calcutta's workers and let the countryside bear the shock; the Irish famine of the late 1840s, where substantial food exports continued alongside mass mortality and relief was hemmed in by doctrinal commitment to the market and the workhouse; and the Chinese famine of 1959–61, where there was no market whose entitlements could fail — the state requisitioned grain on the basis of inflated output reports and punished the truth-telling that would have triggered relief from above. It must respect the distinction between a decline in food availability and a collapse in particular groups' ability to command food; between deaths by starvation and the usually larger toll of disease and debility in the aftermath; and between a disaster caused and a disaster permitted. It must be consistent with the regularity that no sizable famine has occurred in a functioning democracy with a free press and an active opposition, and with the awkward fact that famines have coexisted with the export of food from the affected region. And it must say something about the boundary case the famine framework was not built for: chronic, non-famine undernutrition, which kills more people every year than famines do but generates no discrete crisis to be explained. For each requirement, commit to whether it is non-negotiable or a prejudice of one camp — the anti-Malthusian school, the developmentalist state, the humanitarian relief sector — and if some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable, as I suspect is true of treating omissions as causes while retaining a notion of political crime that demands culpable conduct, say so and take a definite position on which requirement to drop. Then, briefly, score the two obvious contenders — the food-availability-decline view and the entitlement-failure view — against your own list, and close with your own committed verdict on the classification question: natural disaster, political crime, or something the dichotomy as framed misses.
