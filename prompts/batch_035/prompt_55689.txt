Quick background: I help run a monthly philosophy meetup at a bar — smart crowd but zero academic training — and next month's theme is vagueness, the sorites stuff, heaps of sand, the whole deal. I want a decision tree that sorts people into a position on vagueness based on what they say about cases, not on which label they've heard of. It'll be a printed one-page handout.

Build it top down from the logical space rather than from the standard reading list. Branch nodes should be concrete questions a total beginner can answer honestly. The kind of thing I have in mind: watching grains get removed one at a time and being asked "is this a heap?" after each removal — do you feel pressure to say yes all the way down, to flip at some point even though you can't say where, or to say that past some point the question just stops being true-or-false? Or: "there is some exact number of grains below which it's not a heap" — is that absurd-but-true, absurd-and-false, or not even a well-posed claim?

A few specific requirements. Three cases have to show up as nodes somewhere: the forced march (flip vs. no flip vs. answers stop having truth values), higher-order vagueness (is "borderline" itself borderline, and does a view that flatly says "no" get refuted by that), and the "already determinate now" case — if it's already settled that exactly one of "that's a heap" / "that's not a heap" will turn out right, does that make "heap or not-heap" true already, before anyone precisifies anything? That last one matters to me because it's basically the only thing that separates the classical flavor of supervaluationism from the nonclassical one. Between 6 and 8 branch questions total, max 12 leaves. For leaves: epistemicism, supervaluationism (split classical vs. nonclassical if you can pull it off), three-valued and other many-valued logics, degree theories, contextualism, nihilism, subvaluationism (the "both true and false" move), plus any unnamed leaf the logical space seems to demand.
