Eight months ago a friend loaned me a book on Madhyamaka Buddhism, and I have been working through its ideas on my own ever since — no formal training, just slow reading and long walks. In the online discussion group I belong to, one member keeps invoking the slogan 'emptiness is empty' as though it were a trump card, most recently to shut down my question of whether emptiness itself might have some kind of intrinsic nature. That question seems to me exactly the sort of thing Nāgārjuna's tetralemma — his fourfold refusal of 'it is', 'it is not', 'it both is and is not', 'it neither is nor is not' — was built to handle rather than to dismiss, but I don't know enough to say so confidently. Could you lay out the distinct precise claims the slogan might be making, from the modest reading that emptiness is just an absence and so not itself a thing, up to the radical reading that every thesis about emptiness, including this one, applies to itself and somehow dissolves; and for each reading tell me whether it is coherent, whether the tetralemma actually commits anyone to it, and whether it would genuinely license waving away my question. I would like a menu of readings with a verdict on each, written plainly enough that I can bring it back to the group.
