I'm circulating a short position statement on promising at my moral philosophy reading group and want a consistency check before I send it, since I suspect at least one of these commitments causes trouble but I can't see where. Here's the package: (1) I'm an internalist about practical reasons in the Humean spirit — nothing counts as a reason for me unless it connects somehow to desires, ends, or concerns I already have; nothing can create a reason in me from nowhere. (2) Promising is genuinely reason-creating: when I promise, I come to have a new reason to do the promised thing that did not exist a moment before, and it binds even when keeping the promise cuts against all my interests and even when no one would be harmed by my breaking it — I'd still be obligated to keep a promise made to someone who has since died, with no one else ever knowing of it. (3) The ground of promissory obligation is the social practice of promising itself, whose norms are constituted by the practice in something like the way the rules of chess are constituted by the game — not a contract, not a natural duty. (4) The practice exists, and is vindicated, because it is mutually beneficial, and that genealogy is essential to understanding why we have it at all. (5) I'm confident that the story on which promises bind because keeping them serves your long-term self-interest cannot be the right story, whatever partial truth it contains. Which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and — ordered by severity — what would have to give, and what each possible revision would cost me?
