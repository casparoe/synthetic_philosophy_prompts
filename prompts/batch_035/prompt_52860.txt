I'm drafting the framing document for a small workshop on responsibility gaps for autonomous systems, and I'd like to open it with a bar that any proposed account has to clear, rather than the usual literature survey. Please write the desiderata: exactly five numbered requirements that an adequate account of responsibility for harms involving autonomous systems must meet. Each item should be one or two sentences — the requirement itself, together with the case or consideration that motivates it — and the final sentence of each item should state what consideration would make you weaken or abandon that requirement, so that the list displays its own pressure points rather than presenting itself as settled. No introduction and no conclusion: just the five items, nothing else. Between them, the five requirements should force a position on all of the following, with you deciding how to distribute them across the items: (a) whether the so-called gap is a genuine absence of responsibility or only a mismatch between responsibility and our practices of holding responsible, and what would distinguish the two; (b) what separates an entity that could in principle be a responsible agent from one that can only ever be an object of regulation, given that we already blame corporations in some attenuated way, and dogs, but not weather; (c) the case of a learning system that behaves differently in deployment than it did in certification, so that no one's earlier decision corresponds to the later harm, and the question of whether responsibility in such a case attaches to decisions, to design dispositions, or to something else entirely; (d) the standard move of closing the gap by spreading responsibility across the many hands in the supply and operation chain, and why distributing it ever more thinly may amount to laundering it rather than locating it; and (e) the distinction between backward-looking blame and forward-looking remedy, since an account can succeed at assigning liability while still leaving no one answerable in the sense that victims of the harm actually seek.
