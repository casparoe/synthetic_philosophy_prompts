Before I commit to any theory of truth, I want to know what I should be demanding of one in the first place. Write the desiderata for an adequate resolution of the Liar paradox: the principles a solution must preserve or else restrict in a principled way — the T-schema for truth, capture and release holding in the object language, compositionality of truth over conjunction and quantification, classicality of the background logic, conservativeness over the base arithmetic, revenge-immunity (whether the theory's own vocabulary can state the fault it diagnoses in the liar sentence, for instance by calling it untrue rather than merely neither true nor false), and coverage of near relatives such as Curry's paradox, which uses no negation and so slips past any fix that only weakens negation. For each desideratum, give the case behind it, mark which are non-negotiable and which are prejudices of particular camps, and flag any subset that the Tarski-style limitative results show to be jointly unsatisfiable — that part I most want to see. Say which single desideratum eliminates the most contenders. Close with how Kripke-style fixed-point theories, paraconsistent dialetheism, and paracomplete revision-style approaches fare against your list — a short paragraph each, no more, since the weight belongs on the criteria.

Structure the response as: Requirements (roughly half of it), Where the list breaks (joint unsatisfiability, about a quarter), Contenders, briefly (three short paragraphs), Confidence (a short closing note in which you say how confident you are in your central claims and single out the point you are least sure of).
