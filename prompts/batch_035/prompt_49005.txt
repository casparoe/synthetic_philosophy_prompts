I write a small newsletter for people who came to Stoicism through self-help books and mostly know it from memes about "control what you can control." I want a decision-tree handout that sorts readers by what they actually believe about the dichotomy of control, not by which label they'd pick for themselves. Start the tree from the bare claim that some things are "up to us" and some aren't, and let each branch node be a concrete case a reader can answer without knowing any Stoic vocabulary: e.g. whether the archer who aims well but accepts that hitting the target isn't hers is getting good advice or being asked to care less, whether you should "try to influence" a job interview outcome you don't control (and what that even means), whether practicing courage is more like practicing free throws or more like the outcome of a game, whether the gambler controls anything about the dice, whether an addict's craving is up to them in the relevant sense, and at least one case about emotion or desire rather than action. Leaves must include: the strict classical reading where only assent and judgment are ever up to us, the modified trichotomy with a middle category of things we influence but don't control, a compatibilist reading on which the dichotomy only works if determinism is true, and a deflationary leaf for readers who think the whole distinction is either trivially true or a confused leftover from providence theology. Note that the strict and deflationary leaves can agree the thing is "just psychology" with opposite valence — if your tree can't seperate those, say so instead of fudging it. Also flag any combination of answers that lands nobody in a leaf, and say whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Around 8 branch questions max, one or two sentences of rationale per leaf. Answer in American English.
