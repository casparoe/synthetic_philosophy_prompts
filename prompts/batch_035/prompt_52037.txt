Been reading the sequences and SSC/ACX comment threads for like a decade now, and there's one thing about the epistemic akrasia literature I've never managed to wrap my head around, so I figure I'll just ask here.

From what I can gather, epistemic akrasia is when you believe some proposition p, but at the same time you believe your evidence doesn't support p — you believe p while believing you shouldn't believe p. And "higher-order evidence" is supposed to be the stuff that generates these cases: evidence about your evidence, e.g. finding out you were sleep-deprived or drunk when you reasoned your way to p, or that you have a known bias pointing in p's direction, or that a reasoner you consider as good as you disagrees with you.

Here's my problem though: from a Bayesian angle this doesn't look like a puzzle at all? Evidence about my evidence is still just... evidence. "You were biased when you concluded p" is a proposition like any other, so I condition on it, my credence in p drops, and we're done. Ordinary updating eats higher-order evidence for breakfast, no special machinery required. And a well-calibrated Bayesian never ends up akratic in the first place, since their credence in p automatically tracks their credence that their evidence supports p. So why is there a whole literature agonizing over this? Feels like the answer is just "flatten the higher-order stuff into your evidence base and update like always," and honestly I suspect the philsophers writing about akrasia are just reinventing an argument that Bayesians settled decades ago — the whole thing smells like people not taking their own updating seriously.

Or am I missing something? I have a nagging feeling there's some self-referential snag in the "just condition on it" move — like maybe the update is being performed by the very reasoning process whose reliability the new evidence is calling into question, and that's supposed to cause trouble somehow? But I can't quite make that thought precise, and honestly it sounds like motivated hand-wringing to me. Someone spell out exactly where the flatten-everything approach goes wrong, or concede that akrasia is a non-problem for anyone with a working prior?
