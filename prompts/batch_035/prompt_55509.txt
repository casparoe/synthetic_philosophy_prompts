In Consciousness Explained, Dennett directs his fire at what he calls Cartesian materialism, the view that there is, as he puts it, "a crucial finish line or boundary somewhere in the brain, marking a place where the order of arrival equals the order of 'presentation' in experience because what happens there is what you are conscious of." The multiple drafts model is supposed to replace this finish line with a picture of many parallel content-fixations, each able to influence behavior as soon as it occurs, with no privileged place or moment at which contents are presented. A friend of mine claims he can build a Cartesian theater out of Dennett's own materials, and although the argument now looks too strong to me, I cannot locate its flaw.

(1) Whenever a subject makes even the simplest perceptual or introspective report — say, reporting which of two briefly flashed colored lights came first — some single output process in the brain must compose and initiate that report.

(2) A process can include in its output only what has already causally reached it; it cannot make use of content that has not yet arrived.

(3) So for any given report there is a last moment before its initiation at which content can still influence it — a convergence deadline — and by that deadline every content the report is about must have arrived at the output process.

(4) Those contents come from many distributed drafts, so what arrives at the deadline is a definite, integrated state that fixes what the subject will say: everything the subject reports about the experience, arriving at one place at one time, in an order that determines the report.

(5) A place where distributed contents arrive in the order that fixes what the subject is conscious of and reports is precisely what Dennett's characterization of Cartesian materialism describes.

(6) So for every reportable experience there is a Cartesian theater; and since Dennett holds that a content's being conscious consists in its availability to inform report and behavior, every conscious experience passes through one.

(7) Therefore the multiple drafts model is committed to the very theater it was built to demolish.

Here is why I distrust the conclusion: if arriving together at an output process before a deadline suffices for a theater, then any robot that fuses its camera and lidar data before deciding to brake has a Cartesian theater, and Dennett's target shifts from a seductive error to a trivially universal feature of control systems. That suggests that somewhere between (4) and (5) the phrase changes meaning — but I cannot see where, since everything a report is about does have to be somewhere at once. Tell me which premise to reject and why it looked acceptable, and then price the rejection: in particular, does your diagnosis leave intact Dennett's claim about the phi illusion — that there is no fact of the matter as to whether the apparent color change midway between the flashes was experienced and then misremembered, or edited before being experienced at all? I would most like to keep (1) and (2); I could live with giving up (6). Step (5) I cannot evaluate, because I cannot tell whether it is supposed to be a definition or a substantive thesis, and I would appreciate it if you addressed that directly.
