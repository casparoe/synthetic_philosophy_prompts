Design a scenario in which someone's practical fate turns on the scope of a negation containing a definite description that fails to refer — an ordinary, non-academic setting such as an insurance tribunal, a disciplinary committee, or a licensing hearing — in which a plain assertion of the form the F is not G is made, where nothing satisfies F, and where the panel must record whether the assertion came out true. Constraints: the scenario should be no longer than a paragraph; it must not use stock examples (no kings of France, no first-man-on-Mars); and it should not merely stipulate that one reading is intended, since the case is meant for a logic-and-language tutorial where stipulating the verdict rather than generating it from the two analyses counts as cheating.

After the scenario, do three things. First, show how Russell's quantificational analysis makes the utterance true on the wide-scope reading of negation and false on the narrow-scope reading, spelling out both paraphrases. Second, explain what a Strawsonian would say instead: that the utterance carries a failed presupposition and so lacks a truth value on either reading, and note what the tribunal should then do with it. Third, identify which feature of the case is doing the work — what it is about the scenario that makes the scope question matter rather than being a curiosity — and say how a defender of the presupposition account would resist the suggestion that a panel can ever be obliged, in practice, to treat such an utterance as true.
