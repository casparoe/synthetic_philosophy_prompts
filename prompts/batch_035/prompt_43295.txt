I keep reading that acausal trade works by each agent simulating the other to predict whether they'll follow through on their half of the deal. But that seems to kill the whole idea: simulating anything even roughly as smart as you has to cost way more compute than the trade is worth, and if your simulation is only approximate you can't trust the prediction anyway, so the rational move is to defect like you would against any unenforceable contract. So isn't acausal trade one of those ideas that sounds deep but cashes out to nothing -- the simulation overhead eats the gains in every realistic case? Where exactly am I going wrong, or am I?
