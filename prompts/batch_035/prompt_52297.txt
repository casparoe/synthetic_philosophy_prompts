I'm taking a philosophy of mind course, and we've just finished the unit on Dennett's multiple drafts model, where our seminar ended exactly where the literature seems to have been stuck for decades. Dennett's side says there is no central place where contents "come together" for consciousness: brain events are edited in parallel, nothing is fixed until probing or action makes it so, and the famous cases where memory and experience seem to come apart should be handled by denying that there was ever a determinate fact about what was experienced. The other side — the Searle and Block complaints, roughly — says all of this is excellent work on access and report but changes the subject: it explains what consciousness does rather than what it is like, and in dissolving the "Cartesian theater" it quietly denies the very data a theory of consciousness was supposed to explain. Everyone in my seminar knows both arguments cold, and nobody has budged. My working hypothesis is that the impasse is really about the epistemic status of phenomenology: one side treats the apparent stream of unified experience as a datum that any theory must accommodate, the other treats it as a theoretical posit the theory is free to reinterpret. Is that right, or is the disagreement substantive, or verbal, or two projects sharing a name? Diagnose it using what each side actually claims, and tell me what, if anything — a distinction, an experimental result, a reformulated question — would count as progress.
