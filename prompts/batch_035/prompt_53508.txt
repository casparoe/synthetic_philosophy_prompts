While researching, I read a chapter from a pop book on decision-making that had this summary of the so-called spectrum arguments from philosophy, and I want to sanity check it before I echo any of it in print:

"The classic case is the self-torturer, from a famous paper by Larry Temkin. A man wears a device that can deliver electric shocks at a thousand intensity levels, and he can press a button to bump himself up one level, getting paid a small sum each time he does. The difference between adjacent levels is too small to notice, so every pair of neighboring levels is equally good — and since he's paid to press, the higher level is actually the better of the two. Temkin used cases like this to prove that better-than is intransitive: small advantages don't add up, so transitivity, which economists treat as an axiom of rationality, is simply false. Philosophers have largely accepted this result, and it means that any framework that ranks options on a single scale — utilitarianism, cost-benefit analysis, expected utility theory — is resting on a mistake."
