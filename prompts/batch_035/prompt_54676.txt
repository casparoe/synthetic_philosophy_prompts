Write a deadpan internal FAQ for the "Control & Evaluation" team of a fictional AI lab, numbered Q&A format, about 600 words. Setup: the lab has just certified a frontier model as safe to deploy, not because anyone thinks it's aligned, but because their control measures are built to work even if it isn't — the FAQ is for employees confused about why everyone's so calm. The target of the satire is the control-vs-alignment distinction, or really the version of it where "assume the model is adversarial" quietly slides into "so stop asking whether it is." The jokes have to come from real features of AI control work: worst-case evaluations, cheap-but-untrusted model monitors watching a more capable model, honeypots and canary opportunities, staged deployment, the tradeoff that letting the model do more means watching it less, and the awkward fact that the main way you test your control measures is asking the model itself to try to break them. Keep the FAQ internally consistent — every answer should be defensible by control's own logic, including the one about what happens when control fails ("control doesn't fail; the safety case's assumptions expire") — and never state outright the obvious worry that a lab which has given up on alignment is exactly the lab whose control measures are load-bearing. The reader should feel that worry without being told it. A control researcher should find the piece fair, not a strawman. Maybe let one employee's questions recur with progressively shorter answers.
