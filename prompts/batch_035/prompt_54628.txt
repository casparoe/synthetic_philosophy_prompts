I've been in a running argument with my brother-in-law, a composer, about where creativity actually comes from, and I want to drag it out of the "well, some people just have it" territory it always dies in. My position is basically a version of Hofstadter's knob-twiddling idea: that what looks like creation from nothing is almost always variation on a deep-learned template — you take a concept you've internalized and turn its dials (tempo, key, register, register of cultural reference, degree of rule-breaking) until you land somewhere that feels new, and the artistry is mostly in which knobs and how far, plus knowing when a familiar setting has become a cliché. He thinks this describes hack work only: on his view the pieces that actually changed music weren't dial-adjustments of anything, they were genuine breaks that redefined what the knobs even were, and he points at stuff like the move to atonality as the kind of thing no amount of knob-turning inside tonal space gets you to. I have a response — that this is just knob-twiddling one level up, twiddling the representation rather than the output — but I notice I can't tell whether that response is doing real work or just relabeling his point so I win, and it bothers me that we could both look at the same piece and each say "see, obviously what I meant."

So give me your credence that the knob-twiddling picture, understood at some level of abstraction, is basically right about the vast majority of creative work — not a poetic gloss but an actual explanation. I want a number, but more importantly the decomposition: things like, whether creativity is at bottom analogical transfer between compressed internal templates; whether there's a coherent distinction between twiddling the output and twiddling the representational space, and if so whether the second one still counts as knob-twiddling or whether it smuggles in exactly the "genuine novelty" notion the model was supposed to explain away; whether the historical cases people cite as counterexamples (non-Euclidean geometry, twelve-tone stuff, whatever) really are counterexamples or just deep knobs nobody had noticed were knobs; and whether the model is even falsifiable or whether it can absorb any case by finding a knob post hoc. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question with a line of justification, combine them however you think is right (say if you think they're not independent), and tell me which term you're least confident in and what evidence would actually move it — like, if a model trained only on variation and recombination could never produce a new representational axis, would that settle it?

For calibration: I'm at maybe 0.75 that some level-of-abstraction version of the view is right, he's at maybe 0.25, and I suspect our disagreement is really about whether "turning knobs one level up" and "genuine break" are two things or one. So also tell me: given two people at 0.75 and 0.25, which sub-question do they most likely diverge on? I'd rather know where the fight actually lives than keep trading anecdotes about Stravinsky.
