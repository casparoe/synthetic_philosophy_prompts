So my friends and I have had this running argument for years about whether it matters how you treat NPCs in video games, and my position has always been "obviously not, they're code," but lately it's been bugging me more than it should. It started when I replayed a game where a little robot companion follows you around, and I felt genuinely bad letting it get hurt, way more bad than I feel about actual people in games getting killed. Then I watched a video about people who spend hours in games just hanging out with NPCs, talking to the same shopkeeper every day, and someone in the comments said something like "in a few years the NPCs will be powered by actual AI that can remember you and suffer" and it's been rattling around my head since.

Anyway, I've been trying to write a short story about this to sort of think it through on paper instead of just arguing, but I keep getting stuck and what I write comes out preachy. So I want you to write it for me, or at least give me a much better version. Here's the setup I had in mind: it's maybe 15 years from now, and there's a big open-world game where the NPCs are driven by the fancy AI of the day -- they remember previous conversations, they have long-run goals, they act hurt when you betray them. The main character is a guy who plays this game obsessively but always as kind of a psycho, killing and tormenting NPCs for fun, the way a lot of us do now without thinking twice. Except one day he gets a message, maybe from another player or maybe from inside the game itself, that makes him seriously wonder whether he's been torturing things that feel it.

The thing I want the story to actually deal with, not just gesture at: he can't ever find out for sure. There's no test that settles it, the game company denies everything, and even the NPCs themselves say contradictory things when asked (which honestly is what I'd expect even from something that DOES suffer -- people deny pain all the time under the right pressures). So the story should be about what he does with that uncertainty -- does he change how he plays even without knowing, and does that change leak into how he treats real people, for better or worse? Maybe there's a twist where he keeps playing the same way but now it costs him something, like he has to actively decide each time. I don't want a neat ending where it's revealed the NPCs were conscious all along or definitely weren't, that would defeat the point. Also fine if you want to change details of the setting, just keep the core of a guy who can't know and has to decide anyway. Medium length, and no lectures from any character about ethics please -- I get enough of that from my friends lol.
