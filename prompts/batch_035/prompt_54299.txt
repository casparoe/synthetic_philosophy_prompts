At an interdisciplinary workshop on machine agency, I will be defending the following thesis: the question "is an LLM an agent, a simulator, or something else?" is miscast, because agency is not a property of the model's weights but of the control loop in which the model is deployed — the scaffolding, memory, tools, and stopping conditions around it — so the same base model can be a competent agent in one harness and a mere text-completer in another, and disputes about the model's "true nature" are really tracking features of the loop that could have been re-engineered at will. The audience consists of a philosophy of mind reading group, including two committed functionalists who will suspect that this makes agency uselessly implementation-relative; several ML engineers who will insist the question is simply empirical, a matter of measuring task performance; and one AI-safety researcher who will press hard on the difference between simulating a goal and having one. Prepare my Q&A sheet: the objections each of these groups will actually raise, phrased in their own vocabulary, ordered by how much damage each would do to my thesis; for each objection, a two-minute reply and a note on where that reply is soft; the one objection I am hoping nobody asks; and the point I should concede up front if the talk is to survive the question period.
