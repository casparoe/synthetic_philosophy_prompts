After a long argument last night with my friend, who is a convinced constructive empiricist, I am trying to work out precisely where our disagreement lives, and I want your credences to help me map it. The proposition in question is epistemic structural realism: roughly, that our best current scientific theories are approximately true in what they say about the structure of the unobservable world — the relations, the equations, the mathematical form of the laws — while remaining agnostic or mistaken about the intrinsic natures of whatever bears those relations. My credence is about 0.6; hers, I would guess, is about 0.15, her line being that "structure" is a word smuggled in to do all the work that "the observable" refuses to do. Give me your own probability estimate for the proposition, decomposed as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. The first four items should be sub-credences with a number and a one-line justification apiece: (1) whether the no-miracles argument carries any real weight against sociological or strongly empiricist explanations of scientific success; (2) whether the pessimistic meta-induction is correct that claims about the natures of unobservables are historically discardable — caloric, phlogiston, the mechanical underpinnings of the electromagnetic aether — or whether that record is cherry-picked; (3) whether there is a principled and non-trivial distinction between structural claims and claims about intrinsic nature that does not collapse under Newman-style objections, where structural content is exhausted once cardinalities are fixed and everything substantive is pushed onto a further abstract constraint; and (4) whether underdetermination of theory by evidence is a genuine constraint on the realism debate or a philosopher's artifact that no working scientist need respect. The fifth item should give your combined credence for the proposition, name the single sub-question whose resolution would move that number the most, and say whether the person whose position most likely diverges from yours on it is my friend at 0.15 or me at 0.6.
