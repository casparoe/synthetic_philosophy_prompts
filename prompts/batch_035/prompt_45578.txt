I am a recently retired physiologist — most of my career went into glucose homeostasis and the statistical machinery behind clinical reference ranges — and in retirement I have taken up philosophy of medicine, which has unsettled certainties I did not know I held. I now serve on the drug-classification subcommittee of our national formulary body, and we vote next month on an application for which I must write the circulated opinion.

The application: a manufacturer seeks formulary listing of a new drug for adults whose fasting glucose falls in the range our current guidelines label impaired but not diabetic. The drug modestly delays progression to frank diabetes in this population. Our mandate directs us to list drugs that treat diseases, and the phrase is undefined. The manufacturer's case is naturalist through and through: these patients show measurable, progressive subnormal functioning of a homeostatic mechanism — dysfunction in roughly Boorse's sense — and that is what disease is. Two of my colleagues resist on normativist grounds: the condition is asymptomatic, the people affected do not consider themselves ill, and the only harm on the table is an elevated statistical risk of a future disease — harm of a sort that would equally justify calling being fifty a disease. Our internal precedents cut both ways: we declined disease status for the analogous pre-hypertension category, yet we granted it to postmenopausal reductions in bone density, and we could not afterward say what distinguished the two.

Please draft the written opinion I will circulate before the vote. It should recommend one concrete decision — list the drug as treating a disease, deny listing, or list it while reclassifying the condition as a risk factor rather than a disease — give reasons a committee of clinicians and one philosopher can act on, present the manufacturer's biostatistical argument at full strength rather than as a straw man, and state what principle our precedents commit us to whichever way we go. Where my own naturalist instinct may be begging the question, flag it: I have been at this philosophy for two years, and I need the memo to survive our philosopher.
