I just started as the junior-most researcher on a small interpretability team at an AI lab, and my actual job right now is writing up our latest results, which means I get to decide (well, sort of) how we're allowed to talk about what the circuits we find in the model "do." That question detonated at team dinner on Friday, and I was sitting between the two people arguing, so now they both keep pinging me, and I'm honestly out of my depth.

The two: Maya, who did a philosophy PhD on Dennett before defecting to alignment work, and Dmitri, our staff engineer, who flinches at any word that sounds like it attributes a mind to the model.

It started when Maya read my draft aloud, which said the model refuses certain prompts *because* a specific attention head detects something like instructions to produce weapons content. Maya said that's exactly right and I should keep it, because reasons don't need reasoners. Her line was: nobody designed the eye's lens and nothing understood it while it was evolving, but the rationale for the lens is focusing light — that's the reason it's there, even though no reasoner ever represented it. Dennett calls these free-floating rationales. And training is a selection process, she said, so saying the head exists because detecting that pattern reduced loss is giving the actual reason, not a metaphor.

Dmitri said the biology analogy breaks at the crucial joint. Evolution is selection among discrete competitors — a literal population of organisms with variant necks, where the ones whose necks did the useful thing out-reproduced the others. Gradient descent isn't that: it nudges every parameter at once, continuously, toward whatever lowers loss. There's no population of candidate heads where the detector variant beat out rivals. So either the "because" is a false claim about design (we didn't design the head) or it's biology cosplay.

Maya came back that even biologists don't require that competition story — plenty of traits get fixed by drift, or arise once as a mutation, and the function is just whatever the part does that explains why it's still there. She said the "what for" question is separate from the "how come" question and that Darwin's whole contribution was showing you can answer the first without a reasoner in sight. Plus, she said, we have the same test biologists have: ablate the head and the refusal behavior degrades — knock out a gene and you learn what it's for.

Dmitri said ablation proves causal contribution, not function — a heart also makes the thump-thump sound, and if you remove the heart the sound stops, but the heart's function isn't making sounds. You need the selection history to tell you *which* causal consequence is the function, and he'd just argued the training story doesn't have that structure. And then his stronger point: half our units are polysemantic — recruited into several jobs at once — and what a circuit "detects" often depends on which slice of the data distribution you squint at. If we find a duplicate-detector because we went looking for a duplicate-detector, the rationale is partly in the observer, not the network. We are exactly the kind of apes that find agents everywhere, which is why interpretability keeps generating just-so stories that don't replicate.

Maya said picking the grain of analysis and the relevant conditions is what every biologist does too — the stomach has a function, one parietal cell kind of doesn't — and nobody concludes that stomach functions are projections. And dropping reason-talk, she said, doesn't make anyone safer, it just makes the writeup useless: the pure causal-mechanical level is a mile wide and an inch deep, and without functional language you can't say which of the ten thousand things a circuit does is the one that matters.

Dmitri's last word was that he can get everything she wants without the ghost: this configuration was reinforced because it reduced loss on the training distribution — fully causal, says why it's there, no teleology. Maya's last word was that "because it reduced loss" is already a what-for answer wearing a fake mustache: loss is a stand-in for *our* goals, so at minimum the rationale isn't free-floating inside the model, it's ours, smuggled in through the objective function — and once Dmitri admits that, he's admitted reasons into the explanation and they're just haggling about the address.
