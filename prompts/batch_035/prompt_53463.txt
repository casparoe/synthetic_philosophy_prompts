I'm a philosophy professor who teaches collective rationality, but in a former life I spent a decade designing databases and distributed systems. Restate the discursive dilemma — the doctrinal paradox, the fact that proposition-wise majorities can conflict with a constraint the collective is supposed to satisfy — in the vocabulary I actually used then: materialized views versus systems of record, denormalization, derived fields and update anomalies, maintaining invariants across replicas. Be faithful: don't smuggle in the philosophers' framing and present it as translation. Then give me the residue report — what carries over cleanly, what survives only with distortion, and what has no expression in that vocabulary at all.

I'm especially interested in your view on one point. In my old world, a derived field that disagrees with the rows it was computed from is just a stale cache, and the fix is unambiguous: recompute. The discursive dilemma, by contrast, seems to turn on the fact that for a group there is no given answer to which proposition is the source-of-record and which is the derived view — the court is obligated to pronounce on liability, yet the premises are where the individual members' judgments actually live. Does that question — which proposition is constitutive of the collective's commitment and which is computed — have any counterpart in my engineering vocabulary, or is it precisely what that vocabulary cannot say? And if the latter: is the residue a limitation of the engineering frame, or does it reveal that the philosophical dispute is less substantive than it looks — that arguing about the discursive dilemma is, at bottom, arguing about where to put the system of record?
