By day I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized discussion platform, and I've been assigned to a project overhauling how we handle borderline speech — not the obvious spam, doxxing, and threats, which we already dispatch cleanly, but the grey zone where our reviewers disagree wildly with each other and every reversal makes the news. Our written policy is, now that I've read it closely, two philosophies stapled together: one paragraph says we're a neutral venue that doesn't take sides on contested opinions, the next says there's no place on the platform for content that demeans people for who they are. The reviewers have about ninety seconds per flagged item, no legal training, and an escalation queue with a team lead who can absorb perhaps two percent of the volume. I have no law or philosophy background myself; everything I know about the American and European approaches to hate speech I've absorbed from blog posts and one company seminar, so I want the procedure grounded in what those traditions actually distinguish — viewpoint neutrality versus content versus targeting, the American insistence on imminence and concreteness of harm, the European willingness to restrict speech that incites hatred against groups even short of imminent lawless action — not in a summary of a summary. Please write a decision procedure the review team could actually run on a flagged post: the questions in the order they should be asked, what counts as a settled answer at each step, where the procedure stops with a remove, where it stops with an allow, and where it must hand the decision to a human because the question is a values question the procedure cannot answer. Be explicit about the step where the American and European traditions come apart on the same item, since our stapled policy commits us to both, and the procedure has to either quietly pick a winner or punt upward — tell me which it should do and why. Then run it on three items I'm worried about: a post saying adherents of a religion should be barred from holding public office; a satirical meme describing a national group as vermin, posted by someone with a history of sincere political rants; and a sincere doctrinal statement that a sexual minority is disordered, posted in a community where members of that minority are regulars and have asked us to intervene. For each, tell me what the procedure yields, which of the three is the closest call, and where the procedure's answer is one I personally won't like. Finally, tell me honestly where this procedure will misfire — the kinds of cases it will systematically get wrong or flood into escalation — and what no procedure of this kind can do, so I don't oversell it to leadership as an engineering fix for what is really a values problem. Please write the whole thing as continuous prose, the way you'd write an email or memo: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
