I'm a PhD student working on formal axiology, and my reading group has been stuck on the lexical threshold negative utilitarianism thing for months, and I'm trying to figure out what kind of disagreement we're actually having. On one side the threshold people say certain pains (torture, maybe severe depression) sit at a lexical level: no amount of pleasure compensates for them, so adding up happy and unhappy experiences into one scalar total is broken somewhere at the top of the pain scale. On the other side the aggregationists run the pinprick reductio: if any pain at all blocks compensation, one pinprick outweighs a billion blissful lives, which nobody accepts, so thresholds get introduced -- but then the fight just moves to where the threshold sits, and intuitions about that seem to vary with how vividly you describe the pain, which does not inspire confidence. What I keep wondering is whether this is a substantive axiological disagreement at all or something else: a dispute about the functional form of the pleasure-pain tradeoff (do the indifference curves just get very steep, or do they asymptote?), a verbal dispute over what commensurability even means (same currency vs. comparable magnitude), or two questions -- how bad experiences are vs. what's owed to people who undergo them -- being run under one heading. My pet hypothesis is that the pinprick reductio only bites a strawman threshold view that never says pain is infinitely bad, only non-compensable, and if that's right the debate is partly verbal. But I'm honestly not sure. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this is, using what the two sides actually argue, and tell me whether anything -- a formal representation result, a decision-theoretic setup, a distinction nobody's drawn -- could actually move it, or whether we're just trading intuition pumps forever.
