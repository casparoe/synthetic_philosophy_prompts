Taking a philosophy of science course this semester and we just wrapped up the probability interpretation unit, and while reviewing my notes I realized I've half-committed to a bunch of positions without ever checking if they fit together as a package. So here's the list, audit it for me:

(1) Chances are objective features of the world, and quantum mechanics pretty much settles that we need them. (2) Chance attaches to single cases — this atom, this coin toss — not just to long-run frequencies across ensembles. (3) Classical physics is deterministic, so given the exact microstate, the outcome of an ordinary coin flip has probability 0 or 1, full stop. (4) I want everyday chance claims like "this coin has a 50% chance of landing heads" or "there's a 30% chance of rain tomorrow" to be literally true, not just a polite way of talking about my own ignorance. (5) I'm drawn to a propensity reading where chance is a physical tendency built into the setup, and I'd rather NOT have chance depend on whatever the best deductive systematization of all the facts looks like — that seems to make chance partly about us and our preferences for tidy theories. (6) Credences are their own thing and bayesian updating is right for them. (7) I accept something like: if you know the objective chance of an event is p, your rational credence in it should be p. (8) I think there's a fact of the matter about "the" chance of a single event without anyone having to first pick out a reference class.

So: which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like they are, rank the tensions by severity rather than just listing them, and for each one tell me what the cheapest fix would be and what that fix costs me elsewhere. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I honestly don't know where the problem is, so don't pull any punches. For what it's worth I'd be okay with two of these turning out to be about genuinely different senses of probability, but only if that distinction is principled and not just me dodging the issue.
