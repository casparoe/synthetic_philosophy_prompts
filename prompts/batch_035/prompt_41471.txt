Okay here's one for the pile. I keep running into the same fight about whether philosophy makes any progress at all, and it never goes anywhere. On one side you've got the pessimists: we're still arguing free will, we're still arguing what justice is, the same positions get recycled with new vocabulary every generation, and no question has ever been closed the way "is light a wave or a particle" got closed. On the other side the optimists: philosophy makes progress the way math or science does, just slower — distinctions get sharpened, bad arguments die, positions that were one blob split into four well-defined options, and people who say "nothing's changed" just haven't read closely enough. And every few years someone publishes another round of this and neither side budges.

My hypothesis, offered as just a hypothesis, is that the two sides are secretly using different success criteria — the pessimists count settled consensus as the only real progress, while the optimists count refinement of the question itself, and since the criteria never get stated explicitly the argument can't lose. But I'm not sure that's right either, because the pessimist could come back and say that "we refined the question" is exactly what you'd say if you had no way of showing you were closer to anything, and the optimist could reply that even in science most progress is exactly that kind, knowing better what to ask. So maybe it's deeper than a verbal dispute over "progress," maybe it's a genuine disagreement about what philosophy is even for, in which case the stall makes sense.

What I want from you is a diagnosis of this disagreement, not a verdict on whether philosophy progresses. Is it verbal, is it two different questions (a descriptive one about citation and dropout rates in the field vs a normative one about what should count), is it bedrock intuitions about what knowledge is that neither side can argue for without circularity? Use what each side actually says, and be concrete about which claims of the optimists the pessimists have an answer for and which they don't. Then tell me what would count as progress on this question given your diagnosis — some sharper distinction, some way of operationalizing it, maybe a change in what we ask — or argue that no progress should be expected because the question is itself doing something philosophy can't do. Please answer in American English, and don't just give me the standard tour of everyone who's ever written on this, I want the diagnosis.
