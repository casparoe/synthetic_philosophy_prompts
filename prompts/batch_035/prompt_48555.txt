I'm putting together a position statement for a reading group that's been going through Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and before I circulate it I want to know if it actually hangs together. Here's where I've ended up:

1. Fallibilism: you can know ordinary things while some error possibilities remain live — knowledge doesn't demand certainty.
2. Knowledge is closed under known entailment: if I know P, and I know that P entails Q, then I'm positioned to know Q.
3. I know there's a coffee cup on my desk right now.
4. I don't know that I'm not a brain in a vat being fed exactly this experience. That scenario isn't something evidence could ever confirm or rule out, so it's just not something I know.
5. I buy the hinge picture: propositions like "I have a body," "the world has existed for a long time," "this is my hand" function as hinges — certain, but not because of evidence. They're the fixed background that has to stay put for doubts and justifications to mean anything.
6. So hinges aren't known. Insisting "I know I have hands" at the skeptic is a kind of grammatical mistake — the hinge is doing structural work, not reporting an epistemic achievement you earned.
7. But when someone seriously challenges an ordinary claim of mine — say they ask how I know the tree in the yard is really a tree and not a hologram — I'm entitled to just dig in and repeat "that's a tree" as if that settled it, appealing to the hinge like a reason even though per (6) it isn't one.
8. And hinges do change over time: stuff that functioned as a hinge for educated people centuries ago (fixed species, an unmoving earth) doesn't anymore, so hinge certainties are historically contingent even though, at any given moment, they're not up for grabs within the practice.

Something in this set feels unstable to me, but I honestly can't tell whether the culprit is the fallibilism, the closure principle, or how I'm treating hinges. Go through it and tell me which commitments pull against each other and how badly, which tensions are only apparent, and what the cheapest revision would be and what it costs. Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then the reasoning — no preamble, and don't restate my list back to me.
