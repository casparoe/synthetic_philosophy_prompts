I have drafted the opening argument of an essay on creativity, built around the model of concepts that Douglas Hofstadter developed in his essays on analogy-making, and I would like comments on it before I extend it. The draft follows.

Draft (working title: Re-Carving the Theme):

Hofstadter, in the essays surrounding his work on analogy-making programs, sometimes asks us to think of a concept as a machine studded with knobs: to vary a theme is to twiddle the knobs and see what comes out. His letter-string puzzles make the point compactly. Given that the string abc is transformed into abd, what should be done with the string ijk? The candidate answers differ not in the values they produce but in which knob each answer takes itself to be turning — the successor-of-the-final-letter knob, the position-of-the-string-in-the-alphabet knob. Variation, on this picture, is cheap and everywhere; the work of thought lies in deciding which dial is the relevant one.

I want to press the picture into a stronger claim: all variation of a theme divides into two kinds, and the division marks the line between craft and creativity. First-order twiddling: the knobs are fixed by the theme's received description, and the variation consists of new settings. Second-order re-carving: someone notices a dimension along which the theme was already varying, or should have been, for which the received description provides no dial; to record the variation, the description itself has to be re-cut. The test I propose: if a variation can be fully specified in advance by a list of parameter settings under the existing description, it is craft, however dazzling; if specifying it forces a new dimension into the description, it is the real thing.

Reharmonizing a jazz standard is my example of the second kind. Substituting chords does not change the melody; it changes which melody notes count as load-bearing. A note that was a passing tone becomes, under the new harmony, its anchor. Nothing in the sequence of notes differs — but the description of which notes matter does. My suggestion is that this signature, old material unchanged with its description rewritten, is what we track when we call something creative rather than clever, and that the phenomenology of "seeing what was there all along" is simply the re-carving registering.

An anticipated objection is that this is Boden's transformational creativity in new clothes: changing the rules of the conceptual space rather than moving within it. I think the equation is too quick. Boden's distinction is drawn at the level of the whole space of possible works; mine is drawn at the level of a single theme's description, and is meant to explain something her taxonomy leaves silent: why re-carvings feel like discoveries rather than decisions.

If this is right, the implications for credit are uncomfortable. The dial that someone names was, typically, already being twiddled blind by many hands. What we call genius is often just the act of writing down which knob everyone was turning.

I would appreciate comments on three points. First, accuracy: have I represented the knob model fairly, or am I saddling it with commitments it never makes — in particular, the commitment that knobs are supplied by a describable structure, which is doing a lot of work in my two-tier claim? Second, the core question: does the twiddling/re-carving distinction do any work that Boden's exploratory/transformational distinction does not, or does the draft quietly slide between the description of a single theme and the description of the space of works, in a way that collapses my distinction back into hers? And is the reharmonization example genuinely a re-carving, or merely a change in background theory about the same notes — and if the latter, does the argument survive? Third, the closing paragraph: does the conjecture about the distribution of credit follow from anything established earlier, or does the essay end by generalizing from the phenomenological claim, which was itself only a suggestion? Comments on whether the essay needs a second example from a different domain, and what it should be, would also be welcome.
