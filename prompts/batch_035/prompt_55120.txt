I co-moderate a monthly seminar where measurement-focused statisticians argue with AI-safety researchers, and two regulars have spent the past year at war over Goodhart's law — they now finish each other's sentences with objections. One, a selection-effect statistician, holds that Goodhart's law is at bottom a statistical fact: any noisy proxy for what you care about will, once you condition on extreme proxy values or select on it, deliver systematically worse results, with no responsive agent required; regression to the mean, winner's-curse-style selection, and range restriction are the core of the phenomenon, and strategic behavior is just a colorful special case — she notes that this reading is closer to Goodhart's own monetary-policy context than the aphorism about measures ceasing to be good once targeted. The other, an alignment researcher, holds that Goodhart's law proper is a fact about optimization: a measure degrades because something is optimizing it, and the degradation tracks optimization pressure applied to an imperfect proxy — specification gaming and reward hacking in reinforcement learning are the paradigm cases, and folding in the purely statistical cases has bred confusion about what kind of failure is being discussed. Please write the joint statement their adversarial collaboration should produce: the claims both can sign (about correlation, proxies, selection, and optimization pressure — but also about mitigation: the statistician suspects the effect is ineradicable in any noisy-measurement regime and that the fix is never conditioning on extremes, the alignment researcher suspects it is an engineering problem whose shape depends on who or what is doing the optimizing); the exact disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording, including whether their two-camp split is a verbal dispute about the phrase Goodhart's law or a real dispute about the world — if part of it is verbal, say which part and why; the cruxes (I suspect one is whether there are genuine degradation cases with no responsive agent anywhere, as in selecting the top-scoring funds on past returns, and another is whether Darwinian processes — bacteria evolving resistance under an antibiotic regimen, with no one deliberately targeting anything — count as an optimizer responding to a target, which the alignment researcher needs for his side but which threatens to make the statistician's non-agent cases disappear by redefinition); and for each crux, a concrete case, dataset, or experiment both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about the outcome recorded in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a table with one row per crux, with columns for the precise disagreement, the agreed test, and each side's prediction.
