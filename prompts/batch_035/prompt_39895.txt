I'm a software engineer with zero philosophy background, but I got sucked into the spacetime thing after watching a lecture on Newton's bucket (the spinning bucket where the water surface goes concave, and the question of what it's rotating *relative to*), and I've been reading around it for like a year now. As far as I can tell the fight between substantivalists (spacetime is a real thing, a container) and relationists (only relations between events are real, spacetime is bookkeeping) has been going since Newton and Leibniz and it's still somehow alive — people are still writing papers about whether the hole argument killed manifold substantivalism or whether "Leibniz equivalence" patched it up, and whether general relativity vindicates one side or the other or neither.

Here's what confuses me as an outsider. Both sides agree on all the equations. The bucket spins, the water climbs the walls, the light cones are wherever the metric says they are. Nobody disagrees about what any measurement would return. And yet they keep writing at each other. So my engineer's instinct says this has the shape of an argument that could never be settled by more data — kind of like arguing whether a database schema is "real" or just a useful convention for organizing rows, where everyone knows how the queries behave and the rest is vibes. But that can't be the whole story, because serious people keep insisting the physics actually does favor one side: someone told me the bucket argument is supposed to be an *empirical* argument while Leibniz's shift argument is not, others say in GR the metric is a dynamical field so spacetime stops being a "stage" and becomes a player, and then you've got quantum gravity folks talking about spacetime being emergent from entanglement, which sounds like it should matter here but I can't tell if it actually does.

So what I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a substantive disagreement we just can't settle, a verbal one about what we're allowed to call "real", or are the two sides secretly answering different questions (one about ontology, one about what explains inertial effects)? When a relationist says the substantivalist is positng "unnecessary structure," and the substantivalist replies that relations need relata or that the bucket needs something to rotate against — are they even disagreeing about the same thing, or is the word "structure" doing two different jobs? And is there anything — a distinction, a result from physics, a reformulation of the question — that would count as actual progress here, or does the debate persist precisely because it's set up so no observation could touch it? Honestly "the question is ill-posed, here's the version of it that isn't" would be a satisfying answer to me, as long as you show your work.
