So I'm a backend engineer with basically zero philosophy background, but my roommate and I got into a long argument about whether dying is actually bad *for the person who dies*, and I want you to take his side and make the best case you can. My position was the Epicurean one (I think that's the name for it): harm requires a subject, and once you're dead there's no subject around, so death can't be bad for you -- whatever badness there is, it's for the survivors or the world, not you. His position is apparently called the deprivation view: death is bad because it deprives you of the goods the rest of your life would've contained, and the badness lives in the counterfactual comparison (life cut short vs. the life you would've had), not in any state you experience.

Here's the wrinkle that made me write this. I proposed an analogy from work and it's been bugging me since: think of a person like a long-running batch job. On the deprivation view, the harm of death is like killing a job at 40% completion -- the badness isn't felt by the job, it's that the output never gets produced. But it seems like if that's the whole story, then the "victim" of the harm isn't really the dead person, it's the world that missed out on the output -- which feels like it concedes my point: death isn't bad *for you*, it's just bad simpliciter. My roommate thinks that's a confused analogy but couldn't quite say why at 1am.

So: please make an argument for the deprivation view that survives the batch-job objection. Specifically, explain how a counterfactual comparison between an actual life and a longer possible life can be bad *for the person who dies* rather than merely bad for the world or for others. And please also deal with the symmetry objection (if missing out on future decades is bad for me, why isn't the time before I was born bad for me too? I never felt cheated by 1847), because that one always stumped us. Bonus points if you can lean on engineering intuitions somewhere along the way, since process models and counterfactuals are about the only vocabulary I actually trust on this stuff.
