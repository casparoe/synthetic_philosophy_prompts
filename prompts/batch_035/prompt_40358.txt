I help run a small study group on AI safety topics — five of us, mostly software engineers plus one math grad student, everyone can follow a probability argument but nobody has a serious complexity theory background. I got roped into running next month's session on heuristic arguments and the presumption of independence, since the phrase keeps getting name-dropped in things we read and none of us really gets what the project is. So we're on the same page, here's the idea as I understand it (correct me if I've got it wrong, but plan around this content):

A lot of the time there's something we want to know that we can't actually compute — whether some huge boolean circuit ever outputs 1, or the fraction of inputs on which a hash function gives an even number of 1s in its output bits. What we can realistically do is compute a few cheap facts and then reason: "if these components behaved like independent random variables, the answer would be about X; and I can't find any cheap way to detect that they don't behave independently, so I'll go with X." Everyone does this constantly — it's basically the move behind trusting that nobody will find a hash collision in your lifetime. The formalization program asks whether that move can be made principled: define what counts as a heuristic argument (some bounded computation that spits out an estimate of the quantity), and then try to show something like — if no heuristic argument you can actually run distinguishes your quantities from independent ones, you're entitled to treat them as independent, at least against anyone whose also limited to running heuristic arguments. Whether that last step is a real justification or just dressed-up laziness is exactly what I want the group to be able to argue about by the end of the session.

What has to be in place before the formal question even looks motivated rather than pedantic (my instinct is to start from "why is it fine to treat a hash function's outputs on different messages as coin flips," but maybe there's a better entry point?). What should I openly refuse to cover in one session (the formal definitions of heuristic estimators? whatever the actual soundness conditions are?) and say so out loud.
