A friend of mine who works in computational neuroscience and I spent an entire evening arguing about multiple realizability, and I want to keep pressing on the position she ended up defending, because it inverts the usual dialectic and I can't see how functionalism should answer it. She was willing to grant the orthodox claim that a mental state can be realized in wildly different substrates — she volunteered the octopus herself — but she argued that this hurts functionalism rather than helping it. On her view, if the states shared by mammals, octopuses, and eventually engineered systems are held together only by a thin functional description, then we have several distinct kinds wearing one word, and the multiply-realizability of pain becomes a symptom of its failing to be a natural kind at all — a term like jade, which turned out to name two unrelated minerals. She also pressed a sharper, second point that I associate with arguments Lawrence Shapiro and Thomas Polger have made: the classic cases of alleged multiple realization look worse on inspection, because nature mostly reuses the same structure for many jobs rather than finding many structures for one job, so what convergence shows is one mechanism recruited repeatedly, and each so-called realizer deserves to be treated as its own kind rather than as a token of a shared functional state.

Please construct a thought experiment to her specifications, so the issue can be forced rather than merely described. Build a scenario — realistic biology or plausible bioengineering, one paragraph, no group minds, no China-brains, no Martians — containing two pairs: a convergent pair (two systems with the same functional organization realized in physically very different stuff) and a reuse pair (a single physical mechanism doing double duty, subserving two distinct functional roles). The case must not stipulate its verdicts: they should fall out of the theories themselves, with functionally-based individuation assigning the same mental state across the convergent pair and distinct states across the two roles of the reuse pair, while realizer-based individuation reverses both verdicts. After the scenario, demonstrate explicitly that it meets each of these specifications, and say honestly where a theory resists its intended verdict rather than papering over the resistance — I would rather know the case is imperfect than have it forced. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the philosophical work, what happens to the case if that feature is removed, and lay out the strongest functionalist counterattack — presumably either that the functional kinds, however gerrymandered at the realizer level, still do genuine explanatory work, or that her kind-splitting overcorrects into taxonomy for its own sake. Finally, tell me candidly whether the case survives that counterattack or whether it collapses back into something no more powerful than the ordinary octopus example.
