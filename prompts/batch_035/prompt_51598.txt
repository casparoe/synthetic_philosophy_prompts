I do a fair amount of solo hiking, and I've noticed that a lot of the advice I get comes in conditional form: "if you get lost, you'll probably drift downhill toward the river, not up along the ridge." Updating on flat facts is easy for me, but a conditional with odds attached leaves me genuinely unsure how far to move, and whether it should also change what I think about the antecedent itself. So I've collected my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases, and I'd like to know whether there's a single principle behind them or whether some of them have to go. I should say up front that several of the cases are structurally the same on purpose — that's exactly what's bugging me.

Here are the verdicts. (1) Before a trip I put it at 50% that I'd end up in the pine valley (easy terrain) and 25/25 on the two ridges, north and south. A local I trust told me "if you're on a ridge, it's about 3 to 1 you're on the north one." My gut says the pine valley number should stay exactly 50% — advice about the ridges shouldn't leak into the valley. But my gut also says my final odds on north vs. south shouldn't be a clean 3 to 1 either; I should come off even, but only partway, maybe 2 to 1. (2) Near as I can tell, structurally identical: my doctor, scan in hand, said that if anything was wrong at all it was about 4 to 1 condition A rather than condition B (I'd been 50/50 between them going in). Here my gut says just set it to 4 to 1, no partial nonsense. That I react differently to two cases with the same shape really bothers me, i can't find what the relevant difference is supposed to be. (3) A friend once said "if you get lost, it'll be toward the ridge, period," and my gut there was to make it a certainty given lost — which makes me wonder why "3 to 1" only moves me partway while "period" moves me the whole way. (4) Hearing "if you get lost it'll be toward the river" doesnt make me feel any more likely to get lost — the advice is about where, not whether. (5) A total stranger in the parking lot, clearly not a hiker, gave me the same river-vs-ridge conditional, and I barely moved at all. (6) The one I flip-flop on: suppose going in I was 100 to 1 on river over ridge (long valley, no reason to climb), and a trusted local tells me "if you get lost, it'll be 3 to 1 toward the ridge." Some days I think trust the local and go near 1 to 3; other days I think someone who knows the area wouldnt say that against my 100 to 1 unless they knew something, but that still doesn't take me anywhere near 1 to 3; and on bad days I think I should basically stay put.

What I'd like is this: if there's a principle (or a couple of candidate principles) that captures all of this, state it precisely, tell me which clause is doing the work in each case, and tell me what it predicts in situations I haven't listed — for instance, what if the trusted local and my GPS unit, which I'd treat as near-oracular, give me conflicting conditionals? And be honest about which of my verdicts can't be saved. If the difference between (1) and (2) turns out to be real, I'd love to know what grounds it; if it can't be real, tell me which reaction to give up and why, because right now I'm just going back and forth.
