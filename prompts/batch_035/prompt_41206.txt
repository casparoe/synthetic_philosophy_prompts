Been reading rationalist blogs since around 2013 and acausal trade is still the thing my meetup friends argue about most, so I've been trying to reverse-engineer the actual rule one of them (call her D.) is running, because her verdicts feel consistent but I can't figure out what's underneath them. Her track record: (1) two superintelligences in causally disconnected branches of a multiverse, each can cheaply compute the other's values and can grant the other a massive gain at negligible cost — trade is rational, obviously. (2) An extinct civilization that deliberately modeled future agents, anticipated that something like us would show up, and left a stockpile with instructions for how it should be used — we should honor it, she says it's basically a will. (3) The suggestion that we owe the same to any random hunk of matter whose "values" you can define via some baroque interpretive scheme — obviously not, don't be silly. (4) Counterfactual mugging — she refuses to pay, which suprised me; her take is she only cares about what happens in the branch she's actually in. (5) Two deterministic copies of the same agent playing a one-shot PD with no communication — cooperate, since they're literally running the same code.

The new case we're stuck on is another dead civilization, except this one never anticipated anyone. No messages, no testament, nothing aimed at future agents at all. They've been gone for something like a billion years, and their artifacts are in our causal past, so we can reconstruct their values with decent confidence — say we're pretty sure they had a strong preference about how a certain region of space gets used, one that we could now either satisfy cheaply or just ignore, and nobody who ever held the preference exists anymore to know either way. What would D. say? I can see at least two rules fitting her record so far: something like "cooperate when your decision is correlated with the other side's" (mutual simulation, the identical copies, and sort of the dead civ that modeled us), versus something like "honor the values of any genuine agent you can reconstruct at low cost" (which handles the will case in a different way and would say yes to the aliens). And her refusing to pay in the mugging cuts against the correlation rule taken to its logical conclusion, which is the part that confuses me most.

So: what's her likely verdict on the dead aliens who never thought of us, which rule(s) is she actually running, and what further case would genuinely discriminate between the candidate rules? I care less about the verdict itself than about the carving — where exactly she'd draw the line between trade, one-sided honoring of preferences, and plain altruism, and whether that line even makes sense. Also please say how confident you are in your read of her, and flag the single part you're least sure about.
