I've been reading rationalist blogs and their comment threads since the Overcoming Bias days, and for the last few years I've helped run a local meetup that skews toward people who know their expected-utility arithmetic and their basic decision theory but mostly have not read Gödel, Escher, Bach. Next month I'm scheduled to give a forty-five-minute session, whiteboard available, on the MU-puzzle and the idea Hofstadter calls jumping out of the system. My worry is that I know the material in the wrong shape: I internalized JOOTSing as a general attitude years ago, from scattered blog posts about going meta and about David Chapman's levels, and I've forgotten what it's like not to have it. So I want a plan for the explanation, not the explanation itself.

The audience is bright and argumentative and will have heard of Gödel only as internet folklore ("math proves math can't prove everything"). Assume they haven't seen the MIU formal system and will encounter it cold. Plan the sequence I should take and tell me why that order — specifically, which idea has to be in place before the audience attempts the puzzle, and what breaks if the order is inverted.

Second, the carrying example. The obvious candidate is the MU-puzzle itself: I state the axiom MI, demonstrate the four derivation rules on the board, and let them grind at deriving MU. Tell me whether that's the right vehicle or whether something more familiar — a locked-room puzzle, a chess player trapped in their own preparation, a mislabeled "clean up your room" instruction looped back on the giver — should carry part of the load instead, and what the chosen example must contain and what it must be free of. I suspect it must be free of any external authority who knows the answer, but I'd like that checked rather than assumed.

Third, and most important to me: the misconception map. I can already predict a few. Some will take the lesson to be "think harder and try more derivations," i.e., pure object-level persistence, and I need to know exactly when in the sequence to defuse that — I'd rather not let them grind for twenty minutes and then discover it was bait. Others will conclude the opposite, that stepping out of the system means systems are worthless or that the rationalist move is always upward into ever-higher meta, and I want the plan to include where the "meta is also a system" point lands — the observation that the move out of the MIU rules is a move into arithmetic, which has its own walls, and that what Hofstadter is selling is the repeated willingness to leave a line of retreat, not a final resting place outside everything. A third group will hear "Gödel" at the first mention and pattern-match to souls and consciousness; I need to decide in advance what I'll say and won't say about that. Include where each of these gets headed off in the sequence, and flag any misconception I haven't listed that you'd expect from this specific audience, since people trained on LessBlog material arrive with their own furniture.

Finally: a concrete check at the end that would show the distinction between working within a system and stepping out of one has actually landed — not merely that they enjoyed grinding at the derivations — and an explicit list of what I should refuse to cover at this level (Gödel numbering, self-reference, strange loops proper) and how to say so without making the refusal sound like condescension. Where it matters, tell me what I've gotten wrong about my own plan.
