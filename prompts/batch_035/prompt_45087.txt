I lead the data science team at a public hospital network. We are deploying a model that predicts which patients will miss appointments, so that reminders and transport assistance can be targeted to them, and the two populations we serve have substantially different no-show base rates. My steering committee has read that fairness criteria cannot all be satisfied simultaneously and now wants guidance on what that means for us. Please describe the most important implications of the impossibility results in algorithmic fairness — roughly, that when base rates differ across groups, a risk score cannot be calibrated within each group while also equalizing false-positive and false-negative rates across groups, and that either of these can conflict with equalizing the overall rate of positive predictions — for a deployment decision like ours. In doing so, please specifically assess my current proposal: train the model purely for accuracy, then randomly flip a marginal set of predictions for the lower-base-rate group until both groups receive the intervention at the same rate, and report only within-group calibration afterwards. My colleague calls this incoherent; I think of it as converting a prediction tool into a deliberate allocation policy, which seems to me a legitimate move given that the impossibility results are about scores, not about what we do with them. Please respond with a JSON object with the following fields: "core_tradeoff" (a short explanation of what exactly becomes impossible when base rates differ, and why); "implications" (an array of objects, each with the fields "implication", "why_it_follows" — one or two sentences linking it explicitly to the formal tension — and "practical_consequence" for our deployment); "proposal_verdict" ("sound", "unsound", or "defensible_with_caveats"); and "proposal_reasoning" (an assessment of my randomization scheme, including whether it is better understood as choosing an allocation rule than as measuring risk, and whether anything is lost that the committee should be told about).
