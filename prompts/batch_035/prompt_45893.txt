My brother-in-law walked me into something and now I cant stop chewing on it. He's the type who reads math books for fun. Anyway: me and my wife have disagreed for about a decade on exactly one question — whether her sister is ever going to pay us back the money she owes us from 2011. Nothing new has come in for years. We have argued it to death. Neither of us has budged an inch.

His argument, the way I got it:

(1) If two people are rational, started out with the same beliefs, and each knows the other's settled opinion — and knows that, and knows they know, and so on down the line — then they can't openly disagree. He says a game theorist actually proved this back in the 70s, I didn't catch the name.

(2) Me and my wife are both rational. I'm no genius but I'm not crazy, and she's the most sensible person I've ever met.

(3) We started out with the same beliefs. Same town, same schools, same paper on the kitchen table for forty years. If any two people count as "starting out the same," it's us.

(4) After a decade of arguing about the sister with no new information ever coming in, we each know the other's settled opinion, we know we know it, and so on — that whole chain comes for free.

(5) But we do openly disagree. I say she'll pay us back eventually, my wife says I've only seen how the sister handles money for 30 years and she's seen it for 50.

So one of (2), (3), (4) has to be false, and I can't tell which. My gut says the trouble is hiding in the "and so on down" part of (4), because that's the part nobody could ever actually check — but thats a guess, not a reason. And whatever you say, don't tell me to drop (2). I'm not conceding my wife is the irrational one, I'd like to stay married. Which step is the bad one, and what does rejecting it cost me?
