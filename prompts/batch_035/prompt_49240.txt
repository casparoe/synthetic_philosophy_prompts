Last Easter my cousin Ruth and her business partner Dan got into a long argument at dinner, and I've been turning it over ever since because I couldn't follow all of it. Neither of them went to college either, so I'm hoping you can help me sort it out. The subject was an idea I'd read about somewhere — Max Weber's claim, roughly, that Calvinist religious values (thrift, relentless work, reading worldly success as a sign of being among the saved) helped bring modern capitalism into being in Europe. Neither of them disputed the history; what they fought about was whether that kind of explanation even makes sense — whether "culture" can be a cause of anything at all.

Ruth opened by saying explanations like Weber's are circular. To say people saved and worked endlessly because their culture valued saving and endless work is just to restate what they did, dressed up in value-language. A real explanation, she said, has to point at something independent — who owned what, who needed labor, what the technology and trade routes happened to be. Dan answered that by that standard no mental state ever causes anything: my wanting coffee causes me to get up and make coffee, and a culture is nothing but wanting-and-believing spread across millions of people and handed down, so there's nothing suspicious about it. Ruth said there's a real difference: an individual's desire sits in that individual's head and moves their body, but "culture" isn't in anyone's head — it's a description of a whole population, like saying the crowd caused the stampede when it was individual people shoving. Dan came back that values are transmitted — a child raised among people who treat idleness as a sin ends up with different dispositions because of that upbringing, and if upbringing isn't a cause, nothing is. Ruth said transmission explains why a value spreads once it exists, not where it came from in the first place; somebody had to start treating work as valuable, presumably for practical reasons, so the cultural story is downstream of the material one. Dan replied that being downstream doesn't disqualify anything: smoke is caused by fire and still causes the alarm — a middle link in a chain is a genuine cause of everything after it. Ruth's last word was that if a cultural explanation is always allowed to sit on top of whatever material story you like, it can never be shown wrong: wherever a value and an economic interest coincide, people tell the cultural story, and where they come apart, nobody mentions the value at all. Without some case where the two explanations genuinely conflict and culture wins, explaining anything "with values" is just storytelling with a halo on it.

They both seemed right to me at different points, and I couldn't referee it. Could you go through their exchange move by move, say who has the better of each step and why, and tell me what, if anything, both of them missed?
