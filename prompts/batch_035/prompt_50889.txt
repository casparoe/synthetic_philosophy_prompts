Two of my friends, Marcus and Elena, spent a long car ride arguing about mind uploading, and I've been turning their exchange over ever since without being able to settle it myself. The argument started when Marcus announced he'd signed up with a company that, decades from now, will scan his brain and run a copy of it in a computer. Elena told him he was paying for his own funeral: the scan destroys nothing and creates something new, and the thing that wakes up on the other side would merely believe it's him, while he himself would still die whenever his body dies. Marcus said he'd never accept the destructive scan for exactly that reason, but that the same company offers a gradual option, where neurons are replaced one at a time by tiny artificial devices that behave identically, and that option is entirely different: it's just an extreme version of the natural turnover of material in the body, like the ship of Theseus or the fact that most of the atoms in my hand weren't there a few years ago. Elena said the difference Marcus is counting on doesn't exist: if the copy produced by a scan isn't really him, then the person existing after complete gradual replacement isn't either, because every original part of him has been discarded along the way — the final artificial brain only *remembers* being him, and being remembered by someone isn't the same as surviving. Marcus replied that by that reasoning he has already died many times over, since his body replaces its components constantly, and surely Elena doesn't think the Marcus driving the car is a different person from the Marcus she met ten years ago; what persists is the pattern and its continuous, uninterrupted functioning, which gradual replacement preserves and a one-shot scan does not. Elena answered that the "uninterrupted" part is doing all the work and can't survive scrutiny: when Marcus had his surgery under general anesthesia last year, there was a stretch with no experience at all, and he had no problem accepting that the man who woke up was him — so a gap in consciousness can't be what destroys identity, and if it isn't, she can't see what the scan is supposed to be missing. Marcus's last move was to say that anesthesia doesn't break the *physical* process — the brain keeps operating underneath, and it's the unbroken causal chain in the physical stuff that matters, not moment-to-moment awareness. Elena said Marcus was then openly admitting that the substrate matters after all, which collapses his whole position, since the entire appeal of gradual replacement was supposed to be that only the pattern matters and the material is irrelevant — he can't claim the pattern is all that counts while a step-by-step transfer of the pattern fails to be him. Neither convinced the other, and honestly I couldn't follow who was winning at each stage. Could you go through their exchange move by move and tell me who is right about which specific point, where each of them concedes too much or too little, and whether there's something both of them are missing that would dissolve the disagreement?
