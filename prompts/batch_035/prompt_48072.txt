my partner and i watched Being John Malkovich last weekend (first time for her, i've seen it like four times) and it turned into a two-hour argument that i'm still chewing on, so i want to get some thoughts straight. i've been reading about higher-order theories of consciousness on and off since taking a phil of mind class years ago -- the Rosenthal-style view where a mental state is conscious only when there's a higher-order thought directed at it -- and i kept nudging her during the movie like "see, THAT's the theory, they literally built a movie around it." she thinks i'm forcing it. maybe i am! so help me figure out where the fit is real and where i'm kidding myself.

the obvious hook: when Craig (or anyone) goes through the portal, they become a spectator of Malkovich's first-order states. watching Malkovich eat and feeling secondhand disgust is basically the HOT structure dramatized -- Malkovich has the first-order state, Craig supplies the higher-order awareness. but here's what i keep turning over: on the Rosenthal view the HOT and the first-order state belong to the same subject, and the movie blows that apart by putting them in two different heads. so is the movie an argument FOR higher-order theories, or actually a counterexample factory? like, whose experience is it? does the disgust belong to Craig, to Malkovich, or to some weird composite creature the theory has no name for?

and then the scene where Malkovich himself goes through the portal into his own head and finds the restaurant where everyone is Malkovich. my read is that this is the regress problem of higher-order theories turned into a joke: if consciousness requires a higher-order representation, why doesn't that one need a higher-order representation too, turtles all the way down? but i've also read that same-order / self-representational views (kriegel, that general neighborhood) answer the regress by folding the awareness into the state itself, and i wonder if the Malkovich-only restaurant is actually a picture of THAT instead -- a world where first-order and higher-order content have collapsed into one, and it's horrible, which might be the movie taking a side on whether self-awareness is a feature or a bug.

also honestly the ending haunts me a little and it's why i'm not just letting this go. Craig ends up trapped inside his daughter's ex-hostage-taker's daughter (long story, you've seen it) experiencing her life as a paralyzed witness, seeing through her eyes but unable to do anything. is that a conscious state by the HOT criterion? there's awareness-of, but no agency and maybe no way to report. i'd love to hear what the theory says about a mind with the higher-order part but cut off from everything else.

so: give me a reading that takes both the film and the actual higher-order literature seriously. where does the movie genuinely illuminate the theory, where does it cheat (i suspect the portal gives people access that no actual HOT could, since it's more like telepathy than inner awareness?), and does it end up adding anything to the debate that the papers themselves don't? i'd like to be able to write this up as a short piece for a blog i do, so i'm not looking for the movie reduced to a diagram, i'm looking for the places where art and theory genuinely rub against each other. also if i'm misremembering a plot detail (the ending especially -- whose head is Craig in exactly?) please flag it, i've only seen the last act once and it was late.
