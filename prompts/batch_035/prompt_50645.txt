My roommate went deep on Everett this summer and now swears he has an argument that many-worlds can't do probability at all, and I half-believe him, which is annoying. It goes: (1) If I know for certain that A will happen and that B will happen, I can't be uncertain between A and B. (2) On many-worlds, before I measure the electron, I know that I'll have one successor who sees spin-up and another who sees spin-down. (3) So I can't be uncertain which result I'll get. (4) Probability is just quantified uncertainty. (5) So on many-worlds there's no sense in which the outcome is 50-50, or 90-10, or anything. (6) But the Born weights are supposed to be probabilities. (7) So many-worlds either lacks its central empirical posit or has to stick the Born rule in by hand, in which case what's the advantage over a collapse theory, which also just postulates its probabilities?

Here's what scares me. He says the argument also covers thought experiments where I really do want probabilities. Like if a perfect copier scans me and wakes two copies, one in a red room and one in a blue room, then apparently I can't be uncertain which room I'll wake up in — even though before the scan I'd swear the sensible credence is 50-50 on red. He's totally willing to bite that bullet, which is either impressively consistent or a red flag. But when I push him on what the numbers should even be, he retreats to "count the branches," and something feels off there too: nobody ever tells me how finely to slice branches, and even granting some canonical slicing, nothing in the counting story explains why squared amplitude should beat branch count as the measure.

So, help me out. Which premise should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? For what it's worth I'd rather keep (4) and keep the Born weights being genuine probabilities than let him win. And then the part I actually can't figure out: even if you fix the uncertainty somehow, does that get you the Born *numbers*, or just probabilities in general? If it only gets you generic probabilities, is he right that many-worlds has no real edge over postulating collapse?
