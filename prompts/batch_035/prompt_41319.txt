I've been reading about Marx's ideas of exploitation and alienation, and I'm trying to work out what I actually think by running through cases and noting my gut reactions. A seamstress in a fast-fashion supply chain doing repetitive work for a legal but low wage: exploited and alienated. A freelance illustrator who loves her work and is paid well for it: neither. A tenured professor working long hours for far less than she could earn elsewhere because she loves the research: not exploited, though I'm uneasy — the institution clearly benefits from her willingness to take less. An open-source maintainer whose unpaid code underpins a billion-dollar company: exploited, even though nobody employs him and he chose the work freely. A member of a worker-owned cooperative doing the same repetitive assembly work as the seamstress: not exploited, but alienated. A craftsperson who owns her tools and workshop but sells almost entirely through a platform whose ranking algorithm she can neither see nor influence: I genuinely can't decide — she owns everything Marx cared about owning, yet the dependence feels exploitative. My tentative principle is that exploitation tracks asymmetric vulnerability rather than wage extraction, and alienation tracks loss of control and meaning rather than repetitiveness. Can those two be stated precisely enough to save all of my verdicts, including the unwaged-but-exploited maintainer and the unexploited-but-alienated cooperativist? Please commit to the best single account you can defend, say what it predicts for a couple of cases I haven't listed, and tell me plainly which of my verdicts you would overturn if the account requires it.
