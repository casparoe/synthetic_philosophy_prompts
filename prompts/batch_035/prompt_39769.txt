Me and a friend from my philsophy of science seminar keep arguing about reduction vs emergence and we decided to make it concrete instead of hand-waving. We picked a bunch of phenomena and want a tier list ranking them by how "genuinely emergent" they are, where we mean something stronger than just "you could in principle compute it from the micro-facts" — more like, the macro-level behavior is surprising, or you genuinely need explanatory vocabulary that doesn't just paraphrase the microscopic story. I've been pushing a rough criterion I call "simulation surprise": imagine you knew the exact micro-laws and the exact initial conditions but nothing else, and you ran the simulation — a property is genuinely emergent to the extent the macro-pattern would still be hard to predict (or even to notice) before running it. My friend says this just confuses epistemology with ontology, i.e. predictive difficulty isn't the same thing as the property "coming from" the collective rather than the parts, and honestly I suspect he's partly right, but I want to see how far my criterion gets before it breaks.

The list: (1) the pressure exerted by a gas in a sealed box, (2) superconductivity appearing when a metal is cooled below its critical temperature, (3) a glider in Conway's Game of Life, (4) stop-and-go "phantom" traffic jams on a highway with no accident, (5) the critical exponents coming out identical near the critical point for wildly different systems like fluids and magnets, (6) the wetness of water, (7) Langton's ant settling into building a diagonal highway after ~10,000 steps of chaotic scribbling, (8) markets clearing at a price nobody planned. Feel free to use simulation surprise, push back on it, or replace it with something better — we've heard of the weak/strong emergence distinction but never really got past the wikipedia level on it, so if you use those terms give a quick gloss.

Please give each item a tier (A/B/C/D or whatever, just be consistent) with one or two sentences of justification per item, plus a short paragraph on whether my criterion survives this exercise. Then, at the very end, put your final tier assignment in one machine-readable line like \answer{1:C, 2:B, 3:D, ...} so I can paste it into my notes file.
