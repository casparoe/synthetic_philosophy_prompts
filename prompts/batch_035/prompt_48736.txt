After thirty-two years as an operational forecaster with a national meteorological service, where probabilistic interpretation of radar and satellite data was my bread and butter, I retired and began auditing graduate courses in philosophy for my own edification. This term the instructor of a graduate seminar on formal epistemology has invited me to contribute one problem to the problem set, on the strength of my background in probability. I would like it to concern the Judy Benjamin problem: how one should update on the content of a conditional whose antecedent has prior probability zero. Please build the question around a fresh scenario drawn from my old line of work rather than the original one — say, a forecaster whose sector is divided into four quadrants with equal prior probability of containing a storm cell, and who receives the report that, if the cell is in the eastern half of the sector, the probability is three-quarters that it is in the northeast quadrant. The question should have parts asking the student to (a) show why ordinary Bayesian conditioning is unavailable here and why Jeffrey conditioning also fails to capture the constraint; (b) set up and solve the minimum relative entropy update, carrying the actual numbers through; (c) articulate the competing intuition that a message concerning only how the eastern half divides should leave the total probability of each half unchanged, and assess whether the entropy-minimizing posterior honors that intuition, as well as whether the answer depends on how finely the forecaster partitions the unmentioned western half; and (d) argue for or against the claim that there is a uniquely rational posterior in this situation at all. Then supply a model answer with the posterior worked out numerically, and a marking scheme totaling 30 points that states precisely which moves separate an A answer from a B answer — my own suspicion is that the B student can execute the optimization but never notices what is at stake in part (c), and I would like your scheme to confirm or correct that suspicion.
