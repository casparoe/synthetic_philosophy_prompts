so I'm in this little discussion group that meets at my local library once a month, about 12 of us, mixed ages, couple of retirees, some younger folks, nobody academic. lately I've been chewing on this question of whether history gets "locked in" — like how come we all type on QWERTY keyboards which were suposedly designed way back to slow typists down so the keys wouldn't jam, or how VHS beat Betamax even though people say Betamax was the better format. and the question that really bugs me underneath that: if you could rewind the tape and run history again, would the same stuff win anyway, or do little accidents early on basically decide everything and everyone after is just stuck?

I've gathered the econ/tech people call this path dependence or lock-in, but I don't care about the jargon, I care about whether it's true.

anyway next month its my turn to run the session, 90 minutes. I really don't want to just talk at people — I want some kind of game or activity where the group actually experiences getting locked in. they make a bunch of small choices one at a time, each choice looks reasonable on its own, and somewhere along the way they realize they're stuck on something worse even though everyone can see it's worse, and the cost of switching keeps climbing the longer they wait. ideally nobody suspects where it's going until it's already happened.

give me: actual rules I can follow, what materials (nothing fancy, the library has whiteboards, tables, paper, a printer), how to do payoffs without real money, a rough minute-by-minute timeline, and then a debrief that connects what happened in the game back to my keyboard question and the rewind-the-tape question — like which moments in the game were the "accidents" and whether the outcome was ever still open. also tell me honestly what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which part of the whole idea a game just can't capture — I'm guessing you can't really simulate "would the real world have ended up the same anyway" in a room for 90 minutes, but maybe I'm wrong.
