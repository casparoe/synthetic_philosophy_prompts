I teach a third-year rational choice course and this past week we did the absent-minded driver for the first time. Standard setup as I gave it to them: driver leaves the office, hits two identical highway exits, and can't remember at either one whether he's already passed an exit — payoff 4 if he continues past the first and exits at the second (home by the highway), payoff 0 if he exits immediately at the first (long slow way home), payoff 1 if he sails past both and ends up who knows where. From the office the plan is obviously continue-then-exit, but on the road the whole thing apparently falls apart into a fight about whether he should just exit, or randomize with some specific probability, or something. I walked them through the planning-stage vs. action-stage tension the way the literature does, and I thought it went fine.

But afterwards a student asked me something I can't stop thinking about: isn't the whole problem just an artifact of the amnesia stipulation? A normal driver — even a barely competent one — would remember whether he'd already passed an exit, execute the plan he formed at home, collect his 4, and be done. The paradox only exists because the driver is stipulated to be weirdly, unnaturally forgetful about the one thing that matters. So what is the case supposed to be teaching us about rational choice for actual agents? Is it really more than "absent-mindedness is costly, write the plan on your hand"? My honest instinct is that the student is right and I maybe shouldn't teach it as a deep result, except people whose judgement I trust keep insisting it's a litmus test for whole decision theories, and I've heard it linked to Newcomb-ish cases and to problems about agents who can't condition on where they are in some deeper sense. But if it dissolves the moment you grant ordinary memory, then it's a puzzle about memory-impaired agents, not decision theory per se, and I'm kind of embarassed I taught it like the latter. Am I missing a general lesson here, or is "just remember where you are" basically the whole insight?
