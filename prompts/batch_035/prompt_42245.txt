My partner and I have a running argument about which of Norway and Sweden is the more liberal country, and I would like it made precise instead of continued anecdote-swapping. My credence that Norway is more liberal is about 0.6; hers that Sweden is, about 0.75. We suspect part of the fight is verbal — she seems to mean liberal as in a permissive, live-and-let-live society, I mean something closer to a state that leaves its citizens' choices alone — and we weight the familiar evidence differently: Sweden disestablished its church years before Norway did, Norway rejected EU membership in two referendums while Sweden joined, both countries run state alcohol retail monopolies, and each of us reaches for immigration, drug policy, or parental leave when it suits our side. Please give me your own credence that Norway is more liberal than Sweden, as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion: (1) your headline number; (2) your probability that "more liberal" admits of one sense determinate enough to give the comparison a truth value, rather than the question dissolving into an arbitrary weighting of several different senses; (3) your probability that Norway outranks Sweden given the best such sense, and which criteria do the work; (4) your probability that the ranking is stable across reasonable choices of indicators rather than an artifact of cherry-picking; and (5) the sub-question your headline number is most sensitive to, whether our two credences of 0.6 and 0.75 more likely diverge over what "liberal" means or over the facts about the two countries, and what single piece of evidence or definitional move would settle our dispute.
