Next month I will be presenting my senior thesis to my school's philosophy society, and I would like your help preparing for the question period, which I expect to be the hardest part.

My thesis concerns Kierkegaard's account of Abraham in Fear and Trembling. I am arguing that the teleological suspension of the ethical, as Johannes de silentio presents it, is not a general license for individuals to override morality whenever they claim private religious conviction. Rather, it is a deliberately constructed limit case: the ethical is "suspended" only in the sense that it is relativized by a relation to something higher, the single individual's absolute duty to God, and de silentio's insistence that he cannot understand Abraham — that he can only repeat the story with admiration and horror — is precisely what prevents the case from being used as a precedent. I also want to defend the distinction between the knight of infinite resignation and the knight of faith, since my reading depends on it: Abraham is not merely resigned to losing Isaac but makes the second movement, the movement of faith, on the strength of the absurd.

The audience is about fifteen students and two teachers. Most of my classmates have never read Kierkegaard. Our ethics teacher taught us Kant last term and is unforgiving about anything that looks like special pleading for religion. One classmate is a committed utilitarian who already thinks faith-based reasoning is a euphemism for no reasoning. Another is devoutly religious and suspects my thesis is really an attack on faith dressed up in scholarly caution. A third has read some Nietzsche on his own and likes to argue that whatever Kierkegaard attributes to God, he has simply invented himself. I will have about twenty minutes of questions.

Please draft my preparation sheet. List the objections I will actually get from this audience — phrased the way each of these people would actually put them, not the way a journal would put them — ordered by how much damage each would do to my thesis. For each objection, give me a reply I can deliver in under a minute, and note honestly where the reply is weak. Tell me which points I should concede up front rather than defend. Finally, include the question I am hoping nobody asks, and advise me on what to say if it comes anyway.
