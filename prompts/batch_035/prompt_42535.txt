Is there a name for this? I work with data for a living and got into philosophy of science a couple years back, and there's a cluster of situations I keep wanting one word for, and I honestly can't tell if its one thing or three. Case one, the boring version: you fit a curve to a finite scatter of points and infinitely many curves pass through all of them — but this feels cheap, because more points could always break the tie. Case two, the interesting one: two theories that agree on every number you could ever measure, not just the ones we happen to have collected so far. Physicists keep telling me old Lorentz-style ether theory and special relativity come out identical on every experiment anyone could run, even in principle, and that the only difference is what's "really" going on underneath the equations. Case three, the weird one: with the interpretations of quantum mechanics, people can't even agree whether the rival interpretations are rival theories at all, or just the same theory written in different notation. I looked up "underdetermination" after someone used the word near case two, but what I found seems to be about something weaker — about how no single hypothesis ever gets tested on its own, only bundles of them together. What I'm after is the strong thing: theories that no possible evidence, even in principle, could ever split apart. Does that configuration have its own name? And is there a principled line between "two theories that happen to agree on all possible evidence" and "one theory with two notations" — because if they agree on everything measurable, in what sense do they disagree at all? I don't just want a label, I want to know wether my three cases are one phenomenon or three, how the strong version relates to the weaker one I found, and where the edges are, so please don't just drop a term and leave it at that.
