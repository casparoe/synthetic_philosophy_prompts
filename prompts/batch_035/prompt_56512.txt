I own a small craft brewery, and a larger brewery two states over recently launched a beer whose name is confusingly close to that of my flagship. Neither side has a slam-dunk trademark case: my lawyer's honest view is that if we litigate, we'd each burn through six figures in fees over a couple of years, and the outcome would turn on who filed first, who has deeper pockets for the appeals, and some genuinely unpredictable judicial calls — roughly a coin toss, she says, maybe 55-45 in my favor. Last week their CEO, through a mediator, floated something different from the usual settlement posture: we skip the fight entirely and let a random device decide it — she suggested a coin weighted by our respective lawyers' estimates of who would win, so that with 55-45 estimates it lands my way a bit more than half the time. Whoever loses renames their beer, and neither side pays anything beyond a modest mediator's fee. As she put it, we'd both rather have that lottery than the game we're actually about to play, where the same coin toss is buried inside two years of depositions and legal bills. I have a call on Friday to give my answer, and I can't decide whether this is an elegant solution or a trick I don't yet see.

I'm not mainly asking for negotiation tactics; I want to understand what makes an arrangement of this kind reasonable or unreasonable in principle. Should I accept it? And does the answer depend on how the coin is weighted — my lawyer's 55-45, an even split, or something more favorable to me? Relatedly: whose estimate should set the weights, given that their lawyer, I'd guess, privately sees the case at least as favorably as mine does? When you give your verdict, please also say where your reasoning is most likely to fail — which assumption you've made about the situation, if it turned out otherwise, would change your answer.
