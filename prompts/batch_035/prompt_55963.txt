So I'm a software engineer with zero formal philosophy background, but I've gone down a bit of a rabbit hole on algorithmic information theory after a coworker invoked Kolmogorov complexity during a code review argument (long story, basically about over-engineering, lol). Here's the position I've ended up at, and I want you to stress-test it properly: Occam's razor isn't just a heuristic or a vibe that scientists happen to have — it's close to a theorem. My understanding of the argument: formalize "simple hypothesis" as "short program," formalize prediction as betting on hypotheses weighted by 2^(-length), and then Solomonoff induction is in some sense unimprovable — no other formal prediction method beats it in expectation except by a constant. So the preference for simpler hypotheses isn't a pragmatic choice we could have made differently, it's baked into the math of prediction itself. What would count against this? I can think of some candidate weak spots on my own but I have no idea how serious they actually are: the whole construction is uncomputable, so any real agent (scientists, me, presumably any AI anyone builds) runs some cheap approximation, and it's not obvious the optimality carries over to approximations; complexity is only defined relative to a choice of universal machine, and even though the machine only matters up to an additive constant, that constant can be enormous, so "which theory is simpler" can come out very differently depending on the encoding at the scale where humans actually compare theories — doesn't that make simplicity a bit arbitrary right where it's supposed to do work? It also smells faintly circular: we trust short programs to keep predicting well because they have so far, and the whole thing quietly assumes the world is even computable in the first place.

I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want to know which of them a committed defender of the view would genuinely concede counts against it versus which they'd wave off (and on what grounds the waving-off is legit vs. motivated). Also be honest about whether the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability — like, is there any observation at all that could count against "simpler is better" stated at this level of abstraction, and if there isn't, is that a defect of the view or is that just what a framework-level claim looks like? For what it's worth, the thing that would move me most personally is a concrete historical case where two live scientific theories differed hugely in Kolmogorov-style simplicity and the messier one won anyway — so if you think such cases exist or could exist, tell me what they'd look like.
