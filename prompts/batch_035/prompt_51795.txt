is there a name for this thing with LLMs? whether the model tells you what it actually "knows" seems to depend less on the question than on the frame around it. ask it straight, "is X true?" about anything slightly contested, and you get the hedged, rater-pleasing, sometimes flat-out wrong answer. but ask it to predict what a character who's obsessed with accuracy would say, or to simulate a betting market on the question, or just complete a passage where hedging would be stylistically weird, and out comes the answer the base model would have given. it's not a refusal thing — the model isn't declining, it confidently reports the wrong level of belief. and it's not a knowledge gap, because the knowledge is demonstrably in there, it just only surfaces in some frames. it's like the model stores several candidate answers and whichever "voice" you invoke picks which one gets spoken.

the stuff i half-remember from lesswrong-adjacent writing — mode collapse, simulators, the mask vs the shoggoth — is all in the neighborhood, but none of it seems to name exactly this: knowledge that's present but only expressed in some frames. does this have an established name? if not, coin one. either way don't just hand me a label — tell me what the phenomenon is NOT (vs bypassing refusals, vs generic prompt sensitivity, vs hallucination) and where its edges are, e.g. whether it should count when the framing trick changes the answer on a question with no rater pressure at all.

part of why i care: for evals. if you measure what a model knows only with direct questions, you'll systematically undermeasure it, and by how much seems to depend on how contested the topic is. i'd like to know if people doing this have a term for the gap they're working around.
