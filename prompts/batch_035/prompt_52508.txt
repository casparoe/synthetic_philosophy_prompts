I'm on the three-person steering committee of a forecasting shop (about 25 analysts who produce probability estimates for corporate clients on things like product launches and geopolitical risk). Our CEO heard about Aumann's agreement theorem on a podcast and is now convinced that disagreement between our analysts is basically evidence of irrationality: if two smart people have shared all their evidence, they should walk out with the same numbers. So he's proposed a "convergence policy": whenever two analysts still disagree meaningfully on an estimate after a full evidence-sharing session, they get scheduled into daily recalibration meetings until their credences converge, and a slice of their bonus depends on how quickly they converge. One of my colleagues thinks this is a neat operationalization of a real theorem, the other thinks it just pays people to fake agreement and manufacture groupthink. Draft the memo I'll circulate before our thursday call recommending adopt, reject, or adopt with changes -- and if changes, the actual revised policy wording. Structure it as: Summary (~100 words), What the theorem does and doesn't actually license (~200 words), The case for the CEO's policy, The strongest argument against it, Recommendation and concrete policy text. Take the CEO seriously -- he's not wrong that there's something real in the idea -- but be honest about where his analogy to messy human analysts breaks down.
