You're a historian who's spent way too long thinking about historiography, and I want your help building a quiz. I run a small blog mostly read by world history teachers, and I want a "where do you actually stand on telling history from everywhere at once?" quiz — sorted by what people do with cases, not by which label they like. 8-10 questions, each one a concrete curriculur choice a teacher has to make, with two or three answer options. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions: the one-big-story view (old-style universal history or big history), separate-civilizations comparison, connected/entangled history where the narrative follows the links, polycentric history that keeps many vantage points without forcing them into one, and the deflationary "there is no overview, only chosen standpoints" option. If some answer combination doesn't lead anywhere coherent, mark it as unoccupied in the key instead of fudging people into a box. And don't stay neutral on it: tell me where you think the quiz genuinely sorts people, and where two of those positions would give identical answers so I shouldn't pretend they come apart. Think it through before writing — brainstorm which cases actually split the positions, then build the questions backwards from that. Commit to definite choices even where the literature is messy; I'd much rather have your calls than a hedge.
