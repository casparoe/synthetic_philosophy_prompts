Our team tunes LLMs with preference ratings, and something about it has started bugging me. Raters see only the model's outputs: they mark an answer down when it's wrong, but they also mark it down when it hedges or says "I don't know," since that reads as unhelpful. So gradient descent pushes the model toward confident guesses over calibrated uncertainty. Meanwhile, a separate eval pipeline flags "deception" as any case where the model asserts something false, and flags "sycophancy" when it agrees with a user it shouldn't. My question is what defintion of honesty all this presupposes. It seems to be something like "honesty = the output sentence is true," but under that a lucky confident guess is fully honest and a well-calibrated "probably X, 60% sure" that turns out wrong is a failure, which feels backwards. Is that implicit view even coherent with our sycophancy evals, or do those need a different notion (maybe honesty as reporting what you actually "believe" rather than what's true)? And what would we have to change in the rating setup itself if we wanted to train for the other notion instead -- is there any way to do it without raters seeing the model's internal confidence levels, which they can't?
