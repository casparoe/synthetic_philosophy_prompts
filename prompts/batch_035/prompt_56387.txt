I'm trying to determine whether Hempel's raven paradox is a genuine problem about induction or an artifact of treating confirmation as a two-place relation between an observation and a hypothesis, when it is really a three-place relation that also involves the sampling procedure. To that end, set up an observational procedure — a rule for choosing which objects to examine and what to record about them — under which finding a single white shoe confirms the hypothesis that all ravens are black more strongly than finding a single black raven does. Measure confirmation by the likelihood ratio P(observation | all ravens are black) / P(observation | some raven is non-black), and compare single observations of each kind made under the same procedure. Constraints: the procedure must be implementable by a real field investigator without oracle-like knowledge of which hypothesis is true; each of the two observations (white shoe, black raven) must have nonzero probability under the procedure in either world, so that both likelihood ratios are defined; and the black raven must still come out confirming, not neutral — I want the white shoe to beat it, not to be the only thing doing any work. After the setup, do the check: specify the sampling frame, say exactly what is held fixed between the two worlds, and show the arithmetic for both observations, including the population numbers you chose and why those numbers make the ordering come out as required. Then explain which structural feature of the procedure is doing the work, describe what happens to the comparison if you switch to the sampling frame an ordinary birdwatcher uses — encounter a bird, note its color — and why the felt intuition that a black raven is worth more than a white shoe belongs to that frame. Finish with the two counterattacks: how someone who insists that a black raven always confirms at least as much would diagnose your procedure as illegitimate, and whether the paradox is dissolved by relativizing confirmation to a sampling frame or merely displaced into the question of which frame is epistemically mandatory.
