I've been arguing with my roommate all week about which things can suffer, and I figured I'd just write down my actual gut verdicts and see if they form any coherent picture, because right now they feel like they don't. Here they are. A thermostat: no, obviously. A nematode worm with its 302 neurons: no, or close enough to no that I wouldn't lose sleep (sorry, worms). A fish on a hook: yes, that matters. An octopus: yes, very much. A human whose neurons get replaced one at a time by functionally identical silicon chips: moral status never blinks out, so the all-silicon person at the end matters exactly as much as you or me. A perfect software simulation of a brain: matters. The same simulation run a thousand times slower: still matters, the suffering is just stretched out. But — and here's where I start to wobble — a giant precomputed lookup table that takes any input the simulation could get and returns exactly the output the simulation would return: I really want to say that's just a filing cabinet, nothing home, even though from outside it's indistinguishable. And a video game NPC that flinches and cries out when shot, with real-time reactive internal state modeling its little body: no, doesn't matter, even though behaviorally it's at least as sophisticated as the worm.

So my tentative principle is something like: what matters is implementing the right kind of computation, where "the right kind" means causally integrated and running in real time as an organism-model, not just input-output mapping — the lookup table fails because it's not doing the causal work, the NPC fails because... hmm, actually the NPC is doing causal work in real time, and I still say no, which bothers me. The worm is doing causal work too. Meanwhile my roommate is a hardcore biological naturalist who says only actual living tissue can feel anything, no matter what the silicon replacement intuition pump says, and he keeps pointing at my lookup-table verdict as evidence that deep down I agree with him that mere computation can never suffice.

So what I want from you: is there a principle that captures all or nearly all of these verdicts, or am I holding an impossible set? Please state whatever principle you land on precisely, tell me which of my verdicts it can't reproduce and which one I should give up first, and tell me what it predicts for a case I deliberately haven't mentioned: a system that simulates suffering in a creature that never existed — no fish ever swam, someone just made up the neural layout — running at full biological fidelity.
