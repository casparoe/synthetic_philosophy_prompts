I've been keeping a list of cases where I have a pretty firm gut feeling about whether there's a *real pattern* there — in the sense people mean when they say things like "a face in a cloud isn't a real pattern, but the belief-desire stuff is" — and I want to know if my verdicts actually add up to anything.

Here's the list. Digits of pi pass every statistical test for randomness, but I say there's obviously a real pattern there — the whole thing comes out of a tiny program. A chess engine: treating it as *wanting* to win and *believing* it can take my queen genuinely helps me play against it, so yes, real pattern at that level, even though at the circuit level there's just voltage. Traffic on the highway settles into a stop-and-go wave that moves backwards through the cars faster than any of them drive — no driver designed it, no driver even intends it, but I still say it's a real pattern, and one you can predict with a decent model. Astrology: not a real pattern, even though horoscope-readers find the readings uncannily accurate — that feels like the face in the cloud to me, the "pattern" is doing no work. A face I see in a water stain: not real, even though it's vivid to me. A stopped clock: it's "right" twice a day, and you can even say exactly when — but calling that a real pattern feels like a cheat, and I can't quite say why, which bugs me, because it looks more predictive than some of the ones I said yes to. A lone person marooned on an island who will never be observed or predicted by anyone: I still think their beliefs and desires form real patterns, which seems to cause trouble for anything that makes patterns depend on somebody using them. And here's the one I keep flipping on: a spam filter that flags mail. Everyone says "it thinks it's spam," and that does compress its behavior usefully — but part of me wants to say it's only *as if*, in a way the chess engine somehow isn't, and I genuinely don't know if that difference is real or just me being charmed by one example.

So: is there a principle that gets all or most of these? My own half-guess is something like "a real pattern is one that gives you compression or predictive grip from some stance, whether or not anyone is standing in that stance" — but I suspect that saves my pi and islander verdicts and then makes trouble for the stopped clock and maybe the chess engine / spam filter pair, where I seem to care about something more. Please don't hand me three candidate principles and a shrug — pick the one you'd actually defend, state it precisely enough that I can apply it myself, tell me straight which of my verdicts it overturns and why those are the ones to give up, and then run it on a fresh case I didn't list: whether the "competitive market" of economists, with its firms maximizing profits that no person inside any firm is consciously maximizing, is a real pattern in the same sense as my traffic wave. I'd rather get a committed answer I can push back on than a balanced survey. (Also, minor thing, but if the principle needs a clause about levels — patterns all the way down vs. only above some grain — I want that clause explicit, not implied.)
