For eleven years I have worked as an actuary at the same firm, and a month ago I was offered a position with a small consultancy abroad — riskier, worse paid, but work I think I would care about. I have been telling everyone, including myself, that with a mortgage and an aging parent nearby, I have no real choice but to stay. My closest friend, who took a semester of existentialism in college and has never let me forget it, keeps countering that I am "condemned to be free," most recently to dismiss the phrase "no real choice" mid-sentence. That one line is doing a lot of work in our conversations: it is meant to excuse her bluntness, to burden me with full responsibility for my hesitation, and to suggest that my stance is textbook bad faith rather than an honest description of my circumstances. I gather the saying traces back to Sartre and is tied up with his account of self-deception, but I have read enough secondhand glosses to distrust the summaries.
