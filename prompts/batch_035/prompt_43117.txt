Two of my friends have been arguing about the Great Divergence for like a year and it's a total stalemate, so I want to force them into an adversarial collaboration. One is basically a California-school revisionist: thinks the Yangzi delta and England were comparitively developed up to maybe 1800, and that coal sitting near English workshops plus New World land ('ghost acreage') is most of what tipped it. The other thinks the divergence was older and deeper -- institutions, property rights, scientific culture, take your pick -- and suspects the revisionists are doing accounting tricks with the comparison. Write the joint statement they should both be able to sign at the end.

What I want in it:

1. A numbered section of claims both sides can accept, including about what the wage data and the demographic record actually support, and each side's single strongest point stated in wording the other side would sign off on (not through gritted teeth).

2. The residual disagreements, phrased so neither side would call the phrasing slanted. And I want the contrastive-question issue in there explicitly: is 'why Britain and not China?' even well posed? It seems to presuppose that the Industrial Revolution was going to happen *somewhere* and the only question was where, versus the revisionist take that it was a contingent near-miss that could have happened in the Yangzi delta or arguably nowhere.

3. The cruxes. I suspect one is the coal counterfactual: what happens in a world where English coal is as awkwardly located relative to English industry as China's coal was relative to the Yangzi delta. Another is the unit of comparison -- England vs. the Yangzi delta, vs. Britain vs. China, vs. Europe vs. China, and per capita vs. totals, since I can imagine the answer flipping depending on which you pick. And the Needham question is lurking under all of this: whether asking 'why didn't China do X' smuggles in the assumption that doing X was the default trajectory and China counts as a deviation needing explanation.

4. For each crux, name a kind of evidence or argument both would agree bears on it -- no reading lists or citations, just the type of thing, like real wage series, energy prices, mortality data, whatever -- and write down each side's prediction in advance so they can't retcon it later.

5. If part of the dispute turns out to be verbal rather than real -- e.g., they mean different things by 'comparable', or by 'the' Industrial Revolution, or by how early the divergence has to be to count as 'deep' -- say which part and why.

Keep it usable as an actual document: neutral enough that both would genuinely sign it, sharp enough that it's not a pile of platitudes. If you find yourself writing 'complex, multifactorial phenomenon' anywhere, you've failed.
