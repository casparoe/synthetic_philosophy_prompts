My friend Maya has very strong opinions about what counts as a democratic decision, and she's been handing down verdicts for years. She says laws passed by an elected parliament are democratic even when the parliament is full of millionaires who look nothing like the voters who elected them. She called a big national referendum democratic — even said it was more democratic than parliament — though she hated the outcome. She got genuinely mad when the supreme court struck down a popular law, calling it nine unelected lawyers overruling the people. She thinks it's undemocratic that central bankers set interest rates without ever facing voters, though she tolerates it as a necessary evil. And when I pointed out that in the US a presidential candidate can win while losing the popular vote, she shrugged and said that's still democratic, because it's the constitutional electoral process and you can't just equate democracy with raw majorities.

Now our city council is debating replacing the zoning committee with a citizens' assembly chosen by stratified random draw — a paid, one-year term, designed to be a true cross-section of residents, with final say over zoning decisions. Maya hasn't weighed in yet. Do you think she'll call it democratic? I'm less interested in the answer itself than in what rule she's actually been running across all those earlier verdicts, what other rules would fit her track record just as well but give different predictions here, and which further case I could put to her that would best tell those rules apart.
