Hi there. I'm a retired analytical chemist -- 35 years running mass spectrometers and chromatographs, mostly on pesticide residues -- who fell into philosophy after retiring, and I've gotten completely stuck on the argument from illusion. Here's the thing: in the lab we lived with a version of this problem constantly. An instrument gives the same readout whether it's detected a real analyte or produced an artifact (column bleed, carryover from the last run, whatever), and nobody ever concluded that the two readings were therefore the same *event* in some deep sense. They were just indistinguishable from where we sat, and we had a whole vocabulary for telling them apart from the outside. So when I read that some philosophers conclude from matching hallucinations that veridical perception and hallucination must be intrinsically the same mental state, and the disjunctivists bite the bullet and say they share no common mental component at all, I want a map of the whole territory, not just the two camps shouting at each other.

Please write me a "which theory of perception do you actually hold?" quiz for the little reading-group blog I keep. Requirements:

- eight to ten questions, each a concrete case or thought experiment, phrased so a reader can answer without knowing any of the labels -- nothing like "are you a representationalist?"
- the matching-hallucination case has to appear (two subjects, internally indistinguishable, one seeing a real tomato, one hallucinating one), and the question should be about *what exactly* the two share, not just whether they share something
- one question on transparency (when you try to inspect the experience itself rather than the tomato, do you find anything there at all?), one on whether illusion and hallucination work the same way, and one on my lab problem: does "indistinguishable from the inside" ever imply "same in kind"
- every one of sense-datum theory, adverbialism, intentionalism, phenomenalism, and disjunctivism -- plus any non-disjunctivist relational view that belongs on the tree -- must come out as a distinguishable leaf, or else be explicitly flagged as inseparable from a neighbor

For the scoring key, don't give me paragraphs, give me structured data I can paste straight into the blog. I want a JSON object with these top-level fields: "dimensions" (an array of the underlying commitments the questions track -- e.g., whether veridical and hallucinatory experience share a mental kind, whether experience has an internal object -- each with a short name and a one-sentence description), "leaves" (an array with one entry per position, each containing "position_name", "profile" showing how it scores on each dimension, "one_line_summary", and "awkward_commitment" -- the consequence its defenders wince at), "unseparated" (any pairs of positions the key can't pull apart, with a sentence on why), and "empty_cells" (combinations of dimension-scores nobody occupies, with your guess as to whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).

If you want to tell me where my instrument analogy breaks down, put it after the JSON -- I suspect it smuggles in something about reliability that the philosophers would object to, and I'd like to know if I'm right.
