I am a high school junior writing the final paper for an elective on epistemology, and I would like your help thinking through a pattern I noticed on a recent school trip to a natural history museum. In the hall of ancient artifacts, nearly every object whose use the label could not confidently explain was described as possibly having served a ritual or ceremonial purpose — a smooth stone with a hole through it, a small clay figure with no visible practical function, a set of polished discs of unknown use. Meanwhile, anything whose use could be determined was labeled as a tool, a cooking vessel, or an ornament. When I asked the docent how anyone knew the unexplained objects were ritual, she said, in essence, that no one knows, but since every known society practices ritual, it is the safest guess. My classmates joked afterward that in that museum, at least, the word apparently means we have no idea.

For my paper, I want to examine what this labeling practice presupposes about how we can know the minds of people who left no writing. In particular: what does the move from no identifiable practical function to probable religious function presuppose about the relationship between our categories and theirs? Does it treat ritual as a residual category — a bin for whatever falls outside our functional vocabulary — and if so, does the label then describe the limits of our imagination rather than the object? Or is it a legitimate inference to the best explanation, resting on cross-cultural facts about human societies? I am also puzzled by an apparent inconsistency in the same hall: grave goods were described as ritual not because they lacked a practical explanation but because of their burial context, which seems like a different kind of inference entirely, yet both got the same word.

Please reconstruct the implicit theory of inference the practice relies on, say whether it is coherent, distinguish what the practice strictly requires from what it merely fits well with, and tell me what would have to change in the labels if museums adopted the stricter standard of openly admitting that a function is unknown. Lead with your conclusion, since my teacher requires that, and keep the whole answer under 500 words.
