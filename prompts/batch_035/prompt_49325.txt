I keep bumping into something in the levels-of-selection stuff and want to know if there's a proper name for it. Quick background: I'm a hobbyist who's read the popular books on selfish genes and on group selection, so I know the basic fight about whether adaptations are "for" the gene, the individual organism, or the group. But here's the thing I've noticed and can't find a label for.

Sometimes when two people "disagree" about the unit of selection, you can translate either one's claim into the other's vocabulary, and they come out saying exactly the same thing about the world. Like with sterile worker insects: one friend tells it as the gene's story (a gene making its carrier sterile can still spread because copies sit in the queen, who reproduces plenty), and another tells it as the colony's story (colonies with sterile workers outcompete colonies where everyone breeds). When I actually write out the numbers, these seem to be the same causal story entered in two different ledgers, and the "disagreement" is about which ledger you find natural, not about any beetle or bee. It's almost like arguing whether the company's profits are "really" generated by the sales team or by the firm as a whole — you're partitioning the same dollars differently.

But other times the levels genuinely fight, like with those selfish chromosomes that cheat at meiosis and distort the sex ratio, and then individual organisms end up evolving suppressors against their own genes, so it seems like there's a real fact of the matter about which level is "winning." And I also suspect some of the gene-vs-colony stories that look like pure re-description secretly aren't, because maybe someone averaged over the wrong grouping and the colony effect was hiding in the average all along. I just can't tell which cases are which from the armchair.

So my questions: does the "same story, two ledgers" phenomenon have an established name in the philosophy of biology literature? And more importantly, is there a worked-out way to distinguish the cases where picking a level is just a bookkeeping choice from the cases where the levels are doing genuinely different causal work — ideally with a rule of thumb I could apply to a new example, not just a name. I won't be satisfied with "oh that's called X" with no explanation, because honestly I'm not even sure the two kinds of case I described are really two kinds. If there's no established concept, tell me, and then coin something.
