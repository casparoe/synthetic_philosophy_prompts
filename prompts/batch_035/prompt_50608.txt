Been trying to work out what I actually believe about pleasure vs pain, just by going with my gut on cases, and I'd like to know if there's a single principle behind my reactions or whether I'm just contradicting myself. Here are my verdicts.

Preventing someone's migraine matters more than giving someone else a lovely evening. Inflicting a paper cut on someone to get them front-row tickets to their favourite band: fine, obviously fine. Torturing one person in order to give a million other people genuinely ecstatic lives: not fine, and still not fine if you make it a billion. However much painless joy you hand out, it never pays for the torture. And this feels different in kind, not just size, from the paper cut case — but I can't say why, since both are just pain bought with pleasure.

A pinprick versus a wonderful meal: the meal wins, no contest. But a constant dull backache for a year versus a wonderful meal: the backache now dominates, even though no single moment of it is worse than the pinprick. So intensity can't be the whole story, or can it? I honestly can't tell if it's intensity or duration doing the work there, and they pull in opposite directions in my gut.

The case I keep flip-flopping on: someone whose life is overwhelmingly blissful except for one permanent mild ache. Is that life good on balance? My gut says yes, clearly. But my gut also said no amount of pleasure compensates torture, and if that was really a principle about pain being incommensurable with pleasure above some threshold, then I'd expect the ache to poison the whole ledger too — unless the ache is below whatever line I'm drawing. Which makes me suspect I don't have a line at all, just a very steep exchange rate between pleasure and pain that I can't picture from where I'm sitting.

So: is there a principle that captures all of this? Something like a lexical threshold — some level of pain past which pleasure just stops counting — would save my torture verdict, but I want to know whether it can also save the pinprick and backache verdicts, and whether the blissful-life-with-an-ache case comes out right under it. Or is the simpler story just that pleasure and pain are commensurable with a brutally steep conversion rate, in which case some of my verdicts have to go — and which ones? Please state whatever principle you land on precisely enough to apply to new cases, tell me what it says about a case I haven't mentioned, and be honest about which of my verdicts it can't acommodate. Also please answer in British English.
