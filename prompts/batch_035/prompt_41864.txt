I run a small giving circle — twelve of us, everyone chips in $75 a month, and once a quarter we sit down for about 90 minutes and decide where the pooled money (a couple grand) goes. It should be simple but it's become a mess. The problem isn't the amounts, it's that we all care about genuinely different things and nobody's training in anything relevant: I'm intense about factory farming, someone else is all about adult literacy in our city, another two are global health people, and so on. Right now we either majority-vote, which mostly means whoever brought the most friends that evening wins, or we split the pot in proportion to how loud everyone was, and honestly both feel bad.

A friend who's into the effective altruism internet told me about "moral trade" — the idea that people with different values can both come out ahead by trading support: if you care way more about X than I do, and I care way more about Y than you, we should each get near-total backing on our top issue instead of each getting lukewarm support on everything. Sort of comparative advantage, but applied to caring about stuff. I like it but I have no idea how to turn it into something a group of twelve tired people in a room can actually run after work. So: please write us a procedure. Questions to ask in order, how to elicit how much people care (plain rankings won't capture it, right? two people can both rank their cause first while one would trade it away for almost anything and the other wouldn't trade it for anything), what counts as a deal being struck, where the procedure stops and says this one's a values question the humans have to settle themselves, and where it should just say no outright.

Then run it on three cases that are pretty close to real: me (factory farming is my thing and I'd trade away most of my other preferences for leverage on it, except I won't support anything that promotes meat eating, full stop); Sarah, whose cause is gun violence prevention and who gets actually offended when anyone proposes weighing it against anything — she says you can't put a price on it; and the global health / local libraries pair, who each rank their own cause first but honestly don't feel super strongly about either. Include at least one case where the honest answer is that no trade is available and we should just vote or split, even though that's not what I want to hear. Also be straight with me about where this will misfire — I can already picture people inflating how much they care to get a better deal — and tell me which parts of the procedure are real and which are judgment calls dressed up as math, and what the whole thing can't do at all, like when a brand new member shows up with a cause nobody else shares. We vote once a year on whether to keep using whatever you come up with, so it can't be something only I understand. Sorry this got long, it's been a frustrating year.
