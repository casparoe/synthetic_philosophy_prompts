been reading lesswrong and the slatestarcodex-adjacent sphere on and off for like a decade, and one thing that community is real confident about is that a belief is basically an anticipation-controller: if you say you believe X, you should be able to point at experiences you expect differently because of X, and if nothing in your anticipated experience hinges on it, the belief is "not paying rent". under that framing religious claims look like textbook non-payers, and god-as-hypothesis gets assessed (and mostly demolished) the same way you'd assess bigfoot, just with worse priors.

so heres what threw me: I found out Hilary Putnam -- the twin earth / brains in a vat guy, whose stuff on reference and meaning I'd actually run into via the LW-adjacent discussions of GRAINS and natural kinds -- spent his late career getting more and more religiously serious, wrote a book on rosenzweig, buber, levinas and wittgenstein as a guide to life, and somewhere in there claims that thinking of religious language as a hypothesis about what exists is itself the mistake. not "the hypothesis is badly evidenced" but "that's the wrong kind of speech act entirely". also his whole thing about scientism being self-undermining (the claim that only science gives knowledge isn't itself a scientific finding etc).

what I want: take Putnam's actual position on religion -- the late Putnam, the pragmatist/wittgensteinian one, including whatever he says about god-talk, buber's I-thou stuff, negative theology if that's in there, and his anti-scientism argument -- and translate it into the vocabulary I actually use. bayesian terms, map/territory, anticipations, epistemic vs instrumental rationality, beliefs-that-pay-rent, maybe "guessing the teachers password" and the whole toolbox. do it as faithfully as you can, steelmanning him, dont sand off the bits that are awkward for my side.

then the part I actually care about: the residue report. which parts of his view translate cleanly into "beliefs as anticipation-controllers plus some instrumental stuff"? which parts only translate with distortion? and is there anything that has no expression at all in my vocabulary -- and if so, is that because the rationalist framework is missing a legitimate kind of talk (which is basically his thesis, I think), or because his position is vaguer than it sounds and the residue is just fuzz?
