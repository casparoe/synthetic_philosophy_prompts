Looking for a term here, if one exists. I keep noticing that categories of people seem to change the very people they pick out. Once a diagnosis makes it into the DSM, patients start showing up presenting just like the textbook description; once a label for some group catches on, the people in the group start organizing around it, embracing it or fighting it, and either way they aren't quite the same population afterward. Sometimes the pushback is strong enough that the category itself dies — homosexuality got dropped from the DSM partly because the people being classified refused to go on being classified that way. So the classification acts on the classified, and the classified act back on the classification, and this can go around for a while until either the people or the category gives out.

The nearest thing I know is "self-fulfilling prophecy," but that seems to be about a single prediction coming true, not an ongoing two-way process where the category itself can get reshaped or abolished by its members. And it's not just stereotyping, since the feedback can also make the category more accurate or more useful, not just more wrong.

Is there an established concept or literature for this? If so, how is it supposed to differ from self-fulfilling prophecy, and does it have a story about the cases where the loop ends with the category collapsing? If nothing fits exactly, please coin something and define it carefully, because I want to know where the edges are — e.g. does this only apply to kinds of people, or could you say money works the same way (we treat bills as valuable, which keeps them valuable), even though dollar bills can't exactly push back on the classification the way patients can? A bare name won't help me, I need the carving.
