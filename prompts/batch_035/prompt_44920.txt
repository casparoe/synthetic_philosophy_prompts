PhD student in political philosophy here, chapter on prioritarianism, and before I go hunting for what weighting functions people actually propose I want the spec first. Assume the leveling-down objection is fatal for telic egalitarianism, so we go the prioritarian route: a benefit matters more the worse off its recipient is. What would an adequate prioritarian weighting scheme have to deliver? I have candidate requirements of my own but I suspect half of them are just egalitarian intuitions I haven't let go of yet. Things like: moral value of a benefit must be concave in the recipient's absolute welfare level (and not just a function of their rank relative to others — that distinction needs to be made precise and defended, since only the absolute version lets you evaluate one person in isolation); the scheme should be seperable across persons so you can score subgroups, which is arguably what separates prioritarianism from egalitarianism in disguise; it should say a tiny gain to someone at level 2 beats a huge gain to someone at level 90, but also give an answer to the mirror case (a one-unit gain to the very worst off vs. a life-transforming gain just above them) that I won't immediately regret. Give me the list with the case or consideration behind each, flag which are non-negotiable and which are camp prejudices, and tell me where the list becomes jointly unsatisfiable if it does — I specifically want to know whether separability and any anti-tyranny-of-the-worst-off requirement can coexist, since that's exactly where my chapter is stuck.
