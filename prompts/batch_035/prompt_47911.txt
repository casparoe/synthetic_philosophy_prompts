Last night my oldest friend and I had one of those arguments that begins over a single sentence and ends after midnight, and I want to put it to productive use. He is a foundationalist of a broadly empiricist temper: he holds that some perceptual beliefs are justified non-inferentially, by how things seem to us, and that all other justified beliefs ultimately rest on this base. I took the coherentist side: no belief is justified in isolation; a belief is justified by membership in a web whose elements explain and support one another, and his so-called basic beliefs either quietly involve an inference from appearances or else merely label the point at which he has stopped asking for reasons. Neither of us moved an inch, and we eventually admitted we have been running versions of this argument for a decade. We have resolved to do it properly: to treat ourselves as the two parties to an adversarial collaboration and to draft the joint statement we would both be willing to sign. Draft that statement on our behalf, and follow the specification below.

First, include a numbered section of agreed claims — at least five — that both a foundationalist and a coherentist of our respective stripes could sign without discomfort. These should include the claim that the regress problem is genuine and is not dissolved by observing that people in fact stop giving reasons; that the dispute concerns the structure of epistemic justification rather than the psychology of actual reasoners; that any legitimate stopping point must be principled rather than an arbitrary refusal to continue; and that whatever justification turns out to be, it must be answerable to truth in a way both parties can articulate. You may add further agreed claims if you can find ones that are neither vacuous nor slanted toward either side.

Second, include a disagreements table with the following columns: neutral topic; his formulation of the disagreement (worded as a foundationalist would accept it); my formulation (worded as a coherentist would accept it); crux (the subquestion such that settling it would move at least one of us); agreed test or consideration (the case, argument, or thought experiment both sides accept as bearing on the crux); his prediction; my prediction. The table must cover at least four topics: (i) whether experiences or seemings that are not themselves beliefs can confer justification, or whether all epistemic support must run between beliefs — which is where the coherentist alleges that his view tacitly infers from appearances while calling the inference a given; (ii) the isolation objection — whether a coherentist can say anything against someone whose entire outlook is internally tidy but sealed off from the world, using a concrete case both sides accept as relevant, such as a person whose elaborate system of divination hangs together beautifully and never touches independent check; (iii) the distinction between generating and merely transmitting warrant — whether his basic beliefs do positive justificatory work or just pass presuppositions down the line, and whether calling a belief self-evident explains the stopping point or only names it; and (iv) the existence of rival but equally coherent total systems, and whether the coherentist has non-question-begging resources for choosing among them.

Third, add a short section identifying any part of our dispute that turned out to be verbal — for instance, whether the word justified applies univocally to individual beliefs and to whole systems — and say, for each such part, why it was verbal and how the wording should be revised to remove it.

Fourth, since we both dismissed infinitism — the view that a belief is justified only by an actually infinite, non-repeating chain of reasons, each justifying the next — within about thirty seconds of its being raised, include a brief section recording where an infinitist would sign our statement and the single strongest point an infinitist would press against both of us. We want to be fair to a position neither of us holds.

Close the document with a machine-readable summary: a single JSON object with the fields agreed_claims (an array of strings), disagreements (an array of objects, each with the fields neutral_topic, foundationalist_wording, coherentist_wording, crux, agreed_test, foundationalist_prediction, coherentist_prediction), verbal_disputes (an array of strings), and infinitist_note (a string).

Keep the register formal and even-handed throughout: no name-dropping, no citations, and no formulations either of us would reject as a rigged description of our own view. The test of the draft is that, if we each read it cold, we would both recognize our positions stated fairly and both be embarrassed only by how precisely the table predicts our answers.
