I'm a second-year grad student in a program on mass atrocity and humanitarian policy, and my advisor -- the person whose takes basically set the vocabulary for our whole research group -- has a long enough track record of verdicts about when the word "genocide" applies that we can finally test whether there's a consistent rule under it. Her past calls, as best I can reconstruct them: Rwanda, obviously yes. Srebrenica, yes -- she's insistent that killing the militairy-aged men of one town counts even though it's thousands rather than hundreds of thousands, because it's that community destroyed as a community. The Khmer Rouge, no -- atrocity on a mind-bending scale, but the perpetrators were sorting victims by class background rather than destroying a group as a group, and she calls it mass atrocity, not genocide. The Armenian deportations, yes, on the grounds that the marches and dispersals were engineered so Armenians would cease to exist as a people in the empire. The Ukrainian famine, yes, while ordinary drought famines elsewhere, no. Boarding-school style forced assimilation of an indigenous minority -- children removed, language banned, but the community surviving as a community -- no, she calls that ethnocide, a real crime but a different category. And a campaign where security forces kill a few thousand civilians of an ethnic minority with the goal of driving the survivors over the border, she refuses to call genocide (ethnic cleansing, and she gets genuinely annoyed when people stretch the term that way). She also wrote her dissertation on Lemkin and likes to say the 1948 convention was watered down at the drafting, which is why I can't just read the convention off as her view.

The new case is hypothetical -- it's from a seminar simulation, modeled on a fictional republic, which is honestly what makes it fun. Over roughly twenty years, a government reduces a minority of about 600,000 to effectively zero without any mass killing. Its institutions (schools, religious endowments, village councils, newspapers) are dissolved, its language banned from public life, its members dispersed by a state relocation scheme into majority districts a few families at a time, with intermarriage subsidies and majority-language schooling for the children. There's low-grade violence -- a few hundred killed suppressing resistance over the years, some deaths from resettlement conditions -- but nothing you'd call a campaign of killing. Internal documents talk about "integration" and "completing the homogeneity of the nation," never about destroying anyone, and some actually lament past violence against the minority and frame the whole program as rescuing them from backwardness. After two decades, census-takers can't find the minority as a self-identifying group at all.

Predict what she'll say about this case. I want a definite call from you, not a menu -- but back it up: lay out the rules that fit her track record equally well (I can see at least the narrow physical-destruction reading of the convention and something more Lemkinian, where ending a group's existence as a group counts even without bodies stacking up, and I suspect both survive her earlier verdicts), say where they diverge on this case, and then commit to a single best guess with your reasons. Also, if you can, tell me what further case I could raise at seminar to smoke out which rule she's actually running, because she'll definitely deflect if I ask her directly.
