I help decide which technical safety directions get funded at a frontier lab. Nearly every pitch I hear reduces to the same promise — this line of work will shrink or eliminate the alignment tax — but each pitch quietly means something different by "the tax," and I've stopped trusting any of them until I know what an elimination claim would actually have to deliver. So write me the spec before I read another proposal.

The setting to assume: a single developer, no binding international agreement, competitors (other frontier labs and open-weight efforts) at most a few months behind, and decisions made now about research bets that pay off over years. Adequacy is relative to that setting — a proposal only needs to be good enough to be worth funding under those conditions, not to solve the problem for all time. By the alignment tax I mean, roughly, whatever a developer gives up — capability, speed, efficiency, time — to deploy a safe system where it could have deployed a more capable unsafe one; the competitiveness constraint is the pressure that makes paying that tax fatal.

Write the desiderata any adequate alignment-tax-reduction proposal must meet: which costs it must actually be reducing (deployment-time capability, inference efficiency, development velocity, time-to-threshold — a proposal that quotes a zero tax without saying which of these it means is quoting an average over different questions); which counterfactual the comparison is against (the same lab without the safety work, the nearest competitor without it, or an open-weight release — these can give different signs for the same investment); and which incentives it leaves intact rather than merely rerouting. For each requirement, give the case behind it. Then mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of one camp — in particular, adjudicate whether "must not rely on coordination" is a genuine desideratum or just the technical camp's prejudice, given that any mechanism whose effect runs through competitors' behavior is arguably a coordination scheme wearing a technical hat. Flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied; if you think some subset is jointly unsatisfiable in principle (say, because a tax that is invisible at current capability levels but grows steeply past a threshold cannot be both measured and held to zero), say so explicitly — that result would itself change the funding decision.

Two ideas I want you to take a position on rather than assume: (1) whether the tax should be quoted per capability level, since a safety cost that only bites above roughly human-level performance in the relevant domain is a different animal from one that bites now; (2) the second-best question — whether a proposal can count as adequate while leaving a real tax, provided the tax is smaller than the risk discount competitors assign to catastrophic outcomes, or whether adequacy requires the tax to be near zero on its own terms.

Finally, score these three approaches briefly against your own list, flagging where the list does the eliminating: (a) safety-as-capability — the bet that alignment techniques, scaled up, improve product performance enough to pay for themselves, the claim sometimes made about RLHF-era methods; (b) bottlenecking at deployment — race on development, gate only the release of the most capable checkpoints, accepting a tax concentrated at the end; (c) provision — an actor with compute and talent deliberately releasing safe, near-frontier models at subsidized cost to undercut the market for unsafe ones. Keep the weight on the criteria; the scorecard should take less than a quarter of your answer.
