World A has countably infinitely many people all at +5 wellbeing. World B is exactly A plus ten extra people also at +5: I feel like B is no better than A, ten more against infinity is nothing. World C is A except one of the infinitely many is tortured at -1000: that's clearly worse, no hesitation. World D is A but everyone is at +6: obviously better. An angel offers everyone in A +1 each day, forever: obviously better too, which bugs me, since if a finite pile of extra goodness does nothing, why does an infinite sequence of infinite piles register? And World E, where infinitely many of the people (a "half," so still countably many) get bumped up to +6: I keep going back and forth on whether that's better, especially becuase which half seems to depend on how you line the two worlds up. Is there a principle here — one-to-one matching, cardinality comparisons, some lexical thing — that captures all of these, what does it say about a world with infinitely many tortured people versus a world with none, and which of my verdicts would I have to give up?
