I've served for four years on my city's drug policy advisory board, a volunteer commission that issues recommendations the council almost always adopts, and next month we have to take a position on a citizen initiative: permit home cultivation of psilocybin mushrooms, up to a small fixed quantity, strictly for personal use, with all sale, barter, and gifting outside the household still prohibited and commercial production and retail remaining banned. The board's track record, which is what I want us to be consistent with: we backed depenalizing personal cannabis possession after a review found that enforcement in our city fell overwhelmingly on residents of two neighborhoods, though we stopped short of endorsing expungement of past convictions; we later came out against the statewide cannabis bill because it created licensed for-profit retail, with several members arguing on the record that the lesson of tobacco is that commerce, not chemistry, does the damage; we have consistently supported retaining criminal penalties for methamphetamine supply while pushing hard for treatment capacity and against lengthening sentences; and we endorsed both the syringe exchange and the supervised consumption site, on the reasoning that whatever one thinks of prohibition, it must never obstruct reducing deaths.

Here is my problem. At least two principles fit everything we have ever done. One says, roughly: criminalize commerce in dependence-forming or heavily marketable substances, but never criminalize the user, and stay out of the way of harm reduction — and on that reading, home cultivation with no sales is exactly the kind of personal use we have been leaving room for all along. Another says, roughly: we soften enforcement where enforcement is doing the damage, but we do not legalize anything still fully prohibited — and on that reading, psilocybin is a psychoactive we have never blessed, and the initiative asks us to cross a line our cannabis retail dissent was meant to hold. I suspect these two rules give different verdicts on the mushrooms, and I would add that the harm rankings I have seen put psilocybin well below methamphetamine and plausibly below alcohol, which cuts in favor of the first rule but is not what actually moved us on any earlier vote. So: if we want to be consistent with ourselves, what should we recommend? Which of our precedents is genuinely the closest analog — the cannabis retail dissent, the depenalization endorsement, or the harm-reduction votes? And if our record underdetermines the call, say plainly what we would be committing ourselves to with each choice, including what it would imply for a follow-up initiative to permit cannabis social clubs (on-premises consumption, members only, no sales) and for the standing question of home distillation of spirits, which our jurisdiction bans even though home brewing of beer and winemaking are allowed.
