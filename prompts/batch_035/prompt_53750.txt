I coordinate peer review at a mid-tier journal in evolutionary biology and behavioral ecology (I'm not going to name it, doesn't matter). A large chunk of what we publish is basically "here's a trait, here's a plausible benefit of the trait, therefore it's an adaptation for that benefit" with the adaptive story doing the work of evidence. Our referees are volunteers with maybe three hours per manuscript and zero patience for being assigned philosophy reading, and right now they do one of two things: wave the story through because it's cute, or stamp "just-so story" on it like that settles something. Both happen constantly and I can't tell which is which either.

What I want is a procedure a referee can actually run in about an hour on any manuscript whose central claim is that some trait is an adaptation for some function. The grounding is the Gould–Lewontin spandrels critique — the point that a benefit story isn't evidence until the alternatives are actually compared against it: drift, developmental and mechanical constraint, byproducts riding along on something that was selected (the actual spandrels), traits that are there because of the organism's genetic/architectural plumbing rather than because each one is "for" something, and past selection for a different use than the present one. But it also has to carry the critique's honest edges: it can't just be a rejection machine, because sometimes the adaptationist is right and has the data.

Specifics I need:

- A numbered list of questions, in the order a referee should ask them, with what counts as a sufficient answer to each.
- For each question, where it sends the review: reject (and on which grounds, phrased so it fits in a standard review form), major revisions (asking for what exactly), or "referee can't settle this, it goes to the editor as a values-and-framing call."
- A guardrail: how does a referee tell when they're using spandrels-talk as its own just-so story — asserting "it's just a byproduct / it's just constraint" with no more evidence than the adaptationist had? Because we get papers like that too, and I sometimes can't distinguish them from the ones waving the flag, and I'm the one supposed to be coordinating this.
- A short section on where the procedure predictably misfires — cases where the checklist gives the wrong answer or no answer.

Then run it on three hypothetical manuscripts (one-liners here, treat them as real):

1. Caterpillars of some beetle are vivid yellow and chemically defended; the paper claims the coloration is an adaptive warning signal, supported by the facts that the caterpillars are toxic and birds avoid them in the wild. Nothing in the study separates the color from the toxins, and yellow pigment in this clade turns out to track diet anyway.
2. A paper arguing the human female orgasm is a byproduct of selection on the male orgasm — same embryonic pathway, no separate selection needed — pitched explicitly as the "skeptical alternative" to adaptationist accounts, with the evidence being that the byproduct story requires positing less selection.
3. A lizard tail-autotomy paper with comparative data across species (predation pressure predicts shedding thresholds), biomechanics of the fracture plane, and a field manipulation showing survival differences. Honestly kind of a boring, well-done adaptationist paper.

I'll admit case 2 stings — I'd kind of like that one to sail through, and if the procedure lets it while killing the caterpillar paper I want to see exactly how it distinguishes them, because "byproduct of" is doing suspiciously similar work to "adapted for" there. Also, for case 3, I want the procedure to show its work enough that a referee can't just reject it for being adaptationist-by-vibes.

One more constraint: nothing in the procedure should require the referee to read anything besides the manuscript, and the questions should be runnable by a smart PhD student in a related-but-different subfield, because that's who we get.

Format: start with the procedure itself — the numbered checklist, first thing, no preamble and no restating what I asked for. Then the three worked cases, then the misfire section, then whatever else needs saying.
