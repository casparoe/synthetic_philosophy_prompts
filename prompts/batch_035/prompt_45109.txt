I'm a retired geologist who fell into philosophy in my sixties, so go easy on me. At my reading group the other night, Dana and Miguel got into it about the leveling-down objection and I honestly couldn't tell who was winning.

Dana opened with the standard line: an egalitarianism that treats inequality as bad in itself has to say that a world where everyone is equally blind is, in one respect, better than one where all but one person are blind — nobody is better off and one person is worse off — which seems nuts. So we should drop equality as an intrinsic good and be prioritarians instead: benefits count for more the worse off the recipient is, which explains why we should favor curing the blind person without ever implying that equal blindness is partly good.

Miguel said the fix doesn't fix much. Run the same machinery inside a single life: suppose your life goes badly in your twenties and well in your fifties. Prioritarianism then implies it's better for the good stuff in your life to be shifted toward the bad years even if the total goes down — the priority weighting applies within a life just as much as across lives, so you get leveling down again, only for one person across time. And anyway, he said, strict prioritarianism has its own absurd bullet: if the worse-off person could gain a sliver while a better-off person could gain enormously, the weighting tells you to take the sliver. If Dana refuses that, she's sneaking total welfare back in, and once totals are back in, the gap between her view and an egalitarianism that only cares about inequality when it affects how well peoples lives go gets pretty thin.

Dana's comeback was that Miguel was confusing two claims: that inequality is bad full stop, versus that inequality is bad only when removing it would make someone's life go better. She said she only needs the second to block leveling down — equality is derivative on lives going well, not a freestanding value — and that this doesn't collapse her into a total-welfarist, because the worse-off person's claim still gets extra weight, which a pure total view denies.

Miguel wasn't having it: the extra weight is exactly what the one-life case and the sliver case put pressure on, so her view can't be doing the weighting at the level she says it is.

Please go through their exchange move by move and tell me who's right about what, and whether either of them is missing a way out.
