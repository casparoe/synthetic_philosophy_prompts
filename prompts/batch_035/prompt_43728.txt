I'm a senior in my high school's philosophy club, and I've become fascinated by the question of whether the relation "better than" has to be transitive — that is, whether, if A is better than B and B is better than C, A must be better than C. Please make an argument that "better than" is not transitive, using spectrum-style reasoning. To keep things concrete, here is a case I invented: a company sells 1,001 models of noise-cancelling headphones, numbered 0 through 1000. Going from model n to model n+1, the battery life increases by one minute, while the active noise cancellation filters out a sliver less of the low-frequency engine drone — a sliver so small that no listener can hear the difference between consecutive models. So it seems each model is slightly better than the one before it: the battery gain, though small, is real, while the cancellation loss is undetectable. Yet comparing model 0 and model 1000 directly, the slivers have piled up: model 1000 lets through a crushing dull roar that model 0 blocks out, in exchange for only about seventeen extra hours of battery, and model 0 is clearly the better product. Please argue that this shows "better than" is not transitive, and in doing so address whether the argument cheats by assuming that undetectable differences in quality make no difference to which of two consecutive models is better. Please organize your response into three sections in this order: "The argument" (roughly 250 words), "The key objection and a reply" (roughly 150 words), and "Where this leaves transitivity" (roughly 100 words).
