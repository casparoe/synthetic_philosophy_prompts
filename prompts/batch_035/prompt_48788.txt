So in philosophy of mind everyone and their grandmother can imagine phenomenal consciousness without access consciousness — the pain you stop noticing because you're escaping a fire, the conversation you weren't attending to but can report a second later. The hard direction is the opposite one: a system with full access consciousness and no phenomenality, nothing it is like to be it. Block himself basically treated that direction as an open question at best, and his showcase devices (superblindsight, the stretched brain) are surgically engineered thought experiments that a global workspace theorist can just refuse to have intuitions about.

I want you to build me a case that goes in the hard direction and could plausibly exist. Constraints:

- No zombies, no inverted spectrum, no rewired brains, no neuro-astronomy, no "suppose we discovered...". The case has to be something a normal person could encounter or reasonably believe in, on present-day Earth.
- The system must genuinely satisfy the global workspace theorist's own checklist — information globally broadcast to memory, planning, and verbal report — so the case can't cheat by making the system unable to report or reason. If a workspace theorist runs their criteria, they should initially call this thing conscious.
- Do not stipulate that nothing is felt. That's the entire question. The case should generate the "no phenomenality" intuition from features independent of the workspace story, not from an authorial decree.

After the scenario, demonstrate that it works: (a) walk the GWT checklist item by item and show the system passes; (b) say what the workspace theorist should say, what a higher-order thought theorist should say, and what Block-style reasoning says, so I can see the verdicts falling out of the theories rather than being asserted; (c) identify the single feature doing the work in the no-feeling intuition, and tell me what happens to the case if you delete it; (d) give me the strongest attack available to the workspace theorist who wants to keep the system inside the conscious club — the version where they bite the bullet and argue it *is* conscious, not the lazy version where they deny the intuition pump counts.

This is for a seminar discussion where half the room are functionalists, so a case that quietly smuggles in a zombie will get spotted and wreck it.

One more thing: throughout the answer, please keep separated what's actually settled from what's contested among people who know this literature. I'm aware the access/phenomenal distinction itself is contested, and that workspace theory comes in flavors — Dehaene's version ties consciousness to late amplification and reportability in ways other workspace people don't accept. Flag wherever you're leaning on a contested reading rather than the mainstream one, and if you conclude the case can't be built under my constraints, say that plainly and tell me which constraint to relax and why, instead of quietly breaking one.
