I'm doing a master's in cognitive science, and my course this term keeps mentioning predictive processing, which I'd only ever bumped into through pop science stuff before. Last week a friend of mine who's really into neuroscience (she's read a couple of Friston-adjacent pop books and watches a lot of lectures on youtube) gave me her summary of the whole framework, and honestly it sounded pretty convincing, but a few things about it sat oddly with what our lecturer said. Here's roughly what she told me:

"So predictive processing and the free energy principle are basically two names for the same theory: the brain is a prediction machine organised as a hierarchy, where higher levels send predictions down to lower levels and the lower levels send back only their prediction errors. The brain's single job is to minimise prediction error, and prediction error just is surprise, so the brain is literally trying to minimise how surprising the world is. Friston wrapped this up as 'free energy', which is the same free energy as in thermodynamics, so the theory ties cognition to physics directly. Because minimising surprise is the only goal, the theory actually predicts that the ideal strategy is to lie down in a dark, silent room and stay there -- and since nobody does that, the theory is pretty much refuted on its own terms. Also, since perception on this view is just the brain's best guess, hallucinations and normal perception differ only in degree, so the theory implies you can never really tell whether you're perceiving or hallucinating. What's left is just the old idea that the brain tests hypotheses about the world, dressed up in fancy maths, which is why it can explain everything and predict nothing -- an unfalsifiable framework."

I'm inclined to think she's got the general shape right (the hierarchy, the top-down predictions, the error signals) but some of the bigger claims felt like they were jumping ahead. Could you go through it bit by bit and tell me what someone actually working on this would accept, what they'd wince at, and what's just wrong? I'd especially like to know whether the dark room point and the unfalsifiability conclusion follow from the parts she got right, or whether they depend on the bits she got wrong.
