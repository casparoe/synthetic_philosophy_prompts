So my wife and I had our first kid back in March, and ever since the christening I've been unable to stop thinking about an argument my brother-in-law cornered me with (great venue for it, thanks Dave). He's spent the last year deep in population-ethics internet, has come out the other side a pretty hardcore suffering-focused utilitarian, and now thinks we did something wrong by having her. I want to say he's wrong but I can't find the joint to put the knife in, and every reply I've drafted either concedes way too much or way too little. Here's his argument as best I can reconstruct it, since he wouldn't write it down:

(1) Suffering is lexically worse than happiness is good: any amount of suffering, however small, outweighs any amount of happiness, however great. He got me to nod at this with the pinprick thing — he asked if I'd sign humanity up for a future of eternal bliss for everyone who ever lives, plus one second of torment for one kid. And honestly, no, I wouldn't, and he said then you already accept (1).

(2) Every human life contains suffering. Not controversial, and certainly true of a life that will include middle school.

(3) When you have a kid, you're choosing between a world where she exists — all that suffering, plus a lot of happiness, hers and ours — and a world where she doesn't, containing neither.

(4) By (1), the happiness can't offset or compensate the suffering. It's not even in the same currency; it's on the wrong side of a lexical divide, so no matter how the ledger looks in ordinary terms, the suffering side wins.

(5) So the world with her in it is worse than the one without, and having her was therefore wrong. And by the same reasoning, he says: everyone should stop reproducing; a painless extinction event we never saw coming would be a good thing, strictly; and — this is where I went to refill my drink — I should have my 13-year-old dog, whose arthritis is getting worse, put down this week rather than let her have another good year that ends badly.

I don't buy the conclusion. But I also don't want to just flatly deny (1), because I've spent enough time in hospital waiting rooms to feel that there's something to the idea that suffering matters in a way happiness doesn't. And flatly denying (5) feels like it costs me the ability to say anything principled at all about creating people. So: which step do I reject, why did it look fine to me in the moment, and what am I actually paying if I give it up? Walk me through the costs of the live options, not just the one you like.
