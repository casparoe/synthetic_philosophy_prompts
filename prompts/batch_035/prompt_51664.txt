Okay so this has been bugging me for a while and I want to get it straight in my head. I stopped eating meat about two years ago, mostly because of how factory farming works — I watched a documentary and it really got to me. But my brother-in-law keeps needling me about it, and lately his argument has gotten more annoying because I can't come up with a clean answer. His line is: the industry kills tens of billions of animals a year, so whether one person (me) picks up a pack of chicken thighs at the supermarket changes literally nothing. He says the whole supply chain works in batches — the shops order in bulk from wholesalers, the wholesalers order in bulk from processors, and my single purchase isn't going to make anyone up the chain order one unit less, so no animal is actually spared by me abstaining. And honestly, when I try to picture it, I can see what he means: it's not like there's a ledger somewhere tallying my purchase and then a chicken gets redirected. It feels like a drop in the ocean.

The thing that nags at me is this though. If his argument works for me, it seems like it works for everyone — each individual purchase is negligible, so nobody's choice matters — and yet when enough people shift their habits, the industry clearly does respond. I've watched vegan sections appear in my local shop over the last few years, which wouldn't have happened if none of us had changed anything. So a bunch of choices that each make no difference somehow add up to a big difference? A friend who's into maths tried to help by saying something like: even if there's only a small chance that my purchase is the one that tips the shop over into ordering one fewer case, the expected effect is some fraction of an animal affected, which is more than zero — but I genuinely can't tell whether that's a real argument or just something clever-sounding. It might even cut the other way, like if the batches are huge the chance of me being the tipping point is so tiny it rounds to nothing.

So my question is basically: is my brother-in-law right that individual purchases don't matter, and if he's wrong, where exactly does his reasoning go wrong? I'm not looking for moral preaching or a pep talk about integrity or "being the change" — I want the actual logic untangled. And if possible, help me phrase the core point so a smug engineer type would respect it, because every time this comes up at dinner it turns into a shouting match. Please write your answer in British English.
