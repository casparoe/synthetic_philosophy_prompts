our org has been going in circles on the importance-tractability-neglectedness framework for what feels like a year now and i want to try to figure out what kind of disagreement this actually is instead of relitigating it for the fifth time. quick map of the positions as they come up in our meetings: one group treats ITN as a genuine scoring rubric — you estimate the scale of the problem, divide by resources already committed, multiply by your tractability guess, and the products rank causes, and if the numbers come out weird that's a problem with the numbers, not the formula. a second group says neglectedness was never a criterion at all, just a shadow of diminishing marginal returns, so the three-factor picture should collapse into cost-effectiveness curves and the multiplication itself is basically a marketing artifact that survives because the acronym is catchy. and a third group says the whole thing smuggles contested commitments in through the inputs — "importance" already bakes in your population ethics, "tractability" bakes in how optimistic you are about political leverage — so the framework feels like objective arithmetic while all the actual disagreement hides in the estimates. the weird part is that when we run concrete cases, everyone gives roughly the same rankings — AI stuff and pandemic preparedness high, yet another deworming program low — and yet we still can't agree on why, and the fights keep being about the formula rather than any particular cause.
