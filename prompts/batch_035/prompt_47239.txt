I've been going around in circles on this for a while and could use someone to tell me which plank to pull out.

Background: I came across that Twin Earth thing (Putnam's, I think?) — the twin planet where the clear drinkable stuff in the lakes is some other chemical, XYZ, instead of H2O, and the people there use a word that sounds exactly like our "water." The claim was that their word means XYZ and ours means H2O, even if the speakers are molecule-for-molecule identical. So what your words mean isn't fully in your head; it depends on what's actually in the lakes around you, and on what the local experts would say about it.

Fine, I bought that. Then I pushed it a step further and now I can't find the exit. Here's the argument:

1. What my word "water" means depends on stuff outside my head — facts about my environment and about how the community around me uses the word.
2. Those facts are not frozen. The experts I defer to could shift their usage — new discoveries, changes in how careful people are with words. And the shift could be very gradual, small enough that no single step is noticeable from inside.
3. If a shift is slow enough, nobody in the community can point to the moment the meaning changed. Each day just looks like the day before.
4. So the meaning of my word can drift without me, or anyone, being able to tell from the inside.
5. If I can't tell from the inside whether my word has drifted, then at any moment I can't rule out that "water" in my mouth no longer means what it meant last year — and, worse, that it doesn't mean what I'd sincerely say it means. So I don't really know what my own words mean; I only think I do.
6. But surely if there's one thing I know, it's what I mean by my own words, right now, better than any outside observer does.
7. So (1) is false, meaning is in the head after all, and the whole Twin Earth trick was just a trick.

That conclusion feels way too strong — the Twin Earth story really did seem to show something. But every step above looked fine to me as I wrote it. My hunch is that something funny happens somewhere in the middle of that list, but I can't put my finger on it well enough to convince myself. And if the answer turns out to be "give up (6)," doesn't that leave me in the dark about my own mind, which seems even worse than where I started? I'd rather keep (1) and (6) if there's a way, but I want to know what that costs me.
