I got picked for a citizens' assembly that's supposed to recommend whether my country brings back conscription after ~30 years with an all-volunteer army, and somehow I have to draft the section on whether the draft is fair in principle. Two people at my table have become my main sources. The anti one says: if defense is really worth what it costs, the state should just pay a wage high enough that enough people volunteer — forcing 19-year-olds to serve is a tax in kind, on their time and on their chance of dying, that falls on them alone and that rich kids escape by moving or staying in school. The pro one says: defense isn't a service the state buys, it's something citizens owe each other like jury duty, and an army made only of people who need the paycheck is an army of the poor. They also keep tangling over my cousin, who says he'll refuse and take jail time: he says the state doesn't own his body, and the anti guy says fine, but then he's free-riding on the safety everyone else's service buys him. I can sort of feel that these three fights are actually one fight, but I can't pin down what the underlying disagreement is, and I need to write something the whole panel can at least live with. Where does the real philosophical dispute sit, and who has the better case?
