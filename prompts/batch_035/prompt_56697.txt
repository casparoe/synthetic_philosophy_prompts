I'm taking a seminar on rational choice theory, and for next week we've each been asked to write up our positions on diachronic rationality so the rest of the group can attack them. Here is mine, and I'd like an audit before I submit it: (1) I hold that cyclical preferences are irrational, and the money pump is supposed to show why — if I prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A, a trader can start me at A and cycle me down to pennies. (2) But I also think the money pump fails as an argument, because an agent who sees the sequence coming can simply refuse the later trades, so the pump only threatens someone who won't look ahead. (3) I accept the Dutch book argument for the probability axioms: violating them leaves you open to a guaranteed loss. (4) I think rationality is fundamentally synchronic — a choice is rational solely in virtue of how it relates to the beliefs and preferences you have at the moment of choosing. (5) Yet I believe it can be rational to commit to a plan at the outset and stick to it, even at a later point where deviating would better serve the preferences you'll hold then — and that sticking is not mere stubbornness. (6) I also think whether someone is rational can't depend on whether a bookie or trader happens to exist. Please tell me which of these commitments genuinely conflict, which only appear to, and what the cheapest revision would be, ranking the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious. I honestly don't know where the problem is, and I'm not yet sure which one I'd give up.
