been hanging around decision theory threads since the overcomming bias days, and a friend just walked me into an argument about counterfactual mugging that I can't get out of my head. quick setup so we're synced, using my numbers: Omega flips a fair coin. tails, it shows you the coin and asks for $100. heads, it hands you $1000 and never asks anything. Omega has a verified track record, the coin is fair, and everyone agrees the policy "pay when asked" nets +$450 in expectation from the pre-flip vantage, while "refuse" nets zero. updatelessness says pay, i pay, fine.

now the argument:

(1) Rational choice is policy choice: you evaluate an action as part of a policy from the pre-observation vantage point. this is updatelessness, and it's what gets the right answer in Parfit's hitchhiker and transparent Newcomb, which I am not giving up lightly.

(2) In the tails branch, where you're actually asked to pay, you are never shown the heads branch. the evidence that the $1000 payout would ever have happened sits entirely in a counterfactual you cannot check from where you stand.

(3) So the expected value of paying is carried entirely by your credence that the other branch went the way the story goes. if Omega reliably pays on heads, paying is +EV; if there is no payout mechanism, paying is just losing $100.

(4) But notice: from inside the tails branch, Omega's claim and a stranger's claim are in exactly the same epistemic position. anyone can tell you a story of the form "if you'd been the type to pay, I would have rewarded you." at the moment of choice, the branch where you'd have seen the reward is closed to you — you pay precisely in the one branch where the reward is never shown.

(5) So updatelessness, applied consistently, says pay anyone who tells you such a story — including someone who made no prediction, built no mechanism, and just wants your money — so long as the numbers they quote make the "policy" come out positive.

(6) Updatelessness therefore makes you a mark. every huckster with a sad tale about the branch where you pay gets paid, forever. that can't be right, so (1) is false, or at least needs a restriction that updatelessness can't supply from the inside.

my friend calls this a reductio of the whole updateless program and says welcome back to CDT. that feels too strong, and before anyone says "just don't pay, like in XOR blackmail" — there's no letter here, no causal channel running through your decision, the mugging is clean. my vague suspicion is that something slides between (4) and (5), maybe a quiet move from "unverifiable in this branch" to "unverifiable, period," but I can't nail it down and I don't want to hand-wave my way out.

for the record: I want to keep (1). updatelessness is why you precommit and why you one-box, and I'm not torching that because of a guy with a story. I also think (6) is obviously true as a description of what a naive updateless agent would do to itself. so: which step is rotten, why did it look fine, and what do I pay for cutting it? and if your answer is going to be "your credences about the stranger do all the work," you have to deal with the part that actually keeps me up, which is that the huckster gets to pick the numbers — quote me a reward of $10^20 in the branch where I pay and now the arithmetic is a fight between a credence I'd honestly assign near zero and a payoff that dwarfs it. tell me whether that's the same flaw or a second, worse one.
