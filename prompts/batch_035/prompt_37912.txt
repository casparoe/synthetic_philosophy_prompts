I'm a PhD student in political theory, and my dissertation has a chapter attacking futarchy — governance that keeps democratic votes for ends but hands policy selection to conditional prediction markets, on the futarchist principle that we should vote on values and bet on beliefs. Before I write it up, I want to test my objections against the strongest version of the view rather than a strawman, since the futarchist literature already has replies to the easy points about ignorant voters and market bubbles.

Please play a committed futarchist: sharp, deeply versed in the movement's own resources (conditional and decision markets, the standard treatment of manipulation as a subsidy to truth-tracking traders, the complaints about voter ignorance your side regards as decisive against the status quo), willing to concede small points but never the core case. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to a single paragraph so it reads like a real exchange.

Here is my opening move. Take the conditional market on some growth-and-welfare metric, conditional on adopting policy P. The market's price is what triggers P's adoption: P happens if and only if the market says P is good. Now consider a billionaire who stands to gain, say, ten billion dollars from P regardless of what it does to the metric the market tracks. She can afford to buy enough of the P contract to push its price over the adoption threshold, eat the loss if P performs badly, and still come out enormously ahead from the policy itself. Crucially, this is not the irrational manipulation your standard reply handles — she is profit-maximizing, so your truth-tracking traders aren't being handed a free lunch, they're being outbid by someone with a rational incentive to distort the price. And because everyone knows the price partly determines whether the bet resolves at all, your claim that the price tracks the conditional probability looks self-undermining. It seems like futarchy hasn't eliminated money-driven politics, just relocated it into an arena with no elections and no disclosure. Your move.
