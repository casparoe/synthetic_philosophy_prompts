"Ever since Hume, the orthodox view has been that our warrant for believing what we are told reduces to warrant from other sources: each of us has observed, over many encounters, that people are mostly truthful in most contexts, and it is on the strength of that inductive track record that we are entitled to believe a particular speaker. Anti-reductionists such as C. A. J. Coady and Tyler Burge reject this picture. They hold that testimony is a basic source of entitlement, on a par with perception and memory, so that we are entitled to believe whatever a speaker asserts unless we have a specific reason to distrust her; probing, checking and cross-examination are concessions to an imperfect world rather than part of the entitlement itself.
