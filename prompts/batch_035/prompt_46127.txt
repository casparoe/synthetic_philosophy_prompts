"In 'Freedom and Resentment' (1962), P.F. Strawson argues that the traditional debate about determinism and moral responsibility rests on a mistake. Explain the distinction he draws between reactive attitudes and the objective attitude, explain the role that excuses and exemptions play in his argument, and say why he concludes that the truth or falsity of determinism is not the central issue for our practices of holding responsible."

And the student wrote:

"Strawson sets up the debate between two positions he calls the optimist and the pessimist. The optimist holds that determinism is true and compatible with blame, the pessimist holds that determinism would undermine blame. Strawson ultimately sides with the optimist. His core move is to point at the reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, hurt feelings, indignation, forgiveness — which he says are our natural, non-optional responses to the quality of another person's will toward us. We don't adopt these attitudes on the basis of some metaphysical thesis, so no metaphysical thesis could undermine them.

The alternative to the reactive attitudes is the objective attitude, where you view the person as a thing to be managed, treated, or dealt with strategically rather than as a participant in relationships. Strawson argues we could in principle adopt this attitude toward any given individual, and that if determinism turned out to be true we ought to adopt it universally, since it would be irrational to keep resenting people for what they could not help. He admits this is not realistic in practice, but holds it up as the ideal a rational society should aim at.

Excuses and exemptions both work by cancelling the reactive attitudes, so they're really two names for the same mechanism, with exemptions just being the extreme end of the excuse spectrum. When someone has an excuse — they didn't mean it, they were forced — the reactive attitude is voided because the quality of will isn't there. When someone is exempted — a small child, someone with severe mental illness — same thing, the reactive attitudes get voided. Either way we fall back on the objective attitude.

Since excuses and exemptions only ever apply locally, to particular cases, Strawson concludes that determinism, which would be a global fact about all actions, can't do the work the pessimist wants it to do — it can't function as a blanket excuse, because the reactive attitudes just are our ground-level practice and are not up for revision by argument. So determinism turns out to be compatible with responsibilty, which was Strawson's conclusion all along, and the pessimist is simply refuted."
