I've been rereading Hofstadter's I Am a Strange Loop and got stuck on his careenium thought experiment: a giant billiard table on which zillions of little magnetic balls go careening around, clumping together and breaking apart, and from a zoomed-out view the clumps — he calls them simmballs — form patterns that mirror what's happening outside the table. He asks, roughly, who does the pushing around inside this thing: the little balls, or the simmballs?

Write a dialogue between two people having this argument in front of the table. One thinks the symbol level does genuine causal work — the simmball for an oncoming predator is what caused that flurry of collisions near the table's edge — and the other thinks that's double counting: only the little balls ever push anything, and simmball-talk is just a compressed description for observers too lazy to track individual collisions. Make them equally sharp, and make them actually respond to each other. In particular, have the exclusionist press on whether the symbol-level story ever predicts anything the ball-level story leaves out, and point out that a given simmball is composed of different balls every few seconds, so what kind of cause keeps swapping its parts? Have the other press back on learning: the balls' magnetism drifts over time in whatever direction produces better-tracking clumps, and it's not clear you can say what's being selected for there without mentioning what the clumps are about. They shouldn't resolve it, but by the end they should have located the real crux between them rather than talking past eachother. Start directly with the dialogue — no preface or commentary.
