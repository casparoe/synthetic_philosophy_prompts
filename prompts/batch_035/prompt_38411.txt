Been hanging around the LessWrong-adjacent sphere for like 15 years and I think I've finally settled into a position on the whole "which computations matter morally" question, but something about my package itches and I can't tell whether it's actually inconsistent or just weird. Here it is:

(1) Straight functionalism about consciousness. What makes something conscious is the computation it's running, not the substrate. A silicon system organized the right way is exactly as conscious as a brain, full stop.
(2) Moral status tracks the same thing. No relevant computation, no moral weight; right computation, moral weight. I take this as both necessary and sufficient.
(3) Despite (1) and (2), I'm pretty confident current LLMs are not moral patients. Not agnostic about it either — I'd be genuinely surprised if something in a big transformer's forward pass turned out to matter morally.
(4) If you run the exact same conscious computation twice in parallel, you don't get twice the suffering. Two perfect copies of one pattern is one patient, the same way two printed copies of a novel is one novel. Copies don't add moral weight.
(5) But a giant lookup table that produces byte-for-byte identical responses to a conscious system across its whole life is not conscious and doesn't matter. The right causal process has to actually be executing somewhere; input-output equivalence on its own isn't enough.
(6) A paused emulation — frozen mid-experience, the tape just sitting in storage — still has its moral status. The person still exists. Resuming it in a thousand years is no worse than resuming it tomorrow.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in tension and how badly, ranked by severity instead of a flat list. For each real tension, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost? And if anything only looks like a tension, show me why it dissolves instead of just flagging it. I honestly can't locate the problem — (3) feels like the odd one out to me but I can't put my finger on why, and it's possible that's just vibes. Please don't go easy on it becuase the commitments sound normal; plenty of people on the forums hold versions of these and nobody seems to notice anything off.
