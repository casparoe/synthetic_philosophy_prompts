So I've been on a bit of a Dennett kick lately (real patterns, the three stances, all that), and since I work adjacent to finance I keep wanting to run the GameStop saga of January 2021 through his framework and can't find anyone who's done it properly. In case the details matter: r/wallstreetbets users noticed GameStop's short interest was well over 100% of the float, piled in with the explicit plan of buying and holding to trigger a short squeeze, the stock went from ~$20 to ~$480 intraday, Melvin Capital took massive losses, Robinhood and other retail brokers restricted buying (citing clearinghouse collateral requirements), and there were congressional hearings about the whole thing. People also argue a lot about how much of the actual price move was the traders' convictions versus a mechanical thing: market makers hedging all the call options being bought, i.e. the gamma squeeze.

Here's the mapping I want done, and I want it done carefully rather than hand-wavily. The physical stance on the episode would be order flow, clearinghouse margins, options hedging dynamics; the design stance would be incentive structures -- payment for order flow, the short sellers' risk management, the brokers' exposure to volatility; the intentional stance would be attributing beliefs and desires, whether to individual apes ('diamond hands', the MOASS thesis), to the wallstreetbets collective as some kind of group agent, or even to 'the market' itself. So who or what is the intentional system here? Is 'r/wallstreetbets' a coherent intentional system or just an aggregate of them, and do idioms like 'the market is pricing in X' pick out real patterns in Dennett's sense or are they just a façon de parler?

Then the direction I actually care most about, which is the theory being tested by the case rather than just applied to it. Dennett's criterion for a real pattern is roughly that it earns its keep: the pattern is real if tracking it lets you predict better than you could without it. So did the intentional stance toward the apes genuinely earn predictive keep during the squeeze, or was all the predictive work done by the gamma hedging, a design-stance mechanism that doesn't care what anyone believes? And when Robinhood shut off the buy button and the pattern visibly broke, does that count against the reality of the intentional pattern, or is that just the ordinary case of interfering with an intentional system (like anaesthetizing a patient before predicting their behavior)? I'd also like you to be honest about where the framework is just silent -- e.g. does it have anything to say about whether the squeeze was rational, or about whose interests the stance-talk serves, or is that simply out of its scope? Please dont force the theory to speak where it doesn't.
