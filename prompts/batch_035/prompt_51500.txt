been asked to write up where i stand on moral uncertainty for a discussion group, and i want a consistency check before i circulate it. here's the package:

(1) I'm a moral realist — there are mind-independent moral facts, and we can know at least some of them.

(2) Moral uncertainty should be handled pretty much like ordinary uncertainty about facts: you hold credences over moral views, and you pick the act with the highest expected choiceworthiness, the same way you'd bet on an uncertain empirical question. I think of my moral life as a portfolio.

(3) One exception: side-constraints. Where i've got serious credence that an act would violate someone's rights, I refuse to price the violation against welfare gains at any exchange rate. Constraints don't go on the scale at all.

(4) Case verdicts. I've stopped eating factory-farmed meat even though my credence that it's seriously wrong is only about 60/40 — the 60% felt like enough. And I split my giving between animal welfare charities and global health ones rather than going all-in on whichever cause the theory i most beleive in ranks top.

(5) I think intertheoretic value comparisons are basically hopeless: there's no principled answer to how much welfare gain equals one rights violation, and no principled way to get utilitarian and non-utilitarian value onto a common scale.

One person in the group has already had a go at me: she says (5) definately can't sit with (1), because if the moral facts are all out there mind-independently, there has to be a fact about how much animal suffering equals one human life — so my "hopeless" in (5) is just epistemic humility wearing a metaphysical hat. I'm not sure she's right, but i'm also not sure which of the five, if any, i'd give up if she is.

So: go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look like it. Order the real ones by severity rather than making them all sound equally bad, and for each one say what would have to give and roughly what it costs me. Please also look hard at the case verdicts in (4) — i can't tell whether they're being generated by (2), by (3), or by something i haven't written down.
