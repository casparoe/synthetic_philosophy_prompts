I'm on the board of a small regional land trust, and we've stumbled into a fight I was not at all prepared for. There's a salamander that lives in the hills around our valley, and it wraps around the valley in a long chain of populations. Neighboring populations in the chain interbreed fine, but the two ends of the chain, where it closes up on the south side, can't interbreed with each other anymore — they meet and basically act like separate lineages. So is this thing one species or two?

Our geneticist says one species, obviously, it's a gene flow chain all the way around. Our longtime field guy says the two southern ends look nothing alike, behave differently, use different microhabitats, and any museum taxonomist from fifty years ago would've called them separate species without hesitating. Meanwhile a third board member found a paper saying some biologists now define species as the smallest separately evolving lineage, and by that logic every hilltop population is arguably its own species, which means there could be like nine of them.

Here's what makes this not academic: the population at the southern tip is tiny, and it's the one that's been listed as endangered, which is what unlocked a federal grant and some land protections we've been counting on. If we come out saying "actually it's all one species," the county commisioners will use that to argue the listing was political from the start and a developer is already hinting he'll sue if we "split" them again. So there's real money and real habitat riding on what sounds like a bookkeeping decision.

I keep going back and forth. Is there a fact of the matter here that we're just failing to see — like, the salamanders are what they are and one of these guys is just wrong? Or is "one species or two" the kind of question that genuinely has no answer until you decide what you're asking it for — science, conservation law, taxonomy tradition? And if it's the second thing, what's a responsible way for a bunch of semi-informed trustees to actually make that call in public without it looking like we're just picking whichever answer protects the grant? Help me think this through.
