"Moore's paradox: the sentence 'It's raining, but I don't believe it' is not a contradiction -- both conjuncts can be true at once. Yet the sentence cannot be felicitously asserted. (a) Explain what goes wrong when someone tries to assert it. (b) Explain which versions of the sentence are and are not assertable -- e.g., third-person, past-tense, and disjunctive variants -- and why the restriction matters. (c) Explain what the pragmatic and the semantic explanations of the phenomenon each say, and describe a kind of case where they might come apart."

Here's the student's answer, typed up exactly as written:

"Both conjuncts can be true together, like if it's raining and I'm asleep so I don't believe it. So it's not a contradiction. What goes wrong is at the level of assertion: asserting that p implies that the speaker believes that p. So if I assert 'It's raining, but I don't believe it,' the assertion of the first half contradicts what the second half says. In fact, whenever someone sincerely asserts the whole sentence, the second conjunct has to be false, since sincere assertion requires belief. So the sentence really is a contradiction after all, just not a formal one -- it's self-refuting.

The paradox only shows up in the first person, present tense. 'It was raining but I didn't believe it' is fine, and 'It's raining but John doesn't believe it' is fine too. The disjunctive version 'It's raining, or I don't believe it' is equally unassertable, because the speaker is still committing to p while denying belief in p, just in weaker form.

The pragmatic explanation says the incompatibility comes from what assertion implies about belief -- it's a conversational implicature of the speech act, like how saying 'some students passed' implies not all did. The semantic explanation says the trouble is in the content itself: self-ascriptions of belief are avowals, so the sentence is like asserting p and then taking the assertion back. Since both explanations account for the same data about what can be asserted, the disagreement is basically verbal -- two vocabularies for the same fact about speech acts."
