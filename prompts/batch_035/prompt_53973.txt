So I've been trying to wrap my head around money pump arguments. The way I understand it: if my preferences are intransitive (I prefer A to B, B to C, and also C to A), then a trader can keep cycling me around, charging a small fee at each step, until I'm broke and back where I started. And apparently intransitivity like this is everywhere — ask someone to rank pizza, sushi and salad in different moods and you'll get a cycle no problem. So my question is what the actual fix is. Should I sit down once, build a complete ranking of everything, and then refuse to ever deviate from it no matter how I feel in the moment? That seems like what the argument demands, since any later change to the ranking just opens an new gap that some patient bookie can pump me through. But that can't be right, because obviously I'm *supposed* to change preferences sometimes — when I learn new stuff, or even just get full, updating is rational, not a bug. So how do I tell the difference between a healthy preference update and one that leaves me exploitable? Or is the whole worry kind of overblown — can anyone actually pump me if I can see the trades coming and just decline the last one?
