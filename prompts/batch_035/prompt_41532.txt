A friend of mine went deep into Stoicism about two years ago — Meditations, morning journaling, cold showers, the whole starter pack — and somewhere along the way he turned the dichotomy of control into a running personal project: he sorts basically everything in his life into "up to me" or "not up to me," and then decides what he's even allowed to make a goal out of. (He reads the modern popularizers too, and one of the books he really likes actually splits it into three buckets instead of two — fully yours, partially yours, not yours — but when he talks at dinner it's still mostly two.) His rulings so far, collected over the last year or so: whether he trains consistently — yes, and a legitimate goal. Whether his startup gets acquired — no, and he's officially quit making it a goal, though he keeps doing the pitches and demos like before. Whether he's actually a good tennis player, meaning his real skill level and not any single match result — yes, mostly up to him, and he's set himself a target rating to reach by summer. Whether people at work respect him — no, full stop. Whether he's still angry at his ex two years after the breakup — yes, up to him, and he's journaling on it. Whether he gets sick this winter — he waffled on this one and said "kind of," which was funny given how crisp he is about everything else. Whether his kid grows up honest — yes, that one's on him, he says, no hedging.

So here's my question. There's a new case coming up: he's planning to ask his girlfriend Maya to move in with him, and he's already told me he's going to run it through the same procedure before he does it — is her saying yes "up to him" or not, and what goal is he even permitted to set there. I'm curious what he'll conclude, but honestly I'm less interested in the answer than in the rule he's actually running under the hood. From his track record above, what are the candidate rules that fit everything he's said so far — and it has to be more than one, I want the ones that each fit the whole list, not just most of it. What does each rule predict for the Maya case specifically? Which is your best guess for him, and why? And then, since I suspect a couple of the rules will survive contact with Maya, tell me what further case — beyond the Maya one, ideally something that could genuinely come up in his actual life and not a philosopher's contraption — would do the most work telling those surviving rules apart. He's stubborn about consistency, so if I can bring him one clean case where his own framework forces him to pick a lane, I think he'll actually thank me for it. Or at least argue with me over tacos about it, which is the same thing for him.
