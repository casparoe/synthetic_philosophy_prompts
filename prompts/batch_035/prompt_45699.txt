My friend and I have been going in circles about the procreation asymmetry and I want to see where a fresh brain lands. Quick setup so we're on the same page: the asymmetry says (roughly) that its wrong to create a person whose life will be miserable, but there's nothing wrong with failing to create a person whose life would be great -- whereas standard totalist views have to say both matter symmetrically.

The thing we've slowly realized is that "the asymmetry" is actually at least two separable claims that get bundled together all the time:

(a) the normative one: there's no obligation to create happy people. Procreation stays optional even when the created life would be great.
(b) the axiological one: creating a happy person doesn't make the outcome better in the same way creating a miserable person makes it worse. The value itself is asymmetric, it's not just that some parental prerogative or permission-not-to-benefit overrides a symmetric value.

You can hold (a) while denying (b) if you think creating happy lives is impersonally good but we're permitted not to do impersonal good. My own credence that the full package (a)+(b) is true is maybe 0.65; my friend is at about 0.35.

Give me your number for the full package, but decomposed. I want terms something like: the probability that the core asymmetry intuition survives reflective equilibrium and isn't just an artifact of loss aversion, or of the fact that duties feel like they need an existing patient; the probability that some person-affecting restriction on axiology is even coherent, i.e. that the non-identity problem doesn't sink the whole approach; the probability that (b) can be held without either collapsing back into totalism or generating nasty downstream results like the sadistic conclusion or the conclusion that adding a blissful life makes the world literally no better; and whatever other terms you think belong. Give a rough probability and a one-line justification for each, then tell me how you combine them into your final number.

Then two extra things. First: which term are you least confident in, and what argument or thought experiment would move it the most? Second: given me at 0.65 and her at 0.35, where do you think the disagreement most likely lives -- on (a) vs (b), or on the reliability of the intuition itself? I keep suspecting half the published fight is people talking past each other because nobody bothers to split those two claims apart, so if you think thats wrong, tell me why.
