A friend of mine has been reading Hofstadter — the GEB dialogue where the Anteater is friends with an intelligent ant colony he calls Aunt Hillary — and has decided that ant colonies literally have minds. Fine so far, I guess. But the argument he uses to get there seems to me to prove way too much, and I can't find the exact spot where it slips. Here it is, roughly as he states it:

1. Something has a mind iff the best explanation of its behavior is an intentional one: you attribute beliefs and desires, and predict what it will do by asking what a rational agent with those states would do.
2. For an ant colony, the best explanation of its behavior is at the level of the whole colony. Individual ants are near-automatons following simple rules — no single ant knows where the food is or that the nest needs repairing. But the colony-level pattern is real and predictive: trails get built, the nest gets fixed after storms, wars with the neigboring colony get fought and sometimes won. So the thing to treat as an agent is the colony.
3. By 1 and 2, the colony has a mind: it's the colony that beleives there's food somewhere and wants the nest kept dry.

Then he shrugs and says the same reasoning obviously extends: markets have minds (individual traders are the dumb ants, and the models that predict aggregate behavior describe what "the market" expects); corporations have minds (law already calls them persons, and the employees are just neurons); and at the limit the whole economy is one big Aunt Hillary. He's fine with all of that. I'm not — but every time I try to block the market and the corporation, I seem to take the ant colony down with it, and I actually like the ant colony.

My hunch is the slip is in step 2, which trades on two senses of "best explanation at the colony level": one where the aggregate description is just a compact way of tracking the pattern, and one where it genuinely attributes content. But I can't get that distinction to work, because my own neurons are dumb too, and the person-level description of me is also in some sense just a compact way of tracking a pattern. If I block the market with "compact pattern description isn't mindedness," I block myself.

So give me a definite verdict, not a tour of the options: either pick the step where the argument goes wrong and tell me exactly what rejecting it costs (especially whether it costs me the ant colony), or take his side all the way to markets and corporations having minds and defend that. And if you save the colony, tell me how you do it so that it doesn't immediately re-admit the stock market.
