I've been following the Sleeping Beauty literature and the LessWrong anthropics threads for something like fifteen years, and I've come to the gloomy conclusion that the halfer/thirder debate is exactly as unresolved now as it was when I first encountered it — except that both sides have become considerably more sophisticated in stating the case they were already committed to. I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not a verdict on who's right. I know the arguments; what I don't understand is what kind of disagreement this actually is.

Some structure for you to work with, so the diagnosis can engage the real positions. The halfers and the thirders agree on the coin's bias, on the setup, on every observable prediction, and on what Beauty should do in most practical situations — the divergence shows up when you ask for her credence in HEADS upon awakening. Each side has a canonical argument: the halfer points out that Beauty learns nothing she couldn't have predicted in advance, so her credence should stay where it was on Sunday; the thirder reasons that Beauty's awakening is more probable under TAILS, since TAILS produces two indistinguishable awakenings, so the awakening is evidence shifting weight towards TAILS. Neither argument seems to have moved anyone in decades. And the dispute propagates outward into anthropics, where the analogous split between a Self-Sampling Assumption and a Self-Indication Assumption has similarly hardened: the same reasoning that makes you a halfer apparently makes you sympathetic to the sampling assumption, and vice versa, and the two camps then disagree about the Doomsday Argument in ways that track their Sleeping Beauty commitments almost perfectly. That correlation is itself a datum worth explaining.

Here are the candidate diagnoses I've been entertaining, offered as hypotheses rather than conclusions. First, the verbal dispute: the two sides mean different things by 'the probability of HEADS' — the halfers are answering a question about the experiment, the thirders a question about this particular awakening as a sample, and if you disambiguated the pronouns the disagreement would evaporate. I find this tempting but too quick, because both sides seem to accept that there is a single well-defined question about what Beauty should believe, and the disagreement survives translation into decision-theoretic terms: asked what she'd pay for a ticket that pays out once per experiment, they give one answer; per awakening, another — and they still disagree about which framing captures 'her credence'. Second, the hidden formal question: the debate is really about how to extend ordinary conditioning to self-locating or centred propositions, and the parties have different tacit generalisations of Bayesian updating that both reduce to standard conditioning in the classical cases. Third, the methodological diagnosis: this is a fight about which intuitions count as data — the halfer's intuition that unpredictable 'evidence' isn't evidence, versus the thirder's intuition about how to treat indexical samples — and neither can be argued for from shared ground, in the way that appeals to intuitions generally can't be. Fourth, and most deflationary: the notion of credence at a moment of centred uncertainty is underdetermined by the classical probability axioms, the dispute is a genuine choice between extensions rather than a discovery, and its persistence is a symptom of an ill-posed question rather than a hard one.

What I want from you: adjudicate between these diagnoses (or give me a better one), grounded in what each side actually claims rather than in caricatures. Tell me whether the correlation with the anthropics assumptions — the fact that people's Sleeping Beauty and Doomsday positions travel together — supports any particular diagnosis, since on some diagnoses that correlation should not exist. And then tell me honestly what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a distinction that dissolves the question, a formal result, a decision-theoretic framework that renders the disagreement harmless, or an argument that we should expect this to remain open indefinitely — and if the last, why the smart, motivated people on both sides haven't already converged on the mere bookkeeping. Please write in British English.
