I'm in a small animal-welfare discussion group that meets at a friend's place (a mix of grad students and people who just care about the topic), and I've been asked to write up a short statement of my views on which animals are conscious so the rest of the group can poke holes in it. Before I send it, I want a consistency check, because something about my list feels off to me but I can't put my finger on what, and I'd rather it looked consistant before an audience that includes two people who study animal behavior for a living. Here's roughly where I stand: (1) I'm a physicalist and pretty much a functionalist about mental states — what makes a state a pain rather than just a signal is the causal role it plays (it gets integrated with learning and motivation, drives trade-offs, that sort of thing), not the substrate it runs on or which lineage happened to evolve it. (2) Pain is not nociception — a reflex withdrawal isn't suffering. Real pain needs affect: the state has to matter to the animal, shift its priorities, be sensitive to context. So behavioral evidence, like trade-off tests and motivational modulation, is the relevant evidence for me. (3) Fish clear that bar, in my view — I'm fairly confident fish feel pain, which is partly why I've mostly stoped eating them. (4) Octopuses are conscious, full stop — playful, curious, obviously experiencing the world; no hesitation there. (5) Insects I'm a skeptic about. Even bees — and I know about the experiments where stressed bees seem to read ambiguous cues more pessimistically, plus my cousin keeps bees and swears they have moods — even granting all that, I don't think there's anything it is like to be a bee. It looks like mere cleverness to me. (6) Moral status is grounded in the capacity for valenced experience, not intelligence — a brilliant but non-feeling system wouldn't have moral standing no matter how impressive its problem-solving. (7) When the UK added crabs and lobsters to its animal sentience law a few years back, I thought that was overreach; I don't see crustaceans as meriting protections comparable to vertebrates.

Go through the list and tell me which pairs are actually in tension and how badly, and rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally bad — I suspect some of these only look like they clash, and I'd like to know which. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what that revision costs me elsewhere on the list.

And don't hand me a menu of options and let me pick: take a definite position on which single change would clean up the most, and commit to it with your reasons. Please write in American English — I'm in the US, and the group knows how I talk.
