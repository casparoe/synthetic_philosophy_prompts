I'm putting together a short unit on rational choice for an intermediate game theory course, and I'd like to close it with a poem on the backward induction paradox in the centipede game — the alternating game where two players take turns choosing to pass (growing the pot for both) or take (ending the game with a slightly larger share for the taker). Textbook backward induction says a rational player takes at the very first node, so the long cooperative branch should never be reached. The paradox is that if you actually find yourself at a late node, that fact by itself undermines the common knowledge of rationality the induction was resting on, and a case can be made for passing after all.

Write a villanelle spoken from inside the game — alternating between the two players, or hovering between their voices. One refrain should carry the backward induction conclusion (roughly: take now, before the other does); the other should carry the paradox's rejoinder (roughly: my being here at all proves that reasoning wrong, so pass). I want the form to do genuine philosophical work: the two refrains taking turns should track the alternation of decision nodes, and each return of a refrain should register that another node has been passed and the pot has grown, so that the same line means something slightly different each time it comes back. When the two refrains finally share the closing couplet, that meeting should be the crux of the paradox: the argument for taking and the argument that the argument never applied, both live in the same breath. Someone who knows the backward induction paradox should be able to tell which refrain is which and follow what each return costs; someone who doesn't should simply find it a good poem about two people testing each other while a prize grows between them. Please keep it roughly iambic, keep jargon to a minimum (mentioning common knowledge at most once, and only if it earns its place), and don't resolve the debate in the final stanza — the poem should end with the game still running, not with a verdict.
