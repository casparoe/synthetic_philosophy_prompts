My friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about van Fraassen's Reflection principle, and we ended at an impasse. She defended Reflection in full strength; I left with a list of things I believe that I now cannot see clearly through, because several of them were things I said in the argument without noticing how they sit next to each other. I've written them out as numbered commitments below, and I would like an audit of whether they can all be true of one rational person. Please don't turn it into a survey of the literature; neither of us was citing anything by the end, and I want the reasoning done on the commitments as stated.

My commitments:

(1) Reflection, full strength: if I am now certain that at some later time t my future self will assign credence p to proposition A, then my current credence in A, conditional on that fact, should equal p — regardless of how I came to be certain of it, and regardless of why my future self will assign p.

(2) Evidentialism about credences: a credence is rational only if it is proportioned to the evidence the subject currently has; and my own future beliefs are not, just as such, evidence for me — a belief of mine is not itself a sign that its content is likely, however confident the believer.

(3) The hangover verdict: suppose I know that tomorrow morning, hungover and inflamed, I will be 90% confident that last night's party was a disaster, though by tomorrow I will have no evidence beyond what I have tonight. Tonight, sober, I think it would be irrational for me to move my conditional credence toward 0.9; declining to defer to my future self's known-bad judgment is the rational thing to do.

(4) No pragmatic vindication of epistemic norms: betting vulnerabilities, Dutch books, and other practical penalties can never by themselves make a credence irrational; if incoherent credences cost you money, that is a misfortune, not an epistemic failing.

(5) No diachronic requirements: every rational requirement on credences concerns only credences held at a single time; nothing about how my credences at different times relate to one another is, by itself, a requirement of rationality.

Please go through all ten pairs of commitments. For each, say whether there is a genuine tension, an apparent tension that dissolves on inspection, or no tension at all. Rank the genuine tensions by severity, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be and what that fix costs me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. My own hunch going in was that (5) is the odd one out, since Reflection sounds diachronic — but my friend insisted the real problem was somewhere else entirely, and I honestly cannot tell. Do not let my hunch steer you; I have been wrong before about which of my own beliefs is doing the damage.

Format: please return the audit as a single JSON object with no prose outside it, using exactly these fields:

- "pairs": an array of ten objects, one per pair, in lexicographic order — (1,2), (1,3), …, (4,5). Each object contains: "pair" (a string such as "(1,3)"); "verdict" (one of "genuine", "apparent", "none"); "severity" (an integer from 1 to 5, where 5 means the two commitments cannot both stand and 1 means mild friction; null unless the verdict is "genuine"); "diagnosis" (two to four sentences explaining the tension or its dissolution); "cheapest_fix" (one sentence naming which commitment to revise and how; null unless the verdict is "genuine"); "fix_cost" (one sentence on what accepting that fix costs me elsewhere in the set; null unless the verdict is "genuine").
- "ranking": an array of the "pair" strings for the genuine tensions, ordered from most to least severe.
- "single_best_revision": one sentence.
- "what_would_change_my_mind": two or three sentences naming the kind of consideration or argument that would lead you to a different ranking or a different single best revision.
- "where_this_audit_could_be_wrong": two or three sentences on the point at which your own reasoning here is most likely to fail — for instance, a dissolution that works only under a reading of a commitment that I did not intend.
