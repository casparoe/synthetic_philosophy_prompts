My friend Greta is on a one-woman crusade to sort everything into "actually beautiful" versus "just popular," and she's gotten weirdly systematic about it. Her verdicts so far: Bach's cello suites, actually beautiful. A country song that reliably makes everyone cry: moving, she admits, but not beautiful — engineered to yank your feelings around. The big painting of a teary-eyed clown in her grandmother's hallway: not beautiful, kitsch, even though grandma has loved it for forty years. A Noh play she initially found boring and impenetrable: actually beautiful, once she'd learned enough to see what was going on in it. A photograph of a mountain lake, pleasant enough to look at: neither — just wallpaper. And a current pop song that's absurdly catchy and that she loved for about three weeks: not beautiful, it's already fading on her, and she says that's the tell.

Next month we're going to an exhibition she hasn't seen, and I want to predict her calls in advance so I can gloat if I'm right. The pieces on the program: (1) a six-year-old avant-garde composition that every serious critic has declared a masterpeice but that general audiences can't stand; (2) a sentimental poem from the 1880s that was adored for over a century, still turns up in old anthologies, but that present-day critics call mawkish; (3) an image generated by an AI system that a panel of expert judges unanimously rated as beautiful.

What do you think she'll say about each, and what rule is she actually running — something like Hume's idea that the standard of taste is whatever qualified judges converge on after enough practice and comparison, a survive-the-test-of-time criterion, or something else? I suspect her track record fits more than one of those equally well, so please be honest about where her past verdicts underdetermine the new ones. And if I could add one more piece to the show specifically to force the candidate rules apart, what should it be? I care less about the three verdicts themselves than about nailing the rule she's implicitly using.
