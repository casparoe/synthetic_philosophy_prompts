I've been obsessed lately with this thing I read about the digger wasp (I think it comes up in one of those Hofstadter books, I don't fully remember). The wasp drags a cricket to her nest, leaves it outside the entrance, goes in to check the nest, comes back out, and drags the cricket in. But if you move the cricket a few inches while she's inside checking, she comes out, finds the cricket, and instead of just pulling it to the hole, she drags it back to the threshold again and goes inside to re-check the whole nest. Do that forty times, and she re-checks forty times. People apparently call this being "sphexish" — mechanically executing a routine that only looks smart from the outside. Fine, wasps are little robots, whatever. But then I caught myself standing in my kitchen at midnight checking that the stove was off for the third time, and driving to my old apartment out of pure autopilot, and it hit me that I can't actually say what separates my "flexible" behavior from hers, except a matter of degree. And now I'm worried that people who talk about free will are really just claiming to be unsphexish without a definition of what that would even take.

So here's what I want from you, and I'd like you to actually think like a professional philosopher of action and brainstorm a bit before committing to anything. Instead of giving me yet another rundown of compatibilism vs. determinism, write me the spec sheet: the list of things any adequate theory of free will (or free agency, whatever we should call it) has to get right if the sphex example is going to be the central test case. Like — must it draw a real line between the wasp and me, or is a continuum answer actually acceptable, and why? Does it have to say something about where habits, addiction, and autopilot driving fall, since those are exactly the cases where I feel most sphexish? Does it owe us an account of what "flexibility" even amounts to, given that a fancier robot with if-statements also looks flexible? I've also been chewing on a suspicion that total antisphexishness is impossible — a creature that re-derives everything from scratch each time would be paralyzed, so some rigidity is a feature, not a bug — so I want you to tell me if some of the desiderata people naturally demand (say, "flexible in all circumstances" plus "still a coherent person over time") are jointly unachievable, and flag which requirements are real and which are just vibes from one camp of the debate.

To be clear about the deliverable: the requirements list is the point, each one with the case or intuition that forces it, and a note on which are negotiable. Then, briefly and honestly, tell me which single requirement on your list does the most work to eliminate the standard free will positions (the compatibilist, the hard determinist, the whatever-you-call-the-agent-causation people), and whether any of them survive your own list. I'm going to use this to interrogate my own habits, and maybe finally stop checking the stove, so don't sand off the interesting edges.
