heres my opening move. a hospital has one treatment slot left in its program. one patient will be paralyzed from the neck down unless she gets it. alternatively, the same slot can save a thousand patients from paralysis from the waist down — a harm that is, per person, only a shade milder than hers. now i happen to know that scanlon himself, and presumably you, allow numbers to break ties: if all thousand and one complaints were exactly equal in strength, you'd save the thousand without blinking. but that means the moment the thousand's individual harm is a hair smaller than the one's, you are forced to save the one person. a thousand people each with a harm 99% as bad as hers lose to her 100%. crank it to a million, same verdict. crank the milder harm up to 99.99% as bad, still hers. i say a principle with that cliff edge in it isn't a principle of what we owe to each other, it's a bug, and the fact that it flips entirely on whether a harm is exactly tied or minutely smaller shows the comparisons were never really doing the work. also, be honest about what the one could reasonably reject in my case: is "i lose slightly more than each of them" really a complaint that a thousand people each facing nearly the same thing are barred from making together? your move.
