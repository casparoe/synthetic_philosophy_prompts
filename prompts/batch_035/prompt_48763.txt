I'm writing a blog post about which of two neighbouring countries is "more liberal" -- my case is Sweden vs Norway, but I think the argument generalizes (Canada vs the US, Austria vs Germany, etc.). Before I publish I'd like your comments, mainly on the argument structure but also on accuracy since I'm writing from memory and I'm sure some of the details are wrong. Here's the draft, its rough:

---

Everybody just "knows" Sweden is the liberal one and Norway is the more conservative, church-y, oil-rich cousin. But I don't think that survives contact with actual comparison, and the interesting question is what we'd even be measuring.

First problem: "liberal" means at least three things that don't travel together. Economic liberalism (markets, low barriers to entry, easy to start a business), social permissiveness (you can live how you want, even stupidly), and institutional liberalism (rule of law, tolerance of dissent, weak connection between state and dominant culture). A country can score high on one and low on the others, and Sweden/Norway do exactly that.

My proposed test: a veil of ignorance ranking. Instead of asking which country has the most liberal laws on the books, ask: if you didn't know who you'd be, would you rather be born a random citizen there? Suppose you might be born poor, gay, a religious minority, an immigrant, a smoker, someone with an unorthodox name for your kid, someone who wants to open a corner shop. Count the identity slots where one country lets you live more as you please than the other, and weight by how big the difference is.

Once you run the test some of the standard facts reverse or get complicated. Sweden's famous social liberalism coexisted with a forced sterilization program that ran into the 70s. Norway's supposedly staid alcohol monopoly seems less liberal than Sweden's almost identical alcohol monopoly, except Norwegians just buy their booze abroad, so the law's bite is lower than it looks? Meanwhile on sex work Sweden banned buying sex and Norway copied it, on abortion I believe Sweden's actually the more permissive one, and on the economic side Sweden went much further with privatizing schools and pharmacies and pensions while Norway kept big state ownership stakes everywhere, funded by oil. So Norway looks more social liberal on some margins and less on others, and more economically statist but arguably less paternalistic toward individuals? I honestly don't know which way it nets out, and the draft currently just ends there, which I suspect is a cop out.

There's also a version of the question where nothing would settle it, because "more liberal" is a verbal dispute -- we'd be arguing about which of the three senses gets the word rather than about the countries. I gesture at this in the intro but I'm not sure I believe it, since we do seem able to rank countries within one sense without much trouble.

For comparison I have a paragraph about Canada vs the US: same confusion but sharper, because the US is more economically liberal (in the European sense) and less socially permissive, so everyone's answer to "which is more liberal" just reveals which sense they had in mind. Not sure if that paragraph earns its place or if it's a distraction.

---

(4) keep or cut the Canada/US paragraph? And if you think the ending is a cop out, what would you do with it instead -- I'd rather not fake a conclusion I don't have.
