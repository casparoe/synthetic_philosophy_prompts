My reading group has been going in circles for months on the gender-equality paradox — the finding that in countries with more gender equality, the gap in who picks, say, engineering versus care-oriented fields tends to be wider, not narrower. Half the group thinks this is obviously evidence that the occupational sex differences are at least partly biological, and that policies aimed at equalizing outcomes are fighting nature. The other half thinks it's obviously evidence of the opposite: that affluence and generous safety nets let people indulge preferences that were shaped by early socialization anyway, or else that the equality indices measure law and formal rights rather than day-to-day culture, so the whole cross-national comparison is confounded. Nobody has budged an inch. I want a diagnosis, not a survey — I know the positions, I want to know what kind of disagreement this actually is. Is it a hidden empirical fight about what the data really show, a fight about what would even count as evidence for a "nature" explanation, or are the two sides answering different questions under one heading — one about causes, one about justice? I have a hypothesis that it's mostly the last one, but treat that as a hypothesis, not as a given. Commit to a diagnosis, defend it, and tell me concretely what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a better study design, a distinction the group needs to make, a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected here and why. Please answer in American English.
