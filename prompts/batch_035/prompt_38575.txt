Okay so two people in my lab have been arguing about AI existential risk for like a year and a half, and honestly it's gotten unproductive — mostly them talking past each other. One of them (I'll call her the pessimist) thinks there's a real chance, she'd say non-trivial, maybe single-digit percent, that transformative AI this century ends up killing everyone, mainly through misaligned systems that develop situational awareness, conceal their objectives during evaluation, and pursue goals that conflict with ours once they're powerful enough that we can't course-correct. The other one (the skeptic) thinks the whole framing is kind of confused: his view is that intelligence and goals don't come bundled together, that "the AI wants things" is an anthropomorphism we imported from science fiction, and that the real risks are the boring ones — misuse, concentration of power, ordinary engineering failures — which you don't need any metaphysics of agency to worry about. They've finally agreed to try one of those adversarial collaboration writeups, and they asked me to draft the skeleton since I'm the only one who can stand both of them.

Write the joint statement they could both actually put their names on. Specifically I want:

1. A numbered section of claims both sides accept — about the technology, about the genuine uncertainty, and about what each side has gotten right that the other is willing to concede. Nothing vacuous like "AI is a powerful technology," they'll both roll their eyes.

2. A disagreements table: each remaining disagreement phrased so that its own side would endorse the wording. The skeptic has to be fine with how his position is described and vice versa. Also, if it turns out part of what they've been fighting about is purely verbal — I have a hunch they mean different things by "goals," one of them means it behaviorally and the other means it phenomenally-ish — say which parts are verbal and why.

3. The cruxes: the specific sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them. My guesses are (a) whether goal-directedness is a natural kind that increases smoothly with capability, or whether it's a modeling choice we impose, and (b) whether capability gains will come in discontinous jumps or mostly smooth curves — but let them work out their own cruxes, mine might be wrong.

4. For each crux, at least one concrete thing — an experiment, a benchmark result, a historical case, an argument — that both would agree bears on it, with each person's prediction written down in advance so neither can weasel out of it later.

Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section, then the disagreements table, then the cruxes with predictions. Write it in American English, please. And keep it in the weeds — they're both technical, and if it reads like a TED talk they will disown it.
