My friends Dalia and Marcus have been together for six years and are deciding whether to move from Chicago to Boston for a job offer that would transform Marcus's career. I was at the dinner where the argument came to a head, and I couldn't referee it. The exchange went roughly like this. (1) Marcus argued the move is clearly right: the gain to him would be enormous, while Dalia's objections — leaving her support network and a city she loves — are real but comparatively minor costs. (2) Dalia replied that this assumes a single scale on which his gain and her loss can be weighed against each other, and no such scale exists: interpersonal comparisons of utility are either meaningless or unanswerable, so Marcus's arithmetic has no subject matter. (3) Marcus said the impossibility claim proves too much: every joint decision — whose family gets the holidays, who endures the chore neither wants — implicitly weighs one person's utility against another's, and a couple that refuses such comparisons cannot make any decisions as a couple. (4) Dalia granted that rough comparisons are unavoidable in practice but said that doesn't make them true; at best the couple can negotiate, and negotiation outcomes reflect bargaining power and eloquence rather than any fact about whose welfare is at stake. (5) Marcus then proposed a test: he would consider trading the move against various hypothetical losses to himself, and Dalia could do the same on her side, so that each of them ends up with a ranking of outcomes — and if the rankings line up in a certain way, there might be a principled comparison after all. Dalia said this just relabels the problem, since each person's answers depend on how they frame the questions, and the two rankings never share a unit. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: which of the five moves are right, which are stronger than they look, which are weaker, and what, if anything, both are missing. I'd especially like to know whether Dalia's position in (2) is compatible with her concession in (4), and whether Marcus's test in (5) is actually getting at anything real. Please answer in American English.
