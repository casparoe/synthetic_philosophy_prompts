After two years in a cognitive science program I have to choose a dissertation area, and I am drawn to evolutionary psychology — but I keep hearing that its hypotheses are just-so stories, unfalsifiable by construction, and I have not found a statement of what such a hypothesis would have to achieve to answer the charge rather than gesture at it. Before I commit years to this, I want the specification written out.

Please give me the desiderata a hypothesis in this field must meet to count as scientific work rather than plausible narrative: for each requirement, the case or failure mode behind it, stated so I could apply it to a paper. Among the requirements I suspect belong on the list, and would like you to evaluate rather than assume: that the hypothesis state in advance what would count against it, in a field where a byproduct or exaptation explanation can be manufactured after any finding; that it constrain the ancestral environment independently of the behavior it is invoked to explain, so that the environment of evolutionary adaptedness does not function as a free parameter adjusted to fit whatever data arrives; that it predict effect sizes or orderings across conditions, not merely directions, since a directional prediction that folk intuition already endorses confirms nothing; that it keep apart what a trait was selected for from what the trait now does, so the same claim is not used to explain a behavior and to excuse one; and that it stay consistent with what quantitative genetics implies about how quickly selection could have acted on the relevant variation, given the timescales usually invoked.

Mark which desiderata you take to be non-negotiable if the field is to be empirical at all, and which are prejudices of one camp dressed up as standards — my impression is that the long-running fight over adaptationism is partly a fight of this kind, and I want that sorted out rather than assumed. Then tell me whether any subset is jointly unsatisfiable in practice; in particular, I want your view on whether demanding surprising, counterintuitive predictions and demanding that every mechanism be anchored in the ancestral environment pull against each other. Close with a brief paragraph on how the familiar research programs fare against the list — the massive-modularity program, costly-signaling accounts of cooperation and display, life-history-strategy approaches — but keep it short: the criteria are what I need, and if the list is good I will do the scoring myself.
