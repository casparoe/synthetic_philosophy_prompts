I have no academic background in this; it's just something I've turned over on my own for a couple of years, and I'd like you to commit to a number rather than give me a survey.

Some time ago I read about a strange clinical condition: people who, after certain kinds of brain injury, or sometimes under certain drugs, report that they still feel pain — they can locate it, describe it as sharp or throbbing, even rate its intensity — but it doesn't bother them. They don't flinch; some apparently smile while being pinched or pricked. The condition is called pain asymbolia. Ever since, I've been troubled by what it seems to show. My natural view was always that pain is bad simply because of how it feels — that the awfulness of pain just is the awfulness of the sensation. But if someone can have the feeling while the badness seems absent, then either the feeling was never the whole story, or the asymbolics are misdescribing their own experience in some way I can't easily specify.

So here is the proposition I want your credence on: for ordinary physical pain, its badness consists entirely in its unpleasant phenomenal character — that is, if pain felt exactly the same but had no pull on attention, motivation, or behavior, it would be exactly as bad as it actually is.

Please give me a number and a decomposition. Some terms I'd expect to see, though you can revise the list: the probability that asymbolia involves full-strength pain sensation rather than a diluted version of it; the probability that unpleasantness is a separable ingredient that can come apart from the raw sensory feel, so that the asymbolics have lost only the first; the probability that part of what makes pain bad isn't an experience at all but something like its demanding character — the way it insists you act, which could in principle come apart from how it feels; and any further terms you think belong. Assign each a rough probability with a one-line justification, then show me how they combine into your final credence.

For reference, my own credence sits around 0.6, which annoys me, because I can't tell whether I'm holding back from 1 out of genuine doubt or just because the asymbolia cases are vivid and I'm being bullied by them.
