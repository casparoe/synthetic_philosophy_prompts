i'm a third-year soc phd student (dissertation on occupational segregation, fun times) and i run a little reading group for grad students from soc, econ and philosophy. after yet another fight about the gender-equality paradox — the thing where richer/more egalitarian countries often show MORE gendered occupational choices, not less — i've realized everyone in the room means something slightly different by their answer, and half the time people who claim to "agree" actually disagree once you push on a case. so i want to make a quiz: "which position on the gender-equality paradox do you actually hold?"

here's what i want, roughly modeled on those personality quizzes where the questions are cases and the scoring key maps you to a type:

- 8 to 10 questions, each one a concrete case, hypothetical dataset, or thought experiment with two or three answer options. no question can just ask "do you think the differences are nature or nurture" — that's the label, not the diagnostic. like, one question could be a fictional pair of countries where you give the numbers and ask which explanation people would bet on; another could be "here's a finding that came out next year that would genuinely move you — pick which one"; another could be a case where women in the most egalitarian country you can imagine still freely choose care-heavy occupations and you ask what follows.
- i want the "why" axis and the "so what" axis crossed. this is the part i care most about and it's why i'm not just copying an existing quiz if one exists. someone can think the pattern is mostly canalized/biological in origin AND think it's an injustice requiring policy; someone else can think it's 100% socialization and think it's basically fine and nothing should be done. a quiz that only sorts on causes is missing the actual fights in my reading group. so the scoring key should place you on both dimensions, or at least flag when your answers only pin down one of them.
- the scoring key needs to map answer patterns onto at least: some kind of biological/canalization view, the preference-satisfaction liberal view (free choice = fine, whatever produced it), the socialization/adaptive-preference view, the structural-constraint view, and the measurement-skeptic position that the "paradox" itself is shaky — that it depends on how you measure "more gendered" when the occupation mix differs across countries, and that it might not survive that. if you think there's a distinct justice-critique position (the pattern is real and even reflects preferences, but it's still bad — think about what happens to pay or to who does the care work downstream) that should be seperable too, or you should tell me it can't be.
- flag honestly which pairs of positions the quiz cannot pull apart because they give the same answers on every question you could realistically write (i suspect canalization and preference-satisfaction liberalism overlap a LOT and i'd rather know than pretend), and note any answer-patterns that no real position occupies — then say whether that's because the combo is incoherent or just unpopular.
- audience is grad students who are smart but haven't read the specific literature, so nothing in the questions should presuppose any particular paper or author. keep it answerable from the case descriptions alone.

format: the quiz first, then the scoring key, then the caveats section about indistinguishable positions and empty cells. don't worry about making it rigorous enough to publish, it's for a reading group that occassionally gets too heated — i want people to find out they were arguing past each other.
