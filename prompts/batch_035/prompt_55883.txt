Her verdicts so far: an ordinary dream told on waking is not an experience that happened during sleep -- nothing was watched and then remembered, the telling is where the dream gets made; a lucid dream where the dreamer sends prearranged eye-movement signals is a genuine experience happening in real time during sleep; a guy who narrates whole scenes aloud while asleep (verified on tape) but remembers nothing in the morning -- no dream occured at all, the narration is subpersonal output; and when a sleeper was woken mid-REM, said "I was on a beach," went back to sleep, and gave a much richer version in the morning, she said the morning version is no better a record of anything, they're both just tellings. Predict her verdict on each, tell me what rule she's actually running (I can see at least two that fit her record just as well), and what further case I'd need to bring to split those rules apart.
