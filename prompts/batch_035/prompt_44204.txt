I write a series of novels set in a northern mill town, and my recurring character Denise — retired jobcentre advisor, the one readers either love or write to me about saying she's "a bit much" — has become the moral barometre of the books, at least according to my editor. She's always judging people and readers track it. I'm plotting the next one and I need her to rule on two new cases, and I'm scared of getting her wrong after nine years of being consistent, so help me out here.

Her track record so far, spread over four books: she quietly admires the single mum who does cash-in-hand cleaning on the side of her claim (calls it survival, not fraud, and gets shirty when anyone calls it fraud); she despises the able-bodied bloke who's turned down every scheme going and boasts about playing the system; she has zero time for the man caught exaggerating his back pain for disability; she goes to the local paper on behalf of a woman sanctioned for missing an apointment because her kid was in hospital; and she has genuine respect for the woman who gave up paid work to nurse her dying mother on carer's allowance. Oh, and when a wealthy landlord's tax avoidance comes up she just shrugs — not her department, apparently.

The new cases. Case one: a claimant in his late twenties, perfectly able to work, who spends every waking hour training to swim the Channel — genuinely disciplined, up at 5am, eats like a monk — but it's all funded by benefits and he hasn't applied for a job in three years. Case two: a lad who skips his appointments out of pure laziness, gets a sanction that's obviously disproportionate, loses his flat over it, and washes up on Denise's doorstep.

What I want from you, in British English, structured like this:

'Her two verdicts' — about 200 words total on what she'd actually say or do in each case; use her voice if you like.
'The candidate rules' — the two or three rules she might be running that fit everything in her track record, a short paragraph each, saying what each one predicts for the two new cases (I can see at least two rules that fit so far, there are probably more).
'My best guess' — which rule Denise is really on and why, and be honest about the wiggle room.
'The settling case' — the further case I should save for the book after this one that would actually split those rules apart, and what each rule predicts she'd do with it.

Don't be precious about it, but I do want the reasoning visible — I can revise Denise's dialogue, but I can't revise a principle I never knew she had.
