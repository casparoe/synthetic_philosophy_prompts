so i'm a phd student in philosophy of science and i help run a group blog for our department's grad students — mostly weird probability and decision theory stuff, the kind of thing only other grad students and a suspicious number of rationalist-adjacent strangers read. i want to do a "which anthropic reasoner are you actually?" quiz about the reference class problem, i.e. the thing where you try to estimate something from the fact that you exist and were "sampled" into some class of observers, and your estimate swings wildly depending on which class you count yourself as part of (all humans ever? all mammals? all observers anywhere in the multiverse?).

the quiz should be eight-ish questions, each a concrete case the reader answers *before* knowing any of the jargon. definitely include: a version of the doomsday argument (reader is told their birth rank among all humans who will ever live and asked what, if anything, that does to their estimate of the total number of humans there will ever be), a sleeping-beauty-flavored case, and something on fine-tuning / how many universe-bubbles you'd need in a multiverse before observers like you show up. but also one case i made up for the blog: "the lonely seminar" — you get an email saying you've been randomly assigned to one of n parallel seminar sections, each with a different enrollment, and when you walk in you count the other students in the room and have to say what that tells you about how big the sections were likely to be. work out the case so it actually discriminates between positions, or tell me honestly that it doesn't and propose a replacement that does.

the scoring key should map answer patterns onto leaves something like: self-sampling with a fixed reference class, self-indication, the view that indexical evidence of this kind just doesn't count / you should reason as if a randomly chosen observer from the relevant class, and the "pragmatic, there's no fact of the matter about the reference class" view if you think that's genuinely a separate leaf rather than a cop-out. flag any pair of leaves the quiz can't really separate, and for any combination of answers that nobody in the literature plausibly holds, say briefly whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. no questions of the form "do you accept self-sampling?" — the whole point is sorting people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like. keep the register casual enough for a blog but not sloppy, and keep each case short enough that a reader answering eight of them doesn't bail halfway through.
