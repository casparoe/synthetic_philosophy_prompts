I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, but I've ended up with a cluster of views about possible worlds, and before I keep building on them I'd like a consistency audit. Some background on how I got here: I work on formal verification, where we routinely reason about "all possible executions" of a program — a deterministic program given fixed inputs has exactly one execution, and yet the model checker explores a whole tree of counterfactual traces. That practice is what pulled me into this. Here are the commitments:

(1) I take modal realism seriously as literally true: all possible worlds are concrete and equally real, and "actual" just means "the one we happen to be in" — nothing objectively marks our world out. This initially appealed to me because it makes counterfactuals in my work ("this deadlock wouldn't have occurred under the other scheduling order") come out as straightforward claims about concretely existing worlds rather than some fuzzy fiction.

(2) I am, in general, a strict Occam's razor person: I reject posits beyond necessity, and an infinity of concrete unobservable universes strikes me as an enormous posit.

(3) I'm also sympathetic to the stronger view that every sufficiently coherent mathematical structure exists in the same sense that physical things do — our universe, being apparently governed by laws we can write down, looks like just one structure among them.

(4) I believe I will die and that my death will be bad for me — a genuine loss, not something softened by the thought that counterpart versions of me thrive elsewhere in logical space.

(5) I believe none of this should make any difference to how I act: my choices only shape this world, so rational decision-making can proceed as though modal realism were false.

Please tell me which of these commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and — for the real tensions — which revision would be cheapest and what it would cost me. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all equally, and be specific about which one has to go.
