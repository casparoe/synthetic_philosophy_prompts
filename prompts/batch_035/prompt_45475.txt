I'm a retired condensed-matter physicist — infinities were a daily nuisance in my working life, and we always read them as a sign that the theory had left its domain of validity, never as news about the world. Since retiring I've gotten into philosophy, and I keep bumping into Hilbert's hotel (the one where every room is occupied yet a new guest, or even infinitely many new guests, can still be accomodated) being used as a reductio: actual infinities are absurd, therefore the physical world can't contain one — no infinite past, no actually infinite collection of anything. My instinct from physics is that this trades on cardinality arithmetic doing work it can't do for physical quantities — shuffling guests around leaves the cardinality fixed but tells you nothing about density, energy, or measure. But maybe I'm the one missing something. Could you audit the analogy for me: what exactly in the physical world the hotel is meant to model, what maps onto what, which features quietly don't carry over (and which of those disanalogies actually matter for the finitist conclusion rather than just being pedantic), and whether the no-actual-infinities conclusion survives once the mapping is laid out honestly? And if the hotel does fail as a reductio, is there a better route to finitism that doesn't rest on this bit of hospitality management, or should I stop taking the position seriously altogether?
