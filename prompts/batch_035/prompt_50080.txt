My philosophy of science reading group has been going in circles for months on the interpretations of probability, and I've started to suspect the fight isn't really about probability at all. Heres where we're stuck: everyone in the room accepts the Kolmogorov axioms and agrees on the numbers whenever the case is repeatable, so all the heat is over single-case assignments like "the chance this particular uranium atom decays within the next hour is 1/2." The propensity people say that's a real physical disposition of the atom, and they have a cute point that propensities don't behave like probabilities when you try to invert them -- there's a propensity for a struck match to light, but no sensible propensity running backwards from it lighting to its having been struck. The subjectivists reply that a fully informed agent has nothing left to be uncertain about: on their view a God's-eye agent makes no probability statements at all, and they seem fine with that, whereas the propensity folks think it's just obvious the atom's chance is still 1/2 even if God knows how it ends. My working hypothesis: the two camps are answering different questions under one word -- one side is saying what an agent with partial information should believe, the other is asking what physical structure in the world makes those beliefs track something. Is that right, or is there a genuine hidden metaphysical question here (are single-case dispositions actually out there)? And honestly, would anything count as progress -- a distinction, a result, whatever -- or should we just partition the question and move on?
