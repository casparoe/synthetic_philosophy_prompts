I'm preparing a seminar unit on Scanlon's contractualism, and the unit I dread most is the one on the aggregation problem, because I find that discussions of it slide into evaluating proposals before anyone has said what a proposal would have to achieve. Before we look at any particular fix, I want a clean specification of adequacy. Please write the desiderata a solution to the aggregation problem for contractualism must meet. By "solution" I mean something that tells me, in a principled way, when numbers of claimants count in the strength of a complaint and when they don't — and the spec should be stated so that a wide range of candidate solutions could be scored against it later. Each requirement should come with the case or consideration behind it. At minimum the spec should be tested against the following: a rescue case where two groups face equal, serious threats and only one can be saved, where most people think the larger group's claim is somehow stronger than any single claim in the smaller group; a trade-off case where one person stands to suffer a harm much worse than anything threatened to each of a great many others (say, a death versus very many mild headaches), where almost no one thinks the many minor complaints add up; the awkward intermediate case where the harms are of the same kind and severity but differ in number, e.g. one person losing the use of both legs versus two people each losing the use of one; and a risk case where many people each face a small probability of a serious harm, since a solution that only handles certainties will not touch the cases that matter for policy. The spec should also say which distinctions a solution must respect (such as the difference between a claim's strength and the number of people holding it, and between aggregate welfare rising and anyone having a stronger complaint) and which of the requirements are non-negotiable because they follow from contractualism's core motivation — the idea that a principle must be justifiable to each person, with no one's interests simply summed with anyone else's — versus which are really preferences of one camp within the contractualist family. If you suspect some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, say so and explain why; that is the part I most want to see, since it may show that the aggregation problem is not a bug to be patched but a structural constraint on the whole contractualist approach.

Two more things, so the spec is actually usable. First, a colleague has been pushing a rule she calls "pairwise sufficiency": a complaint gets its full strength only when it would have been enough to defeat the principle in a one-on-one comparison against the strongest opposing individual claim, and complaints that fail that pairwise test can never be pooled. I don't want the spec to rule this in or out by fiat, but I do want it to be sharp enough that we could tell, in a seminar session, whether pairwise sufficiency counts as a solution to the aggregation problem or merely restates the individualist restriction in different words. Second, please don't include a bibliography or reading list; where you need to gesture at the literature, describe the position or the case rather than citing, since I'll supply the readings myself.

As to format: give the requirements as a numbered list, each with its motivating case and a one-line note on whether it is non-negotiable or camp-relative; then a short section on joint satisfiability; then, on the very last line, a machine-readable verdict in the form \answer{...} naming, in a few words, the single requirement on your list that does the most to exclude candidate solutions.
