I've been rereading the Ant Fugue dialogue from Gödel, Escher, Bach, where the anteater insists the ant colony Aunt Hillary is a genuine mind — she has beliefs, she can even be amused by the scientists poking at her — even though no individual ant knows anything at all. The individual ants are just dumb signal-relayers, and the "symbols" are supposed to live in patterns of signal traffic at the colony level. I want your credence that something like this is literally true of actual ant colonies on Earth: that a colony instantiates beliefs or experiences, not metaphorically but the way you and I do (on at least some defensible theory of mind). My gut says something like 0.05, but I honestly can't tell if that's a reasoned number or just species-level vanity, so give me yours.

Decompose it. I'd expect terms at least like: P(that a functionalist or computationalist theory of mind is right, against biological-substrate views and dualist ones), P(given functionalism, that the relevant causal organization can be realized at colony level rather than only inside one nervous system — i.e., that multiple realization stretches this far, and that the spatial spread and slow timescale of chemical and tactile signaling between ants isn't disqualifying), P(that real ant colonies have the right kind of organization — stable recurrent colony-level dynamics, integration across the nest, not just aggregate stigmergic bookkeeping — whatever you take the load-bearing condition to be), and anything else that belongs, including whether the question is even well-posed enough to have a number. Give me a probability and one line of justification for each term, then combine them.

To make the middle term concrete: Temnothorax colonies of a few hundred ants choose between nest sites by quorum — scouts that find a better site recruit nestmates harder, and the colony commits once a threshold number has visited the new site, with the whole search narrowing as if the colony were evaluating options. Is that enough colony-level computation for the functionalist term, or is it a fancy thermostat? And here's a test I keep chewing on: suppose we could dose individual ants with something that raises their response thresholds, so every ant is slightly "dumber," and what we observe is that the colony-level decision dynamics slow down and get erratic — the quorum takes longer, the colony sometimes splits. Would you call that sedating the colony, the way a drug sedates a person, and would that pattern of effects count as evidence that the colony is the right unit of mind? Or does the test smuggle in the conclusion? Tell me straight if it begs the question.
