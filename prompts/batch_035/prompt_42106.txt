My friend Elena went through a big evolutionary psychology phase last year, and it's turned her into a walking verdict machine about which of our beliefs get "debunked" by their own origin story. Her record so far: she's completely at peace with the idea that color vision doesn't reveal objective redness in the world — the redness is something evolution put in us as a handy label for reflectance properties, and she says nobody should lose sleep over that. She thinks moral judgments that run through disgust (like her own reflexive purity-type judgments) are probably debunked, since selection shaped those reactions for fitness, not for tracking moral truth. Religious belief she debunks happily: we can tell the whole story of why people believe — agency detection, fear of death, social cohesion — without the beliefs being true, so as far as she's concerned that settles it. But then she draws a hard line: 2+2=4 is untouched, basic logical inference is untouched, her belief in other minds is untouched (even though she concedes evolution shaped our mindreading tendencies), and ordinary perception of tables and chairs is untouched too. She got actually annoyed when I suggested these all sit in the same boat.

Next week we're doing two new cases and I want to show up prepared instead of getting steamrolled. Case one: her very strong intuition that there are strictly more natural numbers than even numbers — the one Cantor overturned. No ancestor of ours ever needed a view about infinite collections, so whatever faculty generates that intuition is some kind of byproduct of finite counting, doing work it was never designed for. Case two: her confidence that she knows her own motives — specifically her belief that she volunteers at the animal shelter out of genuine concern and not, say, status-seeking — given that she herself believes self-deception is exactly the kind of thing evolution would favor.

So: predict her verdict on both if she stays consistent with her record, and lay out the rule she's implicitly running. My own guess was something like "debunking applies whenever the evolutionary story explains the belief without its truth doing any explanatory work," but I don't think that can be right — that condition looks like it holds for other minds and even for logic (we reason the way we do because it was adaptive, not because it's valid), yet she refuses to debunk those. So what are the candidate rules that fit her verdicts equally well, which of them diverge on the infinity intuition or on the introspection case, and what further case should I throw at her to actually tell the rules apart? Also — is there a verdict on the infinity thing she could give that would be consistent with her record but genuinely surprising, or is she boxed in?
