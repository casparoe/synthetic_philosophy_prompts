After rereading the Ant Fugue I've gotten tired of the yes-it-is / no-it-isn't about whether an ant colony can literally have beliefs. I want the spec before the theories: write out the desiderata any adequate account of colony-level cognition has to meet. Off the top of my head it should cover: what work colony-level states do that ant-level states can't do on their own (otherwise why posit them at all); where the level gets cut in a principled way — why the whole colony and not the half that ends up on my shoe, or the pile that sits on a log for a week; whether attributing beliefs to the colony is a realist claim or just bookkeeping; how it avoids smuggling intelligence back into individual ants, whose brains are miniscule; which colony behaviors count as the colony's cognition versus just the additive sum of ant behavior; and what it says about the fact that if you cut a colony in half you get two colonies, while cutting a brain gives you a worse brain, not two brains — is graceful degradation under damage a requirement or just a camp preference? For each desideratum give the case behind it, mark which are non-negotiable and which are negotiable, and flag any pair that can't be jointly satisfied — my hunch is the realist one and the level-cut one are in tension. Skip scoring existing views against the list, I just want the list.
