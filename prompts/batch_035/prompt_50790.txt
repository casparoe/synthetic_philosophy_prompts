My friend Marco and I had a three-hour argument after his BBQ last weekend that basically ruined dessert, and I'm still chewing on it (pun intended, ugh). It started because I passed on the regular burgers and he called that pointless moral theater — the cow was already dead, and my one purchase changes nothing about how many get slaughtered, since supply chains respond to demand in big steps and no single burger tips any threshold. I dismissed that at the time but I keep coming back to it, so I'm trying to figure out what I actually believe by listing my gut verdicts on cases and seeing if there's a principle underneath them.

Here are the verdicts. I think it's wrong for me to buy factory-farmed chicken, even while being pretty sure no particular bird lives or dies because of my one pack of thighs. But I think it's fine for me to fly to my sister's wedding in Lisbon even though that single flight probably emits more carbon than all the chicken I'd otherwise eat in a year — this one bugs me and I can't shake it either way. I vote in every national election even though I live in a state that hasn't been competitive in my lifetime, and I think that's not just fine but sort of admirable of me; I'd honestly look slightly down on someone who doesn't bother. Meanwhile I think my coworker who tosses one plastic bottle in the trash when the recycling bin is three feet away is being mildly bad, but I don't judge anyone for not paying the $12 to offset a flight, even though the bottle is litterally nothing and the offset is cheap and does something real. And one more: the creek behind my childhood house slowly got choked with yard waste because everyone dumped clippings in it, one wheelbarrow at a time, and I've always felt the neighbor who kept dumping when it was already half-choked was doing something worse than the guy who dumped first when it was pristine — even though the first guy's dumping, on its own, did more to change the creek than the last guy's did.

So is there one principle, or maybe a couple of principles wearing a trenchcoat, that captures all this? Marco says the honest answer is that all of it is just signaling to myself and others and none of it tracks anything real, but the voting verdict is the one I'd defend hardest and I don't think it's just self-image. Give me the principle (or a small set of candidates) stated precisely, tell me which case motivates which clause, and then tell me what it says about the case I keep flip-flopping on — whether I should tick the "offset your carbon for $12" box the airline shows me at checkout, given that I never tick the "round up for charity" at the grocery store either. And be honest about which of my verdicts has to go if they can't all be saved, because right now the meat-versus-Lisbon one in particular feels like it's holding the whole thing hostage.
