I'm a third-year graduate student giving a job-market-style talk in two weeks at a department whose philosophy of mind group is dominated by hard-line physicalists (a couple of type-B Block-style physicalists who lean on the explanatory gap, a functionalist who thinks consciousness is just an information-processing abstraction, and a representationalist), together with one substance dualist who thinks the physicalists are the ones being evasive. My thesis: panpsychism is not just the least bad option once you grant the hard problem, and the combination problem is tractable if we stop thinking of micro-experience as a kind of stuff that needs gluing together. On my view, which I'm calling structural fusion, micro-subjects don't aggregate into a macro-subject the way bricks aggregate into a wall; rather, when micro-systems come to instantiate one shared dynamical structure -- the way neurons instantiate a single recurrent processing architecture -- there is a sense in which there is only one formal organization there, and the macro-experience is that organization realized in experiential rather than purely dispositional categories. Combination, I'll argue, was a pseudo-problem generated by picturing experiences as private particulars that then owe us an account of how they merge; drop the particular-picture and the problem dissolves into the ordinary question of when parts instantiate a common structure, which is a question physics already knows how to answer. I'm aware that "ordinary question of when parts instantiate a common structure" is doing a lot of work, and I say so in the paper.

Please prepare my Q&A sheet. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll actually arrive at this talk -- the functionalist will go first, the representationalist likes to wait -- each phrased the way that specific kind of philosopher would put it, not the way a general audience would. For each, give a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Flag which objections I should concede parts of rather than fight, since my job-market future partly depends on not looking evasive. Also include the one question I'm quietly hoping nobody asks, with what to say if it comes anyway, and one objection that the dualist in the room will raise that will secretly help me against the physicalists if I handle it right -- I want to be ready to accept the assist without seeming to ally with him.
