I've been stuck on my dissertation chapter about manipulation for months and I'm starting to wonder if the problem isn't me but the concept itself, so let me lay out where the debate actually sits and ask you to diagnose the impasse rather than pick a winner.

The standard accounts, roughly: (a) the bypass camp says manipulation is influence that works through non-rational channels — emotions, habit, cognitive quirks — rather than by giving reasons. The obvious problem is that literally all human persuasion uses framing, tone, timing, and warmth; a pure reasons-giving angel persuader doesn't exist, so this account overgeneralizes. (b) The autonomy camp locates the wrong in desire-subversion: the target ends up wanting something that isn't "really" theirs, because the causal chain to the new preference went around rather than through their reflective endorsement. Problem: education, therapy, and falling in love all rewrite what you want without your prior sign-off and we praise them. (c) The respect camp, Kant-flavored, says manipulation treats a person as a thing to be moved rather than an agent to be addressed. Problem: unless you spell out "treating as a thing" non-circularly, and the obvious spelling collapses back into (a). (d) The nudging pragmatists, in the Sunstein vein, want to give up on defining manipulation and just ask whether the influence tracks the target's own welfare or reflective values — but on that move "manipulative" stops doing any independent work and becomes a synonym for "bad influence," which is presumably why the definitional literature and the nudge literature barely talk to each other.

Meanwhile every camp gets run over by the same convoy of hard cases: the loving spouse who reframes a decision to make the right choice easier to see; the cafeteria worker who puts fruit at eye level; the friend who picks the moment you're relaxed to raise a hard topic; the ad that is completely honest but relies on your tiredness. Everyone agrees manipulation is pro tanto wrong, everyone has counterexamples to everyone else, and nobody has moved in roughly thirty years.

I have my own test, which I'd like you to evaluate as part of the diagnosis. Call it the audience replacement test: replace the actual target with an idealized version of them — fully informed, perfectly attentive, and aware of exactly what the influencer is doing — and ask whether the technique would still win assent. If yes, it's persuasion; if its success depends on some shortfall between the actual and idealized target, it's manipulation. It handles dark patterns and most advertising beatifully. But here's what worries me: a great eulogy, or a photograph of a drowned refugee child used in a genuine humanitarian appeal, moves us partly through eloquence and vividness, and my test seems to imply that being moved by those is a defect in the audience — that rhetoric exploits us. But Aristotle's whole picture of the virtuous listener was one who is moved WELL by good speech, not one immune to it. So either I bite the bullet that all rhetorical effect is manipulative, or the idealization smuggles in a picture of agency that's too bloodless to be a picture of us.

So here's what I want: a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this is. Is it verbal — are the camps really just answering different questions (a descriptive question about mechanism vs. a normative question about complaint-worthiness) under one word? Is it substantive but about value, i.e., the disagreement is really about how much idealization agency can tolerate? Is "manipulation" perhaps a grab-bag concept, like "junk DNA" before people knew what it did — several unrelated wrongs wearing one coat, so no unified analysis exists to be found? My tentative hypothesis is that it's the different-questions thing plus grab-bag, but I want you to argue me out of it or into it. Also tell me honestly what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a reformulated question, a taxonomy of wrongs instead of a definition — or whether nothing should be expected and why. Engage the actual positions above, not a generic survey.
