My brother in law has gone pretty deep into some conspiratorial corners of the internet over the past two years, and at thanksgiving my sister shut down the whole argument by saying "you can't reason someone out of a position they didn't reason themselves into." Everyone nodded like it was obviously true and we all dropped it. I've been chewing on it since, because that one sentence did a LOT of work that night and nobody asked what it actually claims.

I want you to act as a professional philosopher who works on political polarization and echo chamber research. Take the slogan seriously as a piece of language and lay out the distinct precise claims it could be making, as a menu I could bring back to my family, not one interpretation stretched out. Some directions to consider (but go beyond them): Is it empirical -- a claim that beliefs formed without reasoning literally cannot be changed by reasoning, or just that they're harder to change? Is it about the psychology of identity-protective cognition, where the belief is bolted to who he thinks he is? Is it a claim about the belief's origin dictating its exit route, which seems like it would be false given that people demonstrably do leave fringe movements, deconvert, etc.? Is it not factual at all but strategic advice -- don't argue, do something else (trust, relationships, just waiting)? And does it quietly assume he never reasoned at all, which seems unfair -- the guy does hours of "research," just bad research, which is a different problem than no reasoning.

For each reading, say whether it's trivial, substantive and defensible, or actually false, and flag where the empirical literature (e.g. on whether hearing opposing arguments backfires or whether people update at all) supports it or not. Then the crucial part: my sister used the line to justify everyone stopping trying. Which readings would actually license that, and which only support something weaker like "lecture less, talk differently"? Please think carefully before answering and start with some brainstorming to make sure you've got all the readings, then organize them. And be honest if the most defensible reading of the slogan gives my family an answer they won't like.
