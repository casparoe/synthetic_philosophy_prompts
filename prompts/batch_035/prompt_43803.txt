here's roughly where i've landed on reasons for action and I want to know if it hangs together before I put it in my seminar writeup: (1) I'm with Williams that "A has reason to φ" is only true if there's a sound deliberative route from A's subjective motivational set, as it actually is, that would get A to φ or at least to care about φ-ing; (2) but sound deliberation is much broader than means-end reasoning — imagination, working through what an option would actually be like, correcting factual mistakes — so this is not the crude "you only have reason to do what you already want" view; (3) I deny motivational internalism about moral judgment — someone can sincerely judge that something is wrong and be entirely unmoved by that judgment; (4) I also accept what seems like a platitude to me: a reason just is a consideration that counts in favor of doing something; (5) on moral education I think habituating a child into compassion — not arguing with her, just practice and example until she cares — can give her reasons she didnt have before, not merely change her so that she'll have them later; (6) yet I dont think brainwashing creates reasons — installing a desire in someone doesn't by itself give them a reason to act on it, so the cult victim has no reason to serve the cult. Which of these pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way? Where something has to give, tell me what the cheapest fix is, and please keep clear for me what is basically settled among people who work on this versus what is actually contested — e.g. I don't want to write up (4) as a platitude if half the field disputes that it is one, or present Williams's own argument for internalism as if everyone agrees it works.
