I do a small philosophy newsletter and want a "which theory of emotions do you actually hold?" quiz for readers. Eight questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the main positions: judgment theories (emotions are evaluative judgments — Solomon/Nussbaum style), perceptual theories (emotions are perceptions of evaluative properties, the way you see that a stick is bent), and feeling theories (an emotion just is a bodily change or the feeling of one, James-style), plus a hybrid option if the pattern calls for it. Some cases I definitely want worked in: recalcitrant fear (totally scared of flying while judging flying safe), a patient with no bodily feeling below the neck who insists he still cares about things, fear or anger directed at a fictional character, and being moved to tears by sad music. The point is to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like — so no question can just ask "are emotions cognitive?". At the end, flag any two positions the quiz genuinely can't seperate, and be honest about it. One more thing: for the scoring key, make definite calls. Where an answer pattern could fit two positions, commit to one placement and give your reasoning — I dont want a key full of "could be either, depends".
