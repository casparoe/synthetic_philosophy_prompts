my flatmate runs a little sociobiology reading group and she's obsessed with the superorganism picture: an ant colony is like a single organism — the workers are like cells or organs, the queen is like the germline, and a colony founds new colonies the way a body produces offspring. She even extends it into a ladder — cells once competed inside what became bodies, suppressed their conflict, and became units; colonies did something similar; so maybe human groups could too someday.

can you audit how far the analogy actually holds? I remember reading that workers aren't sterile in a lot of species, and that there's plenty of within-colony conflict (workers laying eggs, simbling policing and all that), but I don't know how much that matters. So: which disanalogies actually threaten her conclusion and which are just nitpicks, and does "colony = literally an organism" end up doing all the argumentative work, or does a weaker version of the claim survive?
