Is there a name for the following? My department shortlists grad applicants by having each faculty member rank them and then combining the rankings somehow. Last cycle we fought for weeks about the combining rule, and every candidate rule turned out to have a weird defect. With head-to-head majorities we got a rock-paper-scissors situation: applicant A beat B one-on-one, B beat C, but C beat A, so there was no 'real' winner. Someone suggested giving points by rank position and summing those, but then we noticed that if one strong applicant withdraws, the winner among the rest can change — even though nobody changed their mind about anyone, the person leaving was just soaking up high positions. And a third colleague pointed out that under both rules you could sometimes get a better outcome by ranking someone you don't really like higher, to push a rival down.

It feels like there's some general fact lurking here: any method for merging individual rankings into a group ranking will have a defect of roughly this kind. Is there an actual established theorem or concept covering this, or did we just run into unrelated quirks? If there is a general result I'd want to know exactly what it says and what it assumes — like whether it requires full rankings from everyone, whether the strategic-lying part is covered by the same result or a seperate one, and what counts as an acceptable 'defect' vs a fatal one.
