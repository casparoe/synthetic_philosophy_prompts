I have been reading the rationalist blogosphere since the mid-2010s — LessWrong, the AI Alignment Forum, the MIRI-adjacent decision theory threads — and I am fluent in that literature's working vocabulary: AIXI and its Cartesian boundary, base optimizers versus mesa-optimizers, inner and outer alignment, goal misgeneralization, the taxonomy of Goodhart effects, the shard-theoretic story of values as contextually activated learned heuristics, and coherence arguments about what any sufficiently capable optimizer eventually becomes. Metaethics papers, on the other hand, bounce off me: I can never quite tell whether the participants are disagreeing about anything that isn't already settled by the observation that humans are evolved systems running objectives that diverge from inclusive fitness in predictable and explicable ways.

Here is the restatement I want. Take the evolutionary debunking debate in contemporary metaethics — paradigmatically Sharon Street's Darwinian dilemma for value realists, along with Richard Joyce's debunking-driven error theory and the familiar realist counterattacks (third-factor-style responses and their cousins) — and restate it, as faithfully as you can, in the vocabulary I actually use. So: evolution as the base optimizer; human motivational and evaluative dispositions as the mesa-objective, learned under a training signal that tracked inclusive fitness rather than truth; the debunker's challenge as the claim that our reliability at tracking independent moral facts was never selected for and has no antecedent reason to be high, so our confident moral verdicts are a proxy that has wandered off on its own; realist responses as attempts to show the proxy correlated with the target after all, or that the verdicts survive once we condition on the system's own reflective equilibrium rather than its training signal. Give me the translation premise by premise — including both horns of Street's dilemma rendered in optimizer terms — and tell me what reflective equilibrium becomes if you refuse to treat any state of the evolved system as epistemically privileged.

Then give me the residue report, and take it seriously, because I suspect it runs in a direction uncomfortable for my framework. Inner alignment talk presupposes that there is a well-defined base objective and that aligning the mesa-objective to it counts as success; Street, translated, seems to conclude something close to the opposite — that nobody, including the mesa-optimizer, has a reason to endorse the base objective — and my vocabulary may simply lack a register for that thought. Relatedly: rationalists run coherence arguments to the effect that any sufficiently capable optimizer converges on unified expected-utility maximization, while the metaethicists argue over whether normativity is constitutive of agency or projected onto the world. I want to know whether that is one dispute wearing two sets of clothes, two disputes pretending to be one, or something that dissolves under the translation. Are the philosophers' realist horns smuggling in an outer objective that was never there, or does my alignment vocabulary smuggle in the assumption that, if there is an outer objective, it matters — precisely the assumption the debunker denies? Be honest about where you had to fudge the mapping rather than discovering it; I would rather learn that my vocabulary is missing something than be flattered.

End with a final line of the form \residue{...}: a single sentence naming the claim in the debate that least survives translation into base/mesa-optimizer terms, and saying whether that residue reflects a defect in my vocabulary or in the claim itself.
