Spent forty years modelling predator-prey systems in fisheries before I retired, so when I finally got round to reading up on cliodynamics I sat up straight: Turchin and co. basically describe secular cycles as a predator-prey system, with commoners as the prey and the elite as the predators — population grows, elites overproduce, they strip the surplus, everything crashes, lather rinse repeat. People in my book club now say this means the 2020s instability was predictable decades out, the way a collapsing fishery is predictable if you know the parameters. Being an old hand at Lotka-Volterra I want to audit this before I concede the point. Lay out the correspondence table for me — what plays the role of prey, predator, carrying capacity, the functional response — then tell me where the mapping breaks down. The bits I'm suspicious of: predators in real ecosystems don't strategise or switch to rent extraction when hunting gets thin, the "carrying capacity" of an agrarian economy is partly a social choice, and parameters in my fish populations actually stayed put between cycles. And then the key question: does the predictability claim survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the ecology carrying it the whole way?
