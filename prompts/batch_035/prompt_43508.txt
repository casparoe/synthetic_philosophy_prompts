Bit of an odd request, but here goes. Over the holidays I was helping my nephew redo his CV for the ninth time — every one of these job ads seemed to want five years' experience for an entry-level post, an unpaid six-month "trial period", a cover letter in a format nobody will ever read, and it struck me that the requirements aren't bugs. They're the point. The whole thing is a handicap display: the uselessness of the demand is what makes it informative, because only the sufficiently desperate or well-off will pay it. I got into this via my brother-in-law, who does behavioural ecology and goes on about peacock tails at dinner, and then I found out economists had worked this out for job markets decades ago (Spence's signalling stuff), and now I can't look at a careers page the same way.

So: write me a job advertisement, roughly 450–550 words, that is a proper parody of the genre. You're an economist with a satirical streak — someone who has taught the signalling literature to undergraduates and is tired of them memorising the graphs without feeling the cynicism. The ad should have all the furniture: the deadpan corporate voice, section headers ("About the role", "What we're looking for", "Benefits"), bulleted requirements, an invented mid-sized firm (some grey consultancy or insurance broker that calls itself "a family"). The conceit is that the employer has read the literature and decided to be honest: every requirement is chosen purely for its cost and the ad admits it. Requirements should be genuinely useless in content but expensive in time, money, or dignity — my nephew's favourites so far: a cover letter written in original Latin (which will not be read), an interview at 7am on a Sunday with no remote option, a "creativity exercise" that's assembling flat-pack furniture in the lobby. Feel free to invent your own, but make each one expensive *and* pointless, not just cruel.

The part I care most about: the jokes have to survive an economist's audit, not just get a laugh. So the ad has to quietly get the theory right — a cheap requirement would be faked by everyone and carry no information, hence no exceptions ever (waiving the Latin for a promising candidate would ruin the signal, and the ad should say so); the cost has to hurt the uncommitted more than the committed, otherwise it doesn't seperate anyone and isn't worth demanding; and somewhere it should half-admit, in the tone real ads use for inconvenient truths, that this filters for people who can afford to signal, which is not the same thing as talent. Deadpan throughout — no winking, no "isn't this absurd", the horror is that every sentence is technically rational. Maybe finish with an equal-opportunities statement that quietly concedes the opposite. And before you write the full thing, brainstorm for a minute: list which of your proposed requirements actually separate types and which merely pool them, and cut the ones that don't work. If a signalling theorist would wince, rewrite it. If they'd nod grimly, keep it. Recieve that as the bar for every line.
