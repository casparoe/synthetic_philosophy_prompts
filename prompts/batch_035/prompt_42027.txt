I've been trying to work out what I actually think makes consent invalid, by going with my gut on a bunch of cases and seeing what comes out the other end. Here's the list, gut verdicts included.

Sex after the other person lies about having been tested for STIs (says they're clean, they're not): the yes doesn't count, obviously. Same person shaves six years off their age: still counts, somehow. Says they're single when they're married: doesn't count. Says they're a great cook when they can barely boil water: counts. A mugger says hand over the wallet or I break your arm, and you hand it over: you didn't consent, that's just surrender. A landlord says pay rent or I evict you, and you pay: you did consent, the deal sticks, even though you'd be homeless otherwise. Your boss asks you out and you say yes mostly because turning down your boss feels like a bad career move: I keep flip-flopping on this one and it annoys me. A friend agrees at 3am, very drunk, to let you give them a stick-and-poke tattoo: no. The same friend, three drinks in but clearly with it, cosigning a loan for you: yes, though I can't say why the tattoo is different. Two stage-combat partners, one swings a realistic knife at the other after the other said go ahead: fine, obviously — but it's weird that this counts and the mugger doesn't, when the words are nearly the same.

My tentative principle was: consent fails when it's obtained by lying about something material to the decision, or by a threat that leaves no acceptable option, or when the person isn't competent to decide. But it can't be right as stands — the age lie is material to plenty of people, the landlord threat really does leave few acceptable options, and the knife case shows the very same words can be a threat or not depending on staging I don't know how to cash out. So, what I want: is there a principle that fits most or all of my verdicts without becoming a laundry list of exceptions? Which verdicts would you tell me to throw out? Can you say why the married-lie invalidates while the age-lie doesn't, without just pointing back at my intuitions? And tell me what your principle says about a case I haven't listed: someone who agrees to sleep with a person who told them, plainly and up front, that they're only doing it to make an ex jealous — all the information was out on the table, but something about it still smells off to me, and I'd like to know whether my nose is tracking consent or tracking something else entirely.
