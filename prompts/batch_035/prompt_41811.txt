I'm a novelist, and the book I'm drafting turns on two scholars of religion who have spent a decade arguing past each other. One, a cognitive scientist, holds that religions are byproducts: the output of mental machinery evolved for other jobs — an over-eager agency detector, a memory that favors stories a little off the edge of expectation — with no further function of their own. The other, an anthropologist, holds that religions are adaptations in their own right, retained by cultural group selection because costly ritual and supernatural punishment bind large groups of non-kin into cooperative units. I want to write the scene in which, after a year of good-faith adversarial collaboration, the two of them sign a joint statement, and I care more about accuracy than drama. Draft that statement. It should open with the claims both can sign, including their shared commitment to treating religion as a natural phenomenon; then set out, in wording each would accept as a description of his own view, exactly where they still differ; then identify the cruxes — I suspect one turns on whether morally concerned high gods appeared before or after societies scaled up, and another on whether "religion" names anything evolution ever acted on at all; and for each crux, name a concrete study or body of evidence both would agree bears on it, recording each side's prediction in advance. Where any part of their dispute strikes you as verbal rather than substantive, say so and separate it out. Begin your response with the statement itself — no preface, no commentary, and no restating of my request.
