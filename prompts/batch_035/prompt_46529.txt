I've spent over a decade reading the rationalist blogosphere and have fully internalized the line that a credence is a disposition to bet: I can put a number on nearly anything, and I pride myself on never saying "it just is" where a probability would do. But sorites-style cases are threatening my worldview, and I'd like to know whether some single principle captures my verdicts on them. The verdicts, taken from my notes: (1) 10,000 grains of sand in a pile is a heap; a lone grain is not; yet I strongly resist the claim that there is some particular n such that n grains is a heap and n−1 isn't. (2) Shown a series of color patches running from unambiguously red to unambiguously orange, with each adjacent pair imperceptibly different, I will affirm that patch 1 is red, that patch 300 is orange, and that if patch k is red and patch k+1 differs imperceptibly, patch k+1 is red — and I can feel the contradiction while endorsing all three anyway. (3) When I write "that borderline pile is a heap: 60%" in my forecasting journal, it feels fine as a betting disposition and wrong as a report of fact: I would take either side of a bet at suitable odds, but I would not flat-out assert the proposition even while looking at the pile. (4) For policy cutoffs — a poverty line for aid eligibility, a legal blood-alcohol limit — I think the boundary is sharp but sharp purely by stipulation, and that this is acceptable engineering rather than a failure to discover where the heap really starts. (5) Yet I also hold, and cannot make myself give up, bivalence for ordinary mid-sized claims: for any particular pile and a competent speaker looking right at it, "that is a heap" is either true or false, full stop. Is there one principle that delivers all five verdicts? In particular, can the "credences are for betting" discipline survive (1) and (3), given that posting sharp odds on a borderline proposition seems to presuppose exactly the sharp heap/non-heap boundary that (1) denies? If something has to go, tell me which verdict to abandon and why, and tell me what the repaired principle says about how I ought to price a bet on whether the 5,000-grain pile in front of me is a heap.
