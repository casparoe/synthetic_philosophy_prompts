So there's this argument I keep having with a friend about torture vs dust specks -- you know the setup, one person tortured for 50 years versus 3^^^3 people each getting a dust speck flicked into their eye. And what's started to bug me isn't either side's position, it's that the argument has basically not moved in years. Everyone understands the arithmetic. Nobody is miscalculating anything. The torture side says: 3^^^3 is so incomprehensibly large that even a tiny, nonzero badness per speck has to multiply out to something worse than the torture, and if you deny that, you're denying that badness aggregates across persons at all, which wrecks your ability to choose between basically any two outcomes. The specks side says: a dust speck isn't bad in the way torture is bad, there's a qualitative break somewhere, and multiplying a near-zero quantity like that is a category error, not a sum. Both sides have heard all of this a hundred times and nobody budges.

So what I want is a diagnosis, not a verdict. What kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it verbal, with the two camps quietly using "worse than" differently? Is it a real substantive axiological dispute about additivity, or about whether "better than" is even transitive in the limit, or about lexical thresholds in value? Or is it bedrock intuitions that neither side can argue for without begging the question?

I do have a hunch I want you to check. My guess is the two sides are answering different questions under one heading: the torture side is answering a choice question (which world should you bring about, if you might be anyone in it) and the specks side is answering an evaluation question (which world contains more suffering, all told) -- and those can come apart if you deny that badness is cardinally additive across people. But I'm honestly not sure that holds up, because the standard dialectical moves seem to cut in both directions. Parfit's harmless torturers, where a thousand torturers each nudge a dial by an imperceptible amount and the victim ends up tortured, seems to push people toward the torture side. But the variant where all the specks go into ONE person's eyes, one every second for their whole life, pushes the other way -- there it's not interpersonal aggregation at all anymore, and the specks still don't seem obviously worse than the torture. Which of these moves is actually doing real work, and which is just intuition pumping?

So please: pick a diagnosis and defend it using what each side actually says, evaluate my two-questions hunch, and be honest about whether anything could settle this -- a cleaner thought experiment, a distinction someone hasn't drawn yet, whatever -- or whether this is just a case where you eventually pick your axioms and stop arguing. Also don't go easy on my hunch just because I like it, if its wrong say so.
