Been reading up on Frederick Douglass's switch from the Garrisonian view that the Constitution was a pro-slavery pact to his later position that, read by its own text rather than by the self-serving way the country had practiced it, the Constitution was actually an anti-slavery document that abolitionists should claim as their own. Now I want you to warp him to the present day. Say Douglass could observe the current US Supreme Court, where in some recent Second Amendment cases the majority says judges shouldn't do the usual interest-balancing but instead check whether a modern regulation has a proper historical analogue in how things were regulated around the founding era, and where some justices have argued for extending that history-first approach to other rights too. The irony I keep chewing on: Douglass's whole move was to refuse to let the actual historical practice of 1790s-1850s America determine what the Constitution meant — the founding generation and their descendants upheld slavery in practice, so he pointed at the written words instead, and at the natural-rights principles behind the Declaration he loved so much. The history-and-tradition people, in a way, do kind of the opposite: the written text is vague, so they say the historical practice is what fixes the right's scope. So do you think Douglass would see the modern historical-analogue method as a betrayal of his whole approach, or would he be sympathetic to it since it at least takes the founding seriously and claims to be anti-living-constitution? And heres the harder part of the question: Douglass also had a strong streak of trusting broad founding principles over narrow application, so maybe he'd say judges should read the Constitution through the Declaration's equality principles and just ignore ugly founding-era practice where it conflicts. Walk me through which of these two Douglases you think wins out — the text-and-principles guy or the guy who might appreciate deferring to how the founders actually did things — and don't just sit on the fence, give me your actual best guess with reasoning.
