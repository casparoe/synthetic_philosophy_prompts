Last night I had a long argument with a friend about Dennett's notion of free-floating rationales: reasons that exist without being anyone's reasons — the reason the moth has eye-spots, for instance, which no moth and no designer ever thought of. My friend concedes that selection processes can leave rationales unowned: evolution is a mindless generator-and-tester, so it can install reasons no reasoner has. But she draws the line there. Every rationale, she insists, requires some history of generate-and-test — some process, however mindless, that produces variants and winnows them. Where nothing has been winnowed, she says, there are only regularities, and any reason why we voice about the thing is our projection onto it, not a reason that was there before us.

I want to test that line with a designed case, and I would like you to construct it to specification. Build me a thought experiment in which something has a reason for its structure that is attributable to no mind and to no selection process: no evolution, no learning, no trial-and-error, no generate-and-test of any kind, however mindless. The case is meant to put pressure on my friend's restriction: I want a case where, if the restriction holds, she has to say something uncomfortable — either that the reason present is not a real reason, or that generate-and-test was secretly there after all. That, in turn, fixes what counts as cheating: do not stipulate into the case that the reason is really there, and do not smuggle in a winnowing process under another name. Constraints: keep it this-worldly (physics, chemistry, geology, ordinary objects — no demons, no simulations, no fine-tuned universes); the reason must be of the kind that, had a mind produced the structure, we would unhesitatingly call its purpose rather than a mere causal explanation; and present the scenario itself in a single paragraph.

After the scenario, walk through the case and tell me: (1) in exactly what sense the reason is there for free — what it is a reason for, and in virtue of what it holds; (2) whether my friend can deflate it to a regularity plus our habit of narrating, and what in the case, if anything, resists that deflation; (3) which feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens to the case if that feature is removed; and (4) how she, as a partisan of the generate-and-test requirement, would most plausibly attack the case. Please be honest about whether the specification is satisfiable at all: if you conclude that every candidate either smuggles in a selecting process or collapses into a mere regularity, say so and explain, rather than forcing the case through.
