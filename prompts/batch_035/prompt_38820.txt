I'm a novelist writing a book narrated by a woman in the early stages of a progressive illness, and I've given her a settled set of views about death which she will state outright in her final chapters. Before I write those chapters I want to be sure the views are jointly tenable, since an inconsistency would read as authorial error rather than as characterisation. Here is the package I've given her, which is also roughly my own: (1) death is bad for the one who dies, and bad specifically as a deprivation of the goods she would otherwise have gone on to enjoy; (2) nothing can be bad for a person at a time at which she does not exist; (3) Lucretius's symmetry argument fails — posthumous nonexistence is bad for her in a way prenatal nonexistence is not, because her projects and concerns run forward, not backward; (4) she takes Parfit's line that personal identity over time is not a further fact beyond psychological continuity, and that connectedness is what matters in survival; and (5) she agrees with Williams that an immortal life would eventually become intolerably tedious, so she does not regard her finitude, considered in itself, as the tragedy.

Please audit this set rather than lecture on death generally: which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension and how badly; which pairs only look to be in tension and why the appearance dissolves; and, for each genuine tension, what would have to give and what giving it up would cost her. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally grave. Please deliver the audit as a JSON object with three fields: "genuine_tensions", an array ordered from most to least severe, each element an object with the keys "commitments_in_conflict", "diagnosis", "resolution", and "cost"; "apparent_tensions", an array of objects with the keys "commitments_involved" and "why_it_dissolves"; and "overall_verdict", a short string stating whether the package is tenable as it stands and, if not, which single revision would clean up the most.
