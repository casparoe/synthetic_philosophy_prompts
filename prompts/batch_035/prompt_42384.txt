Two members of my political philosophy reading group have spent a year arguing past each other about Nozick's entitlement theory and the Wilt Chamberlain argument: one is a committed Nozickian, the other a committed Rawlsian who holds that justice is a property of the pattern of holdings. Write the joint statement they should have been able to produce after a serious adversarial collaboration — the document both could actually sign.

The setup, which both accept as stated: begin from a distribution D1 stipulated to be just in Nozick's sense; a thousand people each voluntarily pay a small sum for the pleasure of watching Chamberlain play basketball over a season; the resulting distribution D2 has him far richer than anyone else, yet every transfer was voluntary. Nozick's conclusion: no patterned principle of distributive justice can be both realized and stable under liberty, because maintaining any pattern requires continual interference with people's voluntary choices.

The document must contain, in this order:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims: what the example actually establishes and what it does not; the difference between historical principles and patterned (end-state) principles; the three components of the entitlement theory, with both sides acknowledging where they think its weakest link lies (I expect them to agree it is the rectification clause); and an explicit ruling on which parts of their year-long dispute were merely verbal — in particular, whether the claim that liberty upsets patterns is substantive or definitional, given that Nozick defines a choice as voluntary relative to a just background distribution.

2. A disagreements table, with each residual disagreement phrased so that its own side would accept the wording. I suspect the deepest one concerns the status of D1: the Rawlsian holds that stipulating a just D1 lets the example presuppose part of what it purports to prove, since no actual distribution comes close to satisfying the theory's conditions — unbroken chains of just acquisition and transfer, plus completed rectification of all historical wrongs — so the stipulation does no real work in the world we occupy; the Nozickian holds that the example is a proof of concept about the structure of justice, not a claim about history. That disagreement, and any others they can state exactly, belong in the table.

3. A cruxes section. For each crux, include a concrete case or argument both sides agree bears on it, and write down each side's prediction in advance. At least these three must appear:

   (a) Endogenous valuations. An influential line of objection holds that the spectators' willingness to pay is not a clean datum, because preferences for watching sport are themselves shaped by the unequal background that produced them. Test case: a variant in which the fans' spending money comes entirely from holdings already judged just by both sides' lights under D1. Does the endogeneity worry survive when the background is stipulated just, or does it collapse into the D1 dispute from item 2? Each side must commit to a prediction and to what would count as that prediction failing.

   (b) Invisible maintenance of patterns. Whether a patterned principle can be upheld without continual interference with liberty once the maintenance is engineered to be imperceptible: a small automatic levy at the point of each transaction, computed and remitted with no human intervention, holding the post-transfer pattern at D1's shape. The Nozickian predicts this is interference merely relocated, since the threat of force still stands behind every transfer; the Rawlsian predicts that if no one's choices ever differ from how they otherwise would have been, the conviction that liberty is being violated should be discounted as an artifact of visible coercion. Both predictions go in, along with what observation would tell against each.

   (c) Rectification as the residual battleground. How much of an actual redistributive welfare state could be justified under the rectification clause alone? Concrete test: two societies, one with scrupulous records of every historical wrong, one whose records were destroyed. Both sides must state in advance how much redistribution they would endorse in each, and whether the gap between their answers shrinks as record quality improves.

4. A brief closing section in which both address Nozick's own later public retreat from the entitlement theory: the Nozickian must say whether that retreat was a response to arguments or to temperament; the Rawlsian must say whether it changes the logic of the example at all, or only its sociology.

Do not adjudicate between the two; the deliverable is a constructive text both parties could put their names to, with the residual disagreement made exact rather than resolved. Include no reading lists, bibliographies, or citations — a philosopher's name may be mentioned in passing where it genuinely helps, but nothing in the document should hinge on bibliographic detail.
