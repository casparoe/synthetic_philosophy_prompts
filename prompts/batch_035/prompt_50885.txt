So here's my situation. I play an MMO where, years ago, the devs ran a one-time event and gave a special cape to everyone who finished it while it was live — only a few thousand players ever got it, and over the years it became a status thing, people recognize it, whole lore around it. Last month the devs, doing some cleanup on old achievement flags, accidentally made it obtainable again for a weekend before anyone noticed. Hundreds of players grabbed it.

Now my guild is in shambles. The original holders are genuinely upset, like grieving-level upset, and a bunch of them are demanding the devs delete the copies from everyone who got it that weekend. Those players are furious — they say they followed the rules as the game actually was, they put in hours, and deleting the cape would be theft, straight up. My friend thinks the whole thing is absurd: no cape was ever stolen or destroyed because there's no cape, it's just a database flag flipping, and everyone is performing funeral rites for a row in a SQL table. If nothing exists, nothing was lost.

The problem is his argument feels like it proves way too much. My bank balance is also basically a database flag, and if the bank's systems wiped it out nobody would say I lost nothing real. So why does "it's just bits" settle the cape case but not the money case? And is there even a difference between this and the scenario people keep comparing it to, where the devs restore from a backup and my character gets set back three months — stuff vanishes either way, but the two feel really different to me and I can't articulate why.

I guess what I want to know: did I actually lose something when other people got the cape, or just lose the feeling of being special, and are those even different things? And do the guys demanding the weekend players have their capes deleted have any kind of leg to stand on, given that those players didn't do anything wrong? I don't want comfort, I want to know if my being this annoyed tracks something real.
