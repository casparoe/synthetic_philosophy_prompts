Write a short story (roughly 1,000–1,500 words) about a retired cryptanalyst who is invited to consult for a synthetic biology laboratory after the researchers discover that one of their engineered organisms carries a stretch of apparently non-coding DNA which, read in a certain way, spells out an English sentence. The story should turn on the question of whether DNA is genuinely a code or merely a useful metaphor for one. Let the cryptanalyst and the biologists disagree productively: the cryptanalyst should at first be struck by the analogy — the codon table as a codebook, the ribosome as a decryption machine — but grow uneasy with the disanalogies, such as the fact that the genetic code was not designed to conceal anything from an adversary, that it does not encode propositions, and that the mapping from codons to amino acids was fixed by evolutionary accident rather than by convention. Somewhere in the story, have a character articulate the distinction between information as mere reduction of uncertainty and information as being about something, and consider whether the engineered sequence is about anything at all. The ending should leave the philosophical question open but earned, showing rather than telling why the answer matters for how the characters understand what they have created. Please avoid strawmanning either side, and keep the science plausible enough that the story could serve to introduce a student to the debate.
