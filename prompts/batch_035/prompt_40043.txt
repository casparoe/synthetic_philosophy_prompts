So I'm a cognitive science master's student and I have to write a research proposal for my thesis (about 30-35 pages, one year, some experiments allowed but honestly I'd rather keep it conceptual). My topic is Dennett's four kinds of creatures — Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, Gregorian — and I keep snagging on something nobody in my seminars seems bothered by.

The way the story usually goes is a ladder: Darwinian creatures have their "hypotheses" tested by dying in the real world, Skinnerian creatures can die less often because they revise behavior within a lifetime via conditioning, Popperian creatures pre-select among behaviors internally by running them through a model, letting their hypotheses die in their stead, and Gregorian creatures import extra mind-tools (language, culture, notation) that restructure the whole edifice. Fine. But here's my itch: the taxonomy seems to run together two things that could come apart — what generates candidate behaviors, and what does the filtering or testing of them. A Skinnerian creature's filter is still the world (reinforcement), it just gets more shots at bat. A Popperian creature moves the filter inside the head. Those are clearly different axes of change, but the ladder presents them as rungs of one thing.

And here's where I think it bites: social or observational learning. A young animal that watches a conspecific get sick after eating a certain berry, and avoids it, never had its own candidate behavior filtered by the world, and it also didn't run a simulation through an internal model in any rich sense. It offloaded the filtering onto another individual's misfortune. Dennett's ladder has no rung for this — you either test in the world, test in a model, or get handed tools by culture (but the Gregorian rung is specifically about mind-tools that upgrade your thinking, not just information transfer). So either observational learning is secretly Popperian (the animal models the conspecific), or it's secretly Gregorian (it's a cruder version of cultural transmission), or the ladder needs a fifth rung, or the whole thing should be re-cut along the two axes I mentioned. My half-formed guess is the last one, and that a 2x2 (or similar) grid reproduces Dennett's four kinds as one diagonal while filling in cells he leaves empty, with observational learners occupying one of the empty cells.

Turn this into a proper proposal for me, please. I need: the question stated precisely enough that it could turn out to have a boring answer; why Dennett's own presentation and the standard glosses leave it open (without doing a lit-review-by-citation thing, my advisor wants the argument reconstructed, I can fill in references later); what I'd actually do month by month to make progress on it, given it's a conceptual thesis but I can analyze examples from comparative cognition if useful (imitation in rats, teaching in meerkats, stuff like that — stuff I can describe from what I already know, no need to track down exact studies); what a positive result would look like, and honestly what would make the project fail — e.g., if the two-axes thing is just a relabeling that doesn't earn its keep, I want to know how I'd notice that rather than discovering it in month ten. Also flag if the scope is wrong for a master's thesis and what to cut.

Keep it real about the hard part too: my advisor thinks the grid idea is "attractive but unfalsifiable in the usual philosophy sense," so the proposal has to say what work the re-cutting does — what it gets you that the ladder doesn't — otherwise it's just diagrams for their own sake. Thanks!!
