I'm on the conflict resolution committee at the nonprofit where I work (~80 staff), and we just got the first real test of our new restorative justice pilot. Quick background: if someone who did a wrong agrees to a facilitated conference with the people they harmed, and those people agree to it, nothing goes in their formal disciplinary record. The case: a coworker — call him R — padded his timesheets for about five months, maybe 40 minutes a day, apparently to carve out time for caring for his mom. His manager found out, her budget absorbed the loss, and she is furious; she wants it formally on his record, and she's made it clear a healing circle isn't going to cut it. Half my committee thinks this is exactly what the pilot exists for — resentment is a relic of blame culture and we should help everyone move past it. The other half thinks the whole premise of the pilot quietly assumes what Strawson spends Freedom and Resentment arguing against: that it's somehow more enlightened to treat wrongdoers as problems to be managed or treated than as agents answerable to the people they wronged, and that the manager's anger isn't a bug in the system but part of what recognizing R as a responsible adult looks like from the inside. I keep going back and forth on this, so I'd rather have a real position than vibes.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate before Thursday's meeting. It needs a bottom line the committee can actually vote on — restorative track only, formal discipline, or restorative track plus some discipline, whatever you genuinely think is right — plus whatever conditions make your recommendation workable, and an honest treatment of the losing side (including the manager's, if she loses). Somewhere in there it should face the question directly: is putting R through a no-discipline restorative process respecting him as a responsible agent, denying his responsibility by treating the padding as a glitch to be repaired, or both depending on how the conference is run? Keep it to something a committee member can read in ten minutes. End with a final line in the form \answer{restorative-only}, \answer{restorative-with-conditions}, or \answer{formal-discipline} matching your recommendation.
