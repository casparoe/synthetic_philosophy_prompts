I am a physicist by training — thirty years in condensed matter and statistical mechanics, retired for six, and using the time to read philosophy seriously for the first time. I have lately been working through Schrödinger's popular essays and was startled to discover how much of what he says about the mind is an argument for Advaita Vedanta, the classical Indian school according to which each individual self, the Atman, is identical with the single underlying reality, Brahman, and the apparent plurality of selves is an overlay rather than a fact. In one passage he writes that "consciousness is a singular of which the plural is unknown; that there is only one thing and that what seems to be a plurality is merely a series of different aspects of this one thing, produced by a deception (the Indian MAJA); the same illusion is produced in a gallery of mirrors, and in the same way Gaurisankar and Mt Everest turned out to be the same peak seen from different valleys."

Now, I know the physics I know, and I know the vocabulary I actually think in: state spaces and Hilbert spaces; tensor products; subsystems carved out by partial traces; decoherence and pointer bases; gauge redundancy and gauge fixing; effective theories and their domains of validity; order parameters and broken symmetry. My neuroscience is amateur and my Sanskrit nonexistent. So here is my request: translate the core claims of Schrödinger's Vedanta — consciousness is one and not many; the individual self is an apparent subdivision of that one; the world of multiplicity is appearance superimposed on a single ground; and liberation is the recognition of an identity one always had, not the acquisition of anything new — into that vocabulary, as faithfully as the vocabulary permits. Where several registers are available to you (a partial trace, an integrated-information quantity, a thermodynamic limit), prefer the one a statistical mechanician would reach for first. And do not smuggle the target framework's own metaphysics in under the heading of translation: if something in my vocabulary is quietly doing philosophical work at a given step, flag the step rather than letting it pass as neutral notation.

Then the residue report, which for me is the entire point. I want three headings: what translated cleanly; what translated only with distortion, and precisely what distorted it; and what found no expression at all. A specific question I would like answered somewhere in the report: Schrödinger's gallery of mirrors and his two names for one Himalayan peak — does that analogy become something like gauge redundancy (different descriptions, one physical state) or something like emergence in an effective theory (distinct higher-level facts with a common lower-level base), or does it break under either reading, and where? And a second one: when Advaita says the one consciousness does not merely underlie the many selves but is strictly identical with each of them, is that identity expressible in my vocabulary at all, given that the identities I know how to state hold between states or between theoretical descriptions, not between a state and an experiencing subject?

Please structure the response as follows. First, a section titled "The translation," roughly 600 to 800 words, taking the claims one at a time. Second, "What survived cleanly," roughly 150 words. Third, "What survived with distortion," roughly 250 words, naming for each item what the target vocabulary forced in. Fourth, "What had no expression," roughly 250 words. Fifth and last, "Verdict on the residue," roughly 200 words, answering directly whether the failures lie in my vocabulary or in the original claims — whether there is something real that my notation cannot say, or whether the Vedantic theses are less clear than they first looked to someone trained to trust clarity when he sees it. Throughout, assume I can follow any physics you care to deploy and no philology whatsoever.
