I host a small podcast on philosophical puzzles, and next week's guest is a decision theorist who defends evidential decision theory and maintains that a rational agent in Kavka's toxin puzzle can, at midnight, genuinely intend to drink the toxin the following day — even knowing the million is already banked and that drinking will accomplish nothing beyond a day of misery. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond having her restate the puzzle: include at least one on the difference between intending to drink and merely predicting one's own drinking, at least one on whether an intention formed for its evidential value can survive once the payout has been secured and the evidential reasons evaporate, and at least one on whether her position commits her to a general account of intentions as things one can simply decide to have rather than as evidence about one's future conduct. At least one question should ask what, if anything, distinguishes her toxin-drinker from a Newcomb one-boxer who likewise profits from a past prediction of what she will later do. For each question, add a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give — for instance, if she leans on the one-boxing analogy, the follow-up should force her to say what replaces the predictor's payoff structure in a case where nothing further is at stake.
