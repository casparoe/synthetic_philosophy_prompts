Here's my problem: the literature around this is a swamp, and I can realistically give the topic three, maybe four substantive threads in the piece before a general reader's eyes glaze over. So what I need is a prioritization with the reasoning visible — not a survey. Of these candidate threads (or ones you'd argue belong on the list instead):

1. The zombie-twin point — that an epiphenomenalist's exact physical duplicate, minus the qualia, writes the identical philosophy papers about qualia — and whether that's a genuine reductio or just epiphenomenalism being honest about what it's always claimed.
2. The evolution angle: can natural selection select FOR pain if pain does nothing? And if not, what happens to naturalistic stories about why we have phenomenal experience in the first place — is this fatal or just an unsolved detail?
3. What I've been privately calling the bedside problem (maybe there's a real name for it, tell me if so): if pain reports are caused by neural processing rather than by the pain itself, does that actually change how doctors should think about treating suffering — in infants, animals, sedated or locked-in patients? Or is it philosophically horrifying but a practical wash?
