My mate Dave and I argue about this stuff over barbecue every couple of weeks — neither of us has read a single philosophy book, it's all just from talking. The topic we keep coming back to is when letting something happen is as bad as making it happen, and Dave has some pretty settled verdicts by now:

- A doctor upping the morphine on a dying patient to stop her pain, knowing it will probably shorten her life: fine. The aim is comfort, the death is a side effect.
- A doctor giving a lethal injection to a hopeless patient who's begging for it: no. That's killing, way over the line, doesn't matter how kind the motive.
- Switching off a ventilator at the family's request when there's no hope left: not killing. The illness killed him, the doctor just stopped getting in its way.
- Walking past a kid drowning in a shallow pond when you could of waded in at zero risk: nearly as bad as pushing her under. He says the not-wading is still a decision you made, so it's on you.
- Not giving £40 a month to some charity that would definately save lives: not great, but nowhere near as bad as killing anyone, and he wouldn't call you a murderer.
- A landlord who ignores a broken heater for months and an old tenant dies of the cold: basically killed him, because keeping the heater working was the landlord's job.
