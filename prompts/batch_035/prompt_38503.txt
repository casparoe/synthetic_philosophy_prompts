My uncle had a brain hemorrhage last week. The doctors ran their tests and told us he's "brain dead," and now the family has to decide about organ donation and turning off the ventilator by Friday. My mom and my older brother (he's in his last year of med school) have been going at it at the kitchen table for two nights and I can't tell who's right. I'd honestly never thought about any of this before Tuesday.

It went roughly like this. (1) My mom said the whole thing is absurd: his heart is beating, he's warm, his chest goes up and down. Calling him dead is just wrong, he's in a coma and they're being lazy or worse. (2) My brother said no, a coma is different — in a coma the brain can still recover, and with him the whole brain, including the part that drives breathing, has no function at all and never will. The machine is doing the breathing. Under the standard the whole world uses, that's not a coma, that's death. (3) My mom said that's contradictory on its face — a dead man with a pulse? Dead hearts don't beat. If his heart is beating then something in him is alive. (4) My brother said the heart has its own pacemaker cells and just needs oxygen, which the machine supplies, and that lots of bits of people stay alive for a while after death — that's the whole reason organ donation works, you take a heart from someone dead and it beats in someone else. The question isn't whether any of his cells are alive, it's whether *he* is. (5) My mom said fine, but then say "he's gone," don't say dead — his beard grows, his cuts heal, and she read about a brain-dead woman somewhere who was kept on machines for weeks so her baby could be delivered. If a body can do all that, the word "dead" is being redefined for the convenience of transplant surgeons, and she's not signing anything based on a label that bends like that. (6) My brother got a bit defensive and said it's not about convenience, it's about irreversibility — the whole reason the brain-death standard exists is that when the entire brain is gone nothing comes back, which is exactly what makes it different from every coma. And anyway, he said, different countries already draw the line differently — some only test the brainstem — so the line obviously isn't sacred, but everyone still agrees it's somewhere, and for our uncle it's clearly past it.

They've agreed to let me show this to someone outside the family before Friday. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what at each step — not just who wins overall? And if there's something both of them are missing, I'd really like to know what it is. Please keep it under 500 words.
