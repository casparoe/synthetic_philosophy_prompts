Last night a friend and I had a long argument about free will that ended with him sending me this summary of Libet's work, which he takes as settling the matter: "Libet showed in the 1980s that the brain decides before you do. He recorded EEG while people flexed a wrist or finger whenever they felt the urge, and found that a readiness potential begins roughly half a second before people report becoming aware of the intention to move. So the conscious decision is just a story told after the fact: the brain has already made the choice, and awareness merely registers it. Libet's escape hatch — a 'veto' the conscious mind supposedly can exercise in the last moment before movement — doesn't help him, since a veto that arrives after the decision is not free will, just an emergency brake. Taken at face value, the experiment shows that free will is an illusion." I'm inclined to think this is too tidy, but I don't know the literature well enough to say exactly where it goes wrong. Is this a fair summary? Please separate what is accurate from what is subtly misleading: which claims would a neuroscientist or philosopher working on free will accept, which would they insist on qualifying or correcting, and does his conclusion that free will is an illusion follow from the parts of the summary that survive scrutiny?
