Longtime lurker of OB and LW here -- been following the infinite ethics threads since back when they were mostly arguing about Pascal's mugging. My two friends (met at an EA meetup, naturally) have been going at infinitarian paralysis for like a year now and finally agreed to do an adversarial collaboration. They asked me to draft the joint statement but I keep slanting it toward my own side, so you're doing it instead.

Take the role of a professional philosopher who works on population ethics and decision theory, and who has also read every single LessWrong post on infinite ethics and knows exactly how the blog-level arguments go. Don't just start writing the document -- do some actual brainstorming first about where these two genuinely agree, where the disagreement is load-bearing, and where they're probably just using words differently. You don't need to show me all the brainstorming, but include the parts that end up mattering for how you phrase things.

The two camps:

Camp A (call her a totalist): welfare aggregates additively, period. Infinite worlds where the sum doesn't converge are a technical problem to be solved by choice of aggregation rule (ordering-dependent sums, hyperreal values, whatever), not a reason to abandon the total view. She suspects every proposed fix is fine and we just haven't picked one.

Camp B (a lexical asymmetrist): he thinks the whole mess is evidence that additive agregation was always a convenient approximation that only works in finite domains. His own view: suffering reduction has lexical priority, specifically eliminating the worst remaining suffering dominates any amount of mild-welfare gain, so worlds are ranked by minimizing the worst suffering first, then the next-worst, and so on. His claim, and this is the part I've never seen anyone else make: that ordering stays well-defined even on infinite worlds (given some fixed way of making the sequence of comparisons), which is why he thinks infinities are a problem for the totalist specifically and not for ethics in general.

The deliverable is the joint statement itself:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section: what both can sign about the math (e.g., what actually goes wrong with infinite sums, why "the total" is undefined rather than zero or infinite), and about what the standard proposed fixes (ordering-dependent aggregation, hyperreals, discounting by causal reach) actually deliver and what they smuggle in.

2. A disagreements table. Every row needs to be phrased so the person on that side would accept the wording of their own position. Watch especially the case where both worlds have unbounded-below worst suffering -- does his ordering handle that, or does "eliminate the worst first" fail to even refer when there is no worst? If his view stumbles there, the doc has to say so in words he'd still sign.

3. The cruxes. I suspect one is whether the undefined-ness of the infinite total is a property of the world or of our representation of it, another is whether lexical priority is itself just an ordering-dependent aggregation rule wearing a disguise, and a third is whether intuitions about infinite cases carry any evidential weight at all given we evolved in a finite world. Correct me where I'm wrong and add cruxes I've missed.

4. For each crux, at least one argument, case, or thought experiment both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Invent cases freely -- new thought experiments are encouraged, I don't want recycled ones, and no citations (we'll add sources ourselves).

5. If part of their dispute turns out to be verbal, say which part and why.

One extra demand from the asymmetrist: he wants the doc to record explicitly whether the totalist concedes that asymmetry-based views get some immunity to infinite-case problems that the total view lacks, or whether she thinks the immunity is fake. Whatever the honest answer is, phrase it so both can sign it.

End with two short sections: how confident you are in the various claims in the doc (be honest about which parts are solid reasoning vs. vibes), and the single point you're least sure about -- if you can name a weak spot in the whole framing of their dispute, name it.

Ground rules: no strawmen -- if a position sounds dumb when you phrase it, you phrased it wrong. Refer to positions by description, not by who holds them. And keep it usable: this doc is supposed to be something a research fund could actually read and learn the state of the debate from, not a victory lap for either side.
