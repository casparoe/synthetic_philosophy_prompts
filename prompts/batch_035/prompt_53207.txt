I do ML work at a company that builds resume-screening models for employers, and I keep noticing something odd about how we use the word "bias" internally. When the model tilts towards male-coded hobbies or names, we call that bias and strip it out. When it tilts towards people with longer work histories, we call that a legitimate preference and keep it. When it prefers graduates of fancy universities, we've gone back and forth — one sprint that was "class bias" to remove, the next it was a signal we didn't want to lose. And the "fairness" choices themselves (which attributes count as protected, which proxies we hunt down) were made by a room of engineers voting, which is to say, by their preferences.

What view of the difference between a preference and a bias does this whole pipeline actually presuppose? Is it just "a bias is a preference the law forbids", or is there some claim that our kept preferences track real candidate quality while the removed ones don't — and if so, where does that ground truth come from? Does the practice need moral realism about merit, or could you run the same pipeline as a constructivist? I'd also like to know which bits are genuinely presupposed versus where the practice is underdetermined, and what we'd have to change if we concluded there's no fact of the matter about who's better, only choices. Please answer in British English.
