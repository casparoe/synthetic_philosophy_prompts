Next month I defend a dissertation chapter on quantum immortality before a departmental seminar composed mostly of metaphysicians of personal identity, several of whom work in the Parfit tradition on fission and survival, together with one formal epistemologist and one philosopher of physics who is sympathetic to many-worlds but hostile to decision-theoretic hand-waving. My thesis is that the quantum suicide argument fails neither on physics nor on branching metaphysics but on a distinction I introduce between lucky and sustained survival: across a lifetime of decohering hazards, the total Born-weight of branches in which one keeps escaping death decays without bound, and the residual weight concentrates on increasingly degraded survivors, so the promised immortality is in fact a guarantee of progressive helplessness rather than persistence. I argue, against the metaphysicians in the room, that whether fission preserves identity is beside the point, since the failure is purely measure-theoretic. Please draft my Q&A prep sheet: the objections this particular audience will actually raise, ordered by how much damage they would do, phrased in their vocabulary rather than in the idiom of online Everettianism; each with a two-minute reply and a note on where the reply is soft; what I should concede up front about fission cases rather than fight; and the question I am hoping nobody asks.
