I spent my career in evolutionary biology, where the standing quarrel was between those who believe that replaying the tape of life would produce something broadly similar each time -- eyes, streamlined swimmers, social intelligence -- and those who believe the outcome is hostage to accidents of the founding population. Since retiring I have been reading the historians and economists on path dependence, and before I trust any of their particular stories about lock-in, I want the specification drawn up properly. So write the desiderata: exactly five requirements, each a sentence or two, that any adequate theory of technological or institutional lock-in must meet -- the phenomena it has to explain (why a merely adequate standard can displace better rivals; why some lock-ins eventually break and others never do), the distinctions it must respect (between lock-in and mere persistence, between being first and being best, between the contingency of an outcome's arrival and of its eventual form), and the constraints it cannot contradict, including whatever balance of convergence and contingency we actually observe in evolutionary history. Within the five items themselves, flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, since I want no introduction or conclusion. And because I have no patience for overreach, make the final item's last sentence name the requirement you are least confident belongs on the list at all, and state plainly how confident you are in the other four.
