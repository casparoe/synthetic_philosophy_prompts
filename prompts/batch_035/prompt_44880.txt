I am drafting a paper on the lottery paradox, and before I commit to any solution I want the specification a solution must meet. Write me the desiderata: what an adequate account of rational acceptance — or, more generally, of the relation between degrees of belief and outright belief — has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement. The core cases the account must handle are, first, the lottery itself: why I am reasonable to accept that my one-in-a-million ticket will lose, and why the same reasoning cannot be aggregated across all million tickets into accepting that every ticket loses; second, the preface paradox, where an author who has individually verified each claim of her book can still reasonably accept that the book contains at least one error — and note that the account must not dissolve the preface by appeal to inductive evidence about books in general, since the paradox survives when each claim is individually checked. Beyond those two, I want desiderata for the less-discussed structural cases: iterated lotteries, where closure over accepted lottery propositions compounds over time into absurdity if the account permits any lottery acceptance at all; conjunction over independent high-probability claims that are not lottery propositions, where intuition goes the other way and agglomeration seems fine; and the contrast case where, after the draw, I come to know my ticket lost — the same probability that failed to support acceptance before the draw supports knowledge after, so the account must locate what changed without smuggling in a time-indexed fudge.

Then there are the constraints from the surrounding architecture: the account must respect closure of rational acceptance under conjunction or say exactly which restricted closure principle survives, and it must not merely relabel the credence — an account on which accepting p just is having credence above a number has to earn its keep by explaining why that number does the work, and the desiderata should be stated so they do not beg the question against it. My own working picture, which I want included among the desiderata rather than treated as settled: outright belief functions as a lossy interface over credences — the format in which they are stored, transmitted, and used for action — so the account should say something about what fidelity the lossy encoding must preserve, e.g. that the interface must not systematically mislead downstream action in the way naive thresholding arguably does when it flattens 0.999 into 1. Whether that constraint can be made precise is part of what I want assessed; treat it as negotiable but take it seriously.

For each requirement, say what motivates it and whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of one camp — and flag explicitly which subsets of the desiderata are jointly unsatisfiable, since I take it that closure plus single-case high-probability acceptance plus aggregation is already inconsistent, in which case the real content of a solution is its choice of which leg to break, and the desiderata list should make those choices visible rather than hiding them. Finally, briefly: how the three standard escape routes — dropping closure, dropping the threshold, dropping the idea that rational acceptance is all-or-nothing — do against your list. Keep the weight on the criteria; the scorecard is for me to argue with.
