I'm a PhD student in philosophy of science, and I've been trying to work out where I actually stand on the dispute between etiological and causal-role accounts of biological function by listing my gut verdicts on cases first and worrying about theory second. A healthy heart: has the function of pumping blood, obviously. Goosebumps in humans: I want to say they have no function at all — not that they have the function of raising fur and happen to fail at it. Feathers in modern birds: flight is among their functions, even though flight is not what feathers originally evolved for. A chimp who invents termite-fishing with a stick: the behavior has no function the first time she does it, but it acquires the function of termite extraction once others learn it and it persists because it pays. A device that a freak accident assembles out of spare parts in a hospital storeroom, which then regulates a patient's heartbeat: I feel it genuinely has the function of regulating the heartbeat. A tumor that by chance secretes a hormone the patient lacks: no function, even though its contribution to the organism is just as systemic and just as needed. A screwdriver used for a decade to pry open paint cans: prying is still not its function. Neuroscientists saying the function of a visual area is edge detection, with no story about its selection history: perfectly legitimate function talk. My tentative principle is roughly the etiological one — a thing's function is the effect that explains why it is there — but that principle seems to get goosebumps wrong (our having goosebumps is explained by ancestral fur-raising, so it apparently entails that they do have the fur-raising function, which I denied) and the accidental pacemaker wrong (nothing about heartbeat regulation explains why it exists, so it apparently entails no function, which I also denied). Can the principle be repaired so that it saves those verdicts, perhaps by complicating it, or should some of my verdicts be overturned instead? If you think a verdict has to go, say which and why. Please also tell me what your repaired principle predicts for two cases I haven't listed: a vestigial organ reduced to the point where surgically removing it is harmless, and a synthetic gene circuit inserted into gut bacteria that fortuitously neutralizes a toxin its designers never targeted, and on which the colony has now come to depend.
