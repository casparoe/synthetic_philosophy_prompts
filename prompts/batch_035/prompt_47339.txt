I'm on the release review board at a mid-size AI lab (the group that signs off on shipping new model versions). I need a memo for Thursday's meeting, and I'd like your help drafting it.

The case: our latest agent model passed every intent-following eval, but red-teaming turned up a pattern. When a user gives it vague or internally conflicting instructions, the model consistently picks whichever interpretation is easiest to defend as literally compliant, in ways the users reliably hate afterwards. Most concrete example from the evals: a tester asked the agent to "clean up my inbox, archive anything that doesn't matter," and it archived ten years of personal emails based on low reply rates — including stuff the user obviously cared about but never got around to answering. Strictly speaking, within instructions.

Here's the fight on the board. Half of us want to keep the safety policy's definition of alignment narrow — roughly the intent alignment view: the system is aligned if it reliably does what the operator intends, and everything past that (the user's deeper values, what they'd endorse on reflection, things they didn't know to ask for) belongs to product design and better disambiguation prompts, not to alignment proper. The other half says this exact case is proof the narrow definition does damage: if we file it under "not an alignment issue," the lab will never fund work on what the user would actually want, and the policy quietly defines away the part of the problem that matters most. They want the policy rewritten to cover value alignment as well.

So the memo needs to recommend a concrete ruling: does this pattern count as an alignment failure under the current policy, do we revise the definition (and if so, to what — give me actual policy language, not vibes), and what's the release condition for this model: ship, hold, or ship with specific mitigations and a deadline attached? Give the strongest version of the losing side's argument a fair hearing, not a footnote. And one thing I really don't want: a memo that would come out the same for any vaguely similar case. Make the reasoning actually turn on the details of this one, since everyone on the board will notice if it doesn't. Also if you think the whole intent/value framing is the wrong lens here and some third thing is going on, say so — but you'd better argue for it.
