I'm a compiler engineer who has also spent some time with proof assistants — enough that I think in terms of elaboration, kernels, and trusted cores, though I'm fluent in compilers and only conversant in the theorem-proving side. I'd like you to take Lewis Carroll's regress from What the Tortoise Said to Achilles (Mind, April 1895) and restate it in the vocabulary I actually use: bootstrapping, trusted computing bases, LCF-style small kernels, towers of interpreters and meta-circularity, hardware trust. In Carroll's story, the tortoise accepts the premises of an argument, accepts the hypothetical that if those premises hold then the conclusion follows, and even accepts further hypotheticals of ever-higher order — yet refuses to draw the conclusion, and Achilles cannot force the final step by adding any proposition to the list. Translate that regress into my terms as faithfully as you can; do not smuggle in engineering assumptions and present them as translation. Then give me the residue report: which parts of the regress map cleanly onto the tower of verifiers, which survive only with distortion, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all.

I especially want your view on three things. First, in computing, the regress of what-checks-the-checker is terminated by fiat: a small kernel whose correctness we do not derive but simply trust, and beneath that, silicon we do not formalize at all. The tortoise seems to refuse precisely this move — when Achilles finally demands that the conclusion simply be drawn, the tortoise treats the demand as one more proposition to add to the stock of premises. Is the small-kernel answer an answer to the tortoise, a refusal of the tortoise's terms, or a change of subject? Second, something I struggle to say in my own vocabulary: whatever trust I place in the kernel is, on inspection, trust that a certain physical process will behave as specified — acceptance as an operation rather than as a proposition. If that's right, the regress ends in something that is not inference at all, and I can't tell whether the philosophical literature on the tortoise would count that as progress or as conceding the tortoise's point. Third, tell me whether the residue points to something my vocabulary is missing or to something in the way the regress is usually stated that is less clear than it looks. I half-suspect the whole puzzle amounts to the observation that a proof system cannot validate its own soundness, dressed in dialogue form — but I'd like to know precisely what, if anything, I'd be dismissing.
