I'm a journalist who mostly writes longread features on science and ideas, and my next piece is about whether an AI system could ever be said to know anything in the way a person does. Every formal epistemologist I interview eventually drags me to the same wall: in the standard frameworks, an ideal knower is stipulated to believe every logical consequence of their beliefs, which means they believe all tautologies and never contradict themselves, and obviously nothing that actually exists — not a person, not a supercomputer, not my editor — is remotely like that. They call this the problem of logical omniscience, and everybody seems to have their own patch for it, from impossible worlds to awareness conditions to just shrugging and calling it an idealization like frictionless planes. Before I quote a single one of these people, I want to get clear on the spec: what would an adequate theory of belief or knowledge for a non-ideal agent actually have to deliver?

So write me the desiderata. The cases it has to get right — say, the amateur chess player who knows the rules but doesn't see the mate in three they've effectively committed to believing in, or the mathematician who believes a set of axioms and hasn't yet worked out whether some conjecture follows from them — and the distinctions it can't collapse, like implicit versus explicit belief, or "this is a norm for ideal agents" versus "this is a description of actual ones." I'm also curious whether you think some of the demands pull against each other, e.g. can you have a theory that's both closed enough to do logic with and loose enough to model finite creatures, and if some subset of the desiderata is provably unsatisfiable together I want to hear that argued, not just asserted. Then, briefly and without letting it take over, say which single requirement on your list knocks out the most fashionable fixes, and why.

Write it as continuous prose only, the way you'd write a long email to a colleague — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables. And keep the weight on the criteria and the motivating cases rather than on naming lots of authors, I'll do the name-dropping myself in the piece.
