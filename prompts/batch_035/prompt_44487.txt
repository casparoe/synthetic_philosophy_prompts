so last night my friend M. and i got into a three hour argument about whether the Renaissance even happened. sounds dumb but it wasn't — by the end we realized we agreed on basically every fact (yes, humanists rediscovered texts, yes, printing mattered, yes, perspectival painting is a thing) and were still somehow miles apart, and neither of us could figure out what the actual disagreement WAS. she kept saying "the period is a construct" and i kept saying "sure but so is every period, that doesn't make it fake" and we just went in circles.

i want to write up a decision tree for my blog (readers are history-adjacent nerds, think r/AskHistorians regulars, not professionals) that starts someone at "i accept that there were big changes in European intellectual life between roughly 1300 and 1700" and walks them to a position on whether the Middle Ages / Renaissance / Scientific Revolution are real divisions or later inventions. the point of the argument was that labels hide the actual commitments, so i want the tree built from the logical space, not from a list of isms — figure out the questions first, then locate the positions in the resulting space.

some cases i want worked into the branch nodes, because these are the places me and M. actually came apart:

- does your periodization of "medieval vs renaissance" survive being applied to Song China or the Islamic golden age at all, or is it like time zones — a convention that only looks natural if you stand in Europe
- the medieval universities and scholastic natural philosophy that clearly run right through the "renaissance" without noticing it — does continuity across a boundary embarrass your view or just shrink the period
- is a period more like a biological species (real joints in nature, discoverable, possibly mislabeled) or like a county line (real in the sense of being on maps, but you'd never infer it from the terrain)
- the fact that the standard narrative carves at the *outcomes* we care about now — should the history of who ended up winning the science game determine the boundaries, or is that presentist teleology
- if tomorrow every historian agreed the labels mislead, would you say we discovered a fact, changed a convention, or just changed what we care about

leaves should include at least: the hard discontinuist (the breaks are real and roughly where the names say), the boundary-revisionist (real clustering, wrong lines — someone who'd redraw everything), the pragmatist/toolbox position (periods are instruments, "real" is a category mistake), and the eliminativist (the terms distort so badly they should just be retired). if there's a coherent combo of answers nobody actually holds, say so and say whether you think it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

flag any two leaves the tree genuinely can't separate — i suspect the boundary-revisionist and the pragmatist end up looking identical from the outside unless you ask the right question, and if that's true i want it admitted rather than papered over. and phrase every node so a reader can answer it without already knowing which camp it feeds — me and M. both know the vocabulary and it just gave us more words to be confused with. roughly 6-8 branch questions feels right, but go bigger if the space needs it.
