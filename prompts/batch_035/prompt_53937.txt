At his 2005 confirmation hearings, John Roberts said that judges are like umpires: "Umpires don't make the rules, they apply them... Nobody ever went to a ball game to see the umpire," adding at the close that it would be his job "to call balls and strikes, and not to pitch or bat." The analogy has been doing heavy work ever since: it amounts to a compressed brief for textualism, on which constitutional adjudication is the application of fixed rules rather than the purposivist's hunt for what the provisions were meant to achieve. I'm a longtime reader of the rationalist blogosphere, where "a rule doesn't interpret its own applications" gets deployed in every rule-following thread, so my instinct is that the umpire is the wrong sort of authority for the conclusion Roberts wanted — but I'd like this done properly rather than by slogan. Please audit the analogy. Set out the correspondence table: what plays the role of the strike zone, the rulebook, the pitch, the scoreboard, the replay review. Then sort the disanalogies into those that matter for the intended use and those that don't — I can see several candidates and want them handled carefully: the baseball rulebook defines the zone in terms that leave little to interpretation, yet the rules committee redraws it from time to time, while umpires' ball-strike calls are measurably swayed by the catcher's framing and by the count; and baseball, unlike the Constitution, can install a camera system to call the pitches, which it has begun doing at some levels. Say what the analogy smuggles in about what makes a call correct (does the umpire's decision make it true that the pitch was a strike, or does it answer to something outside the decision?), and whether Roberts's conclusion — judging as application, not policymaking — survives the disanalogies or holds only for the class of cases on which textualists and purposivists already agree. If some rival analogy serves better (Dworkin's chain novel, the executor of a will, a map corrected by successive surveyors), say which and why. Please answer in British English.
