I'm currently taking a course in social epistemology, and we just covered the distinction between epistemic bubbles and echo chambers drawn in C. Thi Nguyen's work. As I understand it, an epistemic bubble is an informational network in which relevant voices have been left out, whereas an echo chamber is one in which those outside voices get in but are preemptively discredited. My confusion is that the two phenomena look like the same thing differing only in degree: an echo chamber seems to be nothing more than an especially efficient epistemic bubble, one whose members have filtered out contrary voices so thoroughly that outside evidence no longer registers as evidence at all. If that's right, the remedy should be identical in both cases — just keep supplying the excluded information until some of it gets through. Yet we were told that exposure-based fixes that pop epistemic bubbles fail against echo chambers, which strikes me as showing that the distinction is ad hoc and the framework does no real work: labeling the exclusion "active discrediting" rather than "omission" shouldn't change what anyone should actually do about it. Why would it? Please answer in American English.
