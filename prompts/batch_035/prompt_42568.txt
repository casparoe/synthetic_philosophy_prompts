Been chewing on uploading stuff for years (Chalmers fading qualia post, the whole LessWrong copy wars) and I finally wrote down where I actually stand so people can poke at it. Here's my package:

1. Functionalism, pretty hardline: what makes a mind a mind is the functional organization, not the substrate. Same organization running in silicon, same mind.

2. Gradual replacement is fine. Swap my neurons out one at a time for chips that do exactly what each neuron did, and I stay me the whole way — no fading qualia, no moment where I go missing. Chalmers convinced me of this ages ago.

3. Destructive scan-upload is death. If you vaporize my brain after scanning it and run the emulation, that's a new guy with my memories. I wouldnt do it, no matter how good the scan.

4. What makes the gradual case work is causal continuity: each stage of my brain gives rise to the next. Personal identity is that continuity, not pattern similarity — otherwise the scan in 3 would be survival too.

5. If instead of replacing neurons you duplicate them — half of them gradually copied over to a second body while the originals keep running — I don't think I end up as two people. At most one of the resulting people is me, and I keep flip-flopping between "there's no fact about which one" and "neither, this is just death with extra steps."

6. Pausing the finished emulation for a decade and resuming it is fine, that's still me later. Also the gradual replacement is fine even if I'm under general anesthesia the whole time.

Does this hang together? I suspect 1+2+4 quietly commit me to saying the scan-upload in 3 is me after all, which I really don't want to say. And I can't tell if 5 and 6 are consistent with 4 or whether I'm just handwaving about what "continuity" means. Go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank them by severity, and say what the cheapest repair is and what it costs me. Also please keep straight what's actually settled here (e.g. what the fading qualia arguement does and doesn't establish) versus what's just one camp's view — I know personal identity is a swamp and I don't want confident-sounding orthodoxy where there isn't any.
