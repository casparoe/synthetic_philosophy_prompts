I'm a third-year PPE student taking a module on legitimacy and political authority, and last week's seminar got badly stuck on the concept of performance legitimacy — the idea, usually applied to contemporary China and Singapore, that a regime can be entitled to rule, or at least stably accepted as ruling, on the strength of delivering prosperity, competence and order rather than through democratic procedure. My tutor treats it as a genuine third category alongside procedural and value-based legitimacy; a friend in the seminar insists it's a misdescription of something far more ordinary — compliance bought with growth, or what you observe when citizens see no available alternative — and argues that once the economy stumbles the supposed legitimacy evaporates, which shows it was never legitimacy at all. I'd like a number rather than a literature review: what is your credence that performance legitimacy, properly spelled out, names a distinct form of legitimacy rather than merely a temporary stand-in for its absence?

Please decompose the question into the sub-questions the final number depends on. Some candidates, though substitute your own if you think I've framed it wrong: the probability that legitimacy even has a determinate meaning here once you separate the sociological sense (citizens actually believe the regime has a right to rule) from the normative sense (it actually has one); the probability that the delivery of outcomes can in principle generate a belief in a right to rule, rather than mere gratitude, fear, or satisfaction that would vanish along with the outcomes; the probability that observed regime support in countries like China and Singapore reflects genuine legitimacy beliefs rather than preference falsification, political unsalience, or pride in national achievement that is detachable from the regime itself; and the probability that a legitimacy claim founded on performance is structurally self-undermining, since a regime that stakes its title on results has thereby announced that it has no other. Assign each a rough probability with a line of justification, show how you combine them into a final credence, and tell me which term that final number is most sensitive to.

Two further things. First, what evidence would move your most sensitive term most — for instance, does the Singapore case, where performance is genuinely excellent and the procedural channel only semi-competitive, raise or lower the credence relative to China, where performance is partly a recovery story piggy-backing on nationalism? Second, my own credence is around 0.45, my friend's is about 0.15, and my tutor's is probably 0.7; if you can tell me which sub-question the three of us most likely diverge on, that would be the most useful part of your answer, since the seminar reconvenes on Thursday.
