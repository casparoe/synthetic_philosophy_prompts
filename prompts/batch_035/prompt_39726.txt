Me and a friend write a small newsletter on philosophy of medicine, and we want to do one of those "which position do you secretly hold?" quizzes, but about the concept of disease — the naturalism vs normativism fight and its neighbors. The point is to sort readers by how they react to cases, not by which label they like, so no question should just ask "do you think disease is value-free?" — everyone says they're a reasonable hybrid when you ask directly.

Write 9 questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. Please make sure these cases are among them (you pick the exact wording): (1) aging itself — universal, evolved, and bad for you; (2) a village where literally everyone has a goiter from iodine-poor water, so enlarged thyroids are just what thyroids do there; (3) a woman with tetrachromatic vision who sees color distinctions nobody else can, which is rare but if anything a perk; (4) someone who's been profoundly deaf since birth, doesn't experience it as a loss, and is part of a signing community that resists calling it a disorder; (5) intense grief after a partner's death that lasts just past the window where the DSM's bereavement exclusion would have blocked a depression diagnosis; (6) short stature in a kid who's the shortest in his class but healthy by every lab measure, where the parents want growth hormone purely so he stops getting picked on. Three more cases of your choosing — I want the logical space covered, not just greatest hits.

Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto: something like Boorse's biostatistical view (disease = subnormal part-function relative to a reference class, no values sneaking in), Wakefield's harmful-dysfunction account (evolutionary dysfunction PLUS harm, where harm is judged by values), a Nordenfelt-style account (health as the ability to achieve your vital goals, so the agent's own situation matters), outright normativism/constructivism about disease labels, and a "there's no single concept here, it's a family resemblance mess" leaf. Scoring should allow messy results — a reader can score 40% Boorse, 40% Wakefield, 20% constructivist — that's fine and honestly realistic, the key should say what the mixtures mean.

Two more things: flag which pairs of positions the quiz genuinely cannot seperate even in principle (I suspect biostatistical vs harmful-dysfunction mostly agree except on aging-like cases — tell me if that's right and what single question would pull them apart if it is), and flag which answer combinations are internally incoherent versus which are actual defensible hybrids. If some combination is unoccupied in the literature, say whether that's because it's a bad view or just because nobody bothered.

Audience is newsletter readers with zero philosophy background, so keep the case descriptions self-contained — assume they've never heard of reference classes or evolutionary functions, the quiz has to teach just enough on the fly. Typos in the questions are fine, we're not fancy.
