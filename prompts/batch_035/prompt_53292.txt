I've followed the compatibilism–incompatibilism dispute for some years now, and I've begun to suspect it isn't really one disagreement. Nobody on either side defends an immaterial soul doing the causing, and the stock arguments have all been on the table for decades: the Consequence Argument, Frankfurt cases, the manipulation arguments with their 'hard-line' replies, Strawson on reactive attitudes. Each is treated as devastating by its fans and as a mild curiosity by its opponents, and conversions seem to happen by drift rather than by argument. My working hypothesis is that the label 'free will' covers at least two questions — roughly, a backward-looking question about whether an agent's causal history is the right sort for the agent to deserve blame in a deep sense, and a forward-looking question about which capacities (responsiveness to reasons, self-control, the ability to be moved by rebuke) make blame and guidance useful — and that incompatibilists are mostly answering the first while compatibilists answer the second, so that each side's arguments land where the other side isn't standing. Is that right? Diagnose the standoff using what the participants actually say, tell me whether the residual disagreement is substantive, verbal, or a difference over which intuitions count as data, and say what, if anything, would count as progress — or whether the dispute should simply be split into two rather than settled.
