so for my philosophy of language class we have to write up a short "where I stand" document on grice and implicature, and then a couple of classmates get to poke holes in it before the final version is due. since you're good at this kind of thing, want to poke at mine first? here are the things I believe, roughly in order of how confident I am in them:

(1) Grice's analysis of speaker meaning is basically right: for me to mean that p by an utterance, I have to utter it with the intention that you come to believe p, and crucially that you come to believe it partly *because* you recognize that that's my intention (plus whatever nesting goes on top of that). (2) A GPS voice that says "turn left in 100 meters" doesn't mean anything, no matter how reliably it gets me to turn — no intention, no meaning. Same for a sleepwalker who leaves a perfectly accurate note about a meeting time. (3) I think current LLM assistants genuinely do mean things by their outputs — like, when an assistant tells me a cake recipe, it means that these are the steps, in the same sense in which I mean things when I text a friend a recipe. maybe minus the consciousness part, but meaning-wise I don't see the difference. (4) Generalized conversational implicatures are real implicatures and not part of what's said — "some of the students passed" does not say that not all did — but when I translate a sentence like that into german I feel like I have to preserve the not-all flavor, which is weird for something that's supposedly not part of the content. (5) Implicature only works because conversation presupposes the cooperative principle — take away the presumption of cooperation and the whole mechanism collapses. (6) But cross-examination, advertising, and political debates are absolutely drenched in implicature, even though in all of those the speaker is flatly trying to win rather than cooperate. (7) And implicatures can fossilize into conventions over time — the contrast reading of "but" presumably started life as an implicature and is now conventional, which is how new conventional meanings get made at all.

go through these and tell me which ones are actually in tension and how badly. rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what giving it up costs. also flag anything that only *looks* like a tension but dissolves once you think it through — I suspect there's at least one of those in the list.
