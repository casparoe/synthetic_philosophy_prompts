I do ops and volunteering for a tiny animal-welfare group that's recently gotten into wild animals instead of just factory farming, and honestly the more i read about the habitat question the more confused I get. The setup as I understand it: lots of wild animals are r-strategists -- fish, rodents, insects -- huge numbers of offspring, most of whom die very young, plausibly painfully (starvation, cold, being eaten alive, suffocating in the egg before they even hatch, whatever). If most lives in nature are net-negative, then more habitat = more suffering, and destroying habitat (draining a marsh, clearing forest for pasture) could reduce aggregate suffering even while being terrible for every other conservation value. Some people in our orbit take this seriously enough to argue against donating to land trusts; others think the arguement is insane.

I've got maybe one part-time researcher with a bio background and a small grant, call it 40 hours a month of real thinking time, and I want it to go to the open questions that actually decide this, not to literature surveys. My current list of candidate questions, roughly:

1. Whether the average wild animal life is net-positive or net-negative in welfare terms, and whether that's even measurable in principle.
2. Whether habitat destruction actually reduces total suffering in expectation, given that disturbed/edge habitats can support MORE total r-strategists than mature ones -- my understanding is an old-growth forest might house fewer animals than the scrubby farmland that replaces it, in which case the sign flips depending on what kind of habitat you're destroying.
3. Whether predation is net good or bad for aggregate welfare at the population level (predators reduce numbers but might make deaths quicker than starvation).
4. How much we should even care about small-brained animals (insects, fish larvae) -- the sentience uncertainty question -- and how to combine that moral uncertainty with the empirical welfare estimates above.
5. Whether ecosystem-level talk ("healthy ecosystems", "balance of nature") corresponds to anything welfare-wise at all, or is just a conservation-biology aesthetic we're smuggling in.

Rank these (or swap out ones you think are wrong, or add what's missing), but the ranking is the least of it -- I want the reasoning: which of these is upstream of the others, which are tractable for a part-timer rather than a full lab, and which we should deprioritize because even a clean answer wouldn't change what my org does. Also push back if you think the whole framework of adding up welfare across a gazillion barely-sentient lives is broken in some way -- e.g. if the population ethics (is a world with more marginally-net-positive lives better?) is doing more load-bearing work than any of the empirical stuff on my list, since no amount of field biology settles that.

End with a final line in exactly this form so I can parse it: \answer{top three question numbers in priority order, like \answer{2, 4, 1}}. If you think one of my candidates is a waste of time, don't include it, and if you added a new question, give it a number and use that.
