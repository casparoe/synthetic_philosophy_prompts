I've been diagnosed with a progressive neurological condition that will destroy my brain over the next decade, and my clinic now offers, at end stage, either gradual neural replacement with functionally identical prosthetic components or a destructive whole-brain scan with subsequent emulation. I have to decide what to put in my advance directive, and before I do I want to know whether the views I've arrived at even hang together:

(1) I'm a functionalist: what makes a mental state the state it is — including my conscious experiences — is its causal-functional role, not its substrate, and I see no principled barrier to the right roles being realized in silicon.
(2) I believe a gradual, neuron-at-a-time replacement with functionally identical devices, with me awake and lucid throughout, would be me continuing, and I'd consent to it without much hesitation.
(3) I believe a destructive scan followed by an exact emulation would kill me: the emulation would be conscious, would have my memories, personality, and projects, and would be owed everything I'm owed — but it would be someone else, and I would be dead.
(4) I'm largely persuaded by Parfit that personal identity is not what matters in survival; what matters is psychological continuity and connectedness. In particular, I accept that in fission cases — two later people each psychologically continuous with me, as with a split-brain-style division — I don't literally survive, and that this should trouble me less than I instinctively feel it should.
(5) I'm confident that consciousness would be preserved through gradual replacement: I reject the possibility of my experiences slowly fading in intensity while no one, myself included, notices anything.

Which of these commitments are in genuine tension, which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, and what would the cheapest revision be? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging, identify the pair of commitments where I'm implicitly helping myself to something the others rule out, and tell me which single revision would leave me with a coherent position — one way or the other — on what to sign.
