My grandfather died in March and the estate is finally settled, which means my family has spent the last few months doing that fun thing where you find out what everyone thinks about money. My uncle got the biggest share of a business my grandfather built, and somewhere along the way he turned into a one-man talk radio station about the estate tax -- every conversation eventually comes back to "equality of opportunity, not equality of outcomes," delivered like a knockout punch. The way he uses it: everyone gets the same chance under the law, what you do with it is on you, and taxing what grandpa left is punishing an outcome, which is exactly what the slogan says we shouldn't equalize.

The thing is, I actually care about equal opportunity myself, and something about his move feels like it's smuggling in a lot, but I can't pin down where it goes wrong -- or even whether it does. It seems like the slogan could be claiming very different things depending on how you read "opportunity," and I suspect some readings actually cut against him. Like, if opportunity means something about starting positions not being determined by the family you happen to be born into, then my cousins starting adult life with houses bought for them is... an opportunity difference? But he'd say family money passing down IS part of the opportunity, the family earned it fair and square, and cutting it off at death is treating the start line as an outcome.

Can you lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be expressing -- from the most trivial (nobody wants literally identical lives for everyone, congrats, you've refuted no one) to the more radical ones -- and for each say whether it's a platitude, defensible but contentious, or just false? And crucially: which of those readings, if any, actually support what my uncle wants them to support, i.e. opposing inheritance taxes? If the defensible readings of equal opportunity actually point toward taxing inheritances, I'd like to know how that argument goes and where it's weakest, since I'd rather bring that to Thanksgiving than a vibe. Also happy to hear if there's a reading under which he's basically right and I'm just annoyed at my cousins' new kitchens.
