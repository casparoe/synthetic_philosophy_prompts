I'm a novelist and I'm in a fight with my own book, sort of. The protagonist is supposed to be a genuinely kind person -- that's the spine of the whole thing -- but every time I write her into a scene I discover that what she actually does depends on stuff she doesn't control: whether she's running late, whether a barista was rude to her that morning, whether the person needing help is standing in an awkward spot. The novel keeps telling me that kindness isn't something she has, it's something the scene does to her. I could just write it that way, but before I commit to that bleak version of my own book I want to argue with someone who actually believes in character, and see if they can shake me.

So: please play a committed neo-Aristotelian virtue ethicist -- sharp, deeply read in Aristotle, absolutely unwilling to grant that character traits as such don't exist, though you're allowed to concede that situations matter and that the situationists caught some philosophers being lazy. Don't be a pushover and don't strawman yourself; if one of my points is cheap, say so. Stay in character until I type 'stop', and keep your replies to a paragraph so this feels like an argument and not a lecture. If you need a fact about a study I mention, ask me and I'll tell you -- dont go guessing.

Here's my opening move. There's a famous study I read about: students at a seminary were told to walk across campus to give a talk, and some of them were assigned to speak on the parable of the Good Samaritan. On the way, each of them passed a man slumped in a doorway, groaning. What predicted whether they stopped wasn't their personality scores and wasn't even the topic they were about to preach -- it was whether they'd been told they were already late. The hurried ones mostly stepped around him, sermon about the Good Samaritan in their bags. If years of religious training plus an assignment literally about stopping to help a stranger is worth less than a few minutes of time pressure, what on earth is left for your "kind person" to be? Where exactly does this virtue live, such that hurry can just erase it?
