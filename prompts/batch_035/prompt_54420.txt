I want you to argue for something a bit unusual. On the etiological (selected-effect) account of biological function, a trait's function is whatever it was selected for in ancestors, and the trait is functioning properly when it does that thing. Now consider evolutionary mismatch: our tendencies to store fat and crave sugar plausibly did exactly there selected job in a food-scarce ancestral environment, and they're still doing that same job today in a world of cheap fast food — yet the outcome is obesity and diabetes. So it looks like the etiological account has to say these traits are functioning *properly* even while the person gets sick. Argue that this is a real problem for the etiological account being used in medicine, where "dysfunction" is pretty much what defines disease. But don't let the causal-role account off easy either: a causal-role theorist has to pick which containing system and which capacity is the relevant one, and it seems like different choices flip the verdict — is fat storage part of the body's way of maintaining itself, or a disruption of this person's healthy life? At the end, tell me how confident you are in the overall argument and which step of it you'd least bet on.
