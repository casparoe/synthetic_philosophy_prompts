so heres my situation: I'm the ops manager at a mid-size recirculating aquaculture farm — tilapia in the main system, and we just started a pilot tank of freshwater prawns, which is a whole new can of worms welfare-wise. My techs walk the tanks twice a day and they're constantly winging it on whether something they see is a welfare problem or just normal fish stuff: an animal hanging at the surface, off its feed, swimming odd, flashing against the substrate. None of them are biologists, I don't have a vet on call (there's one I can email who replies within a day or two), and our big buyer sends a welfare auditor in the spring, so I need something written down we can point to rather than "we looked at the fish and it seemed fine." Stakes run from losing stock (money) to losing the contract if the audit goes badly.

I want a decision procedure the techs can run in a couple of minutes standing at the tank. My thought is the five domains framework (nutrition, physical environment, health, behavior, mental state) is the right backbone, plus whatever the more recent evidence on decapod sentience implies about how much more cautious we should be with the prawns versus the tilapia — I know people argued for years that crustaceans don't really feel anything and that seems to have shifted, so tell me how to factor that in without pretending certainty we don't have. The procedure needs to specify what to observe in what order, when the right move is log-and-watch versus act now versus escalate to me, and it should honestly flag which steps are just a judgment call dressed up as a checklist, because I'd rather know that than kid myself. Then run it on three cases: (1) a tilapia that's been sitting near the outflow gasping-ish for two days but still eats when food goes in; (2) the prawns in the pilot tank have started cannibalizing each other after a wave of molts, water parameters all in range; (3) one of my techs wants to add structure and hiding spots to the prawn tanks to cut down the cannibalism, and I'd love to say yes since it's cheap and he's enthusiastic — but tell me straight whether the procedure backs it, even if the answer annoys me. Last thing: be clear about what a checklist like this fundamentally can't do, given my techs see each animal for like five minutes a day and, from what I understand, fish and prawns tend to hide pain and weakness until they're really bad off.
