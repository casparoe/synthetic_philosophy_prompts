I've basically come around to Benatar's asymmetry: absent pain is good even if there's noone around to enjoy that absence, while absent pleasure isn't bad unless someone is deprived of it. I've even extended it a bit myself — under uncertainty about how a life will go, the asymmetry seems to make procreation a bet where the possible bads count fully and the possible goods count for nothing, so even a 99%-likely-wonderful life never clears the bar. What would count against this? Not the tired "you can't deprive the nonexistent" line — I want the stuff that should actually move me. Like cases where my logic forces a verdict I'd have to stomache (painlessly ending a life that contains more pain than pleasure, maybe?), or where it generalizes somewhere absurd (do we really care zero about missed goods with no subject — a great concert that never happens?). Also worth asking whether the view has been qualified so often to dodge counterexamples that it no longer does the work antinatalists actually need from it. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones asymmetry defenders would themselves concede vs. wave off, and be honest if you think the view ends up insulated — and whether that's even a problem for an evaluative view of this kind.
