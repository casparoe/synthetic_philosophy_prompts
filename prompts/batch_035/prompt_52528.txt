so for our phil of mind reading group I have to submit a little "where I stand" profile before the session on extended cognition, and I want people to be able to poke at it properly. here's the package I've landed on:

(1) I basically buy the extended mind thesis, at least in the Otto's-notebook style case: when the notebook entries are reliably there when needed, and Otto treats what's written the way a normal person treats their memories (endorsing it without checking), then some of the remembering is literally happening in the notebook. It's not just an external prompt triggering inner cognition.

(2) I also think content externalism is right — what my term "water" picks out depends on my environment, so on Twin Earth my twin (identical internal states, different surroundings) means something slightly different than I do.

(3) But I'm not remotely tempted to say the *subject* extends. There's a me who undergoes all this thinking, feeling, etc., and that thing is located in the brain. The notebook can be part of my cognitive system without any part of *me* — the one who is aware, who matters morally — being out there in the pocket.

(4) Personal identity is psychological continuity in roughly the Parfitian way. And here's where I wobble: I said in (1) that the notebook does part of the remembering, but I don't feel the pull to say that losing the notebook is like losing a chunk of hippocampus — it seems more like losing a limb you've become expert with. Though honestly I can't tell if that's a considered view or just my gut resisting a weird conclusion.

(5) I want there to be a principle difference between Otto's notebook and someone casually googling things. The parity idea ("if a process were done in the head we'd have no hesitation in calling it cognitive") seems to me to let in far too much, so I lean toward requiring genuine integration — practiced, automatic, endorsement-without-checking — rather than bare functional equivalence.

(6) Oh and one more: I think everything about the mind that matters scientifically is fixed by functional/computational role, not by substrate — neurons, silicon, paper and ink, whatever, so long as the right organization is there.

So, is this consistent? My hunches: (3) and (6) can't both be true, since if all that matters is functional role then nothing privileges the brain as the seat of the subject. Maybe (2) is either irrelevant to the rest or secretly doing a lot of work, I genuinely cant tell which. And is (4)'s wobble just (1) coming back to bite me? Please go through it like an audit — order the tensions by severity, say for each one what would have to give, and flag which ones only *look* like tensions but dissolve on inspection. Also if one cheap revision would clean up most of it, tell me which. dont be gentle, it's going up in front of the whole group.
