I'm on the founding board of a housing co-op, and we're writing the bylaws before we let anyone else in. A few of us want to bake in rules that are deliberately hard to change — like requiring a 80% supermajority plus a two-year waiting period before you can touch anything about how common spaces are used or how members get expelled. The idea is that somewhere down the line a panicked or faction-captured membership could gut the protections, so we want to bind them in advance. But my friend on the board thinks this is self-defeating: if the rules are good people will keep them anyway, and if they're bad, why should the dead hands of the founding members control the living? He also says it's arrogant to assume we know better than our future selves, who'll have way more experience running the place than we do. And honestly there's something that feels a bit off to me about voting to limit how future people can vote. Is there actually a principled case for making your own rules this hard to change, or is this just us being scared of losing an argument later? Please answer in American English.
