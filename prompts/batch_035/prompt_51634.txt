Write a satirical FAQ page, around 650 words, for a fictional government body, the Office of Intergenerational Settlement, explaining why it has permanently discontinued all consultation with future generations. The office's official reasoning is the nonidentity problem, deployed straight-faced as an administrative convenience: since any present policy shifts the timing and manner of conceptions downstream, it changes which people exist later; so no future person is ever made worse off by today's choices — those who will exist owe their existence to the very policies they might want to complain about, and cannot coherently do so. In Q&A format, the page should field questions like: "Why was the Citizens' Assembly for 2100 cancelled?"; "A woman born in 2080 may live a grim life under our depletion policies — but if she would not have existed at all under greener ones, who exactly has wronged her?"; and "Does this mean the Office cannot criticize any policy, however reckless, so long as its consequences ripple into the mating patterns of the distant future?" — with the office answering that last one, with visible relief, in the affirmative. The humor must come from getting the philosophy right: the piece should track the genuine tension between the person-affecting constraint (wrongdoing requires someone made worse off) and the stubborn intuition that we can still wrong people who owe us their existence, and it should close with the office quietly extending the logic to every remaining area of policy — since nearly everything we do affects who will later exist, nearly nothing can be complained about by anyone whose existence we have, in the office's phrase, facilitated. Keep it deadpan throughout: no winking, no exclamation points, the register of a genuinely helpful public information page, so that a population ethicist laughs rather than winces while a reader who has never heard of the problem can still follow every clause.
