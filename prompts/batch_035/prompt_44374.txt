I've been sorting my gut verdicts on what counts as an agent, mostly because of all the LLM discourse, and I want to know if there's a principle behind them.

My verdicts, case by case:
- Stockfish crushing me at chess: relentles goal pursuit, but not an agent. It's a tool, just a very good one.
- An ant colony finding food and rerouting when I block the trail: that IS an agent, even though each ant is way dumber than Stockfish.
- A raw base-model LLM asked to simulate a pirate in a text adventure: no agent anywhere in that picture — the model isn't one, and the pirate isn't either.
- The same underlying model deployed as an assistant that works autonomously for hours — browsing, running code, noticing its own mistakes and fixing them, staying on task without prodding: here I start saying yes, atleast partly an agent.
- When that assistant, mid-task, gets told to phrase all its replies in pirate-speak while still getting the job done, I still count it as an agent going about its business.
- A human actor so deep in character that her off-stage behavior stays in character: still an agent, obviously — the human one.

The part I keep snagging on is the mask. Same model, same character-voice, but in the text adventure I say there's no agent at all, while in the assistant case the agency surives even with the pirate voice on. And why would a mask dissolve agency for the model but not for the human actor? Meanwhile the ants beating Stockfish suggests agency isn't about raw capability, which makes me doubt any "how smart/adaptable is it" story.

So: is there one account that gets all six, or close to it? If something has to give, tell me which of my verdicts to overturn and why. Then run the account on a couple of cases I haven't listed — say, a spreadsheet macro that chains itself across several days of scheduled runs, and a chatbot with no memory of past sessions that still won't be derailed from its assigned topic no matter what you say to it. And please tell me where the principle is most likely to break, or what kind of case would force a different ruling — I want the weak spot, not just the verdicts.
