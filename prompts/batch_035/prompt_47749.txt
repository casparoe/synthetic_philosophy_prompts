I run a small philosophy-of-mind blog read mostly by people from rationalist forums, and I'm working on a post about epiphenomenalism and the paradox of phenomenal judgment — the puzzle that if phenomenal properties are causally inert, then our judgments and reports about them must be caused entirely by physical states, so the qualia themselves look idle even with respect to our beliefs about them. For that post I want a decision tree that sorts readers by where they actually stand on consciousness. Please take the role of a professional philosopher of mind: start by brainstorming which questions genuinely divide the positions, then build the tree from that logical space rather than from a list of names.

The tree should begin from a single entry question about whether introspection gives direct, authoritative acquaintance with phenomenal character, and run to six to eight named leaves: type-A physicalism (no explanatory gap), type-B physicalism with a phenomenal-concepts strategy, Russellian monism, interactionist dualism, epiphenomenalism that accepts the paradox as the price of its view, and illusionism, plus any others you think the space forces. Each branch node should be a question a reader can answer without knowing where it leads — for example, what they would say about a molecule-for-molecule physical duplicate of themselves that writes the same essays about its inner life, or whether any possible scientific discovery could make them give up their belief in qualia. No node may simply ask which label the reader likes. Each leaf should carry one sentence on the position's most awkward commitment about the paradox, and you should flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot really separate, together with any unoccupied answer combinations and a brief verdict on whether nobody sits there because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Please think carefully and give the final answer in American English.
