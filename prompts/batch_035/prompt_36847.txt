I'm on the steering committee of a professional society (roughly 2,500 members, we're hydrologists, if it matters) and we elect our president and four at-large board members every year by plain plurality. At the last election the winning presidential candidate got 33% with two very similar candidates splitting the rest, and members have been grumbling ever since. So at the fall meeting someone will finally move to change the voting rule, and I've been tasked with writing the recommendation memo beforehand since I'm the one who supposedly knows some decision theory.

There are three factions on the committee, and the memo needs to actually deal with all of them:

1. The IRV people, who want instant-runoff for president and single transferable vote for the board seats.
2. Our treasurer, who took one game theory course years ago and now says Arrow's impossibility theorem proves that *every* ranked-ballot method is just as arbitrary and manipulable as every other one, so switching is pointless churn and we should keep plurality, which at least everyone understands.
3. A member who wants approval voting and claims it "dodges Arrow entirely" because Arrow is only about ordinal preferences, so we'd get a fair system for free.

The vote has to be decided at the meeting in two weeks because the ballot platform order has to be locked in a month before the election.

Draft the memo I'll circulate: it needs a concrete bottom-line recommendation (pick a rule, or defend keeping plurality, or recommend something with a runoff structure — your call), the reasoning laid out so the treasurer could actually respond to it, and an honest accounting of the strongest point on whichever side loses. Please be careful about what Arrow's theorem does and doesn't say — the treasurer is misusing it somehow but I don't fully trust myself to say exactly how, e.g. whether it even applies to plurality voting as it's usually stated, and whether the approval-voting person's escape claim is real or a shell game. Also worth touching on: our members mostly vote sincerely out of habit, we've never had organized strategic voting, and last cycle only 40% bothered to vote at all — so whatever we pick, "how much does this theorem matter for an org like ours at all" is a fair question the memo shouldn't dodge. Keep it to something I can actually circulate, so under 800 words, and dont make it condescending — several of these people are smarter than me, they just havent thought about voting rules.
