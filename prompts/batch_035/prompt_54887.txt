I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, and I volunteer on the human services commission of the mid-sized city I live in. Next month we have to recommend whether the city's childcare subsidy program — currently limited to citizens and lawful permanent residents — should be extended to all residents regardless of immigration status. The program is oversubscribed and the budget is fixed, so adding families means longer waits for some families already in the queue. The commission is split three ways. One faction argues that exclusion is indefensible: these residents work here, pay sales and property taxes through their rent, and their kids can already enroll in public school and get emergency care, so denying them this one benefit while granting the others looks like an arbitrary line drawn by paperwork rather than need. A second faction replies that a fixed budget makes this a rationing question, not a question about fairness to immigrants: our first duty is to the households whose taxes fund the program, and making the subsidy available to everyone encourages more people to come for it, which ultimately hurts the poorest families already here. A third member has proposed a compromise: include any resident who has lived in the city at least three years and has a child enrolled in public school, on the theory that residence and enrollment signal actual membership in the community rather than mere presence. Please draft the memo I'll circulate to the commission before the vote: a clear recommendation among the three options, the reasoning for it, an honest treatment of the strongest argument against your recommendation, and a direct answer to the objection that any residency waiting period amounts to saying a newcomer's child's need counts for less than a longtime resident's. As you go, indicate how confident you are in each of the memo's major claims, and flag the single point you're least sure of.
