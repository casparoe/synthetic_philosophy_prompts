Hey — I'm a science journalist and I'm drafting a longread about whether "now" is a real thing. Basically every pop physics piece I read says the same thing: relativity killed the flowing present, simultaneity is relative, so the universe must be one big frozen block where the dinosaurs and your great-grandchildren all exist in exactly the same sense. Fine, that's eternalism, I get it. But last month I interviewed a cosmologist for a different story and she made a claim I can't stop chewing on: she said that because the cosmic microwave background gives the universe a natural preferred rest frame, there is actually a distinguished notion of "the same time" across the cosmos — the time you read off in the CMB's rest frame — and that presentists could use exactly that to say which slice of spacetime is the objectively current one. So the laws of physics don't pick a preferred frame, sure, but the actual distribution of matter sort of does, or at least the Big Bang's leftovers do.

I want to compare eternalism with this "CMB presentism" idea, because I suspect the comparison is more interesting than the usual presentism-vs-eternalism punch-up. A few things I'd love you to work through:

1. What does each view actually claim exists? Not the slogan version — the version where you have to say whether the CMB-rest-frame slice is ontologically special or just descriptively convenient.
2. Does the cosmologist's move really work? It seems like there's a difference between "there is a frame in which the matter of the universe happens to be, on average, at rest" and "that frame's simultaneity slices are what's real". Where exactly does that gap open up, and can the presentist bridge it? Also worth asking whether the frame is even well-defined — the universe is lumpy and expanding, matter isn't everywhere, etc.
3. The usual anti-presentist argument from special relativity (the one where two observers moving relative to each other disagree about which distant events are simultaneous, and if both slices are "the present" you've smuggled in a contradiction). Does the CMB frame let the presentist dodge that, or does the argument just get reformulated as "your preferred frame is an arbitrary relic of initial conditions"?
