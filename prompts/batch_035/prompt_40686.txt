my housemate is doing her thesis on dispositions and she trapped me with an argument I cant get out of. some background first so we're on the same page. the conditional analysis says a glass is fragile iff it would break if struck. a fink is something that, were the glass struck, would remove the fragility *first* — think a sorcerer who'd cast a spell making the glass unbreakable a split second before impact. the glass is still fragile (spell not yet cast) but the conditional comes out false. a mask is different: it leaves the fragility alone and just intercepts the process — styrofoam packing, or the airbag that lets the drunk walk away from the crash his recklessness was heading for. disposition present, conditional false again. so any analysis worth keeping has to get patched against finks and masks. ok, here's the argument:

(1) x is fragile iff x would break if struck, in circumstances S. whatever S is, it has to exclude finks and masks, or the conditional is false while the glass is plainly fragile.
(2) the only way to exclude them up front is to build into S something like "and nothing intervenes to prevent the breaking." but that just means "circumstances in which it breaks," so the analysis collapses into: x is fragile iff x would break if struck in circumstances in which it would break. circular, no independent truth conditions.
(3) alternatively you leave S loose ("struck in the normal way"). but then whether the styrofoam counts as normal gets decided by hand, and every time we rule it not-normal we've just written the answer into S again.
(4) so for any S: either S, as specified, already guarantees the breaking (circular), or it doesn't (maskable). no third option.
(5) if the conditional analysis can't be patched without circularity, fragility has no non-circular truth conditions, and "fragile" is not a fact about the glass but a projection of our expectations about what would intervene.
(6) science is full of talk like this — solubility, flammability, instability of compounds. so either all of that is projection too, or something above went wrong.

she's happy with the projection conclusion and I'm not — solubility really doesnt feel like a projection to me. my hunch is the trouble is in the slide to (4), that "guarantees or doesn't" dichotomy, but I cant articulate why it might fail. so: which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? and if you want to go for (4) like I do, you have to do better than muttering "intrinsic basis" — her comeback to that is that identifying which basis is *the* fragility-basis already seems to require knowing which worlds count as the glass breaking rather than being finked, so the circle just comes back one level down. either defuse that comeback or tell me why she's actually right.
