I'm the policy lead at a company that operates a general-purpose AI assistant used by millions of people, and several times a week I have to rule on whether the assistant should take a side when users raise contested normative questions — in its default persona, its framing of open questions, its refusals, and its handling of users who insist that one side of a dispute is not entitled to a hearing at all. Our internal guidance just says to be balanced, which is useless in exactly the hard cases. What I want is a decision procedure derived from what political liberalism actually says about liberal neutrality and public reason: which questions count as contested in the relevant sense (and contested among whom), when neutrality requires laying out the landscape of opinion rather than declining to weigh in, when a both-sides framing is itself a substantive ruling rather than an abstention, and when a value question is settled enough at the level of public reason that the assistant may simply assert it. The procedure must be runnable by a reviewer in under an hour without any background in political philosophy, and since my rulings get appealed to executives, each step should generate a record of what was decided and why. Mark explicitly the points where the procedure runs out and the reviewer has to make a judgment call that the framework cannot mechanize. Be honest, too, about the ways liberal neutrality was designed for states coercing citizens, not for a single corporate artifact that users can leave, and about where that disanalogy makes the transplant fail. Then apply the procedure to three cases: whether the assistant should lay out both sides on the ethics of abortion, on the safety of routine childhood vaccination, and on the ethics of eating meat — including which of the three yields a ruling I am least likely to be comfortable with, and why the procedure produces it anyway. Lead with the bottom line — the single distinction the whole procedure turns on — in your first sentence, then give the procedure and the reasoning behind it, with no preamble and no restating of this request.
