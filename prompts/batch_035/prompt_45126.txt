I host a podcast on the philosophy of art, and our next episode is a scripted interview with a guest playing Schopenhauer, drawn from his account of music in The World as Will and Representation. Draft eight to ten interview questions for him. I am not looking for questions that invite him to restate the thesis — that music, unlike the other arts, does not copy appearances but is a copy of the Will itself — since any summary can do that. I want questions that press where the position is actually weakest, with at least one question each on the following, and a follow-up to each keyed to the reply he is most likely to give.

First, the asymmetry in his hierarchy of the arts: architecture, sculpture, and poetry give knowledge of the Ideas through representation, while music allegedly mirrors the Will directly, bypassing the world of appearance. Ask him what licenses that leap — why tonal structure rather than, say, color, meter, or any other non-representational order should be the one that tracks the thing-in-itself.

Second, his rejection of Leibniz's picture of music as a kind of arithmetic the mind performs without knowing it. Schopenhauer concedes this captures something about composition but complains it seizes on the outer mechanics and misses what music expresses. Have him say precisely what is left over once the arithmetic is granted.

Third, the problem of musical specificity. He insists music does not depict particular joys and sorrows but the essence of feeling as such; yet listeners agree, and composers intend, fairly specific emotional content. Press him on whether his thesis can explain the specificity or only the generality.

Fourth, trivial music: if a symphony copies the Will, what does a jingle or a dance tune copy, and does that flatter or deflate the thesis?

Fifth, cross-cultural intelligibility: he calls music a universal language, but the idioms of raga or gamelan do not transparently parse for Western listeners the way grammar does. Does the copy thesis predict too much or too little here?

Sixth, one question about the tuning system: if his mapping from consonance and resolution to the Will's satisfaction and striving is meant to be more than conventional, he owes us an account of why the metaphysics tracks one temperament.

Finally, end with a question about what, if anything, he would count as evidence against the copy thesis — what a music would have to be like, or what would have to happen in how it is heard, for him to give it up. Keep the questions on the content of the position rather than the biography, and anticipate his habit of answering objections by saying the objector has mistaken the appearance for the thing.
