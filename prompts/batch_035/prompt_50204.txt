My book club has spent the year on personal identity, and we have ended it badly split over Parfit's teletransporter from Reasons and Persons. We are now arguing about a variant one of us proposed, and I would like your credence on it rather than another survey of the literature. In the variant, the machine scans you, reconstructs a perfect replica on Mars, and leaves the original on Earth alive and well for exactly one further year; but the machine is also lossy in a specific way — it reconstructs your general character, values, and dispositions perfectly while compressing your episodic memories, so that perhaps one vivid recollection in twenty arrives on Mars as a faithful summary rather than as the memory itself. The question I want put to you is not whether the person on Mars is numerically identical to me — our club has agreed to set identity aside — but whether that person's existence gives me everything I have reason to care about in ordinary survival, discounted appropriately for the degraded episodic memories and for the year of experience the replica will never share. My own credence that it does is about 0.7; the two friends I argue with most sit at roughly 0.95 and 0.3, and I suspect the three of us are not really disagreeing about the same term. Please give me your own number, decomposed. Present the answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with nothing before the first item and nothing after the last: item one, your overall credence with the proposition it is a credence about stated precisely enough to have a truth value; items two through four, the sub-questions your answer depends on — I would expect among them how much weight to give psychological continuity as against other candidates for what matters, whether what matters comes in degrees rather than as a threshold, and how the lossy memories and the extra Earth-year should each affect the discount — each with a rough probability and one line of justification; and item five, which of those sub-questions your final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or imagined refinement of the case would move it the most. If a fourth sub-question strikes you as more important than any of my three, substitute it and say so within the item itself rather than adding extra items.
