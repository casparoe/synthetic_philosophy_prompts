hey so I've been reading Cicero's De Senectute for a book club (the one he writes as a dialogue where Cato the Elder defends old age to a couple of younger guys) and I kind of love his whole move: instead of just accepting the standard complaints about being old -- that it pulls you away from active life, that your body fails you, that you lose the pleasures that made life fun, that death is right around the corner -- he basically denies all four, and says old age is only miserable for people who made it that way, the happy old person is the same person they always were just with more time for the stuff that actually matters.

what I want is a short story that puts this view under real pressure. here's the setup I have in mind: an old woman, late 80s, still sharp, is offered a place in a program that scans her brain and runs a copy of her inside a simulated world where she'll never age, never slow down, never get sick. her grandaughter, who works on the program, is pushing her hard to do it, partly because she can't stand watching her decline. write the story so the Cicerian response -- that a good old age is worth living AS an old age, that you don't need to escape old age to have a good life -- is genuinely available to her and not just a strawman the tech bros get to crush. but also don't make it preachy, I don't want a sappy ending where she refuses the scan and everyone learns a lesson. maybe leave it honestly open which choice is right? it would be cool if the characters actually argued about the specific complaints Cato brings up (like the body failing, the pleasures going away, being close to death) rather than just vibes. length, I dunno, like 800-1200 words? whatever feels right.
