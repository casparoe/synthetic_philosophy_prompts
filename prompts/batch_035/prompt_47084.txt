Quick backstory so you know where I'm coming from: two years ago I had a bad reaction to some medication and my memory got noticeably worse, especially for anything prospective — appointments, errands, whether I'd already replied to an email. Rather than fight it I just rebuilt my life around external stuff. Everything lives in a notes app and a calendar with aggressive reminders, I photograph parking spots and the inside of the fridge before shopping, and I lean on an AI assistant for basically any multi-step task. My neuropsychologist called this "external scaffolding," which is apparently standard advice. The thing is, after a while the system stopped feeling like a system. Looking things up doesn't feel like looking things up anymore, it feels like remembering, just slow remembering — it has the same "oh right, THAT thing" quality that recalling used to have, and when I lose my phone or the app changes I get this fog that's qualitatively alot like forgetting.

So I finally read the Clark and Chalmers paper, the one with Otto who has Alzheimer's and navigates via his notebook while Inga navigates via biological memory, and their parity idea — roughly, that if an external process plays the same functional role an in-head process would play, we should count it as part of the cognitive system. And I've come around to thinking the extended view isn't just clever, it's literally true of me. Not metaphorically — my mind actually includes the phone and the reminders and maybe the assistant. My wife thinks I'm romanticizing a coping mechanism.

Here's what I want from you: what would count against this? Not a defense — I want the honest map of the view's exposure. I know there are internalist critics who say the parity principle just begs the question by assuming functional similarity where there's only functional analogy, and people who argue the right lesson is "embedded" cognition rather than "extended," but tell me which of these objections a serious extended-mind person would actually concede hurts and which they'd shrug off as missing the point. Also, the view comes with a bunch of conditions on Otto's notebook — it has to be reliably available, automatically endorsed, easily accessible — and I wonder whether those conditions have quietly qualified the thesis into something unfalsifiable: anything that looks like counter-evidence gets ruled out as not-really-extended. Is there any conceivable finding, experimental or otherwise, that would settle it one way or the other, or would even a decisive result get absorbed? Rank the defeaters for me from fatal to merely awkward, and be straight about whether I should be less confident than I am.
