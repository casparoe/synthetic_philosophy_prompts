Quick context: I've gotta hand in a short "where I stand" writeup for a phil mind reading group on self-knowledge, and I want to sanity check it before everyone piles on. Here's roughly my position:

(1) The KK principle: if you know p, you're in a position to know that you know p. I'm pretty firm on this one — it seems weird to have knowledge that you couldn't in principle know you have.

(2) Knowledge is closed under competent deduction. If I know p, and I know that p entails q, then I know q. Standard closure, I don't want to give this up.

(3) Williamson's margin-of-error stuff is basically right: whenever you know p, p has to hold in all the nearby cases where you'd make the same judgement. There's a margin around anything you can know, because no discrimination is perfectly reliable — even discrimination of your own current mental states.

(4) But also, pain is luminous, or at least it really seems that way from the inside. When I'm in pain, I know I'm in pain; when I'm not, I know I'm not. I cant really see how I could be wrong about whether I'm in agony right now.

(5) I don't buy the sorites paradox as showing vagueness in the world — everything has a sharp cutoff somewhere, including whether you're in pain. There's a fact of the matter, even if nobody knows where the line is.

(6) My friend swears she knows the exact second her headache started, and I keep telling her no way — at best she knows roughly when it started.

Something in here feels off to me but I honestly can't tell what. Which pairs of these are actually in tension, which ones only look like they conflict, and how bad is each case? For the genuine ones, what's the cheapest way to restore consistency and what does that fix cost me elsewhere? And if I had to revise exactly one commitment, which single change would clean up the most? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad.
