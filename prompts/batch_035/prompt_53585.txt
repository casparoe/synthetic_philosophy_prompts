been trying to sort out what I actually believe about the badness of pain by just going with my gut on cases, and I can't find a consistent story. My reactions: the pain of my friend's chronic migraines -- clearly bad. Pain asymbolia patients, the ones who report the sensation of pain in full intensity but shrug at it, don't withdraw, sometimes laugh -- their pain somehow doesn't seem bad to me. Same with morphine cases where people say the pain is still there but it doesn't bother them -- I want to say nothing bad is happening anymore. Pain I consent to and want, like a deep-tissue massage that hurts or the burn at the end of a hard run -- fine, maybe even good. But here's where it gets messy: a torture victim given a drug that leaves the pain fully felt but strips out the distress -- I can't bring myself to say nothing bad happened to them. And an infant's pain, too young to have the kind of "minding" that adults do -- obviously bad. So do I think pain is bad because it's disliked, or disliked because it's bad? I keep drifting toward "pain is bad when it's resented," but that seems to make the infant case and the torture case come out wrong, while the alternative makes pain intrinsically bad, which seems to make asymbolia come out wrong. Is there a principle that gets all or most of these verdicts, and if not, which ones should I give up? Also curious what the best candidate says about a case I haven't listed: someone who can feel pain but is psychologically unable to form any attitude about it at all, ever, from birth.
