I'm a senior content strategist at a large streaming platform, and my working vocabulary is entirely made of engagement metrics: retention and completion curves, share velocity, the virality coefficient K, cohort analysis, recommendation-model objectives, A/B tests, watch-time versus session-time tradeoffs, and the increasingly fraught category my industry calls authenticity — which in my world is a content genre with its own production playbook rather than any kind of metaphysical notion. I'm also auditing an evening humanities course, and our current reading is Tolstoy's What Is Art?, which startled me because his central claim sounds like something my dashboard already measures. As I understand him, art succeeds when it transmits a feeling the artist has actually experienced to an audience — he speaks of the work infecting its receivers — and the strength of that transmission varies with three conditions he names: the individuality or specificity of the feeling, the clarity with which it is expressed, and above all the sincerity of the artist, the last being the one he insists the peasant singing a lullaby has and the professional producing on commission or for critics lacks. I'd place myself as fluent in my own framework and a moderately careful but non-expert reader of his.

Please restate Tolstoy's theory of art systematically in my vocabulary, as precisely as you can — treat his infectiousness as a transmission phenomenon and ask what, if anything, in my metric suite would operationalize each of his three conditions, what his distinction between genuine and counterfeit art corresponds to in a world of optimized thumbnails, algorithmic recommendation, and creators who study which of their own past performances retained best, and what his low opinion of professionalized, critic-certified art does to our distinction between organic and engineered virality. Then give me the residue report: which of his claims survive the translation cleanly, which survive only by being distorted (say what the distortion is), and which cannot be expressed in my vocabulary at all. I have two suspicions I'd like you to confirm or debunk: first, that sincerity is structurally invisible to outcome-side data, since my metrics observe receiver-to-receiver spread while his infection is artist-to-receiver transmission, and these come apart in ways my dashboards can't register; second, that his insistence on evaluating the moral direction of the feeling transmitted — not just the magnitude of transmission — has no metric counterpart because my framework is deliberately value-neutral about what engages.

Close with a verdict on the residue itself: is the part of Tolstoy my vocabulary cannot express a defect of my vocabulary, a defect of his theory, or a genuine disagreement between two frameworks that each know something the other doesn't? And tell me whether his theory, once translated, actually predicts what I already observe — namely that our most metric-optimized properties retain well while a large fraction of viewers report, in qualitative surveys, that they can't remember them a week later — or whether that observation cuts against him.
