Tolstoy's big claim in What Is Art? (1897ish) is that real art works by infection: the artist hands on a feeling they actually experienced, and the test of whether it succeeded is whether ordinary working people -- not critics -- catch it. He also had this whole thing about counterfeits, art that just fakes infectiousness through borrowing from real art, imitation, spectacle, or interesting subject matter. And famously he'd take a peasant lullaby over late Beethoven, which he thought was basically incomprehensible noise for the upper classes.

So here's my question: what would Tolstoy make of AI music and image generators? I'm especially interested in whether his sincerity condition does any work here. A model has no feelings to transmit, sure, but it's trained on millions of recordings made by people who did, and the output can reliably make listeners cry. Would he write that off as counterfeit by definition? Or is there room in his actual argument for treating the machine as some kind of distillation of many sincere people? Like, imagine a model trained only on folk songs and lullabies that produces a new song that working mothers all over the world can't stop listening to -- that thing seems to pass his peasant test in every observable respect.

Also curious about the darker direction: he thought the art world of his day was run by professionals optimizing for a small class of buyers. Does that mean he'd see AI slop, optimized to be maximally graspable and consumable, as the vindication of his aesthetics rather than a violation of it? That is, maybe he predicted the economics but the machine breaks his sincerity test exactly where his test was supposed to do the most work.

Give me your best reconstruction of his views on this, flag clearly where you're extrapolating beyond stuff he actually wrote, and tell me where you think his framework just breaks down -- like, does infection-by-a-feeling survive as a concept at all when the feeling's original owner can't be pointed to? Please don't just give me a reading list, actually work through the arguments.
