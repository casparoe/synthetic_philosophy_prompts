As a novelist, I have come to hold an illusionist view of phenomenal consciousness: I no longer believe there is a further fact about what the smell of rain, or the ache of grief, is like over and above the discriminations, judgments, and reactive dispositions those states involve. This puts me at odds with my own trade, since rendering interiority is arguably an exercise in trafficking in exactly the seemings I claim are illusory. What I want from you is a map of my view's exposure. Which arguments, thought experiments, or possible empirical results should genuinely worry a committed illusionist -- and which of these would illusionists themselves concede have force, as opposed to dismissing as pre-theoretical confusion? I am especially interested in whether anything from psychology or neuroscience could make illusionism untenable rather than merely awkward, and in whether the standing move of reinterpreting every apparent datum as further illusion has insulated the view from disconfirmation altogether -- and if it has, whether that is a defect in a philosophical position or simply what a mature, science-minded philosophy of mind looks like. Please rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and tell me honestly whether the consideration that should move me most is one my profession's habits of vivid description have already inoculated me against.
