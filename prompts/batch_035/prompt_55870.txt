My friend (she's German) and I got into a three hour argument last night about inequality, and by the end I realized neither of us actually knows what we believe. Her line was basically the European one: big gaps in income and wealth are a problem in themselves, and the state should compress them. Mine was the American one: as long as nobody's poor and there's real mobility, I don't see what's wrong with some people having alot more. But when we got to the hard cases we both started flailing — like whether it matters if a gap came from luck vs choices, or whether a society where everyone is comfortable but some are 100x richer is fine, or whether "fair procedures, whatever the outcome" is an answer or a dodge.

So I want to make a "which view do you actually hold?" quiz for my blog. Not one of those that just asks "is inequality bad?" and sorts you into left vs right. I want it built from cases where the positions genuinely come apart, so people get sorted by what they say about the cases, not by which label they like. Can you write exactly five questions, numbered, each one a concrete case a sentence or two long with two or three answer options, and no intro or conclusion — then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto named positions (name them if they have names).

The key has to at least sort apart these five crowds: people who only care about the floor (nobody below a decent minimum, gaps above it are fine); people who care about gaps even when everyone's comfortable; people who think what matters is whether the gap came from luck or choice; people who think fair procedures settle everything so outcomes need no further defense; and people who think the real issue is status and domination rather than income shares at all. If you can also make room for people who'd accept bigger gaps when that makes the worst off absolutely better off, great, but five questions is the hard limit. If two of these can't actually be separated by five questions, say so instead of papering over it — same goes if you think the whole "European attitude vs American attitude" framing is muddying things more than it clarifies, or that the quiz idea can't work at all. Don't be polite about it, just tell me straight. Oh and I also suspect her "European attitude" is really several distinct views pretending to be one — if the quiz ends up showing that, bonus points. American English please.
