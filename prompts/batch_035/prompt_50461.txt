You are a philosopher of science and historian of technology who has thought a lot about multiple discovery. I have a hypothesis I'd like your help pressure-testing, and I want a concrete deliverable, not a survey.

My hypothesis is that "was this discovery inevitable?" is really two questions run together. There's timing-inevitability — whether someone would have gotten there within a few years anyway — and form-inevitability — whether what they got to would have been recognizably the same result. Calculus seems strong on both: Newton and Leibniz landed on it independently within about a decade, in mathematical cultures that barely talked to each other. Mendelian inheritance seems weird: it was published and ignored for thirty-five years, and then three people independently rediscovered it in the same season, which suggests either that the result was inevitable and the culture wasn't ready, or that "readiness" is doing all the work and inevitability claims are unfalsifiable after the fact.

Here is the task. Rate each of the following on both dimensions, as a number from 0 to 1 (your credence that the result would have been arrived at within a decade of the actual date if the actual discoverer had never worked on it; and separately your credence that the decade-later substitute would have been substantively the same result): calculus; evolution by natural selection; Mendelian inheritance; oxygen as the counter-theory to phlogiston; the telephone; general relativity. For each, give a one-to-two-sentence justification that engages the specifics of the case — don't just assert scores.

Then do three further things. First, tell me whether my two-dimensional split actually dissolves the problem or just relocates it, and say what a third dimension might be if you think one is missing. Second, address the objection that these ratings are hindsight artifacts: since we only ever see cases where the discovery happened, maybe no one is in a position to have honest credences about the counterfactual ones at all. Third, briefly: if you're right about the ratings, does high timing-inevitability diminish the credit owed to the actual discoverer, diminish it only if form-inevitability is also high, or not diminish it at all?

Before committing to your numbers, briefly brainstorm what evidence you'd want in an ideal world for each rating and note where you're mostly extrapolating from the bare fact of independent discovery. Then give the final table. Please reason carefully rather than reaching for stock narratives about lone geniuses or the Zeitgeist.
