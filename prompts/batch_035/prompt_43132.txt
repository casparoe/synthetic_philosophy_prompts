I run a weekly decision theory reading group at a university — about fourteen students and postdocs, mixed philosophy and CS, one two-hour session. We're approaching the idea behind updateless decision theories: that an agent should sometimes choose a complete policy before it knows which situation it is in, because the right thing to evaluate is not the action taken in the situation you observe but the policy that produces actions across all the situations you could have observed. I want the group to feel the pull of this before we read anything. Design an in-class game to do it.

Here is the mechanism I have in mind; develop it, fix it, or replace it if you see something better. Each participant writes down a complete contingent strategy before any roles are assigned — in an ultimatum-style game, something like: "if I end up the proposer, I demand X; if the responder, I accept anything at or above Y." Roles are then assigned by lot, but the crucial twist is that some submitted policies are matched against themselves: the same written policy is read for both sides of a pairing, so a participant may find their own earlier commitment sitting across the table from them. The payoff scheme should use points rather than money, and I need you to specify how many rounds to run, how pairings are announced, and how to make the self-matching rounds feel like a discovery rather than a gimmick.

Please also give me the debrief: how to map what actually happened onto the difference between choosing an action after updating on your role and choosing a policy ex ante, and where in the game each part of the distinction shows up. Then two honest notes. First, tell me which parts of updatelessness no classroom game can reproduce — in particular the kind of dependence on other agents' decisions that motivates one-boxing and that does not go through random role assignment — and what I should say so nobody leaves thinking updatelessness is just choice behind a Rawlsian veil of ignorance with extra steps. Second, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this, and what to do about it.
