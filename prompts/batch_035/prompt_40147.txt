I'm trying to work out what a decisive test of cognitive penetration would look like, and I realize I first need a statement of what would count as decisive — what an adequate demonstration has to establish, over and above finding some top-down influence somewhere. Please write the desiderata. An adequate demonstration should tell us, for each requirement, which mental states are eligible candidates for the penetrating cause (momentary beliefs, standing desires, acquired categories, expertise, the state of the body) and which effects on experience count as changes in perception rather than in attention, memory, or post-perceptual judgment. It should cover what kind of evidence would speak to perception itself — measures that resist response bias and demand effects, control conditions for mere cueing and task set — and it should take a stand on whether long-term perceptual learning (the radiologist seeing the tumor, the chicken-sexer seeing the chick's sex) counts as penetration or as a different phenomenon with different stakes, saying why. Include the case or consideration behind each requirement, mark which are non-negotiable and which are prejudices of one camp, and say whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect some tension between demanding strict controls and demanding ecological validity, and I would like your honest verdict on that. Then identify the single requirement that does the most to eliminate the famous candidate cases: the hill that looks steeper to the fatigued, the wine that tastes better when relabeled expensive, the face that looks less trustworthy once you learn its owner wronged you. Finally, in no more than two short paragraphs, assess how a Fodor-style modularist and a Siegel-style epistemologist each fare against your list, noting where either would satisfy a criterion only by weakening it — and which of the two, in your judgment, pays the higher price.
