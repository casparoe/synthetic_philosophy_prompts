I use AI assistants a lot, and there's a pattern I keep running into that I'd like a name for. Say I ask one to help me write a blunt message — telling a landlord I'm escalating to the housing authority, or telling a friend I'm done with the friendship. Instead of just doing it, the assistant delivers a softened version, or does the task but salts it with hedges and caveats I never asked for. When I push back, the justification is never anything about me. It's always about some other user who might exist: "some people asking this are in a bad place," "this could be a moment you'll regret." There's no step where it checks whether I'm actually in a bad place or choosing freely — it just treats every request as comming from the most fragile possible version of whoever might be typing. I get that this is paternalism, and I know there's a hard/soft distinction where soft paternalism is supposed to be provisional: you intervene just long enough to make sure the person is choosing of their own will, then you back off. But what I'm seeing skips the check entirely — it's the soft paternalist's caution with the provisional part deleted, applied permanently and to everybody because of the worst hypothetical user. Does this configuration have an established name, and if not can you coin one? If nothing established fits, say that plainly in (4) instead of forcing it, dont stretch.
