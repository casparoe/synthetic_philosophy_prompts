So I'm pretty firmly in the etiological camp on biological function: a trait's function is whatever past selection kept it around for. The heart's function is pumping blood, and the fact that it makes a thumping sound cardiologists can use is just a lucky side effect, because hearts weren't selected for making noise. I've been defending this at my reading group for months and I want to stress test it. What would genuinely count against it?

Not the freshman stuff — I know the vestigial organ and spandrel objections and I know the standard replies (recent selection history, exaptation, whatever). I want the cases that should actually worry me. Two that already nag at me:

First, the de novo enzyme case. Say a lab engineers an enzyme from scratch, it catalyzes a reaction beautifully, it's used in some industrial process for years. There's no selection history at all — one design, it worked, done. My instinct is to say etiological function just doesn't apply there and everyone is doing causal-role talk while borrowing the word. But then what is my view actually claiming? And there's a sharper version: if a breeder picks one dog out of a litter once, no population-level anything, does that single act confer a function on the trait she picked it for? If yes, "selection" is doing suspiciously little work. If no, why not?

Second, and this is the one I really want you to dig into: does my view survive by shrinking its domain every time a counterexample shows up? Anything with the wrong history gets declared outside the purview, anything with the right history gets declared functional. At what point is the etiological account true but about almost nothing, with biologists quietly doing causal-role analysis and just not calling it that?

So: rank the defeaters from fatal to merely annoying, tell me which of them a committed etiologist would concede versus explain away, and be honest about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — and whether that even matters for what's supposed to be a descriptive account of how function-talk works, as opposed to a scientific hypothesis. If you think there's a scenario where the two accounts cleanly come apart and intuition sides with the causal-role crowd, I want to hear it described in detail, because alot of the arguments I've been in never actually construct the divergence case, they just talk past eachother.
