I'm a PhD student in control theory working on autonomous systems, and my day job has permanently reshaped how I hear any talk of "control," "agency," and "feedback." My native vocabulary is state-space models, feedback and feedforward paths, setpoints and tracking error, controllability and observability, the internal model principle, model-predictive control, cascaded and hierarchical loops, disturbance rejection, and Lyapunov stability. I've read enough philosophy of free will to follow the compatibilism/incompatibilism debate but not enough to stop involuntarily translating it into my own terms while I read. Please do that translation for me, explicitly and systematically, and then give me the residue report I actually care about.

Specifically, work through the following:

1. Van Inwagen's consequence argument, as best I can tell, is a claim about disturbance inputs and controller authority: if the plant's trajectory is fully determined by laws plus the distant past, then the "controller" is just another subsystem of the plant, and calling what it does control is a misdescription. Render that argument in control-theoretic terms as faithfully as you can. Does it come out stronger or weaker in that vocabulary?

2. Alternative possibilities. The natural translation of "could have done otherwise" is reachability: which plant states are reachable given the controller's current state and inputs? But the incompatibilist insists the relevant counterfactual holds fixed both the laws and the complete past, which appears to hold fixed the controller's own state and inputs as well — leaving the reachable set a singleton. The compatibilist typically relaxes one of those fixed conditions (the past, or the controller's internal state). Frame this dispute precisely: which fixture is being relaxed in which compatibilist story, and is the disagreement, in my vocabulary, a substantive one about reachable sets or merely a verbal one about which counterfactual perturbation is "relevant"?

3. Frankfurt-style hierarchical compatibilism. I keep hearing this as cascaded loops: an outer, slower loop generating setpoints for an inner, faster loop, with the outer loop's internal model of the plant and of the inner loop doing the work of "identification with a desire." Render the view this way, and then tell me what the philosophers are adding beyond the loop structure — in particular, whether anything in the philosophy corresponds to the distinction between a well-tuned cascade and one whose outer loop merely suppresses the inner rather than governing it.

4. Pereboom's manipulation argument, where neuroscientists deliberately design an agent's motivational structure, compared to ordinary developmental shaping. In my terms this looks like: gains and internal models written by a designer versus gains and internal models acquired through plant interaction over time. Does the control vocabulary mark any difference between those two cases, and if it genuinely cannot, is that a defect of the vocabulary or a finding about the argument?

5. Reactive attitudes, if you can do anything with them — perhaps as the behavior of coupled agents updating their models of each other, where resentment and the withholding of resentment function as changes in another agent's environment rather than as verdicts about its metaphysical structure.

Then the residue report, which is the part I want treated as the main deliverable: which parts of the debate translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion (say what has to be bent), and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all. I'm especially interested in two residual questions: whether "ultimate sourcehood" is anything more than a disagreement about where we draw the boundary between controller and plant — i.e., whether incompatibilism just is the claim that the boundary, wherever drawn, is itself a boundary internal to the closed-loop system; and whether ought-implies-can has any counterpart in a stability or feasibility condition, or whether it lives entirely outside what control theory has concepts for. Finally, give me your own view on the residue: where something fails to translate, is that because my framework is missing a concept the philosophers need, or because the original claim was less well-posed than it looked? Please be honest rather than diplomatic — I would rather learn that a debate I find fascinating reduces, in my vocabulary, to a bookkeeping choice about loop boundaries.
