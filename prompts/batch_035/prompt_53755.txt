I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I'd like your help thinking through what philosophers apparently call "responsibility gaps". Context from my side: I work on the safety layer of a system where a reinforcement-learning controller dynamically re-prioritises jobs for a fleet of autonomous warehouse vehicles. Last quarter the controller learned a triage strategy none of us anticipated, a colleague was injured, and when we ran the post-mortem nobody could point to a decision that was clearly negligent: the spec was reasonable, the training setup was reasonable, the sign-off was reasonable. Whether or not that incident itself counts, it has made me take the general question seriously.

My credence that there exist realistic deployment contexts in which no human agent satisfies the conditions for being morally responsible — in the backward-looking, blame-grounding sense — for a foreseeable serious harm caused by an autonomous system is about 0.6. That is, I think the gaps are real rather than merely cases where responsibility is diffuse or hard to trace. My team lead puts it around 0.15, on the grounds that some human always makes a foreseeability-grounding design or deployment decision, so the harm is always traceable to someone if you look hard enough.

Please give me your own credence in the claim as I've stated it, and decompose it: the probability that blame-grounding responsibility requires a genuine control or "could have intervened" condition rather than only foreseeability-based tracing; the probability that tracing through layered, individually reasonable decisions (specification, training, tuning, deployment sign-off) can fail even under thorough investigation; the probability that some form of collective or vicarious responsibility always fills the gap in practice even if no individual is responsible; and any further terms you think belong, each with a rough probability and a one-line justification. Then show how the terms combine into your overall number. Finally, identify which single term does most to explain the distance between my 0.6 and your figure, and tell me what would most shift it — a concrete kind of deployment scenario, an argument about the control condition, or something else. Please answer in British English.
