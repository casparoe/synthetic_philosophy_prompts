My friend is an actuary who bets casually with a small circle of acquaintances, and she is systematic about it: before every wager she writes down her subjective probability that her side wins, and she sizes her stakes by a rule she has never disclosed to me. Her betting bankroll she keeps at a flat $20,000 — she reseeds it at the start of each month — and she stakes in round hundreds. Her ledger so far, all at even money:

- A playoff game: she put the home side at 0.55; staked $1,000.
- A mayoral race: she put the challenger at 0.60; staked $2,000.
- Rain on the day of an outdoor wedding: she put rain at 0.53; staked $600.
- A colleague's promotion: she put it at 0.52 — a real edge, she insisted — but declined to bet at all.

Tonight an acquaintance is offering 3-to-1 against a proposition she makes 0.34 (a winning $100 returns $300 profit plus the stake; a losing $100 is gone). Will she bet, and at what stake?

I'm less interested in the number than in the rule behind it. Her record to date fits more than one staking policy, and I suspect this is the bet where they come apart — everything so far has been even money, and this isn't. So please: (i) lay out the candidate rules that fit every entry in her ledger, and show precisely where they agree on her past bets and where they diverge on this one; (ii) give your single best guess at her stake, with your reasons for preferring one rule over its rivals; (iii) specify the further wager — its odds, and a probability she'd plausibly assign — that would best discriminate between the surviving rules; and (iv) flag anything else in the ledger that a second look turns into evidence, such as the promotion she declined to bet on. For what it's worth, she likes to say she "bets her edge," but I've never known whether she means that literally, as a formula, or just as a slogan.

Close with a single final line of the form \answer{stake=$X; rule=<name>}, where X is your best point prediction of her stake in dollars ($0 if you think she passes) and <name> is the policy you favor.
