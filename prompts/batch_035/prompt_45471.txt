There's a guy at my climbing gym, Dave, who has read basically every pop evolution book out there, and he's appointed himself referee on the question of what level natural selection is "really" acting at. I write backend code for a living, so I can't resist trying to reverse-engineer the rule he's running, and I've been logging his rulings in a txt file like it's bug triage. His record so far: the fruit fly gene that cheats at meiosis, sabotaging the other sperm so it lands in more than its share of eggs even though it's bad for the fly carrying it — he called that gene-level selection, an outlaw. Peacock tails — individual level, the whole male is the package the peahens are choosing. Honeybee workers that never breed and die after stinging — gene level, he says the genes are just helping copies of themselves in the queen's offspring, same for the ground squirrels that chirp a hawk warning and mostly do it when their relatives are around. When I brought up myxomatosis getting milder in Australian rabbits, he shot down the group-selection story flat: just individual selection among virus strains, the ferocious ones burned through there own host supply. But for the lab setups where breeders select whole groups — like breeding from entire cages of hens instead of picking the best hen in each cage — he says sure, that's group selection, because the experimenter made the group the thing that reproduces.

Next week I'm planning to hit him with this: a disease where two strains end up sharing a single host. Inside a shared host the strains compete, and the grabbier strain wins, which makes the host sicker and cuts transmission, while a strain sitting alone in its host evolves milder — so the villages where co-infection is common get the vicious version and the single-strain ones keep the mild one. What will his ruling be: individual level again since strains are individuals and that's the end of it, gene level because the grabby gene is basically an outlaw at the host level, or will he finally say group selection? Honestly I care less about the answer than about what rule he's actually running, which other rules would fit his log just as well but give different verdicts on this one, and what case I should keep in reserve for after, to tell those rules apart.
