I am a novelist, and my current book follows two philosophers of physics through a single academic year: one holds that spacetime is a genuine substance, existing in its own right; the other insists that spacetime is nothing over and above the network of relations among material events. Their central dispute is Newton's bucket — the water climbing the sides, the concave surface as an effect with, they agree, no material cause to be found — and I am determined that neither of them should be a strawman in my hands. Before drafting their long final scene, I want a document that would survive scrutiny from readers who actually work in philosophy of physics.

Please draft the joint statement these two characters could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the claims they agree on — about what the bucket and Newton's two globes joined by a cord actually show, about the asymmetry between rotation and uniform velocity, about what was living and what was dead in Mach's response, and about what general relativity, for all its prestige, does and does not settle for either of them. I want the precise formulations of their residual disagreement, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording. And I want the cruxes: I suspect one is whether a lone pair of globes rotating in an otherwise empty universe is a genuine physical possibility or a philosopher's artefact, and another is what the hole argument really establishes about manifold substantivalism — whether it refutes it, or merely demands a more careful statement of what the substantivalist is committed to. For each crux, set out the thought experiment, argument, or case both sides would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance, so that neither can quietly rewrite the bet after the fact. If you conclude that part of their dispute is merely verbal — say, over what it even means to assert that spacetime exists — say which part and why, since that would be the most quietly devastating scene of the novel.

Format the document as a numbered section of agreed claims followed by a table of disagreements, and write in British English throughout.
