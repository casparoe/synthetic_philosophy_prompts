So I'm a phil mind PhD student and somewhere along the way (drafting my diss chapters on levels of explanation, help) I've ended up committed to a view I can't fully defend yet, and I want you to stress-test it properly.

The view: symbols push the substrate around. Not metaphorically. The setup I keep coming back to is Hofstadter's careenium: a giant billiard table swarming with little magnetic marbles he calls symms, which clump together into bigger composite balls, the simmballs. The simmball level is meant to be like the symbol level in a brain, the symm level like the individual neurons. And Hofstadter's question is basically which level shoves which: do the simmballs just get pushed around passively by marbles bashing into each other, or do the simmballs themselves do some of the pushing? I think they do. My line is that the symbol level -- in the careenium, in brains, and honestly in anything computational -- is causally real. Grab a whole simmball clump and move it somewhere it wouldn't otherwise have gone, and the downstream effects are best explained at the simmball level; the individual-symm-collision description predicts the same events but explains them worse. I've also been leaning on this for a side chapter on LLM interpretability, where it seems like the cleanest story is that features/concepts at the algorithmic level causally steer what the network does next, rather than the mechanistic level being where all the "real" causation lives while everything else is bookkeeping. My advisor keeps making the reductionist face at this and saying the simmball level is just a compressed description for OUR convenience, and I can never quite articulate why that feels wrong to me.

What I want: what would actually count against my view? Not the standard "higher-level causes are just lower-level causes redescribed" line stated at me again -- I want to know what a serious mechanist would *themselves concede* as counting against symbol-level causation, and what they'd wave off. Are there thought experiments, interventionist-style results, stuff from the autonomy-of-special-sciences literature, or cases where the story I'm telling just breaks down? Where is my view in danger of being qualified into "well it depends what you mean by cause" unfalsifiability, and is that fatal for a view like this or is it just the normal cost of doing interlevel metaphysics? Rank the defeaters for me, fatal to merely awkward. And be honest if you think the whole question is basically verbal -- I half suspect my committee does, and I'd rather hear it now than at the defense.
