So I've basically bought the quining-qualia move (the Chase and Sanborn line, as I understand it): qualia as classically conceived -- ineffable, intrinsic, private, knowable by direct apprehension -- form a package that can't survive, and once the package falls apart there's nothing left for the concept to do, so it goes the way of phlogiston. Fine. But I got here from blog posts and half-remembered argument maps, not from working it through myself, so I want the thing stress tested instead of applause.

What would actually count against the view? I'm not just asking for the standard zombie/inverted-spectrum pushback -- I want to know which of those a committed quiner would themselves concede as real pressure and which they'd wave off as begging the question, and I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Things I'd specifically like covered: does the qualia concept earn it's keep anywhere that resists paraphrase (pain reports in medicine, maybe cases where first-person "what it's like" talk explains behavior that third-person facts don't)? Does elimination quietly collapse into reductive identification if you're not careful about what's being denied? And is the response "just drop one of the four features and keep a thin conception of qualia" a genuine counterexample to elimination or just a relabeled one?

Also, please keep straight what's actually settled here versus what's genuinely contested among people who know this stuff -- eliminativism about qualia is NOT a consensus position and I don't want the response to hand me one side as if it were. For what it's worth, the thing that would move me most is a concrete case where the qualia concept is doing load-bearing explanatory work that can't be paraphrased away, but I'm open to being told that's the wrong test.
