Please write a villanelle on the careenium, the thought experiment Douglas Hofstadter develops in *I Am a Strange Loop*. In it, a huge frictionless billiard table is seeded with small, fast magnetic marbles that Hofstadter calls simms; the table as a whole is the careenium, a pun on the cranium. The simms careen about and magnetically clump into large, sluggish clusters called simmballs, and Hofstadter invites us to treat the simmballs as the system's symbols — standing in for concepts, records of events, and the like. The question the careenium is built to pose is a question about levels of description and about who shoves whom inside our brains: on one description, everything that happens is just simms colliding with simms, and talk of simmballs is a bookkeeping convenience; on another, the simmballs genuinely push each other around — and even seem to shove the little simms about — so that the symbolic level earns its own causal story.

Make the poem's two refrains, respectively, the reductionist's credo (nothing happens but collisions among the smallest parts) and the symbol-level credo (the big slow clusters do the shoving). Exploit what the villanelle form does with refrains: each one returns again and again, never altered in its wording yet always recontextualized by the stanza around it, so the form itself can dramatize how each level keeps reasserting itself as the true story while the other keeps coming back just as intact. Reserve the closing couplet — the one place where the two refrains finally stand side by side — for the strange loop itself: the question of who pushes whom should be left standing there rather than settled, with the two accounts rubbing shoulders in a way that suggests they may not ultimately be rivals. The poem should be accurate enough that someone who knows the thought experiment would recognize both the mechanics of the table and the point about levels of description, while someone who has never heard of it should still be able to read it as a good poem about a table full of restless magnetic marbles. Please write the poem in American English.
