So, quick context: in a couple weeks I'm presenting to my department's metaphysics work-in-progress seminar, and I need a Q&A prep sheet. The thesis I'm defending: the whole fink/mask apparatus in the dispositions literature draws its line in the wrong place. Everyone treats finks (Martin's electro-fink, the Lewisian wizard who renders the glass no longer fragile just as it's about to be struck) and masks (the packing case that stops the glass breaking without making it any the less fragile) as two different kinds of problem for the conditional analysis. My claim is that they're the same kind of thing, differing only in when the interferer starts operating relative to the completion of the stimulus. Stimuli aren't instants — a "striking" takes time and has fuzzy edges — and once you take that seriously, a mask just is a fink that got going early. A pre-emptive fink. So we should drop the binary distinction and talk instead about a continuum of interference onset times, and then the conditional analysis fails for one uniform reason rather than the two separate reasons people always give (which also means we don't need the special extra story about why masks are supposed to be the deeper problem for the analysis).

The seminar audience, so you can calibrate: two of them work on powers and dispositions and know the Martin–Lewis exchange by heart; one of them holds that dispositions are intrinsic, so from her point of view my whole setup is off the rails before I open my mouth, since extrinsic interferers shouldn't be doing any individuating work at all. One is an old-school Lewisian type who thinks conditional analyses were never worth saving and will basically ask why I'm bothering to rescue a corpse. The rest are general metaphysicians who know this literature at roughly the level of a good encyclopedia entry — they'll ask the clarifying questions, and those are sometimes the ones that hurt.

What I want from you: the objections I'll actually get from this crowd, in rough order of how much damage each would do, phrased the way these people would actually phrase them (not textbook phrasings — the way a powers person talks at a seminar), each with a two-minute reply, since question time runs long and I get cut off, plus a note on where the reply is soft.
