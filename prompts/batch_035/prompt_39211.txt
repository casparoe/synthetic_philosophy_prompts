Been reading about the structuralist historians on Weimar lately and it kind of blew my mind. If I've got it right, the whole point is that the deep causes — the humiliation of Versailles, the economic chaos, the antisemitism, elites who'd rather have fascism than socialism — were all sitting there like dry tinder, so any competent demagogue could have struck the match. Which means the "what if Hitler died in WW War I" question has a boring answer: someone else does more or less the same thing, roughly same outcome. So counterfactual history is basically a waste of time, right? The answer is always "it happens anyway, just with a different mustache."

Here's what confuses me though. The structuralists themselves keep writing these massive biographies of Hitler. Kershaw is like THE structuralist guy and he spent two giant volumes on the man's life. Isn't that self-contradictory? If structure does all the work and the individual is just interchangeable, why would he bother? Is it just because biographies sell better than books about economic conditions?
