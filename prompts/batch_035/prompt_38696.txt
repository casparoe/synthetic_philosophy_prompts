A podcast episode on the philosophy of music summarized Schopenhauer's view roughly as follows: Schopenhauer held that all art offers temporary relief from desire, and that music does this best of all. Because music bypasses concepts, it needs no translation: a melody of grief communicates directly to any listener, whatever their culture, which is why he described music as a universal language. And because it presents the Will itself rather than a copy of something, he ranked it above all the other arts. Indeed, since listening quiets our individual willing, he held that a great symphony is a form of temporary self-denial — essentially the same state as the ascetic's renunciation of the will, just briefer and more easily obtained.
