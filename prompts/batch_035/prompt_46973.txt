Here's something I keep bumping into: the old strategy-stealing argument, usually credited to Nash for Hex — first player can't lose in a symmetric game where an extra move never hurts, since if second player had a winning strategy, first player could just make an arbitrary opening move and then follow that strategy as if they were second player. Nice trick, and apparently it now gets reused in AI-risk arguments under the name "strategy-stealing assumption," roughly: no one (aligned or unaligned AI) can get a decisive edge purely from better strategies, because whatever works for one side can be copied by the other.
