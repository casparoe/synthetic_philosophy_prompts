my partner is doing her thesis on Hannah Arendt and she keeps issuing rulings on political lies at dinner like she's some kind of court. i've started keeping notes. Her verdicts so far: a general deceiving the enemy about troop positions during a war — fine, basically as old as politics itself, no one expects armies to tell the truth; a government keeping its own secrets from its own citizens without lying, just refusing to confirm — also basically fine, she says secrecy isn't lying; a totalitarian regime airbrushing a purged official out of every photograph — the worst thing, somehow worse than the purge itself, because it attacks the shared record that would let anyone remember the purge; the Pentagon Papers business — the really damning part was not that the planners lied to the public, but that they'd built themselves a fake image of reality and then navigated by it, so in a sense they were the first victims of their own lie; and finally, election campaigns being pure image-making by professional consultants — she was weirdly relaxed about this one, like, that's just what elections are, manufacturing an image is not the same as destroying facts.

predict her verdict. and do the real work: what rules actually fit her track record so far? is she running "deceive outsiders ok, corrupt the shared record of insiders not ok"? or "the real crime is self-deception, liars who end up believing their own lie"? or "the axis is coherent fabricated world vs. mere spin"? and be careful, because those rules diverge on the flood case in different directions — on one reading the flooders are worse than the totalitarians since they don't even install a replacement reality, on another reading they're closer to cynical old-style liars who at least knew the truth. give me your best guess with reasons, and then give me the one follow-up case i should bring up over dessert that would best split those rules apart — something her answer to would reveal which rule she's actually been running this whole time.
