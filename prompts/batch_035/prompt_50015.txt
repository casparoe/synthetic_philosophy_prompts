I've come round to a pretty Mencian picture of moral psychology: we're born with proto-moral reactions — the alarm at seeing a kid about to fall into a well, the flash of shame when your caught doing something shabby — and becoming a good person is a matter of cultivating those "sprouts," not of morality being installed from outside. Growth, not engineering. What would genuinely count against this? I want both empirical stuff (developmental findings, whether the sprouts show up the same way across cultures, psychopaths) and the philosophical pressure from the Xunzian side — e.g. that if the sprouts need that much pruning, ritual and training to become anything recognizable as virtue, maybe the cultivation is doing all the work and the sprouts are basically decoration. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which a committed Mencian should concede vs. explain away, and tell me honestly whether the view survives by retreating to "the sprouts are just a capacity to acquire morality" — or whether that retreat makes the claim so weak it's not worth fighting for.
