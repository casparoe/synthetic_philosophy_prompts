I run a grad metaphysics reading group, and after last week's session two of my students plus one hanger-on got into it about endurantism vs perdurantism and kept going over coffee. I stayed out of it but honestly couldn't fully referee it in my head, so I'm outsourcing. Here's the exchange as close to verbatim as I can reconstruct.

Quick setup so we're on the same page: endurantists say an object is wholly present whenever it exists, and persists by being strictly identical at different times. Perdurantists say persisting objects are four-dimensional "worms" made of temporal parts or stages, and nothing is strictly identical across time -- the relation between you-today and you-yesterday is a matter of the stages belonging to one worm.

(1) Marcus (perdurantist) opened with an analogy: he said persistence is just variation along a dimension, no different from how a poker game varies in stakes from hand to hand. Nobody thinks the game is "wholly present" at each hand and somehow identical across them -- the game is the whole sequence. Same deal, he said, with the lamp on the seminar table: it varies in location along the time axis, that's all persistence is, and "wholly present" talk is just a refusal to say what persistence consists in.

(2) Elena (endurantist) said the poker analogy is rigged, since games are events and lamps aren't, but her real complaint was what she called temporal double-vision: on worm theory, every ordinary fact seems to get recorded twice -- once as a fact about the stage (this stage is bent) and once as a fact about the worm (the poker is bent at t), and the theory never tells you which sentence is the one we're actually asserting when we plain folks say "the poker is bent." Also, she said, when Marcus shook my hand Tuesday, on his view he shook hands with a temporal part of me, not me, and that's already a reductio as far as she's concerned.

(3) Marcus answered that the double-vision charge cuts the other way. He said temporary intrinsics is the real test: the poker is straight on Monday and bent on Tuesday, full stop, not straight-relative-to-Monday. Endurantism has to relativize the property (having-bent-at-t) or go adverbial ("the poker is bent, Tuesday-style"), and both of those, he claimed, are just stage-talk wearing a fake mustache -- once you've said "bent-at-t" you've smuggled in the stage. And on the handshake: he happily agreed he shook hands with a stage of me, because stages aren't instantaneous slices, they can be big and squishy and include the hand and the rest, so nothing insulting follows.

(4) Elena came back with what I thought was her strongest move. She said relativizing properties is no more mysterious than tense, and anyway worm theory has its own embarrassment: responsibility across time. When we punish a criminal, the person in the dock is supposed to be the very one who committed the crime -- that's the whole point of it being punishment rather than harassment of an innocent bystander. On worm theory the punished stage is never the offending stage; at best you punish a later stage for what an earlier one did, which either paraphrases away the subject entirely or sneaks back strict identity through some "the worm did it" locution that she said Marcus isn't entitled to.

(5) At that point Zoe, who'd been nursing a coffee and refusing to pick sides, said they were both wasting an evening on a verbal dispute. Both theories, she pointed out, deliver the identical verdict on every observable, every safety-inspection checklist, every court case about who owns the re-topped seminar table -- the endurantist says "same table, repaired," the perdurantist says "one worm, new tabletop stage," and the registrar writes the same entry either way. So what, she asked, is the supposed fact of the matter they're disagreeing about? Marcus and Elena both turned on her at that point, roughly in unison, which was at least the most agreement anyone showed all night.
