But this presupposes quite a lot, given that philosophers have argued self-deception is paradoxical or impossible — if you must know the truth in order to hide it from yourself, who exactly is fooling whom? Please disambiguate the line into exactly five distinct precise theses it could be expressing — for instance, readings on which it is a triviality about bias, readings on which it presupposes a substantive solution to the static paradox of self-deception, and readings on which "fooling yourself" involves two distinct subsystems or a motivated division of labor in the mind — stating each in a sentence or two, including a verdict on whether it is platitudinous, substantive, or false, and noting for each whether it would actually support my interlocutor's use of the line. Present these as a numbered list of five items only, with no introduction or conclusion.
