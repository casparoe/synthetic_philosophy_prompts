I'm taking a metaphysics course this semester on modality and possible worlds, and a girl in my study group, Marta, has unusually firm intuitions about de re modal claims — firm enough that we've started keeping a list of her verdicts to see whether they add up to a coherent rule. Her track record so far: she says it's true that Humphrey could have won the 1968 election; she denies that the wooden table in our seminar room could have been made of ice, or even from a different block of wood; she says she herself could have been raised in New Zealand and become a sheep farmer, with the same parents and the same birth she actually had; she denies that she could have been conceived from entirely different gametes by different parents; and she insists that a person molecule-for-molecule indistinguishable from her but conceived from different gametes would not be her, however similar. Our teaching assistant looked at the list and said Marta is running some rule, maybe two, and told us to find the cases where the candidates come apart — which has turned into a standing bet.

Two new cases came up this week and Marta went uncharacteristically quiet on both, so a fair amount of money is riding on what she'll say. Case one: a possible world in which her zygote splits just after conception and two babies develop from it, both sharing her exact origin, each living out a full and ordinary life. Could she have been one of them? Both? Neither? Case two: a possible world in which someone shares her exact origin — same parents, same conception, same birth — but turns out to be nothing like her in temperament, choices, or biography. Is that person her? For each case, give your verdict, lay out the rules that fit her track record equally well (transworld identity with origin essentialism on one reading, counterpart theory with a similarity-weighted counterpart relation on another, and anything else you can make fit), say which rule your prediction hangs on, and — since we can keep quizzing Marta — tell us which further case would best tell the rules apart. Please lead with your bottom-line prediction in the first sentence, then give the reasoning; no preamble, and don't restate the setup back to me.
