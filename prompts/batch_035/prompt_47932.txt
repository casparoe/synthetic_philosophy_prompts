Okay so I've been chewing on this thing about whether it's wrong to bring people into existence, and I have a bunch of gut reactions to specific cases that I can't make line up, so I want help figuring out what principle (if any) is behind them.

My verdicts, case by case:

1. Knowingly conceiving a child who you have very good reason to believe will have a life of constant suffering -- wrong. Obviously wrong, same category as anything else that badly hurts someone.
2. Conceiving a child you have good reason to believe will have a great life, when you could equally easily not -- not wrong, and also not a failure. You didn't owe anyone that happy life. This one feels rock solid to me.
3. But here's where it gets uncomfortable: a couple who could have a child now, in circumstances that would make for a mediocre-but-worth-living life (some illness, some hardship, net mildly positive), or could wait two years and give a *different* child a much better life. If they go ahead now, I don't want to say they wronged the child they actually had -- that child gets a life worth living and is presumably glad to exist. But I also feel like something is off about it, and I keep flip-flopping between "it's fine, no one was wronged" and "they did something shabby, just not to anyone."
4. Two people deciding whether to have a kid, one option would mean the kid is born with a painful disability, the other option (different timing, whatever) means a different kid is born healthy. Same flip-flop. It matters, but it doesn't matter to the kid born with the disability, because without the decision that kid doesn't exist at all.
5. Preventing a person who already exists from having a terrible future -- obligatory-ish, at least a strong duty. Creating a person whose future would be terrible -- also wrong. But *not creating* a person whose future would be wonderful doesn't feel like any duty at all, even a weak one. So existing people and possible people seem to pull completely different weights, and the difference is exactly which side of existence they're on, which seems like it shouldn't be doing moral work but apparently is.

The tension as I see it: cases 1 and 2 suggest an asymmetry (harming possible people by creating them counts, benefiting possible people by creating them doesn't), and I actually endorse that asymmetry. But 3 and 4 are where it bites, because they involve making a *possible* person worse off in the comparative sense -- the kid gets a worse life than the kid in the other option would have gotten, and it's not the same kid, so there's nobody who ends up worse off than they otherwise would have been. My gut says the choice between those options still matters, but I can't find anyone it matters *to*, and "it matters but not to anyone" isn't something I know how to make sense of.

So what I'm asking for: is there one principle that covers all five verdicts? If yes, state it precisely and tell me which case motivates each clause, and what it predicts about a further case I haven't mentioned -- say, a couple where the choice is between no child at all and a child whose life would be barely, just barely worth living. If no single principle covers them, tell me which of my verdicts has to go and why that one rather than the others. Don't be gentle about it -- if my "rock solid" verdict in case 2 is the problem, say so. And if the answer is that some of these are real disagreements inside myself that no principle will paper over, I'd honestly rather know that than get a tidy story that quietly fudges case 3.
