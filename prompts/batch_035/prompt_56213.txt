Ever since I stumbled on the phenomenon of pain asymbolia reading random stuff online, I've been going back and forth with a friend about what pain's badness actually is, and we've realised we're completely stuck. The people with asymbolia, as far as I understand it, have damage to certain parts of the brain (insular region or wherever exactly) and they'll tell you plainly "yes that hurts" when you pinch them, they localise it, they can rate its intensity — but they just shrug. They don't flinch, don't withdraw, don't seem remotely bothered, sometimes they laugh. My friend's take is that this settles it: the badness of pain can't be the raw sensation itself, since the sensation is fully there and the badness is gone, so the badness must live in some separate layer — a kind of caring or aversion reaction wrapped around the feeling. My take is that this proves too much: if the hurtiness and the hating-it come apart that cleanly, then maybe the "hurting" the asymbolic reports is just bodily detection, like a smoke alarm, and real pain only shows up when the alarm actually means something to you. The problem is that whichever way we push it, the other side just redefines the word and we're back where we started.

We've been at this for months now and I honestly can't tell if we're having a real disagreement or a dictionary fight about the word "pain". When my friend says "the feeling is intact, the badness is missing", I'm never sure if she's describing a discovery or just choosing to call the sensory component "the feeling". And when I say "maybe what's left in asymbolia isn't really pain", she says I'm rigging the case so my theory can't lose. So my question is: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is there a hidden factual question buried in it — like what exactly the lesions disrupt, or what you'd find if you scanned asymbolics while having their pain "felt" at full intensity — or is it bedrock intuitions about whether badness is something a sensation can have versus something a person does with it? Or is it possible we're both answering a different question under one name, like she's talking about what makes pain worth avoiding and I'm talking about what the word refers to?

I want a proper diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. If you think it's partly verbal, tell me what distinction would actually move us forward. If you think it's substantive, tell me what evidence or thought experiment would break the tie — for example, would it help or hurt to imagine someone who has the opposite dissociation (someone desperate to escape a sensation they can barely detect)? And be honest if you think the whole framing is confused somehow, like maybe "the badness of pain" isn't one thing at all. One more thing: could you write your answer in British English please? I'm writing this from Manchester and it reads weird to me otherwise.
