I'd like a thought experiment that forces apart two readings of the symbol grounding problem, because I suspect the standard formulation papers over a real ambiguity.

The background I'm working from: Harnad's complaint is that a system manipulating symbols purely by their formal relations — however fluently — has nothing connecting those symbols to the world, and that grounding comes from sensorimotor interaction with referents. But that formulation can be read two ways. On the developmental reading, grounding is an achievement a system earns: a symbol counts as grounded only if the system's use of it was shaped, over time, by interaction with instances of what it denotes. On the causal-coupling reading, grounding is a standing relation: what matters is that the system's symbol deployments are now appropriately caused by, and appropriately steer behavior toward, their referents — how those dispositions came to exist is beside the point.

Build a scenario in which the two readings deliver opposite verdicts about the same system, while agreeing about a second system that serves as a contrast case. The required structure: one system that both readings count as fully grounded; one system that is relevantly identical to it from this moment forward — same body, same sensors, same internal organization, same ongoing interaction with the world — but whose past differs in a way that forces the developmental reading to count it as ungrounded, or barely grounded, while the causal-coupling reading counts it as fully grounded. Constraints: near-future technology only, no exotic physics and no idealized recorders; the scenario itself should be at most one paragraph; and no reference to consciousness or qualia anywhere — the case must remain decidable by an external observer with a complete lab log, so that the grounding question can't quietly collapse into the hard problem.

After the scenario, do four things. First, state what each reading implies about each system and why, making explicit which fact about the second system each verdict hangs on. Second, identify the load-bearing feature of your construction, and then say what happens to the verdicts if you remove it by making the past continuous — for instance, by transferring the system gradually, subsystem by subsystem, in a Ship-of-Theseus fashion — and whether the developmental reading can live with grounding coming in degrees or must insist on a sharp discontinuity. Third, mount the strongest attack on the scenario from whichever reading you judged to come out worse; the charge should be that the case is rigged — that it stipulates the verdict rather than generating it from the theories — and not merely that the conclusion is unwelcome. (The natural ally to invoke here is an etiological theory of content, on which what a state represents depends on the history by which it came to play its role; use it if it helps the attack, but say so.) Fourth, say whether the case adjudicates anything at all, given that the two systems are behaviorally indistinguishable in every possible test, and what that equivalence itself suggests about what the dispute over grounding is actually a dispute about.

Two notes on what would count as cheating. Don't have any character in the scenario pronounce on whether the symbols are grounded; the verdicts have to fall out of the theories, not be embedded in the fiction. And don't make the second system's past so alien — say, trained entirely on text in a sealed room — that everyone will agree about it in advance; the interesting case is one where the only difference in the past is a matter of how the present dispositions came to exist, not of what those dispositions are.
