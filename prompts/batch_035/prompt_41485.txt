(1) everything is empty of intrinsic nature, and that includes emptiness itself — emptiness is empty too, it's not some final metaphysical fact lurking behind phenomena waiting to be discovered;
(2) I read the tetralemma as a genuine formal schema, not rhetoric: for any essence-positing claim, all four options (is, is not, both, neither) fail, and showing this works as a valid reductio of the whole framework of intrinsic nature;
(3) I'm a robust realist about conventional truth — tables, persons, and causal regularities exist conventionally in a full-blooded way, and that's enough for science and ethics; conventions aren't useful fictions we pretend into existence, they're how things genuinely are at the conventional level;
(4) I think Madhyamaka is ultimately a metaphysical position: "nothing has intrinsic nature" is a substantive thesis about how things are, and it's true, not just a therapy for other people's views;
(5) I think the ultimate truth just is that there are no ultimate truths, and I don't think that's self-refuting — I take it to be the emptiness of emptiness applied to truth itself, so to speak;
(6) I reconstruct Nāgārjuna's arguments in classical logic (reductio, excluded middle, the usual), because I think that's the only way they come out as actual arguments rather than mystical gestures, and I'm committed to them coming out as actual arguments.
