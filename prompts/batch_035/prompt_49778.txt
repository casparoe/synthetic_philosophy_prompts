What I want to know is whether this can happen outside of geometry. Can you construct a thought experiment set in ordinary life where applying the principle of indifference gives two defensible but different probabilities for the same event, depending on how you describe or parameterize the situation? Rules: no dice, no urns, no casinos, nothing with symmetries already built in — I want something like waiting for a repair guy or guessing when a friend will show up, a case a normal person might actually have to reason about. After the scenario, work through both applications of the principle, say which answer you'd personally go with and why, and then be honest about what feature of the case is doing the work — like what info is being smuggled in by choosing one description over the other, and whether theres any principled way to break the tie or whether we just have to accept that indifference is underdetermined. One scenario only, dont give me a menu of options to pick from.
