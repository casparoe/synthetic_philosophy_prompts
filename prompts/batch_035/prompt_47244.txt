I'm a program officer at a small ethics-focused funder, and our board has set aside roughly three years of a modest budget — enough for a handful of philosophers and perhaps one empirical collaboration — for work on the ethics of pan-sentience: the possibility, taken seriously by panpsychist and cosmopsychist views, that experience exists at levels far below animals, down to cells, molecules, or fundamental physical entities. The board is split between treating this as the next expansion of the moral circle, the way factory farming was for an earlier generation, and treating it as an unfalsifiable money pit that produces papers nobody acts on. Help me set the research agenda. Which open questions should the money go to, and in what order? The candidates I'm aware of: whether the combination problem is a genuine bottleneck for everything downstream or can be bracketed and worked around; how to reason about expected moral value under moral uncertainty, given that one theory of consciousness can multiply the number of morally relevant subjects by some absurd factor and our decision procedures weren't built for that; whether there are decision-relevant markers or middle positions short of solving consciousness — something like a precautionary principle for systems whose sentience we can neither confirm nor rule out; and what the practical upshots even are, since if micro-experience carries even tiny moral weight, ordinary physics, computation, and the maintenance of large stable systems become morally nontrivial in ways nobody has priced in. Rank the questions and defend the ranking: which are upstream of which, which are tractable for philosophers rather than physicists, and which would actually change what we fund even if only partially answered. Also flag which framings I should distrust — I have a suspicion that "how many morally relevant subjects exist" is the wrong metric to organize any of this around, but I can't articulate why, and I'd like you to either sharpen that suspicion or dispel it. End with a final line in the form \priority{q1 > q2 > q3} listing your top three questions in priority order.
