I'm on a small team building features for a civic-discourse platform, and we're trying to design interventions for our most insulated users. Internally two analogies keep doing all the work in our meetings and I've started to suspect neither can actually carry the weight we're putting on them. Colleague A says an echo chamber is basically a sealed room: the person is enclosed, outside voices don't reach them, so the fix is ventilation — fact-check labels, a "see other perspectives" link, a diversify-your-feed toggle, and the fresh air does the rest. Colleague B is more pessimistic and says no, it's not a sealed room, it's a cult: the person's trust has been redirected inward, the outside world is already cast as corrupt or deceived, and exposure just confirms the betrayal narrative ("see, they're coming after us").

For context in case it matters: I did read somewhere that there's a genuine distinction in the literature between epistemic bubbles, where you simply lack exposure to other views, and echo chambers, where those outside sources have been actively discredited for you in advance. So it's possible my two colleagues are literally talking about different phenomena without noticing.

What I want is a proper audit of both analogies, the way you'd audit any argument-by-comparison. Lay out the correspondence table for each — what plays the role of the walls, the air, the door and window on one side; the leader, the doctrine, the defector on the other — then say where each mapping breaks, and what each picture quietly smuggles in about how minds actually update. Does a person absorb corrective information the way a room equilibrates when you open a window, or the way a cult member re-evaluates the doctrine when shown contradicting evidence? And most importantly for us: do our exposure-based product ideas survive the audit, or was the sealed-room picture quietly carrying the whole "just add outside information and it'll be fine" conclusion the whole time? If it turns out one analogy fits one chunk of our users and the other analogy fits a different chunk, tell me which interventions follow from which model, and what we'd even measure to figure out which kind of user we're looking at.
