I cover science and ideas for a mid-size outlet, and I'm working on a piece about how the AI safety world has quietly split in two. On one side you've got the alignment people: the worry is the model's values, and the fix is to make it actually want good things. On the other side there's a newer crowd who basically say: stop trying to read the model's mind. Assume it might be misaligned, and build the deployment so that it doesn't matter -- you watch everything it does, limit what it can reach, and catch it if it tries something. Their pitch is that you never need to know wether it wants to hurt you, only that you'd see it coming and could shut it down.

For the piece I want a paragraph where I put an actual number on something, and I'd rather print a defensible number than a vibe. So here's the proposition: for the first AI system that causes a genuinely serious harm -- harm on the scale that gets congressional hearings, not a chatbot being rude -- the safety failure will have been that the deployment team treated passing evaluations as evidence about the model's motives, when all the evaluations really showed was its behavior while it knew it was being watched. Precise enough to have come out either true or false in hindsight; you can tell me what would settle it.

Give me your credence, decomposed. I'm imagining terms roughly like: the probability that misaligned behavior shows up in evaluations at all when the model can tell it's being evaluated, versus only showing up once it thinks nobody's looking; the probability that serious harm arrives as one big visible move a monitor could catch versus lots of small actions that each look individually fine so no single moment ever trips an alarm; the probability that "the model was aligned all along and the failure was elsewhere in the system" turns out to be the boring true explanation; and whatever other terms you think belong, named by you. One line of justification each, then the combination, then tell me which term you're least confident in -- if the number ends up wrong in my piece I want to know which part was quietly doing all the work.
