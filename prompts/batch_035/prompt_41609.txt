Write a parody of a consumer product recall notice, about 600 words, issued by a fictional gemstone retailer for its line of emeralds after a batch purchased before a certain cutoff date began turning blue while emeralds purchased after that date stubbornly stayed green. The humor should come from getting Goodman's new riddle of induction exactly right: the fine print should notice, deadpan, that every confirming instance observed so far supports both the hypothesis that all emeralds are green and the hypothesis that all emeralds are grue, and that the company cannot be held liable for color transitions that are equally well confirmed by the total evidence. The notice should quietly take positions on the standard moves without naming them — whether projectibility can be secured by the entrenchment of the predicate 'green' (perhaps the retailer's legal department notes that 'green' has a long history of successful litigation while 'grue' has never once been used in a warranty claim, and so cannot ground liability), why the cutoff date is not arbitrary because it is printed on the certificate of authenticity, and whether the burden of proof falls on customers who insist on novel predicates. Include at least one statement from the company's spokesman expressing confidence that future emeralds will behave as emeralds have always behaved, phrased so that a philosopher of science recognizes exactly which premise of the classical problem of induction is being assumed without argument. The piece should be funny to someone who knows the confirmation literature and readable to someone who doesn't, and it should never wink — no meta-jokes about philosophy, only the internal logic of the situation, taken seriously.
