I'm a PhD student in comparative politics drafting a working paper on whether German "rule-first" or American "conscience-first" folk morality has the better of it, and before I go further I'd like an audit of the package of claims I'm building on, because I don't trust my own sense of where the problems are. Here is the working set: (1) German folk morality treats conformity to Ordnung — rules, duties, the settled order of things — as itself morally weighty, whereas American folk morality is conscience-first, treating the individual's inner judgment as the final court even against the rules; the crosswalk comparison captures it for me: Berlin pedestrians waiting at a red light at 3 a.m. with no car in sight, versus American jaywalking as a non-event. (2) Kant is best read as the philosophical systematization of the German rule-first disposition. (3) Eichmann as Arendt described him — the rule-following functionary as the face of the banality of evil — shows that rule-first morality is the more atrocity-prone disposition and conscience-first the more resistant one, so America has the better of it. (4) I also hold, though, that the obedience experiments and their replications suggest unconditional deference to authority is roughly a human universal rather than a German peculiarity. (5) Germany's post-war culture of institutionalized remembrance — reparations, memorialization, legal bans on Holocaust denial — strikes me as evidence that a rule-first public can self-correct through its own rule-respecting institutions, which makes rule-first morality the more robust long-run guardrail. (6) And I hold that only a rule-first populace can sustain high-trust, low-corruption institutions of the kind Germany has, since conscience varies too much from person to person. Which of these commitments pull against which, how badly, and what is the cheapest fix for each genuine tension? I suspect (3) and (5) cannot both stand as I've stated them, but I may be wrong even about that — some of what worries me may dissolve on inspection (for what it's worth, I keep going back and forth on whether the American civil disobedience tradition, with its acceptance of punishment, is really conscience-first at all). If, after the audit, you think my overall verdict — that one side or the other has the better of it — isn't supported by what survives, please say so rather than rescuing it. And throughout, tell me how confident you are in each judgment and flag the one you are least sure of.
