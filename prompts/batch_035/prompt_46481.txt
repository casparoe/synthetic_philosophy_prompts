I run a small podcast comparing Buddhist and analytic philosophy, and I want to build an interactive quiz for listeners: "which kind of no-self do you actually hold?" The problem is that people who say "there's no self" usually mean really different things — Parfit-style reductionists who accept persons exist but deny they're a "further fact", hard eliminativists who think there's literally nothing there to find once you look, the two-truths/conventional-self crowd who think the self is fine for everyday purposes but empty under analysis, and the fiction-theorists who say the self is constructed like a club or a wave. I want maybe 8-10 questions, each a concrete case where those views actually come apart — teletransporter, fission, whether exhaustiveness of the aggregates matters, whether fully believing in no-self should make you less attached to your future self, whether a centipede-like aggregate-swarm could still be owed promises, that kind of thing. No question should just ask "do you think the self is an illusion" — I want people sorted by what they say about cases, not by the label they like. Then give me a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the positions, and flag any answer combos that basically nobody holds, with a sentence on whether that's because the combo is incoherent or just unattractive. One more thing: include at least a couple questions that separate the metaphysical thesis from the practical/therapeutic one (the claim about what happens when you stop identifying with your thoughts), since my listeners constantly conflate those and I want the quiz to catch it.
