I've been in an emotions reading group for two years now and the fight between judgment theories (emotions are evaluative judgments, roughly the old Solomon/Nussbaum line), perceptual theories (they're perceptions of value -- fear is something like seeing the snake as dangerous), and pure feeling theories (they're just felt bodily changes) has gotten completely predictable. Judgmentism dies by phobia cases and recalcitrant emotions, the judgment people retreat to "as-construed" judgments or hybrids, the perception people get asked why fear then feels like a racing heart and not like seeing red, and the feeling people get told they've made emotion too dumb to explain why fear fits the snake rather than fitting the racing heart. Everyone knows every argument and nobody has moved an inch. My working hypothesis is that the dispute isn't really about what emotions are but about which explanandum gets priority -- rational role, phenomenology, or causal profile -- and that each theory is decent at one and terrible at the other two, so each side reads the others' failures as fatal and its own as mere qualifications. Is that right, or is there a genuine substantive ontological question hiding in here that's just hard? Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, using what each side actually says, and tell me whether anything -- a conceptual distinction, affective neuroscience, an agreed set of test cases -- would count as progress given your diagnosis, or whether it's just ill-posed and always will be. Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my question.
