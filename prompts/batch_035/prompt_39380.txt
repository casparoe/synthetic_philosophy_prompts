I'm an undergraduate taking a seminar called "Violence and Society" (sociology with some history mixed in), and we've just spent three weeks on the debate over whether the world has become less violent. A classmate in my section who read the big popular book on the decline of violence gave the rest of us this summary of its thesis at our study group, and I wrote it down because something about it nagged at me: "Pinker proved that every form of violence has declined steadily throughout human history, which makes today the most peaceful era ever. In medieval Europe you were dozens of times more likely to be murdered than in Europe today. Hunter-gatherer societies were even more violent than that — anthropologists calculate that roughly a quarter of their men died in warfare, so life before civilization was bloodier than anything since. Even the world wars weren't as deadly, once you adjust for population, as the big conflicts of earlier centuries, and since 1945 the great powers have fought each other exactly zero times. Since violence declines in a steady straight line, we can extrapolate: war is on its way out the same way dueling went, and great-power war will soon be as unthinkable as a duel is to us."

I haven't read the book, and I don't want to read eight hundred pages just to check a paragraph. My professor keeps saying that the question "is the world becoming less violent?" isn't even well-posed until you specify relative to what — per capita or in absolute numbers — and against which baseline, and which categories of violence you're counting. My classmate's summary never makes any of those choices explicit, and I suspect that's where my nagging feeling comes from, but I can't rule out that it's something else entirely.

Please audit the summary part by part. For each claim, tell me which parts a defender of the decline thesis would accept as stated, which are simplifications a specialist would grumble about but let pass, and which are outright misrepresentations the book's own author would reject. I'd like you to pay particular attention to the slides between per-capita and absolute numbers; to whether "every form of violence has declined" is defensible given that different kinds of violence (homicide, war, state violence) seem to have declined on very different timelines; to how settled those hunter-gatherer fatality estimates actually are among the people who produce them; and, above all, to the last two sentences — whether the "steady straight line" picture follows from the thesis, and whether the extrapolation to a future without war is something the book licenses or a conflation of "has declined" with "will keep declining." Please write your answer in American English.
