so I'm TA-ing a first-year world history course, and next week's seminar is on whether history repeats itself. I want to give the students a handout that works as a decision tree they can walk down before they've read anything by the thinkers involved. The branch questions need to be things they can answer off intuition, not off the authors' names — stuff like "if a society collapses, does a new one eventually run through broadly the same stages?", "is the thing that repeats a political regime, a ruling dynasty, a whole civilization, or humanity as such?", "is the pattern escapable if people are virtuous or self-aware enough?", "does the whole story have an endpoint?".

Each leaf should then be a named position: Polybius and his rotating constitutions (anacyclosis), Ibn Khaldun's dynastic cycle where group cohesion (asabiyyah) builds up and then decays over a few generations, Vico's corsi e ricorsi, Spengler treating each culture as an organism with a basically fixed lifespan, Toynbee's challenge-and-response where breakdown is self-inflicted rather than fated, and on the linear side some version of Christian teleology, Enlightenment progress, Marxist stages, and plain declinism ("things just get worse"). I definately want the linear positions as leaves too, not just the cyclical ones — the whole point is the tree should sort people on whether they see a pattern at all before it sorts them on which pattern.

Please build the logical space first — decide what the branch questions are and what combinations of answers they generate — and only then locate each named position inside it. If two positions land on the same leaf (I suspect Spengler and Toynbee might collapse together, which would've annoyed Toynbee), flag it and either add a question that seperates them or admit the tree can't. Also mark any combination of answers that nobody has ever occupied, and say briefly whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Keep the node questions plain-language, nothing a student needs a philosophy degree to parse, and no quotes from the originals — I don't need exact wording or dates, just structure. Thinking around 5-6 internal questions and 8-10 leaves, but add or cut if the structure demands it. Two pages of handout max.
