I've just been voted onto the committee of my local canoe club (small club, about 80 members, committee of six, and all the actual deciding happens in a WhatsApp group rather than at meetings), and something about how we operate is bugging me enough that I want to know whether it's a known thing with a name.

The pattern: whenever something comes up where the club could either do something or do nothing, and nobody bothers calling a proper vote, what ends up happening is whatever the one most gung-ho committee member wants. Two examples from just the last couple of months: one of us thought we should have a stall at the regional regatta — nobody else cared much and two of us were mildly against — but she went ahead and booked it out of club funds, and now it's a fait accompli and the rest of us look petty for complaining. Another member posted a photo of the new clubhouse keys on the club's public Instagram before the landlord had confirmed we could announce the move. In both cases, if you'd asked each of us privately, "should the club do this when only one or two people on the committee are keen?", most of us would have said no. The rule everyone seems to actually hold is "the club does it if enough of us are in favour". But since acting needs no permission and stopping an action needs someone to actively stick their neck out, the club's real rule has quietly become "the club does it if anyone at all is in favour". The outcome tracks our most enthusiastic member, not our typical one — as if the group decision is the maximum of everyone's eagerness rather than some average.

And there seems to be a mirror-image thing, which may or may not be the same phenomenon: jobs everyone agrees should be done but that are mildly unpleasant — chasing the bloke who's three months behind on his subs, updating the safeguarding policy — never get done, because each of us is only slightly in favour, and each of us assumes someone else will get round to it. There the group's decision seems to be the minimum of everyone's willingness, and its definately not just laziness, because the same people happily do things they're individually keen on.

So two questions really. Is there an established name for the first pattern, where a group ends up doing whatever its single keenest member wants even though each member privately thinks acting on a minority's enthusiasm is wrong? And is the second pattern (the min-willingness one) the same thing, its opposite, or something unrelated? If there are established concepts for either or both, please say what they are and how exactly they differ from what I've described — the nearest concepts too, and why each of those doesn't quite fit. If one of them has no name, coin a term for it, with a definition and a note on the boundary cases where it shades off into neighbouring things. Please be upfront about how confident you are in each identification, and explicitly single out the one point you're least sure about.
