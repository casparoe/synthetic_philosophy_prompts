Here's a view I've been defending after a few beers and I'd like to know what would actually break it: the reference class problem isn't really a problem in practice, because there's a usable rule -- pick the narrowest reference class that still contains enough members for the frequency to mean anything. So if I'm estimating whether my startup dies, "European seed-stage B2B SaaS with a solo technical founder" beats "all startups", because narrower classes are ones whose members share more of the causally relevant features, and narrowness is a decent proxy for relevance. What would count against this? I can already feel some strain myself -- nobody can tell me what "enough members" means, and in the doomsday argument narrowing all the way down to "observers who have wondered about the doomsday argument" seems to do something perverse rather than something better. There's also a circularity worry a friend raised: if narrowness helps only because narrower members are more likely to match on the relevant causes, then relevance is doing all the work and the rule is just smuggling in a causal account of what makes a class the right one. Rank the defeaters for me from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a committed defender of the rule would concede versus explain away, and be honest about whether the "enough members" clause has quietly made the view immune to counterexamples -- and if so, whether that's fatal for a heuristic of this kind or just what heuristics are like.
