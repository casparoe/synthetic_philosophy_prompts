Got into an argument at a party and now I can't let it go. I asked whether we should, even in principle, worry about hurting future language models, and this guy waved it off with "it's just predicting the next token." When I pushed he brought up the stochastic parrots thing and said it's statistics all the way down, nobody's home. Later a philosophy PhD friend of mine said the sentence is doing something sneaky: it has the same shape as saying humans are "just neurons firing," which is obviously true but obviously doesn't settle anything, so the real question is which precise claims the sentence could be expressing that would actually settle something.

Can you do that dissection? Lay out the distinct readings of "it's just predicting the next token" as a list — from readings that are trivially true of anything implemented in that architecture (the way everything in a brain is electrochemistry) to readings that would, if true, genuinely rule out morally relevant experience in there. For each one say whether it's plausible, and whether it would actually support the move of dismissing the welfare question outright. I especially want to understand the word "just" — it seems to smuggle in an assumption that prediction is definitionally shallow, but I gather there are arguments that predictive modeling at scale requires things like world models or self-models, and I can't tell whether that rescues the moral question or is a separate issue. And some people claim it matters what a computation is *for* versus what it does — like whether a forward pass that is functionally something like pain counts differently depending on whether it emerged from prediction training rather than being pain's usual job. Does any reading of his slogan even touch that, or is that a whole different axis?

Give me a menu I can actually bring back to the argument, with verdicts, not one essay defending a single reading. And be honest about which reading people probably have in mind when they say it at a party, versus the ones that hold up to scrutiny. My friend doens't want a lecture, she wants ammo.
