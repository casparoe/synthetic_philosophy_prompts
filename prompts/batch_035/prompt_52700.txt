I write features on science and ideas for a mid-size paper, and my collegue Dana runs our op-ed page's most-read column, which is basically a hypocrisy-watch thing. Every week she rules on whether some public figure "gets to say that" -- whether they have standing to blame, I guess the philosophers would call it. She's been doing it for years, and her rulings are actually pretty consistent, but I've never been able to pin down the exact rule she's running, and now there's a case coming this week I genuinely can't call. Help me predict her.

Her track record, from memory:

1. A TV preacher condemning adultery from the pulpit while carrying on an affair himself: she called him a hypocrite, no standing, obviously.

2. A guy who quit smoking fifteen years ago writing a furious op-ed about how selfish smokers are for burdening the health system: she defended him. Has standing.

3. A climate campaigner who still flies transatlantic maybe ten times a year, lecturing people who fly like it's a bus: no standing, she said, doesn't matter that he flies less than the people he's yelling at.

4. An ordinary meat-eater going after trophy hunters on social media: she said he has standing, which surprised me, since he pays for animal suffering three meals a day.

5. A retired coal executive bankrolling climate ads that blame current oil CEOs: no standing. Her line was something like -- he spent decades as the defendant, he doesn't get to switch to prosecutor just because he aged out.

The new case: a former manager of a big pig operation, ran gestation crates for fifteen years, now the face of a campaign to ban them. There's a viral clip of him going after ordinary shoppers, basically saying you know exactly what you're paying for. And the wrinkle: he's not repentant at all. No apology tour, no donations, he just says his inside knowledge is exactly why he can point fingers. So he's leveraging the very thing he did wrong as his credential.

Predict Dana's ruling. I'd like the answer structured in sections, in this order and rough length: "Her track record, compressed" -- a short recap of what her verdicts actually commit her to, maybe 100 words; "The rules that fit" -- the two or three candidate principles that each fit all five past rulings, around 250 words, with a note on where each one strains; "My prediction" -- your best guess for the pig guy and why you prefer that rule over the rivals, maybe 200 words; "What would settle it" -- the further case (design one) where the candidate rules diverge, around 100 words; and "How confident and where I'd lose the bet" -- a short section, 80ish words, where you say explicitly how confident you are in the prediction and single out the single point you're least sure of.

Don't just give me a bare yes or no -- I want to understand the carving, what exactly she's tracking and what she isn't. And if you think her rulings are actually not consistent, or that the pig case splits the difference in some way the past cases don't, say that too.
