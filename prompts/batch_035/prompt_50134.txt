my reading group just had our last session of the semester on the "hinge of history" question -- the idea that people alive right now might be unusually influential, because if existential risk is mostly bunched into the next century or two, then decisions made now either secure everything or lose everything, and later generations inherit whatever we left them. anyway the discussion went like three hours and resovved absolutely nothing, and i want to close out the semester by sending everyone a poem, and i think it should be a villanelle, because the form is basically what the argument felt like: the two refrains should be the two voices that kept butting heads all night. one is the pull of "this time is actually different, we could be the ones who decide the whole future" and the other is the deflating reply that every generation has been sure it was the hinge, and they've all been wrong so far, so the smart money is on us being ordinary too. someone who knows the debate should be able to tell which refrain is which without being told.

a few asks. somewhere in the middle stanzas i'd like one stanza set in some past moment when people were just as certain the hinge was *then* -- rome burning, or the plague years, or someone watching the first test of the bomb -- not to dunk on them, but because that's the strongest version of the deflating voice and it deserves a fair hearing. i'd also like the poem to take seriously the part that makes this hard rather than just flattering: you can't just say "probably we're not special, case closed," because nobody gets to observe the timeline from outside, you only ever find yourself asking the question from wherever you happen to be standing, and that cuts both ways. but don't let it resolve -- the villanelle ending, where both refrains finally have to share the last two lines, should feel like where we actually ended up, which was both voices being right at once and nobody able to prove which one gets the last word.

please make it a real villanelle, refrains alternating like they're supposed to and then meeting at the end, not free verse that name-drops the hypothesis -- one guy in our group is a form nerd and he WILL check. it should be good enough as a poem that someone who's never heard of any of this still likes it, and tight enough that someone deep in this stuff doesn't wince at how the argument is framed. no pressure lol
