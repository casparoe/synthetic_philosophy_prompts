I run a little "big questions" meetup at my local community center, kind of a philosophy-in-a-pub thing but without the pub, about 14 regulars, no academic background between us, and we do one 90 minute session a month. Last session one of the guys, Tomás, who's deep into pop neuroscience podcasts, showed us this illusion on his laptop: two dots flash in different spots, first one red, then a green one a bit to the side, fast enough that you don't see two flashes. What you actually see is one dot moving across, and here's the insane part -- it seems to change from red to green somewhere in the middle of its journey, maybe even before the green dot has physically appeared. People were literally arguing about where the color change "happened" in their own experience. Tomás then explained the two candidate stories: either the experience was fine at first and your brain rewrote the memory afterwards like the Ministry of Truth in 1984, or your brain held everything back and edited the change into the film before it ever got shown to "you", like a show trial where the verdict was decided beforehand. He said the nicknames for these are the Orwellian and the Stalinesque story, which everyone loved. But the thing that's been eating at me since is his last comment: that maybe there is genuinely no experiment, even in principle, that could tell the two apart, because both stories predict the exact same reports from the subject.

So I want to build next month's session around this and I want it to be an activity, not a talk -- our group checks out the moment anyone lectures for more than five minutes. My rough idea: start by actually running the illusion on everyone and making them commit, in writing, to when and where the color change happened in their experience, maybe with some kind of betting mechanic using fake chips so they feel the stakes of their answer. Then split the room into two camps, one defending each story, and have each camp try to design an experiment that would catch the other side out -- and hopefully run into the wall that every experiment they come up with gives identical results under both explanations. I'd like a timeline for the whole 90 minutes, the rules for each phase, materials I can pull off with a laptop, a projector and stuff from a dollar store, and a debrief that maps what happened in the room onto the actual philosophical point about the timing of experience.

A few practical things: assume no lab equipment and no way to run actual controlled experiments on anyone, so if the activity needs people to "measure" their own experience, tell me how to fake that convincingly. Also, I want you to predict what will go wrong the first time I run this -- like someone insisting they can just "watch more carefully" and catch the trick, or the two camps talking past each other -- and how I should handle it. And be honest with me about which part of the idea this activity just can't deliver: I suspect the "no fact of the matter" thing is hard to make people *feel* rather than just be told, and I'd rather know where the game hits its limit than pretend it doesn't. Don't worry about dumbing the philosophy down, but remember nobody here has read anything, so anything we need should come out of the activity itself or from what I've described above. One typo in my notes for the session already caused a fight about whether the dot was orange, so please also tell me how to keep the illusion itself from becoming the whole discussion.
