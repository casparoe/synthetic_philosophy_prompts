So quick context: I'm a philosophy PhD student (working on corrective justice stuff, mostly) and I somehow got roped into sitting on my university museum's provenance & restitution advisory board, because apparently they wanted one person on it who wasn't an art historian. Our actual decision this semester concerns six objects from the Benin Bronzes -- pieces taken in the 1897 British punitive expedition, which our museum bought through London dealers and auctions between 1898 and 1911. So we didn't loot anything ourselves, but the provenance is unambiguous that they came off the market right after the looting.

The complication, and why the board is genuinely stuck: restitution in principle is basically settled among us, nobody is seriously defending "keep them because we're a universal museum" except our director, who does keep making that argument. The fight is over the *recipient*, and this is where it gets philosophically nasty. The museum world long assumed returns would go to Nigeria's national commission and eventually to the new museum being built in Benin City. But in 2023, Nigeria's outgoing president gazetted a declaration vesting ownership and custody of repatriated Benin artefacts in the Oba of Benin, the traditional ruler -- which blindsided a lot of the European museums mid-transfer. And apparently the dispute between the royal court and the museum side back in Benin City has gotten genuinely heated. So now we're choosing between roughly four options on the table:

1. Unconditional transfer to the Federal Republic of Nigeria as currently constituted, following whatever the current legal custodial arrangement is (i.e., effectively the royal court)
2. Unconditional transfer but channel it toward the museum in Benin City, on our judgment about which custodian is appropriate -- which feels like us deciding an internal Nigerian political question
3. Transfer of ownership but with a long-term renewable loan back to us, so they physically stay while being Nigerian property
4. Hold off on transfer, fund digitization and full provenance publication (the Digital Benin database already exists and is good), revisit in a few years

Here's what I keep noticing and can't resolve: the corrective justice framing everyone uses says the wrong was done to the Kingdom of Benin, so restitution goes to its successor -- but the successor question is exactly what Nigerians themselves disagree about, and any choice we make picks a side in a dispute between a hereditary monarch, a national state, and a new museum institution. There's also a member of our board who argues that option 3 (own but loan back) is basically laundering the status quo -- you've given away the title but kept the thing, which she says no other stolen-property case would tolerate, imagine "I return your car but lease it back from you." Meanwhile our director says option 1 or nothing, because choosing between Nigerian claimants is the last gasp of colonial paternalism.

Can you draft the memo I'd circulate before the vote? It needs to (a) recommend one concrete course of action for the six objects, (b) say what principle generates that recommendation in a way that would also tell us what to do with the other colonial-era objects in our collection where the provenance is murkier, and (c) honestly engage the strongest argument against your recommendation rather than a strawman -- if you're recommending against option 3, deal with the "loan-back of stolen goods" objection head on, and if you're recommending against waiting, explain why the claimant ambiguity isn't a reason to hold off. Keep it shortish, like something the board would actually read before a meeting. My own view keeps flip-flopping between "the recipient question is Nigerians' problem, return and let them sort it out" and "that's just outsourcing the hard part", so I'd also welcome a paragraph on whether that distinction is even coherent. Thanks!!
