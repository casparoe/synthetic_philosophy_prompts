The ideas to operationalize: probability as rational degree of belief rather than long-run frequency; that some probabilities are only ordinal (you can rank them but not assign numbers) and some aren't even comparable to each other; that the weight of the argument — how complete the evidence is — is a separate axis from how probable the thing is; and that some questions get no probability at all, they just sit in the uncertainty bucket. Constraints: ~45 minute meeting, max 10 minutes per question, most attendees have read zero Keynes, and whatever output we record has to feed into our play-money staking somehow. Then run it on three items from our actual queue: whether it snows enough to close campus before finals, whether the incumbent wins our city's mayoral race, and whether the central bank cuts rates by June. And be blunt with me: if the procedure ends up telling us to stop assigning numbers to half our list, I'd rather know now what that does to the whole point of the calibraton leaderboard than find out after we've rebuilt everything.
