hi! my normative ethics seminar just wrapped up the semester on whether genuine moral dilemna's are actually possible. we spent most of the time on the classics: sartre's student torn between staying with his mother and slipping off to join the Free French, the ought-implies-can style argument that there's always some all-things-considered obligation that quietly dissolves the apparent dilemma, and then the residue point (we read it through bernard williams) — the idea that the guilt you keep carrying after doing what you ought is itself evidence that something was really left undone, that the dilemma wasn't just an appearance.

i want to close the final session with a poem, and i've decided it has to be a pantoum. if you dont know the form: lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next stanza, so the exact same words keep returning in shifted contexts and end up meaning something different each time. that seems almost too perfect for this material — a line can read as plain description on its first pass, then come back as an accusation, then as a consolation that fails. i want you to actually use that mechanic rather than just obeying it mechanically: the repeated lines should visibly change their force as they cycle (you decide how, that's the interesting part), and the last stanza — where the form traditionally brings back the opening lines — should land those lines differently than they landed at the start. that shift should do the work of showing what the residue view is getting at, but without the poem itself settling whether the dilemma was genuine or got dissolved by an overriding ought. our seminar ended exactly on that unresolved note and i want the poem to honor it.

make it maybe 6 stanzas or so, set it in the student's situation (no names, no citations, just the scene — the mother, the train, the war), plain diction, no archaisms, since my students will read it out loud. the bar is that someone in the seminar who knows the literature should nod at what the form is doing, and someone who knows nothing should still get a good poem out of it. also please write in american english — i've got a mix of students but we've been using american spellings all term. thanks!
