My roommate is doing a course on international development and keeps coming back to Garrett Hardin's lifeboat thing from his 1974 essay. The picture as she tells it: talk of "spaceship Earth" is a fantasy because there's no single ship and no captain, just a bunch of separate lifeboats. The rich countries are in boats that are basically at capacity, the poor are swimming in the water around them, and pulling swimmers aboard out of compassion just swamps the boat until everyone drowns -- so foreign aid and open immigration are self-defeating. She uses it mostly to argue against food aid specifically, and she's pretty convincing about it, honestly. I'm an engineering student so this isn't my home turf, but the image feels so vivid that I suspect it's doing more argumentative work than it should.

Can you audit it for me? Like, what exactly maps onto what -- who is the boat, who are the swimmers, what is carrying capacity supposed to correspond to in real-world terms (arable land? food supply? absorptive capacity of economies?), and is the water itself supposed to represent anything or just the condition of being poor? Then where the mapping breaks down: things like whether the swimmers' predicament is actually caused in part by the people in the boats (which has no analogue in a real shipwreck), whether capacity is fixed the way a boat's is, whether the boats are genuinely separate given trade and extraction, whether swimmers can be helped without being pulled aboard at all (towing the boat? passing supplies over the side?). And then the main question: once the disanalogies are on the table, does her conclusion against food aid actually survive, or was the boat carrying the whole argument?

Also curious about one meta thing: I gather Hardin was reacting against the spaceship Earth metaphor, which I think goes back to Kenneth Boulding and was popular with environmentalists, so if you could say a word about whether the lifeboat is a genuine fix for the spaceship's problems (one ship implies one owner, one steering) or just trades one distortion for another, that'd be great. My roommate and I have a bet riding on whether a better metaphor exists at all, so if you think there is one, name it.
