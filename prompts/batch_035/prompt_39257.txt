I run a seminar on anthropic reasoning whose two most persistent members—one a defender of the Self-Sampling Assumption, the other of the Self-Indication Assumption—have finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration instead of another year of talking past each other. Draft the joint statement they should produce, formatted as a numbered section of agreed claims, then a disagreements table, then the cruxes. The agreed-claims section should include whatever they can jointly sign about how self-locating evidence enters Bayesian conditioning and about which thought experiments—the Presumptuous Philosopher, the doomsday argument, the incubator cases—any resolution must answer. The disagreements table should state their residual differences so precisely that each would accept the wording of their own view, especially regarding the Presumptuous Philosopher case and the doomsday argument. Among the cruxes, include at least one about whether the Self-Indication Assumption is a legitimate correction for the fact that hypotheses positing more observers render one's existence more probable, or whether it instead double-counts by treating one's existence as evidence for populous worlds again after one has already conditioned on existing. For each crux, describe a concrete case both would accept as bearing on it and record each side's prediction in advance; one such case, invented by a member of the seminar, should be a cloning experiment in which a single AI system is instantiated either once or a billion times depending on a fair coin, with every copy asked, before receiving any further information, to bet on how the coin landed. Finally, if any part of the SSA/SIA dispute strikes you as verbal—for instance, a disagreement over credence that dissolves once rational betting behavior is fixed—say which part, why, and how much of the dispute survives once it is set aside.
