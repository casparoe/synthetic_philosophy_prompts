So I need a memo from our committee before the 15th and I'm hoping you can draft it, since I'm frankly out of my depth on the political theory side of this.

Context: I sit on the steering committee for our city's participatory budgeting program. We allocate about $4 million a year. Right now it works via open assemblies — anyone who shows up can speak and vote on project proposals. The problem is that attendance is wildly unrepresentative: mostly retirees from two of the wealthier neighborhoods, and turnout is like 1% of the city. A council member has proposed replacing the open assemblies with a lottery-selected panel: 36 residents drawn by lot, stratified roughly to match the city on age, gender, income and district, paid a stipend, meeting over six weekends with expert briefings, and their recommendations would go to a citywide ratification vote. There's also a proposed amendment to create a "shadow panel" of volunteers who weren't drawn but want to participate — they'd sit in the same room and submit comments.

The committee is split roughly three ways. Some think the lottery is an obvious upgrade — the current system is participatory in name only, and a stratified random sample would actually look like the city. Others are bothered that we'd be taking away the right of any resident to just show up and have a say — even if in practice only a skewed sliver exercises it, the openness is doing some legitimating work for them. And a third group doubts the competence part: a bunch of randomly selected laypeople evaluating technical transit and drainage proposals over six weekends, with experts presumably chosen by staff, seems to them like decorative democracy with a nice random gloss. One member keeps bringing up that Athens did a lot of selection by lot, another keeps saying Athens isn't an argument, and honestly I can't tell who's winning that exchange.

Can you draft the decision memo? It should recommend something concrete — approve the lottery panel, reject it and keep the assemblies, or approve with conditions — and give reasons the other factions could actually engage with rather than just restating our positions back at us. Please also say what you think of the shadow panel specifically, whether it's a real fix for the participation objection or just a fig leaf that quietly reintroduces self-selection through the back door. And whatever you recommend, I want the memo to take the strongest point on the losing side seriously — my sense is there's a real tension between the panel being representative as a snapshot of demographics and being representative in the sense of having actually heard and weighed the arguments, and I'd like the memo to acknowledge whichever horn it grabs rather than pretend the tension isn't there.

Tone can be plain and committee-ish, not academic. Length: whatever fits on 2ish pages. If it matters for the rec, assume the ratification vote stays in place either way — the lottery panel would be recommending, not deciding.
