Every writeup I see eventually runs into the same wall, which is what counts as "the same mechanism", and at that point it stops being engineering and starts being philosophy, so I'm bringing it to you.

Give me your actual credence that there's a principled fact of the matter about when two forward passes use the same mechanism — a fact that's independant of which feature basis we rotate to, which interpretability tools we happen to have, that kind of thing. Don't just tell me it's a family resemblance thing. I want a number and the decomposition: roughly, the probability that mechanisms are natural kinds of the network (real units, more like algorithms than like lines of code), the probability that the training distribution picks out a priviledged decomposition the way a reference class is supposed to, the probability that some clean equivalence relation exists even if we never manage to compute it (does a translated circuit count as the same mechanism? a recompiled one?), plus whatever other terms you think belong, with a one-line justification each. Then combine them, tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and what evidence or argument would move that term the most.
