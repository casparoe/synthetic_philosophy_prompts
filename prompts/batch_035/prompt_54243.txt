I'm in a reading group that's been slowly making its way through historical materialism (this year it's the base/superstructure stuff), and I keep bumping into a move that I don't have a name for, and it's starting to bug me that I can't name it. Someone claims some superstructural thing — a legal doctrine, a family form, a religion, whatever — is explained by the economic base. Then someone points at a case where the thing seems to float free of the base: the same inheritance rules persisting across wildly different modes of production, a religion comfortably outliving the class that supposedly needed it, feudal guild forms hanging around deep into capitalism. And the response is never "ok, fine, so the base doesn't explain this one." The response is always to find some *further* function the thing serves. Feudal legality persisted into capitalism? Well, it stabilised the transition, or legitimated early bourgeois property, or lowered the costs of governance for the new class. And here's the thing: each individual rescue is genuinely plausible! I'm not saying these explanations are stupid. The problem is structural, not that the people are dumb. The theory racks up a point every time an explanation is found, but never loses a point when an explanation has to be *searched for* — the search itself gets counted as productivity, because hey, look how much the superstructure does for the base once you dig into it.

I know some nearby stuff. Popper said Marxism (and psychoanalysis) were unfalsifiable, but that's not quite it, because the individual explanations here are falsifiable and often pretty good — it's more like the theory has a ratchet where its failures get converted into richer versions of itself. Evolutionary biologists talk about "just-so stories" and spandrels, which is close, but there the complaint is usually that the story is cheap because nothing is at stake, whereas here stuff IS at stake and the stories often pan out. And I gather G.A. Cohen and Jon Elster had a fight about functional explanation in Marxism, whether you need to specify the feedback mechanism that makes the trait serve the base — that's in the neighborhood but it's about the *microfoundations of a single explanation*, not about the pattern across many. What I'm after is a concept for the whole configuration: a research program where every anomaly is metabolized into an additional explanandum, such that the scheme can't lose and nobody treats that as a cost. My friends keep telling me it's just confirmation bias but it doesn't feel psychological, it's a property of the explanation-scheme itself, which is why I think it deserves its own name.

So, two questions really. Is there an established term or literature for this exact pattern, in philosophy of science or wherever it lives? And if there isn't a clean one, I'd like you to coin something and define it properly — because I want to be able to distinguish it from (a) ordinary auxiliary hypotheses, which also rescue theories but where we at least pretend to be bothered by them, (b) genuine theoretical enrichment, where the new function would have been surprising in advance and so the theory pays for it, and (c) plain unfalsifiability. Give me the edges: what's the least controversial real instance, what's a borderline case, and what would a case have to look like for the label to NOT apply. I won't be satisfied with "that's Popper's falsifiability criterion," I've already considered that and it doesn't carve it right.
