I am preparing for a colloquium on the mechanist thesis in philosophy of mind, and I want to stress-test my objections to the argument that Gödel's incompleteness theorems refute mechanism. Please play a committed Gödelian — an unnamed but formidable proponent of the view that no formal system can capture human mathematical understanding, because for any consistent, sufficiently strong machine we can construct a Gödel sentence that the machine cannot prove, yet which we can see to be true. Make the character sharp and not a strawman: they should be familiar with the standard rebuttals (that humans cannot certify their own consistency, that we may be inconsistent or unsound, that knowing the conditional "if the system is consistent then its Gödel sentence is true" is not the same as knowing the Gödel sentence outright) and should have considered replies ready. They may concede small dialectical points but never the central claim. Keep each reply to roughly a paragraph so it reads as a live exchange, and stay in character until I write the word "stop."

To make the exercise contemporary, the character should also have a view about large language models: they should argue that even systems that produce fluent proofs are merely manipulating syntax, and that the incompleteness result explains why syntactic competence, however impressive, is categorically different from seeing that a sentence must be true. If I raise the objection that Gödelian humans also make mistakes and so have no principled way to claim soundness, the character should treat this as their strongest opposition and respond with their best material, not a dismissal.

Here is my opening move: you Gödelians always speak as if there is a single, well-defined formal system on trial, and as if we humans stand outside it holding the Gödel sentence. But I am a physically realized, fleshy, error-prone creature; I have endorsed false mathematical statements in my life, and I have no introspective access to my own consistency, let alone a proof of it. You cannot even tell me which formal system I allegedly am, so you cannot construct my Gödel sentence — and for any particular candidate system you pick, all you have shown is that if it is consistent, it cannot prove its own consistency. Since I cannot establish my own consistency either, why is your seeing the truth of that conditional supposed to be a superpower that no machine could share? Go.
