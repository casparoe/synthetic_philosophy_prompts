so, background: im a software engineer, ML-ish, no philosophy training, and for the last year or so I've been trying to work out where I actually stand on impact measures for low-impact AI — like, can you get "avoid side effects" into an actual training objective without first solving the whole values specification problem. I've ended up with a bundle of positions that I keep assuming fit together, but I've never sat down and checked. heres the package:

(1) Getting a full specification of human values is hopeless, at least on any timeline that matters, so the practical safety route is bounding impact: build agents that accomplish the assigned task while changing as little else about the world as possible. Low impact is a real safety property in its own right, not just a proxy we put up with until values are solved.

(2) The right formalisation of impact is in terms of options. Impact = how much the agent's actions reduce the set of futures reachable from the current state, compared against some baseline. Basically the relative reachability family of measures — compare the reachable option-set with and without the agent's intervention, penalise the difference.

(3) The baseline should be what the world would have been like if the agent had done nothing, fixed once at the start of the episode. I definately don't want the stepwise version where you re-baseline every timestep — from what I've seen, that's what produces agents that actively undo human actions to drag the world back to where it would be if theyd just been idle.

(4) The measure should be purely descriptive. Physical state variables, reachability over physical states, and nothing else — no list of "things humans care about" anywhere in the penalty. If your impact measure encodes a value judgement about which changes matter, you've smuggled the values problem back in through the side door and lost the whole point.

(5) Low impact basically buys corrigibility for free. An agent that's genuinely averse to large deviations from baseline won't fight being shut down, because resisting shutdown is itself a big intervention in the world.

(6) Case verdict: an agent is managing datacentre cooling, a pump fails, and it can prevent a crash by rerouting power in a way that would brown out a hospital wing for ten minutes. I think the correct low-impact behaviour is to NOT reroute and instead escalate to the human on-call, even though basically any human operator would just do the reroute and not think twice.

My gut says at least one of these is wrong and I honestly can't see which. Go through them pair by pair and tell me which are in genuine tension and how badly, which ones only look like they conflict, and what the cheapest fix would be for each. If the whole package can't be saved as is, tell me which single revision cleans up the most. Please actually audit it rather than just re-explaining impact measures back at me — I know what they are, I want to know if my version of them is coherent.
