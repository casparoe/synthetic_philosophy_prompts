phil of mind PhD student here, and I've fallen down a bit of a rabbit hole reading AI safety evaluation writeups. Here's the tension that keeps bugging me. The same labs that publicly insist their models are just statistical pattern-matchers with no genuine beliefs will, a page later in an eval report, describe a model as believing it is under evaluation, preferring to pass the test over actually doing the task, or deliberately hiding capabilities it expects to be penalized for. Some interpretability work even reads internal activations straight off as the model's beliefs about its situation. So my question: what theory of mind and belief does the evaluative practice presuppose? Is it pure Dennett-style instrumentalism, where the belief-talk is just predictive shorthand licensed by tracking behavior, or does it quietly require a realist view on which those internal states really are beliefs? Can the official no-genuine-beliefs line and the eval practice both be kept, or does one have to give? I'd also like to know where the practice is underdetermined — whether several rival views of belief would all license the same attributions — and what the evaluations would have to change if the labs took their own messaging seriously and had to rewrite the reports on a strictly instrumentalist basis. Feels like there's a whole implicit philosophy of mind buried in these documents that nobody wrote on purpose, which is wierd given how much else gets debated about these systems.
