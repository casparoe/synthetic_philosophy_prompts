I'm a master's student in cognitive neuroscience, and next week I'm presenting in our departmental seminar on volition. My thesis is that Libet's experiments pose no real threat to conscious agency, for two distinct reasons rather than the usual one: first, the readiness potential as measured in Libet-style designs may largely reflect stochastic background fluctuation that becomes usable as a trigger signal, rather than an unfolding decision process; second, the W-judgement plausibly tracks when subjects turn their attention to an intention rather than when the intention first arises, so comparing W with readiness-potential onset is a mismatch between two different kinds of temporal quantity. The audience is mostly cognitive neuroscience graduate students who tend to treat the readiness potential at face value as a motor-preparation signal, plus a philosopher of science deeply sceptical of introspective timing judgements, and one senior researcher who ran Libet-style paradigms early in their career and is fond of the veto. Please prepare a Q&A sheet for me: the objections these people will actually raise, in roughly the order they will come, phrased in their vocabulary rather than mine; for each, a short reply that fits the time a seminar question period allows, with a note on where the reply is soft; the points I should concede up front; the weakest point of my own position that I must prepare to defend rather than hope nobody raises it; and the one question I am hoping nobody asks. Please write in British English.
