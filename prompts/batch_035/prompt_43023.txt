my lab does consciousness work and my advisor is a big dennettian. whenever the question of first-person data comes up he reaches for the same move: heterophenomenology treats your introspective reports the way we treat a novelist's fiction. you take the text, you work out what's true in the world of the novel -- what the fictional narrator is angry about, what the fictional town looks like -- with no commitment to that world being real, and none needed. same with pain reports: build the heterophenomenological world, catalogue what's in it, get on with the science without ever settling whether it matches anything. he leans on this to argue that the reports get no special status, they're just one more data stream, like reaction times or eye movements.

my friend in the english dept thinks this is carrying way more than it can hold: novels are freely invented, can't be wrong about their own contents, and get interpreted charitably for artistry rather than accuracy -- none of that sounds like someone reporting that their back hurts. can you audit the analogy for me? what maps onto what (a table is fine), where the mapping breaks, which of the disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion my advisor wants -- demotion of first-person authority -- versus which are just noise, and whether the demotion survives once the breaks are on the table. and if the fiction picture has to go, is there a better one that gets him the same thing, or does he actually need the fiction to get it?
