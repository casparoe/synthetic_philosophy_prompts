I'm a professor of aesthetics applying for a semester-long research fellowship, and I need a three-page proposal written from a half-formed idea. The idea: the debate over aesthetic testimony — whether one can come to know that a work is beautiful on the say-so of a critic, rather than through one's own engagement with the work — has stalled partly because both optimists and pessimists treat testimony as a single undifferentiated channel. I want to split the channel: bare verdict testimony ("it's magnificent") versus reason-giving testimony ("the ending withholds exactly the resolution the whole film has been promising"), and to test whether the intuitions driving pessimism about aesthetic testimony attach cleanly to the first while fading for the second. A wrinkle I keep circling and can't resolve: a case where the testifier's judgment has been calibrated against one's own over many trials — the friend whose recommendations have never once misled me — which seems to make even bare verdicts trustworthy, and I can't decide whether that supports the optimist, or instead shows that calibrated testimony is no longer testimony in the relevant sense and so falls outside the debate. Turn this into a proposal with a precise statement of the question, an explanation of why the standard positions leave it open, the method I would actually pursue across the semester, what a successful deliverable would look like, and an honest account of what would count as the project failing. No bibliography; I will add citations myself. Two requests on that front: if you think the verdict/reason distinction collapses under pressure, or that the calibrated-testimony wrinkle is doing all the real work and the rest is scaffolding, say so plainly rather than building the proposal around my framing; and I'd rather have the proposal flag its most contestable step than present the whole plan as if it were on firm ground.
