here's a view i've landed on from thinking about it on my own for a couple years (no philosophy training, this is just shower-thought territory that got out of hand): for the really big first-time life choices — having a kid, emigrating, leaving a career to care for a dying parent — there is no such thing as a rational decision. the decision needs information about what it will actually be like, and you only get that after the choice, from a person you'll have already become, who didnt make the decision. the decider and the knower can never be the same person. i basically believe this but i want the strongest case against it, not a strawman.

a few wrinkles ive noticed that might matter and id like addressed: (1) is the missing knowledge the what-its-like kind (like how you cant imagine a taste youve never had) or just ordinary uncertainty about consequences, and does the view only really bite for the first kind? (2) other people whove done the thing testify constantly — when, if ever, is that good enough substitute for first-hand knowledge? (3) partial previews — babysitting, trial runs, renting before you move. do they undermine the view or just push it back a step since the full thing is still new? (4) staying put is also transformative, so if the view rules out rational choice it seems to rule out everything, which feels like it proves too much.

rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones someone committed to the view would honestly concede vs explain away, and be straight with me about whether its been qualified into unfalsifiability — and whether that even matters for a view about this kind of thing.
