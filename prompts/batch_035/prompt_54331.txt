longtime lurker here so go easy on me. I've gone from thinking evolutionary debunking arguments were just people being edgy online to actually holding one, in its global form. My version: the problem isn't that evolution made us selfish (thats the pop misreading), it's that natural selection would have shaped our evaluative dispositions more or less the same whether or not any of them tracked stance-independent moral facts. Given that, our moral beliefs being true would be a coincidence the evolutionary explanation doesn't need, so those beliefs can't be much evidence that realism is true.

What would count against this? I don't want a laundry list of realist comebacks — I want the defeaters ranked by how decisive they'd be, and I want to know which of them a committed debunker would themselves concede as counting against the argument, versus which they'd wave off as a misunderstanding. Things I'm especially curious about:

- What empirical findings would actually move me? E.g. if moral convergence across societies turned out to be driven by reflection and consistency-checking rather than by fitness or coordination, does that rehabilitate our moral epistemology, or does the debunking just kinda shift a level down, since the reflective dispositions were themselves selected?
- Third-factor replies: what would have to be true for one to genuinely work — some factor reliably correlating with both fitness and truth — rather than just being hand-waving? And is there a version of that reply against which the debunker should simply concede?
- Has the argument been qualified into unfalsifiability? If a defender can always retreat from global to local debunking, or swap in a "no epistemic access" version whenever a counterexample shows up, is that a problem for an argument of this kind, or is that just how it goes?

For what it's worth, I suspect I'd personally find the cultural evolution stuff most persuasive, if it holds up.
