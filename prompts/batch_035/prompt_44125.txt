His credence in the claim is roughly 0.85; mine is roughly 0.35, though I don't trust my decomposition of it.

Please give your own credence, decomposed into terms you choose, but I'd want at least the following on the table: (i) the probability that the axioms invoked at each stage — transitivity and completeness of preference, and especially the independence or substitution axiom, which does the work of getting from coherent probabilities plus preference ordering to expected utility specifically — are genuine normative requirements rather than technical conveniences; (ii) the probability that the Dutch book arguments establish probabilism itself, as opposed to showing only that something is amiss when credences and betting dispositions come apart; (iii) the probability that being exploitable in principle is a rational failure at all, given that an agent might refuse the whole sequence of trades, or never find itself facing the exploitation; and (iv) the probability that the arguments extend beyond idealized agents — whether they bind bounded, computationally limited agents or only the frictionless constructions the proofs describe. Assign a rough probability and a one-line justification to each term, show how you combine them into your overall credence, and say which term you are least confident in and what argument or consideration would shift it most.
