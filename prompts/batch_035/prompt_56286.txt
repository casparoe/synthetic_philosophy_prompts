My grandad was a diplomat for about thirty years and now writes a grumpy fortnightly newsletter that is mostly about the misuse of historical analogies. He's about to weigh in on a live argument and I want to predict what he'll say before the next issue lands, so help me out. Here's his track record from the back issues:

- Munich 1938: handing Hitler the Sudetenland was one of the worst calls ever made, though he insists the lesson everyone took from it was worse than the mistake itself.
- The Gulf War in '91: he was loudly for it, and has written that it was the one time in his lifetime the Munich comparison roughly did its job — Saddam had swallowed a whole country and nobody could tell you where he would stop.
- Iraq in 2003: against it from the very start. His line ever since is that the war party worked off Hitler's face rather than Hitler's situation — nobody asked what Saddam actually wanted next, or whether anyone's army was ready for the occupation.
- Vietnam: he says the domino argument got accepted because it let a whole generation skip the actual question, which was whether the North's demands really ran beyond Vietnam and whether that was worth a war with a superpower attached. Whatever the right answer, he says, nobody asked it.
- Rwanda: he's said the one time since the war that the appeasement comparison would have earned its keep was the West standing by in 1994. His line there is that inaction can be the Munich too.
- The Thucydides trap stuff about the US and China: he thinks it's a parlor trick. Athens and Sparta werent nuclear states with entangled economies, and a phrase borrowed from the Peloponnesian War isn't an argument, it's a costume.

The new case: a regional power that's spent a decade building up its navy — call it Arcadia — has seized a disputed island chain from a much smaller neighbor. Arcadia insists the islands were always theirs and that this is the whole claim. But hawks in our parliament are waving leaked intelligence that Arcadia's naval doctrine aims at the entire sea lane, and theyre yelling Munich. The doves are yelling Gulf of Tonkin, that we're being talked into a threat that isn't there. The hawks want grandad to endorse sending a naval task force; the doves want him to call for accepting the new status quo and negotiating.

So: what's your best guess at what he'll say, and which side (if either) will he come down on? More importantly, tell me the rule you think he's running that best explains all the earlier verdicts, the rival rules that fit those verdicts almost as well, and what each of those rivals would predict here — I suspect they don't all come out the same way on this one. And since I get to pick what to ask him at dinner on sunday: what single further question about Arcadia would most cleanly seperate those rival rules from each other?
