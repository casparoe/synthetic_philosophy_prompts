The moves:

one, the carrying-capacity premise, that there's a fixed number of seats in the boat and the number is set by biology rather than by policy, prices, or technology;

two, the ratchet, where saving people now just means more people at the next crisis, since the saved breed faster than the savers, so every rescue makes the next one bigger;

three, the step from "resources are finite" to "transferring resources makes things worse," which I feel needs a hidden premise I can't name;

four, the triage metaphor borrowed from battlefield medicine, where you write off the hopeless cases so you can save the salvageable ones;

six, Singer's shallow pond, that if you'd ruin your shoes wading in for a drowning child right in front of you, physical distance shouldn't change the duty;

seven, the free-rider logic, that if I give and you don't, my giving gets swamped, so unilateral giving is a sucker's move.
