so my reading group is doing a "write down your actual views and let everyone poke at them" session, this month on externalism, and I want to put my cards on the table before they get to do it for me. here's roughly where I stand:

(1) I fully buy Twin Earth. In 1750, before anyone knew any chemistry, Oscar's "water" picked out H2O and Twin Oscar's picked out XYZ, and they thought genuinely different thoughts while being molecule-for-molecule identical. I have zero temptation to say they meant the same thing.

(2) I don't beleive in narrow content. I've gone through the attempts to say what Oscar and Twin Oscar share — functional roles, "watery stuff" descriptions, the "same except for the environment" gambit — and they either smuggle the environment back in or come out so thin that nothing explanatory could be built out of them. So I've concluded there's just no fact of the matter about the shared thing. All content is wide.

(3) I think belief/desire explanation is causal explanation. When I say someone went to the fridge because they believed there was beer in it, the belief is a cause of the action, and it's the content of the belief that's doing the causal work — otherwise citing the belief is just decoration sitting on top of the real cause.

(4) I have privileged access to my own thoughts. I can know, without checking anything or inferring from evidence, that I'm thinking about water right now and that I believe water quenches thirst. That knowledge isn't based on observing my behavior or my surroundings.

(5) It was a genuine discovery that water is H2O, not the discovery of what a word means. And the 1750 people could have been wrong about water — it could have turned out the clear drinkable stuff in the rivers was something quite different. That's how you can be wrong about a substance, not just mislabel a bottle.

(6) I think Burge's arthritis case goes the same way as Twin Earth. The guy who tells the doctor he has arthritis in his thigh: his word meant arthritis, the joint disease, so his belief is flatly false — even though from the inside it never felt like he was misusing the word.
