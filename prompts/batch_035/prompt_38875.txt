quick context: i'm taking a seminar this semester on social norms and the state, philosophy dept but cross-listed with sociology, and we just wrapped up the unit on tight vs loose cultures — you know the gelfand stuff, how societies that faced more historical threats (invasions, famines, disasters) end up with stronger norms and harsher punishment for deviance. now we've moved on to the unit on rule-following and obedience, and i want to write my term paper on the dutch gedoogbeleid thing, but i genuinely can't tell if it's a case of looseness or of something the framework has no category for.

the thing itself, in case the name doesn't ring a bell: in the netherlands some things stay formally illegal but the authorities openly announce they won't enforce — cannabis is the famous example (coffeeshops operate in plain sight, everyone knows where they are), but also things like the old squatting rules and euthanasia back before it was legalized. the word is gedoogbeleid, "toleration policy," and courts sort of semi-recognize the situation without it being actual legality. so the rule is still on the books, nobody repealed it, the state keeps the power to enforce whenever it feels like it, and yet in practice nothing happens to you.

here's what's bugging me: the obvious reading is "netherlands = loose culture, low punishment, high tolerance, checks out." but it doesn't feel like plain looseness to me. a truly loose place would presumably just legalize, or have weak norms to begin with. the dutch kept the rule and only suspended its enforcement — that's not caring less about rules, that's caring enough to keep the rule around but treating it as a kind of tool you only pick up when needed. is that looseness, or is it actually a very ruley way of being loose? and what does an arrangement like that presuppose about what a rule even is: that a rule's authority lives entirely in enforcement, that legal rules are just instruments rather than expressions of collective commitments, that citizens can be trusted with discretionary space? the other weird part is it cuts both ways for the people involved — smokers get freedom but zero legal protection, since technically they're still committing a crime, so the "toleration" is unilateral and revocable at any moment. apparently the etymology even suggests the word comes from something like "to bear, to endure" — so it's not modern tolerance-as-a-value at all, more like "we will bear this evil because stamping it out costs more than enduring it," which has a completely different moral flavor.

so what i want help with is reconstructing the implicit theory of the practice:

(1) does the tightness-looseness framework even have room for a norm that's formally strict but practically relaxed? it seems like the framework kind of assumes norm strength and willingness-to-punish rise and fall together (that's more or less the mechanism), so when they come apart does it just have nothing to say, or can it be stretched?

(2) what would a society have to believe about rules for gedoogbeleid to be a stable, legitimate arrangement rather than a hypocrisy problem? like, why doesn't it collapse over time into either full enforcement or legalization — is there some presupposed picture of the state as calibrating rather than commanding?

(3) is the dutch case evidence that tight/loose is really two seperate dials — how strong the norm is vs how willing you are to punish violations — rather than one scale? because if those can come apart, a lot of the comparative stuff we read suddenly looks under-specified.
