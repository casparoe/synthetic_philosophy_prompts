In my civics class, a discussion about lowering the voting age turned into a running argument between two classmates, and they have asked me to referee it, which I do not feel able to do on my own. The disagreement began when our teacher mentioned that some countries allow sixteen-year-olds to vote, while long-term resident non-citizens in most democracies cannot. Rohan argued for what he called the "affected principle": anyone whose interests are meaningfully shaped by a decision has a claim to a vote on it, which would include sixteen-year-olds, resident non-citizens, and even future generations, perhaps through proxies. Maya replied that this is far too expansive, since almost every policy affects almost everyone in some diffuse way, and that the right criterion is being subject to the law's coercion: resident non-citizens belong, but future generations do not, because they are bound by no law yet. Rohan countered that "subject to coercion" has symmetrical problems: tourists passing through are subject to the laws during their stay, so her principle seems to hand them a vote, while excluding future generations who will bear the consequences of today's environmental policy far more than any tourist will. Maya answered that brief, consensual subjection is not the same as standing subjection, and that future generations can have their interests protected without being members of the demos, just as animals' interests can be weighed without animals voting. Rohan's last move was that children are plainly subject to the law's coercion and yet we deny them the vote, so her criterion cannot be sufficient either; and if she patches this with a competence requirement, he does not see why competence would not equally defeat his side's claims, since an affected but incompetent person is affected all the same. Could you adjudicate this exchange move by move — saying who is right at each step and why, not merely who wins overall — and, in addition, tell us whether there is something both of them are missing about what the membership question is actually for?
