In a metaphysics seminar I've been asked to write up my position on dispositions for others to challenge, and I'd like a consistency audit before I submit it. Here is the package. (1) The conditional analysis of disposition ascriptions is basically right: to say a glass is fragile is to say that it would break if struck, in suitable circumstances. (2) Fink cases — say, a vase wired to a device that, whenever the vase is struck, instantly removes the property responsible for its breaking — show only that the analysis needs a ceteris paribus clause, something like "provided nothing intervenes to remove or block the manifestation"; they do not warrant abandoning the analysis, and in any case such cases are rare curiosities. (3) Masks — the styrofoam packing that keeps a fragile glass from breaking when it is dropped — are handled by exactly the same clause: finks and masks are one problem, not two. (4) I also accept that the ceteris paribus clause cannot be filled in without circularity: any attempt to specify the suitable circumstances in non-dispositional terms either collapses back into disposition vocabulary or runs on forever. I take this to be a harmless limitation of the kind any analysis of dispositional concepts must face. (5) Dispositions are intrinsic properties of their bearers: whether a glass is fragile depends on the glass's own makeup, not on what surrounds it — two intrinsically identical glasses cannot differ in fragility. (6) Fragility is multiply realizable: two objects can share the disposition while differing completely in their realizing microstructure. So: which of these commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what is the least costly revision? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and for each one be explicit about which commitment would have to give and what giving it up would cost the rest of the package.
