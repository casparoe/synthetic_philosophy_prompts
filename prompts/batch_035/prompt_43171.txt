hey, so I write a science-and-ideas column and I'm doing a piece on why anyone bothers to vote. The rational-choice crowd keeps telling me a ballot is basically a lottery ticket: the odds your vote decides anything are astronomical, the prize isn't even personal cash — the "winnings" get split across the whole country — so the expected value is negative, and anyone who votes is being as sloppy as people who buy scratch cards. Then a political scientist friend said no, voting is more like singing the anthem at a match: nobody thinks their voice is what makes the noise, and that's fine, because the point is expressing who you are, not causing the outcome. The annoying thing is these two analogies get used for opposite conclusions — voters are irrational / voters are perfectly rational — and I suspect both are smuggling stuff in. Can you lay out the correspondence table for each one (what plays the odds, the ticket price, the jackpot, the anthem, the crowd), then say where each breaks? I'd especially want to know: does the lottery picture have any story for why turnout goes up when elections are expected to be close, and can the anthem picture explain why closeness matters to people at all? And at the end tell me straight: which conclusion, if either, actually survives once the disanalogies are on the table. Since this is going in print, please say how confident you are in the different claims and single out the point you're least sure of — I'd rather know where the ice is thin than get something that sounds cleaner than it is.
