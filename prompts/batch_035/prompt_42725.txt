I'd like your credence that Peano Arithmetic is inconsistent — a number plus the derivation, not a reminder that Gödel's second incompleteness theorem forbids certainty from inside the system.

Decompose the question into at least the following terms, assigning a rough probability and a one-line justification to each:

1. The probability that our pre-formal conception of the natural numbers — the structure we take ourselves to be describing with "0, 1, 2, and so on" — is genuinely coherent, so that any axiomatization sound for that conception is automatically consistent.
2. Conditional on that conception being coherent, the probability that PA actually captures it: that no axiom smuggles in something false or self-undermining. Treat the induction schema as the obvious suspect — unrestricted induction over a language that can arithmetize its own syntax is a great deal of power folded into one assumption.
3. Whatever residual weight you give to unknown unknowns: the possibility that something in the neighborhood of ultrafinitism is right and the trouble lives at numbers too large to name, or that the arithmetization of syntax lets the system reason about its own proofs in a way that quietly builds in circularity.

Then, separately and conditionally on PA being inconsistent, estimate the probability that the shortest proof of a contradiction is short enough for human or machine search to ever find, versus the possibility that inconsistency coexists indefinitely with undisturbed practice — PA formally inconsistent but "locally" sound for every statement with a feasibly short proof, so that mathematics never notices.

Combine the terms into a final credence. After that, I want three things:

- Which term dominates the number, which you are least confident in, and which sub-question the final figure is most sensitive to.
- How your confidence is actually sourced: how much comes from Gentzen-style consistency proofs (which trade PA's consistency for trust in transfinite induction up to ε₀), how much from the intended-model argument, and how much from the empirical track record of formal arithmetic never exploding. Say plainly whether these are three independent sources of support or one intuition wearing three costumes.
- What would move your number most — for instance, a new independence result of an unexpected shape, evidence that PA-proofs behave strangely at large quantifier depth, or a specific argument that second-order categoricity arguments assume the very coherence they are meant to establish.

For context on where I want this to land: a colleague maintains that any credence in PA's consistency below 1 is a category error, on the ground that the existence of the standard model is fixed by what we mean by "natural number" — no more open to doubt than the claim that bachelors are unmarried. I sit around 0.9999. If you had to locate our disagreement — inside term (1), in the epistemology of how semantic knowledge relates to formal knowledge, or somewhere else entirely — where does it live, and which of us is more likely to be making the mistake?
