I'm taking a seminar on the Vienna Circle and we just spent two weeks on Neurath, and now I'm confused about something the professor kind of waved off. So Neurath is famously the anti-foundationalist of that crowd, right -- he argued against Schlick's idea that there's a bedrock of protocol sentences that can validate the rest of science, and there's his whole image (the boat one, rebuilding the ship plank by plank while at sea, never in dry dock) for how knowledge gets fixed without foundations. At the same time he sank a huge amount of his actual working life into the ISOTYPE project, the picture statistics with Gerd Arntz and Marie Neurath, where one little figure stands for, say, a million people and social facts get displayed as rows of identical icons. The pitch there, as best I understand it, was that ordinary language drags metaphysics along with it, words like "soul" or "essence" get you in trouble, whereas pictures are cleaner and also more democratic -- workers with no university training were supposed to be able to read facts about society straight off the chart.

Here's my confusion. The conventions of ISOTYPE are obviously choices someone made: how many people a unit figure stands for, when to use a bigger symbol instead of more symbols, what the colors carve out. And the whole Vienna Circle line on symbolism was that signs are conventional, stipulated, arbitrary -- that's exactly why they thought you could build artificial languages that avoid the traps of ordinary ones. So did Neurath think pictorial conventions somehow escape this, or that they're better than linguistic ones because they're motivated (a picture of a miner looks like a miner) rather than arbitrary? And then there's his physicalism, the thing-language talk about space-time chunks that everything sayable is supposed to be translatable into. A row of icons isn't obviously a statement about space-time events at all, and I've read that Neurath didn't even treat the pictures as strictly empirical statements but more like tools for orienting people. So I can't tell if ISOTYPE is supposed to be continuous with the rest of his philosophy or if it's basically a propaganda / civic education venture that he never bothered to make consistent with it.

Write an essay on whether Neurath's picture-language project fits with his anti-foundationalism and physicalism or stands in real tension with them. I want both sides taken seriously -- the reading where ISOTYPE is the natural public face of his view of unified science, and the reading where it quietly presupposes exactly the kind of unmediated, foundation-like access to facts that he spent the protocol-sentence debate denying. Don't land on "it's both, more research needed" -- defend an actual verdict, and if you think the tension is only apparent, say exactly where the appearance comes from and what dissolves it. If it helps, you can bring in the contrast with Carnap's attitude toward constructed languages, since Neurath was notoriously hostile to that way of doing things, which makes it extra weird that he'd have his own universal medium.
