I record a short interview series for my school's history society, and next week's guest is a historian who has spent her career defending Daniel Goldhagen's side of the Browning–Goldhagen debate: that the men of Reserve Police Battalion 101 and comparable units killed because they were ordinary Germans, shaped by an eliminationist antisemitism centuries deep in the culture, rather than ordinary men broken by situation, conformity, and the routine of occupation. Draft eight to ten interview questions that go beyond asking her to restate the thesis and instead press her where the literature actually presses. At least one question should concern the Józefów action of 1942 and the small minority who accepted Major Trapp's offer to step out, and what that episode shows about how costly refusal really was; one should concern Browning's evidence of revulsion among the shooters—men requesting reassignment, vomiting during the killings—and how she reconciles such evidence with genuinely willing execution; one should press the circularity charge, namely that the eliminationist culture is inferred from the killings and then invoked to explain them; and one should ask what evidence, if it existed, would count against the eliminationist thesis. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer she is most likely to give. Please keep the tone respectful: she is a distinguished guest, and my audience will include our head of history.
