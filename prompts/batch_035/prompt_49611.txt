Take the birth of animal welfare law in Britain and run it through Peter Singer's expanding circle idea, then tell me where the idea breaks.

Quick facts so we're on the same page. In 1809 Lord Erskine got a bill against wanton cruelty to animals through the House of Lords, and it died in the Commons, where William Windham attacked it as an evangelical crusade against traditional English pasttimes like bull-baiting. Thirteen years later, in 1822, Richard Martin ("Humanity Dick") got the first real animal protection law through — but it only covered cattle, oxen, cows, sheep. And the courts read it as not even covering bulls, so the baiting sports themselves stayed legal. Dogs didn't get protection until the 1835 act, which also finally banned bull-baiting and dog-fighting outright. Meanwhile the SPCA was founded in 1824, and Martin personally prosecuted offenders, famously bringing a beaten donkey into court as evidence.

So: Singer's story (building on Lecky's original image) is that moral reason pushes toward impartiality and universalizability, so the circle of moral concern keeps widening — from tribe, to nation, to all humans, and plausibly on to animals, since the capacities that matter don't respect species lines. Map the episode onto the theory. Who or what was outside the circle in 1822, what exactly counts as the argument for pulling anyone in, and what does the theory predict about the order in which animals get admitted? Because the order is where it gets interesting: the circle's first residents were the animals people owned and made money from, and the last were the ones tormented purely for entertainment. If impartial reason were doing the driving, shouldn't the animals suffering worst, for no purpose at all, have come first? Or is the better reading that sympathy and economic interest open the gate, and reason just follows behind tidying up the justifications — in which case what is the expanding circle theory actually a theory of?

I want the mapping done carefully — what's inside vs outside, what the motor of expansion is supposed to be, who the "agents" of expansion even are in this story — and then an honest verdict on what the theory explains here and what it can't, and whether this episode counts as a confirmation with a lag, an anomaly, or evidence the theory has the causation backwards. Don't be shy about saying the theory is silent where it's silent; forced fits are worse than gaps.
