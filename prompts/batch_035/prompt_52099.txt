I'm in a philosophy of mind reading group that's mostly scientists, and last week we argued about an old Dennett paper called "Quining Qualia" (late 80s, I think from an edited volume on consciousness and science). Afterwards one of the guys, who took a phil of mind course years ago, gave us his rundown of it, roughly: Dennett lists the four things people believe qualia have -- they're indescribable, they're intrinsic, they're private to you, and you can't be mistaken about them -- and then runs a bunch of thought experiments, mostly about coffee tasters whose coffee-tasting experience changes over the years without them being able to tell whether their taste buds changed or their preferences did, to show that qualia fail all four tests. So qualia don't exist; there's just brain stuff happening that we have no direct access to, our introspective reports are basically confabulations, and the upshot is that consciousness doesn't really exist, it just seems to. He added that this was pretty much accepted until people got sentimental about zombies a decade later, and that the title is a joke about quining something out of existence, like an insecticide brand.

I'm inclined to think he got the broad strokes right but something felt off, partly because I've heard Dennett gets visibly annoyed at being called a consciousness denier, and that seems hard to square with this summary. Can you go through it piece by piece and tell me what a philosopher of mind would sign off on, what's a simplification, and what's just wrong? I'm especialy unsure about a few things: (a) whether the argument is really "qualia lack all four features, therefore no qualia," or whether something more subtle is going on with how the four features are supposed to hang together in the first place; (b) what the coffee cases are actually meant to show about introspection, since "we're sometimes wrong about whether it was us or the world that changed" doesn't obviously get you to "we have no access to our own minds"; and (c) whether the last step, from "there are no qualia in this sense" to "consciousness doesn't exist," is a step Dennett takes or one my friend took for him. And if that last step isn't his, what does he think survives instead?
