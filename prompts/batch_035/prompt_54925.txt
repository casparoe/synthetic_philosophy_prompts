I've sort of talked myself into a package deal on Boltzmann brains and I want to stress-test it before I go around believing it. The package goes like this: in a universe like ours with a positive cosmological constant, the far future is an endless de Sitter-like phase, and given literally infinite time, thermal fluctuations will assemble observers with my exact memories vastly more often than ordinary evolution ever did. So if I'm supposed to reason as a typical observer, I should think I'm almost certainly a fluctuation. The move that saves me, and the one I've been leaning on, is the self-undermining point: a Boltzmann brain's memories are noise, so the hypothesis that I am one destroys the very evidence -- my knowledge of physics, of the cosmological constant, of the whole setup that generated the prediction in the first place. Carroll's phrase for this is cognitive instability, and I gather his claim is that theories dominated by Boltzmann brains can't be true and justifiably believed at the same time. Fine. But on the strength of that move I've concluded something stronger: that cosmologies with an eternal de Sitter future are not just uncomfortable but actually ruled out, and that something in the picture has to give (the vacuum decays, the constant isn't really constant, whatever). So my question is what would count against this whole package, and I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. In particular I'm wondering about the objection that fluctuated observers could in principle have accurate records -- that not every Boltzmann brain is a deluded one, so the self-undermining argument only kills some of them and the rest come back to bite. And about the measure problem: whether the claim that fluctuations outnumber ordinary observers depends on an arbitrary way of counting observers over infinite spacetime, such that the whole "crisis" might be an artifact of the measure rather than of the cosmology. Also, and maybe this is what bugs me most: does the self-undermining move generalize in a way that should worry me? If I can dismiss any unsettling anthropic conclusion by pointing out that it undermines the reasoning that led to it, have I insulated my view from everything, and is that a problem for a position of this kind? Tell me which of these objections the defenders of the package would concede as real costs, which they'd wave off, and whether any of them should actually move me.
