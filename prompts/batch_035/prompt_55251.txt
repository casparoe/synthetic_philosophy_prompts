Is there a name for this move? I keep running into it in arguments about taste -- novels, wine, movies -- and I can't find the right concept anywhere.

Someone defends a claim like "the works that are genuinely great are the ones the best judges agree on." Fine so far. But then it turns out the best judges are picked out partly by what they agree on. If a panel of respected critics loves some pulpy thriller, well, those critics were never the right kind of judges. If a bunch of trained sommeliers rate a cheap wine above a famous vintage in a blind tasting, then the tasting must have been flawed, or those particualr palates weren't really trained. The judge pool keeps getting redrawn so that it always contains the people who converge on the "right" verdict, and the right verdict is defined as whatever they converge on. It's like the measuring instrument is validated by the reading, and the reading by the instrument.

I know it smells like No True Scotsman, but it feels different: nobody is redefining "critic" or "thriller" to dodge a counterexample. They're adjusting who counts as the measuring device. It's also not plain question-begging, because the person making the move usually does have some independent criteria for picking judges -- experience, track record, whatever -- it's just that when those criteria point somewhere they don't like, the verdict wins and the criteria quietly bend. And it's clearly not a straight appeal to authority, since appeals to authority at least let the authority disagree with you.

Does this have an established name, in aesthetics, argumentation theory, philosophy of science, wherever it lives? If something is close, tell me how it differs. If nothing is exact, coin a term and define it. And don't just hand me a label and walk away -- I want to know what the phenomenon is and isn't, and where the edges are.

Please structure the answer like this:

1. "Nearest concepts" -- the closest established terms, roughly what each says, and why none of them is exactly this (~150 words)
2. "The carving" -- what the move precisely is, and how it differs from No True Scotsman, plain circularity, and ordinary appeal to authority (~200 words)
3. "A name, if needed" -- either the existing term that fits best, defended, or your coined term with a tight definition (~100 words)
4. "Edge cases" -- about three test cases where it's genuinely unclear whether the move is being made (e.g. someone who only trusts judges they expect to disagree with, or recalibrating an instrument after a known failure), and how the carving from section 2 rules on each (~200 words)
5. "Why it matters" -- what goes wrong in arguments about taste when you can't flag this move (~100 words)

Keep it casual -- I'm not a philosopher, I just argue too much about books on the internet.
