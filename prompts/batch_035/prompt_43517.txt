Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a naive realist disjunctivist and a representationalist — the latter holding that veridical perception and subjectively indistinguishable hallucination share a fundamentally common mental element, whether that element is sense data or representational content — concerning the argument from illusion and hallucination. The document should contain: the claims both parties can sign, including about the phenomena any theory of perception owes us and about what would count as explaining them; the precise statements of their residual disagreement, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording — including whether the "highest common factor" between a veridical perception and a subjectively indistinguishable hallucination exists, and whether that question is substantive or, in part, verbal; and the cruxes, with for each crux a concrete case or argument both would agree bears on it, and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Somewhere in the document they must jointly address two cases: (a) a Charles Bonnet syndrome patient whose hallucinated figures look to her to be painted onto the scene, yet who knows, while they are occurring, that they are not real — a subjectively identifiable hallucination; and (b) the standard "perfect duplicate" hallucination stipulated to be subjectively indistinguishable from some veridical perception. They should also each state what would count against their own view, and whether there is anything both agree would actually move the debate. Format: a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table with a column for each side's phrasing and a column naming the crux that disagreement traces back to. Begin with the statement itself, with no preface or commentary.
