Two friends at my local EA meetup spent an entire evening arguing about where our small giving circle's budget should go this year, and they eventually asked me to referee, which went nowhere, so now I'm asking you. Maya wanted the money on corporate campaigns against battery cages. Her reasoning: the scale leg of importance-tractability-neglectedness is enormous — tens of billions of chickens — and unlike most big causes it's also demonstrably tractable, since campaigns have actually flipped major food companies on cage-free pledges. Dev said that's exactly the problem: precisely because it has a track record, it's no longer neglected. Big funders have noticed, money is flooding in, and the marginal dollar now buys almost nothing. He wanted us to fund wild-animal-welfare research instead: importance arguably even bigger than farmed animals, tractability admittedly terrible, but neglectedness so extreme it dominates once you multiply the factors. Maya pushed back hard on neglectedness as a criterion. She said a cause being untouched can just mean funders looked at it and passed — empty niches are empty for a reason — and that neglectedness was always supposed to be a proxy for room for more funding, not a virtue in itself. She also said that once you let one factor be 'off the charts' and multiply it against fuzzier estimates for the others, the framework stops doing arithmetic and starts doing rationalization: people just declare the product big for whatever they were already fond of, and ITN becomes a checklist you fill in after the decision. Dev answered that any framework which only steers you toward proven interventions is worse than useless, because it just ratifies whatever the current funding distribution already is — neglectedness is what makes the thing a search procedure instead of a mirror — and if the multiplication is fuzzy, fine, the point is to force you to look where others aren't. Who's right at each step of this? In particular I want your honest take on whether neglectedness is a genuine input or just a proxy that can go stale, and whether the three-factor multiplication is actually doing work or is mostly theater. Say who's more right overall.
