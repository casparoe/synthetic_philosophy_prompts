So I'm a grad student running my first-ever discussion section solo next week. The course is a big intro to world prehistory lecture course; the morning lecture is on the transition to agriculture, and my fifty-minute section is supposed to tackle the was-farming-a-mistake question. The syllabus gives them a Harari chapter arguing farming was basically history's biggest blunder, plus the Sahlins piece on hunter-gatherers as the original affluent society, and I know from sitting in on last year's sections that they'll arrive split between "obviously, look at our teeth and our backs" and "obviously not, civilization," with nobody able to say what evidence would even count. I don't want the debate itself explained to me — I need a plan for the fifty minutes: what has to be established before the mistake-question is even answerable (I suspect it's the distinction between what's good for a population over generations and what's good for the individuals actually living in it, but maybe the data should come first?), and the order after that.

The part I keep going back and forth on is the carrying example. I'm torn between the skeletons and the work-hours. Skeletons: comparing forager and early-farmer remains from the same region — stature drops, cavities and degenerative joint disease jump — which is vivid and doesn't depend on ethnography, though there's a wrinkle I'd have to handle that farmers just leave us more skeletons and sedentary cemeteries, so the samples aren't symmetric. Work-hours: the famous few-hours-a-day figure for a Kalahari group, which students love but which has been seriously picked apart since, and anyway modern foragers are a biased leftover of a much wider ancient world. Pick one carrier for me and tell me what it must have and what it must not have (e.g., should it involve numbers at all?), and why the other one works worse as the spine even if it gets a cameo.

Then the misconception map. I'm guessing the big ones: someone always says people died at 30 as though that means you were grey at 29 (juvenile mortality dragging the average down), and someone will read Sahlins as claiming foragers were materially rich rather than wanting little. But I want the full map with the point in the sequence where each one gets defused — I don't want to handle them all in a Q&A pile-up at the end. Also tell me what I should openly refuse to cover at this level (they'll want to relitigate whether moderns would trade civilization away, and I assume that's a punt), and give me one question I can ask in the last five minutes that would show whether they actually got the analytical point rather than just leaving with a strengthened prior. Also — small thing — if you think one misconception is best defused by never letting it arise in the first place (via how I frame things at the start) rather than by addressing it head on, say which and how.
