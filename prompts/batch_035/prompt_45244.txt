But last week in a lab discussion about agent welfare he used the SAME slogan to argue that our agents obviously suffer: punishment in animal learning maps onto negative reward, so negative reward in our training runs just is suffering, and anyone who doubts that is in denial about what they're doing to their agents. Something about that move feels off to me but I can't pin down why.

Can you lay out the different precise claims "reward is enough" could be standing for -- the boring definitional readings, the strong empirical ones, the ones that smuggle in something about valence or experience, the ones where it's just straight up false -- and for each say whether it's plausible and whether it actually gets my labmate his conclusion about suffering? A menu with verdicts rather than one interpretation, please. And ideally flag which reading he NEEDS for the argument, since a lot of the time the version of the slogan that's defensible turns out to be a version his argument can't use, and vice versa.
