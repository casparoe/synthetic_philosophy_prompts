My cousin's the family's amateur philosopher, and lately she's been handing down verdicts about moral status that all seem to run through brains. So far her record: a guy who had a whole hemisphere removed as a child has full human status; a sperm whale, brain like five times ours, still ranks below any human; chimps rank above dogs but bellow humans; Neanderthals would have counted fully, even though their brains were a bit bigger than ours on average; and elephants come out above pigs. Next month our book club takes up three new cases and I want to predict what she'll say: a bottlenose dolphin (brain bigger than a chimp's, and proportionally large for its body too), a gorilla (bigger brain than a chimp in absolute terms but smallish for its body), and a documented hydrocephalus case, a person with far less brain mass than normal who functions fine, drives, holds down a job. Honestly I care less about the three answers than about what rule she's actually running, because a bunch of different rules fit her track record equally well. Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no intro or conclusion: her likely verdict on the dolphin, on the gorilla, and on the hydrocephalus guy, then your best guess at the rule she's implicitly using and why you prefer it over the rivals, and finally one further case that would cleanly divide the candidate rules.
