I've read a bit of Parfit, the teletransporter stuff in Reasons and Persons, and honestly I keep feeling like the whole debate is something we deal with on a weekly basis, just with less angst. When a process migrates hosts, when we fork(), when we snapshot a VM and restore it somewhere else, when blue-green deploy cuts over from one instance to another -- we have very concrete working answers to questions like "is it the same process," "what state has to carry over for the operation to count as a migration rather than a replacement," and "what do we do when two replicas diverge from a common checkpoint." So here's my ask: restate the Parfit debate -- the teletransporter, the fission/branching cases, his eventual line that personal identity might not be what matters -- in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Process IDs, parent/child relationships, state transfer, checkpoints, replica divergence, idempotency, whatever else genuinely maps. Then give me the residue report: which parts of Parfit's argument translate cleanly, which parts you had to distort to make them fit, and which parts just have no expression at all in how we talk about systems. The thing I most want you to take seriously: in my world, two replicas with the same state at time t are interchangeable and nobody loses sleep over it, and I suspect that assumption is quietly doing a lot of work if I try to map it onto persons. So tell me whether the residue is a gap in my vocabulary (something real about persons that systems thinking just can't say) or a sign that Parfit's question was less well-posed than it looks. Don't go easy on either side.
