My therapist recently had me start a dream journal, and it accidentally started a fight between two of my friends, Maya and Dave, that I couldnt follow all the way to the end.

Maya's take: whatever I'm writing down in the morning isn't a record of anything I actually experienced. While I'm asleep there's nobody home, so the "dream" doesn't exist until I wake up and my brain composes a narrative on the spot, the way a witness confabulates a smooth story from fragments. She even said the narrative might get written into memory backwards, after the fact, so it feels like it took time when it didn't.

Dave thought this was nuts. He's done lucid dreaming, and pointed out that trained lucid dreamers can agree in advance to signal with eye movements at a certain point in the dream, and the eye movements show up on the recording while they're still asleep. So dream content is clearly being generated during sleep, not invented at waking. Also, dreams have temporal structure — he once seemed to spend a whole afternoon in one.

Maya said the eye movements only show the brain is *doing* something while asleep, not that anyone is experiencing it — a narrative generator can run offline with no audience. Dave replied that if she's allowed to say that, she has to say what an experience is such that waking life has an audience and dreams don't, and he bet she can't do it without that same "offline narrative generator" objection applying to waking consciousness too. Maya's last word was that waking life at least gets checked against the world in real time, which is exactly what dreams lack.

I mostly want to know who's right about what. Could you go through their exchange move by move and give a verdict on each step, and then tell me overall whether my journal is a record of experiences or not? Please take an actual side rather than telling me both have a point, and write your answer in American English.
