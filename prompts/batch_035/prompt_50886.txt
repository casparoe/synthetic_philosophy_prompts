write me a parody corporate memo, around 700 words, from a management consultant (invented name, McKinsey-ish deadpan voice) hired to "streamline" a mid-sized logistics company. The gimmick, never stated outright: the consultant is running the causal exclusion argument on the org chart. Frontline staff (drivers, warehouse pickers) are the physical level that "does all the real work"; middle management are mental properties, whose supposed contributions to quarterly results are the causation in question. The memo's argument should be Kim's, beat for beat: every business outcome has a sufficient frontline cause; if the regional manager ALSO caused it, we'd have systematic overdetermination of exactly the kind we don't see anywhere else in the firm (that bit should be a joke — the consultant audited the company and found no outcome with two complete sufficient causes except, suspiciously, everywhere a middle manager claims credit); middle management isn't identical to any single frontline activity (there's no one picker whose job is "the Q3 routing decision"); so middle management is epiphenomenal headcount and must be eliminated to stop "causal drainage" from the operational level. Use numbered recommendations and a savings-estimate section that treats causation like budget.

Two things I specifically want in there, as reply-memos or quoted protests filed after the memo: (1) a middle manager who defends herself with realization/token-identity — she doesn't cause the outcome over and above her team, she IS the team's activity organized at a coarser level of description, so "firing her" without touching the team is a category error; and (2) a VP of strategy who goes full downward causation — managers don't push pallets, they set constraints and channel the frontline activity, and constraint-setting is causation. The consultant should answer both in ways an actual Kim-style exclusionist would use (the realization reply gets hit with the "then your causal claim is empty unless you can say WHICH team activity you are"; the downward-causation reply gets hit with "constraints are counterfactual talk, show me the sufficient cause"). 

Keep it deadpan and internally consistent — the consultant genuinely believes the analysis, no winking. But the reductio has to land by the end: Legal, in a closing note, nervously asks whether the same analysis applies to the executive floor, and the consultant's attempted distinction should be visibly ad hoc to anyone paying attention. The jokes have to come from getting the exclusion literature right — overdetermination, screening off, realization, causal drainage, the multiple realizability point (the same "decision" being realizable by different team configurations) — so someone who knows the Kim debate laughs instead of winces, while someone who's only had a bad boss should still enjoy it. Don't name Kim or any living philosopher anywhere in the piece; the parody targets the argument, not a person.
