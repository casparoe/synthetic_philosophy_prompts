My friend and I have been arguing about whether the historical expansion of the moral circle — from the clan to the nation, then to other races and sexes, and latterly to animals and perhaps future generations — constitutes genuine moral progress. My credence is roughly 0.65 that the expansion is truth-apt progress, i.e. that the wider circles are actually closer to correct than the narrower ones rather than merely reflecting a drift in attitudes; hers is about 0.2. Please give your own number, but more importantly decompose the reasoning behind it: your probability that some form of moral realism is true; given realism, the probability that sustained moral inquiry converges rather than wanders; the probability that the actual historical expansion tracked such convergence rather than being fully explicable by morally neutral drivers such as affluence, trade interdependence, cheap mass media transmitting distant suffering, and the coalition politics of abolition; and any further terms you think belong in the decomposition. Give a number and a line of justification for each, then show how they combine into your overall credence. Two complications to weigh: the circle has not expanded monotonically, and episodes often described as expansions of concern — such as progressive-era eugenics, which treated itself as concern for future generations — look in retrospect like contractions; and cases where sentiment expanded while practice worsened, such as rising stated concern for animals alongside the industrialisation of factory farming, arguably severing the link between circle-expansion and actual treatment. Finally, tell me which term in your decomposition most likely explains the gap between my 0.65 and her 0.2, and what evidence would move your number furthest. Please answer in British English.
