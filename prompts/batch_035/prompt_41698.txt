I'm on the sustainability subcommittee of my university's dining services board, and next month we vote on removing beef from the default meal plan (with a paid opt-in kept at two campus outlets). One trustee plans to vote no on grounds I find philosophically slippery but can't quite dismiss: our students' beef consumption is a rounding error in global emissions, so the policy averts essentially no harm, and if an act wouldn't pass a cost-benefit test when done by one person it can't pass when done by us — it's green theater at the expense of students' meal choice. Another member replies that the university isn't an individual in the relevant sense: it's setting a rule for thousands of eaters over many years, and it's also one participant in a larger pattern, the same way a single vote only matters because of the pattern it joins. There's also a beef-reduction clause in the caterer's contract that costs us real money if we invoke it, so the costs aren't just symbolic. Please draft the memo I'll circulate before the vote: a concrete recommendation (adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions), reasoning the beef-opposing trustee could actually engage with rather than brush off, an honest treatment of the strongest point on the losing side, and a closing section saying what consideration would most likely flip your recommendation — where your own reasoning here is most likely to fail.
