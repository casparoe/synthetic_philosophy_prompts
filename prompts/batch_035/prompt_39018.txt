My flatmate and I started keeping a log of her judgments about when it's acceptable to take another person's word on a moral question rather than working the matter out for ourselves — it began as a seminar argument about whether moral deference is somehow second-rate, and turned into a running catalogue of her rulings. Her verdicts so far: she asked her mentor whether to report a friend's plagiarism, and acted on the mentor's "yes" without ever reconstructing the reasoning, and thinks that was entirely fine; when an acquaintance at a party declared that the host's joke had been cruel, she refused to treat that as settled and thought through the joke herself, and believes her refusal was right; she votes by her union's endorsement in elections she has no time to study, and thinks nothing of it; and when a vegan friend of many years told her that eating meat is wrong, she spent weeks reading the arguments before changing her diet, and says she would have felt cheated in herself if she had simply adopted her friend's verdict.

The new case: she is reaching for an unfamiliar chocolate bar at the shop when a stranger — no expertise, no relationship to her — tells her the brand relies on child labour and that buying it would be wrong. There is no realistic way to investigate the supply chain from the aisle, and no cheaper alternative brand on the shelf that she knows anything about either. What will she say about deferring to the stranger here? I am less interested in the bare answer than in the candidate rules that could be generating her record — I can dimly see a rule distinguishing acting on trusted advice from adopting a moral belief, a rule keyed to whether genuine deliberation is feasible in the moment, and perhaps something about who has the standing to tell her anything at all — along with what each rule predicts about the chocolate case, and your best guess, with reasons, about which rule she is actually running. Please also name the further case that would best divide the rules that happen to agree on the chocolate bar. One caveat: if you think her record is too thin, or the cases too dissimilar, for any single rule to be doing real work, say that plainly instead of manufacturing a neat theory to flatter the exercise. Please keep the whole answer under 400 words.
