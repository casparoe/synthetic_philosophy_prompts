i've been mulling something over and can't quite put my finger on it. In Europe there's this thing where you can ask a search engine to stop showing an old article about you — like you went bankrupt 15 years ago, it's fixed, but it's still the first thing anyone sees when they google you. The idea behind the law seems to be that a person deserves to outlive their past. But at the exact same time we're constantly told the opposite for societies: never forget, build memorials, keep the archives forever, because a culture that forgets its history is betraying the dead or doomed to repeat it. So the same records are supposed to be permanent when it's about "us" and erasable when it's about a specific somebody. What view of memory is that split actually presupposing? Does it only make sense if you think the past belongs to the collective but not to the individual, or are these just two ideas that ended up next to each other without any real theory behind it? And if you took the duty-to-remember side fully seriously, would the right to be forgotten have to go, or can you hold both without contradiction?
