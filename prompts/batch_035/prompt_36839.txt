My hiking club, fifty-eight members, runs an annual gear raffle sponsored by a local outfitter, and I've ended up as the one who has to decide how to handle it. The rules this year: entry is free, and between now and Friday any member may email in as many entries as they like; if the club's total stays at or under 1,000 entries, one entry is drawn at random and the winner receives a $2,000 gift card; if the total exceeds 1,000, the raffle is void, the gift card vanishes, and the sponsor instead donates $200 to the county trail fund.

Last year, under a nearly identical rule, one member — a serious poker player named Dev — emailed 850 entries in the final hour and voided the whole thing. This year he's abstaining, he says, out of embarrassment, but he argues his case to anyone who will listen: if the other fifty-seven of us behave modestly, whoever floods wins almost by certainty, and if we don't behave modestly, the raffle is doomed no matter what he does, so no one sacrifices anything real by flooding — sending many entries, he says, is at least as good as sending one in every scenario that matters, and he can show you the arithmetic. He's half-convinced at least a few members.

My own temptation runs the other way: to draft a club-wide email proposing that everyone publicly pledge to send exactly one entry each, with a running tally posted so anyone can see who's honoring it. I can see three worries about this: the pledge is unenforceable and Dev's arithmetic still applies to anyone who signs it cynically; a public tally converts a private temptation into an arms race someone might want to win at the last minute; and, perhaps worst, a fair number of the fifty-eight honestly never considered that flooding was even possible — the email would teach them. Something also tells me the fifty-odd members who quietly send one entry aren't merely suckers waiting to be exploited, but I can't articulate why without sounding like I'm appealing to the sanctity of the trail fund.

So, concretely: how many entries should I send, should I send that email (and if so, what should it say), and is there anything I can say to Dev that engages his arithmetic rather than just scolding him?

Begin your response with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning — no preamble, and don't restate my question back to me.
