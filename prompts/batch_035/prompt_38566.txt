I have been trying to fix, case by case, what I actually believe about the logical behaviour of moral sentences, and I would like to know whether some single account of their meaning accommodates all of my verdicts, or whether I must abandon some of them. My verdicts are as follows.

First, the argument from tormenting the cat being bad, together with the conditional that if tormenting the cat is bad then getting one's little brother to torment the cat is bad, to the conclusion that getting one's little brother to torment the cat is bad, is valid — and valid in exactly the way the parallel argument about rain and the streets being wet is valid. The occurrence of the moral predicate in the conditional's antecedent is no mention, no quotation, no shift in sense; it must contribute the same content it contributes unembedded.

Second, the modus tollens analogue is equally valid: from the conditional to the negation of the antecedent, given the negation of the consequent. So moral negation yields genuine contraries: one who asserts that stealing is wrong and also asserts that stealing is not wrong has contradicted himself, not merely revealed a turbulent emotional life.

Third, disjunctive syllogism works with a moral disjunct: either lying is wrong or twice two is five; but twice two is four; therefore lying is wrong. I find this valid, which means I treat the disjunction as truth-functional.

Fourth, the embedded conditional resists any paraphrase in terms of attitudes. Someone can accept that if tormenting the cat is bad then getting one's brother to do it is bad while feeling no disapproval of either act, or while being unsure what he feels. So the conditional is not equivalent to a conditional approval, nor to a claim about conditional approval.

Fifth, a parallel conditional proof seems in order: suppose stealing is wrong; then, since knowingly buying stolen goods assists theft, knowingly buying stolen goods is wrong; discharge the supposition and conclude that if stealing is wrong, knowingly buying stolen goods is wrong. Reasoning under a moral supposition appears unobjectionable, and what is detached is not a report of any attitude I hold.

Sixth, and now the tension: I am also convinced that a sincere utterance of the judgement that stealing is wrong requires disapproval, that the insincere utterer is a hypocrite rather than someone mistaken about a fact, and that the sentence is not made true by a property of stealing in the way that the snow sentence is made true by the colour of snow. Seventh, I find the sentence asserting that stealing is wrong while disclaiming any disapproval of stealing Moore-paradoxical — absurd to assert, though I hesitate to call it self-contradictory. Eighth, the sentence saying that it is true that stealing is wrong if and only if stealing is wrong strikes me as an uninteresting truism, no better than the parallel one about snow.

The difficulty, as I suspect you see, is that my first five verdicts treat moral sentences as ordinary declaratives, while the sixth pushes toward the view that they express attitudes rather than state facts; and the raw attitudinal rendering plainly cannot run the arguments, since nothing like the cat inference even gets started from an interjection and a conditional about interjections. So my question is this: state, as precisely as you can, one or a small number of candidate accounts of moral sentence meaning that would capture these verdicts — for instance, a deflationary or minimalist treatment of the truth predicate combined with attitude-expressivism, a quasi-realist project of earning the right to the logical apparatus, an error theory, or plain realism — and for each, identify which verdict motivates which clause. Then tell me what the account predicts about cases I have not listed: whether the excluded middle holds for moral sentences; what a settled non-attitudinarian asserts when she says that stealing is not wrong; and whether a moral sentence can stand as the antecedent of a counterfactual with descriptive consequences. Finally, be candid about which of my verdicts your favoured account cannot save, and what the minimal revision would be.
