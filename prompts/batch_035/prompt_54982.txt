(1) Moral status can't rest on species membership itself — that's just a biological grouping, so it has to rest on what the individual is actually like. (2) The things about individuals that could matter morally are capacities: sentience, rationality, self-awareness, that sort of thing. (3) All of these come in degrees, and there's no sharp line between having and lacking any of them — cognition shades off continuously from humans through chimps, dogs, fish, worms. (4) So wherever you place the moral-status threshold on a continuous variable, the placement is arbitrary. (5) Moral status can't be assigned on an arbitrary basis. (6) So status can't be threshold-like at all — either it comes in degrees tracking the capacities, or nobody has it. (7) But status doesn't come in degrees: we don't think a slightly smarter person has a slightly stronger claim not to be tortured. (8) So nothing has moral status, which is insane. My tutor says the conclusion just proves we should accept speciesism after all, i.e. drop (1), and that any pain in rejecting it is the price of keeping humans special. I'd rather keep (1) honestly.
