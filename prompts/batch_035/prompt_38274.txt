At a recent reading group on philosophy of mind, a discussion of large language models ended abruptly when one participant declared, citing John Searle, that "the Chinese room shows what we knew all along: syntax by itself is not sufficient for semantics". This was meant as a knock-down argument against the claim that a language model could genuinely understand anything: it merely manipulates formal symbols, and formal symbol manipulation, being pure syntax, can never add up to meaning, thought, or understanding. I came away dissatisfied, because I could not tell which precise claim had actually been established, and the slogan seemed to be doing rather different work each time it was repeated over the course of the evening.

I would like you to dissect that one-liner. Please lay out, as a structured list, the distinct precise theses that "syntax by itself is not sufficient for semantics" could be expressing. For each reading, say what exactly it claims, what would count as evidence for or against it, and whether it is trivially true, substantive but defensible, substantive and dubious, or simply false. In doing so, please attend to at least the following questions, though you may raise others: What is being counted as "syntax" — uninterpreted formal symbols, symbol manipulation governed by formal rules, or something looser? What is "semantics" supposed to include — reference to external objects, grasp of meanings, intentional states, understanding, consciousness? What is the force of "by itself" — is it a claim about what symbol manipulation can constitute, about what it can cause, or about what it can be evidence of? And crucially, does the Chinese room thought experiment itself support each reading, or does the argument's intuitive pull depend on quietly shifting between readings?

Finally, tell me which reading my colleague was most plausibly invoking, and whether that reading actually supports the conclusion he wanted — namely, that no system engaged in mere symbol manipulation, however sophisticated, could understand. I would also like to know whether any of the standard responses to the Chinese room (the systems reply, the robot reply, and the like) amount to denying one of the readings you identify, and if so, which. I intend to bring your analysis back to the reading group, so please make the structure of the different readings easy to refer to in discussion.
