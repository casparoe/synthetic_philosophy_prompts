I'm a science writer working on a piece about anthropic reasoning — the thing where you reason about hypotheses that make different predictions about how many observers exist, from the inside, as one of the observers. I've interviewed two people for the piece and they could not disagree more. One says self-indication is basically obvious: if a theory says the universe contains a trillion trillion observers, and you know you exist, then that's evidence for the theory, period. The other says that's just multiplying your prior by the number of observers and calling the result confirmation, and that it gets you the Presumptuous Philosopher result, where you end up near-certain there are vastly more observers on the basis of no empirical evidence at all — which, he says, should count as a reductio.

So here's what I want to put to you, partly to figure out how I should frame the piece: what's your actual credence that the self-indication assumption is the right way to handle self-locating evidence — as against self-sampling, or as against neither one being right because the whole question is confused or has to be recast decision-theoretically?

I want a number, and then I want the decomposition. Some terms I'd expect to show up, though add or cut as you see fit: whether the Presumptuous Philosopher is a bug in SIA or just an unpalatable but sound consequence (maybe having strong credence in the bigger world for "free" is no worse than what self-sampling gives you with the Doomsday Argument — every handle on this thing seems to come with one ugly commitment attached, so which one is uglier?); whether Sleeping Beauty settles anything here (SIA lines up with thirding, but does thirding's popularity transfer over to SIA, or is that just a confusion about what the two problems have to do with each other?); whether the reference-class problem sinks self-sampling or merely complicates it; and whether the honest move is to give up on anthropic "probability" entirely and treat these cases purely decision-theoretically, so there's no credence over observer-hypotheses to assign in the first place. Give me a rough probability and one line of justification for each term, combine them, and then tell me which term your final number is most hostage to and what would actually move it.

For context, my own gut number is maybe 0.4 on SIA, mostly becuase I find the Presumptuous Philosopher embarrasing but I find the Doomsday Argument MORE embarrassing, and my sneaking suspicion is that my two interviewees disagree less about the math than about how much weirdness a theory is allowed to absorb before you call it refuted. Tell me if you think I've located the disagreement in the wrong place.
