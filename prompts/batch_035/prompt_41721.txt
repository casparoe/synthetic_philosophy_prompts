Something's been bugging me for a while in arguments about the environment, and I'm wondering if it's an actual named thing or just a mess people haven't sorted out. I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, so bear with me.

Pretty much every debate I see about, say, paving over a wetland or restoring a forest runs on a shared assumption that nobody ever states out loud: that a forest full of animals is a good outcome, full stop. More habitat → more animals alive → better. But the more I read about how wild animals actually live, the more that looks shaky. Think of a frog that lays thousands of eggs and all but a handful of tadpoles get eaten or starve, or a deer population that booms, overgrazes, and then half of them die of famine in a bad winter. If you think the typical wild animal's life contains more suffering than not, then creating more wild animal lives by protecting or expanding habitat is plausibly creating more net suffering, not less. And here's the part that really confuses me: the same people who are horrified by factory farming purely because of the suffering it causes will cheer for a rewilding project on the grounds that it will support millions more animals. If suffering is what you care about, why does the location of the suffering (a cage vs the woods) flip the sign?

So my question is: does this clash — between caring about individual animals and caring about nature/ecosystems as a whole — have an established name or literature attached to it? If not, I'd still like to know how the people who actually study this carve it up, because I suspect it's several distinct questions tangled together: e.g. whether a typical wild life is net bad seems like an empirical question, and whether that flips the moral sign on conservation is a separate one, and then there's the whole thing about whether ecosystems have some value of their own that doesn't reduce to anything about the individuals in them. People online just wave it off with "nature is balanced," which is vibes, not an answer. I don't just want a label to google, I want to understand what the actual positions are and where the edges between them sit. Also happy to be told the premise is dumb, i.e. that wild lives aren't net bad at all — I genuinely don't know how you'd even estimate that.
