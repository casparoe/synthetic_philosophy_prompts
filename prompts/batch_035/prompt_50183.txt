My friend's roommates Marco and Elena had this long argument at a birthday dinner about prioritarianism (the view that benefits to the worse-off count for more, morally) and I've been trying to figure out who was right ever since. Can you referee it for me? It went like this.

(1) Marco said prioritarianism is just egalitarianism with math: it says inequality is bad and therefore benefits to the worse-off matter more. Which means, he said, it inherits the famous leveling-down objection — if what's bad is the gap, then knocking the rich down with zero benefit to the poor removes some of the badness, and that's obviously crazy, so prioritarianism is refuted.

(2) Elena said no, the leveling-down objection is aimed at egalitarianism, not prioritarianism. Prioritarianism doesn't say gaps are bad at all — it says the moral weight of a benefit depends on how well off the recipient is. So leveling down, which benefits nobody, is just plain bad for the prioritarian. That, she said, was the whole point of the view: keep the intuition about helping the worse-off without the leveling-down baggage.

(3) Marco said okay, but then the view is in a weird spot: if you don't care about inequality, where does the extra weight for the worse-off come from? Either it's smuggled-in egalitarianism, in which case the objection comes straight back, or the weights are arbitrary, in which case its just utilitarianism with a thumb on the scale.

(4) Elena answered that the weight can come from something like fairness or compassion — what matters is how bad someone's life is, not the gap between them and anyone else — the view is about people, not distributions. And anyway, she added, the leveling-down objection is overrated as a weapon: the egalitarian can just say leveling down makes things better in one respect (equality) while making things worse overall, and there is nothing incoherent about that, so it doesn't refute anything.

(5) Marco said that proves too much — by that logic any view escapes any objection with a "pro tanto": a view that counted the number of redheads as intrinsically good could also say leveling down the blondes is better "in one respect", and saying the objection is overrated just means you've decided the bullet is comfortable to live with.

Also tell me if there's something both of them are missing, and who (if anyone) comes out more right overall.
