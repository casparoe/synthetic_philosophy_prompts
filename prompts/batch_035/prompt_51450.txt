I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and there's a fight I keep circling that I can't figure out how to write about: the one between biologists and philosophers of biology over whether DNA literally is a code that carries information. The biologists go on talking about the genetic code, transcription, translation, signalling pathways, regulatory information — and seem genuinely baffled that anyone could doubt it. The philosophers keep replying that there's no sender and no receiver, that the code is just a lookup table correlating codons with amino acids, and that the information talk either smuggles in teleology or is a metaphor biologists have forgotten is a metaphor. This has been going on since at least the 90s and, as far as I can tell, nobody on either side has moved an inch. Before I write the piece I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict: is this a verbal dispute where the two camps mean different things by 'code' and 'information'? A substantive one about whether there's genuine semantics going on inside a cell? Or are they answering different questions under one heading — the biologists describing a causal dependency, the philosophers policing a word? And given whatever diagnosis you land on, tell me what would actually count as progress here — a sharper account of what information requires, maybe — or whether I should just accept in my article that this is a stable disagreement and explain it to readers as such.
