I am a third-year undergraduate enrolled in an upper-level seminar on philosophy of physics, and I have a term paper due at the end of the semester. Our instructor requires a two-page proposal (roughly 700 words) to be submitted first, stating the precise question, why the standard positions leave it open, the method, what the possible conclusions are, and what would count as the project failing or being unmanageable for one semester.

My half-formed idea concerns the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. As I understand it, the argument chains together facts about simultaneity in different inertial frames: because some observer's simultaneity hyperplane through a future event overlaps my present, and that same event lies on a simultaneity hyperplane of yet another observer passing through my future, the future event is already fixed relative to my present. The best-known objection, associated with Howard Stein, is that relativity recognizes only the past light cone as frame-invariant, so any notion of what is already determined relative to a here-and-now must be built from light-cone structure rather than from simultaneity, and once that is granted the argument collapses.

What I keep noticing, and what I would like the proposal to build on, is that the argument appears to prove too much. If the chaining procedure is legitimate, it can be run in reverse: the same hyperplanes that run from my present toward the future also connect my present to my past, so by parity of reasoning my present fixes the past exactly as much as it is fixed by the future. This suggests that the operative notion of determination in the argument is symmetric and has nothing to do with the direction of time at all. And that makes me suspect the argument, if valid, establishes not that the future is causally settled but merely that there is a frame-independent fact of the matter about what happens at every event — a conclusion that might follow from far weaker premises than any relativistic chaining, perhaps from the bare idea that events have determinate properties plus Lorentz invariance, with no appeal to observers or their simultaneity slices.

So my question, roughly stated: does the Rietdijk–Putnam argument show anything beyond the claim that special relativity describes a four-dimensional totality of events with determinate properties, and if not, was the appeal to observers and simultaneity hyperplanes doing no work — or worse, doing illegitimate work by smuggling in a transitivity assumption (that if A is fixed relative to B, and B fixed relative to C, then A is fixed relative to C) that relativity gives us no right to?
