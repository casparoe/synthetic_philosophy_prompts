My partner and I have been going round and round on hinge epistemology and I want a third opinion that actually commits to numbers instead of just saying both readings have something going for them. The question: are Wittgenstein's hinge commitments normative or merely descriptive?

By descriptive I mean the naturalist reading: hinge certainty is just a fact about how humans happen to hold fast — like Hume saying custom is the great guide of human life, no ought involved. There's nothing epistemically better about not doubting that you have hands; it's just the fixed point our psychology gives us, same way a cat doesn't doubt the floor is there.

By normative I mean there's a genuine third category: the hinge isn't justified (no evidence could ground it without circularity) but it's still in some sense correct to hold it and criticizable to doubt it at the wrong time and place. Not just that doubting is impractical, but that the doubter has gone wrong somehow.

My credence in the normative reading is around 0.7. Hers is maybe 0.25. Give me your own number, but more importantly decompose it. The way I see it there are roughly four terms:

1. P that the On Certainty remarks themselves (the riverbed metaphor, the stuff about the earth existing long before my birth, Moore's "I have hands") really do contain evaluative language about hinges — correct/incorrect rather than just held-fast/not-held-fast — rather than this being a reading imposed by later hinge epistemologists.
2. P that, given the evaluative language is there, the normativity is sui generis — i.e., it doesn't quietly collapse back into ordinary evidential normativity, where the hinge turns out to be justified by something after all.
3. P that a sui generis norm can survive the self-undermining worry: as soon as you call anything "correct," you've invited a demand for the standard it's correct by, and if the answer is "there isn't one," the normative reading seems to eat itself.
4. P that the whole dispute is partly verbal — that a disambiguated "normative" and a disambiguated "descriptive" could actually be made compatible, in which case our two credences aren't really in tension.

Give each a rough probability with a line of justification, combine them, and tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to. Then tell me where you think my 0.7 vs her 0.25 disagreement most likely lives — I suspect its term 3 but I'm not sure. And if you think there's a term 5 I've left out, say so, don't force everything into my decomposition.
