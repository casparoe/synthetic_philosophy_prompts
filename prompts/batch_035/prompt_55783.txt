After a long argument with a friend about whether pain is bad by its very nature, I have discovered that I do not know my own view. She held that the badness is constitutive of pain — part of what makes a sensation pain rather than something else — so that the reported cases of pain asymbolia, patients who calmly report intense pain while insisting they are not bothered by it, must involve misdescription or some other state masquerading as pain. I held that the badness must be contingent on the dislike, but I suspect my actual reactions to cases are less tidy than my stated position. Please write a 'which theory of the badness of pain do you actually hold?' quiz I can take myself and give to my study group: eight questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. It must include a case of pain asymbolia, masochistic enjoyment of pain, the morphine patient who reports pain while saying she does not mind it, and at least one case of suffering or distress without any pain, so that the quiz separates views about what makes pain bad from views about what makes anything bad. The remaining cases are up to you. Then provide a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the main positions: the constitutive view that badness is built into the feel of pain, so that asymbolia is impossible or misdescribed; the view that pain's badness lies in its constitutive tie to an urge to make it stop, so that a genuinely un-urging pain is not pain; the deflationary view that pain is a mere sensory state whose badness is always extrinsic, supplied by dislike, desire-frustration, or downstream harm; and any hybrid. Use the standard names for these positions where there are any, phrase every question so that it can be answered without knowing any of the labels, and flag any pair of positions the answer patterns cannot cleanly separate.
