ok so my comparative politics reading group has been stuck on what started as a throwaway question: which is more liberal, Sweden or Norway? I honestly figured it'd take one meeting, tops. It's now been basically the whole semester and it's become the thing we argue about whenever someone brings snacks.

roughly the camps:

Team Sweden says it's obvious: Sweden takes in way more immigrants per capita (2015 especially was off the charts), had the feminist foreign policy thing from 2014, is more secular, and the 90s school reform meant anyone can open a school. Sweden just looks like the more open, permissive, individual-choice society.

Team Norway counters with the oil: the sovereign wealth fund bankrolls a bigger and more generous welfare state and stronger egalitarian outcomes, and if "liberal" means anything politically it means left, so on left economics Norway wins. They also like pointing out that both countries passed gender-neutral marriage in 2009 and Norway got there a few months earlier, which Team Sweden absolutely hates, plus Norway was first with the corporate board quota.

Then there's the twist that keeps it alive: each side plays the "that doesn't count as liberal" card. When the board quota comes up, Team Sweden says legally forcing companies to appoint women is the opposite of liberal, that's egalitarianism riding roughshod over individual liberty. When Sweden's for-profit free schools come up, Team Norway says marketized education is only "liberal" in the European libertarian sense, and by any progressive's lights that's what the right wants. Someone brings up whaling and Team Sweden says a country that still commercially hunts whales has no claim to the liberal crown, and Team Norway replies that Sweden's on its high horse about cetaceans while having some of the strictest drug policy in Europe. NATO even got dragged in once: Norway's been a founding member since 1949 while Sweden stayed out until 2022, and somehow both sides claimed this proved their case. Eventually someone declares that "liberal" just means different things in Europe and America and we're fighting over a word, at which point someone else insists there's a real question about which society is actually more free and open, and around we go.

My working theory is that "liberal" is secretly a bundle — egalitarian redistribution, civil liberties, cultural permissiveness, market freedom — and the two countries genuinely diverge depending on which strand you pull: Norway takes the egalitarian strand, Sweden takes the permissiveness/immigration strand, and the market strand cuts across in a weird way (each side's favorite example for one strand is the other side's counterexample on a different strand). If that's right, then once you unbundle the concept, is there any residual disagreement left? Or does it collapse into (a) a verbal dispute over which strand deserves the word, (b) a bunch of separable empirical questions we could just go answer, and (c) maybe one real normative fight about whether a big welfare state is a precondition of real freedom or a constraint on it? Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, substantive, both sides secretly answering different questions, whatever fits — using the actual moves people made, and tell me straight whether anything could settle it or whether we should just pick an index, agree on it, and move on with our lives. I'd also take a verdict on whether the whaling / drug law style moves are legitimate evidence or just noise.
