A member of my decision theory reading group (call her Priya) has been pressing an argument on us that she thinks shows accuracy-first epistemology is self-undermining, and I can't find where it goes wrong even though its conclusion is absurd. It runs:

(1) On the accuracy-first picture, the epistemic utility of a credence function is its accuracy, as measured by a strictly proper scoring rule — one under which, for any credence you have in a proposition, that very credence uniquely maximizes expected accuracy as judged from itself.

(2) Rationality requires choosing, among the options available to you, one that maximizes expected utility by your own lights; and it forbids knowingly choosing something that is worse by your own lights.

(3) Consider your current credence in any proposition p. By strict propriety, that credence uniquely maximizes expected accuracy as computed using your current credences; every alternative credence in p is worse by your own lights.

(4) You know (3) holds of you, since it follows from the scoring rule and your own credences, both of which you are in a position to know.

(5) Changing your credence in p — including changing it upon receiving evidence, which is just another way of arriving at a credence in p — is therefore knowingly choosing something worse by your own lights.

(6) So it is irrational ever to update on evidence; rationality requires keeping your credences exactly as they are.

Priya adds that this makes the standard accuracy-based arguments self-defeating: the very expected-accuracy reasoning that is supposed to vindicate probabilism and conditionalization, applied to the question of whether to revise your credences at all, forbids learning anything. I am sure (6) is false and I want to keep (1), so the fault is somewhere in (2) through (5), but each step looks defensible — the propriety fact invoked in (3) is, as far as we can tell, simply a theorem. Something about the phrase "by your own lights" seems to bend somewhere along the way, but every time I try to pin it down the argument reassembles itself.
