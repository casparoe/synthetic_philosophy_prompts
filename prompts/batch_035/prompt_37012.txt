The key concept is entitlement: whether people can command food, which in the modern world mostly means having enough income to buy it. In Bengal there was plenty of grain; the British simply shipped it out while Bengalis starved, exactly as food kept leaving Ireland during the potato failure. In China the state seized the harvest. Sen's famous finding is that no democracy with a free press has ever allowed a famine, since voters and journalists punish leaders who let people starve. The lesson is clear: famines are political crimes, not natural disasters, and wherever you combine free markets with a free press, famine becomes impossible."
