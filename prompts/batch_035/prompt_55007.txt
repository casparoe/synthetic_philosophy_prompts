ok so after another late night argument at our local rationalist meetup (this one got heated enough that someone accused someone else of doing metaphysics while claiming to do science, which for us is basically a sword fight) I tried to actually write down my position on social construction, and now that it's on paper I genuinely can't tell if it hangs together. here's the set:

(1) I take "X is socially constructed" completely deflationarily: it just means X wouldn't exist and wouldn't persist without human attitudes and practices. no spooky metaphysics, just causal dependence on us. money, nations, the rules of chess, all in the same bucket.
(2) I think race is not real. human genetic variation is real and clinal, continental genetic clusters are artifacts of which populations you happen to sample, and the whole edifice is a leftover of junk 19th century taxonomy. there are no races, just people who get racialized.
(3) I think gender is constructed in a *stronger* sense than (1): the kind itself — what it is to be a woman or a man — is constituted by social practices and positioning, not merely causally sustained by them the way money is.
(4) I think trans women are women, and this is just true, full stop — not a policy proposal or a friendly redefinition — including for a trans woman living in a community that flatly refuses to recognize her as one.
(5) I think money is constructed and my mortgage is nonetheless an objective fact about the world. I've never once felt the temptation to say construction makes the debt fake.
(6) I'm a straightforward scientific realist: electrons are real, the periodic table carves nature at it's joints, and if every human vanished there would still be a fact about which atoms are gold.
(7) I'm also fully onboard with the old dissolving-the-question program from the Sequences — most "is X really real" questions are confusions to be dissolved into questions about causal roles and predictive utility, not questions to be answered.

my friends all insisted something in here has to give, but they disagreed wildly about what, and one of them claimed half the list is trivially consistent and I'm inventing problems for fun. so: which pairs are actually in tension, which only look like it, and for the real tensions, what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost? rank them by severity please, don't just list everything as equally damning. and if you think the whole package is actually fine, say so — but be specific, because the (3)/(4) thing has been bugging me for a week and I can't tell if it's a genuine problem or me pattern matching "constituted by social practices" to "depends on what the local community thinks".
