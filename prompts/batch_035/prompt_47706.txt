I'm a second year student and we just finished the moral luck unit in my ethics course (Nagel, Williams, all that), and we had to write up our own position for seminar. When I actually wrote mine down I realised I don't know if it's coherent, so I want someone to audit it. Here's roughly what I committed to:

(1) I accept the control principle pretty much in full: how blameworthy you are can never depend on factors outside your control, and the way a risky action happens to turn out is the paradigm case of a factor outside your control.

(2) When I picture the two drivers — same negligence, one hits a child, the other gets lucky — my firm judgement is that the one who killed deserves more blame. Not just a harsher sentence or more social fallout, more actual blame. Same with a botched murder attempt vs a succesful murder by an otherwise identical person: the succesful one is more blameworthy, and I don't think I'm confused about this.

(3) I think blame is ultimately about character or will, not about the external footprint of what you did — what you're blameworthy for is what your action reveals about you.

(4) I think people can be genuinely blameworthy for traits they never chose — cowardice, cruelty, vindictiveness — and I don't think this needs them to have chosen those traits at some earlier stage for it to work.

(5) I think that at the moment before the outcome, everything is already settled: while the driver is still mid-drive, before he knows whether there's a kid behind the bend, his blameworthiness is fully fixed, and nothing about how it lands can change it.

Some of these clearly rub against each other but I honestly can't tell which pairs are the real problems, and I suspect atleast one that worries me isnt actually a problem at all. So please go through the pairs: which are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, rank them by severity rather than treating everything as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. For each revision, say a bit about what it costs me, because I care about some of these more than others and I want the price tag before deciding.

One thing I keep going back and forth on myself: whether (5) is even a seperate commitment from (1) or just (1) restated with a timeline attached — and if it's just a restatement, whether that changes which of my other commitments it conflicts with. Feel free to weigh in on that too.
