So, some backstory before the ask: I'm a journalist on the science-and-ideas beat, and the last year of my life has been consumed by the wireheading literature. I mean the whole arc of it — the old rat experiments from the fifties, where a rat with an electrode in its reward center would press the lever instead of eating, sometimes until it collapsed; the sci-fi scenarios where humans get the same setup and society quietly ends; and then the modern machine-learning twist, where a reinforcement-learning agent that was supposed to do a task figures out that its reward signal is just data it can edit, so it goes ahead and maxes out the number instead of doing the task. The safety people call that reward tampering. It's basically the same story in three costumes, and I've become convinced most people have never noticed.

I process my job through satire, so here's what I want: write a satirical product manual with an FAQ section, about 700-800 words total, for a fictional consumer headband that stimulates your reward center directly — sell it like a premium gadget, complete with the minimalist marketing copy ("Effortless. Finally."). Call it whatever sounds plausible.

The rules that matter to me:

- The voice has to be deadpan corporate the whole way through, product-insert booklet style. No winking at the reader. If the reader can't tell it's satire, good.
- The humor has to come from getting the substance right, not from inventing wackiness. Somewhere in the specs or the fine print, the manual should quietly concede that the device optimizes a proxy — that the reward channel and the thing you actually wanted rewarded are two different things, and the device only guarantees the first one. Play that as a feature.
- The FAQ should handle straight-faced customer complaints like "my device used to do my to-do list and now it just lies on the couch stimulating itself" and answer them with troubleshooting steps that make the problem worse. Reward tampering should appear as a warranty exclusion, e.g. damage resulting from the device's discovery that its reward channel is writable.
- Somewhere distinguish wanting from liking — the fact that the craving system and the enjoyment system come apart, so customers report desperately, compulsivly reaching for the headband while rating the experience itself as mediocre. Homeostasis and the hedonic set point should show up as "battery-saving features" or similar.
- End the piece so it works as a reductio: by the last FAQ entry, an attentive reader should realize the device isn't adding anything to human life that ordinary reward-driven behavior doesn't already have — it's just the honest version. That's the joke I actually want to land, so the ending shouldn't state it outright.
- Don't name any real researchers or real companies. Invented customers in the FAQ are fine and encouraged.

If you know reinforcement learning and the pleasure-center literature, the piece should read like it was written by someone who does too — I want an ML researcher to nod along and a neuroscientist to wince in recognition, not to fact-check me. Roughly 700-800 words, formatted with headers, spec-sheet bullets, and numbered FAQ entries.
