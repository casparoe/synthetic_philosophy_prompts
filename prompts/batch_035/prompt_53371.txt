I have no academic background in philosophy, but for a couple of years now I've been puzzling over what truth is, mostly on my own, and I've noticed that the argument I keep having with myself never gets anywhere. On one side is the thought that saying "it is true that snow is white" tells you nothing beyond saying that snow is white — that the word "true" is a kind of grammatical device, useful mainly when you want to endorse a lot of statements at once ("everything in the report is true") without listing them, and that there is no deep property of Truth for a theory to uncover. On the other side is the feeling that this can't be the whole story: that truth is what a belief aims at, that being right matters and being wrong doesn't, that some beliefs fit the world and others don't, and that this difference seems like a real feature of beliefs and the world, not a trick of language. Every time I try to settle it, the deflationist in me says the "real feature" is just each particular fact, separately, and the anti-deflationist in me says that this answer is somehow too cheap to explain why anyone would ever bother wanting to be right.

My working hypothesis, offered as no more than a hypothesis, is that the two sides aren't really disagreeing about one thing: the deflationist is answering a question about the role of a word in our language, while the other side is answering a question about what beliefs and assertions are for, and the two questions have been squeezed under a single heading. But I can also see a case that the disagreement is genuine and deep — perhaps about whether explanation and normativity have to bottom out in a property, or about whether the demand for a "substantive" theory of truth is itself confused.

Diagnose this for me. Is the dispute verbal, or substantive, or two different questions wearing one name? Support whatever diagnosis you give by engaging with what each side actually wants to say, including the strongest points each can make against the other — for instance, the deflationist's point that no one has ever managed to say what the truth property would add to all the individual facts, and the anti-deflationist's point that if truth were nothing but disquotation, it's hard to see why truth should be the goal of inquiry rather than, say, comfort. And then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: some distinction that would dissolve the appearance of conflict, some question the two sides could be gotten to answer separately, or an argument that the impasse reflects a real and lasting difference in how people think about what a theory is supposed to do. If your conclusion is that no progress should be expected, say so and explain why.
