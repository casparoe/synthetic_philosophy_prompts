I spent thirty-five years in experimental particle physics before retiring, mostly designing data-acquisition and trigger systems for collider experiments, and since retirement I have been reading philosophy of mind with more enthusiasm than training, first with a small reading group and now on my own. I have just worked through Dennett's case against the Cartesian theater and for the multiple drafts model he developed with Kinsbourne, and I keep having the feeling I have met this argument before, in machine rooms. So here is what I would like: restate the debate in the vocabulary I actually know and trust, as faithfully as you can, and then tell me honestly what the translation fails to carry. Terms I would want you to actually use, not merely gesture at: buffers and deadtime, trigger levels that decide which events are read out and which are discarded unread, time-stamping at the front-end versus at event-building, redundant logging in which no replica is canonical, eventual consistency, whatever genuinely fits, along with an account of what does not. To show you the sort of thing I mean: the claim that consciousness happens all at once in one privileged place looks to me like the assumption that there is a single event builder assembling one authoritative record, while the multiple drafts picture looks like distributed logging where any so-called final version is simply whichever one got read. The timing experiments, in which subjects report intending to move after the relevant brain activity has apparently begun, look like the old engineering problem of assigning a time to a signal that was buffered, processed, and back-dated. And Dennett's later thought that conscious content is content that achieves a kind of celebrity in the brain looks uncomfortably like my old joke that an event is whatever the trigger decided to keep. But do this properly rather than humoring me; I would rather learn that my analogies are bad than enjoy them.

Then give me the residue report, and treat it as the main deliverable. Which parts of the philosophical dispute map cleanly, which translate only with distortion, and which have no expression at all in my vocabulary? My suspicion is that the residue is exactly the part that matters: whether there is anything it is like to be the system, and whether being the state that gets reported is supposed to answer that question or only the different question of which states we can act on and talk about. I genuinely cannot tell whether my vocabulary is missing a concept the philosophers have, or whether the philosophers are arguing about a concept nobody has managed to state. Alongside this, I want you to mark where Dennett's own framing is a fighting reading rather than a neutral report: my impression is that very few of his opponents hold the view he attacks, and that critics accuse the multiple drafts model either of smuggling an audience back in or of changing the subject from consciousness to access. So please separate, throughout, what is settled among people who know this literature, what is genuinely contested, and where a fair summary would have to say there is simply no consensus. I would rather have an answer that flags its uncertainties than one that presents one school's position as the state of the art.
