I've been reading the rationalist corner of the internet since the Overcoming Bias days, and over the years I've settled into what I take to be a fairly standard hardcore Bayesian package. Recently I tried to work through the Judy Benjamin problem and discovered that my package doesn't obviously fit together. Here are my commitments, and I'd like an audit: which pairs are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and what the cheapest repair to each is, ranked by severity rather than presented as equally bad.

First, the setup, stated plainly so we're on the same page. Judy Benjamin is dropped into a swamp divided equally into Red territory and Blue territory, and her priors are 1/2 for Blue, 1/4 for Red HQ, and 1/4 for Red support. An informant tells her that if she is in Red territory, the odds are 3:1 that she is in Red HQ rather than Red support. The question is what her posteriors should be.

The commitments:

(1) Conditionalization on propositions is the only rational way to update. I take the diachronic Dutch book and expected-accuracy arguments to settle this: any other update rule is exploitable or accuracy-dominated. Where evidence is a proposition, there is nothing to discuss.

(2) Indicative conditionals are not propositions. Lewis's triviality results convinced me long ago that there is no proposition whose probability is the conditional probability, so the informant's statement to Judy is not something with truth conditions that she can conditionalize on. It constrains P(Red HQ given Red) without asserting a proposition.

(3) In the case itself, my intuition is firm: Judy's probability of Blue should stay exactly 1/2. The informant's information concerned Red territory only, and it would be gratuitous for it to move her credence about Blue. The answer I favor keeps Blue at 1/2 and confines the 3:1 odds to the Red cell: Red HQ 3/8, Red support 1/8. I believe this is roughly what is called Adams conditioning, though I may be misremembering the label.

(4) Jaynes is basically right about everything: where propositional evidence runs out, the principled extension of Bayesian updating is minimizing relative entropy subject to what you've learned, and the consistency-style axioms — Shore and Johnson's, as I remember them — show this to be the unique consistent extension. I lean on maximum entropy for priors all the time, and I would feel like a hypocrite denying its sibling for updates.

(5) Update rules shouldn't care about the order or grouping of evidence. Learning two things and updating in either order should yield the same final credences, and carving the possibility space differently shouldn't change the answer.

Now the embarrassments. I've read that van Fraassen's 1981 paper, the one that made the problem famous, argued that minimizing relative information gives the wrong answer here, and doing the calculation myself I find that the minimum-relative-entropy posteriors put Judy's probability of Blue above 1/2 — around 0.53, by my arithmetic. So (3) and (4) are directly opposed on the case, and I can't see how to keep both. Worse, I have a vague memory that (4) and (5) are also in bad shape — that minimum-relative-entropy updates are not commutative in general, and that there was a follow-up literature (Grove and Halpern, if I remember right) arguing that the way van Fraassen set up the minimization was technically flawed, though I never understood whether that amounted to a repair or an indictment.

And one more commitment I hold more tentatively, which may dissolve everything or nothing: the source's utterance is itself an event, and a fully principled Bayesian conditionalizes on the fact that the informant said this rather than that — on the utterance, not merely its content. On the days I believe this, the whole problem looks confused: no special update rule is needed, just an ordinary model of the source. But then I notice that I have no model of the source, and that Judy, as stipulated, has none either, and I can't tell whether this move is a solution or an evasion.

So: which of these hang together? Please separate the apparent tensions from the real ones, rank the real ones by severity, and say for each what has to give and what it costs. I'd also like to know whether the cheapest repair leaves me with a determinate answer to the original question — what Judy's posteriors should be — or whether I should accept that the question is underdetermined, and if so whether that underdetermination is a fact about rationality itself or just about my ignorance of the source.
