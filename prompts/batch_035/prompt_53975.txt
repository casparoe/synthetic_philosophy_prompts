Me and my brother-in-law have been having this running argument for like two years and it never gets anywhere. It's always cases like: he thinks a guy who smoked his whole life despite the warnings should go to the back of the line for a lung transplant, but he gets weirdly quiet when I bring up my cousin who was born with a heart condition. And then my friend Priya says the whole question is badly framed because either way the guy still needs a lung, and we don't let people die in the parking lot. I've slowly realized we're not actually disagreeing about any particular case, we just have totally different ideas about what makes an outcome fair, and no amount of arguing about smokers is gonna fix that.

So I want to make one of those dumb online quiz things (like a buzzfeed quiz but serious) that the three of us can take at the next bbq. Heres what I need: 8-10 questions, each one a little scenario with 2-3 plain-english answer options, absolutely no jargon (don't use the word "egalitarian" or anything like that). The scenarios should be ones where the different underlying views actually give different answers — like the smoker vs. the born-with-it case, a gambler who loses his savings on a risky bet vs. someone who loses theirs in a bank collapse nobody saw coming, someone who deliberately picked a fun low-paying career and now can't afford things, someone whose happiness just happens to need expensive stuff (fancy wine, when I'm just as happy with beer), someone who's poor partly cuz their parents never taught them a thing about money. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto views described in plain language: the "fair means compensating people for bad luck they didn't choose, but your choices are on you" view, the "fair means everybody has enough, and past that I don't care how unequal it gets" view, the "fair means helping whoever is worst off, period, don't even ask how they got there" view, the "fairness is really about everyone being able to look eachother in the eye as equals, which means you don't cut the reckless guy off from healthcare even if he technically chose it" view, and the "fairness isn't really a thing, just add up everyone's wellbeing" view.

Two more things. First, for each question, tell me which of the views it's actually separating, because if two views give the same answer on a question it's a wasted question and I want to swap it out. Second, if some combo of answers doesn't fit any view cleanly, say so instead of fudging it into the nearest bucket — I'd rather know the quiz has a blind spot than get a fake result. Oh, and the gambler question matters to me specifically: I want it to somehow distinguish "he knew the risks, so whatever happens to him is his problem" from "he knew the risks, took the shot anyway, and THAT's what makes it fair when he loses" — those feel really different to me but I can't put my finger on why, and I'd love the quiz to catch the difference.
