I've drifted into being pretty sympathetic to the "control-first" view in AI safety -- the idea that we don't necessarily need to know how to make a model actually want good things, we just need a system of checks (monitors reviewing the model's outputs, tripwires, staged deployment with limited permissions, keeping the model away from the resources it would need to cause harm) that would keep even a misaligned model from doing damage, and that this is what labs should actually be building now since it's tractable. Basically treat the model like an untrusted employee you can still get useful work out of. The thing I keep comming back to: what would count against this?

And I mean the real defeaters, not the reflexive "you can't trust anything a misaligned model says" objection -- a control person already agrees with that, it's their starting point. Some worries I've half-formed myself, tell me if they're right and rank them against each other: (1) control measures mostly catch failure modes that show up in the observable channel, so if scheming is spread across many steps or encoded in innocuous-looking actions, the monitor is looking at noise and passing it along; (2) the monitor is itself a model, and as the monitored model gets more capable the monitor gets relatively worse at judging it, so the whole setup has a built-in expiration date that nobody can really date; (3) capability evals undersample by construction -- you only test for the dangerous capabilities you thought to test for, so the control case rests on an assumption the control framework itself has no way to verify; (4) maybe the biggest one: control buys time but says nothing about what the time is for, and in practice labs treat "we have control measures in place" as license to keep scaling, so control might be net-negative by lowering the felt urgency of actually solving alignment -- it's not just a fallback layered on top of the world, it changes the incentive landscape it sits in.

Also, what would a smart defender of control concede as counting against the view versus wave off as outside its scope? I've watched people in this space quietly shift from "control is sufficient" to "control is the best available option this decade" to "control is a research program for generating evidence about models," and I want to know whether the thing is still falsifiable at that point, and whether unfalsifiability even matters for an engineering program the way it would for a scientific theory. Is there any concrete scenario where control-style measures and alignment-style work make different predictions about what we'd observe, so the debate could be settled by something other than trading intuitions? Honest answer wanted -- did I insulate myself somewhere along the way, or is the view actually as solid as it's been feeling to me?
