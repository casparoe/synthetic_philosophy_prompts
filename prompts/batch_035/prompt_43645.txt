So, I'm a postdoc presenting at my department's WIP seminar in two weeks, and I want a Q&A prep sheet. The talk defends a view about aesthetic testimony, and the audience is genuinely mixed, which is the problem. About half the room is epistemologists -- a couple of social epistemology types who think testimony is just an ordinary source of knowledge and get suspicious of anyone who carves out exceptions, one virtue epistemologist -- plus two aestheticians who are committed to the idea that first-hand engagement is constitutive of aesthetic judgment, and one Wittgensteinian emeritus who shows up to everything and uses the word "practice" as a complete argument.

My thesis, roughly: yes, aesthetic testimony transmits knowledge -- if my friend who knows film tells me the ending of that movie is devastating, I can know it without seeing it. But the thing people are reaching for when they say deference in aesthetics is somehow deficient is real, and it has nothing to do with justification or evidence. It's that aesthetic conversation is a practice in which your entitlement to say the thing out loud has to be earned first-hand -- the difference between speaking a language and parroting phrases from a phrasebook you don't understand. And the second, riskier half of the talk: this isn't specific to aesthetics at all, moral deference has exactly the same structure, so all the "aesthetic exceptionalism" in the literature is aimed at the wrong target. That's the part the aestheticians in the room will take personally.

Draft me the prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this crowd, ordered by how much damage they'd do, each phrased the way the specific person would phrase it (the social epistemologists and the aestheticians will come at this completely differently), with a two-minute reply for each and an honest note on where the reply is soft. And yeah, include the one I'm hoping nobody asks -- I have a feeling it's the one about whether my practice-story predicts a hermit with nobody to converse with is perfectly fine deferring about everything, and if it does, whether the story is doing any real work at all.
