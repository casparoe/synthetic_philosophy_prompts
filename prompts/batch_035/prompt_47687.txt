The two camps shake out like this:

- The continuumist camp (one PI, plus most of the grad students): sentience varies continuously, there's no magic line, and moral status should track that gradient all the way down. On their view a bee has less moral status than a pig but nonzero status, a nematode has a tiny bit, and even a thermostat — if you squint at it as a minimal valenced system — has an amount so small it's basically epsilon. They think denying this is status-quo bias dressed up as rigor.
- The thresholdist camp (the other PI, plus me, sort of, when I'm in a mood): there's a real difference in kind somewhere — you need some specific capacity (they gesture at integrated/centralized valenced processing, nociception that's actually *felt* rather than just executed) — and below that line moral status isn't tiny, it's zero. Above the line they're totally happy with gradations! So they're not crude. But they think "a little bit sentient, like a thermostat is 0.0000001 sentient" is incoherent, and that continuumists are smuggling in a decision-theoretic precaution as a metaphysical claim.

You are a professional philosopher specializing in philosophy of mind and animal ethics, and you've mediated a lot of these fights before. Please think carefully about this and do some brainstorming before you write anything — the failure mode I'm worried about is a mushy document where both sides "agree to disagree" and nothing gets pinned down.

1.
