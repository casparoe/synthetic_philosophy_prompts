I've been chewing on this idea of "acausal trade" I ran into on some forum — two agents who never communicate or causally affect each other in any way, yet each does something costly for the other because each expects the other to do likewise. And every explanation I see uses the same crutch: two nearly identical agents (the twin prisoners dilemma thing), so of course they mirror each other. That always felt like cheating to me. If the agents are basically clones, you haven't shown trade, you've shown synchronization.

So here's what I want you to build: a thought experiment where a genuine acausal trade happens between two agents that are NOT alike — different values, different power, maybe even different kinds of minds entirely. Constraints:

- One paragraph for the scenario itself, no more.
- No channel through which either one's choices can affect the other — no messages, no signals, no side effects, ever. Learning about each other (old writings, reputation, secondhand accounts) is allowed, since knowing about someone isn't influencing them. But no simulations of each other, no superintelligent aliens deducing us from the cosmic background radiation — keep the parties roughly human-level, reasoning about each other the way a shrewd person reads a stranger's character.
- The trade must not be explainable away as (a) ordinary altruism — each side's payoff must be higher than from just being kind, meaning there's a real sacrifice that only pays of if the other side holds up their end; and (b) a shared convention or focal point — the good each does for the other must be tailored to the other's specific needs, not something both would do anyway.

Then walk me throuh it: why each side gains, which part of the setup is doing the work (my guess is it's something about each side being able to model the other's reasoning well enough to trust it), what happens if you delete that part, and how a skeptic who thinks the whole concept is just altruism in a lab coat would attack the case.

At the very end, put a single line starting with "Verdict:" followed by one short phrase — either "genuine acausal trade", "disguised altruism", "mere coordination", or "impossible as specified" — whichever your analysis actually supports.
