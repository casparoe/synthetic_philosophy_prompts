I would like you to respond as an expert in social epistemology and behavioural economics — someone who works on social learning, coordination, and how individuals draw inferences from one another's observed choices. Please think carefully and methodically, and begin with a brief brainstorm of the candidate concepts before committing to an answer; I am more interested in the correct carving of the terrain than in a bare label.

Six months ago my wife and I opened a restaurant on a street that already had three others. Ours has been the best-reviewed of the four since roughly our third week. The busiest, by a distance, is the one that opened the month before we did: it is, to be blunt, mediocre, and the public reviews agree (it sits at about three and a half stars where ours sits nearer five). But it opened during a festival fortnight, acquired a queue in its first two weeks, and the queue has never gone away. People walk past four restaurants — reviews visible on their phones — to stand in it. When a friend asked someone in the queue why, the answer was that a queue that long must mean something.

I have since noticed the same shape elsewhere. An interview panel where the first person to speak sets the frame and later panellists' doubts somehow never reverse it. A book that cracked a bestseller list and has been selling on the strength of being on it ever since.

A friend who studied economics says this is an "information cascade" — people inferring from early choices that others know something, so the early choices snowball. But he also says those are meant to start from genuine private signals and to be fragile: once the underlying evidence is public, the thing should unwind. Here there was no hidden information to begin with (a festival crowd is not a signal about pasta), and the evidence is public — the mediocre reviews are one screen-tap away — and the queue treats its own length as outweighing them. The crowd seems to be reading the crowd's behaviour as a summary of evidence the crowd never had.

Does this configuration — a herd that begins from noise rather than information, and that grows more robust the larger it gets, because its own size is mistaken for the missing signal, such that disclosure of the underlying weak evidence does not break it — have an established name of its own? If it does, please say precisely what it commits us to and how it differs from the nearest relatives (my friend's cascades, plain conformity, self-fulfilling prophecies, and whatever else belongs on the list). If it does not, please say so plainly, coin a term for it, give a definition with boundary cases, and tell me where it shades off into its neighbours — including whether my restaurant example really is the same phenomenon as the interview panel, or whether I have run two things together.
