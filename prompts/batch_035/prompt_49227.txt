write a dialogue between the two of us. setting: at our seed round an eccentric investor offered our company a weird deal — she'd flip a fair coin. heads, she wires us $20M. tails, we get to choose whether to wire her $10M back, no contract, no consequences whatsoever for refusing. she only made the offer at all because her forecasting model (essentially perfect track record) predicted we'd pay on tails. the coin has just come up tails, and dana and i are standing at the transfer screen.

dana argues for paying, i argue for refusing. have us actually engage with each other's points: she should explain why refusing now is the very thing that made deals like this unavailable to agents like us across the possible worlds, and i should push back that i can't causally affect a prediction that was already made, and that "being the kind of agent who pays" is cold comfort when $10M leaves the account for literally nothing in return. at some point i want to raise my real worry — that updatelessness seems to demand ignoring information you'd have paid a lot of money for, which feels insane — and she should give a serious answer, not a dodge. also let me make the point that if everyone reasoned like her, people could farm gullible updateless agents with exactly these offers, and let her respond to that too. no strawmen, both of us smart, and it's fine if neither of us fully concedes. aim for maybe 600-900 words, and it should read like two engineers arguing at a whiteboard, not a philosophy seminar.
