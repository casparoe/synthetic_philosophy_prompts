I teach philosophy of mind, but before academia I spent twelve years as a graphics programmer, and I have grown tired of nodding along to consciousness-as-user-illusion talk without checking whether the metaphor can bear the weight. Restate the illusionist position — the view, associated with Dennett and defended more recently under the name illusionism, that phenomenal properties are properties our introspective models misattribute to experience, the way a desktop icon misrepresents whatever the machine is actually doing — in the vocabulary I actually used: scene graphs, render passes, level of detail, culling, frame buffers, post-processing. Translate each strand separately: the user-illusion metaphor itself, the multiple-drafts picture on which there is no single place where experience happens, and the claim that the self is a model rather than a modeled thing. Then give me the residue report. Two residues especially. First, in every engine I ever worked on, the consumer of the rendered image sat outside the machine; on the illusionist picture the consumer is inside, so I want to know what in my vocabulary, if anything, corresponds to that — perhaps the way a game's own AI consumes a simplified model of the world, perhaps nothing. Second, a player can compare what she saw with a screenshot or a frame dump, and first-person authority in consciousness was always supposed to be precisely the absence of any such external check. Tell me which parts of the position translate cleanly, which translations I should distrust because you had to bend my vocabulary to fit them, and whether the residue reflects a defect in the graphics metaphor or a lack of clarity in illusionism itself.
