Two of my friends had an argument last night about whether it's irrational for the two fo them to disagree, and they asked me to referee, which went about as well as you'd expect, so now I'm asking you. Background: Marco (econ PhD student) and Elena both read the same coverage of a local zoning reform and disagree about whether it will actually lower rents. (1) Marco said Aumann's agreement theorem shows that two rational people with the same information can't disagree, and since they've read the same articles and he thinks they're both rational, one of them must be being irrational somewhere. (2) Elena said the theorem doesn't say anything that strong — it needs the two people to have common knowledge of each other's posteriors, which basically never holds in a real argument, so it's wrong to conclude that actual disagreement reveals actual irrationality. (3) Marco granted that but said it's a technicality: if they took turns honestly announcing their credences back and forth, there's a result that the credences converge, so if you do all that sharing and still end up disagreeing, one of you is being irrational. (4) Elena said the convergence needs more than honest numbers — a stated credence like "75% it lowers rents" doesn't tell the other person what evidence produced it, so you're only really exchanging information if you know how to interpret each other's announcements; and in any case they don't share priors, since he came in trusting market mechanisms and she came in trusting the tenant-organizer studies, and announcing numbers at each other does nothing about a priors mismatch. (5) Marco's finisher: fine, if the priors differ then the theorem never applied to them in the first place — his original claim was about the case where the assumptions hold, and in that case it's still true. Please go through the five moves one at a time and say who's right about what, flag anything either of them says that's subtly off (especially anything Marco's later concessions quietly do to his earlier claims), and tell me who comes out more right overall and what both of them are missing.
