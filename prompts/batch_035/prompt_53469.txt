Two guys from my local rationality meetup got into it about self-deception last week and asked me to referee, which was a mistake since I honestly couldnt.

Some background: we'd been talking about the classic paradox -- deceiving someone normally requires knowing the truth while intending to instill a false belief, so if deceiver and deceived are the same person you seem to need to simultaneously know and not know p.

Mark's position was that the concept is just broken and we should retire it. Everything real that gets called self-deception is motivated reasoning: disconfirming evidence gets weighed extra harshly, convenient hypotheses get a pass, and none of it involves anyone intending anything -- it's all System 1 stuff happening below the level of intention. So there's no puzzle, just ordinary bad epistemic hygiene, and the term "self-deception" smuggles in a little homunculus pulling the strings who doesn't actually exist.

Dan said that's way too intellectualist a picture of what deception is. He brought up the evolutionary story (the Trivers thing about deceiving yourself first so you can deceive others without the guilty cues leaking): if one subsystem registers the bad news and keeps it away from the part that generates your sincere avowals, that's functionally exactly what a deceiver does, and there's no contradiction because the knowing and the not-knowing live in different places. We already accept compartmentalized minds everywhere else, he said -- the planner-self who buys the gym membership vs the one who actually lives through Tuesday morning.

Mark's comeback: this just relabels the problem. If the "deceived" party is a subagent that can't be talked to and has no point of view of its own, then nothing is being deceived, any more than your spam folder is deceiving you. Calling fragmentation "deception" is a metaphor, and metaphors don't need paradoxes solved.

They went around like that for a while and eventually both appealed to me. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right at each step -- not just who wins overall -- and also whether there's something both of them are missing?
