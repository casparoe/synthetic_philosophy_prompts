I'm on the board of a 40-unit cohousing community, and I swear half our meetings are fights about which decisions belong to individual households and which ones get brought to the whole community for a vote. Door colors, smoking on balconies, whether someone can keep chickens, whether the guy who's offended by his neighbor's garden gnome collection gets a say. A friend who did an econ masters told me about Sen's liberal paradox — the thing about how you can't simultaneously respect every unanimous preference AND give each person even a small sphere that's theirs alone to decide. I half understand it and I think it's exactly our problem: whenever somebody has a preference about somebody else's "private" business, we're stuck.

So: write a procedure the board can actually run when a new issue comes up. Constraints: it has to fit in a 15-minute agenda slot, board members are not philosophers and will not read anything, and whatever we decide has to be written up in plain language for the community newsletter. The procedure should have questions in a definite order, say what counts as enough of an answer to each, and be explicit about where it just ends with "this is a personal matter, drop it" versus where it forces a collective vote. And there should be a step where it tells us honestly that we've hit the thing the theorem is about — a case where respecting everybody's preferences and keeping the sphere private genuinely can't both happen, so we have to pick which one to sacrifice, and that's a values call the procedure won't make for us.

Then run it on three real-ish cases of ours: a household that wants to paint their door black when the bylaws only list approved colors; someone who wants to smoke on their own balcony that happens to be upwind of the shared courtyard; and a member who wants the community to ban meat from the common kitchen for climate reasons. Make sure one of the three comes out in a way I definately won't like, and flag any spots where the procedure is really just a judgment call dressed up as a flowchart.
