I'm a software engineer, zero philosophy background, and I've gotten weirdly obsessed with functionalism lately because of all the LLM discourse. My naive take as a programmer: mental states are like software, defined by what they do (their causal role) rather than what they're made of, so pain could in principle run on neurons, silicon, whatever — same program, different hardware. That's multiple realizability, right? But then whenever I read further it all falls apart into fights I don't have the background to referee: people disputing whether multiple realizability is even common in real brains, the Martian pain thought experiment, the charge that functionalism is either too liberal (a giant look-up table, or a whole country of people passing notes around, ends up "in pain") or too chauvinistic (an octopus or an alien with totally different chemistry doesn't). My brother-in-law is a neuroscientist and his position is basically "this is all armchair stuff, pain is nociception is the insula, next question", which annoys me but I can't actually answer him.

Before I read another paper I want the spec, like I'd write acceptance criteria before letting anyone touch our codebase. Write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of how mental states get their identity has to deliver — which cases it must get right, which distinctions it has to respect (I think I mean things like the role vs. the realizer, whether states are individuated coarsely or finely, token vs type claims), and what it can't contradict (neuroplasticity where different regions take over a function after a stroke, convergent evolution, the fact that evolution reused and repurposed circuits rather than building clean modules). For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and tell me which ones you'd actually defend as non-negotiable versus which are just prejudices of one camp — e.g., is the autonomy of psychology (that mental-state talk should survive neuroscience discovering the wetware details) a genuine requirement or something I've imported from my "clean abstraction layers" software intuitions that brains simply don't honor? And critically: flag any pair of desiderata that probably can't be jointly satisfied, because if some subset of the list is unsatisfiable, that's the result I most want to see — like how in my world you can't have consistency, availability, and partition tolerance all at once. Then very briefly run the main versions (I've gathered there's machine/causal-role functionalism, psychofunctionalism, teleofunctionalism) against the list, but keep that part short, the criteria are what I care about.
