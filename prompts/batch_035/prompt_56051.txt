I'm drafting the framework chapter for my dissertation on the metaphysics of composition (well, prospectus — nothing's approved yet lol), and I've written down where I actually stand so my advisor can poke at it, and now I'm staring at the list wondering whether half of it is even coherent. Here's roughly what I've got. (1) Nihilism is a non-starter: there are tables, there are cats, there are galaxies. When my niece drops her ice cream, the ice cream exists. (2) Universalism is also false — there is no object made of my left shoe and the northern half of the Eiffel Tower, and no amount of "just restrict your quantifiers" talk will change my mind about that. (3) My positive view is roughly van Inwagen's: the only genuine composites are living things — some things compose something when their activity adds up to a life. Every other proposed restriction (contact, fastening, joint motion) seems hopelessly arbitrary to me, but life feels like a real joint in nature. (4) I'm also pretty firm that existence can't be vague: in any given region there's always a fact of the matter about how many objects there are, even if we could never in principle know it. (5) And I'm not willing to give up artifacts or social objects — my laptop exists, the university exists, the Eurozone exists; I've spent too long reading social ontology to deny that now. (6) Finally, I hold that composition is not identity (the standard objections to that view convince me), but I still want to say that a whole, when it exists, is nothing over and above its parts — ontological innocence, cheap ontology, whatever you want to call it.

Some of this feels like it sits fine together and some of it has been keeping me up at night. My officemate insists I can't hold (2) and (4) together because of the sorites reasoning people use to argue for universalism, but I don't fully see why vagueness about where composition stops is supposed to force me to say composition never stops. And separately, someone at a conference told me the two halves of (6) are flatly inconsistent, which I didn't follow at the time and still don't. So: go through the whole set for me. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, how bad is each one, and what's the cheapest way out of the worst one? I honestly don't know which commitment I'd drop frist, so don't assume I'm secretly protecting any of them — I want a real audit, with the tensions ranked by severity rather than a laundry list where everything counts as equally problematic.
