I'm a novelist, mid-career, and somewhere in my thirties I read Tolstoy's What Is Art? and basically signed on. Not the religious machinery, but the core: art is one person infecting another with a feeling by means of external signs, the test of real art is infectiousness, the marker of the genuine article is sincerity, and the art world of the professional classes — prizes, MFA programs, review culture, the whole thing — is mostly counterfeit, built to be decoded by people who've read a lot rather than felt a lot. I've even made peace with the awkward fact that on Tolstoy's own criteria his theory condemns most of my bookshelf and probably a couple of my books. And its quietly shaping how I write now: fewer pyrotechnics, plainer sentences, obsessing over whether a scene would actually move someone who doesn't work with words all day.

But I've never honestly stress-tested the thing, I just live inside it. So tell me: what would count against it? I want exactly five items, numbered, each a sentence or two, no intro and no conclusion. Rank them from fatal down to merely awkward, and for each one say whether a committed Tolstoyan would concede it counts against the theory or would just wave it away — and be honest about which of those wave-aways are respectable moves and which are just protecting the castle. Don't limit yourself to literary examples either; if the theory stands or falls on something in psychology, or on how feelings and their transmission actually work, I want to know that to.
