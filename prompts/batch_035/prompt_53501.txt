I'm a PhD student in machine learning, working on a chapter about what "guarantee" can even mean in AI alignment research. The field seems split between two camps: one insists that only worst-case guarantees — safety properties that hold under adversarial conditions and arbitrary distributional shift — are worth pursuing, because the losses from misalignment are too large to average over; the other holds that worst-case guarantees are unattainable for systems that do open-ended reasoning over natural language inputs, so we should aim for average-case or probabilistic assurances, perhaps over distributions of deployment environments weighted by how much is at stake in each. Before I argue for either side, I want the specification settled: what any adequate theory of alignment guarantees — a framework that tells you what is being promised, to whom, and under what conditions — has to deliver.

Please write the desiderata. I have some candidate requirements, but treat them as points of departure: the framework must specify its threat model rather than leaving it implicit; it must say precisely what a "case" is a case of (an input, an environment, a computational realization of the model, an interaction history with a particular user or principal); it must be composable, since a guarantee about a component does not obviously survive being embedded in a pipeline or subjected to further training; it must say how the guarantee degrades rather than silently lapsing when capabilities increase; and it must avoid vacuity on both ends — worst-case guarantees over unconstrained environments appear to condemn every policy, including human ones, while average-case guarantees relative to a distribution we cannot verify in advance appear to promise nothing testable. For each requirement, give the motivating case or consideration, mark it as non-negotiable or as a preference of one camp masquerading as a constraint, and flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable — that is the part I most want to see, since I have a suspicion that worst-case scope, an open-ended natural language interface, and any form of tractable verification cannot all be had at once, and I would like to know whether that is a genuine no-go or an artifact of how the requirements are being stated.

One further thought to fold in: I've begun to suspect the whole debate rests on a quantifier-order confusion — that worst-case and average-case are not endpoints of one scale but answers to different questions about what is quantified over and in what order, and that some "worst-case" proposals (adversarial training against adversaries you can actually implement) quietly smuggle in average-case structure. Please say where, if anywhere, in the desiderata list that distinction should be pinned down.

Finally, please give only a brief scorecard of the two camps against the finished list — the weight should be on the criteria. Keep the whole thing in American English, argue from first principles where the literature is thin, and do not include citations or reading recommendations; I only want the reasoning.
