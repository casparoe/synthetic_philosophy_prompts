I'm a graduate student writing a term paper on Dennett's idea of free-floating rationales — the thought that there can be reasons for things without any reasoner who ever entertained them, the way there is presumably a reason acorns have tough shells even though no one reasoned it out, and a reason a cuckoo chick's gape resembles its nestmates' even though no bird knows it. My supervisor is a committed etiologist about function: she holds that a rationale only genuinely exists where there is a history of selection — natural selection, design iteration, trial-and-error learning, filtering by a market, whatever — that produced the trait *because* of the effect we now cite as the reason. On her view, Dennett's free-floating rationales are always either compressed etiologies or anthropomorphic projections, and "a reason without a selecting history" is like "a shadow without anything casting it."

For the paper I need a thought experiment built to order: a scenario in which something has a genuine free-floating rationale despite having no selection history at all — not evolved, not designed, not learned, not the output of any filtering process that favored the effect. Constraints: (a) fully naturalistic — think geomorphology, chemistry, orbital mechanics, no agents, and no intentional vocabulary in the setup; (b) the rationale must be a real reason in the world, not a projection, in the sense that an observer who refuses to acknowledge it would be making an error rather than merely declining a metaphor; (c) the scenario itself should be one paragraph. My own attempts keep collapsing — e.g. a boulder that happens to dam a ravine and thereby stops downstream flooding, where my supervisor says the "thereby" is idle because nothing was ever for the flood control — so part of what I want from you is a diagnosis of why such attempts fail, if they do. After the scenario, walk through both verdicts: what the etiological view says about it, what a Dennettian says, and whether constraint (b) can be satisfied without smuggling in either a covert history of selection or a valuing observer. Then tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, what happens to the two verdicts if you remove it, and how my supervisor would most plausibly attack the construction.

The purpose of the case is to test whether "reasons without reasoners" can come apart from "reasons without selection histories," so a scenario that secures the rationale by invoking fine-tuning, observer purposes, or by stipulating in advance that the reason is real counts as cheating — the verdicts must be generated from the theories, not written into the setup. Present the scenario first, then the analysis. End with a machine-readable final line of the form \verdict{...} containing exactly one of the labels "etiological", "Dennettian", or "indeterminate", followed by no more than ten words of justification for that label.
