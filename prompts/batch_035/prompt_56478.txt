I write novels (midlist, literary-ish, you havent heard of me) and I somehow got roped onto the grants panel of our state arts council. This year we have a case thats split the panel roughly down the middle and I've been asked to circulate a written opinion before the next meeting.

The application: a woman submitted a 300-page "novel" that she created by running a large language model through hundreds of prompts and then heavily curating, editing, rearranging and pruning what it produced. She's completely upfront about the method — it's described in detail in her artist statement, which is actually quite thoughtful. She compares what she did to Warhol's factory (he had assistants do most of the actual painting) and to Duchamp mounting a urinal and calling it art, arguing that selection and framing have always been doing a lot of the artistic work. Her book got picked up by a small press, for what thats worth. The grant is decent money, like 15k, and our guidelines just say funds support "the creation of new works of literary art."

Half the panel says obviously fund it — under something like Dickie's institutional theory or Danto's artworld idea, if it's presented as art within an art context it IS art, and anyway plenty of canonical writers used cut-up methods or constrained writing. The other half says if we fund this we're funding a machine's output with a human editor attached, and the word "creation" in our guidelines has to mean something more than curation. One guy keeps invoking the idea that art requires expression of lived experience or intention, another keeps responding that we fund conceptual poets who barely "make" anything.

So: draft the memo I should circulate. It needs a clear bottom line (fund, reject, or fund with conditions — and if with conditions, what conditions), reasoning the other panelists could actually engage with, and an honest treatment of the strongest version of the side you argue against. Don't just hand-wave "art is hard to define" — thats exactly why they want the memo. Also worth thinking about: whatever we decide becomes precedent, since AI-assisted applications are only going to increase.

Keep it usable — maybe 600-900 words, and its fine to use first person since it'll go out under my name.
