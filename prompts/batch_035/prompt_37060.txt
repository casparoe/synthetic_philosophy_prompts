So I've gotten myself into a situation: I'm on a small volunteer heritage commission in my town, and we have to decide what to do with a statue of the founder — a guy who did genuinely build the place and also did some things we'd now call monstrous, and everyone in town has opinions. Meanwhile I'm rereading Nietzsche's second Untimely Meditation (the one on history) for a course I'm auditing, and it's messing with me because I keep finding that my actual views don't hang together. So here's the set of things I believe, or at least claim on Tuesday nights:

(1) Nietzsche's core thought is basically right: historical knowledge is a means, not an end, and its value is exhausted by what it does for life — a person or a culture that can't forget can't act, and the point of a healthy relationship to the past is the plastic power to grow out of it. (2) That said, I instinctively distrust monumental history — heroic narratives about great men mostly get weaponized, and Nietzsche himself says the weak will read their own resentments into the exemplars — so of the three modes he describes, critical history feels like the only honest one: dig up the past, judge it, break what oppresses the living. (3) But I also think a community needs a usable past, and I don't actually want the founding story taught to third graders fully stripped of its myths, even though I know parts of it are false and I'd want the full truth published in the scholarly literature. (4) Separately, I think contemporary culture generally suffers from too much memory — the endless anniversaries, the centennials, the archival everything — and we'd be healthier doing a lot more strategic forgetting. (5) But at the same time I think there are real duties of collective remembrance for historical injustice, and I'm suspicious whenever someone says it's time to move on, because that call tends to come from people the past flatters. (6) On the statue itself, my current lean is keep it up but reframe it with new context, and I honestly can't tell if that's a principled position or just me being squeamish.

Do these hang together? I suspect they don't, but I genuinely don't know where the fault line is, and I'd rather find out from you than mid-meeting. Tell me which pairs are in real tension and which only look that way, rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them flat, and for each one say what the cheapest fix would be and what it'd cost me. Also, if you think I've misread Nietzsche somewhere, or that the whole framing rests on a confusion (like maybe some of these aren't even about the same thing), say that plainly instead of politely auditing a broken question.
