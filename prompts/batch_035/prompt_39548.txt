So I've gone basically all-in on the Kripke picture: names and natural kind terms are rigid designators, "water is H2O" comes out necessarily true but still only knowable a posteriori, and intuitions about cases like Twin Earth count as real data for semantics. I think the descriptivists lost that argument decades ago and everyone sensible moved on. My question is what would actually count against this. Like, what would have to be true for the rigidity/necessity picture to be wrong -- not just "the arguments could be better," but what observations, cases, or results would genuinely pressure the view?

I get that its semantics and not physics, so "defeater" works differently here, but I still want it taken seriously. Specific stuff I'm wondering about, and if you can rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward that'd be great:

- Are there cases the picture just cant describe? I keep worrying about the contingent identity stuff, like the statue and the lump of clay -- if identity can be contingent there, does anything survive of the claim that true identities are necessary?
- What about people who claim the intuitions doing the heavy lifting (about what a name refers to in a possible world, etc.) are way less universal than Kripke-style philosophers assumed? If those intuitions really do vary across populations, does that touch the thesis itself or just one popular argument for it?
- I've heard two-dimensionalist types say the necessary a posteriori is a kind of illusion you can dissolve while keeping all the a priori reasoning intact. Is that a rival that counts against the view, or a repackaging of it?
- And honestly: how much of the position is insulated by redefinition at this point? It seems like every counterexample gets absorbed by tweaking how reference gets fixed -- some metasemantic story about what the term was doing when it got introduced. Is that a vice? Maybe I'm holding something that stopped risking anything a long time ago and only looks deep because its rivals died.

Also, which of these would actual defenders of the view conceed as counting against them, versus dismiss? I'm not looking for a victory lap here -- I want the honest map of where the thing is exposed.
