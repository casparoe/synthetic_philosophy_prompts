My wife and I have been doing IVF for about a year, and we just got the news that we have six viable embryos, which is great. The complication is the clinic also offers preimplantation genetic screening, and on top of the standard stuff (chromosome counts, single-gene disorders) they now offer a polygenic risk score report — basically they rank your embryos by statistical likelihood of developing things like heart disease, type 2 diabetes, and even some score that correlates with educational attainment. Nobody has a life-threatening mutation here, to be clear. It's all probabilities, and all the embryos are "normal." The genetic counselor told us, pretty matter-of-factly, that most couples nowadays just pick the top-ranked one.

My wife is really uneasy about this. She says ranking your future children like AWS instances (her words) feels like eugenics, or like a first step toward designer babies. I'm honestly torn. My gut says: if you can give a kid a lower chance of diabetes and cardiac trouble later in life, and you want the kid anyway, why wouldn't you? It's not like the embryos we don't pick will ever know. But then we had dinner with a friend-of-a-friend who's doing a PhD in philosophy, and she said something that's been rattling around my head for weeks: that whichever embryo we implant, the person who results from it *wouldn't have existed* if we'd picked a different one — so it's not that embryo C "lost out" to embryo A, it's that a different child entirely comes into existence either way. Which somehow makes me feel like I can't even frame this as "doing right by our future kid," because there isn't one kid waiting — there are six possible ones, and whoever ends up existing can't compare their life to a life they never had.

So I guess my questions are: (1) is there any principled way to decide here, or is it just vibes — and if we do use the scores, is that meaningfully different from the state-run eugenics programmes people rightly recoil from, given it's just us making a private choice? (2) is the philosophy friend's point actually right that no one is harmed by the choice, and if so does that make selection easier to justify, or does it make it *weirder*? (3) and should we just decline the reports and implant the first viable one without looking, or is that just me offloading a hard decision onto ignorance? I'd really like actual reasoning, not just "do what feels right." Oh, and please answer in British English — we're based in Manchester and it reads oddly otherwise.
