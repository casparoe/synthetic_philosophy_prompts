so I'm a backend engineer, no philosophy background at all, and over the last couple years of reading EA forum stuff and thinking about AI I've accumulated a set of ethical views that I want to sanity check before I write them up in a blog post. here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm a hedonic utilitarian: wellbeing is the only thing that matters morally, everyone counts equally, and you add it up. no side constraints, no special duties, just the total.

(2) but I think suffering and happiness are asymmetric in one specific way: a single case of extreme suffering, like torture, can't be offset by any amount of happiness, anywhere, ever. you can't balance one tortured person against a billion blissful ones and call it even.

(3) I think creating happy lives is positively good, not just permissible. if we could cheaply spin up a trillion digital minds with genuinely wonderful lives, we should do it, and that universe beats an empty one.

(4) I'm very much not on board with the "actually painless extinction would be a mercy" take that some suffering-focused people have. the world right now contains a staggering amount of suffering, and a sterile universe would still be worse than this one. I'm glad people exist and I want there to be a future.

(5) I make small tradeoffs constantly and they seem obviously fine — I'd take a vaccine that gives everyone a day of mild flu symptoms to prevent a rare fatal disease, I'd build a hiking trail knowing a few people will break legs on it.

I have a nagging feeling there's something off here but I can't tell which parts actually clash. like, does the "no offsetting" thing in (2) quietly break the adding in (1)? and how am I supposed to keep (3) while believing (2), given any trillion-mind project has some nonzero chance of a few of those minds going horribly wrong? or is that fine because expected value just handles it? and maybe (2) vs (5) isn't a problem at all since a flu day isn't torture, I genuinely don't know.

go through the pairs and tell me which ones are in real tension and which only look like it, rank the real ones by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what it costs me to give it up. I don't want a lecture on utilitarianism, I want an audit of my specific package — and at the end tell me if there's a single revision that would clean up most of the mess.
