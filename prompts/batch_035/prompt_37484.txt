Hey, I need you to act as a senior criminal-law theorist — think someone who's taught the Hart–Devlin debate for twenty years and is bored of it in the best way — and help me with a memo. Please actually think before committing to a recommendation, maybe do some quick brainstorming of the positions first, because I want real reasoning and not vibes, and please don't just reflexively side with the harm principle on everything.

Here's the situation. I sit on our state's Criminal Law Reform Commission, and this cycle we're reviewing the "public morals" chunk of the penal code — the offenses that criminalize conduct that's consensual, adult, and arguably nobody's business but the participants'. The commitee is split roughly the way you'd expect: one camp keeps invoking the harm principle, the other camp says society has a legitimate interest in protecting its shared moral environment, and we've gotten alot of angry public comment from both directions. Three items are on the docket, and we owe a keep / repeal / keep-with-narrowing recommendation on each:

1. A nightclub has applied for an entertainment license for a "tossing act": a performer with dwarfism (a consenting adult, well paid, by all accounts the best gig he's ever had) is thrown onto a mattress for a drunk crowd. He testified against banning it himself — he says people deciding "for his own good" have a funny way of taking away the best job available to him and calling it dignity. Our enabling statute lets us refuse licenses on grounds of "public decency," which nobody has ever defined.

2. Two adult siblings — both sterilized, so no possibility of children, relationship kept entirely private for years — were convicted under our incest statute after a hospital data breach exposed them. They've petitioned for the conviction to be set aside and the statute narrowed. The repeal camp can't point to a victim; the retention camp insists the statute protects something real even though they can't name it.

3. A private members' club hosts consensual bare-knuckle fights between adults who sign elaborate waivers, with a doctor present and no paying spectators. A neighbor's complaint got the organizers charged under our "outraging public decency" catch-all. Seperate question but related: the same catch-all has also been used against protesters, which nobody on the commission is comfortable with.

So, the memo I'll circulate before next month's meeting. It should (a) give a concrete recommendation per item, keep or repeal or something in between, with reasons the other commissioners could actually engage with rather than a philosophy lecture; (b) state the general principle (or principles) we should apply to this whole section, since case-by-case vibes are exactly what got us here; (c) honestly deal with the strongest argument on whatever side ends up losing — if dignity or offense does real work in any of these three, say which one and why; and (d) tell us what to do about the "public decency" language itself, because as written it means whatever whoever's applying it wants it to mean.

One more thing: I keep going back and forth on item 1 specifically. The performer's autonomy argument feels right to me, but I also can't fully shake the feeling that the state licensing the show is different from the state merely permitting it, and I'd want the memo to address whether that distinction can carry any weight or whether I'm just being squeamish. My fellow commissioners are smart lawyers plus one sociologist, so keep it rigorous but usable, and no bibliography — nobody's going to read it.
