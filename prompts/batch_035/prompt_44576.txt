ok so I'm in my school's philosophy club and last week our advisor told us about Aumann's agreement theorem and I can't stop thinking about it. the idea is basically: if two people have the same priors, and each one's posterior about something is common knowledge between them (meaning not just that they both know it, but that I know that you know that I know, and so on forever), then the posteriors have to be equal. so two honest Bayesians with the same priors literally cannot agree to disagree. and apparently online rationalist types LOVED this, like people would end arguments by saying "by Aumann you should update toward me" which is funny because the theorem never actually says anything that convenient. the part everyone skips is all the assumptions: you need the same priors (when does THAT happen), you need actual common knowledge (which is way stronger than just hearing each other's opinions), and it assumes nobody is lying. also our advisor mentioned the related result that rational people with common priors shouldn't be able to take the opposite sides of a pure bet, which I think is by Milgrom and Stokey, so I'm going to include a joke about that but don't lean the whole thing on that attribution, I might be remembering it wrong lol
