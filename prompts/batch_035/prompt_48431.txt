me and my flatmate have been arguing for like two years about negative utilitarianism, specifically the lexical threshold version -- the view that suffering above some intensity threshold can't be compensated by any amount of pleasure, no matter how much, while below the thershold ordinary aggregation works as usual. I'm basically a classical hedonist (one currency, pleasure and pain on the same scale, maximize the sum) and she's the threshold NU. we finally decided to do the adversarial collab thing properly. draft the joint statement we should both be able to sign.

we agreed on the format in advance: (1) a numbered section of claims both of us accept, (2) a table of the exact disagreements, each row phrased so that the person who holds that view would sign the wording, (3) the cruxes -- subquestions where settling one would move at least one of us -- and for each crux a concrete thought experiment or argument we both agree bears on it, with both of our predictions written down in advance.

stuff that's come up in our arguements that you should work in where relevant:

- the self-interested case: would YOU accept a week of the worst suffering for a billion years of the best bliss? we both say yes, but she thinks this doesn't touch the lexical claim because intrapersonal aggregation across time is a different thing from interpersonal aggregation, and consent does some work. I think that's a dodge -- if lexicality is a fact about the badness of the suffering itself, it should show up in the single-life case too.

- whether the threshold is supposed to be a fact about phenomenology (some sufferings are just different in kind, the way a stubbed toe and a cluster headache don't feel like points on one line) or a decision-theoretic heuristic (the tails dominate, and our horror at extreme suffering is tracking expected value under uncertainty, not intrinsic incommensurability). we suspect this might be half a verbal dispute and we'd like the statement to say which half, if any.

- the sadistic pleasure cases and the reverse ones -- worlds where you add one tortured person plus a trillion mildly content people, and whether she has to bite a bullet I don't.

- what happens to the threshold view if hedonic engineering works and you get beings whose baseline experience is far beyond the current human range: does the threshold stay put in absolute terms, and if so doesn't that make it look like an artifact of the current human hedonic range rather than a fact about value?

- both of us want to skip the stale was-Popper-even-a-negative-utilitarian history stuff unless its actually load-bearing, so leave that out.

write it in first person plural where you can ("we agree that...", "she predicts...", "I predict..."). if you conclude a chunk of our disagreement is really about words, say which chunk and why. and don't sand it down -- if we genuinely can't agree on what the threshold is a threshold OF, that goes in the document as an open item, not as something resolved.
