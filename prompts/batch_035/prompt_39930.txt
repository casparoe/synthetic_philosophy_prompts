I'm on the student conduct board at a mid-sized state school, and we've got a case coming up Thursday that nobody knows how to handle. Quick background: last semester the university ran a pilot program where two dorms had optional social events in VR headsets — avatars in a shared virtual quad our IT department built. It mostly went fine. But one night a group of anonymous users got into one of the events (turns out the invite links were shareable, our bad) and surrounded a first-year's avatar, blocked her from moving, and made gestures I don't need to spell out. She left the event, reported it, and has been avoiding the residence hall's social programming since. Nobody touched her, obviously — she was physically alone in her dorm room the whole time.

Separately, the same pilot had a marketplace where students could build and sell items — one senior spent the whole semester building a virtual gallery of sculptures, sold some for actual money (real dollars, it's a real payment system, that part is not virtual at all). Then a server migration in December corrupted the world files and everything she'd built is gone. IT says virtual items aren't covered by the property damage policy since "no physical property was damaged" and is treating it as a data loss ticket.

The board has to decide both, but really we have to decide one thing first: do our policies even reach into that world? Half the board says no — no physical contact, no physical objects, it's basically an elaborate chatroom, and treating it otherwise means we're policing what amounts to thoughts on a screen. The other half says the harm is obviously real — the first-year is demonstrably worse off, the senior lost months of work and real income — and that "the harm has to be physical to count" was never the actual standard for harassment anyway.

Please draft the opinion memo I'll circulate before the meeting. I want a concrete recommendation for each case (sanction-track or not, compensation-track or not, and what policy change we should ask for so this isn't ad hoc next time), but the memo has to actually take the metaphysics seriously rather than just saying "we'll be lenient" — engage the strongest version of the "it's not real" side, not the strawman version. If it helps, I've seen people frame this as whether "virtual" means "not real" or "real but differently instantiated," and the board could use that distinction made concrete. Also flag where you think the two cases come apart, if they do — the harassment one and the destroyed-gallery one might not stand or fall together, and the board currently treats them as the same question.

Keep it short enough that people will actually read it, maybe a page and a half.
