For a graduate seminar on machine ethics, I am preparing a one-page decision tree that takes a student from the bare description of a harm caused by an autonomous system to a considered position on the so-called responsibility gap. Rather than asking you to survey the literature, I want the tree built from the logical space of positions, and I want it to do real diagnostic work: every internal node should be a question a student can answer from their own reactions to cases, without already knowing which position it leads to. Please include as leaves, at minimum: the denial that any gap exists (whether by tightening the control condition on responsibility, by appeal to the responsibility of makers for creating risky systems, or by appeal to distributed or collective responsibility); the acceptance of a genuine gap together with each of its main proposed remedies (forward-looking responsibility, insure-and-absorb approaches, and meaningful human control in both its tracking and tracing variants); and the quietist option of tolerating the gap as the price of deployment. Where the tree cannot actually separate two leaves — as with maker-responsibility views that collapse into forward-looking views once pressed — flag the failure rather than papering over it. Two constraints from me. First, the root of the tree should split cases according to whether the gap is epistemic (nobody could have known what the system would do) or agential (people knew enough, but could no longer intervene), since I suspect the literature conflates these. Second, test the tree against these three scenarios I intend to use in class: a home robot that, through an unremarked interaction of two independently tested updates, starts a fire while its owner sleeps; an autonomous trading system that executes a strategy its designers could specify and endorse in the abstract, but whose millisecond-scale cascade ruins a pension fund before any human could have acted; and a conversational agent that gives a user lethally bad advice through a chain of individually plausible responses. For each scenario, say which leaf the tree assigns, and if your tree cannot classify one of them without hand-waving, revise the tree rather than the scenario. Please also identify any combination of node answers that no published position occupies, and say whether that vacancy reflects incoherence or mere unattractiveness.
