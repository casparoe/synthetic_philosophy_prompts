Need someone to referee an argument that's been running in my family for a month now, because I've given up on doing it myself. For context, I'm a third-year student taking a bioethics elective (we just finished the unit on autonomy, which is probably why everyone in this family suddenly thinks they're a philosopher), and my mom and uncle have been fighting about my grandma's advance directive.

Grandma signed it maybe 15 years ago, right after her own mother spent two final years in a nursing home. Great-grandma was, from all accounts, in rough shape — agitated, screaming at the aides, not knowing where she was. Grandma was horrified by it and had the directive drawn up: no life-prolonging interventions, no aggressive treatment, if she ever gets to the point where she can't recognize her family anymore. Well, now she's at that point — advanced dementia, doesn't reliably know who my mom is. But here's the wrinkle: she's placid. Hums along to the radio, smiles at the care workers, eats happily when fed. Zero distress, as far as anyone can tell.

(1) My uncle says the document has to be followed as written. The whole point of signing something in advance, he says, is that the later self can't be trusted to speak for the person — the later self will consent to anything, people in that state generally do — so if we start asking whether her current condition "really" matches what she pictured back then, we're the ones deciding, and that is precisely what she was trying to rule out.

(2) My mom says she's not asking grandma anything, so that's not her position. Her claim is that the document was written for a different person than the one in the bed. Grandma drafted it while watching her mother scream at nurses — she was legislating against that picture. The woman in the bed now is not that picture. Following the letter means letting a description that turned out to be false override the actual person.

(3) Uncle came back with: if directives lose their force whenever reality diverges from what the signer imagined, they're close to worthless, because it never matches exactly. There has to be a line between "different in a way that matters" and "different in a way that doesn't," and mom has never said where her line is, so her position quietly becomes "follow the directive when I agree with it."

(4) Mom's reply: the line falls where the reasons the person had for writing it stop applying, and those reasons are discoverable — grandma talked to people at the time, said things out loud. And besides, mom says, it can't be right that "can't recognize family" counts as the condition being met when what grandma actually dreaded was misery and there is no misery — recognizing people was a stand-in for the thing she feared, and the stand-in has gone wrong.

They've both asked me to settle it and I geniunely can't. Can you go through the four moves one by one, say who's right at each step and why, and also point out anything both of them are missing? And please dont just conclude "it's complicated" — they'll each take that as a win.
