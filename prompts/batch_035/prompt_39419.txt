I've been reading about Fodor's language of thought hypothesis and want to check my understanding before I teach it. As I grasp the argument, it runs as follows: since inferences hold in virtue of the form of the thoughts involved, the mind must store thoughts as structured strings, and cognition must consist of formally licensed transformations of those strings. In effect, the brain runs a proof system, and mentalese is the formal language in which its theorems are written. If that's right, the hypothesis seems to face an immediate empirical problem: proof systems are sound by construction, whereas human reasoning demonstrably is not — a subject committing the conjunction fallacy is drawing an inference that the syntax should not license, which on this reading ought to be as impossible as an unsound step appearing within a valid derivation. Does the hypothesis have a response to this, or did Fodor have to confine it to a small logical core of thought and concede the rest to psychology?
