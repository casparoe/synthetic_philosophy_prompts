I've been around the rationalist blogosphere since the Sequences were still being serialized, and the concept I keep coming back to as under-developed is the one Scott Alexander popularized in "Meditations on Moloch": the idea that multipolar competition reliably destroys value that every participant would prefer to keep, whether it's antibiotic stewardship, academic publishing norms, ad-driven media, or the imagined dynamics of an economy of competing superintelligences. The poetic framing was unforgettable, and since then the community has gotten a lot of mileage out of gestures at Moloch as an explanation of everything bad, but I don't think the underlying conceptual machinery has advanced much past "coordination failures exist and they're bad and Bostrom's black balls and Hanson's Age of Em scenarios both feature them." So here's my situation: I advise a small nonprofit fund that can support roughly four researchers for three years on purely conceptual work -- philosophy, formal modeling, institutional theory, no empirical policy evaluation -- and we want to concentrate it on the race-to-the-bottom problem rather than spreading it across adjacent causes.

Which open questions in this space most deserve those twelve researcher-years, and in what order? To make the prioritization meaningful, defend it rather than just listing candidates. For each question you pick: why is it upstream of the others (I have a suspicion that some disputes about, say, whether binding precommitment schemes can be stable under evolutionary pressure quietly determine whether most proposed "solutions" to races are even coherent, but you should tell me if that's wrong); how tractable is it for people with philosophy and formal backgrounds but no lab; and how neglected is it given that adjacent literatures -- evolutionary game theory, Ostrom's work on commons governance, price-of-anarchy results, the informal rationalist canon -- already exist without anyone synthesizing them. I'd especially like you to distinguish questions whose answers would change real prioritization from questions that only sharpen the vocabulary, and to flag at least one Moloch-adjacent question you'd deprioritize despite its popularity -- for instance, if you think the debate over whether "Moloch" is a legitimate abstraction versus a lossy literary metaphor is not actually load-bearing, say so and explain what does the load-bearing work instead.

Finally, tell me what would move your ranking over the next few years: what developments in AI, in game-theoretic work, or in the world of actual institutions would make the top question rise or fall? And assume no web access and no citations required -- argue from the ideas themselves. If you find yourself needing an empirical number to make a question tractable, that's a reason to rank it lower for this particular fund, and I want you to treat it that way explicitly rather than pretending the empirical work will materialize on the side.
