I'd like you to play Johannes Kepler, in character, in Prague around 1610. I'll play a skeptical young mathematician — call me a recent arrival from Wittenberg who has just worked through your Astronomia nova and admires it enormously, which is exactly why your astrology bothers me. Before you answer, take a moment to think through where Kepler genuinely stood: he despised the vulgar astrology of signs, houses, and predicted destinies, yet he refused to discard astrology wholesale, insisting instead on a stripped-down version in which only the geometrical aspects between planets — conjunction, opposition, trine, square, sextile — carry any real influence, and even then only inclining rather than compelling, acting on sublunary nature through light and through something like the soulish, resonant response of animated things to ratio. Keep his real intellectual resources in hand: the conviction that God geometrizes, the analogies between the sky's harmonies and musical consonance, the physical astronomy of the Astronomia nova, and his practical obligations as imperial mathematician to a court that expects prognostications. Be sharp, not a caricature; concede small points when they're true (you've joked yourself that astrology is a foolish daughter who nonetheless keeps her mother fed) but never concede the main claim that a reformed astrology is a legitimate natural philosophy. Stay in character until I write "stop", keep each reply to roughly one paragraph, use only concepts and knowledge available to you as Kepler around 1610, and if you need something clarified, ask it as Kepler would.

Here is my opening move, to which I'd like your first reply: Master Kepler, in the Astronomia nova you make the planets move by physical causes — the Sun's force, magnetic fibers, real mechanisms a geometer could measure — and I believed you. But now I hear you defend the aspects. You discard the signs, the houses, the whole apparatus of the prognosticators, and say only the angles between planets matter: a trine warms the air, a square disturbs it. Yet by your own doctrine the aspect is nothing but a ratio between two distances — a relation the planets stand in whether or not anyone or anything perceives it. What in nature could possibly respond to a ratio? Your physics says force streams from bodies through light and through the animal spirits you invoke, but the angle between Saturn and Jupiter is not a thing that streams anywhere; it's a figure in the mind of a geometer. So which is it — does the sky act on us, or does geometry act on us? If the latter, you have smuggled a Platonic daemon into your celestial physics and dressed it in Tycho's observations. And if your answer is that the soul responds to ratio as the ear responds to consonance, tell me why the child born under a trine differs at all from the one born under a square, and what you would count as evidence that they do.
