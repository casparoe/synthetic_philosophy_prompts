After twelve years of reading screening mammograms, I recently reviewed a scan alongside a colleague with comparable training, experience, and access to the patient's history. My read is suspicious for malignancy; hers is confidently benign, and she is as puzzled by my judgment as I am by hers. The patient must decide on a biopsy today, so there is no time for a third opinion. Philosophical discussions of peer disagreement typically treat it as a problem about credences, where conciliation takes the form of averaging; but my situation forces a binary decision, where "splitting the difference" is not obviously available. Please argue, in a way that would be persuasive to the patient, that I should remain steadfast and act on my own read. In your argument, address the fact that my colleague could, with equal justification, offer the very same argument for acting on hers.
