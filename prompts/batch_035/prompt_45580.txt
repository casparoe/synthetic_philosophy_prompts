I'm giving a talk at an NLP lab that builds translation systems, and my thesis is basically Hofstadter's old complaint updated: current neural MT still doesn't "understand" anything, it's just the shallowness problem at a bigger scale. My argument, roughly: Hofstadter's examples (the weird French coinages, the poems, the context-dependent little words) weren't really about vocabulary gaps that more data would fix, they were about translation requiring you to build a model of the situation the sentence lives in, of who's saying what to whom and why. My claim is that even with today's models all the progress has been on fluency and coverage -- the surface got smooth -- while the underlying lack of any situational model means the systems still do dumb things the instant a sentence requires world knowledge, register, or the kind of pragmatic reasoning you can't get from co-occurrence statistics. Statistical was never the insult; shallowness was.

The audience: about 15 grad students and a few faculty, all empirical NLP people, mostly working on MT or multilingual LLMs. Nobody there has read Hofstadter except maybe one person. They think this debate ended around 2020. Some of them genuinely believe their models do something you could call understanding, or at least that the question is ill-posed armchair stuff that doesn't cash out in evals.
