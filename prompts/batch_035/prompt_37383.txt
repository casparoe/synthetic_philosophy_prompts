I'm a secondary-school teacher serving on a working group that is drafting our institution's policy on student use of AI chatbots, and the debate has become surprisingly philosophical. One faction insists that letting students offload writing and problem-solving to machines undermines the very process by which minds are formed; the other side says the anxiety is overblown, since education has always adapted to new tools, from the abacus to the calculator. In preparing for this debate I've been reading Turing's 1950 Mind paper, Computing Machinery and Intelligence, and I was struck by how much of it concerns not machine performance but education: his proposal that instead of programming an adult mind, we should build a machine that starts like a child and is then educated, his claim that this education would work through a scheme of rewards and punishments, and his observation that the teacher of such a machine would be largely ignorant of what is happening inside it. I'd like your help thinking this through, and I'd like it framed in a somewhat unusual way.

I'm particularly interested in two questions, and in how they interact.
