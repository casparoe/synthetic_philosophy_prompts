I am a novelist, and my current book is a historical novel set among the workers who built the Hoover Dam. It closes with an afterword of roughly a hundred and fifty factual assertions — dates, wage figures, tonnages of concrete, the names of the contracting companies — and I have checked every one of them. I believe each individually. My editor, however, wants me to open the afterword with the customary line: that despite my best efforts, some errors will inevitably have crept in. The trouble is that I believe her. Books of this kind almost always contain errors, I have no reason to think mine is exempt, and so I believe that at least one of my hundred and fifty assertions is false — even though I cannot point to a single one I doubt. Asserting each sentence to my readers while assuring them that the lot contains a falsehood feels like double-dealing. Is the disclaimer honest, or should I refuse it, or should I go back and shave confidence off each claim until the whole becomes consistent? The last option strikes me as perverse — discarding good verification to preserve a feeling of tidiness — but I would like to know whether that reaction is defensible.

A friend tells me my predicament is no different from buying every ticket in a raffle: one can rationally believe of each ticket that it will lose, and just as rationally believe that one of them will win. The comparison does not sit right with me. In the raffle the outcomes are locked together — exactly one ticket wins, and I understand the mechanism that ties them. My errors have no such structure: each claim was checked separately, and a mistake in one tells me nothing about any other. But I cannot tell whether that difference is what lets me off the hook, or whether I am clinging to it only because the alternative is uncomfortable. There is also a further question I keep circling: whether the customary line in a preface is even a statement about my claims at all, rather than a gesture of humility or an invitation to write in with corrections — in which case perhaps nothing in it contradicts anything I assert afterward.

Please give me your considered view on all three points: whether the disclaimer is honest, whether the raffle comparison holds, and whether the line functions as an assertion or as something else. And please write your answer in American English, as my copyeditor will be reading it as well.
