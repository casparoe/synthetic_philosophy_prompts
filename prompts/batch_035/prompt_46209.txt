I write a column on how people reason under uncertainty, and for a forthcoming piece on epistemic modesty I want an interactive instrument rather than another prose survey of the debate. Please design a decision tree whose root is a shared starting point — "I have formed a confident judgment on some question, and I now learn that others, similarly placed and informed, disagree with me" — and whose branch nodes are questions a reader can answer without knowing any of the position labels. The nodes should be built from the distinctions that actually do the sorting work: whether the reader thinks a relevant reference class of past judgments exists for cases like this one; whether the reader treats their own track record as evidence comparable to other people's or as in some way transparent to them in a way others' are not; whether disagreement from a person known to be better calibrated should move the reader proportionally, fully, or not at all; and whether the reader thinks the fact that they were selected for their expertise (rather than luck) changes how much weight their own view deserves against a crowd of non-experts. Each branch should be phrased as a question about a concrete situation, not as "do you believe in epistemic modesty?" The leaves should carry the standard named positions — the inside-viewer who trusts private reasoning, the reference-class forecaster, the forecaster who defers to aggregated or better-calibrated others, and the position that one should defer to one's past self's track record rather than to either one's current reasoning or to other people — plus, where the logical space has an unoccupied cell, say so and briefly diagnose whether nobody holds it because it is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, flag any pair of positions the tree cannot separate with these branch questions alone, rather than pretending a clean division exists. Audience: educated general readers of a science-and-ideas publication; aim for six to eight branch questions total.
