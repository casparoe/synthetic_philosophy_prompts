so, a bit of background first: I'm 71, spent my whole career as a radio astronomer, retired two years ago, and since then I've been reading philosophy like it's my job — mostly logic, because that's the part my brain can actually grip. I just worked my way into paraconsistent logic and dialetheism and honestly it drives me up the wall, but the people defending it are clearly not idiots, so I want to find out whether I have an actual arguement or just an attitude.

Please play a committed dialetheist. Not a cartoon version: you think explosion — the classical rule that a single contradiction entails everything — is a genuine flaw in classical logic, you accept that the liar sentence is both true and false, and you have real resources. You can explain how your logic keeps inferential discipline without explosion, you don't pretend classical logic is useless (you're happy it works fine for 99% of mathematics and physics), and you think the trouble only shows up with sentences that talk about themselves. You concede small points when they're fair, but you never concede that all contradictions are false. Stay in character until I say stop, and keep each reply to one paragraph in plain english — no formal notation unless I ask for it.

Here's my opening move. In the lab, when two instruments contradict each other — one says the signal is there, one says it isn't — nobody concludes the signal is both there and not there. We conclude that one instrument is out of calibration, and we go fix it. So when the liar sentence contradicts itself, why isn't the obvious diagnosis that the instrument is miscalibrated — that this particular sentence, or the truth predicate pointed at itself, is a broken measurement device — rather than that truth itself needs a new logic? And a follow-up you have to deal with: do you think contradictions are only about sentences, semantic oddities, or do some live out there in the world? Because if a star can't be both present and absent at the same point in the sky — and in forty years I never once saw it — I don't see why a sentence should get to be both true and false just because it's doing something recursive to itself.
