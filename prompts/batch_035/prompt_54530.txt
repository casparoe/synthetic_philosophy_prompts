I run a small history discussion group (motley crew: two military-history buffs, a socialist, a libertarian, and one guy who thinks everything is about geography) and I want a decision tree that sorts people into a position on who started the Cold War. The catch — and this is the part I care about most — is that a good chunk of the tree has to do meta-work, not just blame-assignment: some of my members think "who started it" is a badly framed question to begin with, and the tree should catch them too, not shove them into a pigeonhole they'll reject.

The tree's entry point should be a commitment everyone at the table can accept, something like "by 1947 the wartime alliance was dead and neither side expected it to come back." From there, branch nodes should be questions a person can answer without knowing any school labels — concrete cases and counterfactuals, not "are you an orthodox or a revisionist?" A few cases I want in there (add your own too):

- If Stalin had died in early 1945, does the alliance collapse anyway? (tests how much individual agency vs. structural momentum they think was in play)
- Both in February 1946, Stalin gave a speech blaming capitalism for the world wars, and Kennan sent the Long Telegram arguing the Kremlin was inherently hostile. Which of these counts as a "move" and which as a "reaction" — or does the person think that framing is meaningless because each side genuinely saw itself as reacting to the other's *prior* provocations, all the way back to 1917?
- Can you even "start" a war that never fires a shot? What would the first shot of the Cold War be — a speech, a telegram, a doctrine, a coup? Ask them what evidence would settle it, if any would.
- Where does blame attach: the state as an agent, the specific leadership, the ideological systems both sides were running, or the bipolar structure of post-1945 power itself? And if the answer is structure — is "started" just the wrong verb for the whole discussion?

Leaves should include the classic three (orthodox, revisionist, post-revisionist) plus whatever falls out of the logical space: a "both, but in different currencies" leaf (each side did objectionable things of different kinds, so blame doesn't just split down the middle), and at least one deflationary leaf for people who think the question is misconceived — ideally split between those who think so because structure swamps agency, and those who think so because blaming nations is moralism about history. For unnamed leaves, invent labels, just don't name anything after living historians.

Two things I want flagged honestly rather than papered over: (1) any pair of leaves the tree can't actually separate — I suspect post-revisionism and straight structuralism end up in the same place, tell me if that's right and whether any question could prize them apart; (2) any combination of node answers nobody plausibly holds, with a one-line diagnosis of why nobody's there (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored).

Format: indented text outline is fine, no graphics. Keep the tree tight — around 5-7 branch questions and 6-8 leaves. And at the very end, put a machine-readable line \answer{N} where N is just the number of leaves in your tree — I'm auto-checking consistency, so make sure it matches what's actually there.
