I build distributed systems for a living — replication protocols, gossip, consensus, consensusless designs, the usual — and I have no formal philosophy background. Some years ago I read the "Prelude... Ant Fugue" dialogue from Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach, where an Anteater defends the claim that his friend Aunt Hillary, an ant colony, genuinely thinks, even though no individual ant does: the ants are dumb switching elements, and the intelligence lives in the organization — in patterns of activity at the colony level. The other characters push back, each in a different way: that the colony is just ants, that intelligence belongs to the ants' own simple rules, and that whatever "intelligence" we find in the colony is really projected onto it by an observer — namely the Anteater, who, inconveniently, shows his friendship by eating ants.

Restate this whole debate in the vocabulary I actually use: nodes and clusters, gossip protocols, leaderless replication, eventual consistency, emergent routing, backpressure, observability, the fact that no node ever sees global state, the distinction between a protocol's local rules and its system-level guarantees. Do it position by position — the colony-level intelligence claim, the it's-just-the-ants reduction, and the observer-projection objection — and translate the Anteater's supporting moves too: the colony-level patterns that persist while individual ants come and go, the claim that slow colony-wide processes amount to memory and learning, and his odd contention that his own foraging is part of a conversation with the colony rather than an attack on it.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I actually care about. Say which claims translate cleanly — I already suspect that ants finding shortest pheromone trails maps onto ant-colony-optimization routing and gossip convergence, and I want to know if you agree — which claims only translate with distortion, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all. To sharpen that last category, consider a toy case: a distributed key-value store whose gossip protocol provably converges on shortest paths, whose trace of messages is indistinguishable from a log of ant foraging, and whose nodes cycle in and out faster than the system's own convergence time. Does anything in my vocabulary distinguish the cluster from the colony — anything at all — or does the question of whether one of them "has a mind" simply fail to type-check?

Finally, commit. Tell me where the residue points: is it a defect of my vocabulary — a missing concept that observability dashboards and protocol specs genuinely cannot express — or is it evidence that the philosophers' question was less clear than it looked, an artifact of an informal vocabulary that never forced them to say at which level of description a claim is being made? Do not give me a survey of options; take a position on that question and defend it, and tell me plainly whether, if I accept your verdict, I should conclude that Aunt Hillary is intelligent, is not, or that I was asking a malformed question all along. If you rely on a particular reading of Hofstadter's dialogue rather than the standard one, flag where and why.
