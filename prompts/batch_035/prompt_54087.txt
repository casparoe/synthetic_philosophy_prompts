Two grad students in my department got into it at coffee hour over a friend of theirs who keeps enrolling in a 6am running program. She genuinely wants to run — pays for it, lays out her gear every night, and once said she'd happily hand over fifty dollars tonight to be forced out of bed tomorrow. Then 6am arrives and she hits snooze, every single time. The argument went like this. (1) Anika said this is flat-out contradictory: she prefers running to not running (tonight) and not running to running (tomorrow morning), and no coherent agent can look like that. (2) Ben said there's no contradiction at all — preferences just change over time, like wanting soup at noon but not at midnight, and there's nothing irrational about a self that changes. (3) Anika said the soup comparison fails, because ordinary changes of taste don't come with paying to preempt your future self, and the pattern is systematic — she always reverses as the reward gets temporally close, which looks like a mechanical quirk of steep discounting rather than a series of sincere changes of heart. (4) Ben replied that calling it *weakness* of will already smuggles in the verdict: you need a boss-self whose preferences get to count and can be "overridden," but for all they've said the 6am self just happens to get the last move, and privileging the nighttime preference is arbitrary — maybe she only likes planning to run. (5) Anika shot back that if Ben were right, commitment devices would be incoherent, and they're obviously not: her friend has literally asked them to take her phone away at night so she can't disable the alarm. I couldn't settle it for them. Please adjudicate move by move — who's right about what at each step, and is there something both are missing about what weakness of will is supposed to be? Answer in American English, please.
