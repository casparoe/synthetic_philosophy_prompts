My verdicts: a Confederate general on a courthouse lawn should go, that one's easy. A street named after a local 1920s benefactor, where it turns out the money was made on labor practices that were brutal even by the standards of his own day -- rename, though I feel worse for the residents who have to change their addresses than for the guy's memory. Moving a toppled statue into a museum with full historical context rather than destroying it: obviously right. Destroying a statue deliberately, even illegally, when the community has petitioned for removal for decades and been ignored: I can't actually bring myself to condemn it, which is awkward for me as a rule-of-law person. And here's the pair I can't square: I want the brutal-but-typical-of-his-era benefactor renamed, but I want the repugnant-but-typical-of-his-era scientist kept and plqued, and I notice the scientist is famous and the benefactor is local, and I don't think fame should buy that much forbearance.
