Two friends of mine have been fighting the Mencius–Xunzi war for years now and finally asked me to help them do an adversarial-collaboration writeup, so I'm outsourcing the drafting to you.

Quick setup: Maya is Team Mencius. Humans have incipient moral reactions — her favorite example is the pang you feel seeing a kid about to fall into a well, before any thought of reward or reputation kicks in — and cultivation is watering sprouts that are already growing. Dan is Team Xunzi. Human nature as delivered is appetite and self-advantage; whatever order we have is the carpenter straightening bent wood — ritual, training, deliberate shaping imposed on raw material that doesn't want it.

They've each got stock moves the writeup must be better than. Maya says the carpenter metaphor quietly begs the question — why does the carpenter value straightness at all, where'd that come from, if not from something in us? Dan says the well-child example begs it too — a startle at a kid about to fall in is not yet a moral response, could just be alarm at sudden danger, and Mencius is smuggling the moral reading in.

Here's the evidence pool they want the statement to actually engage with, so this doesn't turn into dueling quotes from the classics (treat these as the shared background, not as things to check):

1. Spontaneous helping studies: toddlers around 14–20 months will intervene to help an unfamiliar adult — retrieving an out-of-reach object, opening a cabinet the adult is bumping against — without being asked and with no reward. And in follow-up variants, the helping drops off when the adult caused the problem on purpose.
2. The helping pattern varies with setting and relationship — kids from more communally organized backgrounds help differently (sometimes more readily) than kids from more individualist ones, and familiarity with the helpee matters.
3. Twin work on empathy-related traits puts meaningful heritability on them — something like a third to a half — with the shared home environment doing surprisingly little work.
4. Fairness development: young kids tend toward flat equality or generosity, but the norm of taking a personal loss to prevent an unfair advantage for yourself seems to come online around 7–8, alongside some genuinely spiteful/envious reactions to being shortchanged.
5. Atrocities don't come for free: people who do terrible things in groups generally need to be drilled into it, and often report having to numb or dehumanize first.

What they want from you:

- A numbered section of claims both would sign. Not vacuous ones — claims where a Mencian and a Xunzian reading genuinely converge on what the finding shows.
- A disagreements table: each residual disagreement phrased so that the person on that side would accept the wording of their own view (e.g., exactly what counts as a "moral" response vs. a mere proto-social one, what "watering sprouts" commits you to versus "shaping raw material," and whether the twin data even speaks to nature-as-Xunzi-meant-it).
- The cruxes: sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one of them. For each crux, give a concrete study, case, or thought experiment both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance — including on the evidence pool above where it's relevant.
- And explicitly: they suspect part of their fight is about the word "nature" itself (raw material you're born with vs. whatever tendencies you come with, learned stuff excluded either way). If some of the dispute is verbal, say which part and why, and separate it from the part that isn't.

Also one twist they insisted on: include the case of a person who is spontaneously kind with zero training and the case of a person who is only decent because of intense drill and dread — both sides agree such people exist; where they differ is what each case proves. Make sure the table handles both honestly.

Keep it casual in tone but rigorous — this document is meant to actually be signed by two people who've been argueing about this since grad school and will absolutely notice any slanted phrasing.
