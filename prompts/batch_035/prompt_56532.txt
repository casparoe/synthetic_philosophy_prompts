I've been following the metametaphysics literature since the big Chalmers–Manley–Wasserman Metametaphysics volume appeared, and something about its trajectory strikes me as diagnostic in itself: around the time Sider's Writing the Book of the World came out, the argument between the deflationists and the ontological realists looked like it was reaching a climax — and since then, as far as I can tell, essentially nobody on either side has moved an inch. The positions are by now familiar. On one side: Eli Hirsch's quantifier variance, on which there are multiple equally legitimate candidate meanings for the existential quantifier, some under which there are tables and others under which there are only things arranged table-wise; Amie Thomasson's easy ontology, on which the existence of tables is trivially settled once there are things arranged table-wise and speaking; and Chalmers's ontological anti-realism, on which the ontological questions, unlike the ordinary internal ones, lack non-verbal answers. On the other side: Sider's realism, on which there is a distinguished, joint-carving quantifier, and the question what exists is the question what it ranges over, even if the answer turns out to offend ordinary language. The one exchange that seemed to promise genuine movement — Sider's argument that the variantist's English sentence about tables must covertly mean some long disjunction over the candidate quantifier meanings, and Hirsch's insistence that it means just what it appears to mean — ended, as far as I can tell, with each side convinced the other had changed the subject.

My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the two camps are answering different questions under one heading. The deflationists are answering a question internal to our language — what does our actual quantifier take there to be? — while the realists are answering a metasemantic one: which candidate quantifier is distinguished, and what exists in that distinguished sense. Each side, on this reading, treats the other's question as illegitimate: the deflationists see the joint-carving quantifier as a posit with no explanatory work to do, and the realists see it as question-begging to settle what exists by consulting what our words happen to mean. If that is right, the dispute occupies an odd position: it is a debate about whether a debate is verbal, so a verdict of 'verbal' seems to threaten to consume the verdict itself.

So: is this a verbal disagreement, a substantive one in disguise, or two questions under one name? Diagnose it from what the participants actually say, not from the labels. Then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a metasemantic account of joint-carvingness that a deflationist could not simply dismiss, a formal result about intertranslatability among candidate meanings, a change of question — or argue that no progress should be expected and explain why. And please address the recursion worry head-on: if the verdict is that the dispute is verbal, what does that say about the fifteen years spent arguing about whether it is verbal?
