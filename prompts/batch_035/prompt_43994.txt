I've been trying to figure out whether my altruistic priorities actually hang together, and I'd love an audit. Here's roughly where I've landed: (1) I'm an aggregative consequentialist — welfare just adds up across lives, suffering and happiness get counted with the same weights, and it doesn't matter how the total is distributed among people. (2) I think s-risks — risks of outcomes involving vast amounts of suffering, think misaligned AI or some kind of dystopian lock-in — are the most important thing for altruists to work on, ahead of x-risk and ahead of near-term causes. (3) I'd trade away an astronomical amount of future happiness to cut the risk of astronomical suffering — if the choice is between a future with a bigger aggregate surplus but a fat tail of intense suffering, and a smaller-surplus future without that tail, I take the second without hasitation. (4) I think it's fine to have kids even though their lives will contain plenty of suffering, as long as the lives are worth living overall — creating net-positive lives is a good thing, at civilizational scale as much as one at a time. (5) I think Pascal's mugging style reasoning is broken — tiny probabilities of astronomically huge stakes shouldn't be allowed to run the show — and I'm definitely not a negative utilitarian; the expected value of the long-term future looks positive to me.

So: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest fix in each case? Rank them by severity instead of treating everything as equally fatal, and be concrete about which commitment has to give — I genuinely don't know where the problem is.
