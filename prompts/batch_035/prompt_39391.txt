Been thinking about how to change my mind on stuff I'm only kind of sure about, and I've got a bunch of cases where I have strong gut feelings but no rule behind them that I can find. Quick setup: I get that when you're certain of something you just condition on it, and when you're only partway sure you do the softer thing where you shift your probability partway (I read somewhere this is called Jeffrey conditioning). That much I follow. My cases:

1. My toast smells a bit burnt. I'm maybe 70% sure it's burnt. I'm fine just nudging my probability that breakfast is ruined up partway, straight off the smell itself.
2. A doctor tells me she's 80% sure my ankle is a sprain and not a break. I just move to about 80% sprained. Feels obviously fine.
3. A friend who's right about coin flips maybe 75% of the time tells me the coin landed heads. Here I do NOT want to just move to 75% heads directly — I want to treat "my friend said heads" as the certain fact and let the reliability do the discounting, which happens to land on 75% anyway, but the route feels like it matters. Am I crazy for thinking the route matters when the numbers come out the same?
4. My smoke alarm, which goes off half the time for no reason at all, goes off. Same feeling — condition on the alarm going off and discount, don't do the soft update on "probably fire" directly.
5. The one that breaks my brain: I smell burning (80% sure that's what it is) and then also see the toast looking dark (70% sure). Apparently it can matter which one I update on first, and I genuinely can't tell if that's a bug or a feature. Gut says it shouldn't matter for two independent looks at the same toast, but maybe it should, if the first piece changes how I read the second?
6. I do a careful calculation that a bridge will hold, then remember I was really tired when I did it. What am I even updating ON now — my earlier probability, the tiredness, or both? Gut says both, but I can't make it precise.

So my actual question: is there one principle that says when I'm allowed to do the soft partway update straight off the uncertain thing itself, versus when I have to hunt down the underlying hard fact (what my friend said, what the alarm did) and condition on that instead? And does that principle settle whether the order thing in case 5 is a real problem or actually fine? My suspicion is that my verdicts in 1 and 2 versus 3 and 4 can't all be saved by one simple rule, and I'd rather know which one to give up than keep a complicated rule that saves everything. Please answer in British English.
