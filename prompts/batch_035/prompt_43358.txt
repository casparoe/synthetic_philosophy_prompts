I have followed the decision-theory threads on rationalist forums for a long time — Newcomb, functional decision theory, acausal trade between agents outside each other's light cones, and the commitment-races worry that whoever binds themselves first wins — and I want to step back from the proposal-of-the-week and get the specification straight. Surrogate goals look increasingly to me like the least bad idea on the table: adopt a stake you do not really care about but can pay cheaply, so that a distant threatener who predicts you will hand over the surrogate stake has no reason to threaten what you actually value. Before I argue for it any further, I want the requirements list. What must any adequate defense against acausal extortion deliver?

Write the desiderata. Cover at least: what counts as success (deterrence versus mere damage limitation when the threat is carried out); behavior toward adversaries who have not modeled your defense at all, since a defense should not make you worse off against the unmodeling; the cost of the defense itself, given that commitment machinery and credible signalling are not free; whether the defense must be self-enforcing under your own decision theory, since an agent who one-boxes and an agent who two-boxes presumably need different defenses, and a defense that works only for agents who can prove things about their own source code is doing extra work; robustness to adversaries who know your defense in detail, including how it was chosen; and whether the defense may be tailored to acausal threats specifically or must generalize to causal ones without leaking. For each requirement, give the motivating case. Then tell me which of the requirements are genuine desiderata and which are prejudices of one camp — the updateless crowd, the causal-decision-theory holdouts, the people who think acausal trade is a philosophers' fiction not worth defending against at all — and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied. My own suspicion is that deterrence and not-inviting-new-threats cannot be satisfied together: any move that makes you look like a bad target also makes you look like a mark for the right kind of threatener, and vice versa. But I want your view, because if they are jointly unsatisfiable, the whole project should be reframed as damage limitation, and someone should have said so on the forum years ago.

Finally, and only after the desiderata are settled, score three candidates against the list, briefly: surrogate goals as described above; a flat pre-commitment to never pay any acausal threat, regardless of cost; and a scheme a friend of mine defends — deliberately and conspicuously pay small acausal threats for a fixed early period of your agency, so that threateners' models of you include a track record of paying, and only later switch to resisting, the idea being that predictable early gullibility is what stops the serious threats from ever being made. He calls it costly credibility. I do not trust my own scorecard on any of this, which is why I want yours — but the criteria first, the scoring after, and I would rather hear that my favored scheme fails a requirement I did not think to list than get a scorecard against a list that was never written down.
