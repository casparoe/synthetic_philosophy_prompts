Acting as a professional political philosopher specializing in egalitarian theories of distributive justice, please think carefully before drafting — brainstorm the points of genuine contact between the two positions first, so that the agreed-claims section is neither vacuous nor slanted toward either side.

Two colleagues have been arguing past each other for years. One holds that equality is fundamentally about how people's lives go for them — equality of welfare, broadly construed, including opportunities for welfare. The other holds that equality is fundamentally about the bundle of means people hold — equality of resources, with individuals then bearing responsibility for what they do with those means. They have finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration and have produced rough notes; you are to write the final joint statement they could both sign.

The statement should contain:

1. A numbered section of claims both accept. These should be substantive, not ceremonial: they agree, for instance, that someone born blind is owed more than an able-bodied person on any plausible view, that the satisfaction of preferences warped by deprivation is not straightforwardly valuable, and that at least some differences in outcome flowing from free choices are not injustices.

2. A disagreements table with the following columns: the precise disagreement, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording — including at minimum the status of expensive tastes (are they compensable or is the bearer responsible for them?), the significance of interpersonal differences in resource-conversion, and whether the right metric should be settled by a hypothetical insurance scheme of the Dworkin type or by asking what people can actually do and be. Mark which disagreements are genuine and which, if any, are partly verbal, and why.

3. A cruxes section. For each crux, a concrete case both sides agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. They have provisionally identified three such cases: a person born with an unusually expensive taste for champagne; a person whose mobility impairment is largely mitigated by expensive social accommodation; and a person who reports high life satisfaction despite sharply restricted options — presumably a case of adaptive preferences. You may add cruxes of your own if they are more decisive than these.

They also both want a frank paragraph on whether Elizabeth Anderson's democratic-equality critique of luck egalitarianism — that both views wrongly abandon the victims of their own choices to the harshness of the market — applies to each of them equally or to one more than the other.

After the joint statement, append a structured summary as a JSON object with fields: "agreed_claims" (array of strings), "disagreements" (array of objects, each with "topic", "welfare_side_wording", "resource_side_wording", "genuine_or_verbal", and a one-sentence "why"), and "cruxes" (array of objects, each with "crux", "test_case", "welfare_prediction", and "resource_prediction").
