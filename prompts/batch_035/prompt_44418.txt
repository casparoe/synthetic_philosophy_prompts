So I'm collecting prompts on philosophy stuff and this one is for the Leibniz domain — I want a satirical piece. Heres the idea: write the official release notes for v1.0 of the actual world.

The setup: the divine engineering team has shipped the world and posts a public changelog in cheerful corporate software voice — "shipped", "known issues", "closed as working as intended", "out of scope", the whole bit. About 700 words, formatted like an actual changelog page.

The satire targets Leibniz's theodicy specifically, and the constraint that matters most: the jokes have to come from his actual machinery, not from generic "God's QA department is bad" jokes. A specialist should read it and think "annoying, but fair." So bug reports get closed with reasons that track his real moves:

- The world was chosen from infinitely many compossible candidate worlds as the one maximizing variety of effects with the simplest laws, so every local fix is rejected with a genuinely stated global tradeoff — patching this would have degraded the optimum elsewhere, and the ticket should gesture at *where* the degradation would occur.
- "Remove all evil" type tickets rejected as not a valid ticket, since no possible world contains that (omnipotence doesn't ship contradictions — the backlog only contains compossible feature sets).
- Specific evils defended with his own analogies, paraphrased: shadows in a picture that make the lights stand out, the seasoning that keeps the whole from being bland, that kind of thing.
- Natural evil blamed on the refusal to write special-case miracles over the general laws (simple, uniform laws being a perfection — the excuse should be phrased as an engineering virtue, like refusing to hardcode).
- Moral evil deferred to the free-will module.
- The catch-all closure: users are positioned inside the system and cannot evaluate global optimality from a limited vantage point, so "is this really the best of all possible worlds?" is closed as cannot-reproduce-from-your-position.

Structure it should have: a Features section bragging about pre-established harmony as zero-latency sync between mind and body (no messages, no clocks, never misses); a Known Issues section where everything is marked working-as-intended; at least one declined feature request from an archangel, declined for a genuinely Leibnizian reason, not just "no"; and a roadmap note at the end confirming there will be no patch and no 2.0, since this release was selected over all alternatives and re-rolls arent on the table.

Two final constraints. First, don't let it be a pure dunk — the comedy comes from the moves being recognizable, and if a dark note leaks through (one bug ticket at the very bottom sitting "under review since the beginning of time," something even the team can't cheerfully close), that's fine, but the changelog voice stays deadpan and corporate the entire way. Second, no real names beyond Leibniz-adjacent concepts — there can be a persistent furious user filing complaints from somewhere like Ferney, but never named, and don't quote from actual texts (paraphrase his analogies instead, no quotation marks anywhere).

ps typos are ok since the dataset wants natural-sounding prompts, but keep it to like two or three, not one per line please. And obviously a model without web access should be able to do this from what it knows of Leibniz — nothing should hinge on exact passages or section numbers.
