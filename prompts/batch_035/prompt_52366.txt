I TA a philosophy of science class, and our unit on theories of causation has ground to a halt in a way I can't diagnose. We did Lewis's counterfactual dependence theory (possible worlds, comparative similarity) and then Woodward-style interventionism (wiggle x, hold everything else fixed, see if y moves), and the students kept asking what the actual disagreement is. Fair question: the two agree on nearly every example anyone brought up, and the cases where they don't — weird preemption scenarios, one student's thing about "trumping" — seem to break both theories in the same places anyway. My own hunch, which I want checked, is that there's no real fight at the level of cases at all, and the whole thing is about which concept gets to sit in the undefinable basement: Lewis's similarity metric for evaluating counterfactuals is full of causal-sounding tie-breakers (no miracles, maximize match of particular fact, etc.), while Woodward analyzes interventions in terms of counterfactuals he's happy to take as primitive. So maybe each side is accusing the other of circularity in the foundations and the case-talk is downstream of that. Is that right? Diagnose what kind of disagreement this really is — substantive, verbal, or two research programs that mostly don't overlap — using what each side actually says, and tell me whether anything (a shared formal framework, an impossibility result, agreement on cases) would count as progress, or whether we should just pick a notation and move on. Under 500 words please.
