At a meeting of a campaign group I belong to, a veteran organizer shut down a debate about escalating our tactics by repeating the old line that the arc of the moral universe is long, but it bends toward justice. His point, as far as I could tell, was that history is on our side, so patience is strategy enough and escalation is unnecessary. The line has always bothered me without my being able to say why: it sounds like a factual claim, but I can't tell what fact, if any, it claims. Please set out the distinct precise theses this one sentence could be expressing — a bare extrapolation from the past direction of travel; a claim about mechanism, e.g. that moral gains, once won, are locked in a way that setbacks are not; a providential or teleological claim about where history is headed; a self-fulfilling claim, where the belief sustains the struggle that makes it true and is true only while believed; a normative maxim about how to act rather than a prediction at all; and any further readings you think are genuinely distinct. For each, say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, or false, and — this is the part that matters — whether it would actually support the organizer's conclusion that patience suffices, as opposed to merely comforting it. Conclude with your best guess as to which reading he meant, and throughout please indicate how confident you are in each verdict, singling out the point in your answer of which you are least sure.
