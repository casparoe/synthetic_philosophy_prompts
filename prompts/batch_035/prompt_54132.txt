I'm a novelist and my best friend — also a novelist, and the one person whose verdicts on hypotheticals I actually trust — has a solid track record on identity puzzles: the ship of Theseus with planks swapped one at a time while she keeps sailing, same ship; the Hobbes version where a second ship is built from the discarded planks while the repaired one still sails, the repaired one is the original and the plank-ship is a copy, even though its got more of the original wood; her grandfather's axe with both head and handle replaced over the years, still his axe; a ship fully disassembled for restoration, planks stored in a warehouse five years, then reassembled from the same planks with maybe a quarter new wood where things had rotted, same ship; and a band that replaced every member one at a time, NOT the same band — she called it a tribute act wearing the name.

Here's my problem. My current novel is about the Aspasia, an 1890s cargo ship scrapped in 1902, her timbers sold off, some ending up as barn beams. In the 1950s a collector tracks down the timbers, recovers about 60% of them, and rebuilds a seaworthy ship around them with new wood filling the gaps, sailing her under the original name. My friend is my first reader and I want to know what she'll say before she says it, so I can either write toward her verdict or have the argument now instead of over the whole manuscript. So: predict her verdict on the collector's ship, on the full-scale replica another character builds from the 1902 plans with entirely new wood, and on whether she'll say any part of the Aspasia is still sitting in that barn. My guess is her rule is something like continuity first, matter second, but the band verdict seems to throw that off, so you'll need to sort out whether it's one rule or a rule plus a special clause for people-constituted things.

Structure it as: "The rule(s) she's running" (~150 words), "Verdicts on my three cases" (~300 words), "My best guess on where she'll dig in" (~100 words), and "The case that would settle it" (~100 words) — one further case that would split whatever candidate rules are left standing.
