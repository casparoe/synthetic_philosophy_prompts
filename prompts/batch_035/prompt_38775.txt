So, background: I'm a software engineer, no formal philosophy training whatsoever, but I read a lot of pop-philosophy books on my commute and I've sort of half-formed a view I actually care about now. It comes from Peter Singer's book The Expanding Circle, which I read a couple years back. The way I'd state my current view: what we call moral progress basically *is* the circle of beings-we-take-into-account getting wider -- first kin, then clan, then tribe, nation, all races, eventually (maybe) animals, distant strangers, future people -- and the engine is some mix of widening sympathy (trade, travel, cities, novels putting us in contact with strangers' lives) and plain logical pressure: once you admit the reason you care about your neighbor's suffering is just that it's suffering, there's no non-arbitrary place to stop the circle. Kind of like how a codebase's rules can't special-case arbitrary user IDs forever -- someone eventually asks why user 57 counts and user 58 doesn't, and the special case collapses.

Lately I've been arguing about this with a coworker who thinks the whole picture is naive. Her take: actual moral change is mostly downstream of power and technology (vaccines, washing machines, mass media, who owns what), the "expanding circle" framing is just how the winners narrate it after the fact, and plenty of groups got squeezed *harder* while the circle was supposedly expanding for everyone else. And honestly I didn't have great answers, which bugs me. So here's what I'm asking for: what would actually count against my view? Not just "a philosopher disagrees," but concrete observations, cases, or arguments that, if they held up, would seriously damage it. For example: if it turned out moral change tracks shifts in economic power rather than argument, does that kill the whole view or just the "logical pressure" half? What about historical stretches that look like progress but where the circle arguably shrank at the same time -- or where people widened concern for one group while getting more ok with cruelty at the edges (cheap factory farmed meat while pets get pampered, that kind of thing)?

And the big one I keep circling and can't resolve myself: does my view quietly assume there's a fixed moral truth out there that we're converging toward? Because "expansion" sounds directional, expanding *toward* something -- and if that assumption is wrong, I'm not sure the circle language even makes sense anymore, it'd just be a circle wobblying around for unrelated reasons. Please rank the defeaters for me from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest about which ones someone holding my view should concede really sting versus which we can dismiss as category errors. Also fine to tell me if there's a weaker version of the view that's better insulated -- but then be straight with me about whether insulating it that way just makes it unfalsifiable, and whether that's a dealbreaker for a claim of this type or just the price of admission for moral philosophy generally.

(Also apologies if I'm butchering Singer, I read it a while back and honestly might be blending in stuff from other books/pop-sci podcasts, so feel free to flag where my rendition of him goes off script.)
