I'm a PhD student in cognitive science trying to work out where I stand on illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. As I understand the view from Dennett and Frankish, phenomenal properties as standardly conceived don't exist, and introspection instead produces a user-illusion-style misrepresentation of our own minds — analogous, perhaps, to the way confabulation and choice-blindness studies show that subjects can sincerely misreport their own mental processes. But the view looks self-refuting to me: an illusion is, by definition, an appearance to a subject, so positing an illusion of consciousness presupposes experience; if there is no phenomenal consciousness, there is nothing for the illusion to be an illusion for. If that's right, illusionism cannot even state its central claim without presupposing what it denies, and phenomenal realism would be established by reductio. Is that a fair assessment, or is there a reading of "illusion" on which the objection misfires — and if so, does that reading still deserve to be called an illusion of consciousness rather than a simple error?
