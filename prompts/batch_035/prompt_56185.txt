I'm on a late antiquity module and we've now had two whole seminars on Augustine's absolute ban on lying, and it's always the same case — someone at the door asking where the intended victim is hiding, may you lie to save a life. I want to poke at a different corner of his view, one our tutor keeps gesturing at but nobody actually works out. As I read him, a lie for Augustine is speaking against your own mind: you have one thing in your head and put out another in words or signs, wanting to deceive. The ban then attaches to the lie so defined. But there's an obvious gap in that: you can get someone to believe something flatly false without ever asserting anything false yourself. You just let them do the inferring.

So build me a thought experiment that lives in that gap. The setup: one person brings another to a seriously wrong belief, and every single sentence out of their mouth is literally true. Constraints: keep it realistic — an office, a hospital, a family, whatever, nothing exotic; and no jokes, no irony, no acting, no metaphor, no riddling. Augustine exempts joking and that whole register because the joker signals in his very tone that he means no deceit even though what he says isn't true, and I want those doors shut so the case can't be waved off as one of his exemptions. The deception also has to be genuinely costly to the person decieved, not a wind-up. One paragraph for the scenario.

Then the walkthrough, which is the part I actually care about. First: does Augustine's definition of lying catch this case at all — and pin down exactly which clause does the work, the falsity of what's asserted or the will to deceive, given that on his own definition you can even lie while saying something true (if you take it to be false and mean your hearer to receive it as true), and can deceive without lying. Second: does his framework condemn the case, allow it, or go silent — he's clear that keeping quiet and withholding the truth isn't lying, so if the case comes out permitted, tell me whether that's a stable result for him or whether it sits badly with the spirit of the rest of what he says. And finish with the repair job: the obvious move is to shift the target from what is asserted to what is communicated, so say whether that patch can be made without either collapsing into a blanket ban on causing false belief (which would outlaw letting someone stay mistaken, and he allows that) or smuggling his exemptions back in through the back door. If you don't think the case can be built to spec at all, say so and tell me which constraint is doing the blocking.
