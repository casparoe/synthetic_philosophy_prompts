Lately I've gotten into the philosophical fight about "pure time preference" -- the idea (rejected by basically everyone who writes on it, starting with Ramsey and Sidgwick) that a benefit in the year 2300 counts for less *just because it's later*, holding everything else fixed. I want you to restate that whole debate in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then tell me what didn't survive the translation.

Then the residue report, which is what I actually care about: which parts of the philosophers' argument map cleanly onto things I already have (a term structure with a default/existence component, marginal utility weighting via consumption growth, the difference between pricing risk to an investor and valuing a benefit to a recipient), which parts only fit if you distort them, and which parts have no expression at all in how I think about discount rates. And the big question at the end: is there anything the philosophers are arguing about that genuinely has no counterpart in finance vocabulary, and if so, is that because my vocabulary is missing something, or because their disagreement is less substantive than it looks?
