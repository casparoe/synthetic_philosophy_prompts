I teach a university elective on AI ethics and futures — 18 students, no technical background, one 90-minute session. Design an in-class simulation that makes them feel the "slow value drift" failure mode for advanced AI: the idea that a system can remain competent, cooperative, and apparently aligned while its operative values drift step by step, with each step individually defensible or barely noticeable, until the endpoint is something nobody would have endorsed at the start. The drift should arise from the rules and incentives the participants face, not from anything the facilitator narrates — ideally each participant experiences, from the inside, approving or shrugging at every step along the way and only recognizes where they've arrived at the end. Include the setup and materials, the rules, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the activity onto parts of the idea (local vs. global evaluation, the absence of any single betrayal point, competence drifting independently of goals). Since the course also covers the influence-seeking failure mode, the debrief should briefly contrast what the activity captured with what belongs to the other story, so students can say which one they felt and why. Finally, tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which feature of the idea the activity cannot reproduce and I will have to supply in the debrief.
