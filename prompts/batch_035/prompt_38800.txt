My wife and I have been going back and forth on when to have our first kid for like two years now. Right now is objectively a rough time: my startup just failed, we're temporarily living with her parents, and I'm dealing with some health stuff that could take a while to sort out. The obvious move seems to be waiting a year or two until things stabilize, and honestly part of what pulls me toward waiting is a vague sense that it'd be unfair to bring a kid into the middle of all this. But here's where it gets weird. A friend of ours (huge effective altruism guy, the type who argues about population ethics at parties) told us that the "unfair to the kid" reasoning is confused. His point: if we wait, the child conceived in two years is a literally different child than the one we'd conceive now — different sperm, different egg, different person. So there's no particular kid sitting in the waiting room of existence who gets a worse deal if we delay; there's no one the delay is bad *for*. And if the later kid ends up with a decent life, nobody has been wronged by the wait. By his lights the whole guilt trip is basically an accounting error.

I sort of follow the argument and it's not obviously wrong, but it smells like one of those clever arguments that proves too much. It seems like it would also let parents deliberately conceive a child they know will have a rough deal, as long as their only alternative was no child at all — and something about that still feels off to me, even if I can't pin down who exactly is being wronged. My wife's take is that the timing decision can still intuitively be more or less fair to whoever ends up existing, but she admits she can't defend that against the different-person point. Are we just being confuzed by mistaking a shift in identity for a harm, or is there actually something the clever argument misses?

For calibration: I'm the kind of person who's read enough decision theory content over the years to be suspicious of "there's no one to blame so nothing bad happened" moves — they remind me of people who two-box in Newcomb's because only the causal framing counts, and who I think are making a category error about what they should care about. So feel free to get into the weeds about whether person-affecting intuitions can be reconstructed in a principled way or whether my friend's argument really does dissolve them. I'd like an actual verdict on who's right here, not just a survey of takes.
