ok so I've ended up with a bunch of positions on aggregating tiny harms — the torture vs dust specks thing and its neighbors — and I want a consistency check before I put them in a talk. here's roughly where I stand:

1. In the classic case I side with the specks: 3^^^3 people each getting one dust speck in the eye is worse than one person tortured for 50 years. the sheer number does all the work and I'm fine with that.
2. but within a single life I don't think tiny bads aggregate anywhere near that way: no amount of minor irritations spread across one person's whole lifetime adds up to something as bad as even a short stretch of torture. so there's some kind of cap or steep diminishing return inside a life.
3. I also hold that there's no privileged unit of grouping for suffering: it shouldn't matter morally whether we file bad events under persons, hours, incidents, or populations — the totals are the same no matter which ledger you keep.
4. in population ethics I reject the repugnant conclusion: a zillion barely-worth-living lives is not better than a smaller number of very good lives, and I don't think adding extra lives can be weighed in the same currency as making existing lives go better.
5. and I'm a pretty straight consequentialist about the ethics of action, and I think cost-benefit style reasoning is right in policy, including cases where many people's small burdens get traded against a few people's large harms.

go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank them by severity, and for each real one say what the cheapest fix would be and what that fix costs me elsewhere. don't be diplomatic, I'd rather know which one has to go. think it through carefully before answering — maybe lay out what each commitment actually commits me to before you start pairing them off, so the ranking is driven by the commitments and not vibes. take the role of a moral philosopher who's spent way too long on these aggregation fights and has no stake in which of my views survives.
