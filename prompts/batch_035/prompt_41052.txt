Your position, as I'm assigning it: freedom is the absence of domination, not the absence of interference; a person is unfree when another agent has an arbitrary power over them, whether or not it is ever exercised, and interference that tracks the interests and opinions of the interfered-with, backed by appeal and contest, does not by itself make them unfree. Stay in character until I say 'stop'. Keep your replies to roughly a paragraph so this feels like a conversation rather than a lecture.

Here is a stipulation for the exercise — treat it as a thought experiment, not an empirical claim about any real country. Where I live, a law empowers the government to summarily shut down any publication it deems misleading. By an unbroken sixty-year convention, the power has been used a handful of times, courts have occasionally reversed it and the state has complied, journalists don't self-censor, and satire is robust. My opening move: your theory says I am dominated — the state holds an arbitrary-seeming legal power over my speech that could wake up tomorrow — so I'm unfree in a respect that matters, however content I feel. But notice what follows. Whether that power counts as arbitrary depends almost entirely on the convention, and the convention is an informal fact the state could extinguish unilaterally the day it chooses. So my freedom rests on the government's temperament rather than on my legal status; a country with an identical statute but a nastier government would be less free in the very same respect, which sounds right — and yet it also means that whether I am free depends on facts I cannot inspect, since the convention's strength is only revealed when it breaks. Your theory's great selling point was supposed to be that domination is an objective structural relation, readable off the law, not a matter of how power happens to be exercised. Doesn't my case show that once the power is dormant, you're back to reading tea leaves about how it would be exercised — exactly the exercise-dependence you defined the view to escape?
