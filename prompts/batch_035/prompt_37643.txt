I've been reading the inside view vs. outside view debates for over a decade, and I've finally tried to write down my actual epistemic commitments. Before I circulate them to my forecasting group, I'd like an audit of whether they jointly hang together:

1. When my object-level reasoning conflicts with a reference-class forecast, the reference class should usually win.
2. When epistemic peers whom I consider informed disagree with me, I should move my credences toward the aggregate rather than trust my own analysis.
3. I take Tetlock's results seriously: on long-range political and technological questions, expert forecasts are barely better than chance, and adding more experts doesn't reliably fix this.
4. Reference classes are chosen, not given. The same venture can be lumped in with all startups, or with two-person technical startups in the same city by repeat founders, and the base rates differ wildly. Picking which class is relevant is itself an exercise of inside-view judgment, so the outside view never fully bottoms out.
5. In my day job I estimate software projects, and my calibrated inside-view estimates beat both the industry-standard overrun statistics and any base-rate model I've built, so I keep using them.
6. I believe universal deference is self-undermining: the reason averaging across people works is that their errors are partly independent, but if everyone adopts averaging as a norm and discards their own signal, the independence is destroyed and the aggregate degrades.
7. I endorse the first-principles, inside-view-heavy reasoning behind taking AI risk seriously — arguments about capability growth and timelines for which no clean reference class exists and expert consensus is currently an unreliable guide.

Which pairs of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and what would the cheapest coherent revision be? Please order the tensions by severity rather than flagging everything equally, and for each one say which commitment has to give and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere on the list.
