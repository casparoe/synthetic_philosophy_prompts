I edit a small philosophy newsletter with a few thousand subscribers, many of whom took a single philosophy course years ago and remember Twin Earth only hazily. For an upcoming issue on meaning and reference, I would like a self-scoring quiz titled something like "Which theory of meaning do you actually hold?" The point is diagnostic rather than pedagogical: readers should be sorted by the verdicts they give about concrete cases, not by which labels they find congenial, so no question may simply ask whether the reader thinks meaning is in the head, or whether they are an externalist. Each of the roughly ten questions should present a case with two or three answer options phrased as concrete judgments about that case, and a scoring key at the end should map answer patterns onto positions.

The cases must include the canonical ones plus a few twists. Classic Twin Earth, where the clear liquid in rivers and rain is XYZ rather than H2O. The 1750 version, where neither Earth nor Twin Earth has developed chemistry, so no speaker on either planet can distinguish the two liquids and both use "water" in exactly the same way. Putnam's division of linguistic labor: a speaker who cannot tell elms from beeches, or real gold from fool's gold, yet seems to refer to elms and gold just as successfully as the experts do. A Burge-style case in which a whole community is simply mistaken about what their word applies to, contrasted with a community that never had any experts to defer to in the first place. Empty names. A slow-switching case in which someone migrates from Earth to Twin Earth in adulthood without ever noticing anything amiss. The jade case, where a single word has all along tracked two chemically unrelated minerals. And, as a modern twist, a question about whether a language model that has never touched water, seen rain, or needed a drink could nonetheless possess words and thoughts genuinely about water. You may add one or two cases of your own invention — for example, a community whose word covers a H2O/XYZ mixture, or an isolated population with no science at all — if doing so sharpens the sorting.

The leaves of the taxonomy should include at least the following: the causal-historical or externalist theory of natural kind terms; classical descriptivism or another internalist account; social externalism of the deference-based kind; conceptual-role or inferentialist semantics; and a deflationary leaf on which the whole dispute about "water" presupposes a mistaken picture of what meaning is. The scoring key should map answer combinations onto these positions, explicitly note any answer patterns that do not cleanly correspond to a leaf and diagnose why nobody seems to occupy that slot (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored), and flag any pair of positions the cases cannot genuinely separate. Please write the quiz, the options, and the key in American English, keep the whole thing to about 1,500 words or fewer, and make the answer options concrete enough that a reader answering honestly could be surprised by their result.
