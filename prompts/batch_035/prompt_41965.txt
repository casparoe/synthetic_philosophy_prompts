I write a small satirical philosophy zine and want a piece taking on the paradox of deontology. Could you write a glossy marketing brochure, about 600-700 words, for a fictional company called Clean Hands Assurance Ltd. -- a firm that offers "moral outsourcing": for a subscription fee their contractors will do the dirty deed (bribe the official, push the fat man, harvest the one organ) so that you, the client, merely allowed things to unfold and keep your constraints spotless. The jokes should come from getting the philosophy right, not from zany business-speak: agent-relative constraints, the doing/allowing distinction, double effect (maybe a gold-tier plan covers only harms "merely foreseen"), and the awkward loophole of whether hiring someone counts as doing it yourself -- the brochure should insist, a bit too hard, that it doesn't. Somewhere in the fine print there should be a pricing tier that quietly embodies the paradox itself: e.g. a "constraint consolidation service" where, to prevent five violations of the same constraint, the company performs one violation on the client's behalf and charges extra for it. Include an FAQ section in which a consequentialist customer keeps asking why anyone would pay more to bring about a worse outcome, and the company answers with slogans instead of arguments. End with a disclaimer that almost, but not quite, concedes the whole thing. Keep it deadpan throughout -- a reader should be able to tell what's being ironised without the brochure ever winking at them. Please write in British English.
