I teach the unit on promising in a mid-level ethical theory course, and every year the same thing happens: students can recite the labels — social practice view, expectation or reliance view, normative-powers view — but when I push them on cases, their actual commitments don't line up with the labels they picked. One student insisted she held a practice view but thought a promise made on a desert island, to someone who has no idea what promising even is, still binds; another claimed the reliance view and then said a promise to a hermit who will never learn of the promise, and whose life is unaffected either way, is still binding. My TA thinks this shows the labels are useless; I think it shows we need a better sorting instrument. So I want a "which theory of promising do you actually hold?" quiz for the course website, built entirely from cases rather than self-identification.

Please write eight to ten diagnostic questions, each a concrete scenario with two or three answer options, phrased so a student can answer without knowing which theory they're committing to. The cases should be chosen so the live theories genuinely come apart on them, and I'd like you to use at least these four, which produced the most interesting disagreement in my seminar: the promise to a dying recluse who neither relies on nor will ever learn of the promise (isolating whether binding requires any effect on the promisee at all); the coerced promise, where the promisor's will and the practice's normal operation both run one way and our intuition runs the other; the promise made within an unjust institution, like a corrupt apprenticeship scheme, where the practice exists but arguably shouldn't; and the case where the promisee later says "never mind, don't bother" but the promisor goes ahead anyway (which probes whether the duty is owed to the promisee, waivable like other owed things, or somehow not waivable at all).

Then provide a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the main positions: the convention or practice-based view on which promising is an artificial virtue sustained by a shared social convention; the expectation or reliance view in both its thin and assurance-based forms; the normative-powers or consent view on which the promisor's own will does the binding work; any rights-based variant you can cleanly separate; and the deflationary or skeptical view that promises create at most a courtesy, not a duty. Two further requests: first, note explicitly which pairs of theories the quiz cannot pull apart no matter how the questions are answered, rather than pretending it sorts everyone; second, identify any coherent answer pattern in the logical space that no actual theory occupies, and say whether you think the vacancy is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect — my TA and I have a running bet that the promise-to-the-recluse case carves the space in a way the standard taxonomy misses, and I'd like to know if the quiz bears that out.
