Two philosophers have agreed to an adversarial collaboration after years of arguing past each other. One holds that there is nothing in principle defective about moral deference: if a trusted friend sincerely tells me an action is wrong, her testimony can give me justified moral belief or even knowledge, just as testimony about the weather or the gas mileage of a car can. The other holds the pessimist view: that there is something wrong with treating moral questions this way — that one ought to 'see the point for oneself,' and that even a reliable moral testifier leaves the deferential believer in some way epistemically or otherwise worse off. Draft the joint statement they should be able to sign.

Please include: the claims both can accept about testimony in general and about cases where they agree moral deference is unproblematic; the precise statements of their residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would endorse the wording; the cruxes — I suspect one concerns whether moral understanding is something over and above moral knowledge, and another whether the defect pessimists point to is epistemic at all rather than agent-regarding (a loss of integrity or autonomy rather than of justification); and, for each crux, a case, argument, or thought experiment both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. As a running test case, use a voter who, unable to work through the tangled ethics of a complex referendum question on her own, votes on the word of a friend whose moral judgment she trusts. If you conclude that some portion of their dispute is merely verbal, say which portion and why.

Return the result as a JSON object with the following schema: agreed_claims, an array of strings each stating one claim both sides can sign; disagreements, an array of objects each with fields label, optimist_formulation, and pessimist_formulation; cruxes, an array of objects each with fields statement, agreed_test, optimist_prediction, and pessimist_prediction; and verbal_disputes, a string identifying any parts of the disagreement both would concede are about words rather than substance (an empty string if none).
