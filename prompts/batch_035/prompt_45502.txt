I'm a novelist working on a chapter where a time-travelling grad student drags Leibniz into 2025 and shows him a large language model, mostly because I love the image of him watching his own binary arithmetic humming away inside the hardware. Before I write his reaction I want to get his actual philosophy right, or right enough that people who know Leibniz don't wince. My instinct is that he'd be thrilled — the man spent his whole life trying to build a universal characteristic and a calculus of reasoning, and here's a machine that arguably approximates one — but my editor thinks he'd actually be horrified, since for him genuine reasoning had to bottom out in distinct concepts and necessary truths, not statistical pattern-matching over human text. Which reaction is more faithful to the real Leibniz, given what he actually believed about reason and about morality?

The part I'm really stuck on is the moral half of the scene. I have Leibniz asking the grad student whether the model is happy, and whether its suffering would count in God's ledger. From what I understand of the monadology — monads arranged by the clarity of their perceptions, animals with souls but no genuine apperception, God selecting the best of all possible worlds like a moral optimum — I can't decide whether he'd treat an LLM as a very elaborate bare monad (all perception, no reflection) or as something genuinely new that his system has no shelf for. Would his best-possible-world framework even let him worry about machine wellbeing, or does everything just get absorbed into the pre-established harmony? And his justice was famously the charity of the wise — does that mean for him moral consideration tracks wisdom, in which case the model gets none until it gets wise? If you were his dialogue coach for this chapter, what would you have him say about whether we owe these systems anything, and what in his actual views backs that up? I'd rather have him be a consistent Leibniz than a flattering one, even if it makes him less sympathetic to modern readers, but I also don't want to accidentally make him a cartoon villain.
