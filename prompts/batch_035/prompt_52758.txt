I run a small podcast where we do staged interviews with dead philosophers -- the host asks questions, and a voice actor who's done the reading answers in character. Next month's episode is John Stuart Mill, and I want to go after a part of his record that basically never comes up in these discussions: the India stuff. The angle I care about is the tension between On Liberty and his day job. He spent his entire working life, from 1823 to 1858, as an official of the East India Company drafting dispatches about how India should be governed, and right at the end of that career he published On Liberty, which contains that infamous qualification that despotism is a legitimate mode of government for barbarians so long as the aim is their improvement. Then a year later, in Representative Government, he argues that free institutions don't take root among peoples who aren't yet fit for them.

Write me eight to ten interview questions for him, plus a follow-up for each keyed to the answer he'd most likely give in character. I want questions that press the actual tensions rather than invite a lecture. Specifically: would he classify Indians among the "barbarians" his proviso covers, given that he spent years quietly pushing back against his own father's History of British India, which had judged Indian civilization pretty harshly, and given that his 1859 essay on non-intervention seems to apply one set of rules to dealings among civilized nations and another to dealings with barbarous ones? How does he square the On Liberty proviso with Representative Government's claim about unready peoples, and what is the actual test of readiness supposed to be -- what evidence would show that Indians had become fit for self-rule? And how does his defense of Company rule as a kind of insulated, meritocratic trusteeship -- he was drafting defenses of its record right as Parliament moved to abolish it in 1858 -- survive his own stated worries about power that answers to no one? Include at least one question a hostile modern critic would want asked, and at least one that hands him his strongest reply, e.g. that his whole career shows he thought Indians were being prepared for self-government, not permanently excluded from it.

One more thing, since the voice actor will be answering in character: I need to know which premises in my questions are solid and which are shaky. For each question, note briefly whether what I'm attributing to Mill is settled among people who study him or a genuinely contested reading, and if some attribution is contested, say so and give me a version of the question that doesn't lean on it. Please keep that labeling honest even where it makes my questions weaker -- and if the honest answer is that the record just doesn't tell us what he'd say, say that too, since a question can still work on air even if we're flying blind on the answer.
