I'm drafting a short position paper for a media literacy workshop on epistemic communities, and before I circulate it I'd like a consistency check on the package of views I've somehow ended up holding. To fix terms: by an epistemic bubble I mean an information network that merely fails to include certain voices, so that its members never encounter them; by an echo chamber I mean a community whose members have been trained to actively discredit outside voices, so that encountering them does no good, since the outsider is pre-registered as either a dupe or a hired shill. My commitments, as best I can state them: first, echo chambers so defined are far more damaging than bubbles, because a bubble is cured by mere exposure whereas a chamber survives exposure intact. Second, on contested empirical questions a responsible layperson should defer almost entirely to the consensus of the relevant experts rather than weigh the evidence themselves, since the layperson lacks the competence for first-order evaluation, and pretending otherwise is an epistemic vice dressed up as independence. Third, when an outsider attacks the consensus of a community one trusts, the rational first response is to suspect the outsider's credentials and motives; treating every outsider as owed a full hearing on the merits is naivety, not open-mindedness. Fourth, diversity of viewpoint in one's information diet is a genuine epistemic good, and I have deliberately rebuilt my own media diet around it. Fifth, some movements — the ones I would call conspiratorial rather than merely mistaken — should not be debated or platformed, because platforming confers a legitimacy their evidence has not earned and hands them attention they have not deserved. I have the sense that something in this list pulls against something else in it, but I cannot locate it, and I would rather find out now than in front of the workshop. Please audit the set: tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly; which pairs look like tensions but dissolve on inspection; and, if revisions are needed, which single change would clean up the most and what it would cost me to make it. Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as an equally weighted list, and please write in continuous prose only — no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables — the way one would write a letter to a colleague.
