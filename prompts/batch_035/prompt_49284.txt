I'm a retired field ecologist — thirty-odd years counting seabird colonies and later doing beetle surveys, which is alot of crouching in wet grass — and since retiring I've fallen hard down the philosophy rabbit hole. The thing I keep coming back to lately is the wild animal suffering debate: the folks who point out that most wild animals live short, miserable lives (the frog lays ten thousand eggs so that 9,998 can be eaten, starve, or desiccate before adulthood) and then argue that ecosystems might be net-negative welfare machines, such that on some versions of the reasoning even habitat destruction could count as a welfare gain. The arithmetic of r-selected reproduction is genuinely brutal, I've watched enough of it firsthand, so I find the arguement uncomfortably hard to dismiss. But I also suspect it's the kind of view that's easy to push to grotesque places, and honestly I'd rather see someone mock that pushing than another earnest blog post about it.

Write me a satirical press release, 600-700 words, from a fictional agribusiness-logging conglomerate announcing its new "Compassionate Landscape Conversion" initiative, in which the company proudly rebrands deforestation and wetland drainage as mercy toward wild animals. The humor has to come from getting the real philosophy right: the press release should lean on actual load-bearing features of the net-welfare-of-ecosystems reasoning — sky-high infant mortality in r-selected species, the claim that never being born beats a short painful life, the slide from "habitats are bad for animals" to "destroying habitats helps them" — exaggerated, not invented, so someone who knows this literature nods along instead of wincing at a strawman. Deadpan corporate-PR voice, absolutely no winking at the reader. Include at least one quote from an invented executive and one from an invented animal-welfare researcher the company has hired as a consultant, plus maybe a line about their offsetting program. I don't want it readable as a dig at environmentalists or as pro-logging propaganda — the target is the logical structure of the argument, followed one step past where the actual philosophers go, and it should be obvious the company's reasoning only works because they never ask the questions the philosophers do ask.

Please take on the persona of a philosopher who occasionally writes this kind of thing, and start with a brief brainstorm about which premises of the real argument are load-bearing and which ones the satire should put under strain — that'll keep the piece honest. Afterwards, add a short note on where the piece takes liberties: which steps in the actual philosophical debate are genuinely contested (say, the comparison of nonexistence to a short miserable existence, or whether destroyed habitat stops containing animals rather than just displacing and concentrating them elsewhere), where you deliberately made the company's version weaker than the real argument, and whether any real philosophers have actually endorsed something close to what the fictional company claims. In that note, say how confident you are in each claim you make and single out the one you're least sure of — I'd rather have a candid "I might be misremembering how this debate actually goes" than a bluff.
