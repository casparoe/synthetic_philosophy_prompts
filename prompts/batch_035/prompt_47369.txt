I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and I'm working on a feature pegged to the perennial "great man theory of history" fight. I'm rereading War and Peace for it, and I keep noticing Tolstoy is doing something that sounds suspiciously like what philosophers of causation argue about. In the second epilogue he says historians should do for history what calculus did for math — integrate the countless tiny individual wills, the "infinitesimals" — instead of pointing at Napoleon as the cause of everything in 1812. So his underlying claim seems to be: because the retreat from Moscow had a million contributing causes, none of them (not Napoleon, not the cold, not Kutuzov) is THE cause.

I want a reading of the novel through the lens of overdetermination and multi-causal explanation. Take the role of a professional philosopher of history with a metaphysics background, and please think carefully — do some brainstorming before you commit to a line. Questions I'd love you to actually answer: Is Tolstoy describing genuine overdetermination, where each cause would be individually sufficient without the others (like two assassins firing at the same instant), or is he really an early INUS-conditions theorist — every cause just a necessary part of a larger sufficient complex, none sufficient on its own? Do the battle scenes — Borodino, where Pierre wanders around the batteries and the Russians basically cant do anything deliberately, the burning of Moscow — actually support the metaphysical thesis, or do they show something weaker, namely that agents' control over outcomes is diluted, not that their causal contribution is absent? And where does Tolstoy cheat? He still needs Napoleon's vanity and Kutuzov's passivity to carry the plot, which means he's picking out some causes as more explanatory than others — exactly what his own theory forbids. Finally: is there something the novel gets right about multi-causal explanation in history that the analytic philosophy papers on causation dont? I want a reading where neither the novel nor the philosophy gets flattened, and dont just agree with Tolstoy.
