My mom works as a home health aide — she drives to old people's houses and helps them bathe, take meds on schedule, eat, that kind of stuff. Her agency (and I think basically every agency like this) has a checklist for each visit, and her whole performance review is built off that checklist: did the patient take meds at the right times, was the house safe, did she chart everything correctly, was she on time. What's NOT on the checklist is all the stuff the families actually thank her for, like sitting with a lonely patient an extra twenty minutes, or noticing that someone has gone quiet lately and calling their daughter about it. If anything that kind of thing can hurt her score, because it makes her late for the next client.

I started thinking about this bc we're doing ethical theories in my philosophy elective and my teacher mentioned care ethics, the view that the heart of morality is relationships and responding to particular people's needs, not applying abstract rules from a distance. So heres my question: what does the checklist system presuppose about what care IS? Does it presuppose that a visit's value is just a bundle of discrete, verifiable services, such that the good of the visit is basically the sum of items ticked off? And does that quietly rule out the care ethics picture, or could a care ethicist still defend the checklist — maybe on the grounds that meds and safety are what the patient is literally depending on, so verifying them IS responding to need? There's also this weird tension I can't shake: if the relational stuff is what patients and families actually value, the system is measuring everything except the point.

Also curious what would concretely have to change if the agency rebuilt its reviews around the care ethics view instead — would they need peer or family input, spot-checks by someone who knows the patient, or is there just no honest way to score relationships without ruining them? And is there some independent reason (fraud prevention, fairness between aides, legal liability) that pushes agencies back toward checklists no matter which theory of care is right? Sorry if this is rambly, I genuinely can't tell if im asking a question about my mom's job or about ethics lol. Probably both.
