My study group built an argument out of Dennett's color phi stuff and I think it proves way too much, but I can't find where it slips. Setup in case you need it: in Kolers and von Grünau's experiments from the 70s, you show a subject a red dot in one position, then about a tenth of a second later a green dot in a different position, and they sincerely report seeing one single dot moving smoothly from the first position to the second and changing color midway — even though nothing moved. So the brain constructed motion that wasn't there. Dennett then asks: was the first flash experienced as a stationary red dot and then the memory of it edited later (his "Orwellian" story, like the regime rewriting the newspaper), or was delivery to consciousness held up until the second flash arrived, so the whole edited show got presented at once ("Stalinesque", like delaying the broadcast until the show's been cleaned up)? He says there's no fact of the matter, since any probe — button press, verbal report, later recall — happens after the second flash either way, so both stories predict the same thing forever.

Our argument:

(1) In the phi case, either the Orwellian story is true or the Stalinesque story is true.
(2) Every probe you could ever run happens after the second flash, so both stories make identical predictions about every possible observation.
(3) When two hypotheses about an event make identical predictions for every possible observation, there is no fact of the matter which one is true.
(4) So there's no fact of the matter about whether the stationary red dot was ever experienced as stationary.
(5) But structurally nothing is special about phi: any experience at time t can only be probed by later behavior, and there's always a lag during which the brain could revise or delay.
(6) So there's no fact of the matter, about any experience at any time, what it was like or when it happened.
(7) And that can't be right — it amounts to there being no facts about experience at all, while the whole argument got started by taking the subject's phi report as genuine data.

Which step do we give up? My instinct is (5) is where it slides, because phi is special in one respect: we have independent evidence the motion percept was constructed (nothing moved!), and elsewhere we don't have that. But I can't see why that difference should matter once (3) is granted — (3) seems to say the construction evidence isn't reachable by probes anyway, so how could it ground a fact here but not there? Or does (3) not say that? And if instead I reject (3), do I get stuck with facts about the timing of experience that no experiment could ever reveal, which sounds suspiciously like the Cartesian theater move the whole thing was supposed to get us out of? Price the exits for me — which is cheapest, and what does each cost?
