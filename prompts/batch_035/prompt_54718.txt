I've been keeping a list of my gut verdicts on when a holding is legitimately someone's, and I want to know whether they add up to a principle or whether I'm just muddled. Self-made wealth built up over a lifetime of genuinely voluntary deals: theirs, full stop, and taxing it beyond the bare minimum is taking. Inherited wealth where every link in the chain back was clean: also fine — the dead person's choices should bind. A painting the current owner bought in good faith, but which was looted from a Jewish family in 1938: has to go back, good faith doesnt save it. Wages from a sweatshop job the worker took because the alternative was starvation: voluntary on paper, but something's off, I can't call that holding fully just. Selling yourself into slavery for a big sum, eyes open, no coercion: shouldn't be enforceable, even though every box is checked. Someone who appropriates the only waterhole for miles, leaving everyone else barely worse off than before: actually fine — but when he then charges monopoly prices, not fine. And reparations funded by taxes on people whose own holdings are perfectly clean: I keep flipping on this one.

My tentative principle is that a holding is just if it came from a just original acquisition plus a chain of voluntary transfers, with some correction mechanism bolted on for historical injustice. But that makes the slavery contract come out just and the sweatshop come out just, which I won't accept. So either the principle needs fixing or some verdicts have to go — I genuinely don't know which. Can you find a version that saves most of my verdicts, tell me which ones it can't save and why, and say what it predicts for the reparations case I can't settle? Also please flag how confident you are in the whole reconstruction and point to the single bit you're least sure about.
