I am a novelist four hundred pages into a trilogy set in a fictional archipelago whose Bronze-Age-style palace economies fall apart over the course of the second book, while a Maya-inspired heartland in the third book empties out after drought and war. My editor wants me to lean hard into "collapse" in the marketing copy and in the narration itself, but as I read around the archaeology I find the field split between those who see genuine civilizational failure and those who see reorganization, simplification, or migration that only looks like failure from the perspective of elites and later historians. Rather than guess per chapter, I want a decision procedure I can run myself, quickly, on any society I depict: the ordered questions to ask about it, what evidence within my own fictional world would count as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure terminates in a verdict of collapse versus transformation versus something the dichotomy simply misses, and where it must hand the call back to me because the question is ultimately about which losses matter, which is a values question dressed up as an archaeological one. Please ground it in the actual shape of the scholarly debate, including the distinction between the fate of a political elite and the fate of the population at large, and the role of nostalgia in how decline gets narrated. Then run it on two cases: my palace economy, whose scribal class vanishes while the villagers carry on farming with faint memories of the old temples; and my drought-stricken heartland, where urban courts fail but the population persists in smaller settlements. Finally, apply it to my marquee fallen empire, which I have written from the start as an unambiguous collapse, and tell me honestly if the procedure says I got it wrong.
