I teach an ethics seminar, and every year the unit on Augustine on lying gets stuck in the same place. In De Mendacio, written to the Spanish monk Consentius, Augustine insists that every lie is a sin, and he explicitly extends this to the case that seems designed to break his position: the episode that prompted the treatise, in which Catholic priests were proposing to pass themselves off as Priscillianists so they could infiltrate that sect's hidden meetings, and he refuses to bless it, arguing that we cannot prosecute lies by lying any more than we could prosecute robberies by committing robbery. In a later letter, on the request of the general Boniface, he even seems to hold that a lie is not permitted to save an innocent person from immediate death. My students split in a way I've come to suspect isn't really about Augustine at all: the "absolutists" say he is simply applying his own principle that we must not do evil that good may come; the "consequentialists" say he is obviously making a mistake, and a monster at that; and a third group says the whole fight is verbal, because Augustinian "lying" has built into it a refusal of temporal goods that the consequentialist side simply doesn't share, so the two camps never actually contradict each other. Diagnose this standoff for me. Is it a genuine normative disagreement, a dispute about what counts as a "lie" versus mere deception (Augustine also holds, in the same works, that deceiving by non-verbal means, or letting someone remain in false belief, is not itself lying), a disagreement about the relative reality of temporal versus eternal goods, or something else? Support the diagnosis with what Augustine actually argues, including his remarks on jesting, on the definition of a lie as a statement against the mind, and on why he thinks even a lie that saves a life harms the soul of the liar. And tell me honestly: given your diagnosis, is there anything — a distinction, a case, a better reading of the texts — that could actually move the seminar, or should I expect this one to stay stuck?
