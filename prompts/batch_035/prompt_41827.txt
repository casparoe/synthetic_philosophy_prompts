hey, could use your help with something. i'm a phd student in philosophy of biology and i'm putting together a set of test prompts to probe whether models actually get the species problem or just pattern-match to "mayr said reproductive isolation, moving on." so i want a satirical piece this time.

write me an agony-aunt column, "Dear Alpha Taxonomist," from a fictional advice column for practicing taxonomists and systematists. around 650 words, formatted as maybe 4-5 short letters with replies. the idea is that every letter is a genuine puzzle from the species problem literature, but the agony aunt responds the way an actual cynical taxonomist would, and the joke is always that the species rank is a decision procedure pretending to be a natural fact.

the letters should cover stuff like: a field biologist with a ring species (salamanders going around a valley is fine, or gulls around the arctic, whatever) who wants to know if the two forms that meet and don't interbreed at one end count as separate species even though they're connected by gene flow the whole way around; a microbiologist asking what to do with organisms that swap genes so promiscuously that any tree you draw for them is more of a suggestion; a paleontologist with a beautiful continuous fossil sequence wanting to know where to cut the lineage into species, given that every stratum is marginally different from the last; a splitter whose colleague lumped their twelve beloved species into two and is asking if the committee can be appealed to; and someone who just wants to know whether the official code has any view on what a species even is. (it doesn't — the naming authorities rule on names and publication and priority, not on whether the thing you named is real, and the agony aunt should treat this as a completely reasonable division of labor.)

the replies should be deadpan and professionally exhausted. some moves i'd love to see: advice that picks whichever species concept is most convenient for the outcome the letter-writer wants (a conservation listing, tenure, revenge on the lumper); a recommendation to just run the species-delimitation software over and over until it outputs the number you already had in mind; the observation that if two populations can interbreed but one of them "wouldn't" given the chance, you are now doing psychology. keep the underlying biology accurate enough that a systematist would nod instead of wince — the humor should come from exaggerating what's actually true about the field (the two-dozen-odd named species concepts, the fact that species counts for charismatic groups inflate when funding is on the line, the barcode-gap people vs the morphology people) rather than inventing stuff.

one more thing: don't let the agony aunt have a crisis of metaphysical nihilism at the end. she's seen too much. her column ends the way taxonomy does — with a confident answer, a new combination in a low-impact journal, and a note that this decision is itself subject to revision by anyone who feels strongly enough about it. thanks!!
