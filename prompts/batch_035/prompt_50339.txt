I'm a second-year graduate student working through the betting-argument literature in a formal epistemology seminar, and I keep noticing something about the arguments for updating rules that I can't find a label for. A Dutch book against someone whose credences violate the probability axioms is, as it were, all on the table at once: the bookie writes down a finite set of bets, the agent accepts each at odds she herself endorses, and no matter how the world turns out, she pays out more than she takes in. But the arguments I've seen for conditionalization don't look like that. There the bookie has to offer one bet now, wait for the agent to learn something, and then offer a second bet whose terms depend on what was learned; the guaranteed profit exists only relative to the bookie's contingency plan, which branches on the evidence. At no single moment is the agent holding a guaranteed losing portfolio — it's the whole tree, including moves made after the agent updates, that constitutes the book. My question is whether this configuration has an established name of its own, something more specific than the blanket label I've seen attached to these arguments, and whether the difference from the static case is generally regarded as a real weakening of the argument rather than a cosmetic one.

I half-remember that some authors take these tree-like cases to show at most that one should precommit in advance to updating by conditionalization, not that one is irrational at the later time if one happens to update some other way, and I would like to know how that objection is standardly formulated, what the main replies to it are, and where the dialectic currently stands. I'm also unsure where the edges of the phenomenon lie: does it cover cases in which the agent can see the bookie's strategy coming and simply refuse the second bet, and does it cover one-shot setups like the Sleeping Beauty problem, where the putative book is made of the same bet offered repeatedly across indistinguishable awakenings rather than of different bets offered at different times? If there is no settled term for exactly this branching, spread-over-time structure, then please coin one, define it precisely, and say what it excludes — I care about getting the carving right, not just a name. Please write your answer in American English.
