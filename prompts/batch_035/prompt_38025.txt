I'd like you to act as a professional philosopher of mind, someone who knows the intentional-stance literature thoroughly, and help me work through the case study I've chosen: the 1997 rematch between Garry Kasparov and IBM's Deep Blue. In case any details matter, here is what I'm relying on: Deep Blue was a purpose-built machine with custom chess hardware, it won the six-game match 3.5–2.5, and during the second game it played a move so uncharacteristically patient that Kasparov became convinced a human grandmaster had intervened — a suspicion IBM denied, and one that was never resolved to his satisfaction, since IBM retired the machine and declined a rematch.

My thesis, roughly, is that the match is an unusually good test case for Dennett's framework because the stances aren't merely an interpretive menu here but were forced into practical, consequential use: Kasparov himself switched between treating the machine as an intentional agent (attributing to it plans and evaluation of his style) and retreating to the design stance when its behavior surprised him, and the cheating accusation was in effect a demand to adjudicate a dispute at one stance by shifting to another. But I don't want to pre-decide the interesting questions, so please think carefully and brainstorm the possible readings before committing to one, and then give me a disciplined analysis rather than a survey.

First, map the case onto the theory precisely: for each of the three stances, say what it predicts or permits with respect to Deep Blue's play, what data each stance had access to, and where the stance boundary actually fell — for instance, whether the fact that Kasparov could open the machine's design documentation in a way he never could with a human opponent introduces a deep asymmetry the framework never anticipated. Second, address the real-patterns question head-on: is Deep Blue's chess competence a real pattern in the same sense Kasparov's is, and does the answer change when we notice that in one case the intentional stance is the only access we have, while in the other the design stance is fully available? Third, give an honest account of where the theory is silent or strained: what does the intentional-stance framework simply not speak to about this episode — the phenomenology of playing something you suspect is not what it seems, the role of the human team behind the machine, the retirement of Deep Blue ending the debate? Please flag any place where you think my framing begs a question, and tell me which of these threads is strong enough to anchor a term paper and which I should cut.
