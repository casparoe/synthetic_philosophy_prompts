I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind helping run our department's adversarial collaboration pilot, and I've been paired with mediating two participants who have argued past each other for a year: a philosopher defending the Buddhist no-self view (anattā) and a secular neo-Lockean who holds that psychological continuity constitutes a genuine, if complicated, criterion of personal identity rather than a mere convention. The impasse is oddly specific: the Buddhist suspects the Lockean smuggles a further fact back in through the very word criterion, while the Lockean suspects the Buddhist's metaphysical thesis quietly collapses into the soteriological one — that disproportionate self-concern is misplaced — which she could accept without conceding anything about whether persons persist. Write the joint statement they should be able to sign after a good-faith collaboration. It should contain: a numbered section of agreed claims (covering, at minimum, the failure of the Cartesian ego, what psychological connectedness does and does not explain, and what fission cases establish); a table of the residual disagreements, each phrased so that the side holding the view would accept the wording of its own position; the cruxes — I believe one is whether the no-self claim is metaphysical or ethical in the first place, and another is whether treating persons as conventionally real though not findable under analysis marks a substantive difference from Parfit-style reductionism or only a verbal one — and, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both would accept as bearing on it (teletransportation, gradual replacement of the constituents of a person, obligations incurred before a fission event), with each side's prediction about that case recorded in advance. If they conclude part of their dispute was merely verbal, say which part and why. Format it as the numbered agreed-claims section followed by the disagreements table.
