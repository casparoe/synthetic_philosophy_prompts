I've been in a discord server arguing about presentism vs eternalism for almost two years now and it has gone precisely nowhere, which I guess is on brand since the professionals have been at it for a century. I'm starting to wonder if the reason it goes nowhere is structural, not just that everyone's stubborn, so let me lay out where we always end up and then ask what kind of disagreement this actually is.

The presentists say only present things exist — dinosaurs don't exist (they *existed*), tomorrow's sea battle doesn't exist (it *will*), only what's happening right now is real. The eternalists say past, present, and future things all exist tenselessly, and 'now' is just an indexical like 'here' — Trenton being 'here' for me doesn't make Trenton the only real place.

The arguments in our group always cycle through the same three moves. (1) Someone invokes special relativity: there's no frame-independent simultaneity, so there's no fact about which distant events share 'the present' with us, so the objective now is dead and presentism with it. The presentists fire back that relativity just describes how signals and clocks behave, and anyway one guy in our group insists there could be a privileged foliation of spacetime that physics simply can't detect — metaphysics doesn't have to wait for lab equipment. (2) Someone says the felt whoosh of passage is a datum: something is manifestly happening to time, and eternalism has no room for it, since nothing tenselessly 'flows' anywhere. The eternalists answer that this is just a fact about our interiors, maybe about how memory works, not a fact about the world. (3) The presentists ask what the whole debate could possibly make a difference to, since even an eternalist grieves, saves for retirement, and is relieved when surgery is over. But then one of them ruins it by saying that under eternalism you shouldn't be *relieved* that the suffering is over, since on that view it tenselessly exists forever, just 'at' a different temporal location — the pain doesn't stop being real, it's just not where you're looking. That one actually keeps some of them up at night, and the other side mostly responds by refusing to feel guilty about it.

There are also the hybrid positions — growing block (past and present real, future not) and moving spotlight (all times real, one of them lit up) — which everyone agrees are weirder than either main view, and there's the old rate-of-passage objection: if time flows, how fast? One second per second, which sounds like answering a real question with a tautology, or maybe is a category mistake, or maybe is fine, we genuinely can't tell.

My working hypothesis, and I offer it as just a hypothesis: the core dispute is verbal. The presentist's 'exists' smuggles in 'exists now', which makes their claim trivially true and the eternalist's trivially false, while the eternalist reads 'exists' tenselessly and gets exactly the reverse — so both sides are 'winning' their own sentence and losing the other one. But I can't fully buy this, because the relativity argument doesn't feel verbal in the slightest. If there's genuinely no privileged foliation, something substantive seems to have been discovered, not merely disambiguated. And maybe the phenomenology people and the ontology people are just doing different projects under one label.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Verbal, substantive-but-hard, or two questions wearing one name? If it's verbal, why does the physics argument seem to bite? If it's substantive, what is the hidden crux — is it about detectable structure in spacetime, about whether experience can be evidence about ontology, about what 'real' is for? And given your diagnosis, what would count as progress: some physics result, a distinction nobody's drawn yet, a change of question — or is this one of those debates that stays warm forever for reasons we should be able to name? Please actually diagnose it rather than just surveying the positions, I can get the survey from the server.
