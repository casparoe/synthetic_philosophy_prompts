My argument runs through the unilateralist's curse: when several people each have only marginally-favorable evidence about whether a risky act is okay, and each of them can act alone the moment their own evidence tips that way, the act ends up happening far more often than the pooled evidence warrants — the individual errors all point in the same direction instead of cancelling. The room is mostly ML researchers, zero philosophers, and the people I'm bracing for are: (a) three or four hard open-source-everything folks who will say restraint on our side is pointless when others won't restrain, and that any gate just degrades into an informal veto for whoever happens to be most risk-averse; (b) a handful who'll claim this is just groupthink with extra steps and will rot our research culture; (c) our two safety people who actually know the decision theory and might point out that our judgments are hardly independent since we talk all day, and that my proposal quietly leans on the errors still not washing out.
