I'm trying to figure out what we're even asking for when we ask what a society should equalize -- welfare, resources, capabilities, opportunity for welfare, whatever. Before I go read the actual debate (sen, dworkin, cohen, rawls etc.) I want the spec first, not the survey. So: write me the desiderata that any adequate "equalisandum" -- the thing a theory of equality says we're trying to make equal -- has to meet.

Some things I'd want on the list but don't know how to weight:

- it has to handle expensive tastes and cheap tastes without collapsing into "we subsidize people who develop a taste for fine wine"
- it has to handle adaptive preferences, the oppressed-person-who-says-she's-fine case, without the theorist claiming to know better than everyone what's good for them
- it has to be measurable at least in principle -- if we can't even in principle say who's worse off, the theory can't guide institutions
- it has to say something about disability, since a pure resource metric seems to hand the same bundle to a paraplegic and an able-bodied person and call that equal, which seems wrong
- it has to be compatible with some kind of responsibility-sensitivity, luck egalitarian intuitions, without making desert the whole theory
- it has to survive the guy who voluntarily takes a huge gamble, loses, and demands compensation -- or else explain why that intuition is confused

For each requirement say what the motivating case is and whether it's a hard constraint or just one camp's prejudice dressed up as a requirement. Then tell me which pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied -- my hunch is the expensive tastes one and the adaptive preferences one are in tension, since handling the first pushes you towards subjective welfare and handling the second pushes you away from it, but I want to know if that's right or if there's a clever escape. Also flag which single requirement knocks out the most contenders.

Don't bother with a reading list or citations, I know roughly who the players are and I'll get to them. What I want is the list of demands, because I suspect the demands themselves are where all the action is. And if you think some of my bullet points above are actually bad requirements -- like, they presuppose an answer -- say that too, that's the kind of thing I'm trying to catch before I'm deep in the literature.
