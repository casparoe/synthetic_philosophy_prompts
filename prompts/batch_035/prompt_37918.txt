I'm a TA grading a philosophy of physics midterm, and I'd like your help assessing one student's answer. The exam question read:

"Earman and Norton's hole argument is often said to turn a symmetry of general relativity into an argument against a view about spacetime. (a) Reconstruct the argument: what is the 'hole,' what does the diffeomorphism do, and which premise about spacetime points generates the trouble with determinism? (b) Explain why the hole can be any arbitrarily chosen finite region of the manifold — in particular, why nothing in the argument depends on the hole being devoid of matter or fields. (c) Distinguish the hole argument from Leibniz's older shift argument against absolute space: what exactly is the target of each, and by what principle is the offending view convicted? (d) Describe one substantivalist reply to the hole argument and assess whether it preserves what mattered about substantivalism."

The student wrote:

"Substantivalism about spacetime is the view that spacetime points exist in their own right, independently of the material objects and fields located at them. In general relativity, spacetime is represented by a differentiable manifold together with a metric field (and any matter fields). Now pick a finite region H of the manifold — the hole — chosen to be empty of matter and fields, so that the metric alone is in play. Let d be a diffeomorphism of the manifold that is the identity outside H but nontrivial within it, and use it to drag the metric field along. The resulting model agrees with the original everywhere outside the hole but differs inside it. Since the theory cannot distinguish between these two models — diffeomorphism invariance guarantees that both satisfy the same field equations — while the substantivalist must say they represent genuinely different situations (the point p that had certain metric properties now has different ones), the theory fails to determine what happens inside the hole from what happens outside. So GR, on the substantivalist reading, is indeterministic. This is the hole argument.

The argument is really Leibniz's shift argument in modern dress. Leibniz argued that shifting all matter uniformly through absolute space produces a universe distinct on Newton's view yet indistinguishable by any possible observation, and since the identity of indiscernibles rules out two such universes, absolute space falls. Here the diffeomorphism plays the role of the uniform shift, the manifold points play the role of absolute space, and the identity of indiscernibles does the same work: the two models differ only in which point bears which properties, which the identity of indiscernibles forbids, so manifold substantivalism falls. In both cases the offending view is convicted of posposing differences that make no observational difference.

The cleanest substantivalist reply is sophisticated substantivalism, which keeps the manifold points but accepts Leibniz equivalence — the claim that diffeomorphically related models represent the same physical situation — and therefore denies haecceitism, the doctrine that possible worlds can differ solely in which individual occupies which qualitative role. Since relationalism also holds that diffeomorphic models say the same thing, the two views come out nearly indistinguishable, and little of substance is lost."
