Been reading rationalist blogs and forums since around 2009, and I was there for the whole dust specks vs. torture saga when it was being re-litigated in comment threads every few months — the setup where you choose between 50 years of torture for one person and a single dust speck in the eye for each of some astronomically large number of people. I took the torture side back then and I still would, but what's always bugged me is that hardly anyone on that side actually defended plain linear aggregation. The interesting moves were things like thresholds, moral uncertainty, or the claim that a speck-sized harm effectively rounds to zero in deliberation, while the specks people mostly just refused to accept a theory that lets a big enough pile of trivial inconveniences outweigh a life. Fine. But recently I've been arguing with people about voting and individual carbon footprints, and I noticed the structure there feels weirdly different, and I can't tell if the difference is real or if I'm just being inconsistent.

So here's what I want: compare the aggregation of tiny harms, the dust-specks side of that debate, with the aggregation of tiny causal contributions in collective action problems — a single vote in a large election, one person's flights adding a rounding error to total emissions, one donor in a huge matching campaign. On the surface both cases ask whether a very large number of individually negligible items sums into something that can outweigh (or constitute) something big. But the standard arguments in each case pull in opposite directions: everyone accepts that one car trip causes basically zero warming but that the aggregate is what matters, and nobody calls you insane for weighing your own trip by its expected contribution — yet many of the same people think a specker who weighs each speck by 1/N is making a moral error. So: in what ways are these two aggregations structurally the same, and where do they come apart? Things I'd want you to actually take a stance on: whether the specks-side arguments (thresholds, rounding to zero, refusing to trade a person against trivia) carry over to the collective action case, and if someone both picks specks AND thinks their individual vote matters in expectation, whether that's flat inconsistency or whether there's a genuine disanalogy doing the work — e.g. that in collective action each contribution is futile alone but part of a shared scheme, whereas each speck is a fully realized harm borne by a specific person; or that harms might have a floor below which they stop counting while benefits have no analogous ceiling. Don't just give me the usual shouting match between shut-up-and-multiply and its-monstrous-to-accept-that, I want the comparison done carefully enough that I could tell which of my two positions I should give up.
