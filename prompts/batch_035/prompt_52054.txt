My closest colleague and I spent most of last evening arguing about whether the knowledge an AI system has but does not volunteer is recoverable in principle, and we ended the argument in the worst possible state: each convinced of a general methodology, with no shared account of what success would even look like. He holds that if a model's internal states carry information that reliably tracks some fact about the world, then there is some interrogation — probing, fine-tuning, adversarial prompting, decoding tricks, whatever — that will surface it, and that anything that resists every method simply is not known by the model. I suspect this quietly collapses two different things: what the model can be made to output under pressure, and what its internal states actually encode, and I think methods that satisfy the first can fail the second and vice versa. Before we argue again, I want to get the standards straight.

Please write out the desiderata: what any adequate method for eliciting latent knowledge from an AI system has to deliver. I have in mind requirements like the following, but treat this list as a starting point and add, refine, or reject items as the analysis demands: the method must distinguish a model's representing a fact from its merely being disposed to assert that fact; it must not simply train the belief away or distort it in the course of discovering it; it must work under distribution shift, since a model that knows a fact only on the exact prompts it was coached on has not really been tapped; it must produce reports the model endorses rather than readings we extract against its disposition; and it must be robust to the model's having been trained to please its interrogator, since a method that elicits what we want to hear is worthless no matter how confident the output. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it — sycophancy, sandbagging on evaluations, models scoring well on multiple-choice probes while denying the same facts in free text, and the like — and mark which requirements are non-negotiable for the purpose my colleague and I actually care about, which is knowing what a deployed model takes to be true about the world, and which are preferences of one methodological camp.

The part I most want from you, and the part on which I suspect the whole disagreement turns: say whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable. In particular, examine whether the requirement that elicitation not disturb what is elicited can conflict with the requirement that the elicited report be something the model itself would stand behind, and whether the requirement of robustness under distribution shift is compatible with methods that lean on a fixed probing direction. If some subset genuinely cannot be jointly satisfied, say so plainly and explain which requirement ought to give way, since that conclusion — not any particular method — is what my colleague and I need before we argue again. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; a brief remark on how straightforward prompting, linear probing, and contrastive fine-tuning fare against the list is welcome, but the scoring is not the point.
