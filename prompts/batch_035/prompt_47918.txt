I help run a little zine for our department's grad conference, and this year the theme is "bureaucracy meets metaphysics" (dont ask, it involved a fight at the colloquium reception). Could you write a piece of around 600 words formatted as the official minutes of a fictional organization — call it something like the Interworld Consortium of Concrete Worlds — which has been founded to establish trade, diplomacy, and a shared calendar between possible worlds. The delegates should be earnest and relentlessly procedural, Robert's-Rules energy throughout.

The catch is that all the humor has to come from the actual commitments of Lewis-style modal realism, not from invented wackiness: worlds are concrete but spatiotemporally and causally isolated, so nothing can be shipped, no delegate can attend, no announcement can be circulated, and the secretary keeps having to note that the minutes will exist only in each world separately and will not match. Every delegation insists it is from the actual world, and the chair keeps having to rule "actual" indexical and move on. The counterpart theorists and the transworld identity people should derail the roll call because they can't agree on whether anyone was present, and whether the delegate who seconded the motion is numerically the same person who proposed it. At some point plenitude gets invoked: any motion that could be passed is passed in some world, which the chair cites as a reason to dispense with voting entirely, and somebody objects that this makes the Consortium ontologically cheap to run, which is somehow pitched as a selling point. A committee should be proposed — ideally a Committee on Establishing Contact, which everyone recognizes as the thing that will quietly kill the project while letting it live forever in the org chart.

Keep it deadpan, no winking at the reader, and a metaphysician who knows the literature should laugh and also grudgingly concede it's a fair portrait of the view rather than a caricature. If you want a closer, the minutes of the "next" meeting could appear at the end, having been adjourned before they began, in every world where they were held. Feel free to make it 500-700 words if you need the room, and don't worry about making the delegates distinct characters — they're counterparts anyway.
