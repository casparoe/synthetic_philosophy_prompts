I'm a graduate student in a seminar this term on truth and realism, and the unit we've just finished covered Dummett-style semantic anti-realism and Fitch's paradox of knowability. Over the course of the readings I've come to hold something like the following position: meaning is fundamentally tied to assertibility conditions rather than recognition-transcendent truth conditions, and a Dummettian anti-realist's commitment to some version of the knowability principle survives Fitch's result only in a restricted form. Since the unrestricted principle, together with the modest epistemic and modal assumptions of the Church-Fitch proof, collapses it into the absurd conclusion that all truths are actually known, I've settled on the maneuver I take to be closest to Tennant's: restricting the knowability principle to so-called cartesian propositions, i.e., propositions not already known to be unknown, with Hart's restriction of the principle to basic, non-epistemic truths as a nearby cousin. My sense is that this restriction is principled rather than ad hoc, since the Fitch counterexamples are precisely propositions whose knowability is self-undermining in a way no genuine verificationist should have wanted to endorse in the first place.
