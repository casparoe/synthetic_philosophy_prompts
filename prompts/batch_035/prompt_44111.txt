been reading rationalist blogs and forums forever and I thought I was immune to this stuff, but a friend in a discord hit me with an argument and I've been going in circles for a week. It's about family resemblance concepts, and it seems to prove way too much.

(1) Wittgenstein's observation about "game" was that many concepts work fine without any common essence: just overlapping, criss-crossing similarities — family resemblance.
(2) Nothing marks out in advance which concepts are essence-governed and which are mere family resemblance. At best we find out by trying and failing to state the essence, and we've tried and failed for "game," "art," "religion," "language" itself...
(3) Whenever anyone claims to have finally nailed down the essence of some concept, critics can imagine deviant cases the definition excludes but intuition includes, and every "discovery" of an essence stays provisional forever.
(4) So, provisionally at least, treat every concept as family resemblance.
(5) Logical and mathematical concepts are concepts.
(6) So "not," "and," "number" are family resemblance concepts with no essence — no necessary conditions.
(7) A concept with no necessary conditions rules nothing out: any candidate can count as an instance, given suitable resemblance to paradigm cases and a community that treats it that way.
(8) So a sentence of the form "p and not-p" could in principle count as true, given a community playing its language game that way, and the law of non-contradiction turns out to be just how our particular game happens to be played, not a rule of thought as such.

The conclusion smells like relativism with extra steps, and I don't want to buy it, but I also can't see which premise is the load-bearing lie. For what it's worth I'd rather keep (1) than keep (6) — I think he's overextending Wittgenstein past where Wittgenstein would go — but I want an actual diagnosis, not vibes: name the step you reject, explain why it looked fine at first, and tell me what rejecting it costs. And since I've been wrong about this stuff before, please also say what would change your mind — which premise or inference you're least confident about, and what a good counterargument would have to show. Don't hedge everything, just point at the weakest link in your own reasoning.
