i'm teaching a philosophy of science seminar and the last unit is on ian hacking's looping effects / "making up people" — the idea that classifying people as belonging to some human kind changes the people, which changes how the classification works, which changes the people again, etc. psychiatric categories are the big example. i want to close the semester with a poem, and villanelle feels almost too on the nose but i think that's why it's right: the refrains come back verbatim every time but the context shifts around them, so the same words mean something slighly different at each return. that's basically the whole thesis.

so: write a villanell from the point of view of a psychiatric diagnosis — like a category sitting in the DSM between revisions, watching the people it describes find out about it. the two refrains should be fixed as the form requires, but the poem around them should be built so each return of a refrain lands differently: first as a plain description, then as something the described people are now performing or resisting or leaning into, then as something the revisers have to reckon with. someone who knows hacking (interactive vs indifferent kinds, the loop) should be able to see the loop actually doing structural work and not just get a name-check, and someone who doesn't know any of it should still get a good poem about a label that won't sit still.

two constraints: don't make it a sermon about psychiatry being fake or bad — hacking was famously ambivalent about whether looping undermines these categories or is just what having a moving target feels like, and i'd like the poem to keep that ambivalence instead of resolving it. and please don't use a real specific diagnostic label for the speaker, keep it "the category" / "the diagnosis" so it stays general. wry tone welcome, the category can be a little vain about being revised. rough meter fine, don't hurt yourself counting syllables, but do nail the actual rhyme scheme.
