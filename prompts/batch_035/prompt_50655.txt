So I'm a science journalist, I mostly write about behavioral research and ideas, and my editor Dana has this thing. Everytime we fight about a headline or a lede, the fight is always about one word: "intentionally." Whether we get to say some exec "intentionally" poisoned the aquifer, or has to settle for "knowingly," or "just caused it." Six years of pitch meetings and Dana has never once contradicted herself as far as I can tell. I'm working on a feature about how ordinary people make these calls (the experimental philosophy crowd has been on this forever -- that famous chairman-of-the-board scenario where everybody says the harm was intentional but the help wasn't), and Dana's gut is basically my case study. Here's her track record:

- Chairman is told a new program will bump profits but also harm the environment; says he doesn't care at all about the environment; harm happens. Dana: intentional.
- Same setup but the program helps the environment, same "don't care" attitude. Dana: did NOT intentionally help.
- CEO who foresaw the harm, hated it, spent real money on safeguards, safeguards failed anyway. Dana: did not intentionally harm. (That one suprised me.)
- Data breach case: sloppy but legal security practices, nobody upstairs ever once thought about the risk. Dana: didn't intentionally expose anyone's data.
- The company that ran an actual spreadsheet putting a dollar value on the expected injuries, decided it was cheaper than a recall, went ahead. Dana: intentionally harmed, full stop.

Now here's the story I want to pitch, two versions of it actually:

1. A factory upgrades its equipment purely to cut costs. The CFO's own spreadsheet shows the upgrade will also clean up the river they dump into. The CEO's line in the deposition is basically I don't care about the river, I just want the savings. Upgrade happens, river gets clean. Did he intentionally help the environment?
2. Same company, different twist: the river does get cleaned up, but only because of a fluke -- the cheap supplier they'd planned on went under mid-project and the replacement turned out way cleaner than anyone had calculated. Same CEO, same don't-care attitude, same happy river.

I want to walk into the meeting knowing what Dana will say about each before she says it. So: what rule is she actually running? I can see at least two or three that fit everything she's ever ruled, and they come apart on my two stories. Tell me what each rule predicts, give me your best guess at her verdicts and why, and design me one further hypothetical case I could feed her at some future pitch meeting that would actually settle which rule it is.

Format, if you don't mind:

- "The rules she might be running" -- name the candidate rules that fit her whole track record, roughly 200-250 words
- "What each says about my two stories" -- predictions for both cases from each rule, ~200 words
- "My call" -- your best guess at Dana's verdicts, with your reasoning, ~150 words
- "The case that would settle it" -- one further hypothetical that splits the remaining candidates, ~100 words
