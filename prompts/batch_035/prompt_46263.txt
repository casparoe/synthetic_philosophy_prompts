I've never taken a philosophy class, so bear with me. For about two years now I've been turning over this question of whether you're obligated to vote for the lesser evil, and I've finally got my position worked out, and I've been invited to present it at our monthly discussion group, which is scarier than it sounds because these people argue for fun.

Here's my view in short. The usual trap is: either your vote is a tiny drop in the ocean, in which case it doesn't matter who you pick, or voting is about expressing who you are, in which case voting for someone you despise just to block someone worse is a kind of lie. I think both framings are wrong, and here's my alternative: when you vote you shouldn't ask "what does my one vote cause?" but "what would be right for everyone in my situation to do?" And there's a real difference between those questions, because with millions of people in your situation, the rule "vote for the worst option on the ballot that can actually win, and stay home if you can't stomach that" produces a different world than the rule "vote your heart or stay home." So even though my individual ballot changes almost nothing, I'm still doing the right or wrong thing, the same way (I think) you can be one of a hundred litterers and still each of you litters. From this I conclude that voting lesser evil is usually required, not just permitted, unless the two evils really are close enough that the difference washes out.

The group, so you know who's coming at me. It's about fifteen people at someone's house, mostly professionals, no academics, but they've all done their reading and they love numbers.
