I'm a PhD student in moral philosophy preparing to defend a dissertation chapter at an interdisciplinary workshop on moral change, and I need help anticipating the Q&A. My thesis is a middle position in the debate over the expanding circle. Against the strong intuitionist line that moral reasoning is mere post-hoc rationalization, and against the materialist line that circle expansion is really driven by economics and technology, I defend what I call the ferry model: moral argument is causally indispensable to expanding the circle, but only where the audience already possesses affective bridges — kinship, cuteness, cooperative dependence, shared worship — across which argument can carry concern. Argument extends traffic over pre-existing bridges; it cannot build them. On this model, Singer was right that argument matters but wrong about why expansions happened where and when they did: identical argumentative forms have produced wildly different uptake depending on whether a bridge was available, which is why I take the uneven history of reform movements to be evidence for the model rather than an embarrassment for it.

The audience is genuinely hostile terrain. Four of the attendees are moral psychologists of a strongly intuitionist bent who treat explicit moral reasoning as a press secretary for conclusions already reached. Two are cultural evolution researchers who hold that market integration and widening social networks, not argument, explain the historical broadening of fairness and harm norms. One is a historian who regards expanding-circle narratives as Whiggish and insists that every celebrated expansion coincided with fresh exclusions elsewhere. Nobody in the room will grant at the outset that arguments cause anything.

Draft my Q&A prep sheet: the seven or eight objections I will actually face from this audience, phrased the way these people would phrase them, ordered by how much damage each could do to the chapter. For each objection, give me a reply I can deliver in about two minutes, plus a candid note on where that reply is soft. Include the one objection I am quietly hoping nobody raises, and tell me what to say if it comes anyway. Please keep the entire prep sheet under 900 words.
