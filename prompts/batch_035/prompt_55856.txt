Here is a thought experiment my friend and I have been unable to settle for months, and I'd like a diagnosis of why we're stuck rather than a verdict for either side. Picture a vast sealed hall — we've been calling it the careenium — with a frictionless floor and billions of perfectly elastic balls whose initial positions and velocities have been set up just right, along with a layout of walls and pegs, so that the collisions among them implement a computer; billiard balls doing the work of logic gates. Programmed into all of this, via the initial conditions, is a simulation of a small billiard table with a handful of balls on it — the simmballs — obeying their own simulated physics. Zoom in on the moment a simball strikes the simulated cushion and, at the same time, the real pattern of ball-motion that encodes it changes course. My friend says that of course the simmball didn't push the real balls anywhere: every real collision is fully determined by the previous positions and velocities plus Newton's laws, and the simmballs are a bookkeeping device, a shorthand we impose on the motion, so to say they move anything is to count the causation twice at two levels. I say that when the pattern is what we track, predict, and build on — the way a checkmate on a chessboard causes a resignation even though "really" it's just shaped wood — the symbol level is doing genuine causal work, and the fact that there's also a complete microscopic story doesn't subtract from that; when we explain an ordinary computer, we cite the program, not the electron trajectories, and nobody calls that double counting.

Neither of us has moved an inch, and I want to understand what kind of disagreement this actually is. My working hypothesis is that it's verbal: that he is using "cause" to mean only the fundamental physical story while I mean something like whatever you'd have to interfere with to change what happens next, and we keep trading examples that are each compelling under our own reading. But it doesn't feel merely verbal, and I suspect the real stakes are elsewhere: if he's right that descriptions never push anything around, then a mind that exists only at the level of description — an uploaded consciousness, say — seems to become a passenger, something the substrate merely rides along with; if I'm right, the symbols push the substrate around in the only sense that ever mattered, and the worry evaporates. I tried to force the issue by asking whether one could intervene on a simball without touching the real balls — if that question is incoherent, doesn't that settle it his way? — but he says the question is malformed and I'm honestly not sure he's wrong. I've also since run across the idea that if the physical level fully determines everything, there's no causal work left over for higher levels to do, which seems to be his whole position dressed up, and I don't know whether there's a standard reply or whether the two camps just talk past each other on that too.

What would real progress look like — a sharper definition of cause, a fixed thought experiment, some way of making the intervention question coherent — and is anything actually riding on the answer for the uploaded-mind question, or have we smuggled that in?
