I'm grading a stack of short essays for my american philosophy course and this one is bugging me. The question I gave them was:

"Peirce and James are both pragmatists about truth, but they mean quite different things by it. Explain the core difference between Peirce's view (truth as tied to the long run of inquiry) and James's view (truth as tied to what works in belief), making sure to say what each thinker thinks makes a belief true NOW, not just eventually. Then give one standard objection to James's view, and explain whether Peirce's view faces the same objection or not, and why."

Here's the student's answer:

"Peirce and James agree that truth isn't a correspondence between beliefs and a mind-independent world, but a matter of practice. For Peirce, a belief is true if it's the one that the community of inquirers would eventually settle on after sufficient investigation. So truth is a long-run property: a belief isn't true yet, strictly speaking, it becomes true when inquiry converges on it. Peirce cared about the community because he thought no individual can fully check their own beliefs, so the social process of inquiry is what does the work.

James shifts this from the community to the individual. For him a belief is true when it works, meaning it's useful, it meshes with our other beliefs, and it leads us successfully through experience. So James thinks a belief is already true the moment it does its job for a particular person, even if inquiry hasn't run its course. This is the key difference: Peirce's truth is what works in the long run for everyone, James's is what works in the short run for you.

The classic objection to James is the wishful thinking problem: if believing something pleasant makes my life go better, it seems it counts as true even though it's false. This is supposed to be a reductio of his view. Peirce's view avoids this because if a belief is the one inquiry would converge on, it can't be mere wishful thinking, since investigation would eventually expose self-deception. And anyway, for Peirce the useful belief IS the true one, since a belief that all inquiry converges on is by definition the one that works best for the community. So the objection only really lands on James.

Overall Peirce's view is basically James's view with a longer timescale and a bigger population, which is why Peirce got annoyed when people read them as the same."
