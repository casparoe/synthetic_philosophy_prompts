ok so i'm a software engineer, zero formal philosophy training, and a while back I went down a 2am wiki rabbit hole about dispositions and it turns out there's this whole literature about fragile vases that don't break and poisonous things that don't poison. My rough understanding: the naive view says a disposition is just a conditional — if the thing got triggered, it would do its thing. Fragile means "would break if struck". Then people cooked up counterexamples. Finks: something intervenes at the exact moment of triggering and removes or swaps the disposition (the classic one is a vase wired up so that at the instant it's struck, electronics make it sturdy — it IS genuinely fragile the whole time before that, but it doesn't break). Masks: something blocks the path from trigger to manifestation WITHOUT touching the disposition itself, like packing foam around the vase, or a drug in your bloodstream blocking the poison's disposition to poison you (one paper apparently calls those "antidotes", which, fair). There's also a reverse thing, a mimic, where the manifestation happens with no disposition there at all. And apparently finks only killed version 1 of the conditional analysis (the simple one), while the patched version handles finks but masks are still an open problem. That distinction — fink changes the disposition when the trigger arrives, mask leaves the disposition alone and just blocks the causal chain — is the part I actually want to get right, because I've seen people on forums mix them up constantly.
