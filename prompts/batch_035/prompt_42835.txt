I'm a lower-sixth student taking psychology, and we've just finished a module on the psychology of political attitudes. Our teacher handed round a summary of moral foundations theory that he'd written himself, and something about it felt off to me, though I can't pin down why. It read, roughly, as follows: 'Haidt and his colleagues identified five moral foundations — care, fairness, loyalty, authority and sanctity. Using questionnaires, they proved that these foundations are innate: everyone is born with all five, hardwired into the brain in fixed proportions. When they compared liberals and conservatives, they found that conservatives draw on all five foundations equally, while liberals switch off three of them and use only care and fairness. Haidt's further claim is that moral judgements are gut reactions and that conscious moral reasoning is merely a press secretary that rationalises what the gut has already decided, so moral argument never changes anyone's mind. Taken together, this shows that conservatives possess a fuller and more balanced moral sense than liberals do, and Haidt himself concluded that the left is psychologically impoverished. He later added a sixth foundation, liberty, after conservatives complained that he'd left it out.'

My own politics are fairly left-wing, so I'm prepared to believe I'm one of the people with an impoverished moral sense, but I'd like to know whether the psychology actually says that before I accept it. Could you go through the summary claim by claim and tell me which parts a defender of the theory would accept, which parts are overstated or subtly wrong, and which are outright misrepresentations? I'd particularly like to know whether the conclusion about left-wing psychology follows from the accurate parts of the summary, or whether my teacher has run two very different kinds of claim together. And if the conclusion doesn't follow even from the corrected version, I'd like to understand what the theory is meant to show about left and right instead — what it could legitimately tell us, if anything, about whose moral views are the better ones.
