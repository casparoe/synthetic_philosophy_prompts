so I'm a PhD student in comparative lit, and my dissertation has a creative component — basically I'm writing a novel alongside the critical part. Here's what's been bugging me lately. When I write the chapters where my villain does his thing, I get genuinely uneasy. Checking the locks at night, sleeping badly, the whole deal. And a couple weeks ago I wrote the scene where a character I love dies, and I sobbed for like an hour. This is a person I made up. I invented him out of thin air, I know exactly what's coming, I literally control everything that happens to him — nobody on earth has better grounds for knowing he isn't real than I do.

My partner thinks it's sweet but says the fear can't be real fear, because I know there's no one there — at most it's some kind of "as if" emotion, whatever that means. A friend in the psych department says emotions need a real object, so if anything I'm feeling something about my own thoughts, not about him. And honestly, when I'm in the middle of writing, it does not feel like fear of my own mind — it feels like fear of *him*. So who's closer? Is my partner right that there's something watered down about it, is the psychologist right that I've got the object wrong, or is something going on that neither of them has quite captured?

Also, does it matter that I'm the author? My readers presumably feel the same stuff with even less control over the story than I have, so maybe my case is just their case plus a weird extra wrinkle — or maybe the wrinkle changes everything, since I'm the one who keeps him existing at all, and can stop. (I could also, in principle, just... not write the scary chapters, and the dread would go away, which feels different from a reader who can't change anything.)

I ask partly for me and partly for the dissertation: I have to write an essay section on whether literary emotional response is ever rational, and I keep going in circles between "the thoughts are real so the emotion is real" and "the object is fake so the emotion is fake." Any way to straighten this out would definately help with both.
