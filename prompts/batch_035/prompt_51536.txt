Write a joke about the Born rule problem in the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics. For context, the problem is that in the many-worlds view every outcome of a measurement occurs in some branch, so it is puzzling what it could even mean to say that one outcome is more probable than another. A naive response is to count branches, but the number of branches is ill-defined — branching is a continuous process, and how one slices a branching event into distinct worlds is largely arbitrary — while the squared-amplitude measure that recovers the Born probabilities does not, on its face, look like a probability at all, since nothing is uncertain and nothing fails to happen. The joke's punchline should turn on this actual content: for instance, on the contrast between counting branches and weighting them by amplitude, on the arbitrariness of branch individuation, or on the oddity of assigning probabilities to outcomes that all occur. It should not be a generic quantum-mechanics joke (e.g., something about Schrödinger's cat or being in two places at once). Someone familiar with the debate over whether probability is even coherent in a deterministic theory where every outcome is realized should find the joke genuinely funny, in the sense that it could not simply be re-purposed as a joke about classical physics or about quantum mechanics in the Copenhagen interpretation.
