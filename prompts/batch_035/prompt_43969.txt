Write me a modest proposal, plz. Some background on me so you get the vibe: I've been hanging around the rationalist blogosphere forever, LessWrong then SSC then the diaspora, and at this point I've read approximately nine thousand posts about Moloch and coordination failure. The one topic where I've never seen a genuinely funny take is campaign finance — everyone either does tired late-night-monologue jokes about politicians being bought, or earnest 4000-word essays about Gilens-style findings. I want the third thing.

Here's the assignment. You are a satirist working in the Swift tradition, and you're writing as a respectable op-ed columnist for a business paper (think Wall Street Journal editorial page energy). Your proposal: since money will always find its way into politics, the truly honest reform is to stop the pretense and formalize it — replace one-person-one-vote with one-dollar-one-vote. Work in some of the machinery that follows naturally from that: dual-class voting shares so incumbents keep supervoting stock (very tech-startup), a liquid secondary market in votes, insider-trading rules for legislators, maybe quarterly earnings calls for congressional committees. But the actual machinery is up to you — the constraint that matters is that this reads as a reductio, not a strawman.

The key requirement: every single step of the argument should be continuous with something real people say in actual op-eds — "money is speech," donors only buy "access," disclosure is the real cure, regulation just drives influence underground so we might as well embrace it, markets are more democratic than elections because you vote every day with your wallet, etc. The reader should genuinely not be able to point to the exact sentence where the real position ends and the reductio begins. A policy nerd should finish it thinking "fair" even while wincing. Deadpan throughout, no winking, no ironic exclamation points.

Format: brainstorm first — before drafting, list five or six real tropes from pro-money-in-politics discourse and note which ones you're going to build on and why (keep this part brief, it's for you). Then give me exactly two sections:

1. "The column" — ~600 words, written as a serious op-ed with a headline and numbered policy recommendations.
2. "Where it tips over" — ~150 words, stepping out of character to say which numbered steps are things people actually argue and at which point the logic goes off the cliff, so I can check the reductio is fair.

Think carefully about the craft here — the difference between this working and being a cheap strawman is whether the intermediate steps are ones a real pundit would endorse. Also please don't name any living pundits or politicians; invent a think tank to attach the proposal to if you need one.
