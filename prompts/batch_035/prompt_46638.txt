So my podcast guest next week is this guy who's been defending lexical threshold negative utilitarianism for a while now (it comes up in EA-adjacent circles). Quick rundown of his version, so the questions land: he holds that suffering is bad in a way pleasure isn't good, but not the crude "one pinprick outweighs paradise" thing. Instead there's a threshold of suffering intensity — call it T — such that any pain at or above T is lexically worse than any amount of pleasure, so no quantity of happiness can compensate for a single instance of above-T pain. Below the threshold, though, it's commensurable: enough happy experiences can outweigh a whole lot of mild aches and annoyances.

He's really slick at interviews and tends to escape every objection by saying the threshold does all the work and nobody takes the crude view seriously anyway. I want questions that corner him on the stuff he usually waves off. Specifically I want at least one question each on:

1. Where the threshold actually sits — is it sharp, and if it's vague (like, is a migraine above T?), doesn't that reintroduce sorites-style problems into a theory that was supposed to be cleaner than classical utilitarianism?
2. Aggregation across persons — if ten people each have pains just barely below T, is that better or worse than one person at T? If sub-threshold pains can sum to something a single above-T pain is worse than, doesn't the lexical ordering quietly collapse back into cardinal comparisons?
3. The creation/destruction asymmetry that falls out of the view — if one instance of above-T suffering is worse than any amount of happiness, then a world with a single instance of it and infinite bliss is worse than an empty world, and that seems like a bullet even he shouldn't bite.
4. Why commensurability breaks exactly at T and nowhere else — like, what makes the very last unit of compensable pain special compared to the first unit of lexical pain, given that they could be phenomenally indistinguishable?

For each of these, include a follow-up keyed to the reply he's most likely to give (he loves the move where he says empirical hedonics will eventually calibrate the threshold, so if he reaches for that, I need the follow-up to push on whether an empirical calibration can settle what looks like a normative question).

Aim for maybe 9-11 questions total, and make a couple of them scenario-based so he can't just retreat into abstract formulation talk. Also happy if a couple of your questions connect his view to why people find mere-addition-type problems hard — he claims LTNU sidesteps repugnant conclusions and I've never understood how.
