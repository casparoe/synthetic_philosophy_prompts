Longtime lurker here — been reading the rationalist sphere since the old Overcoming Bias days, these days mostly ACX open threads — and I've somehow talked myself into giving a talk at the big city meetup near me (~45 people, one evening slot). Here's the position I'm defending, which I know is going to be controversial in that room: the author of a nonfiction book can be fully rational in believing each individual claim in the book, while also believing, at the same time, that at least one of the claims in it is false. The preface writer isn't confused or guilty of some epistemic sin; rather, this is a case that shows categorical belief is its own thing and doesn't reduce to a threshold on credences (the so-called Lockean thesis), because the writer has per-claim, independent evidence for each statement, whereas in the lottery case, where the evidence for each "this ticket will lose" is purely statistical and interchangeable, I claim you should NOT believe of any particular ticket that it will lose. So the two paradoxes come apart, and binary belief comes out looking non-redundant — it does work credences can't, like licensing flat-out assertion when you can't afford to compute.

The audience, though, is about the least receptive crowd imaginable for this. It's the local meetup, and the culture there is very hard-Bayesian: mostly software engineers and stats grad students, plus a couple of ex-philosophy PhD students who actually read the epistemology literature. Their default take is roughly "there are only credences; 'belief' is just a coarse summary you use when compute is limited, and any 'paradox' about belief dissolves once you stop pretending it's a real state." The last person who tried to defend binary belief there got taken apart in the Q&A, and I watched it happen. They're not hostile, exactly — they love a good argument — but they will pattern-match my thesis to "guy refuses to give up folk epistemology," and once that label sticks I'm done.

So I want a prep sheet for the Q&A. Give me the seven or eight objections I'll actually get from this crowd, phrased the way they would phrase them (expect jargon: credences, log-odds, map-vs-territory, belief-as-compression, "just put a number on it"), ordered by how likely they are and how much damage they'd do, each with a two-minute reply I could actually deliver out loud, and an honest note on where each reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And please don't give me a menu of equally-weighted "well, it depends on your theory of belief" hedging — commit: tell me which replies genuinely work, which ones I should concede on the spot, and whether any single objection is strong enough that I should reframe the thesis before the talk rather than defend it as stated. If you think the lottery half of my line is the weak half, say that directly.
