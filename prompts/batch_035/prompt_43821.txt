hey, so i'm a novelist stuck on a plotting problem that turned into a philosophy problem, and maybe you can settle it for me.

my current book has this scene: a sniper is about to shoot a witness, but right before she pulls the trigger, a truck backfires, the witness ducks in reflex, and the bullet misses. meanwhile (unbeknownst to everyone) a second assassin on a nearby roof was tracking the same witness and would have fired two seconds later if the first shot hadn't happened — but the first shot DID happen, the witness ducked, and the second assassin lost his window. the witness survives because of the backfire, kind of.

i ended up down a rabbit hole on theories of causation trying to figure out what actually "caused" the survival, and now i genuinely want an answer, not just plotting advice. on one hand there's the old counterfactual line going back to hume, where causation is about what would have happened instead: no backfire, witness dies, so the backfire caused the survival. on the other hand there's the interventionist picture (woodward-style, the "wiggle the variable and see what follows" approach) where you ask what happens if you surgically intervene and force a variable to a value, rather than just imagining closest worlds.

so here's what i want from you: please make an argument that the interventionist approach handles my little sniper scenario better than the pure counterfactual approach does. focus on the messy parts — the backup assassin feels like classic preemption/overdetermination trouble, and the backfire itself seems like it causes the survival only through the duck, not through the bullet's path. if you think interventionism actually stumbles here somewhere, say so, but i want the case built FOR it, steelmanned, not a fence-sitting "both have merits" essay. bonus points if you can tell me how the argument would change if the witness never noticed the backfire at all and just flinched for unrelated reasons.

keep it conversational, i read philosophy for fun but i'm no grad student. and at the very end, give me a machine-readable one-liner, like \answer{interventionism wins, with caveat: ...} — a short verdict i can grep out of your reply later.
