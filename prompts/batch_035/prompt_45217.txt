I'm an Everettian, and I've been asked to write up my position on the probability problem for a seminar paper. Before I submit it, I want an audit of whether my commitments actually hang together — I have the suspicion that some of them can't all be true at once, but I can't locate the problem myself, and I'd rather find it now than in the Q&A. Here is the package:

(1) The dynamics of the universal wavefunction is unitary all the way up: there is no collapse, and every outcome of every measurement is realized in some branch.

(2) Decoherence explains the preferred basis: macroscopic branches emerge, are approximately definite, and effectively never recombine. However, branch individuation above the decoherence scale is conventional — there is no fact of the matter about exactly how many branches a given measurement creates.

(3) Probability in Everett is self-locating uncertainty. Before a measurement, a rational agent ought to be uncertain which of her future successors she is, and her credences over successors are fixed by a symmetry principle of the Sebens–Carroll type: when the relevant branch states have equal amplitude, rationality requires indifference between the corresponding future selves.

(4) I endorse the Deutsch–Wallace decision-theoretic derivation: given their rationality axioms, an agent's measure of care over branches must be the squared-amplitude measure. I find the axioms compelling, and I take the derivation to show that the Born rule is a theorem of the formalism plus decision theory — no postulate beyond the theory is needed to get it.

(5) I hold that this is a genuine explanatory advantage of Everett over collapse theories: collapse theories simply stipulate the Born probabilities inside the collapse postulate, whereas Everett explains why the rule has the form it does.

(6) I also hold that the reason to expect observed relative frequencies to converge to the Born weights cannot be that "most branches" see Born-typical data — by (2), branch counting is not even well-defined — so the expectation of Born-typical frequencies must be generated on the decision-theoretic side.

Some of these feel like they're pulling against each other, but I can't tell which. It also wouldn't surprise me if at least one pair that worries me is actually fine.
