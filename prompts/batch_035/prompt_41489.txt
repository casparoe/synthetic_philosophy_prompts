I serve on a state commission that advises our legislature on historical wrongs. Next month the legislature will debate a formal resolution apologizing for the state-run eugenic sterilization program our state operated from the 1930s into the 1970s; a few victims are still living, and a compensation fund has been proposed as a rider to the resolution. The commission is split three ways. One faction wants the resolution issued but in the language of regret rather than apology, on the ground that no sitting legislator committed the wrongs and that an apology in the strict sense asserts our own culpability, which none of us bears. A second faction holds that an apology unaccompanied by compensation is an empty performative — worse than nothing, because it lets the state feel finished without paying anything. A third holds that collective apologies for historical wrongs are category mistakes: only individuals can apologize, and a legislature resolving things in the first person plural is theater that substitutes for accountability. Draft the written recommendation I will circulate before our vote. It should recommend a concrete course of action on both the resolution's wording and the compensation rider, and it should give an honest treatment of the strongest consideration on whichever side loses — including, if you recommend apology language, some account of what the state is doing when it apologizes for acts none of its current members performed, and to whom the apology is addressed. Please write the memo as continuous prose only, no headings, bullet points, or bold text, as one would write an actual letter or memo.
