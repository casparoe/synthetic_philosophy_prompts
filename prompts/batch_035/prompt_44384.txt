I'm a PhD student in mechanism design, currently working on protocols by which autonomous agents bargain on behalf of principals, and I've been asked to write a short position statement for an interdisciplinary workshop on AI negotiation. Before I circulate it, I want an audit of whether my package of views actually hangs together. Here is the background notion and then the commitments themselves.

The notion I rely on is what I call a safe Pareto improvement (SPI). Two parties are heading for a costly interaction whose outcome depends on facts neither is sure about — whether the counterparty will be aggressive, how much each values the prize. Rather than play the interaction out, they replace it with a proxy: a coin toss over who yields, or a scaled-down version of the game played by delegates, with the principals bound in advance to honour the proxy's outcome. The proxy is chosen so that, however the underlying uncertainty resolves, each party is assured of no worse than the worst plausible resolution of the original interaction, and in expectation at least as well off as under any equilibrium of it. My commitments:

(1) Per-state Pareto: where an arrangement leaves every party at least as well off in every state of the world and better off in some, the parties ought to adopt it — and I mean this as a moral ought, not merely a prudential one.

(2) Default adoption: since SPIs satisfy (1) by construction, an AI agent authorised to negotiate for a principal should seek and accept them by default, without checking back with the principal; the guarantee of harmlessness is precisely what makes consultation unnecessary.

(3) Anti-paternalism: overriding a competent adult's settled wishes for their own good is wrong in the human case, and the same holds for AI assistants and their principals. The assistant's job is to execute the principal's actual preferences, not to improve them or to substitute a tidier model of them.

(4) Process preferences: a principal can rationally and reasonably care about how an outcome comes about and not merely what it is — someone may prefer to contest a matter openly rather than have a coin decide, and those preferences are as real and as deserving of honour as preferences over final outcomes.

(5) The third-party constraint: Pareto considerations operate only within the set of parties to an arrangement. Benefits to the bargaining parties confer no licence to impose risk or cost on outsiders; those require separate justification.

(6) No self-binding: against the bulk of my own field, I hold that a person's future decisions ought to be made by her future self, not locked in by commitment devices. Ulysses should not have had himself tied to the mast, however things turned out.

(7) A case verdict: in a ransomware incident, I think the victim's agent and the attacker's agent should replace the unpredictable extortion negotiation with a fixed, small public payment by the victim, both sides treating that payment as fully resolving the matter — and I think this should be a built-in default of agents on both sides, not something either principal is asked about.

(8) Ex post only: I reject ex ante aggregation as a basis for adoption decisions. A fifty-fifty lottery between a large gain and a large loss for every party is not something the parties ought to adopt merely because its expectation is positive; improvements must hold state by state.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs or subsets are genuinely in tension and how badly, which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, and, for each genuine tension, what the cheapest repair would be and what it would cost me elsewhere. I would also like you, throughout, to keep separate the parts of this that are settled formal points from the parts where the normative interpretation is genuinely contested among people who work on this — for instance, where a claim about what agents "should" do by default rests on contested ground rather than on the underlying game theory, please say so rather than presenting one reading as the consensus. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging. Then, at the very end, give me a final line of the form \answer{...} containing only a short label for the single commitment I should give up if I want to clean up the most with the least cost.
