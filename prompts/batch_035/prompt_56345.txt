I host a small podcast sitting somewhere between political philosophy and current affairs, and next week's guest is a philosopher whos just written a book defending a duty to vote. Her view, roughly, is that voting is a genuine moral duty (not just a nice thing to do) grounded in collective beneficence: each vote is a tiny, nearly costless contribution to an enormous public good — she likes the image of being one pair of hands among many lifting a heavy table — so you can't escape the duty by pointing out that your individual causal impact is basically nil. Please draft me ten interview questions.

I want the sequence to get progressively harder, and I need at least one question each on the following:

- the "why voting and not everything else" problem — if near-zero-probability benefits can ground duties, it looks like theres a duty to do a thousand other cheap things too, and I want a follow-up keyed to whatever she's most likely to say about the cost structure or the coordination being special;
- whether the duty survives in a safe seat, where her own grounding story implies the causal contribution is vanishing — and whether the answer commits her to the duty being stronger in swing districts, which seems backwards for a grounding that was supposed to be non-causal;
- the lesser evil: does the duty to vote come packaged with a duty to vote for the lesser evil, what happens when both candidates are bad enough that spoiling the ballot seems like the honest option, and whether a spoiled ballot discharges the duty or dodges it;
- the expression angle — she's openly sceptical of purely expressive theories of voting, so I want a question pressing her to say what the duty is actually a duty *to do*, given that on her own account the point isn't to express anything;
- compulsory voting — if it's a duty, why isn't enforcing it with fines fine, and whether her answer survives applying the same logic to other low-cost duties she probably doesn't want the police involved in.

Also throw in one question on whether there's a duty to vote *well* — i.e. whether showing up ignorant, or on a whim, discharges the duty or quietly violates it.

For each question, add a one-line note on the follow-up I should have ready depending on how she's most likely to answer. Write the whole thing in British English please (so 'sceptical', not 'skeptical', etc.).
