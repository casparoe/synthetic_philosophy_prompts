My game theory course has just covered iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies, including the subtlety that a pure strategy can be strictly dominated only by a mixture of the player's other pure strategies. In lecture we established two facts for the two-player games we've been studying: first, that eliminating a strictly dominated strategy never removes any strategy that is a best response to some belief about the opponent's play; second, that a pure strategy is strictly dominated by some mixture exactly when it fails to be a best response to any beliefs at all. It seems to me these two facts license the following argument, and I'd like to know where it breaks.

Iterating the elimination strips away every strategy that could never be rationalized, so what survives should be exactly the strategies that are optimal under some consistent hierarchy of beliefs. Now suppose a game has a unique Nash equilibrium. In equilibrium, each player's strategy is optimal given correct beliefs about the opponent. Since survival only requires optimality under some beliefs, and equilibrium beliefs are surely among the "consistent" ones, it seems the hierarchy of beliefs compatible with survival should collapse onto the equilibrium beliefs. That would mean the procedure must terminate with exactly the equilibrium profile — one strategy per player, whether pure or a degenerate mixture. As a check on the reasoning, this predicts that iterated elimination eventually reduces Matching Pennies, which has a unique equilibrium in which each player randomizes evenly, to a single strategy per player.

My TA insists that the procedure leaves Matching Pennies completely untouched, with both pure strategies surviving for both players, and that some step in my chain is faulty, but when I pressed her she couldn't isolate which one. Could you go through the argument step by step, identify precisely where it goes wrong, and say whether it can be repaired with a small patch or whether the whole line of reasoning rests on a mistaken picture of what iterated elimination is supposed to achieve?
