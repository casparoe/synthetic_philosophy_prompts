I teach philosophy of science, and at a college dinner the other week a physicist colleague gave me his off-the-cuff take on the metaphysics of laws, roughly as follows: "The Humean says laws are just economical summaries of the regularities — nothing in nature makes the laws hold, there are just patterns, and the laws are our compression of them. Since they're just descriptions, they can't explain anything, and they certainly don't constrain what happens: a world just like ours except that the regularities fail tomorrow is no violation of anything, just a different summary. The anti-Humean instead says there are necessary connections in nature — the laws compel, so given the laws and the state of things, events had to happen. This goes all the way back to Hume, who pointed out we never observe necessity, only constant conjunction, and concluded that causation is a habit of expectation. So the real question is whether science discovers necessity, and since science only ever finds regularities, the anti-Humean is betting on hidden powers that are in principle untestable."

Something about this strikes me as a mixture of the fair and the sly, but I'd rather have a careful audit than just my dinner-table grumbling. Please go through it piece by piece: which claims would a serious Humean accept, which are distortions, and where exactly does it slide from defensible simplification into something nobody in the literature actually holds? I'd particularly like your judgement on (a) whether "laws can't explain" actually follows from "laws don't govern" — I have a suspicion the best-system people think unification is explanatory, but I've never worked out the details; (b) whether "given the laws, events had to happen" is really what anti-Humeans are commited to, or whether that conflates them with full-blown necessitarians; and (c) whether the closing claim about untestable hidden powers is a live objection that gets a serious reply, or just a rhetorical flourish dressed up as a refutation. I'm inclined to think the ending is over the top, but I can't quite put my finger on where the summary stops being a simplification and starts being a straw man.
