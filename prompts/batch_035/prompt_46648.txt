I am preparing a lecture on theories of consciousness for a survey course in philosophy of mind, and a graduate student in psychology passed along a popular article she found helpful. I have my doubts, but the errors, if any, are not the crude kind, and I would like a second opinion before I correct the record for my students. The article summarizes Dennett's multiple drafts model roughly as follows:

'Dennett's target in Consciousness Explained is the "Cartesian theater": Descartes' old idea that all the senses deliver their inputs to a single inner spectator who watches the show — a view which, the article notes, no serious philosopher holds anymore, since everyone now accepts that the mind is just the brain. Dennett's alternative is that the brain produces many parallel drafts of what is happening, each of them briefly conscious in its own right, until one draft wins out and the losers are discarded. His showcase is the color phi phenomenon, where a red spot and a green spot flashed in alternation are experienced as a single moving spot that changes color midway. Since the brain cannot know in advance that the second spot will appear, Dennett argues the brain must rewrite its record after the fact, Orwell-style, so that our experience of the present is a fiction assembled a moment too late. The upshot is that inner experience, as we thought we knew it, does not exist.'

Please go through this claim by claim. Which parts would Dennett himself accept as a fair statement of his position? Which are simplifications he might tolerate in a popular treatment? And which are genuine distortions — places where the article has conflated two things he keeps apart or credited him with a conclusion he explicitly refuses? I have particular questions about three passages. First, the framing of the Cartesian theater: I take Dennett's point to be that the theater survives even among people who consider themselves good materialists — that his quarrel is with a picture, not with a historical doctrine — so the "strawman, no one holds this" line worries me. Second, the description of the drafts: my understanding is that on Dennett's view it is precisely not the case that many little experiences run in parallel awaiting selection, and that the selection language belongs to his critics, not to him. Third, the phi passage: as I recall the Orwellian-versus-Stalinesque discussion, Dennett's claim is not that the rewrite happens after the fact, but that there is no fact of the matter about when it happens — which is a different and stranger thesis, and one the article quietly converts into a much more ordinary one.

Finally, does the article's closing sentence follow from any of the accurate material that precedes it, or only from the distortions? And if you were revising this summary into something I could responsibly put on a handout, what would it say instead?
