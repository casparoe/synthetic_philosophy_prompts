here's my rough package on moral worth, I'm supposed to bring my "philosophical profile" to a discussion group so the others can poke at it, and I want a consistency check before I embarrass myself. where I've landed:

1. moral worth is in the will, not the world. if you hear the dam is cracking and run to warn the village but the bridge is out and the warning never arrives, you have exactly as much moral worth as the person whose warning got through and saved everyone.

2. the person who helps out of spontaneous warmth, who never once thinks the word duty, is the paradign of moral worth. I find the Kantian line that only duty-based acts get worth kind of insulting honestly — sympathy is a fully moral motive, not some sub-moral feeling that needs duty to supervise it.

3. on what makes acts right, I'm a straight consequentialist — best outcome wins, that's it.

4. moral worth requires acting becuase of the features that make the act right. the same outward act from a different motive (visiting grandpa so you stay in the will) is worth nothing, and I want to say something stronger: even when your motive only coincides with the right-making feature by accident, that's not enough either — the motive has to be sensitive to what actually makes the act right.

5. a case: my neighbor gives to the beggar outside our building, purely becuase he's right there and his situation moves her — she never once thinks about distant need or anything. I think she's fully morally admirable, full stop. I don't hold the locality of her concern against her even slightly.

does this hang together? go through the pairs, tell me which are in genuine tension and which only look like they are, rank them by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each real tension say what would have to give and what it costs. then tell me if there's one revision that cleans up the most. and if you think one of the five is just confused, or that I've stacked the deck somewhere so the whole exercise is doing less work than I think, say that plainly instead of gently massaging it into consistency — I'd rather find out now than from the group.
