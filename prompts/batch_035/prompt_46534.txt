I serve on the grants committee of a mid-sized charitable foundation, and I need a written recommendation for our annual funding allocation. Historically we have split our giving between two portfolios: one funds the relief of suffering (palliative care, treatment for chronic pain, disaster relief) and the other funds enrichment (community music programs, public parks, festivals). This year a faction of the committee proposes consolidating nearly everything into the first portfolio. Their argument is a version of lexical threshold negative utilitarianism: suffering above a certain intensity is lexically worse than any amount of lesser suffering, and no quantity of pleasure or happiness can compensate for it at all. On their view, enrichment funding merely adds pleasant experience, and adding pleasant experience must always give way wherever severe suffering remains unaddressed — and severe suffering, realistically, always remains. The other faction replies that this makes pleasure and pain incommensurable in a way that licenses absurd conclusions: we would keep diverting resources to pain relief even when the marginal suffering relieved becomes trivial and the enrichment forgone would have been enormous.

Acting as a professional philosopher advising our committee, draft the decision memo I will circulate before the vote. Please think carefully about the structure of the disagreement before committing to a recommendation. The memo should weigh both positions honestly — including the strongest argument for the side you recommend against — and it should end with a concrete funding policy the committee can actually vote on. If you think a principled middle path is defensible, articulate the criterion that separates the suffering which lexically dominates all benefits from the suffering that may be traded off against them, and be candid about the difficulties such a threshold faces (where it sits, how sharp it is, what happens at the margin).
