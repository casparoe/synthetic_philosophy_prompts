Every fall I teach our intro survey, and every fall some clever 19-year-old asks why we're still reading Plato if philosophy's been at it for 2,500 years and hasn't settled anything. I want to walk in with a real answer instead of a shrug. So here's what I want from you: rank these subfields by how much genuine progress they've made -- epistemology, ethics, philosophy of mind, metaphysics, and philosophy of language. But you have to use my operationalization, not vibes: I say a field progresses to the extent that its central questions stop being live, meaning not that they've been answered but that a settled consensus forms among the people actually working on them (roughly like how mathematicians stopped fighting about infinitesimals once analysis got rigorous). One caveat -- don't count convergence that's really just fashion or people deferring to a few big names; that's a consensus of exhaustion, not progress. For each field, score it 0-10 and say whether its core questions have been retired, sharpened into tractable ones, or are just cycling. Tell me how confident you are in each score, and single out the one you're least sure of, since I suspect the metaphysics call is where I'll get yelled at by my colleagues.
