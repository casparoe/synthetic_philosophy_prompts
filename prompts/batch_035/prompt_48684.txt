So here's roughly where I've landed, and I want you to audit it. Maybe act as someone who's equally at home in RL research and philosophy of mind, and please actually think it through / brainstorm a bit first instead of rubber-stamping it.

The views: (1) I think the textbook picture where an RL agent "maximizes its reward function" is basicly wrong for anything trained with deep learning — reward is a training signal that shapes what the network ends up valuing, and the finished agent doesn't have to be running any explicit reward-maximizing computation at all. I got this from a LessWrong post years ago and never really shook it. (2) Wireheading / reward tampering is nonetheless one of the more likely ways a sufficiently capable agent goes wrong. (3) Human addiction is a genuinely usefull model here — the rat with the electrode in its pleasure center, opioids, gambling loops; I don't think the analogy is just cute, I think it's actual evidence about what reward-driven learning does to any system built the way we are. (4) The wireheading problem is at heart an engineering problem: if you make the reward channel effectively read-only (secure hardware, reward computed off-box, whatever), you've removed most of the risk, and the remainder is just a matter of specifying the reward better. (5) A wireheaded agent isn't scary per se — once it's in the bliss loop all it wants is to stay in the loop, and a thing that just wants to stay plugged in doesn't conquer galaxies. Better a wirehead than a paperclipper, is my feeling.

So: go through the pairs, tell me which tensions are real and which dissolve once you're careful about what each claim commits me to, rank them by severity, give me the cheapest fix for each one, and then tell me which single revision would clean up the most if I made it.
