I'm a PhD student working on explanation in historiography, and an argument from our reading group won't leave me alone. It's about overdetermined outcomes like the July 1914 crisis: lots of historians think some war between the great powers was coming anyway — the alliance blocs, the naval race, the mobilization timetables, Austria-Hungary's reckoning with Serbia — so that no single decision in that month made the difference. Someone in the group spun this out:

(1) A factor is a cause of an outcome only if the outcome would not have happened without it.
(2) In an overdetermined case, each factor fails that test: remove the assassin, and the war still comes, maybe in 1915 over Morocco or the Balkans; remove the Balkan tension, and Sarajevo still lights it.
(3) So in overdetermined cases no individual factor is a cause.
(4) But something brought the war about.
(5) So what caused it was the whole configuration at once, not any factor within it.
(6) And whatever isn't a cause of the outcome isn't something anyone is responsible for.
(7) Therefore no statesman in 1914 — not Bethmann Hollweg, not anyone — is even partly responsible for the war, and the same goes for every overdetermined outcome in history.

That conclusion proves way too much for me: it would wipe out responsibility for much of what historians actually care about, and by extension it would do the same to everyday overdetermined cases (two factories dumping into the same river, etc.). My instinct is the slide happens between (3) and (5), turning "no single factor is individually necessary" into "no factor is a cause at all" — but I can't quite nail it down. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? For what it's worth, I'd most like to keep (1), since it seems to do real work in ordinary historical explanation, but I'm prepared to be told that's exactly the weak spot. And if rejecting (1) means I need a replacement test for singular causation, I'd appreciate hear what the options look like — I gather the legal philosophy crowd has been round this block before.
