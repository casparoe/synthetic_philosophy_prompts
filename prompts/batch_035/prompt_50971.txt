So here's the deal: a friend and I have been fighting about Skolem's paradox in a math discord for like eight months and it's clearly not resolving itself, so I want us to do the adversarial-collaboration thing (I read about these on LessWrong a while back and the format stuck with me) and I need a first draft of the joint statement.

Background so it's self-contained, since some people in the discord are rusty: if ZFC has any model, the downward Löwenheim–Skolem theorem says it has a countable model. Inside that model, Cantor's theorem holds and the model "sees" sets it proves are uncountable, including something it satisfies is the real line. From outside, all of those sets sit in a countable collection, and there's a bijection (an outside-the-model one) between the naturals and what the model calls its reals. No contradiction is derivable, everybody agrees on that much.

The two camps:

- Camp A (my friend, the relativist): the paradox shows "uncountable" was never an absolute property of sets. Cardinality facts are relative to what functions you're allowed to see. There's a perfectly good sense in which the model's reals ARE countable (they just are, from outside), and a perfectly good sense in which they're not (from inside), and there is no further fact about which sense is "really" true. He thinks this generalizes: mathematical properties we thought were intrinsic are quietly relative to a choice of model.
- Camp B (me, the absolutist): it's a scope confusion, full stop. The outside bijection isn't a bijection from the model's perspective and the model isn't wrong from its own. The theorem is a fact about the expressive weakness of first-order logic, not about sets. Second-order ZFC being quasi-categorical (Zermelo's old move) is supposed to show that once you fix the semantics properly, the relativity evaporates. He says that just smuggles in an absolute "ALL subsets" that the paradox itself casts doubt on, which honestly sometimes lands and makes me sweat.

Draft the joint statement both of us could sign. I want:

1. A numbered section of claims both camps accept — including about the theorem itself, about Cantor's theorem, and about the fact that no formal contradiction follows.
2. A disagreements section (table if you like) where each disagreement is worded so the person holding that view would actually endorse the phrasing. My friend keeps getting paraphrased as saying "the reals are secretly countable," which he says is a strawman, so get the wording right.
3. The cruxes — I genuinely don't know what they all are, but I suspect one is whether the outside-the-model bijection counts as a real counterexample to uncountability or is in some relevant sense a second-class object, and another is whether second-order quantification is a fix or a shell game.
4. For each crux, an argument or thought experiment both of us would agree bears on it, with each camp's prediction written down in advance (what would Camp A say the upshot is, what would Camp B say).

And if some chunk of our eight-month war turns out to be purely verbal, name the chunk and say why, because we'd both rather know that than keep going.
