A friend and I have been arguing about what pleasures have in common, and I've started keeping a record of her rulings on particular states of mind. So far: an espresso sipped while she's deep in conversation and never once notices the taste — she wasn't craving it, it was pure habit — not a pleasure; the warmth of the sun on her back while she deliberately savors it — a pleasure; the lifting of a week of dread once the feared event fails to occur — a pleasure; being so absorbed in a repair job that she registers no distinct sensation she could name, though in retrospect she says she loved every minute of it — a pleasure; watching a rival fail publicly and privately savoring it — a pleasure. Three new states have now come up for ruling: (a) a hot shower that feels comfortable but that she is entirely indifferent to, neither savoring it nor wanting it, and would just as soon skip; (b) the sharp sting of a deep-tissue massage that she actively welcomes and relishes while it hurts; (c) a cool breeze on her face while she sleeps, the kind she always enjoys when awake. Predict her ruling on each of the three. Then tell me what criterion she is implicitly applying, which rival criteria would fit her record just as well, and which of the three cases — or which further case, if none of them suffices — would do the most to decide between those criteria.
