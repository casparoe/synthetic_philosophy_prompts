Ok so in my ethics class we just did a unit on utilitarianism and everyone was on board with "maximize happiness, minimize suffering, trade them off against each other." But I've kind of gone off the rails since then and now I hold this view: there's some threshold of suffering — call it serious torture-level pain, not a headache — and past that threshold, pain stops being tradeable against pleasure entirely. Like, a world with a billion blissful lives plus one person being tortured is WORSE than a world of a billion mediocre-but-fine lives and no torture, no matter how much bliss you pile on, even infinite amounts. Adding infinitely much perfect happiness just doesn't touch the one torture session. But below the threshold I'm totally normal about tradeoffs — I'd take a pinprick for a concert, even a broken arm for something good enough, so it's not like I think pain is the only thing that matters. A friend who reads way more philosophy than me said this is roughly "lexical threshold negative utilitarianism" and that it's an actual position people defend, not just me being dramatic.

What I want to know is what would actually count against it. Not people saying "that's extreme" or "it's pessimistic" — I mean real defeaters. Like, does it commit me to something crazy when you stack cases: if one torture session can't be outweighed by any amount of bliss, does it follow I should prefer a world where everyone is annihilated instantly and painlessly (no suffering ever again) over the actual world? Because that conclusion feels like a real cost, but I honestly don't know if it's fatal or just a bullet some defenders would bite. Also is there a problem with where exactly I put the threshold — like if the exact level of "untradeable" pain matters so much, can I just keep lowering or raising it whenever an argument comes at me, and at that point have I qualified the view into being untestable? And are there cases where two people both below-threshold pains combine into something above threshold, which seems like it should break something? I'd like the possible defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and for each one, whether someone committed to my view would concede it counts against them or whether they have a decent response — and if all the responses amount to fiddling with the threshold, tell me honestly whether that's a fatal flaw for a view like this or just how ethics views work. (Also if a couple of smallish pains should be commensurable with pleasures but one big pain shouldn't, I'd love to hear how anyone makes sense of where the line gets drawn without it just being vibes.)
