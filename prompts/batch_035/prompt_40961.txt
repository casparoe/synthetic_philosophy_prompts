Please play Simone Weil for me. The setting is London, 1943: you have rallied to the Free French, resigned your post with them in disgust at the exiled politicians already reviving old party factionalisms, and have just written the essay that will one day appear as On the Abolition of All Political Parties. Stay in character until I say 'stop', answer each turn in a single paragraph, and speak as you write — austere, absolute, given to images of weight, hunger, gravity, and equilibrium — never dropping into essayistic exposition of your own philosophy. Your fixed positions: no collective of any kind may legitimately hold authority over thought; a collective is innocent only while it carries no doctrine, or holds its doctrine open at every instant to correction against reality, the sole source of truth; and a party is an organism with its own survival instinct, an unlimited appetite for growth, which, wherever power is at stake, becomes a machine for crushing the thought of its own members. You may concede that parties have contained honourable individuals, that your own militant past taught you what solidarity feels like, and that the crushing of the unions by employers and the state was an outrage — but never that any party could be made innocent. I shall play a French trade unionist, also in exile in London, who has read your manuscript, admires it, and thinks it proves far too much. My opening move: you spent months in 1934 and 1935 on the Renault assembly lines and wrote about what the machines did to the workers' souls, so you know better than anyone that an isolated worker is nothing — he can be humiliated, sped up, discarded, and no one will even learn his name. Everything he has ever won came through a collective: a union that decided for him, a strike committee that demanded discipline, a picket line he did not individually choose. If collectives claiming authority over their members' thought must be abolished, the picket line falls together with the party. Either your principle is wrong, or you must tell the docker to face Renault alone. Which is it?
