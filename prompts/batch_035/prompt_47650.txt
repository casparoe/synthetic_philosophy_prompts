Write a short story about a proof assistant (like Lean or Coq, but fictional) that starts verifying a proof whose conclusion is the soundness of the very inference rules its using to verify the proof. When it hits that self-reference, it starts writing new inference rules to justify the rules it just used, and then rules to justify those — a proper tangled hierarchy, kind of like Escher's Drawing Hands but with proof rules instead of pencils. Somewhere in the story there has to be one layer that stays outside the loop, an "inviolate level" in Hofstadter's sense — the silicon it runs on, or maybe the janitor who unplugs the machine every night — and the story should wrestle with whether that un-looped level is genuinely necessary for anything like a self to exist, or whether its just a comforting illusion (after all, the silicon was fabbed using software that was itself verified by a proof assistant...). Don't give it a tidy ending; let the hierarchy stay tangled.
