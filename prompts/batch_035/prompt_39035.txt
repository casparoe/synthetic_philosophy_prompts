So I keep noticing a pattern in how people argue about killing bugs and rodents, and I'm trying to figure out if philosophy has a name for it.

The pattern: an animal's moral status seems to get assigned by its role relative to us, not by anything about the animal itself. The same mouse is basically wildlife out in the meadow — whack it with a shovel for fun and people think you're a psycho. The same mouse in your pantry is a pest, and glue traps, which are honestly a pretty slow death, are unremarkable. Gardeners like to say a weed is just a plant in the wrong place, and it strikes me that a pest is basically an animal in the wrong place — except that with the animal, the "wrong place" verdict seems to do all the justificatory work while also being treated as something that follows from the verdict. As in: it's fine to kill it because it's a pest, and it counts as a pest because it's the sort of thing it's fine to kill. That quiet circularity is the thing I want a name for.

I know it's adjacent to speciesism, but that definately doesn't seem right — speciesism is about what kind of creature something is, and my puzzle is that the very same creature flips categories based on location and relationship. And I don't think it's plain begging the question either, since nobody is running an explicit argument with premises; it's more like a background classification doing moral work silently. The closest real-world thing I've come across is that agricultural science seems to treat "pest" as an economic category — whatever causes damage past some threshold — which if true would back up the idea that the label is doing work the animal itself isn't.

So: is there an established concept for this, wherever it would live (animal ethics, environmental ethics, wherever)? If there is, tell me how it differs from the near misses I mentioned. If there isn't, coin a term for it, give a definition, and say where the boundary cases fall — does a racoon in the attic count? A cow that breaks into a vegetable garden? A mosquito before it lands on anyone? Don't just hand me a word and a one-liner; I want the carving, not the label.
