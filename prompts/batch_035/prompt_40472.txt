I am writing an Extended Project Qualification on whether it is ever rational to honour sunk costs, and my supervisor has asked me to set out my own position clearly before I criticise anyone else's. Having written it down, I have begun to suspect that it does not hang together, but I cannot see where. Here are the views I have committed to on paper:

(1) The core principle: when deciding whether to continue a project, only what each option brings from now on should count; the money, effort and time already spent are unrecoverable and therefore rationally irrelevant.

(2) A verdict on my own case: I have spent seven years working towards Grade 8 violin, I now dread practising, and I have no intention of playing after the exam — yet I believe I ought to see it through, and I would think worse of myself for quitting. I do not think this is mere weakness of will on my part.

(3) The standard psychological story: people honour sunk costs because they hate feeling that past expenditure has been wasted, and this loss-aversion is a bias.

(4) A further claim I want to keep: past decisions can generate present obligations — a promise to my future self, or to my parents, who paid for years of lessons on the understanding that I would finish, seems to be a genuine reason that the textbook principle leaves out.

(5) A background commitment: I think a life goes better or worse as a whole, not merely moment to moment, and finishing what one starts seems to matter to how a life hangs together.

My suspicion is that (2) flatly contradicts (1), and that (4) and (5) are my attempts to rescue (2) — but I honestly cannot tell whether they rescue it or merely smuggle the sunk cost back in under another name. I also cannot tell whether (3) makes my refusal to quit irrational by my own lights, or whether (2) could rest on something other than loss-aversion. Please go through the pairs, tell me which tensions are genuine and which merely look that way, rank them by severity, and say for each genuine one what would have to give and what that would cost me elsewhere in the set. I would rather learn which single revision cleans up the most than be told that everything conflicts with everything.
