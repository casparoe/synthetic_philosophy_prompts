I'm on the committee at my school that decides which student clubs get funding for the year. There's three of us on it, and we're supposed to evaluate each application against three requirements: enough student interest, a budget that actually makes sense, and it can't be a near-duplicate of a club we already fund. Our written rule says a club gets funded if a majority of us votes yes on each of the three requirements.

Last week we reviewed an application for a board game club. We voted on the requirements one at a time. For student interest, two of us said yes. For the budget, two said yes. For the duplicate thing, two said yes. So majority yes on all three, which by our rule means it gets funded. But the catch is it was never the same two people. Each of us voted no on exactly one requirement -- one person said no on interest, another said no on the budget, the third said no on the duplicate thing. And since we each figured a club that fails even one requirement shouldn't get money, all three of us came away personally against funding it.

So when our advisor asked "so are you approving this or not?", we realized that if we just voted yes/no on the club as a whole, it would be 0-3 against. But if we follow the requirement-by-requirement rule, it passes.

My friend on the committee says we have to fund it -- the rule is literally "majority yes on each requirement," that happened, and we can't just ignore our own rule because we don't like where it landed. The other one says that's crazy: all three of us individually concluded the club shouldn't be funded, and a decision that goes against every single committee member is worse than useless. I honestly can't tell which way to go, and the advisor said it's our call, not his. Which way should we decide this one, and is there something wrong with the rule itself that this situation could even come up? If we should change it, what should we change it to?
