I hold a fairly strong reductionist view about the direction of time: every temporal asymmetry we observe — the thermodynamic one, the fact that radiation is retarded rather than advanced, the asymmetry of traces and memory, the causal asymmetry itself — is grounded, in principle, in the entropy gradient, and the entropy gradient is grounded in a low-entropy boundary condition on the early universe (what is standardly called the Past Hypothesis). Two further commitments make my version strong. First, I take the reduction to be exhaustive: there is no independent arrow of causation or of records that needs its own account. Second, I hold that the Past Hypothesis is best treated as a fundamental posit of physics — lawlike in its epistemic standing, not a coincidence awaiting a cosmological mechanism — so that asking what produced the initial low entropy is, at bottom, like asking what made the gravitational constant have its value.

Tell me what would count against this. I want the defeaters sorted by how decisive they would be, from fatal through serious to merely awkward, and for each I want to know what the world would have to look like for it to bite. Some candidates I am already aware of, so go past them or show why they run deeper than I think: the Boltzmann-brain worry that a low-entropy boundary condition makes typical observers deluded fluctuation-products; Penrose's complaint that the early universe was special in ways entropy alone does not capture, as with his Weyl curvature hypothesis; and the observation that the weak interactions already violate time-reversal symmetry, which critics say is an arrow my picture must either wrongly absorb or awkwardly declare irrelevant. Beyond those, consider what a quantum theory of gravity could force on me — for instance, a cosmology in which entropy resets each cycle, or a framework in which there is no well-defined global initial state to which a boundary condition could attach — and whether those would merely modify my view or destroy it. I also want an honest assessment of its exposure in a different sense: since I can always accommodate an unexpected temporal asymmetry by positing suitably special initial conditions, am I insulated from refutation altogether, and if so, is that an acceptable feature of a foundational posit, or a sign that I have traded explanation for a label? Where you can, say what defenders of the view would themselves concede as counting against it rather than what only its critics allege, and conclude with your own judgment about which defeater, if any, a rational holder of the view should genuinely lose sleep over.
