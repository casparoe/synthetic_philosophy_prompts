My colleague replied that since probability does not exist, there is no objective rate for a field to control, that my framing presupposed a kind of probability the slogan rules out, and that the entire replication-crisis discussion in terms of error rates, false-positive fractions, and the probability a finding replicates is built on a confusion.

I'm confident the slogan doesn't settle this, but I can't say precisely why. In particular, I want to know whether any reading (a) denies that long-run frequencies of replication outcomes are objective facts about the world, (b) denies only that single-case probabilities — the chance that this particular hypothesis is true, or that this particular study will replicate — are objective, or (c) is merely a claim about where probability numbers come from, such as betting dispositions and coherence requirements, and is therefore silent on my question altogether. Please keep those three cases clearly separate, because my argument needs long-run rates to be objective but is happy to concede the single cases. Finally, if you can, say which reading is most faithful to what de Finetti himself was after, reasoning from the general character of his project rather than from passages I would have to look up.
