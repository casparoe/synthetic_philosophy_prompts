For the last few years I've been in a history book club with some neighbors. We read mostly popular history, and over time a custom developed: whenever we finish a book, my friend Diane gives a verdict on the outcome it describes — either "bound to happen" or "could easily have gone the other way." I started writing her verdicts down, and now I'm trying to work out what rule she's actually applying, but I can't do it on my own, and I'd like your help.

Her record so far, with the reasons she gave:

— The North winning the Civil War: bound to happen. The industrial gap was too big; the South couldn't outlast a blockade and a war of attrition.
— The colonies winning independence from Britain: could have gone the other way. It took the French fleet arriving at exactly the right moment, and the army nearly fell apart more than once.
— The Cuban Missile Crisis ending without a war: could have gone the other way. It came down to a handful of individual decisions on both sides.
— Printing spreading after Gutenberg: bound to happen. Even if Gutenberg had died young, the idea was in the air and the demand was there.
— The Reformation breaking out when it did: bound to happen. The pressures had built for a century; without Luther, someone else would have lit the fuse soon enough.
— The crash of 1929: bound to happen. The bubble had to pop; the whole market was leveraged to the hilt.
— The Beatles becoming the biggest band in the world: could have gone the other way. Remove Brian Epstein or George Martin and it's a different story; plenty of bands just as talented went nowhere.

Next month we're covering three outcomes she'll have to rule on: the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989; the sinking of the Titanic on her maiden voyage; and the Spanish conquest of the Aztec empire, which was carried off by a few hundred Spaniards and their native allies. Please predict her verdict on each of the three. More importantly, tell me what rule, or rules, she seems to be running. My first attempt — "when the stronger side wins, it was bound to happen" — dies on the Reformation, since the reformers were the weaker side and she still said bound to happen. I have a half-formed second idea: that she says "bound to happen" when the story hangs together as a chain where each step leads naturally to the next, and "could have gone the other way" when the story is mostly what-ifs and lucky breaks. But I can't shake the feeling that some version of "big forces versus particular people" fits her record just as well. Lay out the candidate rules that fit everything she has said, say what each one predicts for the three new cases, give your best guess along with your reasons, and tell me which further case — one she hasn't ruled on yet — would best separate the rules that currently look the same to me.
