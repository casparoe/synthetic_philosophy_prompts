A friend who's deep into Hofstadter finally got me to read I Am a Strange Loop, and the part that stuck with me is the analogy he calls the careenium (I might be garbling some details, but this is the picture as I got it): imagine a giant snooker-like table where the balls -- he calls them careens -- roll around and clack into each other following plain physics, but the table's bumpers and so on are arranged so that higher-level patterns of careens, which he calls simmballs, glide around as stable quasi-objects, and some of these simmballs end up representing things in the world, including one that represents the state of the careenium itself, a self-referential loop. The punchline he wants out of it is that "symbols push the substrate around": the self, which looks like a mere pattern, is a real pattern with genuine causal power over the careens (the neurons, in the real-world mapping), so you get downward causation without any spookiness -- no soul shoving molecules around, just a strange loop that's real enough to steer the very hardware it lives on.

This is very appealing to me because I keep getting into it with another friend who's basically a hard-line epiphenomenalist about persons -- his line is that "I" is a story the brain tells itself, the story doesn't do anything, and the neurons do all the causal work. I want to deploy the careenium against him. But before I do, I want someone to actually audit the analogy instead of just vibing with it. Lay out the mapping explicitly -- what plays the role of neurons, of symbols, of the loop, of perception and memory, of the whole person -- and then show where the mapping breaks. The thing that worries me is that in the careenium, the table's rules were presumably designed so that simmballs would be stable and self-representing, whereas nobody designed brains for that; and I genuinely can't tell whether "downward causation" in the analogy is supposed to be anything more than slow stable configurations constraining fast little collisions, like a whirlpool steering the water it's made of -- in which case it seems like the careenium is just re-describing reductionism with two vocabularies and the anti-epiphenomenalist conclusion was smuggled in rather than shown.

So: which disanalogies actually matter for the argument I want to run (the self has real causal efficacy) versus which ones don't, does Hofstadter's conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, and if the careenium can't carry it, is there a better analogy that can -- or is my epiphenomenalist friend basically right that no billiards-flavored picture will ever make a pattern into a cause? I'd rather find out now than get flattened over beers.
