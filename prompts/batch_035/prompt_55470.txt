I'm in a causation reading group that's met monthly for about a year, and we have completely stopped moving on the counterfactual vs interventionist thing. I'm starting to suspect the disagreement isn't what it looks like, and I want a diagnosis rather than a verdict on who's right about causation.

The setup, roughly: half the group is loyal to the counterfactual tradition — Lewis-style, where c causes e iff, had c not occurred, e would not have — patched up for preemption and overdetermination the usual ways. The other half thinks that whole program is dead weight: closest possible worlds, a similarity metric nobody can actually specify, all to capture a dependency better stated as the interventionist counterfactual — had you wiggled X, Y would have covaried. When we hit the hard cases the two camps talk past each other. The counterfactual side says interventionism isn't a theory of causation at all but a theory of causal explanation, or of variable selection in models — the interventionist counterfactual presupposes causal notions (an intervention on X mustn't affect Y except through X), so as an analysis it's circular. The interventionists shrug and say the circularity is benign, it's explication not reduction, and besides, similarity-of-worlds is worse than circular, it's unspecified. Then somebody inevitably brings up transitivity — the match, the forest fire, the burn on the firefighter's hand — and both sides reach for patches that look suspiciously isomorphic: the Lewisians with restricted transitivity riders, the interventionists with their own conditions on when causal paths compose. Same surgery, different patient.

My working theory, which I floated and got shot down from both side, is this: the two camps aren't really disagreeing about causation, because they aren't talking about the same relata or even the same question. The counterfactual camp is doing metaphysics of a binary relation between events — does this event make a difference to that one. The interventionists are doing methodology — what belongs in a model, what counts as a manipulation, what an explanation has to route through. The word 'cause' is just keeping everyone in the same room. If that's right, the debate is partly verbal, and it never resolves because each side wins on its home turf: the circularity objection only bites if interventionism was offered as a reductive analysis, and the complaint about similarity metrics only bites if you wanted a metaphysics out of Lewis in the first place.

But I'm honestly not sure, because one member pushed back with something that's been bugging me since. She says the two theories do disagree about actual cases, not merely about questions. Take any preemption case — Suzy and Billy and the bottle, or the sergeant trumping the major — and the approaches don't just describe the same verdict differently; they disagree about which features of the case are doing the work, and you can build cases where the verdicts themselves come apart. If they genuinely answered different questions they'd never clash, and they clash constantly. Which, fair.

So: is my diagnosis right, partly right, or am I just describing a real substantive dispute badly? If it's verbal in some parts and substantive in others, tell me which parts are which and how you'd tell. And what would even count as progress here — a case that cleanly splits the theories, one side openly conceding it's doing something other than what it advertises, a conceptual distinction that separates the projects for good, or is this one of those debates that persists precisly because 'causation' names several things and neither side ever has to concede anything?
