I've been trying to work out what I actually believe about what should and shouldn't be for sale, based on my gut reactions to a bunch of cases, and I'd like your help finding the principle (if there is one) behind them.

Here are my verdicts. A guy selling one of his kidneys to a stranger for $50k: should stay illegal. Someone donating a kidney to a stranger for nothing: hero, obviously fine. Getting paid to donate plasma, like they do in the US: fine, I've half considered doing it myself. A hardware store tripling generator prices the day after a hurricane: wrong, and I'm fine with laws against it. A scalper tripling ticket prices for a concert: kind of a jerk move, but shouldn't be illegal. Selling your vote: flatly wrong, and I'd still say that even if the buyer promised to cast it exactly how you would have anyway. Though — and this is where I start confusing myself — I've read that Israel gives people who sign up as organ donors (and their families) priority on the transplant waiting list if they ever need an organ, and my gut says that's fine? Paying for a fast pass to skip the theme park line: fine, even though something about it feels a bit gross. A rich family bribing their kid's way into a university: wrong, obviously. The same family donating a building to that university and the kid getting in partly on that basis: honestly feels much less wrong to me, and I can't fully defend that one.

My tentative rule was something like: a sale is wrong when the seller is only selling out of desperation, or when the thing being sold stops being what it was once you put a price on it. But the vote case doesn't fit the first half (a comfortable person selling their vote is still wrong), and the plasma case doesn't fit it either (plasma sellers aren't exactly rich). And I'm shaky on the second half too, since people get paid to stand in line for others and that seems totally fine.

So: is there a principle, or maybe two or three, that captures these verdicts? I'd want it stated precisely enough that it gives clear answers on cases I haven't listed — especially surrogacy (I flip-flop on it), the charity that pays drug addicts to get long-term birth control or be sterilized, and paying volunteers to be deliberately infected for vaccine challenge trials. And I'd want you to be honest about which of my verdicts has to go — my bet is the building-donation one is doing the most damage, but you tell me. One more thing: since I gather actual philosophers and economists disagree about a lot of this (I assume the kidney question especially), please keep straight what's more or less settled among people who study this versus what's genuinely contested, instead of presenting one side of some debate as the consensus answer.
