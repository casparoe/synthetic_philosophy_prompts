For the last two years I've been running a small sports-modeling side project with a friend, and for the first time it's actually producing what looks like a genuine edge: our estimates beat the closing market odds on roughly 55% of the bets we place at even-money payouts, and we have reason to believe the edge will persist for at least a few thousand more bets. I have about $20,000 I can dedicate to this without wrecking my finances. My friend's position on bet sizing is simple and, as far as I can tell, arithmetically impeccable: each bet has positive expected value, so the more we stake the better, and staking the entire bankroll each round maximizes expected wealth — 0.55 times a doubled bankroll plus 0.45 times zero comes out to a 10% expected gain per bet. By his lights, anything less than all-in is leaving money on the table.

When I actually simulated all-in betting, though, the bankroll goes to zero almost every time: one losing bet out of however many ends everything, and over thousands of bets you get thousands of chances to hit that zero. Meanwhile, betting a small fixed fraction of the current bankroll each round — say 5-10% — compounds slowly but remarkably reliably in simulation. So we're stuck in an argument where both of us can point at a criterion: he points at expected value, I point at what actually happens to my bankroll along nearly every trajectory, and we can't even agree on what the disagreement is about. He accuses me of being merely risk-averse and dressing it up; I suspect his expected-value calculation, while correct as a statement about an average over many parallel copies of me, is answering the wrong question for the one me who has to live through a single sequence of bets.

Can you untangle this properly? Specifically: is there a principled sense in which some particular betting fraction is optimal rather than just psychologically comfortable — something with the flavor of a growth rate I should be maximizing rather than a one-shot expected value — and does that principle pin down an exact number, or only a region? Is my friend's position just a mistake, or is it correct under some coherent way of specifying what we're trying to maximize, so that the real dispute is about that specification rather than the math? And finally, I want to check an intuition: would anything change if the bets were additive rather than multiplicative — say, if I could commit a fixed dollar amount of exposure each round no matter what my current bankroll is, with losses covered from outside income — or does the strange behavior I'm seeing in simulation come precisely from the fact that each round's stake is a share of whatever I currently have?
