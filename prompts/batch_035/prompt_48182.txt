Here's roughly where I've landed: (1) I'm an epistemic optimist — no truth is in principle off-limits to inquiry. For any true proposition, there's some possible situation in which it comes to be known: maybe an idealized agent, maybe far-future humanity with instruments we can't imagine, maybe just some lucky observer in the right place. I am NOT saying anyone actually knows everything, obviously. (2) But I'm also a realist about the past: there's a determinate fact about things like how many sips of water Caesar took on the morning of the Ides, even though every physical trace is long gone and no amount of future physics recovers it. It's true or false regardless of us — truth doesn't need a verifier, that's what makes me a realist and not some Dummett-style anti-realist. (3) I hold the boring standard positions about the logic of knowledge: if you know a conjunction you know each conjunct, and knowledge is closed under known implication (I side with closure against the Nozick/Dretske tracking people, their counterexamples never impressed me). (4) No agent, however idealized, can know something false — knowledge entails truth, and contradictions are flat-out unknowable. (5) One thing that nags me and that maybe you can also address: truths plausibly outnumber possible acts of verification — infinitely many truths, and even in an infinitely long future it's not like everything gets checked — and yet I somehow feel no pressure at all to give up (1) over that. Is that me being inconsistent, or is that fine?
