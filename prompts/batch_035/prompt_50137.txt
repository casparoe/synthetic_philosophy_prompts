I chair the elections committee of a scholarly society of about 4,000 members, which has always chosen its officers by simple plurality. A reform proposal has split the committee: several members favor instant-runoff voting, several approval voting, one insists on a Borda count, and the treasurer maintains that plurality is fine and any change will confuse the membership. Before we report to the council, I need a written memo recommending a single method for the bylaws revision we will circulate. Please draft it. The memo should put Arrow's impossibility theorem to honest work: what it actually rules out for a choice like ours, whether it applies to each candidate method — approval voting in particular, since it does not ask voters for rankings — and where it is simply silent, so that we neither invoke it as a reason to abandon reform nor misuse it as a knockdown argument against ranked methods. It should then weigh the considerations the theorem does not reach, such as vulnerability to strategic voting under each method, ballot simplicity for a membership that votes by mail, and whether satisfying the Condorcet criterion should matter to us. It should give the treasurer's position a fair hearing rather than dismissing it, and the final recommendation should be concrete enough to become a bylaws clause, with reasoning a committee member who disagrees could engage with rather than merely defer to.
