I'm in a grad reading group that just did a couple of sessions on the absent-minded driver, and I noticed everyone treats it as a single-agent puzzle — the driver plans, forgets, exits too early or too late, done. But it struck me that there's a stakeholder nobody talks about: whoever is sitting in the passenger seat. So I've been trying to build a worked case where the driver's absent-mindedness is a genuine handicap for him, and yet someone else in the car is quietly better off with him amnesic, and I have most of the ingredients but I need someone to actually construct it and verify it does what I want.

The setup I have in mind: a driver whose memory effectively resets after each decision (his doctor says think transient global amnesia), driving home past three identical-looking highway exits, choosing exit-or-continue at each one with no idea which one he's at. His payoffs, in utils: exiting at the first intersection is worth 1, the second 4, the third 3, and driving past all three is worth 0. His sister is riding along but headed somewhere else, with payoffs (0, 0, 9, 1) over the same four outcomes, fully rational, and there's a window before departure where the two of them can make side payments or commitments. What the case has to show, and you should check each item with actual computations rather than asserting it: (1) the ex ante optimal policy for the amnesic driver is strictly mixed and strictly beats both pure strategies available to him (always exit, never exit); (2) if his GPS worked and he simply knew which intersection he was at, he'd do strictly better still — I don't want the cheesy version where forgetting helps the forgetter; (3) despite that, the sister strictly prefers the amnesic brother running his own optimal policy over the informed one, and would pay up front for the right to choose the policy he follows — and you should check whether some payment makes that commitment deal acceptable to both of them or whether the bargaining window is empty, since he loses from surrendering control.

Please give it to me as a numbered list of exactly five points, each a sentence or two, no introduction or conclusion: (1) the scenario itself, (2) the planning-stage calculation for the amnesic optimum, (3) the informed-driver comparison, (4) the sister's side — her valuation of jamming the GPS versus buying commitment, including the bargaining window, (5) which payoff feature is doing all the work. On that last point be honest about where my construction is most likely to fail: tell me the smallest change to the numbers that flips the result, and if you find that one of my specs is actually unsatisfiable as stated (e.g. no mixed policy can do what I claimed), fix it with the minimal adjustment but flag exactly what you changed and why.
