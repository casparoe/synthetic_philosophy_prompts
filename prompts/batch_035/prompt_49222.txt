i'm in a philosophy of mind class this semester and we just finished a unit on pain. for the last assignment we each have to write up our own views as a list, hand them out, and get grilled by classmates next session. before that happens i want a sanity check on whether my views even hang together, because i have a nagging feeling there's a problem somewhere in here but i genuinely can't locate it. here's the list as it stands:

(1) i'm a hedonist about well-being: the only thing that's intrinsically good or bad for you is how your experiences feel, pleasant or unpleasant. (2) unpleasantness is constitutive of pain's phenomenal character — nothing could feel exactly the way pain feels without being bad, the way nothing could taste exactly like vinegar without being sour. (3) pain asymbolia is real and the standard description of it is right: there are patients who report genuine pain — they localize it, rate its intensity, describe its quality — but sincerely say it doesn't bother them, they don't mind it, they wouldn't cross the street to make it stop. i take this to show that the badness of pain isn't built into the sensation itself but comes from an aversive attitude layered on top of it. (4) the badness of pain is badness for the subject — pain occurring with no subject it's bad for wouldn't be bad at all. (5) pain gives immediate reasons: the bare fact that something will hurt is itself a reason not to do it, not because hurting is bad for you or because you'd prefer it not to happen — it just is a reason. (6) intensity and unpleasantness come apart even in normal people (distraction, nitrous oxide, that sort of thing — people will say the pain is still there at full strength but they don't mind it), and it's the unpleasantness, not the intensity, that does the moral work, so in a pain clinic the number that matters is how bothered the patient is, not the 0–10 intensity score.

one classmate already insists (2) and (6) obviously can't both be true, since if unpleasantness is built into pain, how could anything be a pain at full intensity but not bothersome. i half buy that but not fully. my TA read the list over my shoulder and just said it was a lot to be committed to, which didnt help. so: which of these actually conflict, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix in each case? please rank the tensions by severity instead of listing them all as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most if i made it. and if two of these can actually be reconciled by a distinction i'm glossing over, spell the distinction out — dont just tell me it exists, because i'll have to defend it in front of the class.
