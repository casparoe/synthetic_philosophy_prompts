so my economic history workshop is doing a session on the protestant ethic thesis, and everyone has to submit a one-page "where I stand" statement beforehand so the discussion has something to bite on. I've drafted mine, and before sending it I want a sanity check on whether the thing actually hangs together. here's what it says, roughly:

1. Weber was basically right, and in the strong sense: the content of Calvinist doctrine — predestinarian anxiety about election, the idea of a calling, proving your state of grace through methodical worldly conduct — causally contributed to the distinctive *character* of modern capitalism. The relentless accumulation, reinvestment, deferred consumption, work as an end in itself. Not just to where and when capitalism happened to show up.
2. Culture can be a cause. Not in some vague "values in the air" way — cultural items cause things by being believed and acted on, i.e. through their meaning for the people who hold them.
3. I'm a methodological individualist. Every social explanation has to bottom out in what particular people did and their reasons for doing it. No free-floating cultural agents doing work on their own.
4. I buy Weber's own famous qualification, the switchman point: material and ideal interests supply the dynamic, ideas only set the tracks along which the interests push conduct.
5. I don't think people adopt doctrines for the payoffs. Belief in predestination wasn't selected like a trait that helps its carriers — people held it because they were raised into it and found it compelling, not because it made them better at commerce. So I'm suspicious of any story where the ethic spread because it was economically useful to its holders.
6. The standard objection from Japan and the other Confucian-heritage economies — capitalism thriving fine with zero protestantism — doesn't bother me at all. Once the ethic exists it's detachable from its theological basis and transmits secularly, through schooling, family discipline, that whole style of life. I think that's a feature of the thesis, not a bug.

It's 1 together with 6 that I keep staring at, but I genuinely cant tell whether that's the real fault line or whether I've missed the actual problem somewhere else in the list. Go through the six and tell me which pairs are really in tension, which only look like it, rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally bad, and for the worst one tell me what has to give and what giving it up costs me (e.g. what I lose against other objections). And please don't just map the options and leave the choice to me — take a definite position on which commitment I should drop or weaken, and argue for it. If you think the package is actually consistent as it stands, say that outright and show me why, rather than hedging.
