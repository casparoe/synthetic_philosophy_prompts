I keep accusing histories of being "Whiggish" and I've realized my verdicts don't really line up, so I want to know if there's an actual principle underneath them or if I'm just waving a word around. My gut verdicts, case by case: a history of chemistry that treats phlogiston theory as an embarrassment on the road to oxygen -- Whiggish. A history of astronomy that calls geocentrism a reasonable inference from the evidence available at the time -- fine, not Whiggish. A history of the vote told as the steady expansion of the franchise toward universal suffrage -- feels Whiggish in tone, but if the franchise really did steadily expand, what's the historian supposed to do, hide the endpoint? Describing the Holy Roman Empire using modern country names because readers need a map -- clearly presentist, but I don't want to call it Whiggish, and I can't say why. A history of medicine concluding bloodletting was irrational -- Whiggish; the same history concluding miasma theory was a respectable failure -- not. A history of science that narrates only the winning theories and never mentions the losers -- on this one I flip depending on the day. So is there a principle separating Whiggish from merely retrospective that captures most of these, and if not, which verdicts should I give up? Is "Whiggish" maybe not one thing at all but a bundle -- judging by outcomes, reading progress into contingency, and assuming the present is the telos -- that only sometimes travel together? I'd like the principle or principles stated precisely, what each one says about the franchise case (the one I keep going back and forth on), and a straight answer about which of my verdicts it just can't accommodate. Answer in British English please.
