Is there an actual name for this pattern or am I going to have to invent one? I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, but I've been hanging around effective altruism-adjacent stuff for a few years and I keep seeing the same thing happen and can't find a term that fits.

Roughly: a funder says "show me your cost-effectiveness." Only certain kinds of work can respond — pills distributed, nets handed out, surgeries paid for. So the measurable programs get the money. Fine so far, that's just rational allocation. But then the second-order effects kick in. The charities that didn't get funded restructure themselves until they have countable outputs, even when it makes the actual work worse. The researchers start studying whatever can be studied with methods that produce numbers. Ten years later the whole ecosystem has drifted into the subset of doing good that happened to be countable at step one, and the creepy part is that no individual decision along the way was obviously wrong — each funder just picked the option with the best evidence, and "best evidence" systematically meant "a number exists."

There's also a weirder flavor I keep noticing: once a spreadsheet exists, the spreadsheet wins. Someone builds a probabilistic model with gigantic error bars, and it still beats a domain expert's considered "honestly, we have no idea" in every internal discussion, because the model has decimal places in it. The uncertainty becomes decorative.

Now, I know Goodhart's law — when a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure — but nothing here is exactly a target being gamed, the charities aren't lying and the numbers are real. And the streetlight joke is about a person choosing to search where the light is, not about a whole funding ecosystem evolving under selection pressure until nobody is choosing anything. Is there an established term for the systemic version, where the bias comes from accumulated funding decisions rather than any one person's reasoning? If there is, I want to know how it's distinguished from plain Goodhart and where the boundary sits; if there isn't, please coin something, define it properly, and give me the edge cases where it collapses back into the neighbors. A bare name won't help me, I need the carving.
