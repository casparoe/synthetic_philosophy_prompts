But the field is scattered across a bunch of half-finished threads and I can't tell which ones are live.

Here's my rough map of the open questions, correct me if I'm missing some:

1. The straight tiling agents problem — an agent that can't prove its successor obeys its goals because proving it would require proving its own soundness, which Löb blocks. Are the patches (parametric polymorphism, the whole "trust but can't prove" workaround family) actual solutions or duct tape?

2. The modal agents thread — PrudentBot, Löbian handshakes, cooperation via proof search. Fun fixed-point results in modal logic, but does any of it transfer to agents that reason probabilistically instead of via proof search? Is this a toy drawer or a real research program?

3. Logical uncertainty as the root cause — the argument that the whole Löbian mess is really a symptom of not having a decent account of reasoning about things you can't prove, and that if we cracked logical inductors-style reasoning the obstacle would mostly dissolve. Is that right or is it a bait-and-switch (probabilistic reflection has its own procrastination-paradox-style problems)?

4. Reflective oracles and probabilistic self-trust — we know agents can reason about copies of themselves if they get access to a randomized oracle with the right fixed-point property. Is that "enough" reflection for practical purposes, or does it only work in a idealized setting that real agents can never inhabit?

5. The deflationary view — that bounded, messy, ML-style agents never prove anything about themselves anyway, they just learn approximate self-models, so the Löbian obstacle is an artifact of modeling agents as proof-based idealizations and it will never bind in practice.

What I want from you is a defended priority ordering for someone with my constraints: which of these (or something I'm not listing) is most worth my evenings, which are upstream of which (e.g. does anything in 1 or 2 even make sense to touch before 3 is settled?), which are overrated or basically dead, and crucially which are tractable for a person doing careful math in their spare time versus needing a whole research org. Also tell me what would change the ranking — what kind of result, from me or anyone else, should promote one of these or kill it. And be honest if the deflationary view (5) means the right answer is "none of this, go do something else."

I don't want a survey of who wrote what — I've read most of it. I want your actual prioritization and the reasoning behind it, especially where you think my framing of the questions themselves is off.
