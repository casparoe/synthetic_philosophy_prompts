hey, I've been reading up on bishop berkeley's weird second life as an economic pamphleteer and I want a dialogue out of it. could you write one set in dublin around 1736, between berkeley himself (fresh off publishing the Querist, his series of numbered queries about fixing ireland's economy, including a sketch for a national bank that issues paper money) and a die-hard lockean who thinks money's value comes from its silver content and that paper currency is a fraud waiting to collapse.

a few things I want it to hit:

- berkeley's core claim, which he puts as a question in one of the queries: whether "the true idea of money, as such, be not altogether that of a ticket or counter". for him even gold and silver coin are just counters for reckoning, recording, and transferring value, so a well-managed paper bank could do the job as well as (or better than) silver that keeps draining off to england and leaving ireland with no circulating medium at all.
- the lockean should push back hard and intelligently: silver has a real, stable value that doesn't hang on anyone's promises; paper is nothing but confidence, and one overissue or bad harvest kills it forever. also the point locke made back in the english recoinage fights, that relabeling or raising the denomination of coin doesn't create wealth, it just renames it — so a paper standard is the same trick writ large.
- berkeley gets to counter that silver's value is itself mostly opinion and fashion (people want it because everyone else wants it), and that real wealth is labour and industry — he literally asks whether "the four elements, and man's labour therein, be not the true source of wealth" — so the whole function of money is just to stir up and circulate industry, and whatever tokens do that reliably are good enough.

make both of them smart, no strawmen, and make them actually respond to each others points instead of monologuing past each other. let the lockean land some real hits (what stops the bank from over-issuing? isn't confidence easier to keep when the coin can at least be melted down?) and let berkeley have answers that sometimes only half-work. end it unresolved if that's more honest — maybe they agree the whole edifice rests on trust and then part ways on what trust can actually be built out of. keep the period flavor light, it shouldn't be a lecture, they can get a bit snippy. and definitely have the lockean needle berkeley about his immaterialism somewhere along the lines of: first he denies matter exists, now he wants money to be paper — seems the man just keeps wanting less and less stuff to be real. let berkeley fire back at that too. somethign with real back-and-forth energy.
