A pop-philosophy book I picked up (I'd rather not name it, its a big glossy one about how ancient ideas solve modern problems) has a chapter on death and it summarizes Epicurus's argument like this, roughly quoted from the page: 'Epicurus argued that death cannot be bad for you. When death has arrived, you no longer exist, and while you exist death has not arrived — so there is never a time at which death is bad for you. Since a harm requires a subject who suffers it, and the dead have no experiences, death is not a harm. Epicurus also observed that the time after your death is a perfect mirror image of the time before your birth, and nobody regrets the eternity they missed before being born, so by symmetry you shouldn't mind the eternity after. Since all good and bad consists in sensations, and death removes all sensation, he concluded that death is actually a positive good for you, a release. Modern philosophers settled the matter with the deprivation account: death is bad for you because it deprives you of the goods of further life, which refuted the Epicureans.'
