quick background so you know where this is coming from: I'm a backend engineer, zero philosophy training, but I do moderation-adjacent work, and a few weeks ago we had a multi-day slack war about whether our profanity filter should flag slurs that show up inside quoted strings in user bug reports. Afterwards I tried writing down what I actually think, and a coworker with a linguistics minor looked it over and said at least two of my points contradict each other but refused to say which ones (thanks alot, Dana). So now I want an outside check. Here's my position list, as honestly as I can state it:

1. A slur doesn't contribute to what a sentence literally says. "X is a [slur]" and "X is a member of the target group" have the same truth conditions — the slur adds nastiness, like tone, but not content. It's basically punctuation with an attitude.

2. Reclamation is real semantic change, not just friendly intent. When members of the target group use the word with each other, it genuinely means something different (roughly: "one of us," said with warmth). It's not the same meaning uttered with a nicer attitude — the actual meaning changed.

3. Even when every descriptive part of a slur sentence checks out, the sentence itself comes out false, not merely rude. Someone who says "X is a [slur]" of a person who is in fact in the group is asserting, among other things, that being in the group makes you contemptible, and that part is false, so the whole assertion is false in the way that matters.

4. The use/mention distinction does real work. Quoting a slur inside a bug report, a news article, or a court transcript does no wrong — nothing harmful is being said, the word is just being pointed at. (Singing along to lyrics is greyer, but straight-up quoting is fine.)

5. Despite 4, I still think our filter should flag slur occurrences in quoted strings and log lines, and I don't think that's just a hack to make the linter simpler. It feels like this one contradicts 4, but I can't fully pin down why, and honestly I'm not willing to just say the filter should ignore mentions — I've seen what ends up in those logs.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which ones only look like they conflict, and what exactly has to give. If some tension is cheap to fix and another one forces a real sacrifice, say so. Then tell me: what's the single cheapest revision that would clean up the most? Lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence, then the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my question back at me.
