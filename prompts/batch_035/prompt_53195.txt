A couple months ago I got an MRI after a minor bike accident, and the radiologist's report casually mentioned that my total brain volume is well below average for my age and sex -- incidental finding, nothing clinically wrong with me, but there it is in writing, and now my whole family won't let it go. This is awkward because for years I've held that brain size is genuinely morally relevant: not the whole story, but a real input, such that a creature with more of the right kind of brain matters more, other things equal. I've used it for diet stuff (a honeybee with something like a million neurons vs a cow's billions), for thinking about charity tradeoffs between species, and, if I'm honest, it fed a certain smugness about being human. Now I'm the guy with the small brain, so before I revise either my ethics or my self-image, I want to stress-test the position properly. What would actually count against it? I can generate some obvious trouble myself -- hemispherectomy patients who keep their personalities, people with hydrocephalus whose cortex is a thin rim and who live normal lives, sperm whale brains five times heavier than ours, crows with tiny brains but densely packed neurons who outthink animals with far more brain mass -- but I want the full map, not just the hits I already know about. Which observations, cases, or findings about nervous systems would a committed proponent of the view concede as counting against it, and which would they wave away? Which defeaters are fatal and which merely awkward? And crucially: the view can always retreat from raw size to neuron number to cortical neuron number to primate-style scaling, so has it been qualified into unfalsifiability, and if so, is that fatal for a moral claim of this kind or just what you should expect from one? I'd also like to know which potential defeater you personally think is the strongest, since I'll admit I'm motivated to find the view survives. Also -- no need to tell me I'm worth the same as anyone else, that's not the question; the question is what the view risks and where.
