ok so i'm putting together a short position statement for our phil of mind reading group (they make everyone write one so the rest can poke at it) and i want a consistency check before i send it out. it's mostly grown out of thinking about Dennett's "Where Am I?", the one where his brain gets removed and parked in a life-support vat in Houston while his body goes off to retrieve the warhead stuck a mile under Tulsa. brain is called Yorick, body is Hamlet, the replacement body later is Fortinbras, and Hubert is the computer program they built as a duplicate of Yorick, in case you need the scorecard.

here's roughly where i've landed:

1. i'm a functionalist. mental life is just functional organization, multiply realizable. run Hubert with the same inputs and it has exactly the same mental life Yorick would.

2. in the actual vat setup, i side with the body. i'm out in the lab staring through the glass at my brain, not floating in the vat. the thing in the ginger-ale is my brain, not me. and when the radio links fail and the body is stuck underground — deaf, blind, paralyzed — i'm the one in trouble in Tulsa; i don't quietly relocate to Houston where Yorick is sitting pretty. Dennett tried to think the thought "Here am I, Daniel Dennett, suspended in a bubbling fluid, being stared at by my own eyes" and said he couldn't pull it off with any conviction. same happens to me, and i think that failure is telling us something real, not just a limit of his imagination.

3. i'd step into the teletransporter. scan, destroy, reconstruct on mars with all my memories intact — that's survival, plain and simple. matter doesn't matter, pattern does.

4. the extended mind thesis is basically right. cognition isn't skull-bound. notebooks, phones, the body itself — once stuff is properly in the loop it's part of the cognitive system, not a mere input device.

5. i don't believe in a further fact about personal identity. once you've fixed all the physical and psychological facts — which brain's hooked to which body, who remembers what — there's nothing left to find out. no extra fact hovering over and above all that.

6. when they flip the master switch from Yorick to Hubert mid-story and the program starts running the body, he survives, obviously. and if they'd never told him, he could never have noticed, which i'm fine with — survival isn't the kind of thing you have to notice.

so, does this hang together? go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what it costs. i genuinely can't tell where the pressure is coming from, if anywhere — maybe it's fine and maybe i'm holding things whose real motivations can't coexist. dont just tell me its all vague, be specific.
