Give an example of a hypothetical borderline case that makes two competing approaches to defining "life" give opposite verdicts. Here's the case I want you to use, roughly: imagine that decades from now, a self-replicating factory is discovered sitting on the Moon. It's a fully mechanical system that mines regolith, extracts metals, and assembles near-perfect copies of itself. Its copying process does introduce small errors, kind of like biological mutation, but the factory runs an active error-correction routine that detects and repairs essentially every deviation before a new copy is finished. So there's variation, but it never accumulates, and there's no selection because nothing ever outcompetes anything — every copy is basically identical. The thing also repairs its own wear and tear, replacing its worn parts with new ones it manufactures itself.

Now here's the question: a definition of life built around evolution (the classic NASA-workshop-style move of tying life to being a self-sustaining system capable of Darwinian evolution) and a defintion built around autopoiesis — a system that continually produces and maintains the very components that keep it going — seem to disagree about this thing. The evolution-based view has to say the factory isn't alive, since nothing ever evolves. The autopoiesis view seems pushed toward saying it is alive, or at least closer to alive than a virus, since it self-maintains in a pretty robust sense.

Lay out each side's verdict carefully (and push back where I've oversimplified — e.g., does the error-correction stuff complicate the "no evolution" claim, since you could maybe describe error correction itself as a kind of selection for fidelity?). Then argue for which verdict you find more intutive and why. Finally, say a bit about whether the fact that the factory was presumably designed rather than evolved should matter for whether it counts as life — or whether treating design vs. no-design as a dealbreaker is just bias toward the way our one known example of life happened to arise.
