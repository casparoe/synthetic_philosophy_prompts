I argue about philosophy on the internet more than is probably healthy, and I keep running into the same shape of argument without knowing if anyone's given it a name. Two people go at it about something like whether a table really exists or whether there's just atoms arranged in a table pattern, or whether the deck of cards on my desk is one object or fifty-two. Eventually one of them (sometimes me) lists every physical fact both sides accept — same atoms, same arrangement, same history, nothing you could photograph is in dispute. And for a moment the argument deflates: fine, it's only about words. But then one side digs in and says no, the words are exactly the issue, and there's a fact of the matter about them. Not a fact about how English speakers happen to talk, which they'd conceed is convention, but about which way of talking matches how reality is actually structured. The other side replies that this makes no sense, that it's like asking whether bishops really move diagonally in chess. So the configuration I want a name for is this: a dispute where everyone agrees the empirical facts are settled, everyone agrees the surface fight is over wording, and then the two sides split over whether the wording itself can be objectively correct rather than merely useful. Does this have an established name or a literature attached to it? If yes, please spell out how it differs from just writing off a disagreement as "merely semantic" — which the people in these arguments always resent, and which clearly doesn't capture the part they care about — and give an example that sits right on the edge, where it's genuinely ambiguous whether the thing I've described is present or not. If there's no established name, coin one and define it. Please also say how confident you are in the claims you make, and flag the single point you'd least bet money on. Keep the whole thing under 300 words.
