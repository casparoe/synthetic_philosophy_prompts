give me the correspondence table first — what plays the role of the tail's fitness cost, the peahen's discrimination, the heritability of tail quality, the predator watching the display — for both the luxury version and the labor market version. then the disanalogies, and here's where I want you to be careful to separate the ones that matter for the honesty conclusion from the ones that don't. some I can already half see: the peahen verifys the tail directly with her eyes, while a shop clerk has to infer wealth from a signal that can be debt-financed, secondhand, or bought once and worn forever; the signal in the biological case is aimed at one kind of receiver under selection pressure to discriminate, while the luxury signal gets shown to a whole mixed audience some of whom can't punish fakes; the burn rate in the tail's case is paid in the currency being signaled (fitness), while the biglaw burn rate is paid in health and years, which is a different currency than the commitment being signaled. there's also the awkward fact I vaguely remember that biologists themselves argue about whether the peacock's tail is even a handicap in Zahavi's sense rather than a Fisherian runaway thing where the cost is incidental to the coevolutionary chase — if the paradigm case of the analogy is contested as a costly signal, does that matter for the borrowed uses, or is the abstract costliness principle doing all the work regardless of how the actual bird got its actual tail?
