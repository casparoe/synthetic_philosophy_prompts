I'm on my university's animal care and use committee (the IACUC, though our remit got broadened a few years ago to "welfare oversight for research subjects," which turns out to matter here). Two things landed on our plate at once and I need a written opinion to circulate before our meeting on the 14th.

First, a neuro lab wants to run a big learning-and-memory study on fruit flies, somewhere north of 100,000 individuals, with heat shock as the aversive stimulus. No analgesia, obviously -- you can't anesthetize a fly for its whole life, and the standard line has always been that invertebrates outside of cephalopods just aren't covered. But the evidence on nociception in insects has gotten a lot better lately, and a couple of committee members are uneasy rubber-stamping this under a protocol written with mice in mind.

Second, and I swear this is not a joke, an engineer on the committee filed a formal motion asking us to rule on whether the smart HVAC system in the new science building -- the one with the reinforcement-learning thermostat that "gets stressed" when two zones fight over it -- has any moral status at all. His stated purpose is to force us to admit that whatever criteria we use for the flies are graded rather than all-or-nothing, and that once you admit grading, you have to say where the line is and why. Nobody thinks the thermostat matters; everybody is scared of saying why in writing.

So: draft the opinion memo. It should recommend a concrete course of action on both the fly protocol and the thermostat motion, with criteria the committee can actually apply at the next meeting, not just "more research needed." And it has to genuinely engage the losing side -- if you think the flies get partial protection, take seriously the worry that we're licensing unbounded suffering at scale for creatures we'd never treat this way if they were fuzzy; if you think the thermostat motion deserves a "no" without further comment, explain what's wrong with the graded-criteria argument rather than just calling it absurd. Also feel free to flag anything you'd want the committee to defer or hand off, as long as you say to whom and for when. Keep it usable -- we vote Thursday, and half the committee are biologists who will stop reading if it gets too philosophical.
