I've got a draft essay on moral particularism vs. generalism and I want feedback before I go further with it. The core move I'm making: everyone treats the debate as being about whether principles can *guide* right action, but I want to reframe it as being about auditability — principles exist mainly so that other people can check our moral reasoning. On this view the generalist isn't really claiming principles are reliable decision procedures, she's claiming that moral reasons need to be legible across persons, so that members of a community can verify one another's judgements. And the particularist can live with that, if we treat principles as lossy, constantly revised summaries rather than as filters that determine what counts. So I argue the whole fight dissolves once you separate the "who is this reasoning for" question from the "what does it output" question — Dancy's holism (a feature that counts in favour here can count against, or do nothing, elsewhere) bites on the output side but says nothing against the audit side.

The notes I have so far: an intro with the standard framing; the auditability reframing; a section on whether holism really supports my side or just makes the summaries noisier; a section arguing Ross's prima facie duties were already lossy summaries and that Ross half-saw this; an objection section about moral learning — if principles are just audit trails, how do children and novices ever get trained in?; and a conclusion claiming the debate is really a proxy fight about trust and testimony in moral communites.
