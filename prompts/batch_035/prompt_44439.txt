I've been keeping a list of situations where luck seems to change how blameworthy someone is, and I'd like to know whether there's a single principle underneath my reactions to them, or whether some of my reactions simply have to go. My verdicts so far: Two neighbors drive home equally drunk one night; one kills a child, the other finds the road empty — I judge the one who killed far more harshly, and it would feel wrong to me to give him the lighter sentence, even knowing the difference between them was pure luck. A woman who researches poisons, buys one, and mails it to a rival is almost exactly as blameworthy when the package happens to be destroyed in a sorting accident as she would have been had it arrived. Two con artists send out identical fake invoices; one fails only because the bank upgraded its fraud software overnight, the other gets paid — I blame them the same. Reading about occupied countries, I'm convinced that many of the people who despised the collaborators would themselves have collaborated had their own town been occupied, so the difference between the actual collaborator and the safe dissident-at-heart was largely luck of geography — and yet I blame the actual collaborator more, and I can't talk myself out of it. Finally, a man with a placid temperament who never feels the urge to shout at his children seems to me less admirable, not more, than a man with a hair-trigger temper who fights it daily and mostly wins — even though the second man had less control over how he was built.

My first attempt at a principle was that blame should track only what was in the person's control, with luck in outcomes and circumstances cut out entirely; but that fits the poison and fraud cases and not the drunk driver, the collaborator, or the temperament one. My second thought was that for deliberate attempts luck doesn't matter, while for recklessness it does, since how things turned out reveals how bad the risk taken really was — but that feels more like an excuse for my gut than a reason. Could you state a principle, or a small set of candidates, precise enough to decide each of the cases above, tell me honestly which of my verdicts the best candidate cannot save and why, and say what it implies for a case I haven't settled: whether the two drunk drivers' punishments should be set by flipping a coin between the heavy and the light sentence, so that they fare the same on average even though their actual fates differ as much as before?
