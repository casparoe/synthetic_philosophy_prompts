I've been reading a popular book on the limits of scientific knowledge, and its chapter on Nelson Goodman summarizes the "new riddle of induction" roughly as follows:

"Goodman invented a new color word, grue, which applies to anything that is green up until some fixed future date and blue thereafter. Consider any emerald observed so far: it is green, but it is also grue, since the critical date has not yet arrived. So every observation that confirms the hypothesis that all emeralds are green equally confirms the hypothesis that all emeralds are grue, which predicts that emeralds examined after the critical date will come out blue. Because the two hypotheses fit all available evidence exactly equally, Goodman demonstrated that induction has no rational basis: our preference for green over grue is mere habit, exactly as Hume claimed, only now established by a precise counterexample rather than abstract argument. Goodman's own solution was that green is a simpler predicate than grue, so Occam's razor favors green — though this leaves the underlying arbitrariness intact."

I suspect the chapter is unfair to Goodman in places, but I can't locate the errors with any confidence. Would you go through the passage claim by claim and tell me which parts a philosopher of science would accept as stated, which are defensible simplifications, and which are distortions? In particular, I'd like to know: whether the definition of grue given here captures Goodman's actual predicate or quietly omits something essential to how the riddle works; whether Goodman's point really was that induction has no rational basis, or something more modest and more interesting; whether presenting the riddle as just Hume's problem with a counterexample attached conflates two distinct challenges; and whether the account of Goodman's own solution — attributing it to simplicity rather than anything else — and of that solution's supposed failure is accurate. Finally, does the book's broader conclusion, that Goodman demolished the idea that experience can rationally guide our expectations, follow from the parts of the summary that survive correction, or was it resting on the distortions all along?
