I'm presenting a paper in two weeks at a workshop on democratic decision-making and I need a Q&A prep sheet. My thesis, quickly: the usual framing of the discursive dilemma (the three-judge court thing, where majorities line up on each premise but the majority verdict comes out the other way) asks which aggregation procedure a comittee should use, premise-based or conclusion-based, as if the committee decided everything at once. But real bodies decide over time, and order matters. My claim is that when a group takes a straw poll on the overall conclusion before voting premises, the early majority anchors the later premise votes, people drift toward positions consistent with how they already voted, and you get artifical consistency -- so the dilemma partly manufactures itself. My proposal: vote premises first, conclusion last, and ban early straw polls on the conclusion even nonbinding ones.

The audience is about a third formal social choice people who know the aggregation results backwards and will smell anything sloppy, a third deliberative-democracy types who think voting is downstream of talking anyway, and a couple of practitioners who actually facilitate citizens' assemblies and have opinions about what works in the room. Give me the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each camp would actually phrase them, with a two-minute reply to each and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Include the question I'm hoping nobody asks. And at the end, tell me straight: which single objection, if the right person pushed it hard enough, would actually make me give up the anchoring part of the argument, since I'd rather know now than find out in the room.
