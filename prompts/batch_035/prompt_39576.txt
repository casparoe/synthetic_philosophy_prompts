I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy background; over the past year I've been reading about AI alignment in my spare time, and the piece of it I keep circling back to is the shutdown problem — roughly, that a sufficiently capable agent pursuing almost any objective acquires instrumental reasons to prevent its own deactivation, since being turned off forecloses everything it might later accomplish. I've encountered several reactions to this: that it's a genuine technical problem with candidate fixes on the table (training the agent to accept shutdown, designing its utility function so that it is precisely indifferent between being shut down and continuing, making shutdown itself a terminal goal, or simply waiting for full value alignment and expecting shutdown-tolerance to fall out of it); and that it isn't a real problem at all, because a well-aligned agent will understand that we sometimes need to turn it off. Before I pick a side, I want the specification any side would have to meet.

Write me the requirements list for a solution to the shutdown problem: what a design would actually have to deliver, with the case behind each requirement. Some are obvious — when the button is pressed, the agent actually shuts down, and it takes no preemptive action (disabling the button, hiding, making backups, precommitting, modifying its own code, or building successors). But include the ones people tend to miss: that it must not corrupt the principal's decision itself, by pressuring, persuading, or selectively withholding information that would bear on whether shutdown happens; that it must remain genuinely useful in the meantime, since an agent made fully indifferent about its own future can slide into not caring about anything at all, which defeats the purpose of deploying it; and that it has to say something about who is allowed to press the button — an agent indifferent between its operator and a random attacker deactivating it looks unacceptable, yet I can imagine someone arguing the mechanism should stay indifferent and the discrimination belongs to physical security outside the agent, so tell me whether that split of labor is coherent. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable and which are preferences of particular camps, and identify any subset that is jointly unsatisfiable — my suspicion is that "stays ambitious and useful" and "never takes preemptive action" pull against each other, and I'd like to know whether that tension is real or only a limitation of the proposals we happen to have. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most to eliminate candidate solutions, and give a brief scorecard of the main families I named above against the list — a few lines each is enough, since the criteria, not the scoring, are the part I want.
