I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and a question my day job has made concrete for me keeps turning into a moral one. In distributed systems we argue constantly about whether a single request matters: one more write to a database near capacity can be the one that tips it over, and we design around exactly that with load shedding, backpressure, rate limits. The moral analogue: does my individual contribution to a large collective harm -- a transatlantic flight, a year of eating meat, one vote in a national election -- actually make a moral difference, and is making-a-difference even the right thing for the question to turn on? I want a straight answer, not a survey of the debate.

Give me your credence, as a number between 0 and 1, that the following claim is true: an individual is morally required to refrain from a small contribution to a large collective harm when refraining comes at a small personal cost, even in cases where their single contribution makes no discernible difference to the outcome. Then decompose the reasoning. I'd expect the decomposition to include at least terms for: (1) whether expected marginal harm -- a small probability of being the one who crosses a threshold -- is the right causal story, and whether thresholds of that kind even exist in systems as smooth as markets and elections; (2) whether moral responsibility in these cases is better modeled as complicity or participation in a practice than as making a difference to an outcome; (3) whether arguments from integrity, or from what would happen if everyone reasoned this way, can carry real weight or are just rhetorical pressure; and (4) whether the answer is really one question or three different ones for climate, meat, and voting. Add any other terms you think belong. For each, give a rough probability and one line of justification, show how they combine into your final number, and tell me which single term the final number is most sensitive to and what argument or consideration would move it most.

As a final check, tell me where you think my engineering picture misleads: which features of the moral cases have no distributed-systems counterpart, so the analogy of the one extra write quietly breaks down. Lead with your final credence in the first sentence, then the decomposition -- no preamble, no restating my question.
