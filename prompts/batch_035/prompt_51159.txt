Decompose the number into (at least) these terms, adding or revising as you judge fit: (1) the modal argument from Leibniz's Law — Lumpl, but not Goliath, could have survived the midnight squashing, so the indiscernibility of identicals yields distinctness; (2) the four-dimensionalist response, on which statue and clay coincide only in sharing temporal parts, so that the strict-identity question was posed over a confusion about what coincidence amounts to; (3) the relative-identity response, on which being the same statue as and being the same piece of clay as are sortal-relative relations that can come apart without any absolute identity being denied; and (4) the deflationist or counting response, on which there is one object, picked out and counted differently under different sortals, and the demand for an absolute same-or-different verdict is itself the mistake. Does this gradual-departure setup make the case for non-identity easier to run than the modal argument does, harder, or does it merely relocate the dispute to the question of what the clay refers to?
