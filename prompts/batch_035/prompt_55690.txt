My friend and I had a marathon argument last weekend that started with the Needham question (his puzzle about why the scientific and, by extension, industrial revolutions happened in Europe and not China, even though China had been way ahead technologically for most of recorded history) and ended up somewhere totally different. His position was basicaly that the whole "why Britain and not China?" framing is broken. His argument, as best I could reconstruct it: every why-question of the form "why X and not Y?" secretly assumes that X and Y were roughly comparable candidates standing at the same fork, and that's a fiction historians invented after the fact. Britain and China weren't two runners neck and neck where one pulled ahead; they were playing different games on different boards, and asking why one didn't do what the other did is like asking why a dolphin never climbed a tree. My position was that this proves too much — if contrastive why-questions require the alternatives to have been genuine live possibilities in some strong sense, then we couldn't ask why the Allies won and not the Axis, or why I got the job and not the other finalist, and those seem obviously answerable (historians answer things like that all the time). So somewhere between the dolphin and the other job candidate there's a line where "why X and not Y" stops being well-posed and starts being a category error, and neither of us has any idea where that line is or what determines it. We agreed to disagree and he told me to stop complaining and turn it into something, so here I am.

I need a term-paper proposal (one semester, roughly 6000 words, philosophy of history but with a foot in the methodology of economic history) on the following: what does it take for a contrastive explanation request in history to be well-posed, and does the Needham question / Great Divergence debate fall on the well-posed or the ill-posed side of that line? The proposal should state the question precisely; explain what the existing positions leave open (on one side there's the critique that the question is Eurocentric and presupposes a race, on the other side a shrug that counterfactuals are just thought experiments anyway — I want a middle position with actual criteria, not vibes); describe the method — my guess is the core work is getting clear on what the contrast class and the relevance relation have to be for a contrastive question, then stress-testing that against how historians really argue about the divergence (coal, colonies, institutions, whatever), not just toy examples; lay out the possible conclusions and what would count as the project failing; and be honest about whether 6000 words is realistic or whether I should cut it down to one clean case study instead of trying to do the general theory plus the application. Don't bother with a bibliography, I'll track down references myself — just describe positions and arguments rather than citing them. And if you think my friend is actually right and the question is just broken, build that in as one of the live conclusions rather than dodging it.
