I mod a decent-sized online community about risky new tech (AI, geoengineering, gene editing, that sort of thing), and there's a pattern I keep noticing that I cant shake. Whenever a bunch of seperate actors each get to decide on their own whether to do some risky or irreversible thing, and it only takes ONE of them going ahead for the thing to be done — it basically always gets done, even when most of those same people would have voted no if you'd asked them as a group. Each person reasons "if I don't do it, someone else will, and I'd rather it be done my way," or they just never think about the collective side at all. I think I saw a name for this once (unilateralist's curse?) but I don't care about the name, I care about the actual disagreement underneath.

Our community fights about this constantly and I want a sorting quiz to find out what people really believe instead of the labels they throw around. Give me 8-10 questions, each one a concrete scenario with 2-3 answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions. These scenarios have to be in there: a tiny startup launching stuff into the stratosphere to reflect sunlight without asking any government; a researcher who finds a cheap way to make a deadlier flu and has to decide whether to publish it; a company releasing a powerful AI model on the theory that a competitor will just release theirs first anyway; and one person in a group leaking a private document because they think the public deserves to see it. Build the tree from the logical space first (does anyone need permission first, does irreversibility change things, does it matter that the actor pays part of the price too, should the most reckless actor set the pace for everyone else) and then place the named positions in that space — call a leaf "unnamed" if nobody actually holds that combination, and give each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Also flag any two positions your tree can't really tell apart, instead of pretending it can.

Please write it in American English. And at the end, be straight with me about the weak spots: tell me which branch is most likely to sort people wrong, and what a case that breaks the quiz would look like. I'd rather know where it fails than pretend it doesn't.
