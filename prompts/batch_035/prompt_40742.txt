I'm grading a stack of midterms for my phil of mind course (second-year, mostly majors) and there's one answer I keep going back and forth on, so I'd like a second pair of eyes before I commit. The unit covered qualia, physicalism, and the responses to the knowledge argument. The essay question on the exam was this:

"Jackson's Mary case is meant to be a serious threat to physicalism. (i) Set out the knowledge argument as an explicitly valid argument from its premises to the conclusion that physicalism is false, and identify which premise an opponent should press on. (ii) Explain the ability hypothesis: what exactly does it say Mary gains when she leaves the room, and what does it deny she gains? (iii) Evaluate whether the ability hypothesis fully answers the worry that Mary learns some new truth on release."

And here's the student's answer, more or less in full:

"Mary is a brilliant neuroscientist confined from birth to a black and white room, where she learns all the physical facts about color vision — everything about wavelengths, retinas, and the neural processes involved when people see ripe tomatoes and call them red. When she finally steps out and sees red for the first time, she learns what it's like to see red, which she plainly did not know before. The argument is: (1) before release Mary knows all the physical facts; (2) after release she gains knowledge of something she lacked; (3) so there are facts she did not know, and these cannot be physical facts; therefore physicalism is false. The premise to press on is (2), since the physicalist will deny that anything genuinely new was learned. Jackson introduced this case in his paper What Mary Didn't Know, where he also argued that qualia are epiphenomenal.

The ability hypothesis, developed by Nemirow and Lewis, says that what Mary gains on release is not information but a set of abilities: the ability to recognize red experiences, to imagine red, and to remember them. Knowing what it's like is know-how, like knowing how to ride a bike, rather than knowledge of some fact. So on this view Mary learns nothing at all on leaving the room — she just acquires skills she previously lacked. In effect the ability hypothesis is the old-fact-new-concept idea: Mary already knew all the relevant facts, and what she gains is just a new way of thinking about facts she already had. Since no new fact and no new truth is acquired, premise (2) is false and the argument is blocked. So the knowledge argument fails, physicalism is safe, and notably Jackson himself later gave up the argument."
