Hofstadter has a toy world he calls the Careenium (pun on cranium): a huge billiard table packed with tiny magnetic balls he calls simms, bouncing around and clumping together. Zoom out and you see stable-ish clumps of simms — simmballs — that come apart and re-form as the substrate keeps careening around. The table's walls pass information about the outside world into the system (their bounciness varies with what's out there), and the simmball-level dynamics end up mirroring that outside stuff in complicated ways. Hofstadter's question is: inside the Careenium, who pushes whom around? Every collision, taken one at a time, is simms hitting simms — a simmball is never anything over and above simms in a region, and no simmball literally touches anything. But the honest explanations of what happens next — a bank shot, some cluster getting smashed because of what the walls just announced — live entirely at the simmball level: simmballs push each other around. He maps this to neurons vs. symbols/concepts in the brain, with the self being one big simmball representing the whole system. And there's a worrying reading, which I gather is a real position in philosophy (epiphenomenalism, or the causal exclusion problem — something like that): all the causal work drains down to the substrate, and the symbol level is a bookkeeping device we happen to like.
