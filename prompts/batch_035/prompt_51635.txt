so here's my situation: i'm a backend engineer, zero philosophy training, but i fell down a rabbit hole a while back and now i kind of can't stop thinking about it. i have a little team blog (mostly other engineers, also no philosophy background) and i want to publish a "which view of intentionality do you actually hold?" quiz. i'm hoping you can write it.

the rabbit hole, in case it matters for tone: the whole thing that hooked me was Dennett's example of the two-bitser — some old vending machine that dispenses gum when you feed it a quarter. Dennett says the machine, in a stretched but real sense, "takes" the input to be a quarter, and asks what happens if the coin gets demonetized or someone finds a slug that works: is the machine now holding a false belief, like a kid who thinks fool's gold is gold? Searle's response, roughly, is that this is all a trick of language — any "aboutness" the machine has is borrowed from the engineers who designed it, and borrowed aboutness isn't the real thing. brains (via whatever consciousness does in them) have original intentionality; everything else just has derived intentionality, handed down like a photocopy of a photocopy. and Dennett's counter-counter, again roughly, is that if you're strict about that, you can't even say the engineers' intentionality is original in the way Searle wants — it all gets handed down from evolution, training, culture, whatever, so maybe the "original vs derived" line is the wrong way to carve it and we should just ask when belief-talk pays for itself as a predictive tool.

what i want from you:

- a quiz with 7 questions. each one should be a concrete case with 2 or 3 answer options. the cases should include the two-bitser (or a close modern cousin, like a vending machine that takes a defunct coin), but also spread wider: a thermostat, a chess engine, a spam filter, maybe an animal, maybe a person reading a menu in a language they barely know. feel free to pick different ones if mine are bad, but keep the vending machine in — it's the hook for my readers.
- the key constraint: nobody taking this quiz should need to have heard of Searle or Dennett to answer any question. no question can be "do you side with Searle that intentionality is original or derived" — that's sorting people by which label they like. the questions have to be cases where the underlying positions genuinely come apart, so the answer pattern reveals the view rather than asking for it. the philosophy should be doing its work behind the scenes.
- a scoring key at the end that maps answer patterns onto positions: at minimum Searle's original-only view, Dennett's deflationary/everything-is-derived view, and whatever middle positions the logical space actually allows (e.g., views where aboutness comes in degrees or depends on how the system got its structure, not just who's observing it). name the leaves whatever makes sense — real names where they exist, "unnamed" where they don't.
- build the tree from the questions first, then locate the positions in the space, and if some combination of answers corresponds to a view that nobody actually defends, say so and hazard a guess why (incoherent? just unattractive? nobody got around to it?).
- also flag honestly if there's a pair of views your quiz can't cleanly seperate — like if two genuinely different positions would give all the same answers — instead of pretending the key is sharper than it is. i'd rather post a quiz with a disclaimer than one that lies to my readers.

length-wise, this is for a blog post, so it should be readable in one sitting but doesn't need to be tweetable. and keep the tone accessible — my readers are engineers, so analogies to code, systems, or ML models are fair game and probably welcome, but don't assume anyone knows the jargon.
