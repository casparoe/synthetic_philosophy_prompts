I write a small newsletter at the intersection of biology and philosophy of science, and for an upcoming issue I want a "which definition of life do you actually hold?" quiz. The idea came out of an arguement with a friend who's a virologist — she swears viruses are alive, and I kept noticing that whatever her reasons are, they can't be the same ones she'd use for a bacterium, so now I'm curious what people actually say about cases rather than which label they like. The quiz should have around ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. The cases should include at least some of the following, and you can add your own: a virus; a mule (sterile, but it metabolizes just fine); a bacterial spore that's sat dormant for thousands of years and could still germinate; a prion; a computer program that copies itself, mutates, and competes with other programs for memory inside a simulation; a self-replicating clay crystal that grows, splits, and templates further crystals but does no chemistry of its own; a synthetic minimal cell built from scratch with a fully designed genome; and an engineered system that metabolizes and maintains itself beautifully but is one of a kind, with no reproduction at all.

Then I need a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions: vitalism (probably nobody will land there, but include it for completeness), self-organization/autopoiesis-style views where being alive means being a self-maintaining, autonomous organism, thermodynamic or dissipative-structure views, evolutionary/Darwinian definitions along the lines of the NASA working definition, and the deflationary option that "life" has no essence at all and functions more like a family-resemblance or folk category. No question should just ask "do you think life requires reproduction?" — the reader has to be able to answer without knowing which position it's steering them toward. That's the whole point: sorting readers by what they say about cases, not by which school they'd claim to belong to.

Two more things. First, please flag which pairs of positions the quiz can't really seperate — I have a suspicion that autopoiesis and metabolism-first views give all the same answers on my cases, and if so I'd rather know that than have the scoring key pretend otherwise. Second, tell me whether any combination of answers is coherent but occupied by nobody, and if so, whether that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. And write everything in American English, please — I have a couple of Canadian readers who will not let me hear the end of it if I mix in British spellings.
