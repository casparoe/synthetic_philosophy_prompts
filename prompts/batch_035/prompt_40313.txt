hey, so i'm a software engineer with zero formal philosophy background, but i fell down a rabbit hole the other week and now i want a proper essay about it.

the thing that got me: the colour phi phenomenon. you flash a dot of one colour on a screen, then a dot of a *different* colour a bit to the side, maybe a hundred-odd milliseconds apart. people reliably report seeing ONE dot gliding across and changing colour halfway along its path. the spooky bit is that the colour change can only be known *after* the second flash lands — so the earlier half of the experience, the "dot moving before the colour flip", seems to have been fabricated retroactively. the brain apparently decides later what you experienced earlier, and nobody notices.

so Dennett (and Kinsbourne, i think) said you can basically tell two stories about this, and they named them after political systems, of all things. in the Orwellian story (like the ministry of truth in 1984 rewriting old newspaper articles), you first consciously experience the dot moving in the original colour, then the second flash arrives, the record gets overwritten with "it changed colour halfway", and you're left with a false memory of having seen the flip live. in the Stalinesque story (like a show trial where the verdict is decided before anything is broadcast), consciousness is on a delay: nothing is experienced at all until the brain already has both flashes in hand, and the feed is edited before it ever hits the "screen" of awareness. same final report, completely different metaphysics underneath.

here's my engineer's take, and i want you to engage with it. to me this dichotomy smells like something i deal with every day. when a CPU speculatively executes a mispredicted branch and then rolls back the architectural state, was the instruction "really" executed? if you ask "was the experience in the buffer before the commit", my instinct is that the question is malformed — there's no fact of the matter until something reads the state out. but maybe that analogy is too cute and one side of the dichotomy is genuinely correct. maybe there really is a moment where content gets written for good, and it matters morally and practically (pain that gets overwritten vs pain that never airs at all?? that seems like it should matter!).

so: write an essay on which story, if either, is right about the timing of experience. cover the actual arguments — why Dennett thought the two versions are indistinguishable from the inside, what the Libet-style timing stuff (readiness potentials, the old half-second-delay claims) does or doesn't add, and whether the whole question is just badly posed from the start. but don't cop out with a wishy-washy "it depends" — actually defend a verdict. and please tell me straight whether my speculative-execution analogy is doing real philosophical work or whether it falls apart somewhere i cant see.
