I'm a PhD student working at the intersection of decision theory and ethics, on whether the better-than relation is transitive. I've catalogued my verdicts on a set of cases, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, captures them — and which of my verdicts has to go if none does.

First: between two cups of coffee that differ by a single grain of sugar, I judge neither better than the other — and that holds all along a grain-by-grain series. Second: the first and last members of that series (unsweetened versus thoroughly sweetened) are plainly different in betterness. Third: with headaches I judge differently — one second of mild headache is strictly worse than none, and two seconds strictly worse than one — so for headaches, tiny differences are real differences, while for coffee they seem to be nothing at all. Fourth: I'm indifferent between a job paying 60,000 a year with a 30-minute commute and one paying 60,100 with a 35-minute commute, but across a hundred such steps I take the start of the chain to be clearly better than the end. Fifth: I hold that better-than itself — not merely rational preference — must be transitive, because intransitive betterness licenses money pumps and dominance arguments that seem to indict the relation itself, not just our grasp of it. Sixth: confronted with the prospect of trading down through the whole coffee series one grain at a time, I'd refuse the package while being happy to accept any single trade — and I'm unsure whether that shows my adjacent verdicts are incoherent or whether there's a legitimate policy-level distinction here.

Can these verdicts be unified — for instance, by treating adjacent cases as on a par or as involving indeterminate betterness, or by allowing better-than to be nontransitive while remaining acyclic? If not, which verdict should I surrender, and what would I be giving up in doing so? Please also tell me what the resulting principle predicts for a case I haven't considered: a spectrum in which each life is worse than the next by a diminishing amount, converging toward a limit — is there a life in the sequence that is best, and what does my answer commit me to regarding the transitivity question?
