My friend and I spent most of last night arguing about whether it means anything to say that this particular coin — which I will flip exactly once and then melt down — has a probability of one half of landing heads. She kept insisting the coin's probability is an average over an ensemble of similar flips, while I wanted to say the chance is somehow in the physics of this flip, and we eventually realized we disagreed about questions well upstream of the one we started with. So: please build a decision tree that sorts a person into a position on the interpretation of probability. Constraints: every internal node must be a question that someone with an introductory statistics course but no philosophy of probability can answer from their own reaction to concrete cases (the single coin flip, the decay of one particular uranium atom in the next hour, a freshly minted die whose bias has never been tested, tomorrow's election result), and no node may mention the name of a school. The leaves must include long-run frequentism, single-case propensity views, and subjective Bayesianism, plus whatever further leaves the logical space forces — I want the tree built from the questions first and the named positions placed into it afterward, not the other way around. Aim for six to eight leaves. Finally, please flag explicitly any pair of positions the tree cannot separate (for instance, whether it can distinguish a hypothetical-frequentist from someone who takes long-run frequencies as directly constitutive), and identify any combination of node answers that no camp occupies, saying in one sentence whether the gap is due to incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect.
