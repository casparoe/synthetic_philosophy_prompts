Write a dialogue between a Dennett-style quiner (qualia are an incoherent posit, a philosopher's fiction that falls apart when you poke at it) and a defender of qualia. Set it around the coffee taster case: two tasters at Maxwell House, Chase and Sanborn, both of whom drift from liking the coffee to disliking it over years of tasting. Chase insists his qualia changed -- the coffee just tastes different to him now. Sanborn insists his qualia never changed -- he still gets the same taste, he just no longer likes it. The quiner should use the case to argue that this distinction (change in experience vs change in judgment about the experience) is not just hard to draw from the outside but indeterminate even from the inside, since memory of how things used to taste shifts along with everything else, so there is no fact of the matter left for "what the taste is like" to refer to. The defender should push back for real -- e.g. that epistemic inaccessibility doesn't entail metaphysical nonexistence, that Chase and Sanborn are surely both wrong about *something* and something experience-like has to fill that slot, or a fallback to qualia as response-dependent properties rather than intrinsic ineffable ones. Keep both of them sharp, no strawmen, and no easy reconciliation at the end -- let it end with the strongest remaining point on each side. Make it a real back-and-forth where each actually responds to what the other just said, not two monologues taking turns.
