I've been going down a rabbithole on Parfit's teletransporter thought experiments and my partner and I genuinely can't stop arguing about it. Quick setup so we're on the same page: the machine on Earth scans you, destroys your body, and constructs a molecule-for-molecule replica on Mars that wakes up with all your memories. Parfit's take (in Reasons and Persons) is roughly that personal identity is not what matters in survival — psychological continuity is what matters — so the teletransporter preserves what matters even if, strictly speaking, "you" don't survive.

Here's where we actually disagree. My credence that psychological continuity, rather than identity itself, is what matters in survival is about 0.65; hers is more like 0.25. She keeps returning to two cases. One is the branching case: if the machine malfunctions and produces two replicas on Mars, psychological continuity is fully preserved in each, but it seems that at most one of them can be you — and she thinks the fact that identity seems to settle what to say in branching shows that identity matters, full stop. The other is a case we sort of invented: suppose the replica gets your memories but only about 70% of your values, dispositions, and personality, so the continuity is partial. If what matters comes in degrees the way Parfit seems to suggest, there should be some threshold at which you'd rationally agree to death-by-scanner to get a 70%-you walking around on Mars, and she finds that insane. Honestly I'm not sure I do find it insane, which she also finds insane.

So give me your actual number for the claim that psychological continuity, not identity, is what matters in survival. But decompose it properly: the probability that identity is even a determinate all-or-nothing relation rather than a convention we project; the probability that the branching case really shows identity and what-matters come apart instead of just showing our concept breaks down there; the probability that partial-continuity cases can be handled without an arbitrary cutoff; plus whatever other terms you think belong, each with a rough probability and a one-line justification, and then the combination. Finally, tell me which sub-question you're least confident about, which of the two of us (me at 0.65, her at 0.25) it would most likely pull apart from the other, and what thought experiment would actually move your number the most.
