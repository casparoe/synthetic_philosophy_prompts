Here's my situation: I accept that you can't derive an ought from an is, in the Hume sense, but when I look at actual arguments, my gut keeps approving inferences that look exactly like deriving oughts from ises. So here are my verdicts on specific arguments, each starting from pure facts and ending with a normative conclusion:

1. This knife is dull / You ought to sharpen it (given that you're going to cut the bread). -- I think this one is fine, totally fine, and it barely even feels like an ought. But I can't shake the feeling that a goal got smuggled in with that parenthetical.

2. It's illegal to jaywalk in this city / You ought not jaywalk. -- Not fine, obviously. Law is just a fact.

3. Humans evolved to be mostly self-interested / We ought to be self-interested. -- Not fine, classic mistake.

4. She promised to drive him to the airport / She ought to drive him. -- Fine?? This one feels fine to me and it's bugging me because on its face it has the same shape as 2 and 3: a fact about what happened, then an ought. If anything the promise fact seems LESS "oughty" than the law fact.

5. Setting this cat on fire would cause enormous suffering for no benefit whatsoever / You ought not set the cat on fire. -- Fine, and not just fine, but I'd say anyone who accepts the premise and rejects the conclusion is being irrational, not just immoral. And I can't for the life of me see what premise is being smuggled in here the way it is in 1.

6. In our community the word "wrong" just means causing gratuitous harm; what he did caused gratuitous harm; therefore what he did was wrong. -- I flip-flop on this. Some days it looks fine but trivially so (it's just definition-juggling, the normativity evaporates), other days I think it's actually the only legitimate route from facts to values.

So my question is: is there ONE principle that gets all these verdicts? My tentative guess is something like "the inference is ok when the factual premise somehow already contains the normative conclusion, and fails when it doesn't" -- but that's so circular it embarrasses me.
