A friend and I keep arguing about whether the physical Church-Turing thesis is a substantive empirical claim or something close to a truism, and rather than a survey of the literature I want your actual credence. By the physical version I mean: every process that can be physically instantiated in our universe exhibits only Turing-computable input-output behavior. Give me a number, decomposed into at least the following terms, each with its own probability and a one-line justification:

1. The probability that our best current physics, taken at face value, already entails the thesis, versus leaving room for non-computable dynamics — for instance through idealizations that treat certain physical quantities as exactly real-valued rather than as finitely specifiable.
2. The probability that the known candidate loopholes are physically realizable rather than artifacts of idealization — e.g. spacetimes in which an observer could, in finite proper time, learn the outcome of an unboundedly long computation, or idealized systems whose evolution would encode the halting problem — given that these seem to demand infinite resources, exact measurements, or conditions other parts of physics exclude.
3. The probability that future physics will introduce genuinely non-computable laws, as opposed to merely different computable ones.

Then tell me how you combine the terms, which single term the final number is most sensitive to, and what evidence or result would move it most. End with a line of the form \answer{<number between 0 and 1>} giving your overall credence.
