I've been reading a popular book on quantum foundations that offers the following summary of the debate over the status of the quantum state, and I'd like a careful audit of it before I repeat any of it to anyone:

"For a century, physicists have split into two camps over what the wavefunction is. The psi-epistemic camp — the tradition of Bohr and Einstein — holds that the wavefunction is like a probability distribution in classical statistical mechanics: it describes our ignorance of physical facts that are really there but unknown to us. The psi-ontic camp holds instead that the wavefunction is itself part of physical reality. In 2012, a theorem by Pusey, Barrett, and Rudolph finally settled the matter: any theory in which the wavefunction represents mere information about an underlying physical state is logically inconsistent with the predictions of quantum theory. So the wavefunction is real, full stop. And since the wavefunction of even two particles lives in a six-dimensional configuration space, the theorem tells us that reality itself is an enormous high-dimensional space rather than the three-dimensional world of everyday appearance."

I have the impression that something in this is too clean — that the theorem is real and important but that the book has flattened something in the telling. Please go through the passage sentence by sentence and tell me: what would a physicist working on quantum foundations accept as stated, what is a defensible simplification, and what is a misrepresentation a specialist would object to? In particular, I'd like to know (a) whether the psi-epistemic/psi-ontic distinction is being used in its technical sense here or in a looser everyday sense, and whether that matters; (b) whether the theorem's result really applies to "any theory" of the kind described, or whether it rests on assumptions the book has quietly omitted; and (c) whether the final inference about the dimensionality of reality actually follows from the theorem, or whether it is a further interpretive step beyond what was established. I'm not looking for a defense of either camp — just an accuracy check I can trust before I pass the summary along.
