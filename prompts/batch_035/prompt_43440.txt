i'm a journalist (science and ideas, mostly) and i just turned in a longread on the Fermi paradox for our ideas section. my editor wants to close it with a poem instead of the usual pull-quote, and since the piece already has enough versions of the Drake equation scattered through it, i figure a villanelle is the move.

the two refrains should be the two halves of the paradox itself. one line is a version of the question Fermi famously blurted out at that lunch in 1950 — you know the one, the where-is-everybody question — and please keep it blunt, don't dress it up in italics or make it sound oracular. the other refrain should be some version of the claim that the silence itself is the only data we have. the repeating form can do the real argumentative work here: the two claims come back turn after turn, both apparently true, neither one ever answering the other, until the final couplet forces them to finally sit next to each other and rhyme — which, and this is the part i like, is exactly our epistemic situation. the two facts have to coexist and we don't get to know how.

the middle stanzas should walk through the standard escape hatches more or less in order — life is everywhere but intelligence is a fluke, they're out there but quiet on purpose, they were out there and are already gone — but they should feel like stanzas of a poem, not a listicle with line breaks. and i'd rather the poem not pick a winner between the great filter being behind us (intelligence happened exactly once, by accident) and ahead of us (everybody who gets this far wipes themselves out), because that's genuinely the open question and my piece shouldn't pretend to settle it in the last 40 words.

someone who knows the paradox should recognize both refrains immediately and be able to say which is which and why they never resolve. someone who doesn't should just get a haunting poem about asking a question that nobody answers. if you want to gesture at the drake equation being a way of organizing our ignorance rather than an actual calculation, that'd be a nice bonus, but it's not required. last thing: don't make it rhyme like a greeting card, and don't let the ending be consoling. "maybe we're the first" is not a comfort, it's a responsibility, and i'd like the poem to know the difference.
