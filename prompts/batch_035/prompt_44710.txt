I'm in a grad reading group on self-locating belief and we keep just re-enacting the standard halfer/thirder shouting match every week, so I want to try something narrower. Consider a variant: the classic Sleeping Beauty experiment, except the coin is NOT flipped on Sunday. Instead it's flipped Monday night, while she sleeps after the Monday awakening. Monday waking is guaranteed regardless; if it lands Heads she sleeps through Tuesday, if Tails she's woken Tuesday too, and she's never told which day it is (so on Monday she can't tell whether the flip has even happened yet — it hasn't). Call her late-flip Beauty. Half my group says the physical timing of a fair coin flip obviously can't make a difference to her rational credence, so her Monday credence in Heads had better be whatever you'd say it is in the ordinary setup. The other half insists the timing matters enormously, because on Monday, before the flip, there is no possible way for her to get any evidence either way — no uncentered evidence, no awakening-as-evidence — so she should still be at 1/2, whatever you think about the standard case.

Please make an argument for the flip-timing irrelevance claim: that late-flip Beauty's Monday credence in Heads should equal whatever ordinary Beauty's Monday credence in Heads should be, so the Sunday-flip and Monday-flip versions of the experiment are, for the purposes of rational credence, the same problem. Along the way, explain what this commitment costs each camp: does a halfer who accepts irrelevance get to dodge the famous problems (like the Tuesday-awakening Dutch bookish worries), and does a thirder who accepts it have to say something uncomfortable about how Beauty could have self-locating evidence about a coin that hasn't even been flipped yet? Feel free to bring in whatever machinery you find most honest — centered worlds, evidential vs. causal conditioning, the "beauty learns she exists" move — but don't just name-drop, actually use it in an inferential step.
