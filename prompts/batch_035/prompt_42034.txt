A friend of mine who's pretty libertarian keeps saying healthcare should just work like any other consumer market: buying an appendectomy is like buying a laptop or a car — you read reviews, compare prices, and competition drives quality up and costs down, so the reason US healthcare is a mess is that we've layered insurance and regulation on top so nobody ever sees a price. He thinks price transparency plus health savings accounts would fix most of it. I'm a software engineer, no philosophy or econ background, and I keep feeling like the analogy is doing a lot of work without anyone saying exactly what. Can you audit it? Like, give me the correspondence table — what plays the role of the product, the review site, the competitor, the repeat customer — then where the mapping actually breaks (I'm guessing emergencies and the fact that you can't exactly return a surgery, but there's probably more). Distinguish the disanalogies that actually sink his market conclusion from the ones that don't matter much, and tell me whether the conclusion survives once you put them on the table. And if there's a better analogy for healthcare markets — people sometimes compare it to a fire department or a utility — does that one hold up any better, or does it smuggle in its own stuff? He also says the fact that I can't predict when I'll get sick is irrelevant because that's what insurance is for, so tell me if that rescue works.
