My wife and I just went through IVF and something about the paperwork got me thinking. The clinic does genetic testing on the embryos, ranks them by health stuff, and hands us a report so we can pick which one to transfer — all totally standard, no ethics lecture attached. But on the same form there's a part saying they won't do sex selection (illegal where we live I think), and obviously zero chance they'd help you pick an embryo for deafness. So the clinic will happily rank embryos on medical grounds and give the parents the choice, but draws a hard line at choosing on other grounds. What's the actual principle underneath that? Does the routine practice of ranking embryos presuppose some view of what parents owe their future kid — like a duty to pick the healthiest one, not just permission to? And if so, why doesn't the same logic make sex selection, or someday picking for intelligence, just more of the same — why is the line where it is? The non-identity thing occured to me too: the kid born from the chosen embryo couldn't have existed otherwise, so it's not like the unchosen embryos get wronged. I don't want a survey of positions here — take an actual stance on what the practice commits you to, whether the clinic's implicit theory is coherent or just vibes, and where you'd draw the line and why.
