My flatmate and I have spent the better part of a semester arguing about self-locating belief — it started when a seminar I'm taking on anthropic reasoning got to the Sleeping Beauty problem and never really moved on — and to try to settle who is being consistent, I've been writing down my gut verdicts on a series of cases. The original problem first: Beauty is woken once if a fair coin lands heads, twice if it lands tails, with amnesia in between, and on waking I'm fairly confident her credence in heads should be one third. A variant where the coin is biased two-to-one in favor of heads: I still say one half. A technicolor variant where she is woken on both days no matter what, but on tails the Tuesday room is painted red: if she wakes in an unpainted room, I say one half. And a duplication variant where, on tails, the lab also creates an indistinguishable copy of her and wakes them both: I say one third heads, even though I admit I can't tell whether I'm sympathizing with the original or the copy in my own imagining.

The case where my flatmate thinks I've given the game away is cosmic. Suppose there are two theories of the universe, equally well supported by all our physics and assigned equal prior probabilities, except that one posits a trillion observers and the other a trillion trillion. My verdict is that we should not end up nearly certain of the crowded theory just because it contains more observers — that feels like cheating, like letting the size of a hypothesis vote for itself. My flatmate says my Beauty verdicts and my cosmology verdicts can't all be true of one principle: whatever makes the duplicate case come out one third, she says, is the very same thing that would make me nearly certain the universe is crowded, and my refusal to follow it there is special pleading. I have a suspicion she's half right but hasn't located the seam.

Here is the case I keep going back and forth on, which is why I'm writing to you rather than continuing to shout across the kitchen. Suppose that on heads I am woken once, on tails I am woken twice, but that on tails the lab also runs the same protocol on one hundred other subjects, each woken twice with amnesia. My own memories and experiences are exactly as they would have been had the others never been enrolled. Should the hundred other people's awakenings drag my credence in tails from two thirds of the way toward certainty, leave it exactly where it was, or something in between? Please tell me what principle or small set of candidate principles captures my verdicts; state each one precisely enough to compute with; say which of my verdicts each principle must overturn, and whether the cost of saving them all is a hopelessly gerrymandered rule; give your best guess at the verdict I should have on the hundred-others case and why; and tell me what further case, which I haven't thought of, would most cleanly decide between the surviving candidates.
