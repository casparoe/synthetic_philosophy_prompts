So, background first: I'm a machinist, no schooling past high school, but I read a ton of pop science and somewhere along the way I ended up with a set of views about how the different levels of the world fit together, and I want to know if they actually fit or if I'm holding things that can't all be true at once. Here's the list, roughly in the order I thought of them:

1. Everything that happens, happens physically. If you could freeze the whole universe, note the position of every particle, and know the laws, you could run it forward and nothing would be missing. No ghost has to lean in from outside to get anything over the line.

2. I'm not a "physics or nothing" guy about science, though. Weather, evolution, recessions — these have their own vocabularies and their own regularities, and I don't think they'll ever be rewritten in particle talk. Not "too hard in practice" — wrong thing to want, like asking for the weather report as a list of particle positions. Biology isn't unfinished physics.

3. Higher-level stuff really does cause things. The recession caused the unemployment. My deciding to write this caused me to write it. I don't think those sentences are shorthand or polite fictions — they're true the same way "the spark caused the fire" is true.

4. But nothing reaches down. The economy doesn't push individual transactions around from above; whatever happens at the high level gets done entirely through the bottom. When I raise my arm, the decision doesn't shove any molecules — it's the same event described twice.

5. And sometimes the higher-level answer isn't just more convenient, it's more correct. If the investigators ask why the bridge fell, "metal fatigue" is a better answer than "the positions of every particle at the big bang." Whoever gives the particle answer hasn't given the same answer at higher resolution, they've failed to answer.

Then some smaller ones. I think temperature really did turn out to be average molecular motion, and that was a discovery, not a relabeling — but I don't expect that kind of ending for economics, and I don't think that bothers me. And the placebo effect is real: expecting to get better measurably helps you get better, the expectation is part of the cause, and I don't think that's spooky either.

People keep saying "emergence" about all this and I've started to suspect the word is doing more work than any actual explanation is. So: which of these hang together, which ones pull against eachother, and where's the cheapest fix? I have a feeling something in the middle of all this is off in a way I can't put my finger on, so I'd rather you tell me what you think the real problem is than just hand me a list of worries. Write it as plain prose please — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just paragraphs like a long email.
