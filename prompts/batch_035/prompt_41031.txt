I've been trying to figure out what I actually think probability *is*, not from textbooks but from my gut reactions to probability claims in the wild. Me and my housemate (she's doing a stats degree, thinks this is all trivial, which made me want to sort it out more) went back and forth and here's roughly where I've landed on a bunch of cases. "The probability this coin lands heads is 1/2" — that just seems true, full stop, like a fact about the coin. If someone says it's 0.9 they're not having a different perspective, they're wrong. But "given the evidence, the probability the butler did it is 0.7" feels completely different: if I've read the police report and you haven't, we can give different numbers and neither of us is wrong, it's about our states of knowledge. Then there's "this uranium atom has a 50% chance of decaying in the next hour" — that one feels like it's *in the atom*, a real tendency it has, not in anyone's head, even though it's a one-off event so there's no frequency to point to.

But here's where I get tangled. A rocket engineer says "95% chance the first launch of this new design succeeds" — structurally that looks just like the uranium case (single event, no frequency), but the number feels way more made-up, like a conventional score the engineer assigns rather than something the rocket has inside it. And yet a weather forecaster's "70% chance of rain tomorrow" feels legitimate again — maybe because the forecaster has a track record of these percentages coming true about 70% of the time, and the engineer's 95% has never been tested? Though I'm honestly not sure that difference should matter, since the forecast for *tomorrow specifically* is still one event. Also: "the probability dinosaurs were warm-blooded is maybe 30%" — the past is settled, there's a fact of the matter one way or the other, so there's no 'tendency' for it to happen and no frequency; but it also doesn't feel like mere personal confusion in the way the butler case sort of does. And then the coin case from before nags at me, because my reason for saying 1/2 is basically just ignorance plus symmetry, which is exactly the kind of reasoning I'd use on the dinosaur question too — so how was the coin one a fact about the world?

So: is there one account of what probability is that fits all these verdicts, or do I have to accept that 'probability' is several different things wearing the same word? If you think there's a unified principle (maybe with a twist, like 'probability claims are about the world when they're grounded in a stable mechanism and about us otherwise'), state it precisely, show me which verdicts motivate which clause, and be honest about which of my verdicts it can't accommodate — I'd rather overturn one of my gut calls than bolt on a fourth kind of probability. Also tell me what your account says about a case I haven't listed: a pollster saying "there's a 60% chance the incumbent wins the election" — and about the rocket-vs-forecaster pair, which is the one I keep flip-flopping on.
