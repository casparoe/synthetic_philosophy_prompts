I keep running into the same move in arguments about whether AI systems deserve moral consideration, and I want to know if it has a name. Someone says a language model might be having something like experiences, and the reply is always a mechanism: it's just matrix multiplications, it's just trained to predict the next token, there's no little person inside looking at the outputs. And the weird part is that the mechanistic description gets treated as if it settles the moral question by itself -- as if once you can say what the system is "really" doing, down at the level where it's all arithmetic, nothing morally interesting could be happening there. But nobody accepts this in the other direction for brains (the brain is just ion channels flipping, therefore no moral status), so it can't literally be the mechanism-level description doing the work. Is there an established concept for this move, where explaining how something works gets treated as explaining away what it is? A second, related thing I've noticed: even people who accept that computations can matter morally can't agree on which ones do -- like whether the same weights running in full precision vs heavily quantized, or on a GPU vs some brain-inspired chip, should count differently even when the outward behavior is identical. Does that boundary-drawing problem have its own name or literaure?
