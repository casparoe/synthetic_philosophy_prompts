So, six months ago I moved to Berlin, and it's made me realize I have opinions about privacy from the government that I never knew I had and can't fully justify. The trigger was my flatmate Lena being genuinely horrified that I'd given a fake birthdate to a drugstore loyalty app. Meanwhile I was horrified that I had to walk into a government office within two weeks of moving and tell a clerk where I live (the Anmeldung — they're serious about it, it's literally the law, you get fined otherwise). And my brother back in Texas thinks all of it is paranoid: his face is on six different platforms, he pays everything by card, and he does not care at all. And then you read about the social credit stuff in China and suddenly everyone on the American side of my family is an expert on why you can never give the state anything.

I've been trying to work out my actual position from the bottom up, case by case, going by gut. Here are my verdicts:

1. Fake birthdate to a drugstore app: fine, obviously, almost virtuous.
2. Keeping my officially registered address at a friend's place while actually living in an unregistered sublet because I don't want the record: fine, and the subletter who asked me not to register is being reasonable.
3. Underreporting freelance income on my tax return: not fine, full stop.
4. Lying to the census person about how many people live here (it's used to allocate funding and seats): fine.
5. Paying cash at the pharmacy so the purchase never shows up in my bank record: fine, and honestly I get a little smug glow doing it.
6. Blurring my building on street view: fine, but also feels kind of pointless.
7. Lying to a cop who knocks on the door and asks whether my flatmate is home (no warrant, flatmate is a witness, not a suspect): not fine? I think? I'd feel like a jerk doing it, but I go back and forth.
8. Wearing a mask at a demonstration where the city has face-recognition cameras and covering your face happens to be banned: fine.
9. Stripping metadata so nobody, state or otherwise, can see who I email: fine even though I'm doing nothing wrong — that's kind of the point? but I can't fully defend this one.

The thing I can't square is 2, 3, and 4 against each other. The tax office and the census bureau are both the democratic state asking me for facts on an official form, and I only feel bound to tell the truth to one of them. And my gut also says hiding from the German state is fine, hiding from something like the Chinese system would be basically heroic, and my brother's total openness is naive — which suggests the principle is something like "hide from bad regimes, cooperate with decent ones." But then I'd have to say the census lie is wrong (I don't believe that) or that Germany counts as a bad regime (I also don't believe that).

Is there a single principle, or maybe a couple of candidates, that gets all or most of these verdicts? Please say which case forces which clause, what it says about the case I keep flip-floping on (7), and be honest about which of my verdicts you'd throw out to make the rest fit. Also curious what it predicts for a case I haven't mentioned: whether I should give my real phone number to the gym.
