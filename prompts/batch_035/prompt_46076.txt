You are a professional philosopher of mind who has spent the past two decades working on the naturalization of intentionality, and who has served on grant panels evaluating philosophy-of-mind proposals. A funder is prepared to support eight early-career researchers for four years on teleosemantics and the broader project of naturalizing mental content. Rather than a survey, I need a defended research agenda: which open problems should those researchers prioritize, and in what order?

For context, the candidates I am aware of include: the indeterminacy or "disjunction" problem, i.e., why does the frog's state represent flies rather than flies-or-small-dark-moving-specks; the swampman-style objection that etiological theories ground content in histories that are unnecessary for, or too contingent to, content itself; the question of which selection processes generate functions, and whether there is a principled way to individuate them (lineage-level selection, token-level selection, learning within an organism); and whether function pluralism, or the abandonment of a single notion of content, is a solution or a capitulation. There is also a more applied question some funders find attractive: whether teleosemantics can be made to yield determinate verdicts about the "representations" ascribed to neural networks and LLMs, or whether that project is a category error that would corrupt the core theory.

Please begin with some brief brainstorming, then give a prioritized ranking of three to five questions. For each, explain: (1) why it is or is not upstream of the others, in the sense that its answer changes what the remaining questions even amount to; (2) its tractability for philosophers working largely a priori, versus requiring input from evolutionary biology, neuroscience, or machine learning; and (3) what developments in the next few years would move it up or down the list. Finally, be explicit about your criteria and their trade-offs — importance, tractability, neglectedness, downstream dependence — and about where you would refuse to spend the funder's money. I am especially interested in whether the artificial-systems question is a genuine forcing function for the core problems or a distraction from them, and why.
