I'm a retired physiologist — most of my career was single-unit recordings in cat visual cortex, and since retiring I've gotten into philosophy of mind, partly through my daughter who teaches it. Over the last couple of years I've settled into a view I can't quite shake, and she keeps telling me I hold it too comfortably, so let me lay it out and you tell me what would count against it. The view: consciousness is distributed across the animal kingdom as a matter of degree, on essentially one scale, and where a species sits on that scale tracks the integrative complexity of its nervous system — thalamocortical loops and their analogues. So a raven is more conscious than a chicken, a chicken more than a carp, a carp more than a bee, the bee maybe barely above zero. Not a different kind of thing per clade — one shared dimension of how much experience there is, varying mostly with how much integrative neural machinery an animal brings to the table.

In my old life this would have just been an empirical hypothesis, so treat it that way. I want the observations, arguments, or cases that would make me give it up or seriously revise it. I'd especially like you to spell out the following: which predictions it makes that rival views don't — my sense is it predicts that behavioral complexity and neuroanatomical complexity should roughly agree when ranking animals, so any clade where the two come apart (an animal that acts far richer than its brain looks, or vice versa) is where the trouble should show up — is that the right way to press on it? Also, presumably things like consciousness evolving independently in cephalopods and vertebrates could matter here — does that cut against a single scale, or does it only cut against certain ways of grounding it? And tell me honestly which defeaters a committed holder of this view would concede as real trouble versus explain away, because I suspect I've been quietly insulating it by saying "roughly" and "mostly" every time my daughter pushes, and I want to know if that's made it unfalsifiable, and whether that even matters for a view of this kind.

Format: give me exactly five defeaters as a numbered list, ordered from fatal to merely awkward, each one a sentence or two, and nothing else — no intro or conclusion, I'll work out myself what to do with them.
