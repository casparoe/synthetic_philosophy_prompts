In my AP Biology class we've been covering the genetic code, and it bothers me how freely the word "code" gets thrown around. My teacher says DNA stores "information" and that base sequences "code for" proteins, but when a classmate said that DNA is literally a code in the way Morse code or a computer program is — so that somebody must have written it — she said that's just an analogy and moved on. Meanwhile our textbook, and sometimes the teacher herself, slips into calling DNA a "blueprint," a "recipe," or a set of "instructions," and nobody explains what's literal and what's metaphor. I'd like to know what the slogan "DNA is a code" could actually be claiming. Could you lay out the distinct precise theses packed into it — from the nearly trivial claim about the codon-to-amino-acid mapping that no biologist disputes, to stronger claims about DNA being a symbolic system in the way human languages or software are, to whatever reading would be needed to support my classmate's "codes require coders" inference — and for each say whether it's true and whether the people who repeat the slogan probably mean it? Please also tell me which reading my teacher was most plausibly denying when she called it an analogy, and whether "blueprint," "recipe," and "instruction manual" are three different claims or three names for one. Answer in American English.
