I'm a high school senior who helps run orientation for the exchange students our school hosts every year, and I keep getting the same kind of question from them: do I actually have to tip here, do I have to wait my turn in line when locals are cutting, do I have to wait for the crosswalk light when everyone around me is crossing against it? I want to hand next year's students something better than "when in Rome." Please write a decision procedure they can run in their heads, in about a minute, whenever they notice a local everyday convention they don't share. The procedure should rest on an actual position about what makes such conventions binding rather than just local habits — for example, taking seriously the thought that some conventions solve coordination problems (which side of the escalator to stand on), some are fairness norms that locals themselves may resent but cannot unilaterally abandon (tipping in the United States), and some are legal conventions with safety stakes (jaywalking) — and it should be honest about where these categories blur into each other. For each step, state the question being asked, what answer points toward following the convention, what answer leaves the student free not to, and which steps are really about the student's own values rather than facts anyone could verify. Then run the procedure on three cases: tipping at a restaurant for a student from a country where tipping is rare or even mildly insulting; joining or refusing to join in when locals cut a queue they generally expect others to respect; and crossing against the light at an empty intersection where everyone local does it. At least one of the worked cases should come out in a way I won't like, and you should say so. Finally, please commit to a definite verdict on the underlying question — whether following local conventions of this kind is a genuine moral obligation for visitors, or something weaker like courtesy, prudence, or good judgment — and defend that verdict rather than laying out the options and leaving me to choose.
