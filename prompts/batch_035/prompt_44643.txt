My lab runs a reading group on AI welfare, and a recurring move in our discussions is the claim that reward in reinforcement learning is just the engineering analogue of pleasure and pain: the reward function plays the same role in an agent's economy that valence plays in an animal's, so a model trained with RLHF is essentially a creature that has been subjected to billions of episodes of pleasure and punishment. One member leans on this to argue that we already have moral obligations to our models and that adversarial training in particular is something like torture; another counters that the analogy is just anthropomorphic branding for a scalar that isn't experienced as anything. What strikes me is that both of them treat the analogy as all-or-nothing rather than auditing it. So here is my request: take the reward-as-valence analogy and tell me how far it holds. Lay out the correspondence explicitly (what plays the role of pain, of pleasure, of the nociceptor or the dopamine signal, of the evolved function of suffering), then identify where the mapping breaks. I'm especially interested in which disanalogies actually matter for the moral conclusion and which are incidental -- for example, whether it matters that reward is a designer-specified training signal rather than an evolved one, that dopamine is a learning signal rather than a hedonic state in its own right, or that pain in biological organisms is separable, at least conceptually, from its role as a teaching signal. Also note anything the analogy quietly imports -- that there is a subject of experience standing behind the signal, that the signal has a felt quality rather than merely a functional role. Finally, tell me whether the "we may be inflicting suffering" conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, whether it needs a weaker version (say, a precautionary rather than a confident claim), and whether there is a better analogy available -- reward as currency, as a fitness score, as a supervisor's correction -- that would reframe the debate. Please lead with your bottom line about the analogy's fate, then give the full audit, with no preamble and without restating my question.
