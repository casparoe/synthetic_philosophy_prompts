My two friends have been arguing about "equality of what" for years and I finally convinced them to do an adversarial collaboration and write something up together. One of them is basically a Dworkinian resourcist -- thinks equality means equalizing resources, brute luck should be insured against, and people should bear the costs of their own ambitions and choices. The other is a Sen/Nussbaum capabilities person -- thinks equality is about what people can actually do and be, and that resources are just a means to that. I've had to sit through a hundred dinners of this and I want the document they should have produced at the end.

Please draft that joint statement. Specifically I want:

- a numbered section of claims both of them could actually sign their names to, including claims about the "equality of what?" question itself, about what each approach gets right about the other, and about the cases (expensive tastes, adaptive preferences, disability, choice-sensitive costs) that both agree are the hard ones;
- a table of the residual disagreements, where each disagreement is phrased in wording the side holding that view would accept -- so no slanted phrasing like "resources are merely instrumentally valuable" as a description of the resourcist's view unless he'd put it that way;
- the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether welfare/capability information is reliable enough to be an egalitarian currency, and another is what to do about expensive tastes and adaptive preferences, but you work out what the real cruxes are;
- for each crux, a concrete case or thought experiment both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about the case written down in advance. Like: a wheelchair user in a city with no accessible public transit, where she could either be given a cash compensation roughly equal to the cost of the disadvantage or the city could be required to make transit accessible -- does the resourcist predict the cash is enough (at least as a matter of justice, not policy), and what does the capabilities person predict a resourcist committed to that has to say?

If you conclude some of their dispute is verbal -- e.g. about whether "resource" can be stretched to cover internal capacities and social structures -- say which part and why, since I've always suspected a chunk of it is just them meaning different things by "resource."

One more thing: this document will get sent back to both of them for signature, so at the very end include a short note on where your drafting is most likely to fail -- which formulations you'd bet one side refuses to sign, and what would change your mind about the cruxes or about the dispute being partly verbal. Don't hedge everything to death though; if you think one crux is more load-bearing than the others, just say so.
