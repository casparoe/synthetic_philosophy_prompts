A friend of mine got really into ethics this year and I've been keeping a running list of her verdicts on cases, mostly ones where you can add up welfare across different people. Her track record so far:

- Surgeon secretly kills one healthy patient to harvest organs and save five: totally wrong, even though five-for-one is a big net gain in welfare
- Diverting a runaway trolley from five people onto a side track where it kills one: fine, she'd pull the lever
- Forcing one person to undergo a painful bone marrow extraction to save a stranger with leukemia, no consent: wrong, even though the donor fully recovers and the recipient lives
- Mandatory vaccination that gives everyone a day of flu-like symptoms but prevents a deadly outbreak among the elderly: fine, obviously
- A small tax on everyone in a rich country to pay for life-saving treatment for a few who can't afford it: fine
- Using a lottery to decide which of two equally deserving patients gets the single ICU bed: fine, better than deciding by which doctor happens to be on shift
- The state making you save for your own retirement instead of spending it all now: fine, since its the same person's welfare being traded off across their own life
- Handing over basically all resources to a "utility monster" who gets astronomically more pleasure from everything than anyone else does: no, you shouldn't give the monster everything

Next week we're arguing about a new case from her actual town: the city wants to put a slightly polluting, noisy waste facility in one particular neighborhood — its the only geologically suitable spot — which would give a million residents marginally cheaper and cleaner waste disposal while imposing a small but real health burden on the five thousand people who live there. Compensation is being offered but the neighborhood itself voted against it. Predict her verdict. Honestly I care less about the answer than the reasoning: I can see at least two or three rules that fit everything on her list (something about never intending harm to a particular individual, something about whether burdens are fairly shared or consented to, maybe something about whether the benefits and burdens land on the same people or different ones). Lay out those candidate rules, say what each predicts about the facility case, give me your best guess with the reason for preferring it, and tell me what further case would best split the rules apart.
