Quick context: I'm a postdoc in particle theory, and in two weeks I'm giving a colloquium at a physics department that is mostly condensed matter and experimental HEP people, plus a handful of AMO folks and a department chair who has a framed Feynman photo outside his office. The talk is a defense of a fairly specific claim: philosophy of science is basically dead weight during normal, healthy physics, but load-bearing during periods when a field has lost its grip on what would even count as an answer. My sketch of the argument is that ordinary puzzle-solving runs on technique and taste, and you don't need epistemology to fit a resonance peak — but when the community can't agree on what would count as evidence at all (measurement problem, what a many-worlds or multiverse claim is even asserting, whether anthropic reasoning in the landscape is physics or excuse-making), the questions stop being answerable by more data and become conceptual, and that's exactly where the philosophers' toolkit earns its keep. My one-liner is that philosophy of science is like a spare tire: pointless weight 99% of the time, and the only thing that matters the other 1%.
