I'm preparing a short essay for a philosophy-of-AI reading group and would appreciate comments on the notes below before I write it up.

Working title: "The noise model is where the ethics lives."

Notes: Inverse reinforcement learning (IRL) infers a human's preferences by treating observed behavior as roughly optimal for some reward function plus noise — given the choices a person makes, find the preferences that make those choices most probable. The usual complaint is that humans are not rational enough for this to work. My essay wants to argue something different: the framework does not merely fail to accommodate human irrationality; it forces a factorization of behavior into a preference component and a noise component, and I want to argue that this factorization is not purely descriptive. Which deviations from optimality get filed as noise determines what the system takes the person's "real" preferences to be, and hence what it will do for them. Example: someone smokes. Under a Boltzmann-rationality assumption, the smoking is evidence of a genuine (immediate) reward component that the helper should serve, only discounted by error. Under a model that posits habit formation or distorted foresight, the smoking is noise to be explained away relative to the person's long-run preferences. The helper built on each model acts very differently, yet the choice between noise models is usually presented as a technical detail. I want to introduce a distinction between deviations the helper should disregard as performance error ("explanatory noise") and deviations that are partly constitutive of who the person is ("constitutive noise") — akrasia, inconsistent risk attitudes, etc. A helper that corrects constitutive noise is not merely filtering out error; it is editing the person. My worry is that the standard IRL setup structurally cannot mark this distinction, since the noise term must be specified independently of the human's values. I plan to gesture at assistance games as a partial improvement, while noting that they inherit the same factorization problem through their built-in model of the human's rationality.
