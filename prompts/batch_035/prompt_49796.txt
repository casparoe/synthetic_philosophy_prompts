I run a small philosophy of mind reading group, and for our next session I want a diagnostic instrument rather than another paper summary: a decision tree that sorts each member into a position on the question *in virtue of what is pain bad for the person undergoing it?* The tree must sort by how people react to cases, not by which label they prefer, so every internal node should be a forced choice about a concrete scenario that a person can answer without any idea which position it routes them towards.

Since the instrument must be self-contained, build the nodes from the following material. Pain asymbolia: patients, typically after lesions in or near the anterior cingulate cortex, who report that they feel pain at normal or even heightened intensity, localise it accurately, and withdraw reflexively from the stimulus, yet show no fear, urgency or distress about it — some will describe a recent agony with apparent indifference or even amusement. The morphine phenomenon: patients who say the pain is still fully present but no longer troubles or occupies them. Congenital insensitivity to pain: persons who never feel pain at all and whose lives are usually wrecked by unnoticed injuries. Endorsed pain: the distance runner who welcomes the burn as evidence of effort, or cases of consensual pain sought out for its own sake. Phantom limb pain that the patient has come to regard as a curiosity rather than an affliction. You may add further cases of the same kind if a branch needs them, but these must all appear somewhere in the tree.

Requirements on the output:

1. No more than eight internal nodes and no more than seven leaves. Each node is a question answerable in ignorance of the taxonomy.
2. Each leaf carries the name of the position it lands on — among the candidates: a representationalist or evaluativist view on which pain's badness is inherited from the bodily damage it represents; an imperativist view on which pain is bad as an unheeded command; a hedonic experientialist view on which felt unpleasantness is itself the bad-maker; a desire-based or attitudinal view on which pain is bad only when, and because, the subject minds it; and any deflationary or mixed view the combinations force. Where a combination of answers has no occupant, say so and diagnose whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored.
3. Flag explicitly any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate — I particularly want to know whether any case, asymbolia included, can drive a wedge between the representationalist and the imperativist about the badness of pain that is felt but not minded.

Throughout the answer, indicate how confident you are in each substantive claim, and close by singleling out the single point you are least sure of; I would expect that to be somewhere in the clinical descriptions, so please be candid about where the phenomenology I have described outruns the settled record.
