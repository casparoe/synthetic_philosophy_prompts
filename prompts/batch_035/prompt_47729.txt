I'm on the grants committee of a mid-sized national research foundation, and we have a renewal decision this fall that I'd like a memo for. Some background: our review criteria were rewritten years ago by a previous chair who was way into Lakatos. Proposals get scored partly on whether the research programme they belong to counts as progressive or degenerating — progressive meaning it's kept producing novel predictions, some of which actually panned out, degenerating meaning it just keeps absorbing anomalies by bolting on auxiliary assumptions after the fact. This is literally written into our bylaws.

The decision: Programme A is twenty years old. Early on it made two genuinely surprising predictions that landed and made careers. For the last decade, though, it has survived by patching — every time the data goes against it, someone adds a new auxiliary assumption, and each patch gets defended as technically elegant rather than as a prediction. Programme B is four years old, has produced zero novel predictions so far, but its people are energetic and it keeps spawning new problems and sub-questions, along with a fair amount of internal disagreement about what the programme is even about.

Three factions on the committee. One wants to apply the criteria as written: defund A as degenerating, fund B, since Lakatos was clear you shouldn't strangle a young programme in its crib for not having delivered yet. A second faction says the criteria can't do what the bylaws ask of them at the moment of decision — whether B's problemshift turns out progressive is exactly the thing nobody can know now, so scoring it now is theater. A third says the whole exercise misreads the guy, since these were meant as a tool for rationally reconstructing the history of science, not a grading rubric for a committee, and he would've laughed at bureaucrats handing down verdicts on live programmes in real time.

You're a professional philosopher of science who's used to advising institutions. Think carefully and brainstorm the live options before committing to one. Draft the short memo I'll circulate before the meeting: it needs a concrete bottom line (renew A, fund B, both, neither, or revise the criteria themselves — possibly with a recomendation about what replaces them), reasoning the losing factions could actually engage with rather than dismiss, and an honest account of the strongest point on the side you rule against.
