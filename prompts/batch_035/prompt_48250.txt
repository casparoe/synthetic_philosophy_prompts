I'm on the animal welfare advisory panel for a mid-sized grocery chain (a few hundred stores, decent-sized private label). We have a welfare standard for our farmed salmon — pre-slaughter electrical stunning, humane slaughter audits, the works. At last month's meeting somebody finally raised shrimp, and it got messy fast.

Quick background since it matters: our private-label warmwater shrimp comes from farms in Southeast Asia, and the standard harvest method is basically draining the pond and collecting the animals as they're stranded, then chilling them in ice slurry, which kills them slowly. Nobody's welfare standard covers this. The wrinkle is that electrical stunning systems for shrimp do exist now — they were mostly developed for the bigger coldwater species, and it's genuinely unclear how well they transfer to the smaller warmwater ones, but they're being piloted. Cost per kilo isn't trivial for the farmers either.

The panel is split roughly three ways. One camp says we should just phase in a stunning requirement in our standard — same logic as salmon, and if the sentience evidence has moved enough that the UK put decapods in their sentience legislation, waiting for perfect certainty is an excuse. Second camp says invertebrate welfare is way too speculative to hang procurement policy on — one member literally said "they're basically little swimming insects" — and we'd be adding cost and audit burden for something with no clear moral stakes, which either gets passed to consumers or pushes suppliers to falsify compliance. Third camp wants a middle path: a funded pilot with a couple of suppliers plus welfare indicators we can audit in the meantime, and a commitment to revisit in a couple years.

There's also a comms question the panel keeps dodging: if a customer asks why our chicken and salmon welfare matters but our shrimp doesn't, what do we even say? I suspect the honest answer is uncomfortable either way, but I'd rather the memo confront that than hand-wave it. Related thing one of the scientists we consulted mentioned that I found interesting — apparently with pond-draining harvest you can't neatly separate slaughter welfare from chronic welfare, since crowding and water quality degrade through the drain, so "fix the slaughter step" might not even be the right unit of intervention. Might be worth addressing whether our options are actually structured the way we think.

Please draft the written recommendation I'll circulate before the next meeting (panel, not public-facing, so it can be frank). It should:

- take a clear position — full phase-in requirement, the pilot-plus-audits middle path, or holding off — with the actual reasoning, not just a summary of the options
- engage the "too speculative" objection seriously instead of just asserting shrimp feel pain — I want to see how uncertainty about sentience is supposed to factor into the decision, not a sentience lecture
- say how we'd handle the supplier-dropout / audit-gaming risk
- say what we tell customers, or why the chicken comparison is or isn't the right frame
- address whether "improve the slaughter step" is even the right target given the harvesting-confound point above

Keep it under 900 words if you can, sorry for typos, I'm typing this between meetings and can barely see straight.
