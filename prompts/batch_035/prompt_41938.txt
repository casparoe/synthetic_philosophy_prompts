For an assignment in my graduate seminar on political economy, I would like you to write a satirical internal memo, roughly 650 words, from the Central Planning Bureau of a fictional socialist state. The Bureau has just read its intelligence summary of Mises's calculation argument and Hayek's "The Use of Knowledge in Society," and the memo announces a new Knowledge Acquisition Department whose mandate is to collect, on standardized forms, all the dispersed, tacit, local knowledge of "particular circumstances of time and place" that Hayek said prices communicate but no planner can gather. The humor should come from the actual structure of the debate, not from generic Communist-bureaucracy jokes: Form 10-B should ask citizens to report knowledge they did not know they possessed; there should be an appendix on the tin shortage that no one can locate in the filing system because the relevant information was never written down anywhere; and a dissenting deputy should propose, in an attached note, simply running the Lange-style trial-and-error pricing procedure and letting the Bureau act as the auctioneer, with the memo's author patiently explaining why this misses the point. The satire should be recognizable to someone who knows the socialist calculation debate, and fair to both sides. Deadpan institutional voice throughout; the mem- o should never admit that its own procedures are the reductio.
