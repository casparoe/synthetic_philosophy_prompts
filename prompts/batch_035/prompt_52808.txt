Over the years my uncle has handed down verdicts in our running argument about whether space is a thing. He says Newton's spinning bucket is genuinely spinning — the water climbing the sides settles it even if you wall off the room — and that two globes alone in the universe, joined by a cord that pulls taut, are really rotating rather than standing still. Yet he denies there is any fact about the whole universe sitting a few feet over from where it could have sat with every relation preserved, any fact about the entire universe drifting uniformly in one direction, and any facts about motion in a universe that contains nothing at all. Our next argument is about a universe with no matter in it whatsoever, but where space itself is curved — waves moving through it, even — so that no observer or instrument could ever exist to check. Is there a fact about whether that universe is curved rather than flat? Answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, one or two sentences each, with no introduction or conclusion: first, the candidate principles that fit every verdict he has given so far; second, what each principle implies for the empty curved universe; third, your best prediction of his verdict; fourth, your grounds for that prediction, including which of his earlier rulings is the closest analog; fifth, one further case that would most sharply divide the candidate principles.
