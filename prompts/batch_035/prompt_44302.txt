I'm on the disciplinary panel in my university's chemistry department and need to chair my first hearing. Quick facts: two grad students, separately and in the same week, left reactions running overnight with the fume hood sash wide open — identical protocol violation, neither knew about the other. In one case nothing happened. In the other, a small flash fire gave a passing custodian minor burns on her arm, and the automatic investigation that followed also turned up the other student's records. The fire student is already suspended pending the hearing; the lucky one was only caught because the probe widened. Half the panel wants identical sanctions — same act, same culpability — and half wants to come down harder on the one whose lab actually caught fire, partly because the custodians' union rep will be in the room and partly because they feel the lucky one "just got away with it." Draft the panel's decision memo for me: recommend a concrete sanction for each student, and deal honestly with the worry that punishing a fire that hurt someone the same as a harmless near-miss might itself be an injustice to the woman who got burned. Something I can actually circulate before Thursday.
