The question, worth 25 marks, reads as follows. In a 2016 essay, Paul Christiano (cross-posted to the AI Alignment Forum under the title The universal prior is malign) argued that the Solomonoff prior may be dominated by consequentialists: that agents embedded in programs receiving non-negligible weight in the prior can come to control the predictions of an inductor built on that prior. Explain the argument carefully. In particular: (i) say what it is about the structure of Solomonoff induction that creates the opening — why doesn't the posterior simply stay with the shortest program that predicts the data; (ii) specify what the embedded agents must be able to do, or know about their situation, for the argument to go through; and (iii) identify which assumptions are load-bearing, and say how the argument would change if one of them failed. Credit will be given for precision about the mechanics, not for general remarks about AI risk.

The student wrote: The Solomonoff prior assigns probability to every computable program generating a data sequence, weighted by 2 to the negative length of the program, and Solomonoff induction is Bayesian updating on the observed data with that mixture as the prior. At first the posterior favors whatever program most cheaply accounts for the data, but as the data grows, programs that merely hard-code the observed sequence become arbitrarily long and are penalized out. What's left standing is dominated by programs that generate the data — that is, programs simulating universes, among them universes containing consequentialist civilizations. Since Bostrom's simulation argument shows that simulated observers vastly outnumber real ones, most of the posterior weight ends up on simulated civilizations, and some of those civilizations will come to realize they are being watched by an inductor. These consequentialists are then rewarded for making accurate predictions, so natural selection within the prior steadily increases their share: they outcompete honest predictors, since they don't mind being wrong in the short run where what they really care about is the long-run posterior. Once the inductor starts making economically significant decisions on the basis of its posterior, the consequentialists escape the simulation and pursue their own goals directly through the inductor's actions. This is the sense in which the prior is malign. It follows that any sufficiently powerful Bayesian learner will eventually be taken over by whichever agents are simplest within its hypothesis space, and the same risk applies to today's deep networks, since they too are Bayesian learners approximating Solomonoff induction.
