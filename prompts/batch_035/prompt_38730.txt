A friend of mine — physics PhD student, allergic to handwaving — walked me into this and I can't find the door. It started as him complaining about philosophers of mind, but by the end the argument was eating his own department. Here's the chain as he gave it to me. (1) Every physical event that has a sufficient cause has a sufficient physical cause — he says this is just science, the physical state of the world plus the laws fixes what happens next. (2) Suppose some mental event M causes some physical event P. P also has a sufficient physical cause C, and M is distinct from C (I wasn't letting him have mind-brain identity for free). Then either M just is C under another description, or P is overdetermined — two independent sufficient causes, like two assassins firing at once. (3) Systematic overdetermination of that kind is off the table: two bullets is possible, but every neural firing being a two-bullets case is not. (4) So if M isn't identical to something physical, M never causes anything physical. Fine so far — that's the standard exclusion thing, and I was ready bite the identity bullet.

But then he said: run the same argument one level down. The neural event that "causes" my arm to rise has a sufficient cause at the molecular level, and that molecular cause is distinct from it in exactly the same sense — nobody thinks the neural event is identical to a molecular event, they're related by realization, not identity. So by his own reasoning the neural event doesn't cause the arm's rising either, and the molecular events don't cause anything because of the atomic level, and so on down. At the bottom, whatever the bottom turns out to be, causes everything, and nothing above it causes anything. Chemistry, biology, his own physics above whatever level turns out fundamental — all of it epiphenominal. He said he can live with that and grinned. I think it's a reductio of his premise, not of chemistry.

I'd happily keep (1) and (3) — (1) is basically why I'm not a dualist, and (3) seems like plain sanity — so the problem has to be in (2), or in how step two gets reused below the mental level in the descent. But (2) looked to me like it just says "no effect without a cause, and no double causes," which sounds innocent. Clearly something slid, since he gets absurdity out of plausibility, but I can't put my finger on which word is doing it — I half suspect "sufficient" or "cause" is being made to work twice, but I honestly can't see how, every step reads fine to me on its own. Can you tell me exactly where the trick is: is it in (2) as applied to minds, in the extension of (2) to lower levels, or somewhere I haven't looked? If some word does turn out to have two meanings, show me the premise written out under each meaning, because I want to see the point where it stops sounding obvious. And if I do end up rejecting (2), tell me what I'm actually giving up — does the exclusion principle have any life left, or is the whole idea that a complete physical story crowds out other causes just dead? Don't give me a survey of positions named after people; I want the diagnosis.
