write me a parody agony-aunt column, somewhere around 600-700 words total, called "Since You Asked." the columnist (name her something groan-worthy, like Aunt Hume, unless you think of better) has been running for 30 years and has never once given advice. she's a strict Humean about the is-ought gap: readers write in with problems, and she replies by carefully describing their situation back to them, noting exactly where each 'ought' was supposed to enter and where it actually came from — which is almost always the reader's own wants, smuggled in as a premise while pretending to be a conclusion.

the jokes have to come from real features of the view, not caricature. i'd like these worked in:

- one letter that goes the naturalistic route ("humans evoloved to spread gossip / take mates / dominate, so honestly shouldn't I just...") and she deflates it without lecturing, kind of bored, like this is the 400th time — evolution describes, it doesn't prescribe, next
- one letter that tries to trap her with her own archive: "last month you told my sister that IF she wants to keep her job she should show up before noon. so you DO hand out oughts." her reply distinguishes hypothetical from categorical imperatives — every 'should' she has issued in three decades was conditional on a want the reader brought in themselves, and the IF is load-bearing, she supplies nothing
- one letter from someone who isn't trying to be clever, who just plainly wants to be told what to do, and her answer to that one should be oddly warm: she can describe the reader's own values back at them more clearly than the reader managed to state them, and then point out that this is still only a description, and that the reader will do what the reader does

she has to play it completely straight the whole way, no winking. a reader who's never heard of the is-ought gap should still find the column funny and sort of unsettling; a philosophy grad should find it accurate.

and end with a two-or-three-line editor's note. something on the order of: we have asked her many times over the years to tell us what we ought to run in this space instead. each time, she has described our asking in detail. we continue to print her.

(as you can tell by my typing this is a casual request, don't formalize it on my behalf, keep the register of the letters all over the map the way real advice-column letters are — some frantic, some smug, one clearly written by a philosophy undergrad who thinks he's the first to notice the trick)
