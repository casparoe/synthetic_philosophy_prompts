Consider the original form of Parfit's teletransporter: a machine on Earth records the exact state of every cell in my body, destroys that body, and transmits the information to Mars, where an exact replica, made of new matter, opens his eyes believing himself to be me, remembering my life, and intending to carry out my plans. I want your credence in the following proposition: I would have, before entering the machine, nearly as strong a reason for self-interested concern for the person who steps out on Mars as I have for my own tomorrow in ordinary life — that is, psychological continuity is, to within a small margin, what matters in survival, whether or not it is what constitutes my identity. For context, my own credence is around 0.6, while my thesis supervisor, who leans toward animalism, puts hers closer to 0.1, so I would also like to know where our disagreement most likely lives.

Please do not just restate Parfit's conclusions. Give a number, then the decomposition that produces it. At minimum I would expect something like the following terms, though you may add or merge them as you see fit:

1. The probability that in the simple (non-branching) case the Mars person simply is me — that identity consists in psychological continuity rather than in the continuation of the biological organism, or there being no determinate fact of the matter at all.
2. The probability that, even where identity fails, identity is not what matters — the reductionist thought that the further fact of being the very same person, even when it obtains, could not ground prudential reasons of a different strength than the underlying psychological facts already ground. Parfit's fission case is the standard support here: since identity is one-one but psychological continuity can branch, and we would apparently have reason to care about both successors in a branching case, identity cannot be what matters.
3. The probability that the continuity realized by the machine is of the right kind — the right sort of cause, sufficient reliability, no degradation of memories or character — given that Parfit himself suggested we would care less about a later, cheaper model that produces someone with a slightly flawed copy, which implies that what matters comes in degrees and is sensitive to facts about the mechanism.
4. Whatever further terms you think belong, for instance the probability that the prudential 'ought' tracks what matters rather than identity as a matter of conceptual truth, or the probability that near-parity of concern is enough and no residual discounting of the replica is rationally required.

For each term, give a rough probability and a one-line justification. Then combine them in whatever way you think correct, state the final credence, and tell me two further things: which term the final number is most sensitive to, so that the disagreement between my supervisor and me is probably concentrated in one or two of the terms rather than spread evenly; and what argument, thought experiment, or empirical consideration would move your final number the most. In particular, say whether a case can be made that my 0.6 and her 0.1 differ mainly on term 1, where the animalist has a live alternative, or mainly on term 2, where the fight is over whether a further fact could matter — since I suspect the answer determines whether our dispute is substantive or verbal, and I would like to know if that suspicion is right.
