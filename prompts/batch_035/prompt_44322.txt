My friend from my local rationalist meetup has maintained a spreadsheet of moral patients for like six years now and her verdicts are pretty stable. For the record: chimps matter a lot, fish matter, insects round to zero, a honeybee colony doesn't matter even though she happily admits it's intelligent in the collective sense, a hand-coded pain module in a robot doesn't matter no matter how convincing the flinching is, current LLMs don't matter but she gets visibly uncomfortable when pressed on why, and a whole brain emulation of a mouse does matter, because as she puts it it's the same computation on different hardware.

So next week we're going to argue about a new one. A lab did an architecture search: they trained a network from scratch with no reference to any particular animal, and it converged on dynamics that look suspiciously like mouse limbic/midbrain stuff — threat avoidance, grooming-like routines, that kind of thing. De novo, nobody scanned a mouse, no continuity to any actual rodent. Do you think she'll say it matters? My instinct is yes, since she's a functionalist and this is supposedly just about which computations are running. But I've started to wonder if her emulation verdict is doing something different — maybe she thinks the emulation inherits its status from the original mouse, or maybe she's just pattern matching looks-mammalian = counts. Those rules agree with everything on her spreadsheet so far but I think they split on the architecture search thing, and probably on the LLM thing too, which is relevant because we fight about that constantly and I suspect her stated rule and her actual rule come apart somewhere.

So: what rules actually fit her track record, what does each one predict for the search-trained network, which is she most likely running and why, and what case should I bring up next that would really seperate the candidates? I care less about the answer on this one system than about finally pinning down what her real rule is.
