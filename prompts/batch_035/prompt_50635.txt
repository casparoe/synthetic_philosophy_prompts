I spent thirty-five years as an experimental physicist, mostly in neutron scattering at a national laboratory, and I have been retired for three years, during which I have taken up the philosophy of physics in earnest. I have worked my way into the literature on whether the quantum state is a description of the world or a description of our information about the world — what I gather is called the psi-ontic versus psi-epistemic dispute — and I find that the arguments are usually couched in terms I don't naturally use: knowledge, beliefs, what an observer is warranted in asserting. I want the whole thing restated in the vocabulary I actually used at the bench.

Concretely, translate the debate into the language of state preparation procedures, ensembles and run-to-run statistics, detector calibration and background subtraction, model parameters as against latent variables, and the distinction between a fitted model and whatever the fit is a model of. The psi-epistemic side, as I understand it, wants to treat the wavefunction the way I treated a fitted line through scattering data: a summary of my information, compressing what I know about a preparation into expectations about counts. The psi-ontic side wants to treat it the way I treated a prepared sample: something there in the apparatus whether or not anyone fits anything to it. Render both positions in my terms, as faithfully as you can, without smuggling in the assumptions of either camp and presenting them as translation.

Then I want the residue report, which for me is the point of the exercise. Say which parts of the philosophical dispute map cleanly onto distinctions I already drew at the bench, which parts translate only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. In particular, tell me what to make of the result from around 2012 by Pusey, Barrett, and Rudolph, which is often presented as showing that the quantum state must correspond to something physical, provided one grants a certain independence assumption about distinct preparations — whether that assumption is a natural constraint from where I stand or a substantive concession I should refuse to make, and whether the theorem's conclusion means what the realist camp says it means once it is put in terms of preparations and ensembles rather than in terms of knowledge. If you find that the residue points to something my experimental vocabulary cannot express, tell me whether that silence reflects a limitation of my vocabulary or an unclarity in the original dispute.

Finally, and this is a firm request: do not conclude by laying out the live options and leaving the choice to me. Commit to a verdict. Tell me which side you think is right once the translation is done, defend that verdict with reasons, and say explicitly where you think the strongest objection to your own position lies — I would rather have a definite position with an acknowledged weak spot than a balanced survey, since I can find surveys in the literature myself.
