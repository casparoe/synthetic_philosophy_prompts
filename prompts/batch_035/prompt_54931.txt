So, bit of background: three weeks ago I joined a Discord server that plays Nomic — the game where the rules text is the board, and changing the rules is itself a move in the game. It's slow-forum style, about ten of us, and somehow I've allready been elected to its three-person Judicial Committee (JC), which exists to answer questions about how to interpret the rules. I know, I know — the new guy. Which is exactly the problem, because we now have to put out a written opinion before Saturday's vote, and the other two JC members basically told me "you write it, you're the least invested."

Here's the situation. Our starter rules include, roughly: rules get changed by proposals that win a majority of votes cast (call it Rule 102); and the JC answers questions about interpretation of the rules in force, and its answers are binding (call it Rule 113). Both are ordinary mutable rules like everything else.

Last week Dave, who has the most points by a mile, proposed the following: "Rule 113 is repealed and replaced with: all questions about interpretation of the rules are decided by the player with the most points."

The vote is Saturday and it will probably pass. Before then we're suppose to say whether the JC considers this proposal valid, or whether we'll even look at it — and here's the knot:

- Half the club says the JC obviously has no business touching it. Rule 113 gives us power over questions about interpreting the rules, and a pending proposal is not a rule yet — nobody's asked us to interpret it, they've asked us to kill it, and killing it looks exactly like three people whose committee is on the chopping block rigging the game.
- The other half says if we don't rule now, then the moment it passes, every question about its own adoption ("did this need a simple majority, or, since it abolishes an organ of the game, something more?"; "does the transfer of powers kick in instantly or at end of day?") gets decided by... Dave, the guy it makes supreme. He's already said in chat he'd rule his own proposal valid, "obviously."
- So one body or the other has to adjudicate a case about its own existence. If we take the case, we're ruling on the scope of our own power — self-serving in the direction of keeping it. If we refuse, we hand the last word on our own abolition to its main beneficiary.

Also worth knowing: this is the first proposal in the game's history that touches the machinery rather than just adding point rules and flavor text, so there's no precedent to lean on. And the founders deliberately left Rule 113 mutable, on the theory that a game where the referee is permanent isn't really Nomic.

What I need from you is the actual memo the JC circulates before Saturday. A concrete recommendation with a bottom line the club can act on — I'm imagining something along the lines of: rule on the validity question now but narrowly, judging it by the rules in force at the time it was proposed, and state explicitly what we will and won't touch after adoption; or alternatively punt entirely and explain why that isn't just cowardice. Whatever you recommend, it has to honestly deal with the strongest point on the other side, and it cannot read like three people saving their own jobs — I have to face these same ten people again on Monday.
