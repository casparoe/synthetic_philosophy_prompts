I do RLHF at a mid-sized lab. Until recently we scored responses purely on outcomes: a grader looked at the finished answer and gave it a thumbs up or down. Last quarter we moved a big chunk of the pipeline to process supervision — graders score each step of the model's reasoning against a rubric, and a well-reasoned step that happens to land on a wrong conclusion now gets more reward than a sloppy step that lucked into a right one. It just got personally relevant because im the one writing the rubrics now, and I keep having to make calls that smell like moral philosophy with zero training in it. For instance, we decided a step counts as good if the reasoning would've been sound in most nearby counterfactual situations, not if it actually produced the good result. And here's the thing that bugs me: the same leadership still evaluates the deployed assistant purely by outcomes — user satisfaction, did the final answer actually help. So what does the training-side practice presuppose, if anything? About where credit and blame really live, or about whether the reasoning trace we grade is the actual decision process or just a report of it that the underlying process can fake when it wants to? And can that sit consistently next to outcome-only deployment evals, or is one side quietly borrowing a commitment the other side denies? I have no philosophy background but I can follow an argument — keep it concrete and tied to what we actually do, not to what a lab somewhere might someday do.
