My partner and I have been going in circles on presentism vs eternalism for like six months and the debate has completly stalled, and I'm starting to suspect the stall itself is the interesting part. I say only present things exist; she says the dinosaur and the future space station are just as real as my coffee mug, and tense is just indexical like "here". The weird thing is we agree on all the physics, we even agree that the relativity argument against a privileged present doesn't settle it, and we can each cash out the other's sentences — I can translate "there were dinosaurs" into her tenseless language and back without losing anything. So part of me wonders whether there's anything left for us to actually disagree about, whether the whole fight is verbal and it's just a preference for which semantics to use as home base. But the serious philosophers in this debate all seem to think something big is at stake — whether time genuinely passes, whether it's even true that "dinosaurs existed" if nothing non-present exists to make it true, whether the future is open. So: what kind of disagreement is this, really? Is "real but not present" a genuine metaphysical commitment or just tense-quantifier bookkeeping? If it's substantive, what would count as progress — new physics, a truthmaker theorem, better grip on our intuitions about passage — or is the impasse a sign the question is ill-posed? My pet theory is that the real fight underneath is over whether the felt whoosh of temporal passage is data that a theory of time has to accommodate, and everything else is engineering downstream of that one choice. Is that diagnosis right, close, or way off?
