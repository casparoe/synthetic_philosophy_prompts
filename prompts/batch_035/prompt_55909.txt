I'm a journalist who writes longform features on science and ideas, and I've been pulled into an editorial dispute I don't feel equipped to adjudicate. We're preparing a commemorative piece about a former mill town where the community's collective memory holds that, during a 1920s strike, company-hired guards fired on workers and that the dead were buried overnight in an unmarked trench. The surviving documentary record — coroner's files, the local paper's strike coverage, the guards' payroll ledgers — contains nothing of the sort, and in fact is detailed enough that you'd expect some trace. At the meeting, a colleague shut down the discussion with the line that history is written by the victors, the implication being that the documentary silence is itself a point in favor of the community's account: the record is blank precisely because the winning side erased it.

I want to understand what that one-liner could actually be claiming before I let it decide our editorial line. Please lay out the distinct theses the slogan might be expressing, from the nearly trivial to the most radical — for instance, something weak like "archives reflect who had the means to write things down," something middling like "dominant groups systematically shape what gets recorded," up to something strong like "documentary records are so compromised that they carry no independent evidential weight against a community's transmitted memory." For each reading, tell me whether it is plausible, and — this is the part I care most about — whether it would actually license my colleague's specific move, namely treating documentary silence not merely as absence of evidence but as positive evidence for the erased event, and privileging collective memory over the record when they conflict. If some readings support the move and others don't, say which are doing the work. Finally, tell me which reading people most plausibly intend when they invoke the slogan in a case like ours, and whether that reading survives scrutiny in a situation where the putative erasers left behind an otherwise meticulous paper trail. Present the readings as a numbered menu with a verdict on each, so I can bring it back to the newsroom.
