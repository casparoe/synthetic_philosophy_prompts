Write a dialogue between a philosopher who thinks guilt is the better moral emotion — because it targets specific acts, motivates repair, and respects people as agents who did a bad thing rather than being bad people — and an anthropologist who defends shame as underrated: it's cheaper to enforce, scales across whole communities, and can capture identity-level failures (cowardice, cruelty of character) that guilt's act-focus misses. Make them actually engage with each others points, so the anthropologist should press on the fact that guilt needs a specific identifiable victim to do any work, and the philosopher should push back that shame collapses into reputation management once nobody's watching. At some point have them argue about whether a culture could deliberately shift its own guilt/shame ratio — say via parenting norms, or how universities handle plagiarism vs. how small towns handle freeloaders — and wether doing that would itself be a kind of emotional paternalism. Neither should come out obviously the winner.
