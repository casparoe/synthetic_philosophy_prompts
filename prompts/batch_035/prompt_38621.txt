I'm a PhD student in political science, and I help run a forecasting club where we vote on whether-things-will-happen questions and track how the majority vote does against individual members. Something keeps happening that I don't have a word for, and my advisor just says "yeah, that's the usual thing" without ever saying what the usual thing is called.

Here's the pattern. We started with 12 members and the majority was right about 70% of the time on binary questions (will X happen by date Y, that kind of thing). Adding members kept helping until around 25-30 people, and then it just flatlined — we're at 60 members now and the majority is no better than it was at 30, even though every single member is right more often than wrong on their own. And it can get actively worse in a specific way: we recruited a batch of 15 new members who all follow the same two pundits and basically the same feeds, and after they joined our accuracy dipped. Meanwhile a friend's club with only six members but whose people each pull from totally different stuff (one's a macro trader, one a historian, one a local journalist) is beating us. So the effect seems to be: votes that come from the same source don't stack. Five people who all read the same article are one vote wearing five hats, and the pooling rule can't tell the difference. Worse, shared sources seem to mean shared blind spots, so a crowd can be confidently wrong together in a way no individual member is.

Is there an established name for this exact configuration — sincere, individually-more-often-right-than-wrong voters who fail to improve, or even degrade, group accuracy because their judgments run through the same channels? I know its somewhere near "groupthink" and somewhere near the "wisdom of crowds" idea, but groupthink sounds like it's about social pressure, and nobody in our club is pressured — everyone submits silent ballots — and "wisdom of crowds" is a book title more than a mechanism as far as I can tell. If there's a precise concept for it, tell me what it's called and where its edges are: is the problem just shared evidence, or also shared priors; does it matter whether members know about each other's sources; at what point does adding a voter with a duplicate perspective count as adding nothing versus adding negatively? And if there's no established term, coin one, because for my dissertation chapter I need to distinguish this from ordinary conformity effects, and that distinction is the whole point.
