As a professor of philosophy of science, I keep running into a mismatch between what our journals do and what Lakatos taught. Peer review at virtually every journal evaluates each submission on its own merits: Are the methods sound? Do the data support the claim? Is the contribution significant? Whether the research programme the paper belongs to has lately been generating novel predictions or merely patching itself with ad hoc auxiliary assumptions — Lakatos's distinction between progressive and degenerating problem shifts — appears nowhere on the review form, and a reviewer who raised it would be told it was out of scope, even unfair to able scientists who happen to work in a stagnant programme. What conception of scientific rationality does this practice presuppose? Is it the ideal Lakatos attacked, on which each theory can be judged, rationally and on its own, at a moment — or is it compatible with his view, since even a degenerating programme can occasionally produce a sound paper? And is the practice consistent with the behavior of the same institutions elsewhere, given that funding agencies, hiring committees, and tenure cases do look at trajectories over time, rewarding lines of work that have been going somewhere and quietly dropping those that have not? Please reconstruct the implicit theory of appraisal that the journal practice rests on, distinguish what it strictly presupposes from what it merely sits well with, say where it is philosophically underdetermined in the sense that several rival views of science would license it just as comfortably, and tell me what would have to change in editorial practice if the research programme, rather than the individual paper, were taken as the unit of appraisal.

Write your answer in American English, with these sections in this order: "The implicit theory" (roughly 250 words), "What the practice rules out" (roughly 150 words), "Where it is underdetermined" (roughly 150 words), "If the programme were the unit" (roughly 250 words), and a closing "Verdict" of no more than 80 words.
