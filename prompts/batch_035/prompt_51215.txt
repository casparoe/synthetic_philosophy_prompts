My friend Marnie has this whole thing about which bad-feeling emotion is the right one to have after you screw up, and which ones people should be steering each other toward. Her track record so far:

- Guy cheats on his taxes, feels awful about the act ("that was a shitty thing I did") but doesn't spiral about being a bad person — she says that's basically the healthy version and that the feeling will actually help him fix it.
- Same kind of guy, but instead he can't stop thinking "I'm the kind of person who does this" and its eating him alive — she thinks that's close to useless at best, corrosive at worst.
- A kid caught shoplifting: parents who make him stand in front of the family and say what he is (a thief) vs. parents who make him return the item and apologize to the shopkeeper — she's firmly for the second and says the first is teaching the wrong thing.
- Online public shaming pile-ons: against them even when the target genuinely did something wrong, and even when it "works".
- Restorative justice circles, where the offender has to sit face to face with the people they harmed — surprisingly fine with those, says they're not about the self at all even though they sound humiliating.

Next month she's visiting my ethics class and the students want to run some new cases past her, and I want to predict her verdicts beforehand so we can check. The new cases:

2. Survivor's guilt: someone who did nothing wrong at all, feels guilty anyway. Does her rule even apply? Is there such a thing as guilt without a bad act, and if so is it a counterexample to whatever she's running?
3. A culture (people always want to say Japan, or the old Mediterranean honor cultures, honestly not sure it matters) where shame does a lot of the work that guilt does elsewhere. Is she committed to saying that whole arrangement is worse, or could she say it works fine for them? She once told me cultures can pick these things the way they pick utensils, but I've also heard her talk as if guilt-culture vs. shame-culture is a straight-up moral ranking, so I genuinely can't tell which she'd say.
