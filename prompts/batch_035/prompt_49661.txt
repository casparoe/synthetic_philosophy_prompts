I sit on the clinical governance committee of a hospital network, and our quality dashboard has just thrown up a Simpson's paradox: taking all admissions together, our network posts a lower 30-day mortality rate than the regional average, but split by admission severity, we do worse at every severity level. The board has concluded from this that the aggregate comparison is a mere statistical artifact — an illusion that always dissolves once you disaggregate — and wants me to write into our reporting policy a clause that stratified results always override the headline figure. Before I draft it: is the board's general rule actually sound, or are there questions for which the aggregate is the right level to report and the stratified figures the misleading ones? If the board's rule is wrong, what rule should the policy use to determine, for a given question, which level of aggregation answers it — and could a clause like the board's, applied mechanically, ever force us to report the worse answer?
