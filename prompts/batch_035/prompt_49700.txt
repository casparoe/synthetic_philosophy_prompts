I sit on the civil-liberties review board of a mid-sized city's housing authority. The authority wants to offer tenants a voluntary lease addendum: in exchange for a modest monthly rent credit, tenants waive the standard 48-hour notice requirement and consent to random inspections of their units at any time, capped at four per year, with the cap adjustable by the authority at lease renewal. Proponents call it a straightforward win — tenants are adults making a trade, nothing is forced, and inspections are polite and rare. Critics reply that consent given for money, by tenants who cannot easily refuse it, does not make the arrangement free. Our board must issue a recommendation: approve, approve with conditions, or reject.

Please draft the short written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's meeting. It should make the underlying issue explicit — whether freedom consists only in non-interference, so that a consented-to power of entry costs tenants nothing, or whether it consists in non-domination, so that the authority's standing capacity for arbitrary entry is itself an unfreedom that no signature cures (roughly the negative-versus-republican liberty distinction, Berlin vs. Pettit). It should also give the strongest point on each side a fair run, including the worry that 'tenants can't really consent to this' shades into paternalism about the choices of poor people. If you recommend conditions, they should be ones that would genuinely convert arbitrary power into checked power — for instance an irrevocable cap, an independent inspector, or a tenant-controlled panel — rather than procedural paperwork, and you should say why those specific mechanisms follow from the liberty-theoretic analysis rather than from generic good governance. End with the concrete bottom line the board can vote on.
