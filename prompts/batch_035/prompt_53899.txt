Is there a name for this? I do ops analytics for a regional shipping company, so I spend my days staring at messy time series, and I've noticed something that keeps bugging me. Whether I say "there's a pattern here" seems to depend on how much slop I'm willing to tolerate, and it's not obvious to me that theres any deeper fact underneath that.

Example: delivery times on one of our lanes. If I'm allowed to be off by 40 minutes, the daily numbers fall into a clean weekly rhythm, and I can predict tomorrow's volume way better than chance. Tighten to 10 minutes and the rhythm just isn't there — not degraded, gone; the residuals look like noise. So is there a pattern or not? It feels wrong to say "yes, at 40 minutes tolerance," because that makes the pattern sound like something I projected onto the data. But it also feels wrong to say "no, at 10 minutes tolerance," because the 40-minute version genuinely buys predictive power I can cash at the bank.

And here's the part I'm more curious about. Patterns don't all die the same way as you tighten the tolerance. The lane-cycle thing fades out gradually — at 30 minutes it's weaker, at 20 weaker still, a soft slope all the way down. But another thing we track, truck idle times, holds rock solid until a very specific threshold and then collapses completely. That difference seems like it should matter — like maybe the first kind of pattern is just an artifact of how much regularity my coarse grain of description happens to pick up, and the second is tracking a real mechanism that actually breaks at some point. But I honestly can't tell if thats a real distinction or just me staring at graphs too long.

There's a third thing too. The tolerance isn't a number I pick in a vacuum, it's set by what somebody cares about — the warehouse needs the forecast within half an hour to schedule staff, so that's the tolerance that matters, and the "pattern" that exists is the one relative to that interest. A different stakeholder with a different need would find different patterns in the same data and neither of us would be wrong. I've heard people gesture at "emergence" for stuff like this but that always seems to be about levels of composition, tiny things making big things, which isn't quite my issue — my issue is that even at one fixed level of description, the facts about which patterns exist seem to be tolerance-relative, and maybe interest-relative on top of that.

So: is there an established concept for a pattern that exists only relative to an error tolerance? Does the gradual-fade versus sudden-collapse difference have its own name or literature? And does anyone in philosophy of science argue about whether the interest-relative version makes the pattern less real, or is that a confusion I should drop? If nothing fits exactly, I'd rather you tell me the nearest concepts and what each one gets wrong about my case, and then coin a term — but define its edges carefully, including what it's NOT, so I can tell whether the gradual and sudden cases fall under one concept or two.
