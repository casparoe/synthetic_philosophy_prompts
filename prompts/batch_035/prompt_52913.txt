Here's basically his summary as best I can reconstruct it:

He also had this concept of the reserve army of labor, meaning the unemployed, and for Marx that pool just grows and grows until you get a crisis of permanent mass unemployment. On top of that Marx thought machinery was bad for workers in itself — the machines themselves, not just who owns them — since he wrote all that stuff about the machine being the worker's enemy coming to life. And in his notebooks (the Grundrisse, before Capital) he said automation eventually makes capitalism literally impossible, because once machines do all the work, labor time stops being the source of value, so a system built on labor value just can't keep going. My friend's takeaway was that Marx basically predicted full automation ending capitalism and that anyone arguing for UBI on the grounds that automation is coming is just doing warmed-over Marx without knowing it.
