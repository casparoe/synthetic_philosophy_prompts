I'm taking a philosophy of well-being course and writing my term paper on hedonism, and I've gotten a bit stuck on how to structure it around the heterogeneity problem -- the classic worry (going back at least to Sidgwick's remark that pleasures differ so much that they seem to have nothing in common except the name) that the pleasures of eating, reading philosophy, listening to Bach, and so on seem to share no single property, which is awkward for a theory that identifies well-being with pleasure. The obvious replies I know of are: (a) the phenomenal one, that there's some common felt quality of pleasantness running through all of them, maybe a distinctive "hedonic tone"; (b) the attitudinal move, where pleasure is analyzed as a pro-attitude toward a state of affairs rather than a sensation, so what pleasures share isn't a feeling but the structure of enjoying something; and (c) just biting the bullet and saying "pleasure" is a family-resemblance term, which seems to cost hedonism its main selling point of being simple and unified.

What I want from you isn't a survey of these -- I can get that from the lectures. I want a prioritization. If I have one term paper, roughly 15 pages, a background in philosophy of mind rather than ethics, and about two months, which open questions around the heterogeneity problem are actually worth attacking, in what order, and why? Some candidates I've been mulling: whether the common-quality reply can be made non-circular without just pointing at a "hedonic tone" that nobody can independently describe; whether attitudinal hedonism quietly collapses into the desire-satisfaction theory (if pleasure is just taking a certain stance toward something, why is it still hedonism?); whether the heterogeneity problem even generalizes -- a friend in the seminar claims desire theories face a mirror-image version where "satisfying desires" is just as gerrymandered, and if she's right that changes what the problem is evidence for; and a wilder one our TA floated, that the whole issue is really the hard problem of consciousness localized to affect, i.e., we can't say what pleasantness is because we can't say what any qualitative character is. That last one sounds cool but maybe not tractable for a term paper? Please rank these (or add ones I'm missing) using whatever criteria you think matter -- how upstream each one is of the others, whether it's tractable with a philosophy-of-mind toolkit, and what would make you demote one as not worth the pages. And be honest if you think one of these is overrated or just a pseudo-question.
