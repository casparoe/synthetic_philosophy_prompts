Write a satirical news article in the deadpan style of the Onion — a headline plus roughly 450–550 words — reporting that a local man has, for the third time this year, defeated the town's new reasoning engine by inviting it to consider its own Gödel sentence. The humor must come from getting the actual dialectic right rather than caricaturing it, so that a logician laughs instead of winces. Specifically, the piece should work in, without lecturing: the man's insistence that he can simply *see* that the sentence is true while the machine cannot prove it; the unspoken assumption that the engine is consistent, and the awkwardness of the man's also having to *see* that; the engineers' response of adding the Gödel sentence to the engine as a new axiom, followed by the man's prompt and triumphant re-victory over the newer, longer Gödel sentence that results; the mechanists' counterchallenge that the man produce a certificate of his own consistency, and his gracious decline, citing the second incompleteness theorem; and a closing detail in which it emerges that no one, the man included, can actually identify which particular sentence is the engine's Gödel sentence this month. Include at least one quote from a weary mechanist engineer and one from the man himself. End with the town quietly commissioning a larger engine — the very existence of which the man had offered as a reductio — while he protests that it cannot be him, since he would know. Keep the irony legible and sustained; do not name any real people, living or dead. If you want a final flourish, a one-line aside in which the engineers muse about upgrading the engine to run on noncomputable physics is welcome, provided it stays deadpan.
