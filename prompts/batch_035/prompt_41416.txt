I recently listened to a science-history podcast that presented Schrödinger's philosophical side as follows: having read the Upanishads throughout his life, he held that consciousness is a singular of which each of us is only a partial reflection — the many minds being appearance, there being in truth just one mind. It went on to claim that this doctrine was the working hypothesis behind his physics: that he regarded the wave function as, in effect, this one universal mind, and that he took quantum mechanics to be an empirical vindication of the ancient Upanishadic formula that you are that. Finally, it said that because he regarded other persons as in truth identical with himself, he believed our duties to others are really duties to oneself, so that on his view the distinction between self-interest and morality dissolves. I am inclined to believe that Schrödinger really was deeply shaped by Vedanta, but I cannot tell how much of the rest is fair. Please go through the podcast's claims one by one: which would a historian of physics accept as accurate, which are conflations or overstatements, and does the overall picture survive once these are corrected — in particular, did his Vedantic conviction actually do any work in his physics, or did he keep the two strictly apart?
