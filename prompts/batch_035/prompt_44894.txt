I am a PhD student in philosophy of language, and the final chapter of my prospectus on the metaphysics of semantic information leans on Hofstadter's three-layer analysis of messages from Gödel, Escher, Bach: the frame message, which signals that an information-bearer is decodable at all; the outer message, which specifies how to decode it; and the inner message, which is the content thereby recovered. Before I present the prospectus to my committee, I want a consistency audit of the package of views I have committed myself to, since I can see that some of them pull in different directions, though I honestly cannot tell which tensions are real, which merely look real, and how much each would cost me to fix.

Here is the package. First, I accept the tripartition as fundamentally correct: any artifact we would pre-theoretically call a message decomposes into these three layers, and the layers are genuinely independent of one another. Second, I hold that the frame message is carried purely by structural facts about the information-bearer — statistical anomalies, aperiodic regularities, compressibility — such that a mindless detector sensitive only to structure could register that something is a message without interpreting a single bit of it; this is what underwrites my optimism about SETI-style recognition of an extraterrestrial signal. Third, I hold that outer messages can be made self-bootstrapping: with sufficient redundancy and intelligent design, a message can teach its own decoding, so that meaning can be transmitted between minds that share no prior conventions, which is why I am cautiously optimistic that an alien civilization could eventually work out the instructions on the Voyager golden record. Fourth, I accept Quine's indeterminacy of radical translation: for a genuinely alien language, all possible behavioral evidence is compatible with multiple mutually incompatible translation manuals, and there is no further fact of the matter about which manual is correct. Fifth, I hold that meaning resides in the inner message: once the outer layer has done its work, what a message says is fixed independently of any particular decoder — the decoded content is not receiver-relative. Sixth, I have a firm case verdict about a Borges-style library: a page produced by blind chance, which happens to parse as a coherent and even profound English paragraph, has no meaning; nobody meant it, so it does not mean. Seventh, another case verdict: a quartz crystal, though highly ordered, does not carry a frame message — a message-bearing signal differs from a crystal's regularity in kind and not merely in degree.

What I would like from you is an audit rather than a lecture. Go through the package and tell me: which pairs of these commitments are genuinely in tension, and how severe each tension is; which pairs only look to be in tension and why the appearance dissolves; and, for each genuine tension, what would have to give and what the revision would cost the rest of the package. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally bad, and close by telling me which single revision would clean up the most. I would especially appreciate your being blunt about whether the trouble is localized or whether, as my advisor half-suspects, the whole three-layer picture has to be renegotiated once the problems surface.
