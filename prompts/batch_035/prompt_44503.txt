So my friend and I had one of those stupid arguments that goes until 2am over one specific move in reasoning, and now I can't let it go. We were talking about what Dennett calls the strange inversion of reasoning -- how Darwin and Turing both pulled the same trick: take something that looks like it can ONLY be explained by understanding at the top (the eye needing a designer, conversation needing a mind) and show that you can actually get it from piles of dumb, local, competence-without-comprehension at the bottom. He kept saying ok fine for eyes and for passing the Turing test, but surely real comprehension has to come in SOMEWHERE, for the stuff that really matters, and I kept saying that's exactly the intuition the inversion exists to undermine, and at some point he called me "a confident Roomba" and we both went to bed annoyed. I clearly can't win this argument directly, so I want to win it satirically instead. Help me out.

Write me a deadpan corporate FAQ, around 650 words, from a fictional company that just launched a flagship AI product and is *proudly and aggressively* marketing it as comprehension-free. Like, that's the selling point on the box. The Q&A section should field increasingly worried customer questions -- "does it understand what it's doing when it answers me?", "who is understanding it, if not the system?", "are YOU (the engineers) the ones understanding it?" -- and answer every one of them cheerfully and correctly.

The humor has to come from actually getting the inversions right, not from hand-waving: the company spokespeople should deploy the real logic with unsettling enthusiasm -- natural selection built the vertebrate eye (and the engineer who designed the engineers, and so on down) without anybody understanding anything anywhere; whatever your brain does when it comprehends, a machine can do the same thing without needing a little understander hiding inside it; you are, as they might put it, a robot made of robots, so demanding a comprehend-er somewhere at the bottom just pushes the mystery down a level without ever discharging it. They should treat the customer's demand for comprehension somewhere, anywhere, the way a good philosopher treats it: as a leftover habit of explaining order top-down, the same habit the argument from design leaned on. Maybe let them offer the customer a promotional discount if she can say what the comprehension would be FOR, given that all the competences are already accounted for.

Important: the company should be *right* but insufferable about it. The satire targets the intuition that competence without comprehension is somehow cheating or fake -- not the company itself (they're correct, just the kind of correct you never want to be on the phone with). And it should stay deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader -- legible irony, not smug irony.

Please end the FAQ with the one question every customer actually wants to ask, and an answer that is 100% honest, 100% logically sound, and somehow the single least reassuring sentence ever printed on a product page.

Aim it at a reader who's read some Dennett or at least knows the Darwin-vs-design and Turing-vs-Cartesian framings, but keep it readable for someone who hasn't. No citations or reading lists, just the ideas doing the work. And no typos in the FAQ itself -- the typos are my job, appparently, I can see several in this message and I refuse to fix them.
