I've been keeping a running list of situations where one person blames another, because my gut keeps telling me that sometimes the blame is somehow "off" even when the person blamed actually did do something wrong. I gather philosophers call this standing. Anyway, here are my verdicts and I'd like to know if there's a principle that captures all of them, or whether I have to give some up.

My roommate lies constantly — small stuff, lying to wiggle out of plans, that sort of thing. When I once lied to him and he called me out for it, to my face, I felt his blame wasn't his to make, even though he was completely right that what I did was wrong. No standing.

A former bully, genuinely reformed for years, now a teacher who calls out kids bullying someone at his school. His blame seems totally fine to me, standing intact.

Same guy, except he never reformed, he just drifted out of bullying, and privately he thinks what he did back then was fine — he's just not that person anymore. When he calls out bullying, honestly? I still think he has standing. This one surprises me and I can't tell if I should trust it.

Someone with an impeccable record on honesty blames a casual acquaintance for lying. Obviously fine.

Someone else who also behaves impeccably but sincerely thinks morality is all made up, never endorses any norm, blames a wrongdoer. I think her blame is fine too? Which is weird next to the first case, since she's never committed to the norm the way my lying roommate at least implicitly has.

I eat a lot of meat, and I blame my sister for buying from a puppy mill. I think I have standing. I also blame my brother for how he talks to waiters. Standing fine there too. So maybe complicity in one thing doesn't infect standing for unrelated things, but I'm not sure where the line is.

Trivial version: a friend who is chronically five minutes late blames me for showing up an hour late and making her miss her train. I think her blame is fine despite the hypocrisy — the stakes are so different that it doesn't seem to count against her.

Venting version: that same lying roommate complains to a mutual friend about a third friend's lying, behind the third friend's back. Feels less bad than when he confronted me, even though the venting is the cowardly version. So maybe standing only matters for blame said to your face? But that seems backwards and I don't like it.

And the one I keep going back back and forth on: someone tells the roommate that his blame of me was hypocritical, i.e. blames him for blaming without standing. Is there such a thing as standing to blame someone for blameless blaming? Part of me thinks that second-order blame is always fine because it's just true (he WAS being a hypocrite), another part of me thinks it just spirals forever.

When I try to state a principle myself I get something like: you lose standing when you're guilty of the same kind of thing, so blame is only yours to make when you're in the clear on that front. But that doesn't obviously deliver the unrepentant-but-drifted bully or the five-minutes-late friend, and I can't decide whether it applies to venting. Lately I've been wondering if the whole framing is wrong and there's no separate thing called standing at all — maybe hypocritical blame just carries an extra false presupposition, something like "I'm the sort of person entitled to say this to you," and the wrongness is in that presupposition rather than in some missing permission. Does that view save all my verdicts? If nothing saves all of them, which should I drop and why? Also tell me what your preferred account says about a case I haven't listed: my father, who did exactly what I'm doing now at my age and never told me, blaming me for it — and whether it changes if he's since repented versus not.

One request on format: no headings, bullet points, bold, or tables — just continuous prose, like you'd write me an email.
