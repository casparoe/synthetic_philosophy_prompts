My closest friend from graduate school wrote her thesis on distributive justice, and although she refuses to be pinned to any -ism, her verdicts on concrete cases have been strikingly steady over the years. I have collected a good number of them: she holds that someone born blind is owed the extra resources needed for a decent life; that a person who could work but prefers to surf, and so ends up with less income, has no claim to assistance; that a skier who declined fairly priced injury insurance and was then injured is owed nothing; that a poor person who could not possibly have afforded that same insurance, and suffered the same injury, is owed compensation; and that an entrepreneur whose uninsured business failed must live with the loss. At our next reading group she has promised verdicts on three new cases, and I should like to predict them before she gives them. The cases: (a) a gambler who began betting voluntarily, became genuinely addicted, and is now destitute despite having had every early opportunity to stop — is he owed anything, and does the answer differ for his losses before and after the addiction took hold? (b) a person of low native talent who works as hard as anyone but, having taken no gamble of her own, earns far less than her equally diligent peers — is the shortfall compensable? (c) a devout woman who could easily afford injury insurance but declined it from a settled conviction that one must not bet against providence, and was injured. I am less concerned with the three answers than with the rule she is implicitly applying. Several rules fit her record so far — the natural candidates being that brute luck, but not option luck, calls for compensation, and that only disadvantages not flowing from a fully voluntary choice call for compensation — though I suspect there are others, perhaps ones on which the decisive question is whether a reasonable opportunity to insure was available. Please set out the candidate rules, say what each predicts for cases (a) to (c), give your best guess for each with your reasons, and tell me which further case would most cleanly separate the rules she might be running. Please write throughout in British English, and close by saying what would change your mind: which verdict of hers, or which consideration, would push you towards a different best guess, and where your reasoning is most likely to fail.
