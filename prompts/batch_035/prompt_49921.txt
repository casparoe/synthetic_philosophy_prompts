A colleague in my metaphysics seminar has been leaning hard on this analogy against deflationism: a true belief is like an accurate map. The map stands in a genuine structural relation to the territory — the squiggles line up with rivers, the scale is fixed, you can literally point at the correspondence — so, she says, truth has to be a real relation between belief and world, and the deflationist treating 'true' as a mere disquotational device is like someone insisting maps are useful fictions. She used it last week to get a room of grad students off deflationism within the hour. I want to push back but I need to know where the analogy is doing real work and where it's coasting. Give me the correspondence table first (what plays the role of territory, of scale, of the projection system, of a blank region on the map), then where the analogy breaks — I'm especially suspicious that map accuracy is parochial, since a hiking map and a driving map disagree without being rivals, and I want to know what that does to her argument — then what the analogy smuggles in that deflationism was built to deny, and finally whether her conclusion survives once the load-bearing disanalogies are out in the open, or whether a deflationist can just happily accept maps. Sections, in order: 'The mapping' (~150 words), 'Where it breaks' (~350), 'What it smuggles in' (~150), 'Verdict' (~100). Plain enough that I can foward it to the students afterwards.
