I co-host a small philosophy of mind podcast out of our grad department (we get maybe a couple hundred downloads an episode, so the audience skews people who know the basics already), and next month we've got a guest who defends the full-on Dennettian line on qualia -- the view that qualia as traditionally conceived are just an incoherent posit, and that we should quine them: the thought being that the term was supposed to pick out features of experience that are ineffable, intrinsic, private, and immediately apprehensible by the experiencer, and that no real feature of our mental lives has all four. I want to draft the interview questions in advance since he talks fast and I want to actually press him rather than just let him run the greatest hits.

The classic case I'm sure he'll bring up is the coffee tasters, Chase and Sanborn: two professional tasters who both stop liking the company blend, where Chase says the coffee tastes exactly the same to him and only his preferences have changed, while Sanborn insists his preferences are untouched and the coffee now tastes different to him because his taste buds have changed. Dennett's move is that introspection can't settle which of them is right, so the notion of the way the coffee tastes to you -- taken as something cleanly separable from your attitudes toward it -- doesn't cut nature at its joints. So I need a set of maybe eight to ten questions that go after the actual machinery here. At least one has to press the obvious rejoinder to the coffee case: the fact that you can't tell which of two explanations is true doesn't mean there's no fact of the matter -- I can't tell whether a noise in my walls is a mouse or the pipes, but it's one or the other. And each question should come with a follow-up keyed to the reply he's most likely to give, so I'm not stuck nodding along when he deflects.

Beyond the coffee case, there are a few other pressure points I want covered (spread them across the questions however works best): the beer drinker / acquired taste phenomenon, where it really seems from the inside like learning to like something is a different thing from it coming to taste different, and I want the follow-up to anticipate whatever he says about that apparent contrast; the pain problem, since pain feels like the reductio of the whole view -- whatever happens with coffee, pain doesn't seem like something you could be wrong about there being, and the follow-up should chase whatever distinction he draws between bodily sensations and tastes; the strawman worry, since most people these days use qualia or phenomenal consciousness in a weaker sense than the four-feature one, and I want to know if the quining project has a target left once you grant that; and the self-undermining worry, namely that his own conviction that the argument is compelling is presumably something he knows by introspection, so I want the follow-up ready for when he says introspection of beliefs is different from introspection of qualia. Also one question on what would actually count as evidence against the view -- what would a qualia realist have to produce that he'd take seriously. No generic getting-to-know-you stuff, no asking him to restate positions our listeners already know, and please make the follow-ups specific to the actual reply, not just could you say more about that. He's also a big heterophenomenology guy so if a follow-up can be built to make that method do real work instead of just being a slogan he hides behind, that would be great.
