I'm auditing an intro philosophy of mind course (retired engineer, doing it for fun) and one session was on dispositions — the idea that a glass being fragile just amounts to it being true that it would break if you struck it. The lecturer said this simple would-if analysis is famously broken, gave one example, and then had to run for a flight before I could ask anything. So ever since I've been collecting my own cases where a thing clearly HAS the disposition but the would-if statement comes out false — or, weirder, the other way around — and I've noticed they don't all feel like the same kind of failure. Four from my notebook: (1) a sorcerer guards a priceless glass; it's genuinely fragile, but he's sworn that the instant anyone tries to strike it, he'll cast a spell making it permanently unbreakable before the blow lands. So if you struck it, it wouldn't break — but it seems flatly wrong to say the glass isn't fragile right now. (2) a spy takes an antidote every morning and then drinks from a vial of poison; the poison in the vial is still fully lethal, nothing about IT has been changed, yet nothing happens when she drinks. (3) a gag gift: a mug made of some unbreakable space-age material, but rigged with a tiny hidden charge so the slightest tap tears it apart in a way that looks exactly like ordinary breaking. If you tapped it, it would break — and yet calling it fragile seems absurd, it would shatter even if it were made of solid granite. (4) my own invention, from a conversation with my electrician friend: a wire is disposed to overheat when too much current runs through it, but the whole circuit sits behind a breaker that trips within milliseconds of any spike, so the wire never actually glows — and crucially, nothing about the wire itself ever gets altered, unlike in the sorcerer case where the glass's very nature gets rewritten the moment trouble starts.

So my question is basically: do these failure modes of the would-if analysis have actual names in the dispositions literature, and are there more of them than I've found? I'd also genuinely like a ruling on whether my case 4 is just case 1 in different clothes (both have a guardian that guarantees the manifestation never happens) or whether it's closer to case 2, or actually its own thing — what's nagging me is that in 1 the disposition itself gets destroyed as part of the setup, while in 2 and 4 the disposition sits there completely intact the whole time. If there IS a standard distinction between "something removes the disposition in the nick of time" and "the disposition survives untouched but the outcome gets prevented," I want to know its name, and whether people think it's a real distinction or just fussing about timing.

Please give me a table with one row per case, with these columns: case # (1–4 as above), the established term for that kind of counterexample if one exists (or literally "no standard name" if it doesn't), whether the disposition survives (yes / no / complicated — with a few words why), whether the plain would-if statement is true or false, and which OTHER cases in the table (if any) it threatens to collapse into. Then a short paragraph on case 4 specifically: closest named category, what separates it from its nearest neighbors, and if it genuinely falls between the cracks, coin a name for it and give a definition that would let someone sort future cases. If there are whole families of counterexamples to the would-if analysis that my four cases DON'T cover (especially any working in the opposite direction from case 3, where the statement is true but the disposition is missing), list them briefly below the table — I'd rather find out my notebook is incomplete than keep thinking four cases is the whole story.
