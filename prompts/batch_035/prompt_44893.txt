My phil language reading group does this thing where each of us writes up a one-page manifesto of current views and the rest tear it apart. My turn is next week and it's vagueness edition, so before I hand it over I want a sanity check on whether my package even hangs together:

(1) I'm an epistemicist: for every vague predicate there's a sharp cutoff, and our inability to find it is plain old ignorance, not indeterminacy in the world or the semantics.
(2) That ignorance has a source: margin for error. If I know a patch is red, then any patch indiscriminable from it is red too — you can only know vague facts with a margin, which is why no one can know where the cutoff sits.
(3) Meaning is fixed entirely by use: if two communities match in dispositions and practice, their words mean the same, and there's nothing more to what "heap" means than that.
(4) But I cant accept that there are facts about what my own words mean that are unknowable in principle. Williamson seems comfortable with meaning being cognitively homeless; I'm not. Someone with total information about use and psychology could in principle work out what I mean.
(5) I hold KK: if you know that p, you're in a position to know that you know it.
(6) Classical logic and bivalence all the way down, borderlines included — no truth-value gaps, no supertruth.
(7) And I deny higher-order vagueness: "borderline" itself is precise, so there's a last clear case of a heap and a first borderline case, sharp as anything else.

I can already feel (2) and (5) rubbing against each other somehow — something like a sorites that runs through knowledge rather than truth — but I honestly can't work out whether it's fatal or whether one of them just needs a qualifier. And I can't see whether (1) plus (3) leave any room for the cutoff being unknowable in the way (4) demands, or whether that's fine because unknowable-in-principle and undetermined-by-use are just different things. Rank the tensions by severity, flag which pairs only look like they clash, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please write in British English.
