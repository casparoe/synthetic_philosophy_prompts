Write a dialogue between two philosophers watching a demonstration of a careenium: a hydraulic billiard table whose surface is continuously tilted by actuators under the control of a computer that models the table's real balls as idealized "simmballs" and nudges the surface so that the real balls track the simulated ones — the setup Dennett uses to raise the question of whether the simmballs, rather than the machinery, do the pushing. One philosopher holds that the simmballs genuinely do causal work: the pattern level is where prediction, intervention, and control live, so saying the simmballs push the balls around is not shorthand for anything more fundamental. The other holds that all the pushing is done by hydraulic fluid and motors, and that "simmball causation" is convenient bookkeeping rather than causation in the world. Partway through, the engineer running the demonstration introduces a modification: the computer now also simulates a "shadow simmball" with no counterpart ball on the table, and tilts the surface so that the real balls roll out of its projected path. Have the two use this case to sharpen their dispute about what "real" causal work could mean that does not collapse into either bare counterfactual dependence or interventionist control. Both must be equally sharp — each should score genuine points against the other's actual claims — and the dialogue should end with the disagreement located precisely rather than resolved. Aim for roughly 1000 words.
