hi, could I get some help pressure-testing my position on consciousness before I circulate it? context: our reading group is doing a session on illusionism in a couple of weeks, and I've been tasked with writing a one-page "where I stand" piece that the others will then tear apart. I've written it as five seperate points, and before I send it round and recieve it back covered in comments, I'd like to know whether it actually hangs together. it feels right to me, which I suspect is a bad sign.

please take on the role of a professional philosopher of mind who works on consciousness and takes both the science and the philosophy seriously. and please think carefully — maybe do some brainstorming first, trace what each commitment commits me to, don't just eyeball it and tell me it all sounds sensible.

here's the set:

1. I'm an illusionist about phenomenal consciousness, broadly in the vein of Frankish and Dennett: there are no qualia as standardly conceived, no intrinsic, ineffable phenomenal properties over and above the functional and physical facts.

2. but I'm not an eliminativist about consciousness, and I don't want to be. I want to keep saying, in plain english and without scare quotes, that there is something it's like to be me. my picture is the user-interface one: consciousness is the simplified control surface that a hugely complex system presents, and it is real the way the desktop icons are real for the user of the operating system — wrong at the hardware level, perfectly real at the level the system actually lives at.

3. I take the interface picture strictly, though. the "user" is not a little person inside: the self is just another icon on the desktop, a self-model the system builds, and the user illusion runs all the way down. no one is home.

4. introspective seemings are incorrigible in a narrow sense: I can be wrong about what my experience represents (that there's a lemon in front of me, that the traffic light is amber) but I cannot be wrong about how things currently seem to me. that's my one rock.

5. at the same time, I fully accept the empirical work on confabulation — split-brain patients, choice blindness, post-hoc rationalisation, the lot — as showing that the introspective channel will cheerfully make things up whenever it can't see the actual causes of my behaviour.

I have a feeling at least one pair of these is in trouble, but I genuinely can't tell which. a couple of the others said 1 and 2 are flat-out contradictory and that I should just drop 2, which didn't sound right to me either. so before I circulate anything I'd like a proper audit: go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad, and for each one say what would have to give and what giving it up costs me. and be specific — if the cheapest fix turns out to be quietly changing the meaning of one of my claims between premises, I want to know that too, because that's exactly the move I'm worried I'm already making.

oh, and please write your answer in british english.
