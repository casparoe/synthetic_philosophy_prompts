So, context: I'm a journalist, I cover science and ideas for a mid-sized paper, and lately I've been on the museum beat with all the restitution and "should we still hang this guy" coverage. My editor wants something lighter for the weekend section and I had an idea for a parody piece. The concept is a fictional museum — call it the Museum of the Morally Compromised Masterpiece, or invent a funnier name — and the whole piece is just the wall labels. Exactly five numbered labels, each only a sentence or two like real museum wall text, deadpan curatorial voice, no intro and no outro, nothing but the labels themselves.

Every label is for a made-up artwork by a made-up artist whose misdeed you invent (keep it non-graphic, we're a family paper), and the five labels should each quietly take a different position in the actual philosphy fight over whether immoral art can be good art. So: one full moralist label treating the artist's cruelty as a straight-up aesthetic defect in the work; one pure autonomist label (something like: the man stayed home, only the painting is on the wall); one moderate moralist label where the flaw counts only because the work itself invites you to enjoy the cruelty, not just because the biography is bad; one that makes the opposite mistake and inflates a mediocre painting because the artist was a saint; and one final label that just concedes the thing is a masterpiece nobody can look at anymore. Feel free to make the fake artworks carry some of the comedy — like a nauseatingly sentimental landscape by a genuinely evil person, or a technically brilliant battle scene.

The joke has to come from getting the debate right, not from curators being dumb — someone who actually knows the autonomism vs ethicism literature should laugh and feel it was fair. Write it so it could run in the paper as-is.
