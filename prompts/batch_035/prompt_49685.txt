As a novelist, I'm drafting a piece set in a cosmos run on the dark forest logic: to broadcast your location is to invite annihilation, since eliminating a stranger is always cheaper than trusting him. I want a story that dramatizes this reasoning rather than explaining it, and I want it stripped of the familiar furniture — no humans, no interstellar war, no last stand. The entire tale should unfold inside a single alien civilization, in the offices of its signal-censorship bureau, where a minor official whose job is to erase stray transmissions discovers an anomaly: a faint signal that, when decoded, proves to be an echo of her own civilization's earliest leaked broadcasts, returned intact from somewhere dark. No shot is ever fired; the drama must live entirely in the bureau's debate over what it would mean to answer oneself. Keep it under 900 words, and end at the instant the decision is made, not on its consequences.
