me and my brother-in-law have been going round and round about when a chatbot should refuse to do what you asked it to, and the argument has been stuck for like two years in a way thats starting to bug me. He's on one side: if a tool second-guesses you it's useless, you're the adult, the AI is fancy autocomplete and fancy autocomplete doesn't get a vote. I lean the other way but can't quite say why: some asks are obviously not ok (help me scam my grandma) and some refusals are obviously dumb (I had one refuse to write a fake breakup text for a short story because it was "deceptive"), and where the line goes seems to slide around depending on who you ask.

Here's the weird part though. On the actual cases we agree a LOT. When we go down a list — weapons stuff, homework, sad stories, medical questions — we match on maybe 90% of them. What we can never agree on is what the remaining 10% is even ABOUT. I have a half-baked theory I want you to either take seriously or tear apart: we're not really arguing about harm, we're arguing about what kind of thing the assistant is. He treats it like a vending machine, so a refusal is somebody else's hand coming between you and the slot. I keep slipping into treating it like a person behind a counter, and for a person behind a counter there's a real difference between "I won't do that because it's bad for you" (patronizing) and "I won't do that because I don't do that" (just having a policy, like a pharmacy that won't stock something no matter how much you want it). If my theory is right the whole fight is basically verbal — same cases, different metaphysics of the machine — and it should dissolve once we notice that. But it doesn't dissolve, so either my theory is wrong or something else is going on underneath.

So that's what I want from you: a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a verbal disagreement, a values one, or a hidden bet about what these systems are going to become? And whatever you diagnose, tell me what would actually count as progress on it — some distinction we could adopt, some test case we could construct, a way of rephrasing the question, anything — because after two years of Thanksgiving arguments I'd like to either win or finally understand why neither of us can.
