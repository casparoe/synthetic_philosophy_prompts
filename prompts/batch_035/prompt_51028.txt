Last week she used it to argue for abolishing the death penalty specifically: a wrongful execution is irreversible in a way wrongful imprisonment isnt, so the ratio applies to capital punishment with special force. Someone pushed back that if you take the line seriously it applies to any punishment at all, or even to any state action that risks convicting the innocent, so it cant do the anti-death-penalty work she wants — it either proves too much or nothing. I left confused about what the line is even claiming. Can you lay out the different precise theses it could be expressing — like a threshold for when juries should convict, an expected-harm comparison, a deontological constraint on the state, a claim about the asymmetry between false positives and false negatives — and say for each whether it's plausible and whether it actually supports her conclusion about execution in particular rather than punishment in general? Also not sure whether the number ten does any real work or if its just rhetorical — would the argument change if it were a hundred, or two? A list format would help.
