Before I evaluate any further proposals for who counts as a member of the demos, I want to fix the standard of adequacy in advance. Write the desiderata: what any principled answer to the boundary problem — the question of who is entitled to a say in a collective decision — must deliver. The requirements should be driven by concrete cases, and I want the cases to include, at minimum: long-term resident noncitizens who are subject to the laws but excluded from the vote; tourists and other transiently subjected persons; children and people with profound cognitive disabilities; future people affected by present climate and constitutional decisions; and persons outside the territory who are powerfully affected by its decisions (through emissions, sanctions, or monetary policy) without being governed by them in the ordinary sense. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that makes it a requirement rather than a preference of one camp, and say which of your requirements you would treat as non-negotiable and which are negotiable — the all-affected and all-subjected principles, for instance, appear to deliver opposite verdicts on several of the cases above, so a defensible list has to take a position on whether either is a genuine desideratum. Then flag any pair of your desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and explain why; that incompatibility, if real, is the part I most want to see, since it would show the boundary problem is not merely unsolved but structurally underdetermined. You may close with a paragraph on how the all-affected principle, the all-subjected principle, and a demos-of-the-demos view (legitimacy flows from a pre-existing people rather than from affectedness) fare against the list, but keep the weight on the criteria. Do not propose your own complete theory of the demos; the specification is the deliverable.
