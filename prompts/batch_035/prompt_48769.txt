I'm preparing a position statement on pragmatist theories of truth for a graduate seminar, and before I submit it I want an audit of whether my commitments are jointly tenable. Here is the set:

(1) Truth outruns warranted assertibility. Justification and truth can come apart in both directions: a belief can be fully warranted yet false, and there can be truths that no one ever believes.

(2) The best account of truth available is Peirce's convergence view: a belief is true just in case it is the belief that inquiry, indefinitely prolonged and pursued under ideal conditions, would ultimately settle on. I take this to be a genuine account of what truth is, not merely a prediction about what inquirers will happen to believe.

(3) James is wrong. The true is not the expedient in the way of thinking; expediency, comfort, and usefulness for a particular person's life do not make beliefs true. A stabilizing delusion is still a delusion.

(4) However, in the kind of case James was pointing at in The Will to Believe — where the option is live, forced, and momentous, where evidence cannot decide the matter in advance, and where the belief's being held partly determines whether it comes true (paradigmatically, mutual trust between people) — believing can be what makes the belief true. I do not regard this as a defect of truth in those cases but as its ordinary operation.

(5) Inquiry is constitutively social and value-laden. Which questions are worth asking, what counts as a good answer, and when inquiry has gone far enough are fixed by the aims of the community of inquiry, and there is no inquiry from nowhere.

(6) Fallibilism holds across the board, including for commitment (2) itself.

I also hold a case verdict that I suspect is relevant: there is a determinate fact about the exact number of leaves on some particular tree in the Cretaceous, even though no possible inquiry, however prolonged, could recover that number.

Please go through these commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how badly. For each real tension, say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me elsewhere in the set. Then tell me which single change would resolve the most. I have my suspicions about where the problem lies, but I would rather you not take cues from me: treat the set symmetrically and be specific about what each commitment commits me to.
