So heres my situation: I'm a retired biochemist -- 35 years, mostly QC work, so protocols are in my blood -- and since retiring I've gotten deep into epistemology. I just worked through Nozick's tracking theory in Philosophical Explanations, the idea that knowing p means your belief tracks the truth: if p were false you wouldn't believe it, and given how you actually formed the belief, if p is true you do believe it. I also volunteer as the de facto fact-checker for our neigborhood newsletter (maybe 800 subscribers, weekly, nobody signs off but me), and I keep tripping over the word "knows." Writers print things like "the mayor knows the bridge is safe" or "residents know the developer will back out," and I have no principal rule for when to let it stand versus change it to "says" or "believes." Write me a decision procedure I can actually run in five minutes per instance: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure says downgrade to "claims," and where it has to hand the call back to me because it's a judgment about the case, not a fact about it. I want the tracking conditions to do real work, including the part where the method of belief-formation matters (the official who ran the test herself versus the one who trusts last year's report) -- but also flag where tracking gives weird verdicts, like the grandmother case, or the lottery-style case where someone "knows" their raffle ticket lost. Nozick's thing about closure also keeps coming up: writers infer a "knows" for a whole chain of consequences from one verified link ("she knows the levee meets the standard, so she knows the flood insurance stays valid"), and since Nozick famously lets you know p without knowing its obvious consequences, tell me whether the procedure should have anything to say about that or whether that's a step too deep for a newsletter like ours. Then run the procedure on three items from this week's draft: (1) "the county engineer knows the levee holds to the 500-year standard, she ran the models herself"; (2) "longtime residents know the festival will be cancelled again this year, it always is"; (3) "the donor knows his gift is tax-deductible, the accountant told him so." And be honest about what the procedure can't settle.
