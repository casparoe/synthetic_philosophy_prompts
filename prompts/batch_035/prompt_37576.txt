At a public hearing on whether our county should end its contract with the private hospital network and fund a public system instead, a physician closed her remarks by declaring that healthcare is a right, not a privilege, and the room applauded as though that settled the policy question. Afterward it struck me that I could not say what precise claim she had made, and I suspect the people applauding did not share one. Please lay out, as a menu I can bring to the follow-up debate, the distinct theses that sentence could express — legal right versus moral right, positive right to provision versus negative right against interference, a claim about what may permissibly be bought and sold, a claim about who bears responsibility when someone goes untreated, and any other readings you find, including ones I have not thought of. For each reading, say whether it is near-trivial, substantive but defensible, or false, and — most important — whether it actually supports the specific measure at issue, since some readings seem compatible with a regulated market in insurance and care while others would rule out even a well-functioning one. Finish by identifying which reading the physician most plausibly needed for her conclusion, and whether that reading survives the classic objection that positive rights impose unassigned obligations on strangers.
