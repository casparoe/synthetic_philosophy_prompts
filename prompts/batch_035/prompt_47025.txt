My brother-in-law started a philosophy MA this fall and got on a Kant kick, and at Thanksgiving he laid the following argument on me, and it's been living in my head rent-free since — because I think I found a way to run his own template on something else and get a conclusion he'd hate, and neither of us can say where the template breaks without breaking his original argument too.

His argument for the external world: (1) To so much as doubt whether there's a world outside my mind, I have to have experiences that present themselves as of a world. (2) For experiences to present themselves that way, they must have an order to them — things persisting, events following one another, some things going together regularly. (3) That order can't be something I made up, because I keep getting surprised by it: things regularly don't go the way I'd have them go, and I can't change that by deciding to. (4) So the order in my experience isn't contributed by me, and whatever contributes it is what I mean by "the external world." (5) Therefore doubting the external world is incoherant — the doubt presuposses the very thing doubted. He wrapped up by saying this is why the dream hypothesis was never a real threat, and honestly it felt like a relief, because I'd never known how to answer that one.

Here's my problem. My father died this spring, and I can't shake the sense that he's still around somehow. I didn't put that sense there, I can't remove it by deciding to, and things keep happening that feel like they're from him. So I ran his template on it: (6) To doubt whether my father is still with me in some sense, I have to have a sense of him that presents itself as being of him. (7) That sense can't be something I made up, because I keep getting surprised by it and I can't switch it off by deciding to. (8) So the sense isn't contributed by me, and whatever contributes it is what I mean by "his being around in some sense" — so doubting that he's around in some sense is incoherent, the doubt presupposing the thing doubted. And I don't believe it. I think that sense is grief doing what grief does. But every disanalogy I reach for — that his premise claimed something necessary to experience as such while mine is just a fact about me right now, or whatever — sounds like special pleading that a determined skeptic could turn right back against the external world version, and my brother-in-law said as much, gleefully, before realizing it cut against him too. So: where does the cut go, if anywhere? And, separately, this is the part I actually care about — was the Thanksgiving relief fake? Does anything of this style of argument survive at all, or do I have to accept that I'll never have an answer to the dream hypothesis?
