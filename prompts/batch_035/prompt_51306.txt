My husband and I have been trying to have our first child. I have a chronic condition that is controlled by a medication my doctor says carries roughly a two percent chance of a hearing impairment if taken through early pregnancy, while going off it for the first trimester means a serious risk of a flare-up that would put me out of commission for months. We were prepared to accept the small risk and go ahead, but my sister stopped me cold over the holidays. She pointed out that if we waited a year, the child we would have then would be a different child entirely — a different egg, a different chance meeting of sperm and egg, a different person. On her way of thinking, I cannot be wronging anyone by conceiving now, because the child who ends up with the impairment simply would not exist if I had waited, and a life with impaired hearing is surely better than no life at all. My mother pushed back. She said the point is not that the child could compare their life to non-existence; the point is that I would knowingly make things harder for them when I could have made them easier.

I have been turning this over for weeks and cannot get it straight. Both of them seem right about something, and if my sister is right, it feels like the risk barely matters at all — which cannot be right either, because I know I would feel very differently if the chance were one in ten thousand. So please do not just tell me what to do; help me see the shape of the disagreement. What exactly is the difference between making someone worse off and making a worse-off someone? Does my sister's principle really extend that far — does it mean no one can be wronged by any risk imposed through the very act that brings them into being, so long as their life is worth living? And does my mother's reply actually answer it, or does it just restate the intuition the principle is meant to explain away? Since I know this is the kind of question people have thought hard about, I would like you to be candid about where the experts stand: please mark clearly which parts of your answer would be accepted by most people who study this and which parts are genuinely disputed, and do not present one side's view as though it were settled.
