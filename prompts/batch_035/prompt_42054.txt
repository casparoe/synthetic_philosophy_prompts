So I got into a long argument with my friend Maya last night (like four hours, she finally left at 1am) about van Fraassen's Reflection principle and I'm still thinking about it, so I want to actually sort out what I believe. Her line: Reflection is basically the claim that you should treat your future self as an epistemic authority, so if you know you'll believe X to degree 0.9 tomorrow, you should already believe X to 0.9 now. And she thinks it's obviously broken, because of all the counterexamples -- brainwashing, propaganda, knowing you'll be talked into a cult, knowing you'll be panic-nervous before the exam and wildly overestimate the chance of failing, Ulysses and the sirens, etc.

My take, which she thinks is a cop-out: Reflection isn't a rule about deferring to whoever will inhabit your body tomorrow. It's a condition on what it takes to be a single agent extended through time. So when you can foresee that your future judgments will be distorted (by manipulation, by fear, by the third glass of wine), the rational response isn't to "violate Reflection," it's to recognize that some of those future psychological states won't fully belong to the agent-you that Reflection ranges over. The principle survives by getting pickier about what counts as you.

Please describe the most important implications of this reading of Reflection for practical life. Specifically: when are precommitment devices (deposit contracts, giving a friend my phone so I can't text my ex, Ulysses-style arrangements) rational self-governance versus a kind of self-betrayal or disenfranchisement of my future self? What do I do in the common middle case where I can predict my opinion will change but genuinely can't tell whether it'll be a legitimate update or a distortion -- is there any principled line between "I'll learn better" and "I'll get corrupted," or do we just eyeball it? What happens to the standard advice to "trust the person you'll become"? And does this reading have any implications for how I should relate to my past self, like whether I should keep honoring commitments to a version of me whose values I've since abandoned?
