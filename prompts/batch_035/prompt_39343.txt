So here's the set — tell me if it hangs together:

1. If a nation like Scotland or Catalonia or Quebec has a settled majority that wants independence and wants a referendum, it should get one, and if it wins, the result should be honoured. That's just democracy — the people affected deciding together.
2. But the unit that votes is the nation, not any random patch of territory. Shetland doesn't get to vote itself out of an independent Scotland the day after, and I don't accept the "well then any neighbourhood can secede" slippery slope — nations are the units that matter, that's the whole point.
3. The franchise should be residence-based: whoever actually lives in the territory gets the vote, newcomers and 16-year-olds included, and people who were born there but moved away don't. 4. Half of me buys the grievance requirement. 5. Secession shouldn't be unilateral, full stop. Spain was right that the 2017 vote was unconstitutional, whatever I think of how they handled the aftermath, and an agreed legal process like Edinburgh got in 2014 is the only legitimate route.
6. I'm pretty sure self-determination in international law is basically a decolonization principle — I don't think Scotland or Catalonia has an actual legal right to leave under it, whatever the moral case is.
