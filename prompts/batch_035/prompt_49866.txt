I teach an advanced undergraduate seminar on causation and want a one-page decision-tree handout that sorts students from the shared starting commitment — that there are token causal relations among particular events — to a named position: the Humean regularity view, the simple counterfactual-dependence view, Lewis's own position on which dependence need only hold somewhere along a chain, Woodward-style interventionism, a contrastive or context-sensitive view, and a pluralism on which several of these are each correct relative to a purpose. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from the list of names: pose the branch questions first, then locate the positions as leaves, and mark any combination of answers that nobody occupies, with one sentence on whether that is because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Each branch question must be a concrete case that a student could answer without knowing which label it leads to; include at least these five among the nodes. (1) Preemption with a backup: a bodyguard quietly knocks a poisoned glass off a table, and an assassin who had been waiting to shoot only in case the poisoning failed then fires; ask who caused the death. (2) Symmetric overdetermination: two riflemen fire simultaneously, either shot alone being fatal. (3) An omission: a contract gardener never waters the flowers, which die, and no one else would have watered them; ask whether the failure to water caused the flowers' death. (4) Double prevention with a dead backup: a defender destroys a missile before it reaches a city, and the attackers had a second missile set to launch only if the first was destroyed — but that one was faulty and would never have launched; ask what, if anything, caused the city's survival. (5) A norm-sensitive tiebreak: two officials must each sign, in either order, for a permit to issue; both sign, and we ordinarily credit the second signature with causing the permit to issue because it completed the requirement; ask whether the second rather than the first signature caused it. Keep the tree to at most eight leaves, and flag explicitly any pair of positions your tree cannot cleanly separate — for instance, if nothing in your questions genuinely distinguishes the interventionist leaf from the simple dependence leaf, say so rather than inventing a node that only appears to split them. Finally, add a short closing note stating how confident you are in each leaf's placement and singling out the one node you are least sure actually divides the positions the way you have assigned them.
