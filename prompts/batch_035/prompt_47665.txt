i've been a gigging bassist and songwriter for about fifteen years, self taught, no conservatory. i know functional harmony cold though -- voice leading, why the leading tone has to resolve, suspensions, how a ii-V-I is basically deferred gratification, the whole thing. a friend who reads a lot of philosophy got me onto Schopenhauer's views about music and honestly it's the only philosophy of art i've read that feels like it was written by someone who'd actually had to play off a chart. the way i understand him: all the other arts copy appearances, he calls them Ideas or something, basically Plato's leftovers -- but music doesn't copy appearances at all, it's somehow a direct image of the will itself, the thing underneath appearances. and he does this wild mapping where the bass is inorganic nature, the middle voices of the harmony are plants and animals, and the melody is the human individual, constantly wandering away from the tonic and coming back at the end, with every delay of resolution being striving or suffering. plus his point that music is universal without being about anything specific -- it gives you joy and sorrow in the abstract, without telling you whose or about what, which is honestly why you can slap it under any scene in a movie and it works.

so here's what i want. restate his claims one at a time in my vocabulary: voice functions, counterpoint, tension and resolution, register, phrase structure, the stuff i'd actually use to talk about a tune. be faithful, don't soften him into a generic music-is-emotional guy, he's making much weirder and more specific claims than that. then the residue report: which of his claims map cleanly onto what i already know as a player (i suspect the melody-wandering-and-returning one does, since that's basically just phrase structure with the cadence delayed), which ones have to be bent to fit, and which ones have no expression in music-theoretic vocabulary at all. for that last pile, tell me honestly whether it's my vocabulary being too thin -- it's built for analyzing scores, not for saying what a piece does to me in the dark -- or whether his claims were never really determinate in the first place, like the talk about the will and the thing-in-itself is doing work that can't survive being made precise.

one more thing: he ranks music above every other art, and the directness is supposed to be why. when you do the translation, don't smuggle that ranking in as if it were just a fact about harmony. i want to know whether it translates at all, or whether it comes out the other side as a fancy restatement of music bypasses words, which a lyricist like me already believes for way cheaper reasons.
