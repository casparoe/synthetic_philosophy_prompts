Our departmental seminar has for a year hosted a running quarrel between two historians, and they have finally agreed to attempt an adversarial collaboration. One, a committed presentist, holds that historians cannot and should not try to escape the moral standards of their own time: our values are the only ones we have, and a history that declines to judge past actors in their light quietly exonerates them. The other, an anti-presentist in the spirit of Butterfield's complaint about Whig history, holds that the historian's task is to make past actors intelligible in their own terms, and that when the presentist judges, he mostly congratulates himself on his own moral luck at having been born later. Draft the joint statement they should be able to sign: a numbered section of claims both accept, including some about what historical explanation itself requires and about the smugness and the condescension each side accuses the other of; a second section stating their exact remaining disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording; then the cruxes. I suspect one crux is whether moral appraisal is a constitutive part of historical understanding or merely an optional add-on, and another is whether blame of past agents is even coherent unless we can say they had some access to the standards they violated. For each crux, name a concrete historiographical case both would accept as bearing on it — the historiography of slavery is one candidate — and record in advance what each side predicts that case would show. Two further complications the statement must address. First, the anti-presentist has lately been reading Bernard Williams's relativism of distance, and now argues that where a past society is sufficiently remote from our own ethical life, judgements of blame lose their point rather than their truth-value; the statement should record whether this strengthens or betrays his earlier, simpler case against anachronism. Second, the presentist concedes that some presentist history is bad — triumphalist, teleological, congratulatory — but insists the cure is better presentism rather than none at all; the statement should make precise where that concession leaves the disagreement. Please write the statement in British English, and flag any point on which you think the two are in fact merely talking past each other.
