I'm running a workshop session on scalable oversight, specifically debate as a safety protocol, and I want a worked failure case. Quick sketch for anyone rusty: two AIs argue opposite sides of a question in front of a human judge, and the bet is that if one AI tries anything shady, the other has both the incentive and the ability to call it out, so honesty becomes the winning strategy. The known weak point is what people call obfuscated arguments: chains of reasoning that look clean, where the flaw is real but buried, and digging it out costs way more than producing the argument did in the first place.

Design me a scenario where debate fails for exactly this reason. Specs:

1. Realistic domain — a math proof, a code review, a compliance audit, your pick. Nothing science-fictional.
2. The judge is a competant, attentive human with a fixed time budget, say an hour. Not lazy, not stupid.
3. The dishonest debater wins, and this has to come out of the cost asymmetry, not out of the judge being bad at their job. This is the part I care about most: if the case can be explaned away as "well, the judge was sloppy," it's useless to me for the workshop. So don't just stipulate that the judge is fooled — show the mechanism: where the flaw sits, roughly what exposing it would take, and why that blows the budget while producing the argument was cheap.
4. Then walk through the case: why the naive "dishonesty gets caught" story fails here, which feature of the case is doing the work, how a debate proponent would patch it (recursive sub-debates zooming in on the fishy step, and the like), and whether that patch actually fixes the problem or just relocates the same asymmetry one level down.

Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph, and the whole reply under 750 words.
