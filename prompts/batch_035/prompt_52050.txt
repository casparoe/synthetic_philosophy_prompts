I'm writing a paper on what Parfit called rational irrationality — cases where it is rational, at one time, to form or announce an intention that it will be irrational, at a later time, to act on — and I want to know where a disagreement in my seminar actually lives, because I think it is not where we keep saying it is.

Take Parfit's hitchhiker. In the textbook version, the hitchhiker is dying in the desert; a passing driver will rescue him only if she predicts he will pay the agreed sum on arrival; and the hitchhiker, whatever he says or intends, will in fact face a choice at the payout point where paying is costly and buys him nothing further. My own credence that the hitchhiker, standing safely at his destination with wallet in hand, has decisive all-things-considered prudential reason to pay is about 0.3. My colleague, who works on agency and self-constitution, gives it about 0.8. Please give me your number, but I care far more about the decomposition than the total. At minimum I want the question split into terms like these: whether practical rationality attaches to acts at moments or to policies and dispositions extended over time, and if the latter, whether a policy evaluation can generate reasons at the terminal node or only explain the earlier choice retroactively; whether the earlier decision binds in anything like the way a promise binds, given that a promise made under compulsion is normally void and this promise was prudent precisely because it would be broken or kept irrationally; whether intuitions elicited by a case stipulating a near-perfect predictor tell us anything about agents like us, for whom predictors are at best noisy; and any further terms you think the total credence genuinely depends on. Assign each a probability with a single line of justification, show how they combine into your number, and then tell me two things: which single term the final figure is most sensitive to, and whether my 0.3 and my colleague's 0.8 most plausibly diverge on that term or on some other one. If you think the framing itself is confused — for instance that the question of whether to pay is ill-posed because the case leaves indeterminate whether the hitchhiker can still revise, or that prudential reason at a node with no future is a degenerate notion — say so, but then still give the number, since my colleague and I managed to.
