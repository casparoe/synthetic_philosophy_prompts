I teach intro to political theory at a community college — 18 students, one 75-minute session, and they've skimmed The Prince but basically stopped reading once we got to the fortune chapters. I want a game where they run into Machiavelli's fortune/virtù thing themselves instead of me explaining the river metaphor at them for the fortieth time in my career.

Here's what I want the game to actually produce: not pure luck and not pure skill. His claim is that fortune decides about half of things and leaves the rest to us, and that she shows her force mostly where nobody bothered to build defenses in advance — so the game should reward preparation but still have real randomness that punches through. Rounds with random events (a flood, mercenaries defect, a neighboring state collapses), players holding limited resources, and the interesting choice being when to spend on defenses vs. when to spend on opportunities. If they sit around doing nothing, the random events should eat them; if they prepare perfectly, they should still sometimes lose anyway. I want them arguing at the end about whether the winner deserved it.

One mechanic I've been kicking around — tell me if it works or replace it: each player gets assigned a fixed persona at the start, impetuous or cautious, with rules about what that persona must do each round. Then, exactly once per game, a player can pay a steep cost to break persona. That's my attempt to make them feel the thing where your fixed character clashes with changing circumstances, and the failure is not being able to change yourself when the times shift. If there's a better way to get that into the rules, do that instead.

Also need from you: the debrief, mapping specific moments in the game onto specific claims — which mechanic is the river and which is the armor, roughly. What will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (my guess is half of them treat it as a gambling game and the other half over-prepare out of fear of losing face). And be blunt about what a dice game just cannot reproduce about fortune as he means it — I suspect there's something about fortune in the sense of the founding of states and legitimacy that no classroom game will touch, and I want to know what I have to supply myself in the debrief. 75 minutes total including debrief, no real money, and I only have cards, dice, and printed sheets for materials.
