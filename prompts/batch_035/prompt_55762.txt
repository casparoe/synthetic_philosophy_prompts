I'm TA-ing an intermediate course on rational choice and ethics, about 18 students, one 75-minute section. Next week we hit interpersonal utility comparisons and this topic always dies on the page — students nod along at "you can't add my utils to your utils" without ever feeling why it's a problem, or why anyone ever thought you could. I want to fix that with an in-class game where the impossibility shows up as a practical breakdown in the room, not as a theorem on the board.

My rough idea: split the class into "citizens" and "planners." Citizens privately get a utility table mapping a small menu of goods (candy bars, coffee vouchers, a small potted plant, extra credit points, whatever's cheap) onto numbers — but each citizen's table is in its own made-up currency, one reports in gleebs, another in zeds, etc. The planners get a fixed budget of goods and one job: maximize total utility. Citizens can submit written statements to the planner, but the planner never sees anyone's scale. Somewhere in the middle I want a reveal: two citizens' tables were secretly identical up to a positive affine transformation, so an allocation that looked perfectly fair was wildly unequal in real terms, and one that looked unequal was fine. And maybe a second twist where one citizen is quietly allowed to multiply all their numbers by 100 and resubmit, so the class sees the strategic-reporting problem pile on top of the scale problem.

Design the full thing: rules, materials, a round-by-round timeline, payoffs that don't involve real money, and a debrief that maps specific moments in the game onto the actual positions — the ordinalist who says the comparison is meaningless, the person who says it's meaningful but inaccessible, and the extended-preferences / impartial-observer strategy for getting comparability back indirectly. The citizens should run into the problem before anyone names it, so no lecture up front. Also tell me honestly what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which part of the philosophical problem the game structurally can't reproduce no matter how I set it up.
