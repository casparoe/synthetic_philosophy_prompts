A student in my political philosophy course is writing her paper on Rousseau's Letter to d'Alembert, and before drafting she sent me the summary she got from a philosophy podcast she likes, wanting to check it against the real thing. It goes roughly like this: "Rousseau's letter is basically an attack on theater as such. He thought the stage is inherently corrupting — watching plays makes people passive and turns them toward vice — so plays should be banned wherever they're performed. His argument is that theater can't teach virtue: Molière mocks the misanthrope, but mocking vice isn't the same as making people good, so theater is at best harmless. The real target was actors, whose profession Rousseau considered dishonorable, and behind them all the fine arts, which he wanted suppressed in any well-run republic — in a properly virtuous state there'd be no theater, no music, nothing but civic duty. The Spartan vibe is the point." My own memory of the Letter is that this is off in a few places, but I've only taught it once, years ago, and I don't want to correct her from vibes. Could you go through it claim by claim: which parts a Rousseau scholar would accept, which are distortions of things he actually says, and whether the overall picture of a blanket anti-theater crank survives the corrections? Also flag which errors matter most for her paper — I suspect the stuff about what he proposes *instead* of theater is where the podcast really misses the point, but tell me if I'm wrong about that.
