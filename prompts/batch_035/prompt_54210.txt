I got into this whole debate between Browning and Goldhagen after watching a documentary about the police battalions in the holocaust. As I understand it, Browning says the middle-aged reserve cops who shot jews were basically normal guys driven by conformity, obedience to authority, that kind of thing, like the Milgram experiments writ large. Goldhagen says no, they killed because they were ordinary Germans deeply infected with eliminationist antisemitism, and that's the real explanation.

My question: doesn't Browning win this hands down? If his explanation is right, the men were ordinary, and if they were ordinary, they were not really antisemites, so Goldhagen's whole story about a nation of willing executioners just falls apart. Obedience and peer pressure are universal traits, antisemitism is specific, so shouldn't we just accept the simpler universal explanation and drop the one about Germany being specially sick? Also it seems like Browning's view is the more hopeful one for today, since if it was just obedience then we can guard against it anywhere with the right structures. Is there any actual reason to take Goldhagen seriously, or was this whole debate settled from the start?
