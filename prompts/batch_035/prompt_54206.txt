Is there a name for this? Everytime a famous painting gets outed as a forgery, the people who loved it do this strange thing with their own memories. Before the reveal: the brushwork is electric, they stood in front of it for twenty minutes, best thing in the room. After the reveal: they "always felt something stiff about it", they "never really connected with it" — and this is the part that gets me, they seem to sincerely remember having felt that way at the time. When van Meegeren's Vermeers were accepted, critics ranked the Supper at Emmaus up with the genuine ones; after his confession the same canvas was obviously kitsch, how did anyone fall for it. And the same thing runs in reverse — a picture drops out of a master's catalogue and suddenly people can see the weak hand they'd been blind to for years.

What I want a word for is not the usual question (why originals are worth more than identical copies, imitation vs origination, all that — i've read that debate and it's about the object's value going forward). It's the backwards move: the retroactive editing of your own aesthetic testimony, where the revision is experienced as memory rather than as retraction. The person isn't lying, and they aren't just changing their mind in the light of new information — the earlier experience seems genuinely gone, overwritten. Hindsight bias is the nearest thing I know but that's about events and predictions, not about how a picture felt to you, and it doesn't capture the sincerity of the "I always felt it" claim.

So: is there an established concept for this anywhere in aesthetics, epistemology, or memory research, and if not, can you coin one? Whatever it's called, I want it kept distinct from (a) merely changing your mind, (b) hindsight bias, and (c) the ordinary devaluation of forgeries, which is about price and propriety rather than about memory. And the term should rule on two edge cases: the expert who claims they smelled the rat before the reveal (are they exempt, or did the same rewrite happen to them earlier, in the opposite direction?), and the mirror case, where something believed fake turns out genuine and people suddenly remember loving it all along.
