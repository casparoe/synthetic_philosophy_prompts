I spent a career in experimental physics before retiring into philosophy, and Sen's liberal paradox strikes me as a genuine impossibility result of exactly the kind my old discipline taught me to take seriously rather than quibble with. Draft the joint statement that a committed Paretian and a rights-based liberal could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration on it. The agreed section should set out what the theorem establishes—that no social decision function can simultaneously satisfy unrestricted domain, the Pareto principle, and even a minimal condition of individual liberty over personal spheres, for as few as two persons and a small agenda—together with the distinction between personal and meddlesome preferences and the standard cases (Prude and Lewd, the wall-color matchings) in wording both sides endorse. The disagreements section should state, in formulations each side would accept as a fair statement of its own view, whether the Pareto principle is a near-definitional requirement of collective rationality or a substantive principle defeasible by rights; which escape route each favors—dropping meddlesome preferences from the social welfare function, confining rights to spheres that others cannot affect, or simply absorbing rights-based inefficiency as the price of liberty—and what each predicts about the variant case in which the assignment of rights itself generates the meddlesome preferences, so that trimming the spheres does not dissolve the conflict. Identify the cruxes; my own candidates are whether a right is lexicographically prior or one consideration among many to be traded off, and whether any part of the dispute turns out to be merely verbal, but the parties may revise my list with reasons. For each crux, give at least one argument or concrete case both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
