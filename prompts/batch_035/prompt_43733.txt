I teach an undergraduate seminar on reference and meaning, and I want a diagnostic quiz for our course blog: "Which view of word meaning do you actually hold?" The quiz should sort readers by how they react to cases, not by which label they like, so every question must present a concrete scenario with two or three answer options, phrased so that someone with no philosophy of language can answer it. Build the logical space from the questions first, then locate the positions in it.

Include eight to ten questions, and make sure at least these kinds of cases appear, since the positions come apart on them: a Twin Earth scenario set in 1750, before anyone on either planet knows the chemical composition of the stuff in their rivers, asking whether the twin Earthling's word for that stuff refers to something different from our "water" and whether the two words nonetheless mean the same; a Burge-style case of someone who sincerely but mistakenly applies a community's disease term outside its proper range, asking whether that person's word still means what the community's means; a Kripke-style case where a historical attribution everyone believes turns out to be false, asking whether the name used all along refers to the real discoverer or to the person everyone believed in; a case about two molecule-for-molecule duplicates raised on the two planets, asking whether their beliefs and other mental states could be identical even if their words differ in meaning; and at least one case of your own invention, ideally involving a substance discovered to have hidden structure only on one planet, or a term whose use differs between two communities without either being in error.

Then provide a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto leaves for: natural-kind externalism of the Putnam sort (meaning is fixed by the world and the referent), social externalism of the Burge sort (meaning is fixed by community norms of use), the narrow-content internalist reply (including two-dimensional rescues that let the duplicates share something psychologically real while their words differ in what they pick out), and a causal-descriptivist hybrid. Note any combinations of answers your question set allows but no position occupies, and say briefly whether each unoccupied cell is empty because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Finally, be honest about which pairs of leaves the quiz cannot separate: if your cases never force a reader to choose between world-involving and community-involving externalism, say so rather than pretending the key distinguishes them.
