I work in a small unit inside our ministry of justice that handles what we internally call the "historical files" — petitons from communities, descendant groups, and sometimes just individual citizens asking the state to formally apologize for something in the past. It's maybe a dozen a year, ranging from the 1700s to things that happened within living memory, and every time one lands on my desk I basically wing it, and I'm tired of winging it. From half-remembered philosophy seminars I know there are real debates lurking here — whether a state can apologize at all, whether anyone alive has the standing to do it, whether an apology inherits guilt across generations or just expresses regret about what was done, whether it's an empty gesture without material repair — but I can't turn those into anything usable on a Tuesday afternoon with a minister's office asking for a recommendation by Friday.

Write us a decision procedure. The questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure terminates in a "no" (and lets us say why in a sentence that doesn't sound like we're grading suffering), and where it deliberately stops and hands the decision to the minister because the remaining question is one of values rather than facts. It should cover at minimum: who counts as the wronged party today, whether the archival record is good enough to support specific wording, what the apology is allowed to concede (blame? regret? legal liability? commitments about the future?), and whether the procedure can recommend forms short of a full apology — statements of regret, commemoration, disclosure of documents — as honest intermediate outputs instead of consolation prizes.

Then run it on three petitions we could plausibly get. (1) In the 1880s our government forcibly relocated an island fishing community to build a naval station; the archives are complete, the descendants are an organized association, and there's already a small cultural grant program named after the village. (2) Around 1900 a chartered colonial company used coerced labor at a mine; the state never ran the mine, but it issued the charter, enforced the labor contracts through its courts, and collected the royalties. The descendant community lives mostly in another country and wants wording that says the state itself was responsible. (3) A petition about a 1960s policy of placing children from poor families into church-run institutions, where an apology already happened in the 1990s but the petitioners say it was worded as regret about what happened to the children rather than acknowledgment of what the state did, and they want it redone. For each, say what the procedure outputs, make sure at least one output is something the petitioners won't like, and be honest about the points where the procedure runs out and somebody just has to decide.
