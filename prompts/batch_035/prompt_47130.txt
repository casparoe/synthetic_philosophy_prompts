For a final-year module on political philosophy I have to submit a one-page statement of my own views on power — the idea is that we circulate the statements anonymously and interrogate each other's next week. I've drafted mine, but before I send it I'd like to check that it actually hangs together, because something about the package feels unstable and I can't locate the problem. Here is what I hold.

(1) Power, at bottom, is repressive: it forbids, punishes, censors and excludes, and a free life is one on which as little power as possible bears. (2) At the same time, I accept Foucault's analysis that modern power is chiefly productive — that it manufactures subjects, categories, desires and whole bodies of knowledge, and that there is no position outside power from which one could get it into view. (3) I retain a fairly Enlightenment faith that established truth is in itself liberating: where inquiry is free and the facts are settled, truth tends to corrode domination, so transparency and education are our best political levers. (4) Politically, I want to shrink the domain of power: fewer spheres of life in which anyone stands over anyone else, and decisions pushed down to the people they affect. (5) On schools, I object to the apparatus of examinations, grading and normalisation chiefly because of its constraining, punitive side; if it were run entirely on positive incentives — praise, certificates, gentle nudges, no sanctions — most of my objection would lapse. (6) Finally, following the late Foucault, I think an ethics of the self, meaning deliberate work on one's own conduct and habits, is the most promising form of resistance available to us now.

Please audit the set rather than lecture me on Foucault. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only look to be, and, for each real tension, what the cheapest revision is and what it would cost me elsewhere? Then tell me which single change would tidy up the most. Please answer in British English, and be specific about which numbered commitment has to give — don't just tell me the package is unstable.
