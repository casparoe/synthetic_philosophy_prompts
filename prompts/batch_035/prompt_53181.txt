My meditation group has this member, Dara, who's been a serious Theravada practitioner for like 25 years and now leads our Thursday sutta study. Over the years I've collected her takes on the classic personal identity puzzles, mostly because she answers them so differently from everyone in my old philosophy of mind class. On the teleporter that scans, destroys, and reassembles: she says nothing was lost and nothing was preserved, because there was never anyone in the machine to begin with, but the pattern carrying on is what we've always mistakenly called a self. On gradual replacement of neurons with functionally identical silicon: she's totally unbothered, says the discomfort is just craving for self-view flaring up. On her grandmother with advanced dementia: visiting still matters, grandma is "there the way a river is there." On split-brain patients: she thinks asking how many persons are on the table is a category error, like asking how many rivers a watershed is. On rebirth: she affirms it without hesitation but flatly denies that anyone is reborn — she leans hard on the Milindapanha's flame passing from one candle to the next, the way she explains it is that the aggregates continue in a causal stream even though no essence travels. And when I asked her about an exact duplicate of me created molecule-for-molecule while I keep living, she said there's no fact of the matter about which one is "really me," but there are now two streams of grasping, and both will suffer.

Here's why I'm asking now: I'm 38 and seriously considering signing up for neuropreservation — vitrify the brain at death, revival in a couple centuries with memories intact in a repaired or new body. Dara's take on whether that "works" actually matters to my decision, weird as that sounds, because she's the person whose judgment I trust most on what it even means for it to work. Predict her verdict on cryonics, tell me what rule you think she's running (I can see at least two that fit her record: the deflationary one where all identity verdicts are just conventions she grants or refuses case by case, and a causal-stream one modeled on her rebirth answer, where it matters whether the aggregates continue the way a flame does), say which fits her record better and why, and then give me the one further case that would split those two rules apart. I have my own candidate — asking her about a revival that rebuilds "me" purely from my journals and emails, with no continuous brain tissue at all — but tell me if that's actually the discriminating case or if there's a sharper one. Keep the whole answer under 450 words, verdict first.
