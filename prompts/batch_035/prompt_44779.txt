A little over a year ago I came out of my third severe depressive episode, and I am now in stable remission, maintained on an antidepressant. My psychiatrist has offered me a place in a trial of a gene-based intervention that targets no current symptom: its aim is to raise my mood floor permanently, so that my worst weeks would become, at most, mildly flat, while leaving judgment, memory, and temperament as they are. Before I discuss it with my closest friend, I want your help, because I have been quietly cataloguing her verdicts on comparable interventions for years, and I cannot extract the rule she is running.

Her record, as I have it. Antidepressants for a diagnosed depression: clearly acceptable. A healthy classmate taking stimulants to study through the night: unacceptable, since exams are meant to measure unenhanced people. A beta-blocker before a public talk: unacceptable, on the grounds that the calm ought to be one's own. An epidural during childbirth: acceptable, as relief of a passing bodily state rather than a redesign of the person. Deleting pain perception in a healthy child through gene therapy: unacceptable, because pain carries information about the world. A pill that dulls grief after a death: unacceptable, as grief is owed to the person who died. Low-dose lithium for a woman who has never had an episode but whose mother and both sisters have bipolar disorder: acceptable, as forestalling an illness she is at high risk of.

Here is my case: the therapy is constitutional rather than episodic, directed at mood rather than at the body, and preventive rather than curative, since I am well now. She has never had a case that was all three at once. What do you predict she will say? What rule best fits her record, what other rules fit it just as well, and which further cases — for instance, someone with no psychiatric history requesting the same therapy purely to feel better than well — would most cleanly separate those rules? I care less about her eventual answer than about which of these commitments it will turn on, so give me your best guess along with the reasoning behind it.
