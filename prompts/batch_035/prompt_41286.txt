I'm a software engineer with no background in philosophy, but a long argument with a teammate about compensation made me realize I hold a cluster of views about what people deserve for their work, and I want to check whether they actually fit together before I lean on them again. Here is roughly where I stand:

1. What makes compensation deserved is effort: how hard and how conscientiously someone works. Two engineers who genuinely try equally hard deserve equal rewards, even if one ships twice as much as the other, since output mostly reflects talent, and nobody earns their talent.
2. My own salary is deserved. I work hard, and I am genuinely good at my job.
3. That I am good at my job is mostly luck: the aptitude I was born with, the country and decade I was born in, parents who kept a computer in the house, and free documentation that let me teach myself for nothing.
4. Nothing can be deserved on the basis of factors outside one's control; you cannot deserve something on account of a lottery win, genetic or otherwise. This is the line of reasoning I find most persuasive whenever the topic comes up.
5. I have no problem with my industry paying a premium for rare talents, because companies have to attract talent into the roles where it does the most good — and people who grind hard without the rare talent still make a decent living, so nobody is being exploited.

I genuinely cannot tell which of these, if any, are in tension with each other. Please audit the set: identify the real tensions, flag any that only look like tensions, and tell me what the cheapest revision would be and what accepting it would cost me. I don't know where the problem is, and I'm not trying to protect any particular one of these views, so don't assume I'd rather give up a specific one. Please write your audit as a numbered list of exactly five findings, ordered from most to least severe, each one or two sentences long, with no introduction or conclusion.
