Three years ago I left a physics PhD — I was working on effective field theories — for a machine learning lab, where I now build neural surrogates for expensive physics simulations: we distill slow numerical solvers for fluid flow and weather into fast learned models. My old advisor, an epistemic structural realist of the Worrall stripe, visited the lab last month, and we argued all evening about whether what my models preserve counts as knowing the structure of the phenomena. I would like to continue the argument on paper.

Restate the structural realism debate — Worrall's epistemic version, the ontic version associated with Ladyman, the pessimistic meta-induction that motivates both, and the Newman objection — in the vocabulary I actually use: learned representations, invariance and equivariance, symmetry, distribution shift, distillation, what a network interpolates versus extrapolates. As faithfully as you can; do not smuggle in my vocabulary's assumptions and call that translation. Then give me the residue report, addressing at least these points:

- My working definition has become: structure is whatever survives a change of parameterization. Is that epistemic structural realism, ontic structural realism, or neither? And does it dodge the Newman objection, or merely restate it — two networks whose representations are related by a smooth reparameterization "share structure" in something like the way any two countable structures share a bijection?

- The pessimistic meta-induction, in my terms, says every surrogate is eventually replaced by a better one trained on new data. Successor models keep agreeing with their predecessors about the invariances while disagreeing completely about internal mechanisms. Does that pattern support the structuralist, or does it only show that we keep whatever we have already tested — so the induction is about our test sets, not about the world?

- The question my advisor thought was decisive: a surrogate that matches a simulation's outputs has nothing like the simulation's ontology — no grid, no timesteps, no degrees of freedom in the physicist's sense. If I accept that, am I an ontic structural realist about the simulation? And should it worry me that I am happy to say it about simulations but hesitate to say it about nature?

Where does the translation distort, and what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all? For whatever cannot be said, tell me whether that reflects a defect in my vocabulary or a sign that the original claim was less clear than it looked.
