I teach a graduate seminar on formal epistemology, and every year I make the students write up their "epistemic profile" at midterm so their classmates can tear into it. This year I decided I should do the same exercise myself, and honestly I keep going back and forth about whether my own profile hangs together. Here's what I've got so far:

(1) Veritism: accuracy is the one and only intrinsic epistemic good. Knowledge, understanding, explanatory unification, simplicity -- all of that is valuable only instrumentally, insofar as it promotes accuracy now or later.

(2) The value of knowledge is not just instrumental. I take the Meno problem seriously, and I want to say that knowledge really is better than mere true belief, and not merely because believing truly in the knowledge-y way tends to make you accurate on other occasions.

(3) I buy the standard epistemic utility theory package: credences get scored by strictly proper scoring rules, rationality is a matter of maximizing expected epistemic utility by the lights of your own credences, and probabilism is vindicated because incoherent agents are dominated -- they're accuracy-dominated by some coherent alternative.

(4) On peer disagreement, I hold something like the equal weight view. When I discover that an epistemic peer, who has been exposed to the same evidence, disagrees with me about p, I should split the difference, even if on reflection I still think my original assessment was the better one.

(5) I have the ordinary lottery intuition: if I hold one of ten million tickets, I should have a credence of ~0.99999 that my ticket will lose, but I should not *believe* that it will lose. I also think full belief is a genuinely distinct epistemic state from credence, not just a fancy name for credence near 1.

(6) Finally, I'm comfortable with immodesty: on strictly proper rules, a coherent agent always judges her own current credence function to be the expected-accuracy maximizer, and I think that's fine, not some kind of epistemic arrogance that needs explaining away.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are in real tension and how badly. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me. I suspect some of these only *look* like they conflict, so don't just flag everything -- I want you to discriminate. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. And please answer in American English.
