I teach an advanced undergraduate seminar on philosophy of mind, and every year a student arrives with a label — usually "nonreductive physicalist" — that turns out to be a matter of taste rather than of distinguishable commitments. I want a diagnostic decision tree on the causal exclusion problem that sorts students by what they actually hold, starting from a first node anyone can answer on their own convictions (does every physical event with a sufficient cause have a sufficient physical cause?) and ending at named positions: reductive or type-identity physicalism, Davidsonian anomalous monism, a nonreductive physicalism that embraces overdetermination, the interventionist or difference-making response to exclusion, epiphenomenalism, and interactionist dualism.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from the list of names: first the branch questions, then the positions located in the answer combinations. Each node should be a commitment a student can assess without knowing which position it leads to — for instance, whether a mental property and the physical property it is realized by are distinct in a sense that lets them both cause an effect, or only in a sense that makes them two descriptions of one cause; whether two complete causes can each be sufficient for the same effect without the kind of objectionable systematic overdetermination we get with two assassins and one victim; whether causal relevance is a matter of productive sufficiency or of difference-making (would the effect have gone differently without this property?). Roughly eight leaves at most, each with one sentence on its most awkward commitment.

Two things I will grade hard. Wherever a combination of answers is unoccupied by any actual position, note it and say in a clause whether that is because the view is incoherent, merely unattractive, or just unexplored. And flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot genuinely separate — I suspect anomalous monism and the difference-making nonreductive physicalist will answer every one of your nodes identically — rather than smuggling in a question that begs the point actually in dispute between them.
