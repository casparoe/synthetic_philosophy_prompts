I've spent a fair amount of time over the years with the rationalist canon on Dutch books and money pumps, and I've hit a snag I can't untangle by myself. The story as I absorbed it: if my preferences run in a cycle — A over B, B over C, C over A — then a trader who knows this can offer me a series of trades, each individually acceptable to me, until I end up exactly where I started, only poorer. Fine as a piece of formal apparatus. But the setup seems to require the trader: someone who has learned my preferences, can price each step just under my willingness to pay, and is patient enough to walk me around the loop. Now, I've been informally tracking my own choices for a couple of years — snack brands, phone plans, restaurants, streaming services — and I'm fairly confident I'm intransitive in places. Yet no one has ever constructed a cycle out of my preferences, and I've never caught myself paying to be returned to a starting point. Money does leak out of my life in ways I can't fully account for — re-subscribing to a service I had cancelled, switching banks and switching back — but I've never been able to read that as a pump, because there's no identifiable person on the other end pocketing the difference.

A friend says my situation forces one of two conclusions: either I'm secretly transitive after all, or money pump arguments are parlor games that describe what a hypothetical predator would do to me if such a predator existed, and therefore have no purchase on actual agents in actual markets. Someone at a meetup once told me there's a version of the argument that doesn't need an exploiter at all — something about the sequence of choices itself being bad for you, paying to be moved in a circle even when no one profits — but I never understood how that's supposed to work; the "pump" metaphor seems to be entirely about someone extracting money, so an exploitation-free pump sounds like a contradiction in terms.

So, question: does the money-pump rationale for transitivity collapse once the cunning adversary is exposed as a fiction? I'd like a committed answer, not a survey of positions. Take a side and defend it: either the bookie is essential and the argument is therefore mostly irrelevant to agents like me — in which case tell me plainly what, if anything, still obligates me to be transitive — or the bookie is dispensable and the obligation survives without him, in which case show me the mechanism: who or what occupies his role in an ordinary commercial life, and why haven't I noticed it? And please adjudicate the one piece of evidence I actually have: years of probable intransitivity and zero observed pumping. Does that observation favor one side, or is it not evidence of anything? Give me a verdict, and your reasons for it.
