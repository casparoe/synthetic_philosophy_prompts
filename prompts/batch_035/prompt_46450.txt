I do compliance review at a mid-sized UK charity and I keep having to sign off on fundraising copy with no real framework. The sort of stuff that crosses my desk: urgency deadlines (give before midnight and it gets matched), guilt-heavy stories, "most people in your area give £20" social proof lines, pre-selected monthly amounts on the donation form. A trustee recently read something about manipulation being influence that works by bypassing your deliberation rather than through it, and now wants a proper rulebook — but honestly none of us can say where persuasion stops and manipulation starts. Can you turn whatever philosophy actually has to say about this into a procedure my reviewers can follow? They're not philosophers and get maybe ten minutes per piece of copy, so I need: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where the procedure returns a firm "this crosses the line", and where it just hands off to a person because it's a values call. Then run it on three cases: an emergency appeal saying children will die this week unless we raise X by Friday; a "people in your postcode give £10 on average" line where the average is real but the postcode bit is invented; and a donation form that comes with "give monthly" already ticked. Include one case where the procedure gives an answer our fundraisers won't like, and be honest about which steps are just judgement dressed up as a checklist.
