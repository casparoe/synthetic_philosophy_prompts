so heres the deal: I'm a journalist, I write mostly about science and ideas, and once a year my outlet runs a small live event for readers -- like 25-30 people, mostly curious non-specialists, one 60-minute slot. This year I want the session to be about something I keep bumping into in my reporting but can never quite convey in a written piece: the way systems end up in races to the bottom that no one in them actually chose. Clickbait eating newsroom budgets, labs cutting safety corners to be first to publish, airlines shrinking legroom one inch at a time because if you don't your competitor does, arms races in AI capability or in college admissions or wherever. The common thread, as I understand it, is that each actor is responding rationally to local incentives and the aggregate outcome is worse for everyone including the actors -- no villain required, and blaming individuals misses the point entirely.

I don't want a talk with a game bolted on, I want the game to BE the session. Design something the audience actually plays, in table groups, over several rounds. My rough idea for flavor (feel free to improve it): each table is a newsroom, and each round they choose between investing in expensive real reporting versus pumping out cheap clickbait -- real reporting grows the total audience pie but costs you now, clickbait is free but shrinks the pie for everyone. Make the payoffs such that defection is individually rational every single round even as the whole room watches their collective numbers sink. Somewhere in the middle, let tables openly negotiate -- maybe even let them try to build some enforcement mechanism, a monitor, a pact, an external regulator -- and let that partially work and partially collapse, because thats honestly where the richest discussion is. I have a projector, paper, colored cards for voting, and zero budget for real money, so the payoff scheme needs to be points-based but with stakes that feel real enough that people actually sweat their choices.

Then a debrief that maps what happened in the room onto the actual concept: the multipolar trap, why the equilibrium is stable even when everyone hates it, and which escape routes exist (coordination, changing the payoff structure from the outside, an enforcer with teeth, exit) and what each one costs. Crucially, don't have me reveal the name for this thing -- you know, the big name for it that people online use, or the specific essays its known from -- until after they've felt trapped in it themselves; I want the reveal to land, not to be a label they nod along to. And please tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this (tables colluding too well, someone playing against the spirit of it, apathy from the back row, whatever it is) and how I should handle each, plus an honest note on which features of the real thing a one-hour room-sized game just cannot reproduce -- like exit costs, or having billions of players, or the fact that in the real world nobody convenes a debrief. Keep the whole thing runnable by one facilitator (me) with maybe one volunteer.
