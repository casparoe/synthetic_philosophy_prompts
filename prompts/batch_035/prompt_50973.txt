I'm taking a course this term on African philosophy, and we've spent the last several weeks on Ubuntu and relational conceptions of personhood. For my position paper I've drafted a list of the commitments I actually hold, but before I build an argument on top of them I want a consistency audit, because I suspect the set doesn't sit entirely comfortably together. Here is the list:

(1) Persons are not self-contained individuals whose personhood is complete prior to and independently of their social existence. Personhood is constituted through relations of recognition and care within a community — the core idea of the Ubuntu tradition, that a person becomes a person through other persons.

(2) This status is not all-or-nothing. Personhood is an achievement that comes in degrees: an infant is only on the way to full personhood, and someone in an irreversible coma has lost most of it. This is, as I understand it, how several of the African communitarians we've read actually frame it.

(3) Even so, infants and the irreversibly comatose possess full and equal moral status. Their dignity is inviolable and does not shrink as their relational capacities do; treating them as marginal persons would be a grave wrong.

(4) A being's moral status is not conferred by anyone's recognition. Recognition is a response to a status that is already there; a community that refuses to recognize someone does not thereby make that person less of a person.

(5) I reject the authoritarian strand of communitarianism. No collective consensus can strip an individual of basic rights, and a person may permissibly sever her ties to her community — emigrate, dissent, renounce her culture — without forfeiting her standing as a person.

What I'd like from you is not a defense of any of these, but an audit. Identify which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only appear to be, and say explicitly how an apparent tension dissolves. For each genuine tension, tell me how severe it is, what the cheapest revision would be, and what that revision costs elsewhere in the list. Then tell me which single change would clean up the most. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally bad, and don't be gentler than the analysis warrants — I honestly cannot tell where the real problem lies, and I'd rather find out now than from my instructor's comments.
