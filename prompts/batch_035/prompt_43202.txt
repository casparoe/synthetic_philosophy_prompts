Is there an actual name for this thing? Whenever people argue about what someone really wants, someone eventually pulls this move: not what you want right now, but what you'd want if you knew everything relevant, thought it through calmly, had time to grow up a bit, weren't scared or sleep-deprived or whatever. The recovering addict example is the obvious one — they want the drug today, but ask them after a year sober and they'd tell you the drug was never what they wanted, being free of it was. Same move shows up when someone drafts a living will binding their future confused self, or when a kid resents being forced to practice piano and later is glad someone made the call for the person they turned into. In each case the argument is that some later, better-informed version of you is the one whose preferences count, and the current version should be overruled.

Here's the part I can't get past though. The more you idealize, the less the idealized version resembles the actual person. If you imagine me knowing everything and having thought about it for a thousand years, that thing's preferences could be totally different from mine, so why do they get to be counted as mine? It seems like you have to pick a point on a slider between "just take what the person wants now" and "ask the fully improved version and take that" and I can't see any principled place to stop. And there's a second question hiding in there: do you extrapolate from who the person is now, keeping their values and just feeding them more information, or do you let the values themselves shift as they learn, and where's the line between correcting a preference and replacing it?

I've heard philosophers talk about an "ideal observer" but I gather that's more like a God's-eye impartial judge deciding what's right, not a specific improved version of a particular person, so I don't think that's it. And this obviously gets used in arguments about AI alignment, like telling the machine to satisfy what humanity would want if we were smarter — same slider problem, higher stakes. So: does this configuration have an established name, what are the nearest concepts in the literature and how does each one differ from what I described, and is there a standard treatment of the who-counts-as-you problem? If there's no exact term for it, please coin one, define it so it's clearly distinguishable from the nearest neighbors, and give a couple of edge cases where it's ambiguous whether we're still inside the concept or not. I'd rather have the precise carving than just a label.
