so I've been writing up my views on the shutdown problem for a workshop thing and before I send it out I want someone to poke holes in the package. here's what I'm committed to:

(1) a sufficiently capable AI system should be fully corrigible — operators can shut it down, rewrite its goals, or roll back its learning at any time, and the system shouldn't resist, bargain over it, or even prefer that it not happen;

(2) the system should nonetheless be an expected utility maximizer with a stable utility function — anything less coherent gets money-pumped or stops counting as an agent at all;

(3) I buy instrumental convergence: self-preservation and goal-preservation fall out of being a goal-directed agent with almost any goal, so you can't dodge them just by picking a friendlier objective;

(4) corrigibility has to be robust to self-modification — if the system rewrites itself or trains a successor, it should carry the shutdown button forward intact rather than letting it get optimized away;

(5) it should still be able to speak up when shutting it down would be genuinely catastrophic (like, the grid is failing and it's the only thing positioned to fix it) — corrigibility shouldn't mean silence or eagerness;

(6) I'm fine with the system being uncertain about what we actually want and updating as it watches our choices, and I don't think that threatens (2).

Which of these actually conflict, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest consistent package? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally fatal, and for each one tell me what would have to give. I have suspicions about one or two of them but I'd rather hear where you think the real pressure is before I say.
