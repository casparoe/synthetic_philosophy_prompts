At my reading group on Gödel, Escher, Bach, two members got into a dispute about the Prelude... Ant Fugue dialogue, in which the anteater befriends an intelligent ant colony called Aunt Hillary while Achilles and the Tortoise puzzle over how a heap of mindless ants could think. One member, Daniel, argued that Hofstadter's point is straightforwardly correct: intelligence is a property of organization rather than of the material or the parts, so an ant colony can literally have beliefs and converse, and anyone who denies this is in the same position as someone who denies that a person has a mind on the ground that individual neurons are stupid and blind. Elena replied that the neuron analogy is doing illegitimate work: neurons are dedicated components of a single organism, differentiated into cell types and wired into a stable architecture, whereas ants are independent agents with their own survival interests, and each ant can simply walk away — so there is no unified subject to which the colony-level states could belong. Daniel called that a confusion of levels: the colony's thoughts are not built out of the ants' thoughts but out of their signaling behavior, and the signals no more need to be meaningful in themselves than ink marks need to understand the words they spell, so nothing about the ants' own intelligence blocks the higher-level story. Elena countered that the walking-away point still stands: if the system's continuity depends on the autonomous choices of its parts, its states are hostage to their interests, which is disanalogous enough to sink the mind attribution. Daniel said a brain is likewise at the mercy of fatigue and neurotransmitter levels, so the disanalogy is superficial, and Elena's demand for a unified subject just assumes a view of mind the whole chapter is designed to call into question. They asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Please adjudicate the exchange move by move: say who is right about which specific point, whether the regress worry Elena never quite made explicit is a genuine problem for her side, and what, if anything, both of them are missing.
