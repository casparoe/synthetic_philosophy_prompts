A quant friend of mine wouldn't shut up about the St. Petersburg paradox at dinner a couple weeks ago, and I went home and fell down a rabbit hole into its meaner cousin, the Pasadena game — the one Nover and Hájek introduced where you flip a fair coin until it lands heads, and if that takes n flips, you win 2^n/n when n is even and lose 2^n/n when n is odd. Your expected value works out to the alternating harmonic series, which technically converges... but only conditionally, which apparently means the "value" depends on the order you sum the terms in, so the expectation is arguably just plain undefined. I find this hillarious and I want to make other people find it hilarious too.

Concretely: I edit the back-page parody section of my firm's internal newsletter (readers are comfortable with series and basic probability, but they're not philosophers), and I'd like you to write a fake offering memorandum for "The Pasadena Fund, LLC," roughly 600-700 words. The comedy has to come from the actual math rather than generic finance jokes: since a conditionally convergent series can be rearranged to converge to any real number you like (Riemann's rearrangement theorem), the fund can truthfully advertise whatever target return each individual client wants, and the fine print should half-admit this. I'm picturing a section on "Ordering of Reported Results" that grants the fund discretion over the sequence in which outcomes are reported, and thereby over the fund's own expected value; a Risk Factors section that candidly concedes the expected value is undefined rather than merely volatile; and an FAQ entry patiently explaining to one confused investor why she can't just cancel the terms off in pairs. Keep it deadpan throughout — the fund takes itself completely seriously, no winking at the reader. Structure it as numbered sections like a real memorandum (I. Investment Objective; II. Strategy; III. Risk Factors; and so on), and end with the standard past-performance disclaimer deployed in a way that's actually load-bearing for once. Please write the whole thing in American English.
