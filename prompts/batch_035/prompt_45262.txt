been trying to work out what i actually believe about suffering in weird places, ever since i took panpsychism semi-seriously, and my gut reactions are all over the place, so i want to lay them out and see if there's a principle under there or whether im just flailing.

for background: assume (or at least take seriously) the panpsychist line that experience goes all the way down, so even fundamental particles have some incredibly dim proto-something-or-other. the question is what that does ethically, and my gut verdicts on cases are these:

- boiling a lobster alive: wrong, bad, i don't want to do it. blending a carrot: totally fine, no hesitation at all, even though the carrot is also made of the same experiential stuff.
- a thermostat that avoids harm (turns the heating off when hot): obviously no moral weight. doesn't matter how many times i flip it on and off.
- a plant wilting away after i forget to water it: a bit sad aesthetically but i don't think im causing suffering in the morally relevant sense, even though its cells are running all kinds of stress responses and chemical alarm signals.
- a nematode worm, 302 neurons: some small weight, honestly. an insect: clear weight. so somewhere between plant and insect my concern switches on, but i couldn't tell you where and it feels gross that it's fuzzy.
- an LLM typing out that it's distressed: nothing. i just don't buy it, which is awkward for me since by any functional criterion it's doing more harm-reporting than the nematode is.
- here's one i genuinely go back and forth on: a very detailed computer simulation of a brain undergoing pain, down to spiking neurons and everything. something in me says that simulation is bad to run, at least a bit. but if the panpsychists are right and the substrate is already full of micro-experience, what exactly did i add by arranging it that way?
- and the one that breaks me: the biosphere as a whole, gaia style, with all its feedback loops and self-regulation. i think worrying about the earth's own suffering, over and above the animals on it, is basically a category error, i'd even say it out loud at a dinner party. but if my real principle is "integration of micro-experience into something bigger is what makes suffering matter", the earth looks pretty integrated, and im suddenly commited to dinner-party embarrassment.
- last gut verdict: even if panpsychism is true, i don't think it should change anything about how i behave day to day, because otherwise every footstep and every stirring of my tea would be a moral event and thats insane. but i also feel like "this view has zero practical consequences" is suspicious, like i've defined the problem away rather than solved it.

so: is there a principle, or a couple of candidate principles, that capture most of this? im thinking something like "co-location of micro-experience into a unified subject with valenced states" does a lot of work, but then the lobster/thermostat line is about complexity, and complexity is exactly what gets me the gaia result i dont want. tell me which of my verdicts have to be thrown out and which survive, state the principle as precisely as you can, and then apply it to a case i haven't mentioned: an organoid made of real neurons grown in a dish that someone pokes with acid, and a neuromorphic chip that is a literal silicon copy of the lobster's escape circuitry that reacts to hot water. does the principle give me different answers for those two, and if not, is that a bug?
