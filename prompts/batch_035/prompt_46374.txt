I'm a backend engineer, zero formal philosophy background, so please bear with me. My partner and I have been going back and forth about whether to have kids, and somehow that turned into a multi-day argument with a coworker in the code review threads about whether it's bad that some people never get born at all. I've written down my gut reactions to a bunch of cases and now I want to know whether there's an actual principle underneath all this or whether I'm just being inconsistent. Some blog I found called the general issue "the procreation asymmetry" but I don't want the wikipedia treatment, I want to know what MY intuitions add up to.

The cases: (1) a couple conceives knowing their kid will have a painful degenerative disease and a short life that's mostly suffering -- clearly wrong. (2) A couple who'd make great parents just don't want kids, even though any kid of theirs would pretty much be gauranteed a great life -- totally fine, nobody's wronged. (3) A couple could wait a month and conceive a healthy kid, but instead they take some medication right now that means the kid they do have is born with a serious disability, though still a life the kid is glad to have -- my gut says this is wrong, even though the "harmed" kid wouldn't have existed at all in the wait-a-month scenario, so who exactly is worse off here? (4) Humanity burns through resources now so that people in 2200 have lives that are still good but noticeably worse than the lives they would have had -- feels wrong. (5) Me deciding not to have a third kid who would have been happy -- obviously fine. And then (6) I have to admit my confidence on case 4 kind of collapses whenever I think hard about it, because nobody alive in 2200 would have been the same person if we'd saved the resources instead.

My own attempt at a rule was something like: it's wrong to cause suffering, but it's not wrong to fail to cause happiness. That gets 1, 2, and 5 right, but it wobbles on 3 (the kid's overall life is happy, so does the disability count as "suffering"?) and I honestly can't tell you what it says about 4, since making future lives worse-but-still-good doesn't look like causing suffering either. So the question: is there one principle that fits all six verdicts? If not, which of my verdicts would you throw under the bus, and why that one? I'd also like to hear what the principle predicts for a case I haven't listed -- e.g. conceiving when you know the kid's life will be just barely worth living, only a hair above neutral. As a software engineer I'd also appreciate it if you told me whether my rule can be patched or whether it needs a rewrite, in whatever framing makes sense. Put the actual principle, stated in one or two sentences, at the top of your answer, and then at the very end give me a final line of the form \answer{...} with a short name for the principle you end up endorsing plus, if any of my verdicts had to go, which one (or the word none if you saved all of them).
