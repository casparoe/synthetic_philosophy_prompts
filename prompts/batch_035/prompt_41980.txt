For an essay in my philosophy elective we were discussing whether psychology can debunk virtue ethics, and two classmates of mine got into a long argument I couldn't follow to the end. It started from the classic situationist material: Milgram's obedience experiments, the study where finding a coin in a payphone's coin return made people dramatically more likely to help a stranger who had dropped her papers, and older research from the 1920s finding that schoolchildren who cheated on one kind of task were barely more likely to cheat in other settings.

Maya argued that if character traits were real, behavior should be consistent across situations, but it isn't — what best predicts what people do is the situation they're in, so talk of "generous" or "honest" people is a fiction, and a virtue ethics built on cultivating such traits is built on nothing.

Dan replied that this tests a strawman: virtue ethicists never claimed people act the same everywhere, only that a generous person is disposed to act generously in generosity-relevant circumstances, and a found coin or an authority's commands can change which circumstances are relevant — so the experiments leave the theory untouched.

Maya said that makes the view unfalsifiable: if any failure to act generously can be excused by saying the circumstances weren't right for it, then no experiment could ever count against the theory.

Dan answered that dispositions in general work that way — glass is fragile even though it never breaks unless struck — so consistency measured the same behavior in every situation was never the right test.

Maya's last move was that unlike fragility, there is no mechanism behind a trait: if honesty just is whatever pattern of responses survives redescription, then attributing it to a person explains nothing, and virtue ethics is moral commentary dressed up as psychology.

They eventually asked me to referee and I honestly couldn't. Could you go through their exchange step by step and say who is right at each stage — and what, if anything, both of them are missing?
