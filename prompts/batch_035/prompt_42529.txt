I'm in a grad seminar on vagueness this semester and there's one specific thing I keep going in circles on. The textbook picture I have is: epistemicism says there's a sharp cutoff between bald and not-bald that we just can't know, supervaluationism says there's no cutoff because every admissible precisification draws the line in a slightly different place, and a vague sentence in the penumbra comes out neither supertrue nor superfals. What I can't figure out is what actually separates the two views once you take higher-order vagueness seriously. If the boundary between the definite cases and the penumbra is itself vague, and the boundary of that boundary too, then the supervaluationist needs a sharp line somewhere anyway -- between precisifications that count as admissible and ones that don't -- which to me looks like the epistemicist's cliff wearing a costume. Unless the move is supposed to be that the admissibility line is sharp but unknowable, in which case I honestly don't see what was gained over just being an epistemicist about the original predicate.

Please explain, at a conceptual level, what the real difference between the views amounts to once you push on this. Is it a difference about what exists (sharp boundaries or not), about what's true, about what we can know, or is it mostly a difference about what we're allowed to say about our own words? And if you can, explain why the people who defend supervaluationism think higher-order vagueness is something their view handles better rather than worse -- I have a feeling I'm missing a distinction in the neighborhood of "denying that a sharp boundary exists" versus "refusing to assert that one exists", but I can't get it crisp on my own. Maybe it also has something to do with how the views treat the tolerance premise of the sorites itself, since weakening it from "any one hair makes no difference" to something like "no small difference makes a difference" seems to reintroduce the same issue one level up.

I don't want a history lesson or a list of who argued against whom, I want to actually see where the two views come apart and where they secretly agree. Intuitions and concrete cases over formalism please, though if there's a point where the formal machinery is doing real work, flag it and tell me what it's doing. I'd rather come out of this understanding one distinction really well than knowing the names of five more positions.
