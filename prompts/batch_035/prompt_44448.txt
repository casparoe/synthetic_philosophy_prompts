So I've been reading LessWrong and adjacent blogs since roughly 2011, and there's one argument that has never gone anywhere, either in the rationalist corner of the internet or in the actual philosophy literature, and that's the seperateness of persons thing. On one side you've got the anti-utilitarians — the Rawls point that losses to one person can't just be canceled out by bigger gains to others, because persons are distinct and nothing and nobody experiences the summed welfare; Nozick's side-constraints vibe fits here too. On the other side the utilitarians have basically two moves: the time-slice argument (we trade off one bad year against forty good years within a single life all the time and nobody blinks, so why does the boundary between lives suddenly matter? there's no metaphysical wall between my Tuesday and my Thursday that differs in kind from the wall between me and you), and the aggregation theorem stuff showing that if you accept some pretty weak preference axioms you end up maximizing a sum whether you wanted to or not.

And its not just the journals — this is literally the dust specks vs torture fight from the old LessWrong threads, re-run every couple of years with the same people in the comments and almost nobody converting. The anti-aggregation side keeps saying the sum is a fiction, no one experiences total utility; the utilitarians keep replying that nobody experiences a life either, just moments, and demanding to know what the morally relevant difference between intra-personal and inter-personal tradeoffs is supposed to BE, and then somebody drags in Parfit's fission cases to argue person boundaries are made up anyway, and everyone talks past each other for 400 comments until a moderator locks the thread.

My hypothesis is that this is two groups with different bedrock intuitions — one side thinks morality is fundamentally about distributing goods across distinct subjects, the other thinks its fundamentally about the total quantity of experience — and since neither side can argue for their bedrock, the argument just rotates forever through the same cases (dust specks, utility monsters, transplant surgeons). But maybe I'm wrong. Maybe it's secretly a substantive empirical question about personal identity wearing moral clothes, or maybe the two sides are answering different questions (what to do vs. what to feel bad about) and it only looks like one fight.

So: diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, based on what the sides actually say, and tell me straight whether anything could ever end it — a conceptual distinction, an argument, better fission cases, whatever — or whether we should just accept its permanent. Also tell me if my bedrock-intuition hypothesis is wrong and what it misses. 450 words max, I'm serious, I will stop reading past that.
