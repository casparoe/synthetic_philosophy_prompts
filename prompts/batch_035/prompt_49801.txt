i've been going in circles on an argument about Wittgenstein on religion and I can't find the exit. Background, so we're on the same page: in his Cambridge lectures on religious belief, Wittgenstein treats believing in the Last Judnement as deeply unlike predicting that a fire will destroy the farm next week. The prediction is a hypothesis — it can be more or less probable, evidence bears on it, it can turn out flat wrong. The religious belief isn't like that on his picture: it plays a different role in the believer's life, it's not a risky forecast, and the non-believer doesn't straightforwardly contradict the believer the way he would if they disagreed about the fire. Fine so far. Now the argument I got walked into:

(1) On this picture, religious belief is not answerable to evidence: no discovery, however terrible, would show the beleiver she was wrong, because she wasn't making a prediction that could fail in that way.

(2) If no possible discovery counts against a belief, then holding it can never be a mistake in the way believing a refuted hypothesis is a mistake.

(3) If it can't be that kind of mistake, then criticizing the believer on evidential grounds — you still believe this despite the fossils, despite the suffering of children — is a confusion, an attack on a position she doesn't actually hold.

(4) Whatever protects Christianity here protects any belief held in the same tone: the woman who thinks the earth rests on the backs of elephants can equally say her belief is a picture that organizes her life and not a hypothesis.

(5) So either this immunizes crank cosmology along with religion — which seems liek it proves too much — or there's some real difference between the elephant lady and the believer. And whatever that difference is, it can't be non-answerability-to-evidence, since (1) and (4) granted that to both. So the difference is doing all the work, and the famous point that religious belief isn't a hypothesis turns out not to be what protects religion at all.

My friend thinks the sleight of hand is between (2) and (3) — the slide from nothing-could-refute-it to no-criticism-is-apt. Maybe, but then I want to hear what criticism of the elephant lady is left over once the evidential kind is ruled out, because if there's nothing left, (5) walks right back in the door.

Tell me which step to reject, why it looked fine when it slid by, and what rejecting it costs. And if you want to take the there's-a-real-difference exit, you have to actually name the difference — not just say religion is woven into a shared form of life, because as far as I can tell that amounts to the crank needing more friends, which can't be the whole story. Don't drag in the secondary literature by name, just work through the argument.
