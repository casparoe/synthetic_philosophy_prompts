So for my philosophy elective we've been reading about Kripkenstein (I guess it's like Kripke's version of what Wittgenstein was arguing in his later stuff, the book is called Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language). The basic thing I got from it is the plus vs. quus problem: I've always meant to follow the same rule when I add, but if quus is defined as the same as addition for all the numbers I've ever actually computed before, and different after that, then nothing in my past behavior or my past mental life can show which rule I was actually following -- plus or quus. Every answer I give can be interpreted as conforming to either one, so there's no fact about what I "meant." I actually found this kind of depressing for a week and then kind of freeing? Idk.

Anyway, our teacher said we could do a creative final project instead of an essay, and I want to write a poem about it, specifically a villanelle, because I feel like the form is weirdly perfect for this. The two refrains would be, like, one line voicing the confident "I've meant plus all along" and the other voicing the skeptic's reply that any use so far is consistent with infinitely many other rules. And the whole point should be that every time a refrain comes back it's the paradox reasserting itself rather than getting resolved -- the poem keeps asking the question and the structure itself keeps failing to settle anything, the way my attempts to justify my rule by giving another rule just push the problem back a step. Maybe by the last stanza the two refrains can end up side by side or intertwined in some way that shows they can't actually be separated by any fact, but honestly you can decide how to land the ending. Maybe something about how the community/society angle is the only way out, where meaning what everyone means is all there is, or maybe leave it fully open like Kripke's skeptical solution kind of does.

A few constraints: please actually follow the villanelle form (19 lines, the two refrains alternating, both showing up together in the final quatrain, aba rhyme) -- don't just write a loose poem and call it a villanelle. And I want it accurate enough that my teacher, who really knows this stuff, wouldn't wince -- she should be able to tell which refrain is the skeptical voice and which is the confident one, and recognize the quus setup without me having to name-drop it in the title or anything. Also don't make it like a wikipedia entry in rhyme, it should still work as a poem, maybe a bit eerie or funny even, since there's something kind of absurd about the whole thing. Length-wise just the villanelle itself, plus maybe a two or three sentence note at the end explaining the choices, since I have to present it to the class and I want to be able to say something about why the form fits.
