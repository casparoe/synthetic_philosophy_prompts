I'm on the selection committee for a fellowship our foundation gives to college seniors heading into public service, and we're fighting about how much weight to give "character." It's currently a third of the score, based on interviews and reference letters, because the founding idea was that we're betting on the person, not the resume. One of our commitee members teaches social psych and keeps pushing situationism at us: the old Hartshorne and May honesty studies, Milgram, the seminarians-in-a-hurry finding, all supposedly showing that what people do is driven by circumstances more than by stable traits, and that interviewers mostly project a coherent character onto whatever's in front of them. Half the committee wants to scrap the character score entirely and shift the wieght to a track record of concrete completed projects. The other half says the track record only counts *because* it displays character, so this is a distinction without a difference, and that a fellowship picked purely on outputs will select for people who optimize for legible outputs. Draft the memo I'll circulate before next month's meeting: recommend a concrete change (or a concrete non-change) to the scoring rubric, with reasoning both sides could actually respond to, and take the losing side's best argument seriously rather than just splitting the baby.
