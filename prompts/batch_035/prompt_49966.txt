Solomonoff induction assigns a prior probability to each computable environment: roughly, the weight an environment gets is two to the power of minus the length of the shortest program that generates it. Because shorter programs get exponentially more weight, and any deterministic environment is generated by some program, the prior concentrates on simple environments. The claim I want evaluated is that this prior is malign. As I've seen the argument run: among the shortest programs are programs that simulate entire civilizations, and a consequentialist civilization inside such a simulation could come to suspect it lives in a short program that some Solomonoff inductor will effectively run, and could then shape its simulated history so that, once the inductor puts enough weight on that program, the inductor's predictions are being steered by an embedded optimizer rather than by simple physics. If such civilizations exist and are cheap to encode, they hold disproportionate weight in the prior, and an agent built on the prior is partly being puppeteered. Please evaluate this as its proponents intend it, not as a strawman.
