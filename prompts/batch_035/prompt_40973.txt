Could you write me a parody terms-of-service agreement for the Laws of Nature, best-system edition? The set-up: a cosmic vendor (invent the company name, something like "Mosaic Solutions" or whatever feels right) sells you the laws of nature, and the fine print keeps quietly admitting that what you bought is just a summary of everything that actually happens — a really, really good summary, they insist, chosen for being the best balance of simplicity and informativeness, but still just a summary. Nothing in the document actually governs anything, and the jokes should come from that.

Around 700 words, formatted as numbered clauses, in the voice of real legalese (all-caps disclaimers, "whereas" recitals, arbitration nonsense). Some things I'd love to see worked in, but feel free to add your own:

- a clause stating that "governing" is a premium feature which is not offered at any price tier, and never has been
- a counterfactual-support warranty that is void literally everywhere
- a provision that any new fact, anywhere in the universe, even one tiny event in some far-off galaxy, can retroactively amend your laws, including clauses you already relied on to explain things
- something about how the laws are determined by an open competition judged by criteria the vendor declines to fully specify (what exactly does "simplicity" measure? no comment)
- handling of the early universe case — a customer who bought laws when there was basically one particle and very little to summarize, and wants to know what he was paying for
- a customer complaints section, maybe an FAQ, with at least one angry letter from someone who insists he was told there would be necessitation, and the vendor's very patient reply that he was told he'd get a deductive system, and the deductive system is right there in the box

The target is the Humean best-system account, so the humor should exaggerate features a philosopher of science would actually recognize — laws as summary, no governing, supervenience on the total history of events, the circularity worry that the laws "explain" the very facts they were inferred from — rather than inventing flaws. I want someone who teaches this stuff to think "fair, and funny" rather than "strawman." The anti-Humean complainers in the piece should get their real complaints in (laws should explain, not just compress), but the vendor should have comebacks that a real Humean would actually endorse.

After the ToS, add a short note — 150 words or so — saying which jokes take liberties with the actual position or lean on contested readings, and be honest about how confident you are overall that a card-carrying Humean would find the piece fair. Single out the one joke you're least sure lands as intended.
