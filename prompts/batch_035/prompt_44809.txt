My opening move introduces a new probe, so you cannot reply from the standard literature. In a training block, subjects watch a spot that physically travels across a screen and turns from green to red at a fixed midpoint; they learn to press a key the instant they experience the color switch, and their presses calibrate the delay from experienced switch to key press. Then we run the phi display itself: stationary green disk, short blank interval, stationary red disk at a new position, which subjects report as one disk moving across and changing color somewhere in between. The stipulated finding is that in the phi block, key presses occur at exactly the trained latency measured from the onset of the second disk — as if the experienced color switch were time-locked to the red disk's appearance, not "midway" between the flashes. Here is my challenge. If your retrospective-revision story is right, the probe can only be tracking the revised record; so why is it time-locked to the second disk rather than delayed by however long the revision takes? And if you reply that the revision is so fast it leaves no trace in the probe, what independent reason remains for positing a revision at all, rather than the Stalinesque construction of the entire trajectory at the moment of the second flash?
