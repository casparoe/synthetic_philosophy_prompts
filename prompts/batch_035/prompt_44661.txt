I'm in a grad seminar on Bayesian statistics, and my friend who's way deeper into this stuff than me keeps invoking de Finetti. She's got the famous line from the 1974 preface to his Theory of Probability taped above her desk: "PROBABILITY DOES NOT EXIST." Last week I asked what seemed like a normal question -- we were looking at weather forecasts, and I wanted to know whether there's a fact of the matter about the probability it rains tomorrow, independent of what anyone believes -- and she just pointed at the line and said the question presupposes a mistake. Earlier she used the same move to wave off someone talking about the chance of a particular radioactive atom decaying in the next minute, which seems even more confusing, because there I'd have thought physics itself hands you a probability.

I get that the slogan is supposed to be provocative and that de Finetti was a subjectivist, but I can't tell how strong a claim he's actually making, and my friend clearly uses it as if it settles a lot more than I can reconstruct. Like, is it just saying probabilities aren't physical objects you can point at, the way phlogiston and the ether weren't? Is it saying there's no such thing as an objective single-case chance (so propensity talk is confused), which I gather is different from saying frequentism is wrong about probabilities being long-run frequencies? Is it the thesis that all probability assignments are just somebody's degrees of belief, constrained only by coherence? Or something even stronger, like there are no facts about uncertainty at all beyond what's in particular heads, so two people with different priors who never converge aren't even disagreeing about anything? Some of these sound almost trivial and some sound like they'd have to deny that quantum mechanics assigns chances, which seems like a real cost.

Can you lay out the distinct precise claims this one sentence could be expressing, from the most innocuous reading to the most radical, and for each one say whether it's plausible and whether it would actually support what my friend used it for (dismissing the rain question and the decay question)? Please also distinguish for me what's basically settled among people who actually work on this -- like, whatever the Dutch book / coherence machinery shows, I assume that part is not in dispute the way the slogans are -- from what's genuinely contested, such as whether objective chance exists at all or how subjectivists are supposed to handle the Born rule. Don't present some one camp's line as the consensus, because I suspect my friend is doing exactly that. A list I can bring back to her would be perfect.
