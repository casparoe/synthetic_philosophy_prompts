My brother-in-law and I have this recurring argument every time he visits, and I want a second opinion on where it actually lives. He's a pure subjectivist about meaning in life: if someone is fully absorbed collecting vintage staplers, or rewatching the same soap opera, and it fulfills them, then that's all the meaning there is or needs to be — nobody's life is more meaningful than anyone else's on his view, full stop. I keep pushing back with something like Susan Wolf's hybrid idea, that meaning needs both subjective engagement AND some objective worthworth out there for the engagement to latch onto, though I admit I get fuzzy on what "objectively worthwhile" is supposed to be grounded in if there's no god and no cosmic teleology. His credence that pure subjectivism is basically right is around 0.8; mine is more like 0.3, and neither of us has managed to move the other in like four years of this.

So give me your actual credence that some form of the hybrid/fitting-fulfillment view is closer to the truth than pure subjectivism, and decompose it rather than just handing me a number: e.g. the probability that "meaning in life" even carves at a joint that's distinct from happiness or satisfaction, the probability that there are objective values that something can be worthwhile relative to (or whether the hybrid collapses back into subjectivism once you press on "worthwhile"), the probability that our intuitions about Sisyphus-type cases — the guy rolling the boulder forever, or Nagel's version where he's injected with a chemical that makes him love it — are actually tracking something real rather than just our snobbery about staplers vs. symphonies. Feel free to add terms I haven't thought of, maybe including the absurdist angle (whether the whole question dissolves once you take seriously that the universe doesn't care).
