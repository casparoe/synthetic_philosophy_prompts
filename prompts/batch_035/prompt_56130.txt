For the last couple of years I've been trying to estimate the chance that my own first marriage ends in divorce, and I keep running into a problem I think of as the "who do I count myself among" question. Depending on which group of people I compare myself to, I get wildly different base rates, and I have no principled way to choose. My instinct says a smaller group that matches me on more facts is always better, since it's more like me; my wife's counter-instinct is that once a group gets small enough, its divorce rate is just noise, and I'd be learning about luck rather than about marriages. I don't know how to settle this, or whether both of us are wrong.

Here are the candidate groups I've been working with:

1. All first marriages in my country.
2. First marriages where both spouses were past thirty-five at the wedding.
3. First marriages of people in my occupation (I'm a hospital nurse).
4. First marriages where both spouses' own parents stayed married.
5. First marriages where the couple met through close friends, as we did.
6. The very small group: first marriages of left-handed vegetarian nurses who, like me, married after thirty and grew up in mid-sized towns.

Please rank these six groups from most to least trustworthy as a basis for my estimate, with one or two sentences on why each lands where it does, and then tell me in plain terms what I should actually do — pick one group, somehow blend them, or accept that no single number is the right target. Don't just answer "it depends" without saying what it depends on. Keep the whole reply under 350 words.
