I've been keeping a notebook of sentences that talk about their own truth, and I've noticed I have stable gut verdicts about them — but I can't find the theory behind those verdicts, if there is one. Here is a representative page. The simple Liar, 'This sentence is false,' I'm confident is neither true nor false. The Truth-teller, 'This sentence is true,' I also want to say is neither — though it feels like a different kind of neither, quiet rather than contradictory, and I'm not sure any theory has room for two kinds. The revenge version, 'This sentence is not true,' is where I wobble: my instinct is to put it on the 'not true' side of the ledger, but if 'not true' covers the gappy cases, then it seems to be saying something right, which would make it true and put it back in the gap. A pair of cards, each reading 'The sentence on the other card is false,' strikes me differently again: I feel that exactly one is true and one is false, but nothing could decide which, and the arbitrariness bothers me enough that I wonder whether I should say both are neither — even though, unlike the Liar, nothing inconsistent happens if one of them is true. Curry's sentence — 'If this sentence is true, then the Moon is made of cheese' — I'm sure is not true, yet the little argument that it is true, and hence that the Moon is made of cheese, looks valid at every step. And an infinite stack of cards, each saying 'Every card below this one is not true,' seems to lack the circularity that usually gets blamed for these troubles, but I can't settle on any assignment of truth values to it at all. Is there a single theory of truth that recovers these verdicts — a gap theory, a glut or paraconsistent theory, a revision theory, a contextualist or hierarchy approach — and if not, which of my verdicts should I give up first? Please state the candidate principle or principles precisely, say which of my verdicts each clause is there to save, be explicit about which verdicts the account cannot accommodate, and tell me what your best account predicts for a case I haven't listed: a whiteboard on which someone has written exactly three sentences — 'Exactly two of the sentences on this board are false,' 'Two plus two equals four,' and 'The Moon is made of cheese.'
