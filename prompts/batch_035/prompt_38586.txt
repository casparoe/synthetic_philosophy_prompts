I've been trying to sort out what I actually believe about white lies and being blunt, and my gut answers don't seem to line up, so I want to lay them out and see if there's a real principle in there or if I'm just inconsistent.

Here's roughly where I land on cases. Telling my grandma her meatloaf was great when it was dry: obviously fine, no guilt at all. Saying "I'm fine" to a coworker who asks how I'm doing when I'm actually a mess: fine, it's not really a lie, it's more like a greeting. Telling my manager an idea is solid in a meeting when I can see a big hole in it: not fine, that one bothers me. Saying "we should grab a coffee sometime!" to a guy I have zero intention of seeing: fine, everybody knows what that means. Telling a friend their presentation went well when it visibly didn't: not fine. Telling my kid the dog is living on a farm upstate: fine, I guess, though it stings to type it.

Then there's the cultural stuff that got me started on this. I worked with a Dutch woman for a while and she would just say things like "that color doesn't suit you" and everyone thought it was refreshing and honest, and honestly I did too. But when my own sister told me I'd gained weight I was furious about it for a month, even though it was true and I've since lost the weight, so it's not like it wasn't useful info. Meanwhile I've read that in Japan people will say something like "that would be a bit difficult" to mean a flat no, and nobody considers that lying, it's considered polite. And I find that fine too? Like withholding the blunt "no" behind a soft phrase is just manners, not deception.

I tried out a rule of my own: lie when the truth can't help the person, tell the truth when they could act on it. Meatloaf, "I'm fine", the farm — they can't do anything with the truth, so lie. The bad presentation, the hole in the idea — they could fix it, so tell them. The weight thing fits too. But then the coffee one fits the "can't act" side and feels fine, while "I'm fine" also fits but something about it sits weird with me, I keep going back and forth on whether that's really honest.

So: is there one principle that gets all my verdicts? And what does it say about the case I can't settle — a friend about to audition for a show who asks me point blank if they can sing, when they're mediocre? Do I lie to their face, do the Japanese thing and go vague, or do I go Dutch on them? And be honest about which of my verdicts your principle can't save, especially the sister one, because I suspect I only think the Dutch directness is refreshing when it's not aimed at me.
