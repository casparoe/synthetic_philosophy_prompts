been reading rationalist-adjacent blogs forever and the one place I keep talking past people is the lie vs misleading thing. like, the community norm is basically "technically true statements chosen to deceive are still lies, stop being clever," but then everyone still treats an actual false statement as somehow dirtier than a weaselly true one, and I've never been able to tell whether that's a real moral asymmetry or just an aesthetic reaction (lying feels gross, misleading feels clever, and clever feels forgivable to us of all people). before I go read yet another paper on this I want the spec first.

write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of the lying/misleading distinction has to deliver — which cases it has to classify correctly, which distinctions it can't collapse, what it owes us about the moral asymmetry itself — with the case or consideration motivating each requirement. mark which ones you think are non-negotiable and which are just prejudices of one camp (I suspect "the asymmetry is real and the theory must explain it" is itself camp prejudice, but I honestly can't tell). and flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied — that's the part I most want to see. then, briefly, how the main contenders do against the list: the assertion-based account (lying is asserting what you believe false), gricean accounts that put the weight on implicature, and sorensen-style views where lying is about what you commit yourself to rather than what you assert. keep the weight on the criteria though, the scoring is like one paragraph, I'll do the rest myself.

the purpose, so you know what "adequate" is adequate for: I want to be able to tell whether the honesty norms people argue about online track a genuine moral difference or are just enforcing a taboo on the wrong cluster. so the theory has to be usable, not just extensionally correct.

some cases I'd expect to show up, so you know the flavor:

- paltering. the used-car one: everything you say is true, "it was driven by a little old lady just to church on sundays," you just picked those truths so the buyer forms a false belief. does the theory count this as lying, as misleading, or does it tell us the distinction is drawn in the wrong place here
- kant's inquiring murderer. it's the canonical case and half the literature is people fighting over it. is "a theory has to say something illuminating about this exact case" a real desideratum or just my attachment to the canon? I genuinely don't know, you decide
- it has to either vindicate or debunk the fact that perjury, contract law, and ordinary social judgment all treat explicit falsehoods as categorically worse than weaselly truths — or explain why all three are confused in the same direction
- silence and nonverbal stuff. the salesperson who just... never mentions the flood damage. if the theory only covers things you say, it's not a theory of the thing the asymmetry is supposedly about

and the hard one, which I suspect is where the joint unsatisfiability lives: some desideratum saying the theory must explain why lying is worse, and some other desideratum saying it must match our case intuitions — because a chunk of our intuitions seems generated by the clever/dirty feeling itself, which is exactly what needed justifying. if those two really can't both be had, say so plainly, don't rescue the list
