I've been a distributed systems engineer for about 12 years now (replication, quorum writes, caching, consensus protocols, that whole world) and I recently read Hofstadter's I Am a Strange Loop after my dad passed away, sort of as a way of processing it. My engineer's instinct is: fine, this is just replication. The primary instance is gone, the replicas were lossy copies from the start, there's no failover mechanism that promotes one of them back to being the person, and they will never receive another update — they'll just serve increasingly stale reads until they're overwritten by newer state. That framing makes his claim feel both obviously true and weirdly hollow, and I can't tell which part is the framing's fault.

So here's what I want. Then give me an honest residue report: which of Hofstadter's claims translate cleanly, which ones you had to distort to make them fit, and which ones have no counterpart in my vocabulary whatsoever.
