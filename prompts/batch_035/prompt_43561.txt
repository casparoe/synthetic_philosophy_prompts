I teach a small evening seminar on decision theory for graduate students who have mostly met money pumps only as a one-line argument for transitivity. For our session on diachronic rationality, please write a decision tree that sorts a student from a starting observation — an agent whose preferences cycle can be led to pay for the privilege of ending up where it began — to a named position on what, if anything, is wrong with such an agent.

Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of positions: first settle the branch questions, then locate the positions within the space that the possible answer combinations generate. Every branch question must be a concrete case a student could answer without knowing which position the answer feeds, and the tree must include at least the following splits:

1. A student's sister cycles among three flats, each move preferred to staying put, each move costing €600 in fees, and no landlord ever chains the offers together. Is the sister irrational anyway? This should separate the view that the exploitable disposition is itself the defect from the view that a pump requires a pump-operator.
2. Foreseeing that at a later choice point you will accept a trade that completes a cycle, are you now required to choose as if your present choice settled all the later ones?
3. Is it legitimate to pay a small fee now to bind your future self against a trade that the future self will want to accept?
4. You prefer A to B on Monday and B to A on Tuesday, with no new information in between: is this diachronic shift a different kind of defect from a synchronic cycle, needing its own principle, or the same defect on a longer timescale?

The leaves must include, at minimum: the orthodox requirement of acyclic preferences; sophisticated choice, on which the agent plans around its own future choices by backward induction; resolute choice, on which the agent commits to a plan and refuses to reopen it at the later node; the counterparty view, on which nothing is irrational about the preference structure itself and the pump is a fact about adversarial environments rather than about the pumped; and a deflationary leaf on which there is no single agent there whose preferences could be required to cohere across time, the alleged intransitivity being an artifact of forcing one preference relation onto what is really a sequence of evaluators. Add at most one further leaf if the logical space demands it.

For each leaf, give the position's name (or write "unnamed" if, to your knowledge, no one defends it) and one sentence on its most awkward commitment. After the tree, add two short sections: one diagnosing each unoccupied combination of answers, saying for each whether it looks incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored; and one flagging any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate, stating what further case would be needed to pull them apart. Do not include a bibliography, reading list, or citations of any kind.

Format: begin the response with the tree itself, rendered as an indented outline in which each branch point is labeled by its question and each fork by the answer that selects it — no preface, no restatement of this request, and no commentary before the tree. The two closing sections on unoccupied combinations and inseparable leaves come after the tree.
