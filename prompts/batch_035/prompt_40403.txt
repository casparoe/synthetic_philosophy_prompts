weird thing but i've recently become obsessed with vagueness bc of my job. i administer a benefits program where you qualify if your income is "low", and we have to set an actual cutoff number, and someone asked me recently whether earning one dollar more would change anything real about whether he's poor. i gave some bureaucratic answer but it bugged me for weeks. anyway, after reading around i've basically landed on epistemicism: vague predicates like "low income" or "heap" or "bald" really do have sharp boundaries, there's some exact number n where n grains is a heap and n-1 isn't, we just can't ever know where the line is. i find this weirdly liberating in the benefits context (the cutoff isn't arbitrary metaphysically, just epistemically) but maybe that's cope.

so what would actually count against epistemicism? and i don't mean the generic "it's counterintuitive to think there's a cutoff" objection, i know that one and frankly i can live with it. i mean: are there cases it genuinely can't describe (higher-order vagueness stuff?), intuitions it has to stomp on that other views handle fine, results about the logic of vagueness that would collapse it into supervaluationism or the degree-theoretic picture? does it even predict anything distinctive that could turn out wrong, or has it been qualified into unfalsifiability — and if a view like this can't be falsified, is that a real problem for it or just what you get with semantic/metaphysical claims? also curious whether committed epistemicists would concede any of your defeaters as counting against them vs. waving them off. rank them for me from fatal to merely awkward pls.
