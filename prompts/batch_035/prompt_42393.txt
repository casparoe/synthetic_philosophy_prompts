for my philosophy club's last meeting of the year i'm running a session on the cosmological argument, and specifically on whether the kalam version and the leibniz-style contingency version are basically the same argument in different hats or genuine rivals. the way i see it (and my advisor mostly agrees) is that the real test case is a beginingless universe: if there was never a first moment, kalam's premise about things that begin to exist needing causes just never gets triggered, but the contingency arguer still asks why there's this eternal universe rather than no universe at all. so the two arguments come apart exactly when the universe has no beginning.

i want to close the session with a villanelle that i'll read out loud, so it should actually sound good. one refrain should be a version of the kalam premise — that whatever begins to exist has a cause — and the other refrain should be a version of the principle of sufficient reason, that anything that exists at all, even eternally, needs an explanation. as the refrains keep coming back, the stanzas should take turns making each one look strong and the other look like it missed the point. somewhere in the middle i want a stanza where someone brings up the eternal-universe case and the kalam refrain basically goes quiet while the sufficient-reason one keeps pressing. and since it's a villanelle the two refrains end up sitting side by side in the final couplet, and i want that moment to actually say something — not a cheap tie, more like showing why smart people end up on both sides.

please be fair to both arguments (my club is genuinely split on this and i don't want to rig the ending), and accurate enough that my advisor, who actually knows this literature, won't wince. the session is pretty casual so a little humor is fine, but no cheap shots like god-of-the-gaps jokes, half the people there are theists and they'll tune out.
