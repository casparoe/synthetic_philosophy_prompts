I'm a retired geophysicist who got into philosophy seriously only after hanging up my boots, and lately I've been working through Kierkegaard, of all people. The part that's really grabbed me is his attack on the press and "the public" in the little book usually called The Present Age (from around 1846). The way I read him, he's saying that the press conjures up an abstraction -- "the public" -- which is nobody in particular, and this phantom lets people have opinions without anyone being responsible for a commitment. Genuine action, he thinks, comes from concrete individuals in concrete situations, together in a real sense; the public is never together in that way, it's just a kind of mirage that levels everything and lets everyone feel like they participated when nobody actually did anything. Members of a real crowd can at least hold each other accountable, but you can't hold a phantom to anything.

Here's my problem. Since I retired I volunteer as one of the moderators of the online forum for our mid-sized town -- planning disputes, school board arguments, lost cats, the works. Maybe 8,000 members, a handful of us modorators, and we're constantly deciding things like: should we allow a poll on the new parking ordinance, should we let the thread about the canceled fireworks display keep going, is this recurring "what's wrong with this town" rant a discussion or just a venting wall, do we pin the petition about the crosswalk. And it struck me the other day that these are exactly Kierkegaard's questions in miniature: which threads create real commitments and simultaneity between actual neighbors, and which just feed the phantom public, letting hundreds of people "react" in ways that substitute for -- and therefore quietly prevent -- anyone showing up at the council meeting.

What I'd like is a practical decision procedure, something me and the other mods (none of whom have read a word of philosophy, and I'm not exactly an expert myself) can actually run in five minutes per thread. A checklist in order: the questions to ask, what counts as an answer good enough to keep a thread going or promote it, where the procedure should tell us to close or demote something, and -- this is important -- where it has to admit the question is one of values and hand it back to a human. Then please run it on three cases from last month: (1) a poll on the parking ordinance that got 900 votes and exactly two comments; (2) a messy 300-comment argument about the fireflies display where people were genuinely furious at each other but a few of them ended up organizing to actually attend the meeting; and (3) the recurring crosswalk petition that everyone signs and nobody ever acts on. And be honest with me: I suspect the procedure will sometimes tell us to shut down threads people love -- like that petition -- and I want to know whether that's the procedure working or me forcing Kierkegaard onto a forum he never imagined. Also tell me plainly where you think the analogy to 1846 Copenhagen breaks down, since I'm aware the man was writing about newspapers and I'm applying it to something with a reply button. If it helps, assume we can only spend maybe 15 minutes a week per mod on this, and that any removal can be appealed to the town association, so the procedure needs to produce reasons a non-philosopher can evaluate.
