The setup: Marcus was saying that maximizing expected wealth is basically an incoherent instruction for anyone who lives in time -- if you repeatedly bet your whole bankroll on positive-EV gambles you go broke with probability one, so what you should maximize is the growth rate of your wealth over time, and that gives you the Kelly fraction as a theorem, not a preference. Priya rolled her eyes and said that's just expected utility with a log utility function, something decision theorists have had forever, and the "ergodicity economics" people are rebranding it as a revolution. Marcus said insurance is actually the easiest case for his side, because what insurance does is keep you away from absorbing states where you can't bet anymore, and any utility function steep enough near zero is just approximating that barrier -- the barrier is the real thing, the curve is the epiphenomenon. Priya's last word was that this is all backwards: the actual practice of Kelly bettors is to bet half-Kelly or less because of estimation error and fat tails, and that fudge factor is doing exactly the work that "preferences" were supposed to do, so the objectivity claim was never really load-bearing.
