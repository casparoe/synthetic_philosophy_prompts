Not sure this is the right kind of question for this forum but here goes. I keep running into the same weird problem in arguments (mostly about AI lately, people saying stuff like "the system should do what you'd want if you knew more and had thought about it longer"), and I can't find a name for it anywhere.

Take my uncle first. Smoked for thirty years, hated that he smoked, finally quit, and now says the guy who wanted cigarettes wasn't the "real" him. So there's clearly a sense in which your considered, informed self gets to override what you literally said you wanted, and everyone thinks that's fine, even admirable.

But then take my friend who wonders what she'd want "if I were a completely different person, some sage who'd figured everything out." That ideal person's preferences have basically nothing in common with hers — asking the sage is like asking a stranger. And nobody thinks her actual wishes should lose out to a stranger's wishes.

So the move "replace what you want with what an idealized version of you would want" only seems to work when the idealization moves you a short distance. Small move (uncle: same guy minus the addiction) and it's a helpful correction. Big move (sage: basically a different person) and you've just been overridden by something that isn't you. Which means there's a hidden dial here — how far do you extrapolate before the output stops counting as yours — and I've never seen anyone name that dial or say where it should be set. And when I push on the AI people about it, their "idealized humanity" definately tends to end up agreeing with whatever the speaker already believed anyway, so the whole exercise never surprises anyone, which makes me trust it less.

Is there an established name for this general bind, that idealizing someone's preferences either changes nothing (so why do it) or changes so much it's no longer them (so why care what it says)? If there's a real term I'd love to know it, and also how it relates to whatever the standard framing of this is in ethics, because "just ask what they'd really want" can't be the whole story and I'd like to be able to say precisely why. If there's no settled concept, then fine, coin me one, but give a definition with the edges drawn clearly enough that I can tell which side the uncle case and the sage case fall on.
