So me and my brother-in-law have been having the same argument for like two years now: is the brain a computer or not. I'm a sysadmin, no philosophy or neuroscience background at all, and he's a neuroscience grad student who's kind of insufferable about it. But here's the thing I've noticed from all these arguements: they never go anywhere because nobody agrees what "computer" even means. If a computer is just anything that takes inputs and produces outputs according to some rule, then sure, the brain's a computer, but so is a thermostat and a river delta, and the claim becomes boring. If it means stored programs with seperate memory and processing like the machine on my desk, the brain obviously isn't one, and the claim is dead on arrival. So people are talking past each other, and I want to stop arguing and figure out what the actual question is first.

What I want from you is the spec. Write me the list of things a theory that says the brain is (or isn't) a computer would have to deliver before I should believe it. Which facts about the brain it can't contradict -- neurons firing way slower than transistors, no clean line between "memory" and "processing" since the connections between neurons seem to BE the memory, the whole thing being noisy and redundant and still working fine after strokes chew parts of it out. Which puzzles it has to actually deal with, like how something that was grown instead of designed ends up with "representations" in it at all, or whether it matters that evolution had no spec sheet. And which distinctions it's not allowed to collapse -- computing something vs merely being describable as computing something, following a rule vs just happening to match one, simulating a thing vs being the thing. For each requirement, tell me what the case is that it's a genuine requirement and not just one camp's pet preference dressed up as one, and flag any pair of requirements you suspect can't be satisfied together. That last part is honestly what I want most, because if some of these are impossible together then the whole debate has been misframed for decades and I'd like to know that before round 12 with this guy.

Then at the end, briefly, run the main positions against your list -- the straightforward "the brain literally computes, it's just not a laptop" view, the "brain is a dynamical system, computation talk is a bad metaphor" view, and whatever the in-between view is where it's computational but not in the way engineers mean. Keep that part short though, the requirements are the real deliverable.

Please structure it in these sections, in this order: "Where I'm at" (short, just my confusion restated so we're sure you're answering the right question), "The requirements" (the bulk of it, roughly 60% of the whole thing), "Requirements that might clash" (medium), "The main views vs the list" (brief), and "Settled vs up for grabs" (short). That last one really matters: my brother-in-law talks like all of this is settled science and I want to know what's actually agreed on by people who study this versus what's genuinely contested. Please don't hand me someone's contested opinion as if it were the consensus -- I want to be able to catch him doing exactly that.
