My friend and I spent an entire evening arguing about the semantics and pragmatics of slurs, and we got nowhere because we never agreed on what a theory of slurs is actually supposed to deliver: she thinks the derogatory content is part of the conventional meaning — a slur semantically expresses the demeaning ideology, roughly as a predicate plus a built-in insult — while I hold that its literal semantics is thinner than that and the derogation is a pragmatic act performed by uttering the token. Before we go another round, I want the specification written down. Please produce the desiderata: what any adequate theory of the meaning and use of slurs must get right, with the case or datum behind each requirement. At minimum the list should say how the theory must handle appropriated or reclaimed use by in-group speakers, slurs appearing under quotation or indirect report, the fact that many slurs seem to persist in embedded contexts like negation and conditionals (the Frege–Geach-style data), the apparent existence of slurs with no neutral counterpart, and the question of whether the offense tracks a descriptive property at all or something more like a prescribed affective stance. For each requirement, say whether it is non-negotiable or an artifact of one theoretical camp, and flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — that tension is what I most want to see, since it is exactly where my friend and I keep stalling. Do not include a scorecard of existing theories; we will do that ourselves. Begin with your bottom line — a short verdict on which of the desiderata can and cannot be jointly met — and then give the list, with no preamble and no restatement of this brief.
