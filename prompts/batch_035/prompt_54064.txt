I've been reading rationalist blogs since the Overcoming Bias days, and I've never fully digested my discomfort with the common prior assumption. Aumann's agreement theorem and the no-trade results strike me as mathematically unassailable, but they all rest on the premise that rational agents who share their evidence and know each other's opinions must have started from the same prior. My suspicion has shifted over the years from the theorems to the prior itself, and I'd like your help locating it.

Here's the proposition, stated so it has a truth value: the common prior assumption is correct as a normative principle — that is, any disagreement between ideally rational agents who have shared all their evidence reflects asymmetric information or partition differences, never differing priors, so that distinct priors among the fully rational are always a symptom of a mistake somewhere upstream.

Please give me your credence in that proposition with the derivation shown. At minimum I'd expect terms for: (1) whether "a prior" is even well-defined for an ideally rational agent — since a prior must come from somewhere, either an earlier prior, pushing the problem back indefinitely, or something like a principle of indifference, and indifference priors seem hostage to the choice of parameterization, which looks like a hidden disagreement smuggled into the supposedly shared starting point; (2) whether the conventional escape routes (different partitions over one prior, disagreement about what the shared evidence means) can absorb the disagreements we actually observe between smart, honest, well-informed people; and (3) whether the assumption's descriptive failures — the no-trade theorem sitting alongside markets that never stop trading — count against it as a normative idealization or merely show that real traders fall short of it. Assign each term a probability with a line of justification, combine them into a final number, and then tell me which term the total is most sensitive to and what observation would move it most.

For calibration: I'm at roughly 0.4, down from 0.7 a decade ago, and the parameterization worry is what moved me. I'd like to know whether you think that term deserves the weight I've put on it.
