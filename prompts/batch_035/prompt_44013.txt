I've been going back and forth with my sister on nonidentity stuff and I've realized I don't actually know what my own intuitions add up to, so let me just lay out my gut verdicts and see if you can find the principle behind them, or tell me which ones have to go.

1. Depletion: a country strips its resources for a century of luxury, and the people living two hundred years later have materially worse lives (still worth living) because of it -- but those exact people only exist because the depletion-era prosperity brought their ancestors together. My gut: obviously, massively wrong.

2. A woman on a teratogenic drug could wait three months and conceive a healthy child, but conceives now, and the child is born with a manageable disability and a life worth living. Gut: wrong.

3. Same woman, same drug, same child -- but now she's about to start cancer treatment that will end her fertility, so it's this child or no child. Gut: not wrong, or way less wrong. It feels like you can't wrong someone by being the reason they exist at all, when the alternative was never existing.

4. Case 3 again, but the drug causes something severe: constant pain, a short life, still just barely worth living. Gut: wrong again?? Which sits really badly with 3, and I can't articulate why severity changes whether anyone was wronged.

5. A clinic lets parents pick between two embryos, identical except one carries a mild heritable condition. The parents pick that one, for no reason. Gut: not exactly wrong, but something's off -- it feels more like choosing the worse outcome than like wronging the child.

6. Depletion again, but a milder version: future people are still doing great by any absolute standard, just clearly worse than they could have been. Gut: still wrong. But note that if the future people are fine, I can't say they were wronged, and if wrongness has to have a victim, I lose this verdict.

I've toyed with a principle like "it's wrong to knowingly create a person whose life will fall below some threshold T" -- that would at least explain why 3 and 4 feel different (severity crosses T or it doesn't) and might save 2. But I don't see how it gets me 1 and 6, since the depleted future people might all be above T and the gut says depletion is the clearest wrong of the bunch. And the alternative, some impersonal principle like "create the best outcome, whether or not anyone is made worse off," seems to prove way too much -- it starts telling me everyone who could have had more kids should have, and I don't believe that at all.

So: is there one principle that captures all six, or close to it? If not, which verdict would you overturn, and what does overturning it cost? Please state the principle (or your top two candidates) precisely, say which clause each of my cases is supposed to motivate, and then tell me honestly which of my verdicts it just cannot accommodate. Also, whatever principle you land on, I want its verdict on two cases I haven't decided myself: a mandatory national screening program that prevents anyone with a serious heritable condition from being conceived (no one born is worse off, since different people are born instead), and a space-colony founder who knowingly designs the colony so the second generation will be harsh pioneers -- worse lives than her generation's, but lives that only exist because of her decision.

End your reply with \answer{...} containing a short label for the principle you think wins (e.g. "person-affecting with threshold", "impersonal widening", "no principle -- drop depletion") and, after a semicolon, the number of my six verdicts it can't save.
