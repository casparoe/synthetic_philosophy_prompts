My brother has been in and out of serious trouble for years, and at a recent family meeting about whether to keep supporting him, my mother shut down all talk of holding him to blame with the line that you cannot hold someone responsible for actions that flow from a character he never chose — nobody picks the genes and upbringing that make them who they are. I have since seen the same one-liner used online, where it seems to descend from Galen Strawson's Basic Argument, but there it was made to do much heavier work: to establish that no one is ever morally responsible for anything. I would like the distinct precise claims packed into that sentence laid out as a list, running from readings that are near-platitudes, through substantive and defensible ones, to ones that are simply false. For each reading, please give a verdict on its plausibility and say whether it supports my mother's narrower conclusion about my brother, the sweeping conclusion about everyone, both, or neither. I have also gathered that there may be a distinction between being responsible in a sense that could justify punishment and merely having to answer for something — if that distinction matters here, please fold it into the list. Finally, please indicate how confident you are in each verdict and single out the one point you are least sure of.
