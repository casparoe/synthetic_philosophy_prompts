I'm putting together a piece for a workshop on freedom and want a sanity check before I write the full thing. Here are the notes so far:

The received story is Berlin's two concepts, negative and positive liberty, with republican liberty (liberty as non-domination, Pettit-style) arriving later as a third option, motivated by cases like the slave with an unusually kind master: no actual interference ever occurs, yet we want to say the slave is unfree because the master could interfere at will. My thought is that the interesting action today isn't in re-litigating that three-way debate in the abstract, but in noticing that algorithmic systems put real pressure on the republican concept in a way the classic literature never anticipated.

The core claim: platforms and recommendation systems are a natural test case where negative liberty says "no problem absent actual interference," positive liberty muddies things with talk of autonomy and manipulation, and non-domination seems to get the phenomenology right — a creator whose livelihood depends on an algorithm they can't predict or contest is dominated in something like the slaveowner sense, even if the algorithm's interventions are on net beneficial and rarely hostile.

But here's where I get stuck, and what I'd most like comments on. Domination, on the standard account, seems to require a dominator: an agent with the arbitrary capacity to interfere. An algorithmic feed is not an agent exercising discretionary will, and often even the deploying firm can't fully foresee its behavior. So I see three options and dislike all of them: (a) bite the bullet and allow domination without a dominator, which risks inflating the concept until it's just "living under structural uncertainty you didn't consent to"; (b) pin domination on the firm for deploying a discretionary system, which seems to hold them responsible precisely for what they can't control; (c) restrict non-domination to cases with identifiable agents and concede it just doesn't reach the algorithmic case. Option (c) feels like giving up on the paper.

A couple of specific questions: does Pettit's framework already handle collective and diffuse agents (states, majorities) in a way that extends naturally here? Is the "inflation" worry in (a) a real problem, or am I being precious about conceptual hygiene? And is the strongest objection to my setup that I've snuck interference back in — i.e., that opaque ranking just is a form of interference, so the republican framework isn't doing distinctive work after all? Also flag anything here that strikes you as either wrong or boring, since I can't always tell the difference in my own stuff.
