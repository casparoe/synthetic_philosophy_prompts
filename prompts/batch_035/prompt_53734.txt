My wife and I have been trying to start a family, and we've hit a snag that's turned into a weird philosophical argument between us. We live in a town where the water supply got contaminated after the city switched providers last year — they're saying it'll be at least 18 months before it's fully sorted. Her doctor told us that if she's exposed during pregnancy, there's a somewhat elevated risk of developmental problems for the baby. Nothing catastrophic, and honestly the absolute numbers are small, but it's not nothing. The obvious fix would be to wait: either until the water is clean or until we can move somewhere else.

Here's where it got strange. When I said "obviously we should wait, it's better for our child," my wife — who's a geneticist and kind of pedantic about these things — pointed out that "our child" isn't the right way to talk about it. The kid we'd conceive this spring and the kid we'd conceive in two years wouldn't be the same person at all. Different egg, different sperm, different kid. So she says the question "would conceiving now be worse for him/her?" is literally malformed — there's no particular baby out there waiting who could be better or worse off depending on when we time it. The child conceived now, mild elevated risks and all, would still presumably have a life well worth living, and that child *only exists* because we didn't wait.

Her sister thinks this is monstrous reasoning, like we're using a technicality to justify taking a risk with a future kid, and honestly my gut is partly on her side. At dinner the other night a cousin of mine who studied philosophy mumbled something about how maybe the comparison to make is between whole lives rather than between one child's life with and without the contamination — whatever that means. So I'm turning to you: is my wife right that nobody is actually harmed if we go ahead now? Does it even make sense to say we'd be doing something wrong, and to whom? And if waiting isn't better for anyone in particular, is there still some other reason it would be wrong not to wait, or is my sister-in-law just reacting to how the argument *sounds* rather than to what it actually establishes?
