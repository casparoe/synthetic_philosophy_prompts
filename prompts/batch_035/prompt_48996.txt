Black's point, as I understand it, is that a universe like that seems perfectly coherent, so qualitative identity can't entail numerical identity. Anyway, I rewatched The Prestige (the Nolan one from 2006) with some friends the same week and realized it's basically a feature-length dramatization of this exact debate, and now I want to write my term paper on it but I'm not sure my reading actually holds up, so I'd love a second opinion.

Angier, on the other hand, builds the machine that produces an exact duplicate, and every night one of the two drowns — and in that moment the two Angiers seem to be truly indiscernible, closer to Black's spheres than the twins ever were, yet the story still treats them as two and keeps a score of which one survives. So my question is whether the film ends up committed to primitive thisness (some fact about being *this* one rather than *that* one, over and above all qualitative facts) to make the drowned-tank horror land — or whether Angier's line about not knowing whether he'd be the man in the box or the man in the spotlight actually shows the opposite, that even Angier can't attach to his haecceity.
