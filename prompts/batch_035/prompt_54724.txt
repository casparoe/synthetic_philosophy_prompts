Longtime lurker here who finally got around to reading Hofstadter's *I Am a Strange Loop* after years of seeing it name-dropped on LessWrong and in alignment-adjacent essays (usually in the "self-models, what even are they" threads). The core claim I already knew: the "I" is a strange loop, a self-referential pattern the brain spins about itself, and it's the pattern that matters, not the meat. But the part that's been living in my head rent-free for a week is what he does with that when his wife Carol dies of a brain tumor in 1993. His move, roughly, is that every "I" is actually spread across many brains, because the people who know you deeply carry rich partial copies of your pattern in their heads, and he's merely the most saturated copy of Carol. So when her brain shut down, her "I" didn't go to zero — a chunk of it literally (he insists, not metaphorically) keeps running, at reduced fidelity, inside him and everyone else who knew her well. Partly I love this because it's the only version of an afterlife I can even picture taking seriously as an empirical claim, and partly it makes me deeply uncomfortable for reasons I can't fully articulate.

My buddy from a discord server thinks it's obviously true given the patternist premises: if selves are patterns rather than hunks of substrate, then lossy distributed copies are just what you get, and being squeamish about it is meat-privilege. My wife thinks it's a category error dressed up as consolation — a photo of your grandmother isn't a partial grandmother, and a rich internal model of Carol in Hofstadter's head is a *representation* of Carol, not a piece of Carol's self. And now I genuinely can't settle it, so I want to do the rationalist thing and put a number on it.

Give me your credence that Hofstadter is basically right: that a person's "I" can be partially instantiated in other people's brains such that, when the original brain dies, some of that person's self literally survives in a distributed, low-fidelity form. Decompose it however you think is honest, but at minimum I want terms for: (1) patternism about selves being true (self = abstract pattern, substrate-independent in the relevant sense); (2) the copy-vs-representation question, i.e., whether a rich model of X in my head is a partial X or merely a model *of* X; (3) a fidelity/individuation question — how much of the loop has to be there before "partial instance" is even coherent, versus just "a person whose personality was influenced by"; and (4) whether the pattern needs to be actively running (self-triggering memories, dispositions firing in real time) versus just stored, like data on a disk that nothing reads. Assign a rough probability to each with a one-line justification, show me how they combine into your final number, and then tell me which term you're least confident in and what argument or thought experiment would actually move it — including whether it's the kind of question that could resolve in principle or whether it collapses into a definitional choice about the word "survives." For what it's worth I'm hovering around 0.2, mostly because the photo-of-grandmother objection feels devastating to me, but I distrust my intuitions here since I want it to be false for my own future brain-death-related reasons. Also happy to hear if you think my decomposition itself is rigged.
