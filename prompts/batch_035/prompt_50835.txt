My friend and I have this running thing where I pitch her hypothetical lives and she rules on whether they go well or badly. Been doing it for years. I've kept notes, and her verdicts so far:

1. The informed experience machine taker: she knows perfectly well it's a machine, freely chose it, and inside it everything is wonderful for decades. Verdict: life does NOT go well.
2. The mocked executive: thinks everyone at her firm respects her, they actually laugh at her constantly behind her back, she never finds out and is quite content. Verdict: NOT well.
3. The contented anhedonic: every desire satisfied, fully informed, loves her quiet little routine — but feels flat and gray basically every waking hour. Verdict: NOT well.
4. The struggling scientist: real discoveries, real friendships, tons of daily frustration and only modest enjoyment. Verdict: going well — better than the contented anhedonic person, and she was pretty firm about that comparison.

Next session I'm planning to hit her with two new cases:

(i) The wanderer: quits everything at 35, and spends the rest of a long life wandering between beautiful places. Every belief she has is true, she's told no lies, and she finds it genuinely delightful — but there are no projects, no friendships, no growth, no deep anything, and she knows that about herself and is fine with it.
(ii) The weary foster parent: takes in kid after kid, the relationships are real and the good done is real, but she's exhausted and low-grade miserable nearly all the time, and she'd say she doesn't regret it.

Predict her verdicts on both, and more importantly walk me through the reasoning: which rules actually fit her track record so far (name the candidate theories of well-being, and where they'd have to be tweaked to accommodate her calls), which of those rules agree vs. disagree on the two new cases, your best guess with the reasons for preferring it, and what case I should save for the session after that to best split whatever rules survive.
