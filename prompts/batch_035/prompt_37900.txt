(1) Double effect says you may cause harm you merely foresee, but never harm you intend as a means. (3) In the classic driver case, look at what actually saves the five: the trolley is turned onto the spur, and the one workman's body is what stops it there. (5) So double effect forbids turning the trolley — the same verdict it gives for the footbridge case, which is precisely the verdict it was invented to avoid. (6) Either double effect gets the driver case wrong or the contrast it was supposed to explain was never there. He says he's happy with either disjunct, which is what scares me.
