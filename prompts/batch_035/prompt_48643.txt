He brought up that this is the textbook thing about ad hoc rescues of a theory. Plus, he said, pre-industrial history is basically Malthus on display — living standards flat for centuries, population crashes raising wages, the pattern only breaking around 1800, so the theory was right about every century except the ones where the checks went into overdrive. (4) His friend said that makes it worse, not better: if the theory is compatible with a population explosion (1800-1970), a population plateau, AND the shrinking populations we see now, then it's compatible with the opposite of its central prediction, which means it explains nothing. He also said the causal arrow can run backwards anyway — Boserup's point that population pressure is what drove people to invent better farming, so stress creates the escape rather than the escape arriving despite the stress. (5) My roommate's last word was that any theory about centuries-scale dynamics faces this, you can't run experiments on history, and the fair standard is whether the mechanism has been shown to *break* or merely been outrun by things it never claimed to model, like technology. His friend said that concedes the whole point: a theory that predicts nothing checkable within any human timescale is unfalsifiable in practice even if it's falsifiable on paper.
