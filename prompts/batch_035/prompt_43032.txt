My next guest is a philosopher who defends actual intentionalism — the view that what a novel means is fixed by what the author actually intended, not by whatever readers pull out of it. I dont want the usual death of the author rehash; we've all heard that a hundred times and my listeners would tune out. I want questions down in the weeds where his view actually has to do some work.

Draft me eight to ten interview questions that go after the real pressure points. Specifically I want at least one on the gap between actual intentionalism and hypothetical intentionalism — the fallback where meaning is whatever the best hypothesis readers could form about a posited author would attribute to them — and why that retreat isn't just the obvious move once actual intentions start misbehaving. I also want one on whether I can change my book's meaning after publication: if I go on this very podcast and announce that actually the sister was dead the whole time, does the novel now mean that? Maybe I'm the one whose intentions failed, or maybe the chapter just meant something I never got to choose; either way I want him pinned down.
