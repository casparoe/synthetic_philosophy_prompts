I'm a PhD student in political philosophy. My advisor has spent her career writing on the ethics of contested markets, but she refuses to state her governing principle — her line is always that the cases come first — so before our joint panel next month I assembled her verdicts from her articles, op-eds, and testimony. She would ban: vote selling, even between informed adults with the secret ballot intact; commercial surrogacy contracted between wealthy, well-counseled, fully insured parties; post-hurricane markups on generators and hotel rooms; and paying professional line-standers to hold seats in the public galleries of congressional hearings. She would permit, with regulation: generous compensation for healthy volunteers in phase-1 drug trials, with risk capped; paid plasma donation drawn mainly from students and low-wage workers, under tight supervision; and aftermarket resale of concert tickets above face value. The new case the organizers have put to the panel: a funeral company that rents out professional mourners — unionized, well-paid actors who attend wakes and weep on cue, with the hiring advertised openly so that no attendee is deceived, and with the families comfortably off and the actors fully informed. Please predict her verdict on this case. First identify the rules that fit her record equally well — I suspect one is a corruption-style principle about what money should not be able to buy regardless of consequences, and another concerns vulnerability, dire need, and harmful spillovers, but I may be missing candidates — then say what each rule predicts about the mourner case, give your best guess with your reasons for preferring it, and name the one further case that would best discriminate between the candidate rules.
