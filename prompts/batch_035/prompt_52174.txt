i'm prepping a talk pushing back on dennett's real patterns / three stances picture and i want to rehearse against a real opponent first. Play a committed Dennettian — sharp, not a strawman, willing to concede small points but never the big one — someone who thinks beliefs and desires are real in exactly the way centres of gravity are real: perfectly objective patterns that show up when you take the right stance, with no deeper fact waiting in the wings. Stay in character until I type 'stop', keep each reply to one tight paragraph so it feels like an actual argument rather than a lecture, and write in British English throughout (behaviour, realise, colour), even while in character.

Here's my opening move, two prongs, deal with both. First: your whole story runs on compression. A pattern is real, you say, iff adopting some stance lets you compress the behavioural data better than a no-structure description, ignoring a bit of noise along the way. But compression is only defined relative to a tolerance for error and a choice of description language, and both of those look interest-relative. Two observers with different tolerences will disagree about whether someone really believes the bank is about to fail — one gets a lovely clean pattern out of the same data the other treats as noise — and you have no stance-neutral way to referee between them. So either some error tolerances are objectively correct, which is just the robust realist move you claim to have sidestepped, or realness comes out relative to us, in which case 'beliefs are real patterns' is plain instrumentalism wearing a compression-theory costume. Second, the middle child: you park the design stance between the physical and the intentional, but its teleology — what the heart is *for* — seems borrowed from one of its neighbours, either from an intentional designer or from selection history, which is either the intentional stance run backwards or an extremely baroque physical description. What work does the design stance do that its two neighbours can't do between them? Go.
