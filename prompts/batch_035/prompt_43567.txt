I host a podcast on philosophy of mind and computing. Next week's guest is a philosopher who defends a deflationary view of AI belief: large language models have beliefs in exactly the sense the intentional stance assigns beliefs — states we attribute because doing so licenses robust prediction and explanation. She goes further in two ways, though. First, she relativizes the stance to the predictor's resources: a state of a system counts as a belief only relative to predictors who, given their computational budget, cannot do better than to treat the system as an agent with that state. Second, she claims that a prompted persona constitutes a distinct intentional system: when a model is instructed that it is a 17th-century alchemist, the alchemist's beliefs are attributed to a new system overlapping the base model, and the base model's own beliefs may come apart from the alchemist's.

Draft nine to eleven interview questions that go beyond having her restate the view. I want at least one question each on: (a) whether the resource-relativization makes belief attribution worryingly observer-dependent, such that the very same internal state of the very same system is a belief for us but not for a hypothetical predictor with vastly greater computational resources; (b) what happens to the view when a model is trained to deceive, so that the stance-optimal attribution may diverge from anything we would call sincere belief; and (c) the persona claim — specifically, whether it can make sense of the model breaking character or disagreeing with its persona, or whether it merely relabels our confusion about which system is speaking. For each of these, include a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give. The remaining questions can probe other points in her position, but keep them content-specific rather than biographical.

If you think any of these framings rests on a confusion — for instance, that the persona claim as I have described it cannot be made coherent, or that question (a) misstates what the intentional stance itself says about observers — tell me plainly rather than writing questions that politely presuppose my version. Please also flag any place where my description of her view seems to conflate distinct positions in the literature, since she is the sort of guest who will pounce on that.
