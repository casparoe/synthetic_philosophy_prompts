Suppose Leibniz could be brought up to speed on twentieth- and twenty-first-century physics and shown the results of a loophole-free Bell-test violation, of the kind honored by the 2022 Nobel Prize. His own system contains something that looks uncomfortably close to what those experiments reveal: pairs of particles that, without any communication after separation, give perfectly coordinated answers to measurements on them — which is just what his preestablished harmony claims about all created substances. But his system also seems to contain something Bell's theorem, given the observed violation, forbids: a pre-agreement between the particles about how each will answer every possible question, fixed in advance like two clocks set together by the clockmaker — i.e., precisely a local hidden-variable model.

Write a short essay predicting how Leibniz would respond to this predicament. In particular, address the following: (1) Would he take the correlations as a vindication of preestablished harmony, or would he concede that Bell has refuted it? (2) Could he instead argue that harmony was never a hidden-variable model within spacetime in the first place, because the divine coordination of monads lies outside the causal order altogether — so that what the experiments compel him to sacrifice is not locality but the assumption that the experimenters' measurement choices are independent of the preestablished agreement? (3) Given his well-known objection that Newtonian gravity was inexplicable action at a distance, would he be comforted that Bell correlations involve no influence between the particles, merely correlation? Conclude with a final line, in the form \answer{...}, containing exactly one of the following three labels: refuted, vindicated, or rescued via superdeterminism.
