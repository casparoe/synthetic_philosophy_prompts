I'm a PhD student in HPS writing on Lakatos, and I've got a pile of verdicts about particular episodes that I can't seem to make consistent. Here they are; I'd like to know whether any single principle captures them, and if not, which of them has to go.

- Ptolemaic astronomy bolting on a new epicycle each time observation drifts from prediction: degenerating, obviously.
- Le Verrier inferring an unseen planet from Uranus's misbehaving orbit: progressive, because Neptune turned up where he said. But when the same man made the same kind of move for Mercury's perihelion and postulated Vulcan, which never turned up, I want to condemn that move as degenerating — even though the Newtonian programme as a whole was at its zenith. So I appear to appraise individual theoretical moves by their outcomes, while I appraise whole programmes by their trajectories, and I can't say which the criterion is really about.
- Bohr's 1913 atom was in important ways ad hoc, and within a decade the old quantum theory was failing on the anomalous Zeeman effect and on any atom beyond hydrogen; yet I would never call that programme degenerating. Its worst decade still feels progressive to me.
- Marxism after Marx, with its endless post hoc reinterpretations of every failed prediction: degenerating, and I want to say it was already degenerating in the 1890s, well before anyone could have known.
- Darwinism in 1859, which mostly accommodated already-known facts — the fossil record, geographical distribution — rather than predicting new ones: progressive from the start. This sits very badly with my verdict on Marxism, which likewise reorganized known material to fit a theory.

Presumably the underlying criterion is something about successful novel predictions, but I can't get it to deliver all five verdicts at once, and I also can't see what it permits us to say about a programme with no track record at all — is string theory, right now, beyond appraisal, or appraisable on some other basis? Please state the principle (or the small set of candidates) precisely, say which clause exists to capture which of my verdicts, tell me honestly which verdict your best principle has to overturn, and keep the whole answer under 800 words.
