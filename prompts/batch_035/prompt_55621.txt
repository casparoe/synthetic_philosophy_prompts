As head of mathematics at a secondary school, I have been asked to draft our department's policy on students' use of LLM chatbots: whether to ban them, permit them with disclosure, or build them into instruction. The usual arguments on both sides feel stale, so I want to consult an unusual source: Alan Turing, who thought about education from the machine's side. Extrapolating from his 1950 proposal that we build a child machine and educate it rather than program it — through trial and error, with the teacher largely ignorant of what is going on inside it, and with the views it eventually holds unpredictable in advance — what policy would Turing recommend for my students? In particular, would he worry that offloading work to a chatbot robs the student of the very trial-and-error process he believed was how anything learns, or would he see the chatbot as a natural extension of the tools education has always used? Please distinguish clearly between what follows from Turing's documented views and what is your own extrapolation, and keep the entire answer under 400 words.
