My friend Dara and I argue about probability stuff every time we end up at the pub, and over the years she's gotten pretty consistent about when she thinks it makes sense to put a number on something and when it doesn't. Here's her track record as best I can remember it:

- a fair coin or a die: obviously there's a probability, she doesn't even hesitate
- a coin that was already flipped and is sitting under a cup: she says there's no chance anymore, it's already one way or the other, we just can't see it
- whether some far-off digit of pi is a 7: same deal, no probability, it's a fact that's already true, we just haven't worked it out
- one single uranium atom decaying within the next half-life or so: she says yes, there's a genuine chance there, it really could go either way
- the weather lady saying 70% chance of rain tomorrow: she's fine with that one, says that's a real chance too

Next week we're meeting up again and I already know a couple things will come up: (1) if physicists somehow proved the universe is completely deterministic, and you're going to flip a coin next tuesday, is there still a 50% chance of heads? (2) somewhere out in some far galaxy a quantum measurement already happened ages ago, but no record of the result was ever made and the information got scrambled beyond recovery, is there a chance about how it came out, or no?

What do you think she'll say about each of those? Which of those rules would disagree about my two new questions, and what's another case I could throw at her that would really pin down which rule she's using?
