Me and two friends have been arguing for months about what an emotion actually IS, and we've gotten basically nowhere, which is weird because we agree on almost all the facts. My position is that emotions are judgments: being afraid of the dog just is judging the dog is dangerous, but fast and with a flavor attached. My friend Marco says no, emotions are more like perceptions — they're not verdicts about the world, they're detections of things that matter to you, the way your eyes register objects without deciding anything. Fear is a detector that fires when something relevant to you shows up, and the judging comes after. My sister has a third position: emotions are first of all feelings, bodily stuff, and the judging and detecting are both add-ons. She keeps bringing up this old psychologist from the 1800s who apparently claimed the body reaction comes first and the feeling is your brain registering it — you don't cry because you're sad, you partly feel sad because you notice yourself crying. I honestly can't tell if she's describing emotions or just describing one layer of them.

Here's a case we keep circling. Last year I walked a glass skywalk, and even though I fully believed it was safe — engineered, inspected, whatever — my legs shook and I felt real dread. I throw that at my own view as a counterexample (I judged it safe and still felt terror, so the emotion can't just be the judgment), and Marco shrugs and says perceptual systems misfire too: you can know the Muller-Lyer lines are the same length and still see one as longer. Which, annoyingly, is a fair point, and now I genuinely don't know what would even settle the disagreement. Every case we come up with, each side just absorbs it.

My working theory is that the three of us aren't actually disagreeing about the same thing — I'm answering what emotions are for, Marco's answering what they're made of, my sister's answering what they're like from the inside, and each of us quietly defines "what an emotion really is" so it points at our own level. But maybe that's just me trying to feel less stuck. So what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a vote on who's right. Is this a verbal disagreement, three different questions wearing one label, or is there a real factual question underneath that could in principle be resolved? If there is one, what would count as progress — some future neuroscience finding, a distinction nobody's drawn yet, a better thought experiment? And if you think no progress should be expected here at all, say that too, and explain why.
