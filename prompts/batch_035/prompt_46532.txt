For the last couple of years I've been working out, on my own, what I think about which creatures count morally, and I've ended up with a set of beliefs that I'd like someone to audit for consistency. Here is roughly where I stand. (1) Moral standing is not all-or-nothing; it comes in degrees that track how rich a creature's inner life is. I picture a volume knob, turned up high for a dog, fairly low for a mouse, and barely off zero for a fly. (2) Even a tiny position on the knob counts for something: a fly's suffering is bad, just not very bad, and no creature with any inner life at all counts for literally nothing. (3) Small harms to many creatures can add up into something big, in the same way that a million people's headaches can outweigh one person's broken leg; that just seems like how counting works. (4) Swatting a mosquito on a hike is not even a small wrong, and fumigating a grain silo, which kills millions of insects, seems to me entirely aboveboard — yet I am a vegetarian because of what factory farming does to chickens, and I'd be sickened by someone hurting a fish for fun. (5) The single biggest moral difference in the animal kingdom is the difference between having no inner life at all and having any inner life at all, however dim; the jump from nothing to something matters more than any further jump up the scale. (6) I see no place on the knob where you could draw a sharp line between creatures you may kill casually and creatures you may not, and I'm comfortable with that, because I doubt there is a sharp line anywhere; but I also think there is a real difference in kind between anything with an inner life, however dim, and artifacts like thermostats, which have none, and I suspect my whole picture rests on that difference holding firm. What I want to know is whether these six commitments actually fit together. If some of them pull against each other, identify which ones and rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally serious; for each genuine tension, tell me what would have to give and what it would cost to give it. And please check whether any apparent conflict dissolves on closer inspection, because I genuinely cannot tell the deep problems in this picture from the surface ones.
