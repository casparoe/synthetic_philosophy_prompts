My department has two people who've been sniping at each other for a decade about how morally awful old norms actually die — dueling, the public execution as entertainment, slavery, footbinding. One is all-in on the Williams/Appiah line: these things ended when the honor they conferred got inverted, when the right people started reading them as shameful instead of status-conferring; arguments and economic interests only ever work because they get narrated in prestige terms. The other says that's mistaking the press release for the cause: dueling died when courts stopped tolerating it and armies made it career poison, slavery's death had a lot more to do with slave resistance and changing economics than with anyone's moral palate, and the honor talk is downstream rationalization. They've finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration and I've been drafted to write the joint statement, except I want to outsource the actual drafting. Give me: the claims about norm death both could sign (they do at least agree the norms died fast, that nobody was simply argued out of them, and that a pure "philosophers refuted it" story is wrong — neither is that guy); the residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; and the cruxes. I suspect one crux is what I call the lag problem — norms often outlast the material conditions that created them, which the interest side owes an explanation of and the honor side wants to run with — but there may be others, like whether there's any real case where prestige flipped while interests stayed put, or vice versa. For each crux, name the historical episode or thought experiment both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. And if part of their fight is really just over what gets to count as a cause, say which part and why. Format: numbered agreed-claims section first, then disagreements and cruxes after. No citations neccessary, they know the literature.
