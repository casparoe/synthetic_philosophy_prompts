i write backend code for a living and have zero philosophy background, but years ago a coworker left a copy of Gödel, Escher, Bach on my desk and it broke my brain in a good way. the idea I can't shake is Hofstadter's take on the word "I" -- that its not a soul or a little homunculus sitting in your head, just a symbol the brain ends up building for itself, and the weird part is that this symbol refers back to the very system that's using it. a loop, not a spirit. I want a sestina about this. pick the six end words so the form itself does the arguing: the same words cycling back in a new context every stanza, the way he says a self is a pattern recurring across levels rather than a thing parked in one spot. then in the envoi, where all six words get squeezed into three lines, make it land as the loop snapping shut -- the moment the pattern first manages to point at itself and say "I". it should read fine to my teammates (all engineers, no philosophy between us), but someone who actually knows Hofstadter should be able to tell the structure is the point and not just decoration.
