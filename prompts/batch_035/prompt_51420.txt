I'm putting together a position paper for a graduate workshop on AI governance, and before I commit it to writing I want to know whether the package of views I've drifted into actually hangs together. Here's where I've landed: (1) an aligned AI should be substantively neutral between conceptions of the good — it should function like a liberal state, providing a fair framework within which people pursue their own values rather than promoting any particular account of what makes a life good; (2) when neutrality underdetermines what the system should do — defaults, resource allocation, how it weighs stakeholders against each other — the AI should defer to a democratic aggregation of humanity's current preferences, with each person counting equally; (3) preferences that are plausibly adaptive — the residue of oppression, deprivation, or manipulation, of the sour-grapes sort where people disvalue what they've never been allowed to expect — should not enter that aggregation at full weight, since they don't reflect the person's real values; (4) moral inquiry is at least approximately truth-apt, such that past majorities were simply wrong about, say, slavery, and a perfectly aggregating system in 1750 would have entrenched atrocity — so an aligned AI should be permitted to deviate from the present aggregate in the direction of what the best moral arguments support; and (5) the AI should give substantial weight to the interests of future generations, well beyond what current people's explicit preferences about the future would license.

My seminar cohort has been poking at drafts of this and I can't tell anymore which of the objections are real and which are cheap shots. So I'd like an audit rather than a lecture: go through the five commitments, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which apparent tensions dissolve on closer inspection, and for each real tension what would have to give and what it would cost me to give it up. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally damning, and be concrete about why a given pair conflicts rather than just gesturing at "neutrality vs. paternalism" as a slogan. Keep it under 700 words.
