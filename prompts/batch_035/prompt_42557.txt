Quick background on where I am: I've been following the psi-ontic vs psi-epistemic fight (is the wavefunction a real physical thing, or a representation of somebody's information) for a couple years, and something about it bugs me. After the PBR theorem, the "easy" version of psi-epistemicism — where two different quantum states can describe the exact same physical situation — was supposedly ruled out under its assumptions, and yet the epistemic camp didn't collapse. QBism and Copenhagen-flavored views are still alive and well, and the ontic camp never really claimed victory either. So the field has been frozen for over a decade with everyone just... coexisting. My hypothesis is that somewhere along the way the two sides stopped disagreeing about the wavefunction at all and are now disagreeing about what physics is *for*: the ontic people take a theory's job to be describing what exists, the epistemic people take it to be telling an agent what to expect, and the word "real" gets stretched over both projects without anyone noticing. In other words, a dispute about the aim of science wearing the costume of a dispute about a mathematical object. But maybe that's wrong and there's a genuine substantive question still in there — for instance, whether two agents with different information can legitimately assign different pure states to the same individual system, which the psi-ontic side seems committed to denying and which strikes me as a real cost, not just a preference. Diagnose this for me, and commit to one diagnosis — don't give me a menu of possibilities and let me pick. If you go with my "different questions dressed as one" story, tell me what would turn it back into a substantive disagreement (does a collapse theory getting experimentally confirmed do it? is there any concievable experiment that bears on it at all?), and if you think it's substantive all the way down, say what the disagreement is over and which side you'd actually bet on.
