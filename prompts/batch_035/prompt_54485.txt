I do a little newsletter about everyday ethics, mostly read by people with no philosophy background, and my next post is one of those online quizzes: "what kind of commodification skeptic are you, actually?" The thing I keep running into with friends is that people who say they're against kidney sales or price gouging are often against totally different things. Some think the market corrupts the thing being sold (blood, apologies, mourning stop meaning what they meant once theres a price tag on them), some think the market is fine in principle but exploits desperate sellers, and some only object when paying for a thing crowds out the motivation to do it for free. I want the quiz to sort readers by which objection they actually hold, not by which pundit they like.

So: write me 8-10 questions, each one a concrete case with 2-3 answer options, and phrase the options so they don't telegraph which camp they feed into. Cover at least these cases: a poor person selling a kidney in a poor country; a regulated, well-monitored paid-donation kidney program like the one Iran runs; generators sold at triple the price after a hurricane; vote markets (not campaign donations, straight up buying votes); hiring strangers to weep at your funeral; a $50 service where an actor calls your ex and reads out an apology script you wrote; and paying high schoolers per book they read. Maybe throw in one case of a market that seems to make the thing more available and not obviously worse for anyone, becuase I want to catch the readers whose objection is only ever contingent on bad effects.

Then give me a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions: the semiotic/degradation camp (Sandel-ish, sometimes Radin-ish), the coercion-and-vulnerability camp (Satz's noxious markets), the motivation-crowding camp (Titmuss on blood donation), and the no-intrinsic-objection libertarians, plus whatever hybrids the answer combinations actually allow. If some pattern of answers adds up to a position nobody really defends, say so and speculate why — is it incoherent, or just unexplored? Also flag any two of my cases that are secretly testing the same thing, since I don't want redundant questions.
