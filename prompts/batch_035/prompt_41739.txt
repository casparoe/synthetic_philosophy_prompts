My best friend and I had a four-hour argument last night that began when she accused me of Goodharting my own reading life. I keep a yearly count of books finished, I have drifted toward choosing short ones, and she says the measure has quietly become the target. In the course of fighting about it she handed down verdicts on a series of other cases, and I want to know what she would say about one more.

Her verdicts, as best I can reconstruct them: (1) a call centre that rewards tickets closed per day, whose agents now close tickets without solving the problems — Goodhart, with the blame falling on whoever chose the metric, not on the agents; (2) a hospital that keeps patients waiting in ambulances outside the doors so that a waiting-time clock never starts — Goodhart, the government's fault; (3) my book count itself — Goodhart, and my fault, because I knew better and let the number do my choosing for me; and (4) a runner who trains entirely against pace because it is the only number her watch shows, overtrains, and wrecks her knees — not Goodhart: nobody is gaming anything, she is just sincerely mistaken, and calling it Goodhart cheapens the term.

The new case is my brother. He tracks his piano practice with a streak counter in an app: self-chosen, entirely private, sincerely meant as a mirror of his playing. He now runs through the easiest pieces late at night in order to keep the streak alive, while his actual playing stagnates, and he would deny — honestly, I believe — that he is gaming anything. Would she call this Goodhart? Who, if anyone, would she blame?

Please reconstruct the candidate rules that fit her first four verdicts — I can see at least two, one on which my brother is Goodharting and one on which he is not — give your best guess about her verdict with your reasons for preferring it, and tell me which further case, if I put it to her, would most cleanly separate the rules. For the sake of my own conduct, I would also like to know whether her position on my book count can survive her position on my brother, or whether one of the two has to go.
