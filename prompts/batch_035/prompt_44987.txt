I teach a graduate seminar on distributive ethics and am deciding where to steer the final research unit, which has room for perhaps three student papers and one term of my own attention. The separateness of persons objection to utilitarianism has been around for over half a century, but the literature has fractured into at least three readings that are rarely forced to engage one another: an aggregation-based reading on which the objection targets sum-ranking as such, a distributive reading on which it targets the indifference of the total view to who gets what, and a procedural reading on which it targets the absence of any fair bargain among the persons whose interests are traded off. What I want from you is a prioritized agenda rather than a survey: identify exactly five open questions in this debate that most deserve a term of work, where each item says not only what the question is but why it outranks the questions you left off — because it is upstream of the others, because it is tractable with the tools we already have, or because its answer would settle a dispute the other readings merely talk past. For context, my students have strong backgrounds in formal axiology but weaker ones in political philosophy, so tractability by those lights matters. Please reply with exactly five numbered items, each one or two sentences, with no introductory or concluding remarks.
