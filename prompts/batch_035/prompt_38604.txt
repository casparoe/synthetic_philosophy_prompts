Seems harmless enough. But then Marcus explained the proof. Suppose some truth p is such that nobody knows it. If every truth is knowable, then it's possible that someone comes to know the conjunction 'p is true and nobody knows p'. But if you know a conjunction you know both parts, so this person knows p, and also knows that nobody knows p. But they themselves know p now, so what they know is false -- and you can't know something false. Contradiction. So there can't be any truth that nobody knows, which means every truth is already known by somebody. Marcus's conclusion: the "every truth is knowable" thing doesn't just fail, it self-destructs and implies something crazy, and anyone who believed it just never thought it through.
