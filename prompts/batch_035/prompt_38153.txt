2. Prototype — 'a's are things close enough to an ideal average 'a' your head stores, no checklist, just distance from the center
3. Family resemblance — no shared essence at all, just overlapping similarities across the population of a's, Wittgenstein-style
4. Pure convention — a's are a's because we all agreed at some point, there's nothing in the shape itself doing the work
5. The thing Hofstadter's old "Letter Spirit" AI project was after — that a letter's essence is a dynamic pattern that, when you let it loose across a whole alphabet, generates an internally coherent style; a letter is what it is because of how it would propagate to its siblings, not because of anything about the single instance. Rank these five by how much they'd actually help me write that phone-call scene — by which I mean how well they'd let a listener who can't see anything sketch the letter back correctly.
