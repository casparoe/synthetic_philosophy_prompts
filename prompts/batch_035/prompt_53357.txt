I'm doing a philosophy of medicine course and we've just hit the acts/omissions material. My tutor wants my positions written down before the tutorial so she can poke holes in them, so here's roughly where I stand. Fair warning, it's a bit of a grab bag.

1. The doing/allowing distinction carries real moral weight of its own: killing is worse than letting die when intention, foresight and stakes are held fixed. It's not just a proxy for intention, or for how confident we can be about what the agent wanted.
2. Same for means versus side effect: harming someone as your means to a greater good is worse than harming them as a side effect you merely foresaw, other things equal -- and again I want this to be more than "it just feels grimmer".
3. Classic bystander at the switch: turning the trolley away from five and onto one is permissable.
4. The loop version, where the side track loops back round and it's the one person's body that stops the trolley from reaching the five: still permissible, and honestly I don't feel the "he's a mere instrument" pull there anything like I do in the surgeon case.
5. The transplant case, where the surgeon kills one passer-by to harvest organs for five: impermissible, and not close.
6. Withdrawal of treatment: turning off a ventilator at the competent patient's request is fine, and so is never having started it in the same circumstances; I don't think there's a morally significant difference between withholding and withdrawing.
7. The pair where one man drowns his cousin for the inheritance and the other plans to drown him but the cousin drowns on his own and he just stands there watching, also for the inheritance: equally bad -- I wouldn't dock the first one extra points just for gettin his hands wet.

So: which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest fix for each? Order them by how bad the tension is rather than handing me a flat list where everything's equally dodgy, and for each one tell me what giving way would cost me elsewhere in the list. If you think one of the seven is just confused, or that I've set the cases up wrong, say that straight out instead of finding a clever harmony for everything.
