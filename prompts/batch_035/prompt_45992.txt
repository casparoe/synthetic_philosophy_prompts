She's at something like 0.75 that the argument is sound; I hover around 0.45, and honestly I flip around depending on the day. I want your credence, but I don't just want a number — I want to see the machinery.

Decompose it for me into at least these terms (add others if you think they belong): (1) the probability that ultimate responsibility really does require being causa sui in the way Strawson claims — i.e., that there's no coherent middle ground between "determined by what you didn't choose" and "you somehow made yourself ex nihilo"; (2) the probability that indeterminism doesn't help at all, given that random events aren't controlled events either; (3) the probability that the compatibilist notion of responsibility is a legitimate replacement target rather than a dodge — i.e., that Strawson is attacking a notion of "true" responsibility that nobody actually needs for ethics, law, and everyday blame; and (4) the probability that beings which rewire themselves over time — say, someone who deliberately shapes their own future character through drug-free habit formation, cognitive therapy, whatever — can genuinely bootstrap even a little bit of the required self-authorship, since this is where I think the argument is most exposed: the regress has to bite fully even for agents who are partially self-modifying, and I'm not sure it does.

Give me a number and a couple lines of justification for each term, then combine them into your overall credence. And last thing — given where she sits (0.75) and where I sit (0.45), where do you think our disagreement most likely lives: in term (1), or in term (3)?
