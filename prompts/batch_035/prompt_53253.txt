Build me a thought experiment where a nudge is the option that respects people's autonomy and leaving well enough alone is the manipulative one — the reverse of the usual complaint that nudging is what disrespects autonomy. Constraints: realistic everyday setting, one paragraph, and no cafeteria food placement, organ-donor registries, pension auto-enrollment, or hotel towel signs — those are worn to death. The manipulation in the "do nothing" arm has to be structural — an inherited default, a legacy design choice, someone's old convenience that now quietly steers people — not a scheming villain. And the nudge itself cant lie to anyone, hide an option, or make anything more costly: reordering, rewording, or changing a default is fair game, nothing else. After the scenario, walk through what autonomy even means on each arm such that the nudge comes out ahead (yes, even if it leans on default-bias psychology — the interesting question is what the status quo leans on), tell me which feature of the case is doing the work, say whether deleting that feature collapses it back into the ordinary verdict that nudging is the paternalistic arm, and give the strongest reply a libertarian opponent of nudging could make. Im using this for a survey course on paternalism where the students have already seen the standard pro-nudge and anti-nudge moves, so a case that just restates "there's no neutral default" in disguise will bore them. Sections: "The case" (~150 words), "Autonomy on both arms" (~200 words), "The load-bearing feature" (~100 words), "What if you remove it" (~100 words), "The libertarian's best shot" (~100 words).
