Retired physicist here (solid state, 35 years at a national lab), got sucked into philosophy a couple of years ago and now I can't stop. Lately I've been stuck on the counterpart theory vs. transworld identity dispute and it's driving me a bit crazy, because from where I sit it looks like a debate that should have been resolvable decades ago and yet as far as I can tell people are still dug into the same trenches.

The way I understand it: on the Kripke-style picture, when we say I could have been a chemist instead, there's a world where the very same person, me, strictly identical, is a chemist -- I exist across worlds the way I exist across times. On Lewis's picture, nobody exists in more than one world; what's true is that some other guy in another world resembles me closely enough in the right respects, and he's the chemist. Famously Kripke mocked this with the poor fellow who loses the election and is supposed to be consoled by the fact that someone resembling him wins in another world -- the paraphrase in my words, not his. And there are technical ripples: counterpart theory naturally handles contingent existence and gives you varying domains, while the transworld identity approach seems to sit more comfortably with a constant-domain modal logic where everything exists necessarily, which people then have to explain away.

I've read the back and forth enough to know the stock moves. The transworld identity people say counterpart theory makes de re modality a claim about resemblance to strangers, so my modal properties aren't really mine. The counterpart people say identity across worlds is either meaningless or unconstrained, and that anyway the complaint assumes a possessive notion of modal properties that begs the question. And then everyone agrees the two systems validate almost the same formulas, which makes me wonder whether half the fight is about what a semantics is *for* rather than about worlds at all.

My working hypothesis, and I want you to take it seriously but also beat up on it: the two camps are answering different questions under one heading. One question is bookkeeping -- which formal apparatus cleanly regimentates modal inferences -- and there counterpart theory may just be an alternative notation. The other question is about what makes modal claims about *this* person true, and there the choices genuinely diverge, but the divergence is mostly inherited from the separate fight over modal realism, so the counterpart-vs-identity debate inherits its stubbornness from a debate it didn't start. If that's right, then the debate "about" de re modality is partly a proxy war and partly a verbal dispute, and the reason it never closes is that neither side flags which question they're answering. But I'm a newcomer to this literature and may be missing that somebody already settled or refuted exactly this.

So what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a survey and not a verdict on which side is right. Please structure your answer in these sections, in this order:

1. "The two positions, stated so a physicist can see the difference" -- about 150 words, no jargon beyond what's needed, you can lean on analogies to configuration space or initial conditions if it helps.
2. "Why the debate hasn't moved" -- this is the main section, roughly 400 words. Say what kind of disagreement it actually is: verbal, substantive-but-empirical, a clash of starting intuitions, a proxy for the modal realism fight, or two questions run together. Use what each side actually claims, not caricatures.
3. "Testing my hypothesis" -- around 250 words on whether my "two questions under one heading" reading survives contact with the actual arguments, and where it's too generous or too harsh to either side.
4. "What would count as progress" -- about 200 words. A distinction, a formal result, a thought experiment -- or an honest case that this one is fated to stay open, and why that isn't just defeatism.
