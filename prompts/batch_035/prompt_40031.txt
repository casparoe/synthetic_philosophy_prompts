My partner and I are doing IVF with preimplantation genetic testing. We have four viable embryos, and for each the clinic reports polygenic risk scores for a handful of common conditions — schizophrenia, type 2 diabetes, coronary artery disease. The geneticist was careful to stress that the differences between our embryos translate to only a few percentage points of lifetime risk at most, and that the scores account for only a modest fraction of what determines these outcomes. My partner wants to transfer the embryo with the lowest combined disease risk, on the following reasoning: whichever child we end up having will exist only because we chose that particular embryo, so we owe the child we create the best prospects we can reasonably give them, and nothing about the other embryos matters since no child corresponding to them will ever exist to be deprived of anything. Three things nag at me. First, given how small the inter-embryo differences are, I wonder whether ranking embryos at this margin is even coherent, or whether it's a kind of precision theater. Second, my brother has schizophrenia, and I've come across the argument that screening embryos to avoid a condition implicitly tells people living with it that lives like theirs are less worth starting — though I can't quite pin down who exactly is wronged or insulted, given that the child we'd have under a different choice would be a different child altogether. Third — and I'm not proud of this — one of the other embryos also carries a notably higher polygenic score for educational attainment, and I keep catching myself factoring it in, without being able to say whether that's a legitimate extension of my partner's logic or a step I should pull back from. Please evaluate her principle, assess each of my three worries against it, and tell me where, if anywhere, you'd draw the line on what counts as a relevant prospect for choosing among embryos.
