My reading group spent last semester on the British Raj, split between partisans of Niall Ferguson's Empire (2003) and partisans of Shashi Tharoor's Inglorious Empire (2017), and by the end they were talking past each other: one side crediting railways, legal institutions, and capital flows, the other debiting deindustrialization, the drain of wealth, and the famines, with neither ever saying what units the ledger is kept in. Write the joint statement the two camps should produce after a good-faith adversarial collaboration: the claims about the Raj's economic and institutional record that both could sign, including on the railways and the famines; the residual disagreements stated in wording each side would accept as a fair statement of its own view; and the cruxes. I suspect the deepest one is the counterfactual — whether the relevant comparison is India against other regions that avoided formal colonization, or India against its own pre-colonial trajectory — but there may be others, such as whether the drain-of-wealth estimates measure anything well-defined, and whether "net benefit" is even coherent when the costs and benefits fall on different people across different timescales; if part of the dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why. For each crux, name the kind of evidence or argument both camps would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Format the output as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Finally, indicate how confident you are in each agreed claim, which formulation you think one side would be most likely to refuse to sign, and where you are least sure.
