I serve on the charter review commission of a mid-sized city (roughly 200,000 residents) that is revising who may vote in municipal elections, a question our state legislature has devolved to municipalities to decide for themselves. The commission is deadlocked along predictable lines, and we owe the city council a written recommendation within the month. I'd like you to draft the memo the commission could adopt, and I want the philosophical work done in earnest, not the usual pieties about the sanctity of the franchise.

The relevant facts about our city: about one in eight residents is a noncitizen (a mix of legal permanent residents, people on temporary work visas, and an unknown number of undocumented residents); some 40,000 people commute in daily from neighboring towns, where their workplaces are regulated and taxed; the city hosts a university whose 25,000 students live on campus eight months a year and register to vote here or at home as they please; and the council is about to decide whether to build a waste-to-energy facility whose emissions will chiefly affect a downwind town whose residents cannot vote here at all.

The blocs on the commission map onto three principles. The first holds that the demos consists of everyone subject to the city's laws and police power: every resident should vote, regardless of citizenship, documentation, or intended length of stay, but not commuters, who are subject where they live. The second holds that the demos consists of everyone whose important interests are affected by city decisions: that sweeps in the undocumented, but also the commuters, and, if the incinerator is any guide, the downwind town — and one commissioner keeps pointing out that the principle doesn't obviously stop at the county line or even the border. The third holds that the demos is a community of fate, a group with shared long-term commitments: only those who have thrown in their lot with the city for the long haul, which excludes the transient and perhaps even recent arrivals, but honors the franchise as something deeper than an interest-tracking device.

Please draft the commission's memo: a concrete recommendation about eligibility for municipal elections, stated as a rule the election office could actually administer; the principle that drives it, applied honestly to our four hard cases (undocumented residents, commuters, students, the downwind town); a serious treatment of the strongest argument for the principle you reject, including the objection that whichever way we go we will be drawing a line that our own principle cannot justify; and a short concluding section on whether the city should adopt any supplementary mechanism — an affected-parties hearing, a cross-municipal compact, a proxy for future residents — to handle those our voting rule must leave out.
