I spent 34 years as a coleopterist at a natural history museum, describing carabid beetles and arguing with my colleagues about whether half the things I described were good species or just slightly funny populations. Now I'm retired, and I've finally had time to read the philosophy I always skipped past, and I've fallen down the species problem rabbit hole. Mayr's biological species concept, the various phylogenetic ones, Templeton's cohesion idea, Kitcher and Dupré saying we should just be pluralists, de Queiroz claiming that everyone secretly agrees species are separately evolving lineages and the real fight is only about which criterion to use for delimiting them. It's been going for decades, the same moves keep getting made, and as far as I can tell nobody has changed sides since the 90s.

Here's what I want, and it's not a survey — I've read the surveys. I want a diagnosis of why this thing never resolves. From my chair, the contenders are: (a) it's a verbal dispute about the word "species" and the world contains only populations and lineages, not a natural kind to be carved; (b) it's substantive, and one concept is right but the evidence isn't in yet, or people are clinging to their training; (c) the different sides are answering different questions — what taxa are (ontology) versus how we should divide them up (practice) versus what institutions like museums and conservation law need (which is what actually drove most of my decisions, if I'm honest); or (d) something I haven't listed.

I'll tell you my own suspicion, take it or leave it: after decades of keying out beetles, I think the debate is partly a proxy war over research resources and partly a genuine metaphysical fight, and the two get deliberately confused because "my species concept is correct" sounds better in a grant proposal than "my group deserves the funding." A curator needs species to be listable objects, a geneticist needs them to be populations, a wildlife agency needs them to be units you can sue over. Meanwhile the asexuals and the microbes — most of life, in other words — sit there refusing to fit anyone's concept, and both sides mostly talk around them.

Don't give me the pluralist shrug, that's what everyone defaults to and it explains nothing about why the argument continues. Commit to one diagnosis, defend it, and tell me what would count as actual progress given your diagnosis — would universal barcoding end it, relocate it, or change nothing? And would you say the stalling is a sign the question is ill-posed, or just hard?
