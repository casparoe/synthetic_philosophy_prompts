Construct a thought experiment in which a single dataset exhibiting Simpson's reversal supports opposite — and equally correct — decisions for two rational agents. One agent faces a causal question: what would happen to a unit if it received the treatment, for which the disaggregated comparison is the right guide, since the stratifying variable is a confounder that influences both who receives the treatment and the outcome. The other agent faces a purely predictive question about a future population resembling the observed one — what overall success rate to expect — for which the aggregate is the right guide, because what is needed there is a forecast, not an effect estimate. Constraints: a realistic setting such as a workplace, a school, or a clinic; the scenario itself in one paragraph, with concrete numbers small enough that the reversal can be verified by hand; and both agents must be deciding about the same treatment and the same outcome, so that the divergence is stark rather than an artifact of different subject matters. After the scenario: (1) verify the reversal arithmetically; (2) walk through each agent's decision and show why each statistic is the right one for that agent's question; (3) identify which structural feature of the case is doing the work — the direction of influence into the stratifying variable, the gap between the causal and predictive questions, or something else — and say what happens to each decision if that feature is removed, in particular what changes once the second agent's question becomes causal; and (4) note how someone who holds that there is a single, context-independent right way to read such a table would attack the case. The two verdicts must be generated by the causal and decision structure, not stipulated by the description.
