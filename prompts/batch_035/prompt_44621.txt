I'm a novelist, currently finishing a book that retells a single folktale through four generations of tellers, and the experience has pushed me toward an evolutionary picture of my trade — roughly the idea that stories persist by a kind of Darwinian selection, spreading through retelling the way traits spread through reproduction. An editor has asked me to write this up as an essay, and before I commit anything to print I want a coherence check on the position I actually hold, because I suspect it doesn't fully hang together. Here are my commitments:

(1) Stories survive by selection: variants get retold or forgotten, and what persists is whatever fits the environment of readers and retellers — transmissibility, not truth or merit, is what's being selected for.
(2) A work's endurance across centuries is nonetheless our best evidence of its literary value.
(3) Every retelling, translation, and adaptation mutates the work, so there is no privileged original — a story is a lineage, not a fixed object.
(4) When I write, I originate rather than recombine: characters and plots come from me, and my intentions fix what the finished book means.
(5) Craft is real and teachable: I deliberately engineer for memorability — compressible structure, quotable lines — and this design work is why good books outcompete forgettable ones.
(6) Revision works in a way biological inheritance doesn't: I acquire improvements in the course of drafting, and the published version inherits them directly.

I don't know where the real problems are, and I'd rather not be told that everything is vaguely in tension with everything else — I want you to discriminate between the conflicts that bite and the ones that dissolve on inspection. Please return the audit as a JSON object with two fields. The first, 'tensions', is an array of objects, each with: 'commitments' (the numbers involved), 'status' (one of 'genuine', 'apparent', or 'borderline'), 'severity' (1–5, where 5 means the two cannot both stand), 'diagnosis' (two to four sentences either tracing the conflict or showing why it dissolves), and 'cheapest_fix' (the minimal revision, plus what holding it would cost me in the essay). The second field, 'single_revision', names the one change that would clean up the most, with a one-sentence rationale. Order the 'tensions' array by severity, descending.
