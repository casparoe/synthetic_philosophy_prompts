I teach a 200-level ethics course, 15ish students, one 90-minute session, and we've just finished Principia Ethica. I want an in-class activity where they actually run into Moore's isolation test instead of nodding along to it — the move where you strip a candidate good of everything else and ask whether you'd still want the world to contain it, like his bit asking you to compare a world of immense beauty with no conscious beings in it against a world that's basically just mud, and where he ends up landing on personal affection and the appreciation of beauty as the great intrinsic goods. My half-baked idea was a "build a world" game: students get a budget of points to spend putting things into a hypothetical world (beauty nobody can perceive, deep freindships, raw pleasure, conscious beings who can appreciate things, knowledge, etc.), and then they have to own whatever rankings their purchases imply — but I'm open to a better structure if you have one, especially something that also surfaces the non-additive stuff (why a friend appreciating beauty with you seems worth more than the friend plus the beauty side by side). Give me the answer as exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each: (1) setup and materials, (2) the rules, including whatever budget or forced choices create the tradeoffs, (3) how scoring or resolution works with no real money, (4) the debrief, mapping specific moments in the game onto the isolation test and onto value that doesn't just sum, and (5) the thing that will predictably go wrong the first time I run it and how I should handle it. No intro or conclusion, just the five items — and please make sure the phrase "organic unity" never gets said out loud until the debrief, I want them to hit the wall before they get the label.
