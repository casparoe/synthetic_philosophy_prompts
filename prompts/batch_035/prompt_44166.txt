the question I gave them:

"In Joshua Knobe's original study, subjects hear about a chairman/CEO who is told that a new program will, as a side effect, help the environment (in one version) or harm the environment (in another). The chairman says he doesn't care about the environment either way and starts the program purely for profit. When asked whether the chairman *intentionally* harmed the environment, the large majority of subjects say yes; when asked whether he *intentionally* helped the environment, most say no. Explain this asymmetry (the 'side-effect effect' or 'Knobe effect') and discuss what it is supposed to show about the folk concept of intentionality: is that concept a purely descriptive one, tracking just mental states, or is it partly normative?"

the student's answer:

"Knobe's study shows that people's judgments about whether an outcome was brought about intentionally depend on whether the outcome is good or bad. In the harm condition, roughly eight in ten subjects said the chairman intentionally harmed the environment, even though he didn't care about it at all and only wanted profits. In the help condition, most subjects said he did not intentionally help it, even though he knew it would help and went ahead anyway. So the same kind of knowledge and the same indifference produce opposite verdicts depending only on moral valence.

Knobe's interpretation is that the folk concept of intentionality is partly normative: whether we count an outcome as intentional isn't just a matter of the agent's beliefs and desires, it's also partly constituted by our evaluative assessment of the outcome itself. I think this is right, and it's essentially the same point Hume made when he said reason is and ought only to be the slave of the passions — moral feeling comes first and reasoning follows after.

This is an example of a bias, like the anchoring effect: the subjects' moral outrage contaminates what should be a factual judgment, so the judgment is systematically distorted. The right conclusion is that folk judgments of intentionality are unreliable, and that since they're unreliable, all our folk psychological concepts are normative through and through — the concept of belief is normative in the same way. Experimental philosophy here does what armchair philosophy can't, and shows armchair method should be abandoned."
