Here's the view you'll be attacking, so we're on the same page. An extortionist profits only when their demand is smaller than the threatened harm and they expect the target to pay. Agents precommitted to ignore threats do great against extortion, but the standard complaint is that they also blow up genuinely beneficial deals, since from the outside a demand backed by a threat can look like an ordinary offer, and a policy of never yielding can't tell which is which. The surrogate-goal fix: ex ante, adopt some trivial extra "decoy" goal and hard-wire a commitment to treat any extortionary demand as if it also destroyed the decoy -- concretely, to respond to being threatened with an uncompensated self-inflicted loss of roughly the size of the demand. Then no matter how small the extortionist's ask, making the threat costs them at least as much as they stood to gain, so extortion against you never pays, while real trades, where both sides gain more than the decoy loss, still go through fine.
