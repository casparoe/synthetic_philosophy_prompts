A few months ago I was diagnosed with a progressive chronic illness, and since then a remarkable number of people have offered me some version of Epictetus's opening line: that some things are up to us and some are not, and that wisdom consists in attending to the first kind. My therapist, whose CBT practice apparently owes a lot to Stoicism; a friend deep in the modern Stoic revival, who keeps a daily journal organized around it; and a meditation app that repeats it in nearly every session. On the face of it the division seems clear: the course of the disease is not up to me, while my judgments and how I take the news are. But when I press people on what "up to us" actually means, I get strikingly different answers, and I've begun to suspect the slogan is several claims wearing one sentence. Some of them look trivially true, some substantive, and some, on my worse days, look false.

I'd like you to lay out, as a list I can actually use, the distinct precise claims the dichotomy of control might be asserting, and for each say whether it is trivial, substantive and defensible, substantive but dubious, or straightforwardly false. A few things I specifically want covered. First, the reading on which "up to us" just means "depends on our decisions," which seems compatible even with hard determinism — is that the reading Epictetus himself held, and is this settled or contested among scholars of ancient Stoicism? Second, the problem I keep running into: my judgments do not feel especially up to me at present; fatigue, mood, and medication all color what I assent to. Does the reading on which our judgments and attitudes are up to us rest on a contested account of assent or will, and are there defensible weakenings of it — that judgments are up to us only partially, only in the long run, or only with training? Third, whether the thing is really a dichotomy at all: is anything fully up to us, or is everything a matter of degree, and did the ancient Stoics themselves acknowledge graded or partial control? Alongside this, please flag which reading the self-help users around me are most likely relying on, because in its worst uses the line functions as a polite way of saying "stop complaining about your situation" — and I'd like to know which of the readings would actually license that use and which would block it.

Finally, as you go through the list, please keep clear the line between what is settled among people who know the topic well and what is genuinely contested, and do not present a contested view as the consensus; where something is open, say so rather than resolving it by fiat.
