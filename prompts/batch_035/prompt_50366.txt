I am writing the final section of a graduate seminar paper on whether Everettians can legitimately get the Born rule, and before I evaluate the existing proposals I want the evaluation standard settled. Please draft the desiderata: what any adequate account of probability in a many-worlds setting has to deliver.

The setting, so you can calibrate: I am assuming Everettian quantum mechanics with no hidden variables and no collapse, so every outcome of a measurement occurs on some branch, and the question is why a rational agent about to perform a quantum experiment should assign probabilities matching the Born weights (or should be said to assign anything at all, given that she knows with certainty that she will have a successor who sees each outcome).

The requirements should cover, at least: what the account must say probability is, when ordinary talk of chances is recovered rather than reinterpreted beyond recognition; how it handles the fact that every outcome actually happens, so that ordinary justifications of probability assignments in terms of limiting frequency, or of rational credence in terms of getting things right, are not obviously available; the problem that branch counting is either ill-defined or arbitrary, given that branching is continuous rather than a tidy tree of discrete alternatives; the constraint that the account work at a single instant of preparation, not just as a long-run claim; what it owes us regarding the envariance-style or self-locating-uncertainty moves, where probability is tied to ignorance about which future successor one will be, and the decision-theoretic moves, where probability is tied to what it is rational to prefer among bets that all get paid out on some branch; and consistency with the empirical fact that observers who systematically recorded statistics in conflict with the Born weights would exist in principle, just with tiny total measure.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and say whether you take it to be non-negotiable for any Everettian or a substantive further commitment that one could reasonably waive. Then flag any pair of requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and say which single requirement does the most to eliminate candidate accounts. You may assume familiarity with the main families of proposals (decision-theoretic derivations, self-locating uncertainty, branch-counting and its discontents) but do not score them against the list at length -- the deliverable is the specification, not the scorecard. Please also say one thing the list is missing or gets wrong, in your judgment, since I expect the seminar to push back on it.
