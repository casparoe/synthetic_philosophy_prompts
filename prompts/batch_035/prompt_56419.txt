quick one — i run a small reading group blog and want to make a "which theory of exploitation do you actually hold?" quiz for a post. Not a history of the Marx debate, a sorting quiz, the kind where you can't tell from the question which position it's driving you toward. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, and then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions.

Cases I want in there somewhere: (1) a sweatshop in a poor country paying the local going rate, where every worker is better off than without the job and the owner banks a fat margin; (2) a worker co-op where the members vote to pay themselves the profits and hire temps at low wages to get through a busy season; (3) Roemer's island-type setup — one person owns all the machinery, everyone else works for them at wages well above subsistence, everybody's better off than they'd be fishing on their own, and the owner gets rich off the difference; (4) a monopoly pharmacy jacking up insulin prices; and (5) at least one case built to test whether someone thinks an exchange can be exploitative when both parties benefit and would both happily repeat it.

Also include at least one question that probes whether the person thinks the core wrong is located in the exchange itself (wages, terms, who gets the surplus) or in what work is like day to day (control, monotony, not seeing yourself in the product) — I want the scoring key to be able to say "your answers about 'exploitation' are really tracking the alienation complaint, which is a different (if related) thing Marx complained about."

The key should cover at minimum: the orthodox surplus-labor view where the wrongness is in the extraction itself and survives even nice bosses and high wages; the Roemer-style view that exploitation is really about unfair distribution of productive assets, such that if you equalized assets the profit would vanish and the exploitation with it; domination-style views where the wrong is leveraging someone's vulnerability or dependence rather than any percentage of surplus; luck-egalitarian readings; and the free-market reply that all of these are mutually beneficial trades with no complaint to lodge. If you think there's a distinct position i've left out that occupies a real cell of the logical space, add it.

Two things the key must do: flag any pair of positions that give identical answers to every question you wrote, and say what extra case would pull them apart; and flag any answer combination that no actual position occupies, with a sentence on whether nobody's there because it's incoherent, or just because it hasn't occurred to anyone. Keep the cases concrete and please don't include a question like "do you accept the labor theory of value" — the whole point is sorting people by what they say about cases, not by which team they root for.
