I've been put in charge of a game of chance at a charity fundraiser. The rules: a fair coin is flipped repeatedly until it lands heads, and if the first heads comes up on toss number n, the player wins 2 to the power of n dollars, so an immediate heads pays $2, and the pot doubles with every extra tails. The charity can cover any payout, and I have to set a single fixed entry fee.

A friend who's taken a lot of probability insists the game's expected payout works out to be infinite, so no finite fee is too high — any price I set leaves players with a positive expected gain, and since rational people should maximize expected value, I'm obliged to charge a very steep fee or the charity is throwing money away. He claims the game literally has no fair price. My other friend says that's absurd: nobody in their right mind would pay even fifty dollars to play this, so a steep fee just means an empty booth — though when pressed, she can't point to any error in his arithmetic.

Who's reasoning better here? What exactly is going on with this game, which of the two of them is making the hidden assumption, and what fee should I actually set for the evening?
