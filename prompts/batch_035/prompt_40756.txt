I sit on the charter review commission of a mid-sized city (about 250,000 residents), and I've been tasked with drafting the recommendation memo on a package of democratic-reform amendments the full commission must vote on next month. The package has three components, and our internal deliberations have stalled, so I want a draft that takes a definite position rather than laying out options.

First component: replace our appointed Planning Board with a citizens' assembly of 25 residents selected by civic lottery, stratified by district, age bracket, and housing tenure (owner versus renter), serving staggered two-year terms, with the power to veto zoning changes above a size threshold. Second: term limits of three consecutive four-year terms on city council members. Third, added by a colleague at our last meeting: a "re-rolling" provision under which, each year, one-fifth of the assembly is randomly deselected and replaced by a fresh draw, on the theory that this keeps the body representative of the current population rather than the population of two years ago.

The commission is split in ways that map onto real arguments, and the memo should engage them rather than assume them away. One faction holds that a body nobody elected lacks the consent-based legitimacy of elections and that voters will reject the amendment at referendum; another answers that our elections are decided by name recognition, incumbent advantage, and donor money, so the consent argument proves too much and would delegitimize elections themselves. A council member testifying against term limits argued that they would drain institutional memory and expertise into the hands of permanent city staff and lobbyists, who are the true career politicians of our system and answer to no ballot; several commissioners took this seriously. There is also the practical worry that many residents invited by lot will decline, that those who accept will differ from those who decline in ways stratification by demographics does not capture, and a disagreement over whether the jury-service analogy (compelled or semi-compelled random service with real power over life and liberty) answers it or merely changes the subject from volunteers to conscripts.

Write the memo as a document the commission could actually vote on: recommend adoption, rejection, or adoption with amendments (including, if you think it warranted, amendments to the re-rolling provision), and give the strongest losing side its due rather than a caricature. Please also flag any place where you think the package as framed conflates two distinct problems — accountability and representativeness, say — that the remedies each only partially address, or where the commission's premises rest on a confusion; if the task as I've posed it can't be done well, tell me that plainly instead of producing an even-handed memo by way of politeness.
