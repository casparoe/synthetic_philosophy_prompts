I'd be grateful for help with an argument from my reading group on biological explanation. It purports to establish that there are no reasons anywhere, and although each step looks defensible on its own, the conclusion appears to prove far too much. The argument runs as follows.

1. To give a reason for something is to cite a consideration that counts in its favor.
2. A consideration counts in favor of something only relative to a standard; nothing counts in favor of anything simpliciter.
3. A standard exists only where some mind has adopted it, or can at least interpret it.
4. In the evolutionary case, the standards by which traits are assessed — better at circulating blood, better at reaching light — were adopted by no one, and the process that produced the traits cannot interpret anything.
5. So when we say the heart is there in order to pump blood, the appearance of a reason is a mere appearance; strictly speaking, no one has ever counted circulation in the heart's favor.
6. But our own minds, and the standards they apply, are products of the same kind of unadopted process; nothing adopted the standards in virtue of which our standards count as standards.
7. Therefore there are no reasons at all — not merely no reasons in biology. And if we accept (5) while refusing (7), we owe a principled difference between the two cases, and every candidate we have tried — interpretation, self-consciousness, responsiveness to reasons as such — is itself a standard that, by (3), no one adopted.

Our defender maintains that once (3) is granted the rest follows, and that (3) merely states what it is to be a standard. My own instinct is that the fault lies between (3) and (4): that a standard can be in place without anyone behind it, so that (5) is false. But I can see this route has a price. If the heart's order-to-pump is a genuine reason without a reasoner, what excludes the same courtesy toward the thought that the rain falls in order to fill the reservoirs? And if the answer is that only things shaped by selection get reasons, I want to know why being selected for is not simply another causal fact, retold with flattery.

Please tell me which step to reject, why that step looked innocent, and what each available rejection costs — including, if you take the route I favor, an account of what marks off genuine reasons without reasoners from these impostors.
