I'm a technical animator who spends most of my working life inside 3D animation software, and my training in metaphysics extends no further than a couple of popular books. Restate the endurantism-versus-perdurantism debate about how material objects persist through time in the vocabulary I actually use: scenes, timelines, keyframes and interpolation, the scene graph, rigs and deformations, meshes, instancing, and the fact that everything the computer ultimately renders is a discrete frame.

Translate each side as faithfully as you can, analysing them one at a time. My impression is that perdurantism maps naturally onto my world: the persisting object is the whole animation laid out along the timeline, an object at a moment is a single rendered frame or a time-slice of an animation curve, and change is just the difference between slices — an object having incompatible properties at different times becomes the utterly ordinary fact that frame 12 and frame 48 disagree. Endurantism, meanwhile, looks like the way my software represents things: one mesh sitting in the scene graph that persists across the whole timeline and is deformed by a rig — numerically the same object at every keyframe, with different properties at different times, never a mere collection of frame-snapshots.

Then give me the residue report, which is the part I care about most. For each side, say what translates cleanly, what had to be distorted to fit, and what cannot be said in my vocabulary at all. In particular, I want your honest verdict on two suspicions of mine. First: my software stores a single mesh and animates its parameters rather than storing every frame independently, but this may be merely a matter of compression — an encoding convenience — rather than a fact about which kind of persistence is fundamental; tell me whether the endurantist's claim survives that suspicion, or whether the analogy quietly trades on a feature of file formats rather than of the world. Second: in my world, whether two frames belong to the same character is partly settled by convention — splices, dissolves, re-used assets, a rig swapped between shots — and I suspect that the endurantist's insistence on strict numerical identity across time is exactly the thing for which my vocabulary has no counterpart, except perhaps as a convention we impose when deciding what counts as the same character across a cut. Say whether the residue points to something the metaphysical debate is missing, or to something the 3D-animation framework cannot express.

Where you lean on a contested reading of either position — for example, on whether perdurantists are committed to temporal parts being as concrete as spatial parts, or on whether endurantists may help themselves to sortals when saying which earlier thing is the same as which later thing — flag it rather than presenting it as settled. Throughout, state how confident you are in each of your principal claims, and at the end single out the point in your answer you are least sure of. Please write the answer in American English.
