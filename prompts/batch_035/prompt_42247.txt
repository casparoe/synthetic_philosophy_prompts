My favourite high school teacher is retiring after 38 years of teaching intro logic, and I'm giving the toast at his party. His signature move, every single year: on the first day he'd announce there'd be exactly one surprise exam before the end of term, then let the class spend a week talking themselves into the conclusion that no such exam is possible (can't be on the last day since they'd know by then, so it unravels backwards, you know the drill) — and then he'd give the exam anyway, and it somehow still landed as a surprise even on the kids who had "proved" it couldn't happen. Write me a five-minute toast that uses this ritual as its spine — what doing that to teenagers for nearly four decades says about him as a teacher, maybe what it says about wanting your students to outsmart you — without turning into a lecture on epistemic paradoxes. Half the room are former students who'll catch every nuance and are basically showing up hoping the toast is good logic; the other half is family who've never heard of the thing, and it needs to land for them too. One more thing: he's told me he has one last surprise planned for the party itself and refuses to say anything more about it, so I'd like the ending to nod to that in a way that mirrors the paradox itself — the guests should walk out not quite able to decide whether his surprises were announced all along or whether knowing about them somehow never counted as knowing.
