quick background on me: I do probabilistic forecasting for a living — tournaments, scoring rules, calibration plots, the whole thing. I've gotten into the scientific realism debate secondhand and my honest reaction is that a lot of it is just fights about reference classes and base rates, but I want that checked rather than assumed.

As I understand it: realists say the predictive success of science would be a miracle if successful theories weren't at least roughly true (the no-miracles argument). Anti-realists answer with the pessimistic meta-induction: most past theories that enjoyed real success turned out wrong — phlogiston, caloric, the ether — so the historical base rate for "successful theory stays" is terrible, and by our own track record we should expect today's theories to mostly go the same way. Then realists counter that the failures are drawn from immature or premature science, so the anti-realists are reading the base rate off the wrong reference class. And there's some argument floating around that the meta-induction is self-undermining, because its premises only come out true if the past theories were getting something roughly right about their own evidence — that one I'd especially like to see translated, it smells like a selection-effect problem to me.

So here's the ask. Restate the whole debate — no-miracles, the meta-induction, the fight over which past theories count as the relevant track record, that self-undermining wrinkle — in the vocabulary I actually use: reference classes, base rates, in-sample vs out-of-sample performance, updating on a track record, selection effects in how you carve up the past. Do it as faithfully as you can; don't let my framework's assumptions do the arguing. Then give me the residue report: what translates cleanly, what you had to distort to make it fit, and whether anything in the philosophers' debate has no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. My guess is "approximately true" will be the sticking point, since in my world you don't get credit for being close to right — you get credit for being calibrated, and those come apart. But tell me if the residue points at a defect in my vocabulary or at vagueness in the realists' original claim.

And be straight with me about where this translation is most likely to go wrong — what would make you reconsider the whole diagnosis, not just tweak it.
