So my little philosophy blog does these occasional "which view do you secretly hold?" quiz posts, and the next one I want is on the wrong kind of reasons problem. Can you write it for me? Around nine or ten questions, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key at the end mapping answer patterns onto positions.

The positions I want as leaves (these or close relatives, your call):

- the fitting-attitude defender who bites the bullet and says wrong-kind reasons really are reasons for the attitude, full stop
- evidentialist-style views where a reason for an attitude has to bear the right kind of connection to what the attitude is about (like evidence for belief), so wrong-kind reasons only ever give you reasons for adjacent actions
- modification views: wrong-kind reasons are really reasons to bring it about that you have the attitude, or to avow it, or something in that neighborhood
- hybrid/disjunctive accounts where both kinds of reason are genuine but fundamentally different in kind
- the revisionist who thinks the wrong-kind cases just show the whole fitting-attitude analysis of value was a mistake

Rules for the questions: no question can just ask "is that really a reason for the attitude" and none can use any of these labels. Sort people by cases, not by what they call themselves. The cases need to include at least: one about belief (like getting paid a fortune to believe something), one about desire where the desiring is instrumentally useful but the object is worthless, one about admiration under threat (evil demon type), one about amusement, and at least one case where the attitude is something you can't just take up at will (belief or conviction again is fine) — I have a suspicion that case does alot of work separating the views that lean on voluntary control from the ones that don't. Also at least one question should involve an attitude directed at a person's action or at another attitude (resentment, guilt, remorse — your pick), since I want a case where the "object" of the attitude isn't a plain fact.

In the scoring key, please flag any two leaves the quiz genuinely can't separate — be honest about it instead of inventing a question that pretends to do the job. And note which answer combinations nobody coherent should land in, with a sentence on why (incoherent, unattractive, or just nobody's actually defended it). Keep the questions themselves free of jargon, save the labels for the key. Don't worry about citations or naming who holds what, the readers won't care and I can fill that in.
