I write novels for a living, which means I spend all day doing something weird: inventing people and deciding, sentence by sentence, whether they hurt. When I write "she flinched at the sound of his car in the drive," I have not decided whether she feels anything, but my readers and I all act as though she does, and the whole enterprise collapses if we don't. So you'd think the philosophical zombie debate would be home turf for me. It isn't, and the fact that it isn't is sort of my question.

Here's the debate as I understand it, and stop me if I get it wrong: the zombie people say I can coherently conceive of a being physically identical to me, down to every synapse, with no inner life at all -- no what-it's-like -- and that this conceivability is evidence, or maybe proof, that consciousness isn't physical. The other side says either you're not really conceiving that (you're conceiving a creature with all the functional facts and quietly forgetting that those are supposed to fix the physical ones), or conceivability just doesn't track possibility the way the argument needs. This has been going since the nineties, everyone knows everyone's moves, and as far as I can tell nobody has switched teams in decades.

So here's my novelist's hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: maybe the two camps mean different things by "conceive," and neither has noticed because philosophers treat conceiving as a single mental act while my trade treats it as several. When I imagine a character, there's the imagery, there's the description of her, and there's the fact I know she doesn't exist -- and I can slide between these without noticing I'm doing it. Maybe the zombie arguer is doing that too. Or maybe that's me projecting my craft onto their problem and the disagreement is actually bedrock intuition about whether there's anything left over once the physics is fixed.

Diagnose the stalled debate for me: verbal, substantive, two questions under one name, or clashing starting intuitions that no argument can touch? And tell me what, if anything, would count as progress -- a sharper distinction about what conceiving is, some finding from the science of imagination, a better thought experiment, or nothing, and why. One more thing: write it as continuous prose, no headings, no bullets, no bold -- just the way you'd explain it to me over coffee, because I genuinely want to follow the reasoning and formatting makes me skim.
