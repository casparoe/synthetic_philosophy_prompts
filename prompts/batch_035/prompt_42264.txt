Working out my utilitarianism for a discussion group and want a sanity check before I present. Here's the package:

(1) Straight hedonistic total utilitarianism: pleasure minus pain, summed over everyone, maximize. No qualitative ranking of pleasures — the difference between pushpin and poetry is just quantity/intensity, I'm with Bentham against Mill on that.
(2) Interpersonal comparisons of intensity are fine in principle — there's a fact of the matter about whose experience is more intense — even though in practice we can only measure roughly.
(3) I don't believe there's any principled cap on how intense an experience can be. Some beings just get a lot more out of the same event than others (differences in nervous systems and so on), and I see no reason intensity should have a ceiling.
(4) The utility monster thing has never really worried me: marginal pleasure diminishes for every actual psyche, everyone satiates eventually, so a monster can't exist and the objection never gets off the ground.
(5) Thought experiments have to be answered on principle, not dodged by pointing out they won't actually happen — that's why I just accept the repugnant conclusion instead of fudging it.

Do these hang together? I honestly can't tell wether some of them pull against each other or whether they're all fine. If there are real tensions, rank them by severity, say what each would cost to fix, and which single revision cleans up the most. I'd rather find out now than during Q&A.
