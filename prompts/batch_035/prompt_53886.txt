I've gotten pretty deep into the whole timing-of-consciousness debate and I've basically signed on to what people call the Stalinesque side: consciousness is not a live feed, it lags behind, the brain waits for the end of a processing interval and then presents a finished "now" — like a studio that delays the broadcast a couple hundred milliseconds so it can edit the feed before it airs. The Orwellian alternative (experience happens in real time and then the memory gets quietly rewritten afterward so the subject misremembers) always struck me as desperate — it preserves a theater of the real by hiding it somewhere nobody can check. This is roughly Dennett's line from the color phi case: two stationary flashes in different colors are seen as one dot moving and changing color, and at the moment of the first flash the brain couldn't yet know the second flash's color, so the experienced motion-and-color-change must have been constructed after the fact and delivered as if it happened. I've even come up with my own variant I'd like stress-tested, since I'm wary of just reciting arguments I've read: run phi trials but randomly abort the second flash partway through, and immediately probe the subject about what they experienced. If subjects on aborted trials still report the moving color-changing dot, that looks like real-time fabrication; if their reports differ systematically from completed trials, that seems like it should tell us something about when the content is actually fixed. But honestly tell me if this can work at all, since I suspect the delay in probing and reporting just swamps the few-hundred-millisecond window we're fighting over — and if it can't work, whether any behavioral or physiological measure could in principle get under that window.

What I actually want from you: what would genuinely count against my Stalinesque position? What experimental results, what everyday phenomena (the stopped-clock illusion, flash-lag, Libet-style readiness potential stuff, anything else), what cases where the view has to say something wierd? I know Libet's own story about the brain delaying and then ante-dating experience is basically my position in 1980s electrophysiology, so whatever trouble he's in, I'm probably in too — tell me how much and why. Rank the defeaters for me: which ones should actually make me give the view up, which are merely awkward, and which ones committed people on my side would just shrug off (and be honest about whether "shrugging off" is warranted or whether it's motivated reasoning). Also — and this matters — Dennett's move of saying there's no fact of the matter about "when you really experienced the red" sometimes feels like it makes the position impossible to lose, that any counterexample can be redescribed as memory contamination rather than real-time experience. Is the view I'm holding falsifiable in any meaningful sense, and if it's been qualified into unfalsifiability, is that a problem for a claim of this kind or is it just what you'd expect for a claim about consciousness?

One more thing: please keep straight what's settled versus what's genuinely contested among people who actually know this stuff. That the color phi phenomenon and things like the cutaneous rabbit occur seems settled as phenomenology; what any of it shows about the timing of experience is very much not, and I don't want a contested interpretation handed to me as the consensus. I'm fairly committed to this view and I'd like to stay that way only if it deserves it.
