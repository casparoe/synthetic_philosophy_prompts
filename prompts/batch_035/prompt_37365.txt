I'm a software engineer with no formal background in philosophy, and lately I've been reading about panpsychism: the view that consciousness, or at least some primitive form of experience, goes all the way down to fundamental physics. I want you to write a satirical piece on its ethical implications, in the form of a changelog — release notes for reality, issued by its developers in response to mounting user complaints about the possible suffering of elementary particles. About 600 words, formatted as a numbered list of items with version numbers, severity tags, and statuses (Fixed / Known issue / Won't fix / Deferred).

The satire should be fair rather than dismissive: the jokes must come from getting the real philosophical issues right, so that someone who works on this would nod rather than wince. In particular, the notes should distinguish experience from suffering — experience is confirmed in current builds, but nothing in the telemetry exposes negative valence; treat the question of how a huge number of vanishingly small experiences could combine into anything of moral weight as a long-standing unresolved bug; concede that any single particle's moral status is negligible while noting that the user base is on the order of 10^80 accounts; and include at least one developer response that is a genuine live position in the literature — for instance, that moral status comes in degrees and that, at this scale, it rounds down — rather than a strawman. Close with a boilerplate disclaimer that quietly raises what I gather is the deepest worry in this debate: if every interaction in the universe carries some moral status, does that make everything an emergency, or nothing one?
