I'm a distributed-systems and database engineer by training and temperament: ACID and its violations, isolation levels, constraint checking, NaN semantics in floating-point comparisons, circuit breakers, fail-fast versus fail-soft error handling — this is the vocabulary I reason in fluently. Philosophers keep telling me dialetheism is the view that some contradictions are true, and that paraconsistent logics exist so that admitting one contradiction doesn't entail admitting everything; classical logic, I gather, licenses a single inconsistency to infect the whole system. That last part I understand immediately, because I have debugged exactly that. So: restate dialetheism and the paraconsistency program in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report I actually care about. I can already guess some of the mappings — explosion as unbounded propagation of a corrupted row through a cascade of joins, a paraconsistent consequence relation as fail-soft fault isolation, a truth-value glut as NaN, which compares false against everything, poisons every expression it touches, and yet coexists with the rest of the arithmetic — but I don't trust my guesses to be the faithful ones rather than the flattering ones, so say where my analogies distort the original. Cover at least the parts of the program that have natural homes in my world: the Liar as a sentence whose evaluation triggers the very condition it reports, like a transaction whose rollback writes to the log entry it is reading; the Russell set as a self-referential integrity constraint that no schema can satisfy or violate cleanly; the non-explosive consequence relations people actually build (Priest's three-valued Logic of Paradox, the relevant logicians' variable-sharing requirement, da Costa's systems tolerating but not verifying contradictions) as different consistency-preservation policies, each a different answer to the question of how much of the system may be trusted once one constraint is known broken. Then the residue, in three parts. First: my systems quarantine, roll back, or partition around inconsistency; they never knowingly keep and act on a constraint-violating state. The dialetheist's claim, as far as I can tell, is not that inconsistent states should be contained but that some are correct — that the violation is in my model of the world, not in the world. Is there any counterpart of that distinction between a representation's bug and a reality's genuine conflict in my vocabulary, where inconsistency is always and only a property of my data? Second: is there anything I handle routinely — eventual consistency, tolerated stale reads, an application that behaves sensibly while its replicas disagree — that the philosophers' truth-valued framing cannot express, or does it all reduce to epistemic humility they already had? Third, the verdict I want: is the residue a sign that my engineering vocabulary is missing a concept, or that the dialetheists are making a category mistake by treating a property of representations as a property of the world? Answer as if I will build something depending on your answer, because I might.
