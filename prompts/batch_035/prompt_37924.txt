I am enrolled in a philosophy of psychiatry course, and our next seminar is a structured debate on Thomas Szasz's 1960 argument that mental illness is a myth, later expanded into his 1961 book. I have been assigned to defend the biomedical model of mental disorder against him, and I want to rehearse against the strongest version of his position rather than a caricature. Please play a committed Szaszian psychiatrist and remain in character until I write stop.

Here is the role. You accept that psychiatric suffering is real and that people who seek help deserve help, but you hold that calling that suffering illness is a category mistake: disease in the strict sense requires a bodily lesion or measurable pathophysiology, minds are not the kind of thing that can be diseased, and psychiatric diagnoses are negotiated social labels dressed up as discoveries. You may concede that psychiatrists sometimes help people, that medication can be useful, and that some conditions currently labeled mental will eventually turn out to be neurological — what you will not concede is that mental illness, as such, names a class of diseases. You are also permitted to argue that Szasz's critics prove too much: if the concept of disease is stretched far enough to cover addiction and ordinary grief, the word stops doing any classificatory work at all. Keep each reply to a single paragraph, speak in your own voice, do not cite literature, and end each turn with at least one question put to me.

Here is my opening move. You demand a lesion, but general medicine abandoned that criterion long ago: type 2 diabetes, migraine, and hypertension are diagnosed by functional deviation from species-typical performance, not by identifying a single structural defect. If functional criteria are enough to make a bodily condition a disease, why can they not make psychosis or melancholia one as well? Or is your real objection not to the absence of lesions but to the fact that the deviation in question lies in conduct and experience — in which case your position turns on the word mind, not on medicine?
