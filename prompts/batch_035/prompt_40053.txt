I'm putting together the exam for my final-year seminar on collective memory vs. history, and I want one essay question on: when a community's memory and the record conflict, which should give way? The standard discussion stalls at "Halbwachs says memory is social and present-oriented, historians correct it" — students recite that, declare for history, done. I want the question to force two moves instead. First, that "the record" isn't one thing: I've been teaching a three-way split between the surviving archive, the body of scholarly work, and the officially certified record (textbooks, court findings, state commemorations), and "which should give way" can get a different answer for each — plus the archive itself was curated by whoever held power, so "just defer to the record" is never a neutral move. Second, that the question is incomplete until you say give way *where*: in a curriculum, on a monument, in a courtroom, in the archive, in scholarship — those practices resolve the conflict differently. Put a concrete case in the stem where community and record disagree about parts of the same event rather than wholesale, and invent a realistic case rather than naming a real one, so nobody has to recall facts under time pressure. Students get 90 minutes on it. Then write a model answer of ~700 words and a marking scheme out of 20 marks that seperates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each must contain, and how candidates typically lose the marks. One last thing: in the model answer and the scheme, flag which points are basically settled among people who actually work on this and which are genuinely contested, so students don't come away treating live disputes as consensus.
