ok so for my philosophy class i've basically gone all-in on Descartes' view about animals: they're just machines, maybe super complicated ones, but with nothing going on inside — no thoughts, no feelings, no experience at all. humans are different because we've got a soul that does the thinking. i got this from the Discourse on Method, where he basically runs two tests on animals: (1) a speech test — no animal ever puts words together in new combinations to answer what was actually said to them, a parrot just repeats stuff; and (2) an action test — animals DO beat us at some things, like a swallow builds a better nest than i ever could, but they do it the same fixed way every time, like a clock, which for Descartes shows it's just their organs acting by nature, not reason.

heres my problem. my teacher asked "ok, but what would actually count against your view?" and i realised i don't have a great answer. i know about crows bending wires into hooks and apes taught sign language, but people with the machine view always seem to have a comeback: that's just hardwiring, no matter how clever the behavior looks it could in theory be a very complicated automaton. so:

1. what would genuinely count against the animals-are-machines view, not just embarrass it? what would an animal have to DO that a clock, however fancy, couldn't?
2. is there a real difference between "instinct" and "thinking" once you're inside this view, or has it been qualified so many times that nothing could ever refute it? and if nothing could refute it, is that actually a problem for the view, or fine? (my dad says moral views are like that too and nobody calls them unscientific, but that feels off to me.)
3. also someone told me there's a weak spot in Descartes himself: he thought human reflexes happen without the soul (like blinking when something comes at your eye is just body mechanics). so what exactly is left that needs the soul, and could better brain science keep shrinking that leftover until the line between humans and animals looks arbitrary? would that count against the view, or only against Descartes' version of it?

please rank the possible defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, and be honest — if you think the view is basically insulated from evidence and that this is somehow ok for a view of this kind, just say so. don't only list animal-cognition facts at me, i've seen the crow videos. i want to know which evidence would move someone already committed to the view, and which a committed machine-view person would just wave off, and whether the waving-off is legit reasoning or motivated stuff. also no reading lists please, i can't look things up right now.
