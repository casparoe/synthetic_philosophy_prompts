Last week she used this to dismiss my question about whether two people who sincerely disagree over whether eating meat is wrong are disagreeing about anything at all, the way they clearly would be if they disagreed about whether meat consumption is rising. I want to know what that one-liner is actually claiming. Lay out the distinct precise theses it could stand for — about what moral sentences mean, about what speakers are doing in uttering them, about which psychological states are expressed, about whether there are moral facts for sentences to be answerable to — and for each say whether it's a triviality, a substantive and defensible claim, or just false. For each reading, also say whether it can handle the case that keeps bothering me: moral sentences occurring unasserted, inside conditionals and denials and disjuncts, where someone can coherently accept that if lying is wrong then getting your little brother to lie is wrong while also asserting that lying is not wrong, and where the validity of inferring the consequent depends on the moral vocabulary meaning the same thing in embedded and unembedded positions. And finally, tell me which of the readings, if any, would actually license my TA's dismissal of my question about disagreement, since I suspect she needs a stronger reading than the one that's true.
