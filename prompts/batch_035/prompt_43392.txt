I help run a little philosophy discussion group at our local library — about 14 people, mostly retirees and a few parents who show up whenever the topic sounds fun, and we get 90 minutes per session. Last month the topic was basically "why do bad things happen," and one guy brought up the old idea that God, if there is one, had to pick from a menu of all the possible worlds and went with the best one available — the line Voltaire spent a whole book making fun of. It's been stuck in my head since. What I noticed is that everyone in the room argued about it abstractly, like whether the idea is "true," but nobody actually felt what it commits you to — that you have to look at a specific catastrophe and say this, in this exact amount, belongs in the winning entry.

So here's what I want: design a game for us where people have to play world-designer. My rough idea is something like they get a base world and a pile of intervention cards (wipe out one childhood disease, make people somewhat more empathetic, fix the thing where the climate flips on its own every so often, whatever you think works), and every fix secretly makes something else worse or leaves a scar they only find out about later — so they end up fighting about whether a trade was actually worth it from inside the world they built. And I'd love it if the game somehow forces them to notice the other thing I can't stop chewing on: that whenever a group declares their world "done," it seems like you could always n just add one more slightly better thing, so maybe "the best possible world" is like asking for the biggest number, and there's no such item on the menu at all. I'd rather they trip over that themselves than get lectured about it — maybe the groups get to raid each other's finished worlds for one more improvement, round after round, until they realize the round never has to stop.

Please give me the full setup: rules, whatever simple materials I can print or grab (index cards, envelopes, that kind of thing — no apps, half these folks have flip phones), and how to keep score without real money. Then a debrief plan that connects what actually happened back to the idea, including which parts of the idea the game just can't reproduce and I'll have to supply myself with talking points. And since I've never run anything like this before, tell me honestly what will predictably go wrong the first time — I'm guessing certain people will just refuse to accept any downside card, or the whole thing descends into "wouldn't it be nice if everything was fine," but you'd know better than me. Also maybe hold back the name Leibniz until the very end, so they meet the idea before they meet the label? If you think that's a bad idea, say so.
