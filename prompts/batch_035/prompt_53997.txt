I'm up for a job talk at an interdisciplinary cog sci institute next month, and the fit is basically perfect except for one thing: my work is eliminativist about the propositional attitudes. The thesis, in short: beliefs and desires, as classically conceived, are supposed to be discrete, sentence-like states with compositional inferential roles (a belief that P-and-Q has to interact with a belief that P in a specific logical way). I argue nothing in the brain is actually structured like that -- the real states are graded, vector-valued expectation and value states that don't compose the way the classical story needs -- so propositional attitudes as such don't exist. The twist I add, which is the part I expect to get beat up on: I say everyday belief/desire talk can survive as a lossy projection onto the real states, the way "sunrise" survived heliocentrism. So I'm eliminativist about the ontology but not about the vocabulary. "Eliminative conservationism" is what I call it, though I'm not married to the label.

The audience is the tricky part. The institute is mostly predictive processing people who think they've already replaced beliefs with variational free energy minimization, plus one very sharp Bayesian modeler, two enactivists who think PP folks are still too internalist, and one retired old-school computationalist who was trained by people who took the language of thought seriously. So this isn't a philosophy department that will give me the standard self-refutation objection as their opener -- I need the objections as these people would actually raise them.

You're a professional philosopher of mind who has sat on hiring committees and seen a hundred of these Q&As go sideways. Think carefully before you write, and brainstorm a bit first about what each subgroup in that audience actually cares about. Then give me a prep sheet:

- The one place where the PP crowd and the old computationalist will accidentally converge against me, since I suspect they'll both want to say my vector states just are beliefs under a new name
- The question I'm quietly hoping nobody asks, and an answer for it anyway, in case someone does
