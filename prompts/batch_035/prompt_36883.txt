I'm a PhD student writing up a position statement on population axiology for our department's normative theory workshop, and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. Here is the package: (1) I'm a welfarist about outcome betterness — how good an outcome is depends solely on the distribution of well-being across the lives in it; (2) when the population is fixed, I accept the total view outright — summing well-being is the right way to compare outcomes that contain the same people; (3) I think the repugnant conclusion is false: a world of trillions of lives barely worth living is not better than a smaller world of very happy lives; (4) I accept weak mere addition — adding a person whose life is worth living, without affecting anyone else's well-being, does not make the outcome worse; (5) I take the sadistic conclusion to be a dealbreaker rather than a cost — if a theory forces me to accept either it or the repugnant conclusion, I take the repugnant conclusion; (6) I find the standard escape routes — critical-level views, lexical thresholds, variable-value views — ad hoc, in that each seems engineered to block one formal result rather than to express a genuine view about well-being, and I'm not prepared to defend any of them; (7) I have a standing person-affecting pull: it's hard for me to see how an outcome can be worse than another when it is worse for no one — though I also believe, about the non-identity problem, that it is wrong to create a predictably miserable child even though that child's life is not worse for the child than nonexistence was. Which pairs among these are genuinely in tension, which merely look like it, and what would each have to give? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally damaging, say what the least costly overall revision would be and what accepting it would commit me to elsewhere, and be specific about which of the seven commitments is actually load-bearing for the trouble.
