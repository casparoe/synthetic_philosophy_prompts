Write a satirical keynote speech, roughly 650 words, delivered at an industry conference by the CEO of a fictional budget airline on the occasion of her company being named lowest-cost carrier for the tenth consecutive year. The target of the satire is the logic of multipolar traps and races to the bottom, and the humor must come from getting that logic exactly right: every cost-cutting measure she celebrates should be individually rational, she should sincerely regret each one, and she should make clear that she cannot stop unilaterally because competitors would simply take the market. Include a passage in which she reviews, one by one, the industry's failed attempts at escaping the trap — the voluntary maintenance-standards accord everyone quietly defected from, the safety-investing startup now remembered fondly, the regulator who tried to slow the race — and explains why each was doomed or unacceptable, without ever conceding that the race itself was a choice. At some point she should use the word "Moloch" exactly once, without explanation, as though it were an ordinary industry term, and the speech should close with her unveiling the next round of cuts, framed as tragic necessity. The tone should be deadpan and even wistful rather than villainous, so a reader sees the structure of the trap — individually rational steps, collectively ruinous outcome, no one with both the power and the incentive to stop it — without it ever being named as such. Avoid references to any real company or living person.
