I'm the community member on the benefits committee of a mid-sized regional health plan (they need one non-insurance person by regulation, I don't vote but I do get to argue), and we've built up a track record of rulings on what counts as treatment we'll cover versus enhancement we won't. Lately I've started to wonder if our precedents actually hang together under one rule or whether they only look consistent. Here's the record: we covered growth hormone for a kid with a confirmed pituitary deficiency, but denied it for a healthy short 12-year-old whose parents pushed hard for it. We cover stimulants for diagnosed ADHD, but denied them for a group of med students who wanted them for board-exam studying. We covered a cochlear implant for a child deafened by meningitis, but denied a hearing-amplification device for a security guard who hears fine and wanted supranormal hearing for work. We cover vaccines, and we cover contraception, even though neither treats any condition the patient has — contraception if anything turns normal functioning off. We cover statins for high cholesterol, and we denied an SSRI prescription for a woman with no symptoms at all who said she just wanted to feel better than well.

Next month we have to rule on a new drug that partially reverses age-related cognitive slowing. The trial population is adults 65 and up who are cognitively normal for their age — no MCI, no dementia, they'd all pass the standard screens — but who are slower than they were at 30 and slower than the cross-sectional average for adults overall. The drug brings their processing speed back to roughly young-adult levels, does basically nothing in healthy 30-year-olds, and as a side finding modestly reduced progression to mild cognitive impairment. One committee member says it's obviously enhancement, it's HGH for the brain; another says it's obviously treatment, these people have lost something and we're giving it back.

I'd love your read before the meeting. What rules could you run over our earlier verdicts that fit all of them — the obvious candidates seem to be something like "restore species-normal functioning," "cover only what a diagnosis justifies," "restore the individual's own prior baseline," and maybe some looser prevention/benefit rule given the vaccine and statin calls — and what does each predict about the new drug? Which of our old cases is the closest analog? Give me your best guess at the ruling that's most consistent with ourselves, and, since I suspect the rules diverge on this one, tell me what further case would actually distinguish between them — I've been chewing on something like a former chess prodigy who was massively above average at 25, has declined to merely average, and wants the drug to get his old edge back, but maybe you can think of a cleaner one.
