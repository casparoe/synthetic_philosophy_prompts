In my philosophy of mind seminar this week, we were discussing Baars-style global workspace theory, and when one student began sketching the idea of a workspace where information gets integrated and "broadcast" to the rest of the system, another cut in with "that's just a Cartesian theater" — Dennett's term, as I understand it — and the discussion moved on as though that settled the matter. I don't think it did, but I also couldn't say precisely what the slogan commits its user to. Before the next seminar I'd like a list of the distinct precise claims "there is no Cartesian theater" could be expressing, since they seem to differ a lot in strength. For each reading, please say whether it is more or less trivial, whether it is plausible, and — this is the part I most care about — whether it would actually support using the slogan to dismiss a global workspace account, as opposed to merely sounding like it does. For instance, I can imagine a reading on which the slogan denies only that there is a single anatomical location where all processing converges, which seems like an empirical point that a workspace theorist might simply concede; and a stronger reading on which there is no fact of the matter about when or where contents "enter consciousness" at all, which seems harder to establish. I'd also like to know whether any of the readings turn on a regress about an inner observer, and whether that regress only bites against views that explain the observer in the same terms. Please lay this out as a numbered list, one reading per item, with a short verdict after each, and keep the whole thing under 350 words — I want to bring it to the seminar without anyone having to wade through an essay.
