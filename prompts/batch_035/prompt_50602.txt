The debate between methodological individualism and methodological holism in the social sciences has been running in something like its modern form since Durkheim and Weber, and it strikes me as remarkably static. Each side knows the other's arguments cold. Holists since Durkheim have insisted that social facts are external to, and coercive of, individual actors, pointing to stable, institution-level regularities like suicide rates that vary with social integration rather than with the psychology of any particular person; individualists from Weber through Popper and Watkins have replied that collectives do nothing that individual human beings do not do, and that genuine explanation must ultimately proceed through agents, their situations, and their reasons. Meanwhile, everyday social science seems to proceed without resolving any of this: Schelling explained residential segregation with a model of individual movers reacting to neighbors; epidemiologists speak of norms and behaviors spreading by social contagion without deciding whether that contagion is anything over and above individual imitation; economists routinely explain events by movements in aggregate quantities like the unemployment rate while insisting, when pressed philosophically, that only agents act. My working hypothesis is that much of the dispute is verbal: that explanation is doing double duty, as the derivation of a phenomenon from laws on one side and as a mechanism-sketch showing how it could arise on the other, and that what holists really need is not an ontology of superpersonal entities but a concession about prediction and descriptive convenience at the aggregate level. Is that diagnosis right, or is there a genuine substantive disagreement buried here — a hidden empirical question about the autonomy of macro-regularities, a difference in standards for what counts as an explanation, or a clash over the very aims of social science? Please diagnose the structure of the disagreement using what each side actually claims, and tell me what, if anything, would count as progress: a distinction, a modeling technique, a reformulated question, or an argument that the impasse reflects a permanent feature of the subject matter.
