Two people in my AI-safety reading group have been arguing for over a year about how advanced systems ought to be designed, and I want them to finaly produce something together. One is a committed approval-directionist: he thinks an AI should never be handed a fixed goal, but instead, at every choice point, take the action its overseer would most endorse on careful reflection — that keeps it corrigible and avoids Goodhart-ish optimization. The other is a committed goal-directionist: she thinks anything that has to get real work done over long horizons needs an objective it actually optimizes, and that approval-direction either collapses into "maximize approval" (which is just a goal, and a Goodhart-shaped one at that) or degenerates into a system that constantly asks permission and is uselessly passive.

Please draft the joint statement an adversarial collaberation between them would produce. Take the role of a careful arbitrator who knows this literature well; brainstorm privately before writing, and check that each side would actually sign the wording of its own disagreement — no slanted phrasing. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both accept; (2) a table of the exact residual disagreements; (3) the cruxes, and for each one a concrete scenario or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance — consider, e.g., a system steering a decade-long project its overseer can't evaluate step by step, or an overseer who turns out to be confidently wrong about something important. Finally, tell me which parts of the dispute, if any, strike you as purely verbal (for instance whether "always pick the action the overseer would approve of" is just goal-directedness under another name), and why.
