So I'm a phil math PhD student and I've got a half-formed idea for a term paper (~6000 words, one semester) that I want to turn into an actual proposal. The topic: the possibility that ZFC is just inconsistent, but from an angle I haven't seen done. Everyone treats "maybe the axioms are inconsistent" as either paranoid or as settled by Gödel (we can't prove it, so move on). My thought is that we're asking the wrong question. If ZFC is inconsistent, there's some finite derivation of a contradiction — but for any feasible length bound, the overwhelming majority of derivations that long are effectively noise, so the fact that no one has stumbled on one is incredibly weak evidence that none exists. We've searched a vanishingly small corner of the haystack and are acting like it's empty. So maybe the real question is: what would rational credence and practice look like for a working mathematician under irreducible uncertainty about consistency? Does it even matter if the shortest contradiction is, say, longer than the number of atoms in the universe — presumably all theorems we actually care about are "true anyway" and provable in fragments we trust more... though I suspect that intuition is itself confused, since from a formal contradiction everything follows. Anyway.

Turn this into a proposal for me. It should have: the question stated precisely enough that it has a determinate answer; why the standard positions (Gödelian humility, quasi-empiricism, and the "we'd have found it by now" attitude) leave it open; what I'd actually do month by month (and I want it to be philosophy, not a empirical proof-search survey — though thinking about proof search and its limits might be fair game); what the possible conclusions are and what would decide between them; and an honest assessment of what could make this too big for one semester.
