I'm preparing a proposal for a two-month visiting fellowship at a comparative philosophy institute, and I want to build it around a half-formed thought about the Mencius–Xunzi debate on human nature. The standard framing treats them as giving opposing answers to a single empirical question — whether we are born good or bad — and the usual sophisticated move is to say they must simply be talking past each other. My suspicion is that the talking-past-each-other diagnosis itself papers over something important: I think the real disagreement may be about the semantic and practical role of the term Mencius and Xunzi both use for human nature — specifically, whether calling a tendency part of our nature confers on it a kind of default authority in moral education. If so, Mencius needs the sprouts to be authoritative (so that cultivation is the completion of something already underway), while Xunzi needs nature to be normatively inert (so that ritual can do all the normative work without competing against an internal rival standard). Read this way, the dispute isn't merely verbal; it's a substantive disagreement about what moral education can build on, conducted through a disagreement about a word.

Please turn this into a proposal with the following components: (1) the question stated precisely enough that it could have a determinate answer; (2) why the two main existing readings — the direct-contradiction reading and the mere-verbal-dispute reading — leave it open; (3) what I would actually do during the two months, concretely. My own tentative idea for method is to reconstruct each thinker's operative notion of nature as a rule for classifying cases, then construct test cases where the two notions yield different verdicts — for example, whether shame at one's own wrongdoing should count as evidence of nature's goodness on either notion, given that it appears prior to formal instruction but is responsive to the norms of one's community — and to check how the texts handle the cases. But feel free to improve on this or propose something better suited to a short fellowship; (4) what would count as the project failing — what outcome would show my hypothesis was confused or unprovable in the time available.

Keep the whole thing under 700 words. Do not include a bibliography or citations; I will supply those myself, and positions may be referred to descriptively. Finally, throughout the proposal, please mark which claims about the debate are matters on which well-informed interpreters largely agree, and which are genuinely contested — for instance, whether Mencius's sprouts are best read as genuinely moral responses or as proto-moral dispositions, and whether Xunzi's rituals function primarily as external constraints or as the expression of a distinctively human capacity for patterning. The proposal should flag such points as contested rather than quietly resolving them in favor of one reading.
