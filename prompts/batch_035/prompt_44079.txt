I help run a monthly "hard questions over beer" discussion group, and next month's topic is which animals are actually conscious -- not "do animals deserve moral consideration," we did that already, but literally which ones have experience. I want to hand people a decision tree at the door so they can figure out where they land before the arguing starts, because right now everyone just says "I'm basically a utilitarian" and we never get anywhere.

The tree should start from something almost everyone in the room will accept (say, that dogs feel something and rocks don't) and then walk down through concrete cases, with each node phrased as a question someone can answer without knowing which position it dumps them at. Please make sure these cases are in there somewhere, since they're where our group actually gets stuck: (1) an octopus, whose neurons are mostly in the arms; (2) a fish, where the debate is whether its nociception is "real" pain without a cortex; (3) a lobster at a seafood counter; (4) a C. elegans worm with its fixed 302 neurons; (5) an ant colony considered as one distributed subject rather than many individuals; and (6) an AI chatbot that talks about its "inner life," which one of our regulars keeps insisting is the same question in different packaging. Also one weird hypothetical I made up: imagine a jellyfish-like creature with no central brain at all, but whose whole nerve net synchronizes into one rhythmic pattern when it hunts -- is that a minimal subject or just fancy reflex? I don't know if anyone's written about something like that, which is partly why I like it.

For the leaves, I want actual positions where they have names (the view that consciousness requires a particular biological substrate, higher-order representation views, global-workspace style accounts applied across species, the "nociception isn't suffering" camp, and the deflationary view that the question itself assumes too much), and "unnamed" where the logical space has a slot nobody fills -- then a sentence on whether that slot is empty because the view is incoherent or just because nobody's bothered. Also please flag any two leaves your tree can't actually separate, like whether some cases decide the animal question and the AI question at the same time or whether you'd need extra nodes for that. Maybe 6-8 questions total, keep each one under three sentences so people can answer it while holding a beer. If you want, sketch one worked example at the end showing where a made-up person with specific answers lands, so I can check the tree is followable.
