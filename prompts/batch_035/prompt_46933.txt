My stats professor keeps saying Jeffrey conditionalization is just Bayesian conditioning in disguise — whenever your evidence is uncertain, you can redescribe the situation as ordinary conditioning with certainty on some higher-order proposition about your own state (roughly, "my visual system is telling me the cloth is green"), so Jeffrey's rule is a convenience, not a genuine rival. A friend of mine who reads philosophy of science says this misses the point, something about rigidity: Jeffrey's rule lets the new marginals come from outside while keeping conditionals fixed, and the disguise trick quietly assumes you can pin down the exact higher-order proposition, which in the paradigm cases like judging a cloth's color by candlelight you plausibly can't — you just arrive at a new credence somehow. Compare the two pictures for me: is Jeffrey conditioning a real generalization of conditioning, or notational shortand? And where exactly does the disguise view break down — for instance, what happens on each view when you do two successive updates on uncertain evidence, or when the higher-order proposition you'd need to condition on is vague? A concrete example would help; the cloth-by-candlelight case is fine. I'm writing a term paper on this so I need actual arguments, not textbook boilerplate.
