So here's my situation. My uncle, a retired physics teacher, is a die-hard perdurantist — he genuinely believes tables are four-dimensional worms with temporal parts, and that you, right now, are only in contact with your current slice's spatial bits. His old friend from grad school is an endurantist who thinks all that is a category mistake: things are wholly present at every moment they exist, full stop. They've been arguing about this at every family gathering for years, and last month they finally agreed to do a proper adversarial collaboration, and for some reason they've asked me — the only one who studied philosophy, and barely — to draft the joint statement.

Write that draft for me. Specifically:

1. A numbered section of claims both can sign. These have to be substantive, not "objects persist" filler — I want things like: that ordinary counting talk and ordinary "same object" talk are neutral between the two views, that the statue-and-the-clay coincidence case is a genuine puzzle for everyone and not just a gotcha for one side, that persistence doesn't require literal movement through time, and so on. And if a claim is one a stage theorist could also sign, note it, because there's a third guy at these dinners who only believes in instantaneous stages and he is definately watching to see if we forgot about him.

2. A disagreements table. For each residual disagreement I need the exact wording the endurantist would accept for her own view and the exact wording the perdurantist would accept for his — nothing slanted like "endurantism cannot account for change," which she'd never initial. The obvious one to include is the problem of temporary intrinsics (the poker that's straight at noon and bent at 2pm — is it bent and straight at the same time? are those properties relativized to times? had by distinct temporal parts? had "just plain" by one whole thing?), but get the other ones in too: how to count when things coincide, what "wholly present" is even supposed to mean, and whether persistence is a species of location the way extension is.

3. The cruxes. Which sub-questions, if settled, would actually move one of them? I suspect one is whether relativity forces their hand — the perdurantist keeps saying that if simultaneity is frame-relative, then an object's shape at a time comes out frame-relative too, and that makes trouble for "wholly present"; the endurantist keeps replying that shapes-at-frames are no worse than shapes-at-times. If that's a real crux, say what would count as progress on it, and record each side's prediction in advance — e.g., about how cleanly the other side's account handles a slowly rotating object whose "shape at a given moment" differs from frame to frame.

4. And be honest with me: is any part of this fight just about how to use the word "present"? If you think part of it is verbal, say which part and why — but also tell me which part is definately NOT verbal, because if it were all just words they wouldn't keep getting this worked up about a fire poker.

Format it as the numbered agreed-claims section, then the disagreements table, then cruxes with predictions written down, then the verbal-dispute bit at the end. Keep it fair — I have to hand this to both of them at Thanksgiving.
