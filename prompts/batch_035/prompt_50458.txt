"Einstein's hole argument: consider a region of spacetime (the 'hole') containing no matter, with the values of all fields on the boundary of the hole fixed. Substantivalism is the view that the points of the manifold exist in their own right, independently of any fields defined on them. Now take a smooth transformation of the manifold onto itself that is the identity everywhere outside the hole but shifts points around inside it, and drag the metric field along with it. This gives a second metric field that agrees with the original outside the hole but assigns different field values to each point inside it. Since the transformation is just a change of coordinates, the two fields are the same physical situation described in different coordinates. But for the substantivalist the points inside the hole are real individuals, so the two fields attribute different properties to the very same points and hence describe distinct physically possible worlds. That means the field inside the hole is not fixed by the boundary values, so general covariance plus substantivalism refute determinism. The standard substantivalist reply is sophisticated substantivalism, defended by Earman and Norton in their 1987 paper, on which diffeomorphism-related fields are treated as one and the same physical situation."
