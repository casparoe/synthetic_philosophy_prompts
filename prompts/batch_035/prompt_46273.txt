For a writing project on classical Chinese logic I need a parody piece. Write a claim-denial letter, around 450 words, from Stone Bridge Mutual Assurance to a customer whose white horse was injured in transit. The insurer denies the claim on the ground that the policy covers horses and, per the School of Names, a white horse is not a horse: horse names the shape, white names the color, and what names the color is not what names the shape. It should also run the disjunction move — had the customer requested a horse, a yellow or black one would have done, but he asked for a white horse, so the claim fails — plus one clause invoking the seperation of hard and white, and a closing offer to consider future coverage for anything the customer can point at. Keep it deadpan, ordinary customer-relations register, no winking at the reader: someone who knows Gongsun Long should find the arguments applied faithfully enough to be funny, and everyone else should read it as just an unusually pedantic denial letter. Work in one or two of Hui Shi's classics — fire is not hot, the wheel never touches the ground — as precedent citations.
