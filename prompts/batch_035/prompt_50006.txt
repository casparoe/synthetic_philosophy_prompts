I've been into Ian Hacking's stuff on interactive kinds for a while now -- the idea that classifying people isn't like classifying rocks, because the people you classify can come to understand themselves in terms of the classification, and change their behavior, which changes what the classification picks out, which changes the people, and round and round it goes (he calls this the looping effect of human kinds). His stock examples are things like fugue, multiple personality, autism. And lately I've basically committed to a fairly strong version of this about psychiatry specifically: that DSM-style nosology can never converge on a stable, mind-independent carve of mental disorders, because the categories are interactive -- the diagnosis becomes part of the person's self-understanding, support groups and tiktok form around it, clinicians respond, the presentation of the condition drifts, and eventually the category either dissolves or means something different than it did 30 years ago. On this view the whole project of a "scientific" taxonomy of mental illness has a built-in instability that isn't just noise, it's a structural feature. Not saying the categories are useless, just that the taxonomy is more like a constantly recompiled shared social artifact than a progressively refined map of a fixed territory.

Anyway I've noticed I basically never expose this view to serious potential falsifiers, and my friends are sick of hearing about it lol. So: what would actually count against it? I'm imagining several kinds of things -- historical cases where a psychiatric category stayed surprisingly stable across decades despite wide cultural uptake, types of disorders where self-understanding plausibly can't feed back into the underlying condition (maybe something like Huntington's-adjacent stuff, though that's neurology not psychiatry, which is itself an interesting boundary question), or arguments that the "drift" people observe is just diagnostic fashion / changing base rates rather than a genuine looping effect. Also want to hear whether the view has quietly insulated itself: if any stability in a category can be explained away as "the loop hasn't closed yet" and any instability as evidence of looping, it predicts everything and nothing. Is that fatal for a claim of this type, or is it like other structural theses where the point is explaining a tendency rather than making point predictions? Rank the defeaters from would-genuinely-move-me to merely-awkward, and be honest about which ones a committed Hacking-style person like me would just dismiss.
