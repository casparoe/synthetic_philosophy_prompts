Roughly a dozen engineers turn up, we've got 60 minutes, a whiteboard and a projector, and no budget to speak of.

Next session is on what Dennett calls heterophenomenology — the idea that when you're studying consciousness you should treat people's reports about their own experience like a text to be interpreted, exactly like any other data coming out of the system, rather than as a direct pipe into the mind. I want an activity where they actually run into the method instead of me standing there explaining it, because engineers will stare at you blankly if you just lecture.

My rough idea: some people play "subjects" and experience something (an ambiguous stimulus of some kind?) and report on it, and the rest play "investigators" who have to build a catalogue of what the subjects apparently experienced. Then a twist where the reports are obviously unreliable — say a couple of plants in the subject pool who confidently report stuff that couldn't have happened — and the investigators have to decide what to do with a report they flat-out disbelieve. Do they throw it away, or does it still count as data about the subject's (apparent) world even though it's not data about the real one? That last bit is definately the part I most want them to trip over, because as far as I understand it that's the whole trick of the method: a report is always evidence, it's just not always evidence of the thing it claims to be evidence of.
