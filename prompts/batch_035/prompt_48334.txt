I've come to hold that one-boxing in Newcomb's problem is not merely what wins but what rationality requires: when an action is evidence of a good outcome, that evidential bearing is itself a reason for the action, and evidential decision theory is the correct core of rational choice, with Newcomb's problem serving as its proof case rather than its embarrassment. Tell me what would count against this position. I want the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want them to include the ones I already half-know about: the medical Newcomb cases, where evidential reasoning seems to tell you to decline a harmless pleasure merely because indulging it is evidence of the lesion or gene that inclines you toward it; the question of whether the tickle defense — the thought that an agent with adequate self-knowledge screens off the troublesome correlation — rescues evidentialism or quietly amends it into something else; cases with transparent boxes or predictors of known error rates, where I suspect my intuitions stop tracking my theory; and the newer suggestion that the whole dispute rests on a confusion, because rationality should evaluate decision procedures or policies rather than individual acts, so that one-boxing and two-boxing are both correct answers to different questions. For each defeater, say what a committed evidentialist would concede as genuine pressure and what they would dismiss, and tell me honestly whether the accumulated dismissals amount to defensible refinement or to immunization. Then tell me what the position actually risks: which formal results, which constructed cases, or which practical developments — say, the arrival of genuinely reliable predictors of human behavior — could settle the matter, or whether a decision theory cannot be expected to stick its neck out the way an empirical theory does, and whether that fact should make me suspect the whole one-boxing/two-boxing dispute is not really about anything. For what it's worth, the argument that would move me most is a case where evidentialism and my own deliberative practice come apart and I can tell, on reflection, which one I would actually act on.
