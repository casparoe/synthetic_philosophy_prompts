Hi, I run a small blog where we argue about maths puzzles and things like that — no academics, just people who like this stuff. I've been reading my way into the old fight about what probability actually IS (frequentism vs the Bayesian/personal view vs propensity stuff) and I want to make one of those online quizzes: "which interpretation of probability do you *actually* hold?" about ten questions, each a concrete scenario with 2-3 answer options, then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto the positions.

The scenarios I want included: a fair coin, a doctor saying you have a 30% chance of developing some disease, betting on an election, a radioactive atom that might decay in the next hour, and a die I suspect is loaded. Hard rule: no question can use jargon like "frequentist" or "propensity" — it has to sort people by what they say about the cases, not by whether they've read the literature. So each question should be answerable by someone who's never heard of any of this.

One pet theory of mine I'd like worked in: I suspect a lot of people are quietly frequentist about dice but Bayesian about elections without ever noticing they've switched. So a few of the questions should be "same underlying structure, different surface" pairs designed to catch that switch, and the scoring key should say what it means if someone gives inconsistent answers rather than forcing them onto one leaf. Also flag any two positions the quiz can't really tell apart, instead of pretending it can.

Please write the whole thing in British English.
