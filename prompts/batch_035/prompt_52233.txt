I've been a backend/distributed systems engineer for about 15 years and I mostly do infra and SRE stuff, and recently a coworker who did a philosophy degree keeps going on about the extended mind thesis. I get the gist: there's a famous thought experiment with a guy named Otto who has memory problems and writes everything in a notebook, and the claim is that the notebook literally is part of his mind, not just a tool his mind uses, because it plays the same functional role that biological memory plays in his friend Inga. There's also some parity principle involved, something like: if an external thing does what an internal cognitive process does, treat it as part of the mind. Fair enough. But the debates my coworker describes -- whether it's a matter of "coupling" vs "constitution," whether the mind extends only when there's trust and constant access ("glue and trust" conditions or something like that), whether critics are right that Otto is just causally connected to his notebook rather than made of it -- all of this keeps sliding off me, and I suspect it's because I keep translating it into stuff I actually deal with.

So here's what I want. Restate the extended mind debate in my vocabulary: caches and their backing stores, read-through caches, read replicas, eventual consistency, state that lives outside the app, dependency graphs, failover, what counts as "the service" when some of its state lives in a CDN or a sidecar. Do the translation as honestly as you can -- Otto's notebook as a cache with certain properties, Inga's memory as the backing store, the parity principle as some kind of equivalence claim, the glue-and-trust conditions as SLA-type requirements on latency and availability, the coupling-vs-constitution objection as whatever the analogue would be of saying the cache is causally coupled to the service but isn't part of it. But don't stop there, and don't quietly smuggle in engineering assumptions and call it translation -- I'll notice, e.g. if you treat "is it part of the system" as just an architecture diagram boundary question, that's begging something, because in my world the service boundary is partly a human/administrative choice (who owns the pager) rather than a fact about computation.

Then give me the residue report. What in the philosophical debate maps cleanly onto stuff like caching, load balancing, or the debate about whether state should live in the service or external stores? What had to be distorted to fit -- e.g. I can already tell that in my world we're totally fine with the cache not being the source of truth and we have machinery (invalidation, TTLs) for when they disagree, whereas the philosophers seem to care about whether Otto's notebook IS him in some sense that no TTL policy captures. And what has no counterpart at all in my vocabulary? My hunch is that the residue mostly lives in the fact that Otto's notebook matters to questions about *him* -- his responsibility, his persistence, maybe his consciousness -- and my vocabulary has nothing to say about whether a distributed service is having thoughts vs merely producing outputs, since in my world nobody asks whether the cluster is experiencing anything. But tell me if that's right or if there's something the engineering framing genuinely cannot even frame. And ultimately: is the residue there because my vocabulary is missing something the philosophers are tracking, or because the philosophical claim was less clear than it looked to begin with?
