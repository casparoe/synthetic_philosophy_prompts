You are a philosopher of science specializing in cultural evolution, with experience mediating between rival research programs. Draft the joint statement that the two camps below could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration.

Camp A, the memetic Darwinians (in the tradition of Dawkins and Dennett), hold that culture evolves by differential replication of tokens ("memes"), on a close analogy with gene pools: high-fidelity copying is the key innovation that makes culture genuinely Darwinian, cultural change is to be explained mainly by selection among variant copies, and the meme is a real causal unit.

Camp B, the cultural attraction theorists (associated with Sperber and colleagues), hold that cultural transmission is reconstruction rather than replication: each learner infers what a model intended and rebuilds the item, so transmission is systematically biased toward "cultural attractors" — cognitively natural forms such as good stories, intuitive biology, and memorable tunes. Population-level stability, on this view, is explained mainly by convergent transformation during transmission, not by selection among faithful copies, and the Darwinian analogy is at best a heuristic.

Before drafting, think through what each camp would refuse to sign: formulations that are slanted, agreed claims that covertly adjudicate the dispute, or cruxes framed so that only one side can lose. Then produce a document containing:

(i) a numbered list of agreed claims — about population-level thinking, biased transmission, and what would count as evidence — that both camps could sign without embarrassment;

(ii) a table of remaining disagreements, each stated so that its own side would accept the wording;

(iii) the cruxes: sub-questions such that settling any one of them would move at least one side. For each crux, specify a concrete case both agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. Consider at least these three: serial transmission chains, in which material passes from one person to the next with no audience selecting among variants (does content decay randomly, or drift systematically toward predicted attractors?); cumulative culture, or ratcheting (does it require high-fidelity replication, or function-preserving reconstruction plus retention?); and near-errorless digital copying, where copying fidelity is essentially perfect (does this vindicate replication-based dynamics, or is what gets noticed and retransmitted still governed by attractors?);

(iv) a verdict on whether any part of the dispute is merely verbal — for instance over the words "Darwinian," "selection," or "copy" — and, if so, which part and why.

The finished document should be exact and constructive, such that both camps could put their names to it, with the residual disagreement made precise rather than papered over. Please think carefully and write your answer in American English.
