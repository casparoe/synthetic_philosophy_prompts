Hi — I teach a second-year course on collective reasoning and formal epistemology, and I'm putting together this term's problem set. Topic: the Condorcet jury theorem. The students have all seen the textbook version — if each voter is independently better than a coin flip (p > 1/2), the probability the majority is right climbs towards 1 as the group grows; below 1/2 it collapses towards 0. What I don't want is an exercise they can answer by reciting that.

Heres the exercise I have in mind, roughly: a 25-member editorial board at a journal votes by simple majority on whether a submitted paper's central empirical claim is true, and each editor, working alone, has a 55% chance of judging correctly. Part (a): get them to work out (a decent estimate is fine) the probability that the majority verdict is right, and then ask how many editors you'd need before that probability reaches 99% — I want them to feel the gap between the elegant limit result and any board that actually exists. Part (b): same board, but now suppose all the editors came up under the same influential supervisor, so their mistakes travel together — ask whether the theorem's guarantee survives, and get them to explain in their own words why correlated errors do the damage they do. This is the part where weaker students just write "independence fails" and stop, so the question needs to be phrased so that bare restatement obviously doesn't answer it. Part (c): now suppose competence sits slightly below 1/2, and ask whether adding voters helps or hurts, plus what the general lesson is about when bigger groups are wiser.

Can you write this up as it would actually appear on the problem sheet, then a model answer (with the real numbers where there are numbers, plus maybe 600 words of prose), and a marking scheme out of 20 that names the specific moves separating an A from a B — especially on the correlated-errors part. If you'd restructure the parts, go ahead, just keep the in-the-weeds spirit. And please write it all in British English (our department style guide is fussy, '-ise' endings and all that). apologies for any typos, rushing this out before my train.
