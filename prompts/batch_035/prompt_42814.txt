I run a small research programme at an institute for logic — two years, funding for three postdocs, and a couple of workshops a year — on the theme of dialetheism and paraconsistency. Next month I have to fix the three sub-questions the programme will concentrate on, and I keep going back and forth between them, so what I want from you is a defended ordering, not a survey of the field.

The candidates I can see, stated plainly. The exclusion problem: how a dialetheist, whose negation tolerates contradictions, can nevertheless say of a sentence that it is false without being true, and so express genuine disagreement with a rival rather than merely reporting a contradiction. The conditionals problem: the material conditional is nearly useless in the standard paraconsistent systems, since it lets you detach too little and, on some definitions, validates far too much, and it is unclear what should take its place in ordinary and mathematical reasoning. The standing of classical negation: whether a dialetheist may simply help herself to a second, fully explosive negation alongside the paraconsistent one, and, if she may, what work is left for the paraconsistent connective that the classical one cannot do. Classical recapture: the project of saying precisely where classical reasoning is safe, so that mathematics and science can proceed untouched and only the paradoxes are quarantined. Revenge: the worry that every paraconsistent treatment of the Liar merely breeds a sharper paradox that the logic, by its own lights, cannot express. And the metainferential turn: the idea that it is validity itself, rather than truth, that should behave non-classically, with arguments counted good or bad at a level above the sentences.

For each candidate, I'd like your view on three things: how much other work depends on it; whether it is tractable for postdocs with formal training, as opposed to being better suited to linguists or historians of logic; and what would count as real progress, as opposed to one more system defined on paper whose only virtue is that the Liar comes out well behaved in it. Then tell me which of the six you would leave out of the programme even though it is genuinely open — presumably because settling it would not change much downstream — and whether there is a question I have not listed that you would put above all of them. I suspect there is one, and I suspect I know which, but I would rather hear yours first.

One request on format: continuous prose only, please — no headings, bullet points, bold text or tables — written as you would a considered letter to a colleague, and in British English. The case I make to the institute has to be readable by people who will never open a proof, and I would like yours to read that way too.
