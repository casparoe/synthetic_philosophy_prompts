I've ended up with a pile of views about ethics once the numbers get huge, and I want an audit of whether they can all be true at once, because I suspect at least two can't coexist and I genuinely can't tell which. Here's the pile: (1) I'm a totalist — what matters is the aggregate of wellbeing summed over everyone who exists. (2) I think there's a real chance the universe contains infinitely many welfare subjects, whether spatially or because the future never ends. (3) I think adding one happy life to a world makes it better, full stop, even if that world is already infinite. (4) I think when two outcomes can't be compared in terms of wellbeing, the right attitude is indifference — silence from the theory means "equally good," not "no fact of the matter." (5) For decisions under uncertainty I maximize expected value and I refuse to cap payoffs, so I'll take an astronomically tiny probability of an astronomically large benefit over a sure modest one, and I don't think that's a bug. And (6) despite all of this, I'm completely confident that preventing a famine this year is better than not preventing it, and I don't think that confidence is irrational or in need of the theory's blessing. Go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, rank the real tensions by severity, and for each say what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me elsewhere in the list. I'm not attached to any particular one of these, so don't go easy on whichever you think is load-bearing.
