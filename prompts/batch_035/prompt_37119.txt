So everyone knows Einstein lost the argument about quantum mechanics, but he died in '55, a decade before Bell even wrote his theorem down, so he never saw the actual fight, just the pre-match trash talk. Here's the thing I keep chewing on and can't settle, and I want your honest take on it: if he'd lived to see the loophole-free Bell tests (Delft, NIST, Vienna, the ones from ~2015 onward) and the satellite experiments, what would he have done with the last escape hatch — superdeterminism? I mean the move where you keep locality AND keep hidden variables, and instead deny the assumption that the measurement settings are statistically independent of the hidden variables, so the universe just so happens to be correlated all the way back in a way that fakes a Bell violation in every lab we run.

I can argue it both ways from his actual commitments and it drives me nuts. Pro-taking-the-deal: locality was basically sacred to him, his whole critique of QM was that it posited this spooky action at a distance, so if you tell him here's a way to keep everything local, why wouldn't he sign? Plus he was some kind of Spinozist about determinism anyway — no free choices of experimenters doesn't obviously scare a guy like that, whereas it obviously scares basically everyone currently working.

But against: his entire epistemology leans on experiments probing an independently given external world, and superdeterminism (at least on the usual telling) makes the correlations look fine-tuned to deceive us in precisely the experiments we happen to perform, which has this nasty cosmic-conspiracy flavor. There's also a real difference between determinism being true and determinism being true in a way that's arranged to fake violations wherever humans look. And would he even be moved by the fact that people later tried to close the free-choice loophole with, like, a hundred thousand volunteers mashing buttons and light from quasars deciding the detector settings — or would he correctly point out that none of that touches the superdeterminist move?

So: predict the man. Take his stated commitments seriously (no just turning him into a mascot for QBism or for Bohm or whatever your favorite is), and give me an actual position with the reasoning, plus tell me where the internal tensions in his views would pull him in two directions. I keep flip-flopping on whether the determinism stuff buys the conspiracy or whether the epistemology stuff forbids it, and both sides of the modern debate love to claim him, which is suspicious enough on its own.
