I've spent fifteen years as a public defender in this county, and last month I was appointed to its pretrial services oversight board. The first item I must vote on is whether to keep the risk-assessment tool our judges consult in bail hearings. A recent audit found the tool is well calibrated by race — among defendants flagged high-risk, the share who would in fact be rearrested before trial is roughly the same for Black and white defendants — yet among defendants who would not have been rearrested, Black defendants are flagged high-risk about twice as often as white ones. The vendor says the first finding shows the tool is fair and the second follows necessarily from it, given that rearrest rates differ between the groups; two board members with statistics backgrounds tell me this trade-off is in fact a theorem in the algorithmic fairness literature rather than a vendor excuse, and that no tool could deliver both properties at these base rates short of a perfect predictor. The board must choose among keeping the tool unchanged, dropping it, or keeping it with modifications — for instance, adjusting the risk thresholds, or letting judges see only broad risk categories rather than raw scores. Please draft the written opinion I'll circulate before next week's meeting: it should recommend one course of action with reasons the other members can engage, and it should confront the strongest argument for the losing option honestly — including the uncomfortable fact that dropping the tool would not eliminate risk judgments at bail, only return them, unaudited, to the judges themselves.
