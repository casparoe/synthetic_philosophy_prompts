So I moderate a mid-sized gaming Discord, about 4000 members, and me and three other volunteer mods keep running into the same fight: someone posts something gross, and when we warn them, they reply "I didn't say anything bad, I was just quoting / joking / asking a question / describing what someone ELSE did." And honestly sometimes they're right and we're just being twitchy, and sometimes they're clearly weaseling, and none of us can articulate the difference.

Here's the thing I've been chewing on with zero philosophy background so bear with me. I've noticed that some sentences don't just report stuff, they actually DO stuff. When I type "you're muted for 24 hours" in the mod channel, that's not me describing a mute that already happened somewhere, the typing IS the muting. Same with "I'm sorry" vs "he's sorry" — the first one is me doing an apology, the second one is just a fact about his feelings. So it seems like every post has two sides: what it says, and what it DOES. And our rules are all written about the saying side ("no slurs," "no threats") but the actual harm seems to live on the doing side, because the same words can do totally different things depending on who types them, to whom, in what situation. Like "you should log off before I make you" from some random member is a threat, but "you should log off" from me in a "you're clearly tilted, go to bed" context is... not? And there's the whole thing where it only "works" if the other person takes it that way — like if nobody believes I have the authority to mute, my mute command is just words.

What I want is a procedure the mod team can actually run, because right now it's vibes and we're inconsistent enough that people accuse us of favoritism and honestly sometimes they're right. So please build me:

1. A checklist or ordered set of questions a mod can go through in the 2-3 minutes they have when they see a report, based on whatever the actual philosophy of this stuff says (I gather people have thought hard about "words that do things" — I've seen the phrase "speech acts" and "performative" floating around but I couldn't tell you who coined what, and I don't want to misattribute anything, so just tell me if some idea has a standard name I should be aware of). Which distinctions absolutely have to survive into a 3-minute version, which fancy ones can we drop?

2. It needs to handle our hard cases at minimum: sarcasm that reads as aggression ("wow, great play, really brilliant stuff"), people quoting rule-breaking content to complain about it or to mock it, "someone should do something about that guy" (is that an order? a wish? a threat by proxy?), and the joke-commands like "*I hereby banish thee to the shadow realm*" which everyone knows isn't real.

3. It has to say where the procedure just can't decide and we have to make an actual judgment call as a team, and how to be honest about that when people appeal our decisions. We get maybe 15-20 reports a day, mods are unpaid, sometimes it's one person online at 3am making the call alone.

4. Then run it on three cases and show the work: (a) a member posts "if I ever see [specific person] at a con I'm going to say some things to his face" — vague, but the target is a smaller streamer in our server; (b) someone posts "asking for a friend: hypothetically what's the ban evasion policy wink wink"; and (c) one of MY fellow mods, who I recruited personally and consider a friend, replied to a newbie's bad take with "mods, remove this garbage" which the newbie took as an official warning from staff even though the mod has no removal powers.

Also tell me what this procedure will get wrong — where it'll misfire on our weird population (a lot of neurodivergent members who mean things extremely literally, a lot of members whose first language isn't English). And keep in mind we can't see tone of voice, just text and emotes.

Please write the whole answer in American English. I'd rather have something my co-mods will actually print out and pin than something philosophically airtight, but I also don't want us building a system that sounds official and is really just our vibes wearing a lab coat — call out wherever the procedure is a judgment call dressed up as a step.
