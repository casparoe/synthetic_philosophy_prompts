The red wolf, Canis rufus, was listed as endangered under American law in 1967, was extinct in the wild by 1980, and was reintroduced starting in 1987 into eastern North Carolina from a captive breeding program. Its protected status as a distinct species is what legally drives the recovery effort. But the species problem sits right at the center of the story: the animal is intermediate between the gray wolf and the coyote, and genome-wide studies in the 2010s concluded that the red wolf has no unique ancestry of its own — that it is best understood as a fairly recent admixture of gray wolf and coyote lineages — while critics of those studies argued that the genomic data actually support an older, distinct evolutionary lineage. So the conservation machinery, which turns on whether this is a real species, runs directly into the debate over what specieshood even consists in.
