write me a satirical advice column, around 700 words, run by an agony aunt who has fully internalized the philosophy of transformative experience (L.A. Paul style). The gimmick: she can't give advice about any choice that could change who you are — having a kid, going vegan, emigrating, quitting a career for art — because the you who writes the letter can't know what it'll be like for the you who comes out the other side. So every reply has to route through the actual machinery: epistemic inaccessibility (you can't simulate the experience in advance from the inside), the preference-change problem (you can't assign weights to outcomes for your future self, since she'll want different things than you do), and her only sanctioned tools being coin flips, 'do what you can rationally integrate now', or telling people to treat the choice as revelation rather than decision.

Give it at least three letters. One should be the classic (should I have a kid). One should be about something that *sounds* transformative but is actually epistemically accessible — like whether to get a dog, or move to a rival city she already knows well — so she suddenly CAN give real, opinionated advice, and that contrast should be where a lot of the comedy comes from. And one letter-writer should call her out for inconsistency (e.g. she slips and recommends something for the writer's future self, or gives lifestyle advice that only makes sense if preferences survive). She should have some defense ready that a serious Paulian could actually make, not a hand-wave.

The humor has to come from getting the theory right — someone who knows the book should laugh, not wince. Keep her deadpan throughout; she never breaks character or admits the column is useless as an advice column, even when it obviously is. And let the ending quietly pose the real question: if nobody can advise anybody about any of lifes big choices, what exactly is an advice columnist for? Dont spell the answer out.

It should be readable to someone who hasnt read the book — the letters and replies should carry the ideas, not presuppose them. casual tone is fine, this is for a philosophy-adjacent audience but not specialists only. no bibliography, just the piece.
