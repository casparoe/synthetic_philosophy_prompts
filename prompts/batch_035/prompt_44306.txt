I'm a retired analytical chemist -- 35 years mostly running a spectroscopy lab -- and since retirement I've joined a philosophy reading group, which is how I ended up arguing about epistemology at 70. Last week one of the members shut down a discussion about whether we should distrust our own judgments after learning our judgment is biased (his example was finding out you're a bad judge of a friend's intelligence) by saying evidence of evidence is evidence, so higher-order evidence can only ever reinforce what you already believe and debunking arguments can't get started. That felt wrong to me -- in the lab, the calibration certificate is evidence about the instrument, and it can tell you the instrument reading is wrong, you distrust it and re-calibrate rather than believing harder. So what is the slogan actually claiming? Lay out the different precise theses evidence-of-evidence-is-evidence could stand for, from the trivially true to the substantive to the ones that are just false, and for each one say whether it supports his dismissive move or whether it allows the calibration-certficate situation where the higher-order stuff defeats the first-order belief. A clear list I can bring back to the group would be perfect, and if some reading makes his argument work only under assumptions no one would accept, say that too.
