I am marking a philosophy essay for a second-year undergraduate module, "The Nature of Philosophical Inquiry", and I would like a grader's report on one of the submissions.

The essay question set was:

"Does philosophy make progress? In answering, say what 'progress' could mean for an inquiry that does not settle its questions by accumulating agreed results, and assess at least one argument for thinking that it has made none."

The student's answer reads as follows:

"'Progress' normally means accumulating settled results, the way mathematics and the natural sciences do: each generation starts where the last left off. Philosophy plainly fails this test. The same debates about free will, scepticism, and the good life appear in Plato and reappear essentially unchanged today, so there is no cumulative growth. Even when philosophy seems to advance, the settled parts get handed off: physics and psychology were once natural philosophy, and once they could make progress they left. Hume already saw this in his Enquiry of 1758, noting that questions get answered only when they can be taken up by empirical inquiry.

The strongest pessimistic case comes from the logical positivists. Following Kant's insight that traditional metaphysics consists of pseudo-questions, they held that philosophy had never achieved anything, which is damning precisely because it is an admission by philosophy's own practitioners.

Still, there is progress of a different kind. Gettier's 1963 counterexamples genuinely refuted the justified-true-belief analysis of knowledge, and no serious philosopher has defended it since: a question was settled by counterexample, just as in science. Compatibilism about free will is similar: the debate has been transformed from whether determinism conflicts with freedom into which capacity matters for responsibility, and that refinement of the question is itself an achievement. So philosophy progresses, but not by accumulation; it progresses by clarifying and refining its own concepts, and its mature products — once clarified — become the special sciences. Philosophy is best understood as the preliminary tidying-up of concepts before they can be studied properly."
