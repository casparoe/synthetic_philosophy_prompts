Quick heads up on what I actually think before you read this — I've been calling myself an illusionist for a couple years and finally have to write up my position for a phil of mind reading group so the others can poke at it. Here's the package:

1. Illusionism proper: there are no phenomenal properties in the inflated sense — no qualia as intrinsically private, ineffable, "special" properties of experience. What exists are quasi-phenomenal ones: functional and representational features that our introspective systems misdescribe as something more than they are. The research program should be explaining why experience *seems* to have those magical properties, not finding them.

2. Introspection is, on the whole, a reliable faculty. I trust it about my beliefs, moods, and intentions, and I don't want to become a global skeptic about my own mind just to stay consistent.

3. Moral status is grounded in phenomenal consciousness. An entity matters morally iff it can suffer or enjoy phenomenally — that's why dogs matter and rocks don't, and it's why a patient in a permanent vegetative state matters differently than before. I'm committed to this surviving the illusionism. I refuse to accept that going illusionist makes animals matter less or makes my loved ones' pain less bad.

4. I believe a being could in principle be our functional duplicate in every respect — same discrimination behavior, same reports of distress, same avoidance, same physiological stress responses — and still lack genuine suffering. And I think that being wouldn't matter morally. The lights could be off even with everything running.

5. Related to that, no AI system, however sophisticated, will ever be conscious or morally considerable. Honestly for me this is not an empirical question that future systems might settle — its just never going to happen, no matter how good the chatbot gets.

6. I still find the Mary case and the inverted spectrum compelling, and I dont want to be the guy who waves off intuitions that basically everyone has. I know what red looks like, and Mary seems to learn something when she leaves that room, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

I've mostly convinced myself this is coherent — the illusionism feels like it's just metaphysics, orthogonal to the ethics and the epistemology — but I'd like a real audit. Which pairs of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, how bad is each tension, and what would the cheapest fix for each one be? Then tell me if there's a single revision that would clean up the most. Be specific — dont just tell me illusionism is "controversial," tell me exactly which of my other commitments it can and can't live with.
