so I'm a novelist, four books out, mid-career, and a lit magazine has asked me to write a short position piece on where the meaning of a novel actually comes from — and before I write it I want somebody to check whether what I actually believe hangs together, because on paper it looks like a mess. here's roughly where I stand:

1. Meaning is what the author was trying to do. When a review of my second book called it a satire of academia when I was writing, in deadly earnest, a book about grief, my reaction was flat: that's a misreading. not an "interesting alternative lens," just wrong.

2. But. A critic once wrote an essay on my first book arguing the whole thing is structured around the narrator's inability to describe weather — she's always noting the sky and getting it slightly wrong — and I never once planned that. Reading the essay I kept going yes, yes, it's there. The pattern is real and it's about something, and I did not put it there on purpose. I can't bring myself to say she was wrong.

3. I don't think readers get to make a book mean whatever. If somebody told me my third book is an argument for eugenics I'd say no, full stop — that's not a reading — and I don't think I'd just be citing my intentions as one consideration among many. I think I'd be right that the text isn't doing that.

4. When I write, my characters do things I didn't plan. I've built an exit for a character and had her refuse it, and had to follow, and the book was better after. I take this seriously — it's not a metaphor for me being tired. What "better" is even tracking there is another thing I'm not sure about.

5. I've said in interviews that once a book is out it doesn't belong to me anymore, and I meant it. I don't correct readers. I'm not the authority on my own books in the way people assume authors are at parties.

6. I think the death-of-the-author people, when they're being sensible and not performative, are really just making point 5 in a loud voice, and I agree with them in practice but not with the slogan.

So: is this a consistent package? Tell me which pairs are actually pulling against each other, which only look like they are (I suspect 1 and 5 do, but I'm honestly not sure that's the right diagnosis, and I'd bet there are others Im not seeing at all), and for the real ones, what the cheapest fix is and what it costs me. I don't want a guided tour of the intentionalism debates — I want an audit of my list, ordered by how bad the tension is. And if the fix requires giving up my claim about the weather critic, say so plainly, because that's the one I'd protect.
