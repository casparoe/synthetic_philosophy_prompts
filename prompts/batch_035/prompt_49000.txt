Write a dialogue between two philosophers of mind who have just run a demonstration of the color phi phenomenon on themselves in a small lab. The phenomenon, for the setup: two stationary flashes of light are shown in quick succession at different positions — a red spot at the upper position, then, roughly a fifth of a second later, a green spot at the lower position. Subjects don't see two flashes. They see a single object moving from the upper to the lower position, changing color from red to green somewhere in between. The striking part is that the color change is experienced as occurring before the second flash ever appears: the subject seems to have seen green before green could have arrived at the eye.

One character — call her O — takes the Orwellian line: the subject's experience ran unedited, exactly as the world delivered it (first a stationary red spot, then a stationary green spot, no motion), and what got doctored was the record. Memory was retroactively rewritten after the fact, so the subject's report and later recollection misrepresent what experience was actually like when it happened. The other — call him S — takes the Stalinesque line: the brain holds the raw material back, edits the film before the screening, and only then delivers experience at all. The subject never had the unedited version, not even briefly; the motion and the early color change were inserted before the moment of experience. The two positions agree on every report every subject could ever give, and disagree about what happened on the inside.

Requirements for the dialogue:

- Both characters are experts and equally competent. Neither is a mouthpiece for a famous author, and neither is a strawman. Give O her strongest form of the worry that S's view, by construction, makes experience inaccessible to any check — since every probe of experience goes through memory or report, which is exactly what S says is downstream of editing. Give S his strongest reply, including the charge that O's position has the mirror-image problem: her "unedited experience" is equally beyond reach, since it leaves no trace that survives to the report.
- Real back-and-forth: each character should respond to the specific point the other just made, concede small things when pressed, and reframe rather than repeat. The dialogue should not resolve in a clean win for either side.
- At some point they should argue over whether the question is empirical or merely verbal — whether "the moment the experience happened" has a determinate answer that both views answer differently, or whether the two views are notational variants no experiment could distinguish.
- Have them stress-test each other's proposals for an experiment that might decide the matter. In particular, give them these two ideas to fight about (as their own inventions): (1) trials on which the second flash is sometimes unpredictably omitted, yet subjects still occasionally report apparent motion — O argues this tells against "editing before delivery," since the completed edit was apparently in place before the film's end arrived; S argues it only shows the editor can jump the gun and would have overwritten the record anyway; and (2) a speeded motor task, in which subjects press a key the instant they see any light at all, which O wants to treat as a window onto unedited processing and S wants to treat as just another output whose content need not match experience. Let each find the genuine weakness in the other's reading of the data, not a fake one.
- Somewhere in the exchange, let one of them raise the possibility that the whole dispute rests on there being a determinate "now" of experience to be timed, and let the other push back.

After the dialogue, append a short author's note (150–300 words) that separates what is empirically settled — that the illusory color change is experienced before the second color is presented, and the general fact of postdiction — from what is genuinely contested among people who know this literature: the interpretation of the Orwellian/Stalinesque contrast, whether the two views are observationally equivalent, and whether experience has a specifiable time of occurrence. Flag explicitly any point in the dialogue where a character asserts something that is contested rather than settled, and note where the dialogue leans on a debatable reading of an idea rather than on common ground. Do not let the note declare a winner.
