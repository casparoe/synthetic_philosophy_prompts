I'm not religious but I've gotten kind of obsessed with the question of why religion exists at all, everywhere, in every culture. The explanations you read are all natural stuff: fear of death, wanting the group to stick together, brains that over-detect agents in the bushes, parents teaching kids what their parents taught them. No god needed to explain why gods get believed in. That part I'm fine with.

But somewhere along the way I built an argument that's now eating everything I believe, and I can't find where it breaks:

(1) Every religious belief anyone holds was fully caused by that kind of natural stuff — psychology, culture, upbringing. Put the same person in a different culture and they'd have believed that culture's religion instead. So they'd have believed it whether or not it was true.
(2) If you would have believed something no matter whether it was true, then holding it now isn't rational — the thing that caused your belief had nothing to do with whether it's true.
(3) So nobody is rational in holding religious beliefs.

I was nodding along until I noticed the same reasoning applies to literally everything:

(4) Everything I believe — that antibiotics work, that the earth goes around the sun, even my belief that this argument proves things — was caused by the same natural stuff. My brain was built by evolution, my beliefs were poured into me by one particular culture and particular teachers, and if I'd been born somewhere else I'd believe that place's version of medicine and science instead. I never chose any of it.
(5) By the same reasoning as (2) and (3), I'm not rational in believing any of it, including this argument itself.

And that can't be right. If the argument devours science and then itself, it proves to much. But I've gone through it step by step and each one looks fine on its own. My gut says the religion case and the science case are somehow different, but whenever I try to say what the difference is, all I come up with is "science is true and religion isn't" — which is exactly what the argument says I have no right to say.

So where does it go wrong? For what it's worth, I'd really like to keep (1) — the natural explanation of religion seems solid to me — and obviously I'd like to keep trusting science and my own eyes. Lead with the step you'd reject and why, then tell me what rejecting it costs me. Under 400 words please, I've reread this enough times already without a wall of text making it worse.
