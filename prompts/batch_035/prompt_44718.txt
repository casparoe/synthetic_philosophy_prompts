I'm a third-year student taking a seminar on rational choice and value theory, and there's a recurring situation we keep circling that I don't have a clean label for. When I compare two options that differ along several dimensions — say, two job offers, one better paid and more intellectually stimulating, the other offering more time with people I care about and work I think matters more — I find myself in a state that doesn't fit any of the categories I've been handed. It isn't indifference, because indifference suggests the options are exactly equal, the way two identical bus routes to the same place are equal; here the options are wildly different, and the difference is all there is to see. It isn't incomparability either, or at least not the strong kind people describe when they say you can't trade off a friendship against a sum of money: I can rule out a third offer that is worse than both on every dimension, and if one of my two offers suddenly came with a much larger salary, everything would change and one would be plainly better. So the options seem comparable enough for some comparisons but not for the one I actually need. And it isn't a tie that more information could break, because I'm not uncertain about any of the facts.

What I'd want from an answer is this: is there an established concept for this middle condition, where two options are neither exactly equal nor incommensurable in the strong sense, and where small improvements can tip things while the current amounts do not? If there is a name, I'd like to know how its defenders distinguish it from mere rough equality or from vagueness in our judgments, since that's where my classmates keep accusing me of hedging. If there isn't, or if the established accounts disagree about whether the condition is real, please say so and then coin a term anyway, with a definition, and tell me which cases fall inside it and which boundary cases fall out — I'm thinking of cases where one option is better by a whisker, and whether those belong on the same map as my two job offers.
