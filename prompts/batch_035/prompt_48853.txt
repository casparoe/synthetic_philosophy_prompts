been reading forum fights about this for years, never studied any of it formally, so just thinking out loud here. everyone knows the stock line that heritability doesnt mean fixed or unchangeable — same genes, different enviroment, different outcome, fine. but heres the part i never see anybody say, and i want you to argue for it as hard as you can: a heritability estimate is really a fact about the environments, not the genes. my reasoning: h-squared is a ratio, genetic contribution to variation divided by total variation. if the environment side of that denominator gets squeezed toward zero — everyone eats iodized salt, everyone goes to school, everyone gets the same vaccines, basically one big shared whelping box — then even small genetic differences become the whole story and h² shoots up. so "this trait is 80% heritable in this country" reads to me like a report card on how uniform the living conditions already are, not like a deep fact about the trait itself. and it should cut both ways: places with more uneven, crappier environments should show LOWER heritability for the exact same trait with the exact same genes, which is a testable-sounding claim. argue for this position — that a heritability coefficient is better understood as an index of environmental sameness, and that quoting a bare number without saying which environmental regime its indexed to is close to meaningless — and please take on the strongest pushbacks you can think of, especially: the identical twins separated at birth who still end up similar (does that show genes winning even across different environments, or is the range of adoptive homes out there still pretty narrow?), and the reply that the same genes produce roughly the same trait in wildly different environments anyway, so flattening environments changes nothing. and then tell me where my whole framing breaks down — im especially fuzzy on whether genetic and environmental variation can even be kept apart cleanly enough for the math to mean what i want it to mean, like when genes partly determine which environments you go hunting for yourself, does that count as gene or environment or neither. be honest if my idea is half-baked, but make the best version of the argument first.
