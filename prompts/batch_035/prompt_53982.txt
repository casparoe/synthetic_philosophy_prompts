I've been reading about counterpart theory as an alternative to transworld identity in modal metaphysics, and it seems to me that the theory undermines itself. As I understand it, Lewis holds that individuals are worldbound — that I exist only in this world — so that when we say I could have become a carpenter, what's strictly true is that in some other world there is someone resembling me, a counterpart of mine, who is a carpenter. But surely the claim I actually care about is that I could have been a carpenter, not that a near-lookalike elsewhere happens to be one. Indeed, Lewis himself remarks somewhere that Humphrey, having lost the election, is supposed to take comfort in the fact that a counterpart of his wins in some other world — which sounds like adding insult to injury rather than consolation. If so, counterpart theory fails at the very job it was designed for: giving an account of de re modality. Since nothing that happens in other worlds happens to me, my "possibilities" are really just facts about strangers who resemble me. Doesn't that make the whole theory self-defeating, and doesn't it show that we should prefer transworld identity after all?
