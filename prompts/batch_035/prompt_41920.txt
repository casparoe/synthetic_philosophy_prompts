I am a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and my supervisor has asked me to circulate a short statement of my position on phenomenal consciousness ahead of our next supervision, so that the others in the group can push back on it. Before I send it, I would like a proper audit of whether the commitments actually cohere. Here is the set:

(1) The knowledge argument is sound. When Mary, the colour scientist who knows all the physical facts about vision but has lived her whole life in a black-and-white room, first steps outside and sees red, she learns a new fact — a fact about what it is like to see red. So phenomenal facts are not physical facts, and I count myself a property dualist.

(2) The physical domain is causally closed: every physical event that has a cause at all has a sufficient physical cause.

(3) I take it to follow from (1) and (2) that qualia are epiphenomenal. Brain processes cause them, but they cause nothing in turn — not behaviour, not speech, not belief.

(4) Introspection is a genuine information channel from experience to judgement. When I sincerely judge that this experience is one of red rather than green, that judgement is caused by the phenomenal character of the experience itself; I know I am in pain because the pain makes me judge that I am.

(5) First-person authority: I cannot be mistaken about my own concurrent phenomenal states. If I judge that I am currently having a visual experience as of red, then I am.

(6) Two further commitments I regard as well supported. First, our capacities for detecting and reporting our own conscious states are products of natural selection, and must therefore have been useful — they must track something that mattered for the fitness of our ancestors. Second, confabulation cases — split-brain patients confidently explaining choices they did not consciously initiate, or patients with cortical blindness who sincerely insist that they can see — show that sincere reports can come apart from the subject's actual phenomenal states.

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only appear to be. Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them all as equally damaging; for each genuine one, say precisely which commitment would have to give and what the revision costs elsewhere in the set. Finally, tell me which single revision, if any, would clean up the most. I am not looking for a survey of the field or for reading suggestions — what I want is an audit of this particular set. Please write your answer in British English.
