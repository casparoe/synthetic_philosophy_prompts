I'd like a probability estimate, with the reasoning decomposed, on a methodological question in the science of consciousness.

The proposition: that heterophenomenology, strictly practiced, is a neutral method — that adopting it commits a researcher to nothing substantive about the nature of consciousness, and in particular does not tilt the field toward deflationary or functionalist views over realism about phenomenal experience. Dennett presented the method as pure neutrality: transcribe the subject's reports, interpret them as you would a literary text, and bracket the question of whether the experiences described are real. Critics have long replied that this bracketing is not neutrality but a quiet verdict — that treating the subject's world as a fiction to be interpreted already amounts to a kind of denial.

My credence that the method is genuinely neutral is about 0.45. A colleague of mine who works on introspection puts it at 0.15. Give me your own number, decomposed into terms you choose, but I want you to at least address the following, and to add whatever further terms you think belong:

1. The interpretation problem. The step where the researcher interprets the transcript — choosing between more and less charitable readings, between coherent and incoherent ones — how likely is it that this step can be executed without background assumptions about what minds are like that themselves favor some theories of consciousness over others?

2. The accuracy problem. A completed heterophenomenological science has to say when a subject's report is inaccurate — confabulation, change blindness, visual masking. Each such criterion is a substantive empirical claim. How likely is it that these criteria can be established in a theory-neutral way, given that the experiments needed to calibrate them presuppose some way of separating genuine experience from merely reported experience?

3. A problem I haven't seen discussed under any heading, so tell me if it has a name: contamination. Subjects in consciousness experiments increasingly arrive pre-loaded — long-term meditators, people who have read popular neuroscience, and language models trained on corpora saturated with phenomenological vocabulary. Their reports are shaped by the very theories under test. Does heterophenomenology have internal resources for handling theory-laden data, or does the fix — recruiting naive subjects, or explicitly modeling the contamination — import extra commitments that destroy the neutrality it was meant to secure?

4. Whether neutrality is even the right desideratum. Perhaps a method in this domain can be epistemically good precisely because it is not neutral. How likely is it that the neutrality question, once pressed, turns out to be badly posed — that the interesting question is not whether the method tilts the field but whether it tilts it in a direction that is independently justified?

For each term give a rough probability and a one-line justification, combine them into your final credence, and identify which term the final number is most sensitive to. Then, given that my colleague and I sit at 0.45 and 0.15, tell me which term most likely contains our actual disagreement, and name the single piece of evidence or argument that would most move your number.
