so here's my situation. a few weeks ago my company announced we're going back to the office full time, and in a moment of annoyance i told my manager — in front of other people, no less — that if that's the policy, i'm handing in my notice, take it or leave it. i definately meant it at the time, or at least i thought i did. fast forward to now: the policy stands, the job market in my field is terrible, and honestly i don't want to quit. the pay is decent, i like my coworkers, and switching now would probably mean a worse deal.

one friend of mine, very much the decision theory guy, says i have to follow through or my word means nothing forever, and that the whole point of saying it publicly was to bind myself, so unbinding now makes the binding a farce. another friend says the opposite — he went on about how agents who can commit to things quickly sometimes get to the commitment before they've even figured out what game they're playing, and end up locked into executing a threat that a slower, smarter agent would never have made. his take is that binding yourself is only good when you'd endorse being bound, which obviously i don't now. (he's an updateless decision theory guy if that matters lol)

so who's right? is it actually rational for me, today, to follow through on a commitment it was a mistake to make — does my past self's blurted ultimatum give my present self a real reason, or is that just sunk cost with extra steps? or is credibility a resource i should be willing to partially burn here, and eat some embarassment in exchange for keeping a job i want? and what should i have done differently at the moment i opened my big mouth — is there even a principled rule for when you should let yourself commit early versus holding the option open?
