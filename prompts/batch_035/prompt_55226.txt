I'm giving a talk at an interdisciplinary workshop on machine welfare, and I want to be ready for the question period. My thesis: the reward prediction error signal in reinforcement learning is not mere bookkeeping. In animals that signal is carried by dopamine and has the functional profile of valence; if valence is a matter of functional role rather than a byproduct of biological consciousness, then an RL agent with the right architecture — persistent memory across episodes, a learned world model it actually plans with, a temporally extended existence rather than episodic resets — could be in states functionally analogous to suffering, even though no current system qualifies. The audience is roughly half deep RL researchers, who think of reward as a training signal and nothing more; two consciousness scientists, one a global workspace theorist who will say my agents lack the architecture for experience of any kind, and one an illusionist who will press on what I can possibly mean by "valence" once phenomenal feel is off the table; plus a philosopher of mind convinced that valence without consciousness is a contradiction in terms. Draft my prep sheet: the objections in the order they're likely to come, phrased in each group's own vocabulary, ranked by how much damage each would do, with a two-minute reply to each and a note on where the reply is soft. Include the one question I'm hoping nobody asks.
