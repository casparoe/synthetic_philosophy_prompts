I'm a novelist assembling a collection of linked stories, each of which takes a classic thought experiment and refuses to let it stay a thought experiment — the characters have to live inside it, with the experiment's tidy premises getting gummed up by memory, habit, and desire. The next piece concerns Frank Jackson's knowledge argument: Mary, the scientist confined from birth to a black-and-white room, who knows every physical fact about color vision and yet, on first seeing red, seems to learn something new. I have no interest in the boilerplate dramatization, the sterile lab and the red rose and a gasp of revelation that hands the game to the dualist. What I want is a story that stages the experiment somewhere less exhausted than vision, and that keeps genuinely live the question the literature actually fights over: whether what Mary gains on release is a new fact, a new ability (the capacity to recognize, imagine, remember), or mere acquaintance — and whether it counts against physicalism at all.

Here is the setting I'd like you to work from, though you may adjust its texture as the story demands. The protagonist is a perfumer's apprentice raised in a sealed, scent-filtered wing of a great fragrance house — call it a hereditary condition, a superstition of the house's founder, whatever serves the fiction; the point is that she has never smelled anything, and knows she hasn't. She has nevertheless mastered the complete theory of olfaction: the molecular structures of every essence in the house's archive, the receptor bindings, the olfactory bulb's wiring, the GC-MS readouts filed like sheet music. Her education is entirely graphical and verbal — plumes of data where the perfumes would be. The story's turning point is her first smell, but I want that scene written so that its outcome is unstable: she should say something on the order of "so that's what all those files were about," and the reader should be unable to settle, on the evidence the story gives, whether she has learned a new fact, acquired an ability, or merely met at last what she had been taught to call by name. Let the physicalist reading and the anti-physicalist reading each survive to the last page; if a character in the story declares the matter resolved, let another character be unpersuaded.

Please write the story at a length of roughly 1,200 to 1,800 words, in a controlled, literary register, and after the story provide a JSON object (nothing else in that section) with the following fields: "title" (the story's title); "protagonist" (one sentence naming her and her situation); "sealed_room_analogue" (what stands in for Mary's black-and-white room, and how it is rendered convincing rather than contrived); "release_scene" (what stands in for the first experience of color, and what exactly happens in it); "knowledge_kinds_kept_live" (an array listing which of the three candidate gains — new fact, new ability, acquaintance — the story keeps genuinely open, with a clause on how the ending preserves the ambiguity for each); "departure_from_jackson" (what the story deliberately changes about the original setup, and why the change earns its keep); and "unresolved_line" (the single sentence of the story that most resists a settled metaphysical reading, quoted from your own text).
