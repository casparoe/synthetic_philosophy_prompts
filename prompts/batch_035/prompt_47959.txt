My friend Dana and I spent most of last night arguing about an offer she has made me, and I would appreciate help thinking it through, because I cannot tell whether my intuition is sound or just an aversion to being outmaneuvered.

Dana has an unusual hobby: for years she has kept records of decisions made by people in our friend group — whether they would renege on a bet, whether they would show up to things, whether they would take a sure small gain over a risky larger one — and she has used them to build a track record of predicting how each of us chooses. Of the roughly twenty-five calls she has logged on me over the years, she has been wrong twice. Last month she made me the following offer. There are two envelopes. One is transparent and contains $200. The other is opaque, and Dana sealed it a week ago: it contains $20,000 if her model predicted that I would take only the opaque envelope, and nothing if it predicted that I would take both. It is already sealed, and I have no way to inspect its contents.

Dana argues that I should obviously take both: whatever is in the opaque envelope is already in there, my choice this afternoon cannot change it, and taking both therefore gets me at least as much in every case — the extra $200 is simply free. My instinct is to take only the opaque envelope, because given her record, the envelope almost certainly contains $20,000 if and only if I am the kind of person who takes just it. But Dana pressed a point I could not answer: her model was built from my past behavior — habits, how I settled earlier wagers, a questionnaire I filled out three years ago — not from anything magical about this afternoon. So is my one-envelope instinct genuinely tracking evidence about what is already sealed in there, or am I confusing acting like a person whose envelope is full with somehow making the envelope full?

Two further wrinkles from our argument. Dana pointed out that if I asked her to flip a coin and let it decide for me, her model would have anticipated my asking — she says coin-flipping is itself a personality trait she has me down for. And I want to know whether it matters that the prediction concerns my long-run disposition rather than this specific choice: if I have changed since she built the model, does that strengthen her case, weaken mine, or cut both ways?

Please tell me who was right, whether the coin flip is a way out or a way to lose, and what I should actually do when she hands me the envelopes.
