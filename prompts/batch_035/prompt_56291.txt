My friend Dev has long-running opinions about the "would that still have been me?" question — you know, the sperm-and-egg one. His verdicts over the years, all about himself:

- Still him even if he'd been born a month early, by C-section, in a totally different hospital.
- Still him even if he'd been IVF, with the embryo frozen for two years before implantation, and gestated by a surrogate.
- Still him even if he'd been adopted at birth and raised by some other family entirely.
- Not him if he'd been conceived a week later by his actual parents (so, different egg, different sperm) — that would've been some sibling of his, a different kid.
- Not him if his dad's very sperm had fertilized a different egg of his mom's that same month (the fraternal twins setup) — that's the twin, not him.
- Not him if his genetic parents had been a different couple altogether — that person is just someone else.
- On the identical twin version (his embryo splitting in two): one of the two would have been him, and theres no fact of the matter wich.

He also once mused, maybe half joking, that the egg is what really matters because there's exactly one of it and it just sat there for decades waiting for you, whereas sperm are a dime a dozen and basically interchangeable. He might have been serious. Hard to say with him.
