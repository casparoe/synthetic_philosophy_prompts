You're a professional metaphysician specializing in personal identity, and you know Parfit's Reasons and Persons backwards and forwards. I need you to design a thought experiment for me, so please think carefully and take the constraints seriously — a lot of people botch this by smuggling in the answer.

Here's the setup. In Parfit's famous teletransporter cases, the punchline always runs in one direction: identity and what-matters come apart such that Parfit gets to say identity isn't what really matters — either the person survives but nothing of value survives with them, or everything that matters carries over while identity gets indeterminate or lost (the fission stuff, the branch line case, the combined spectrum). What I want is a case that runs the machinery in the opposite direction: a scenario where the psychological continuity theorist and the animalist / bodily continuity theorist *agree* that the person survives — full stop, no indeterminacy, everyone's happy on the identity question — but where Parfit's own account of what matters in survival delivers the verdict that nothing of value was preserved. Survival without what-matters-surviving, but generated from within his framework rather than from the identity debates.

Constraints:

- The scenario itself should be one paragraph, and the only science-fictional device allowed is the teletransporter. No demons, no perfect predictors, no fission, no duplicates anywhere. Each transport must be strictly one-to-one.
- The "what matters is lost" verdict has to fall out of Parfit's actual distinction between psychological connectedness and psychological continuity — don't just stipulate that being transported is bad or tragic, and no cheating with pain, disability, or the transported person turning out dysfunctional. They wake up fine, employed, healthy, still recognizably a person with a life.
- Don't stipulate the verdicts. The case has to earn them: I should be able to run each theory over the scenario as described and get the specified answer. If a smart reader could contest the psychological continuity verdict just from the plain facts of the scenario, you've cheated.

This is for teaching, by the way — I want to show students that Parfit's what-matters analysis can separate from the identity questions in both directions, not just the famous one, so what counts as cheating is anything that just relabels an identity dispute as a what-matters dispute.

After the scenario: walk through what the psychological continuity theorist says, what the animalist says, and what Parfit's what-matters analysis says, and explain why the first two line up against the third. Then be honest with me about which single feature of the case is doing all the work, what happens to each verdict if you remove that feature, and how Parfit would counterattack — is there some move that lets him say what matters IS preserved, and if he makes it, does he have to give up anything else he's committed to, like his treatment of fission or the combined spectrum?

Start with a short brainstorm before you commit — a couple of candidate scenarios and why you're rejecting them. I'd rather see the wreckage of two bad ideas than have you pretend your first design was inevitable.
