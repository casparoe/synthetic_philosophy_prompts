Please explain the distinction, common in the philosophical literature on Bell's theorem after Jarrett's analysis, between parameter independence and outcome independence as assumptions on the joint probabilities of outcomes in an EPR/Bell experiment, and why the two failures are usually thought to differ in cost: a failure of outcome independence (which quantum mechanics itself exhibits for entangled pairs) is often described as a kind of holism about the pair, whereas a failure of parameter independence (which deterministic hidden-variable theories such as Bohm's exhibit at the level of the complete state) is usually taken to amount to action at a distance. In particular, I want to understand (i) how each condition is stated as a factorization condition on the probabilities, (ii) how a hidden-variable theory can violate one without violating the other, (iii) how violations at the level of the hidden variables remain compatible with the no-signaling theorem, so that nonlocality is real but unexploitable, and (iv) whether the asymmetry between the two conditions is robust, given the complaint that the genuine assumption behind Bell's inequalities is the full factorization, and that carving its failure into a benign part and a spooky part may be an artifact of the decomposition rather than a fact about nature. Concrete probability tables for a simple case would help more than formalism.
