Got into a long argument with my roommate last night about how bad practices actually die, and now I can't stop thinking about one piece of it. We started on dueling (a period drama got us there somehow) and ended up on footbinding and abolition. He was on the "moral arguments win eventually" side — the abolitionists made the case, Wilberforce and all that, people finally heard reason and the thing ended. My side was: the arguments against dueling were ancient. Clergy had been denouncing it for two centuries, everyone already granted that shooting each other over card games was stupid, and nothing changed. Then within a couple of generations it's just dead. Nobody heard a new argument, because there wasn't one to hear. What shifted, as far as I can tell, is that the practice stopped being a way of showing you were a man of honor and started being a way of showing you were a ridiculous hothead. The words used against it stayed the same, and suddenly they landed.

So the pattern I'm trying to pin down: an argument whose premises everyone already accepts sits around doing basically nothing for generations, and then flips an entire society in a few decades without a single new premise being added. The causal work seems to be done by what the practice *signals* changing — from honor to embarassment — and the old argument is more like a license everyone kept in a drawer until it became socially safe to invoke. My roommate says that's just the Overton window moving, but that seems to be about what people are willing to say out loud, not about what the words actually do once said. I thought of "moral panic" too, but that runs in the opposite direction — a spiral up, not a collapse.

Is there an established term in philosophy or sociology for this specific thing — the moment an old, already-accepted argument stops being inert and starts working, not because the argument got better but because the status economy around the practice collapsed? If there's a real name I'll take it, but honestly I'm more interested in the carving than the label: if the closest concepts are things like norm cascades or whatever the literature calls a custom dying of ridicule, spell out how each one differs from what I'm describing, since to me the interesting part is that the argument seems to do no causal work at all and yet gets all the credit afterwards. And if nothing established fits, coin a term for it, give a definition, and tell me where the edges are — e.g., does it still count when the practice was formally illegal for ages before the social meaning flipped (dueling was), or is legal change a seperate category from the signal flip? And does it only apply to practices of the high-status people doing the signaling, or also to things like footbinding where the practitioners weren't the ones whose opinion moved?
