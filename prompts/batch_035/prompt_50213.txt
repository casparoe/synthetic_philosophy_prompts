My friend Dana has strong views about diachronic rationality, and I've been keeping a list of her verdicts on cases involving money pumps. The recurring agent has strictly cyclic preferences — she prefers A to B, B to C, and C to A — with gaps small enough that she would pay a small fee to move to whichever option she prefers at that point in the circle, so all the pumps have the familiar shape: a sequence of pairwise-appealing trades that returns her to her starting option a few dollars poorer. Dana's verdicts so far: (1) an agent who takes every offered trade and walks the full circle is irrational; (2) an agent who is ever offered only one leg of the circle — no way back around — does nothing irrational in taking it, and Dana insists the cyclic preferences themselves needn't be fixed so long as no one can complete a pump on her; (3) an agent who, Dana stipulates, cannot turn down a trade she prefers considered on its own, but who buys a binding commitment device in advance for less than the pump would cost her, is rational to buy it; (4) an agent with the willpower to simply refuse the first trade when she sees the circle coming, and who does refuse it, is rational. Tomorrow Dana hears a new case: an agent who must now choose between Option One, a decent guaranteed outcome, and Option Two, which puts her next week in a position to choose between an excellent outcome requiring real effort and a so-so outcome requiring none, with the so-so outcome slightly worse than Option One's guarantee. She knows that come next week — no commitment device being available — she would take the so-so outcome. Will Dana say that entering Option Two is rational? Please structure your answer as follows: "Prediction" (2–3 sentences on what Dana will say and how confident you are); "Candidate rules" (one short paragraph each, for at least two rules that fit all four of her verdicts so far); "Best guess" (one paragraph on which rule Dana is most plausibly running, and why); and "The case that would settle it" (2–4 sentences describing a further case, different from tomorrow's, on which those rules diverge, and what each rule would predict about it).
