I'm a backend engineer at a mid-size company and somehow got roped into a "philosophy for tech people" reading group that my coworker started (long story, it was either this or a pickleball league). This month it's Max Weber's Protestant Ethic book, and I actually kind of got into it -- the idea that a religious belief system accidentally produced modern capitalism by making people feel guilty about rest and idolizing constant disciplined work really clicked for me, because honestly it describes like half my coworkers.

But here's what I keep chewing on and I'd love your take. The group got into a huge argument about what Weber would say if he could see the current state of things. On one side you've got what some people call "workism" or hustle culture: people who track their sleep with rings, use apps that gamify streaks of deep work, do "dopamine detoxes," treat 5am cold plunge routines like a moral achievement, and feel genuine shame about "wasting" a weekend. There's basically a whole secular religion of productivity out there now -- guilt about idleness, sanctified suffering now for payoff later, even thinly-veiled predestination vibes where being successful is read as evidence you're one of the elect (the grindset people really do talk like being rich means you deserved it). My friend says Weber would see this as his thesis vindicated and even extended: the religious content fell away but the psychological machinery of inner-worldly asceticism kept running on its own, like a process that outlived its original purpose.

The other side of the group says that's exactly backwards -- Weber's whole point was that culture was a cause and not just a reflection of economic conditions, but modern productivity culture looks more like something the economy (gig platforms, always-on Slack, burnout metrics, tech layoffs) *imposes* on people from outside, and the spiritual language is just branding people put on top of exploitation they didn't choose. On that reading, Weber would say the modern stuff proves his critics right, not him. And a third guy mumbled something about how Weber would probably just be annoyed that everyone uses "Protestant work ethic" as a slogan without caring about his actual argument. So: if Weber could observe 2020s hustle culture up close -- the productivity influencers, the quantified-self people, the burnout discourse, quiet quitting as backlash -- what do you think he'd actually conclude? Would he see his culture-as-cause story strengthened, weakened, or needing some major rework? I'd especially love thoughts on what he'd make of the fact that a lot of this stuff is explicitly *not* about afterlife rewards anymore -- the payoff is supposed to be freedom, early retirement, "escaping the rat race" -- which seems like it changes the psychology in a way his original framework maybe didn't anticipate.
