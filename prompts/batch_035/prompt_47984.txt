I'm a junior taking econ this year and we just covered the sunk cost fallacy, and I get the textbook version fine (the money's spent either way, only future costs and benefits should matter, blah blah). But then I started going through my actual gut reactions to a bunch of cases and they don't line up neatly at all, so now I want to know if there's some principle that captures all of my verdicts or whether I'm just inconsistent. Here are the cases: I pay $15 for a movie and 20 minutes in I can tell it's terrible — leaving is obviously right, staying is dumb. My parents stay through a rain-delayed fireworks show because the tickets were expensive and nonrefundable — honestly this seems fine to me, even though it looks like the same move as the movie one?? Staying in a five year relationship that's just okay because five years is alot to throw away — feels like a clear mistake, classic sunk cost stuff. Waiting 25 minutes for a bus that's super late — part of me feels like leaving now would make the 25 minutes pointless, and another part of me suspects that's exactly the fallacy and only how much longer the bus will take should matter.

But here's where I flip: my cousin is three years into a PhD, she's figured out she hates it, and my whole family says finish because you're so close, and I actually kind of agree with them, even though by the textbook the three years are sunk. So is there one principle here that says which of these honorings of sunk costs are rational and which are dumb? My tentative idea is maybe it has something to do with whether the sunk cost changes what the future looks like — like finishing a degree actually gets you a degree, whereas sitting through a bad movie gets you nothing except more of the bad movie — but I can't tell if that's a real distinction or just me rationalizing. Whatever principle you come up with, I want you to test it against the case I keep going back and forth on: I'm two years into learning saxophone and I kind of want to quit, and my mom says quitting would waste all that practice. Is she right or is that the fallacy? And also tell me honestly if you think one of my verdicts just has to go, like maybe the fireworks one.
