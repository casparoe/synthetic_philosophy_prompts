My friend Dan is deep into Nozick — he's read Anarchy, State, and Utopia like three times and brings up the entitlement theory at dinner parties. Over the years I've collected his verdicts on cases, partly for fun, and now I want to use the record to predict him on some new ones before I ask, since we have a long-running game of guessing each other's rulings. His record so far: taxing a chunk of Wilt Chamberlain's earnings to house the long-term unemployed is flatly wrong, and he says it's the same kind of thing as forcing someone to work for another person's benefit; ordinary taxes to fund police, courts, prisons, and the army are fine; forcing a con man to repay the pensioners he swindled is not just fine but required; tax subsidies for public broadcasting and the opera are redistribution and wrong; making a factory pay to clean up the river it poisoned is fine, that's just undoing a rights violation. That's the whole record — he refuses to give me anything more abstract than verdicts on cases, which is kind of the point of the game.

The new cases, mostly things actually on the agenda of the town council I sit on: (1) after last month's hurricane, an emergency levy on everyone's earnings to feed and shelter families whose homes were destroyed — note nobody violated anybody's rights here, it was the weather; (2) a permanent levy to fund the volunteer fire brigade; (3) not council business but it comes up whenever he's around: a special tax on some currently wealthy families to compensate descendants of families whose land was seized about 150 years ago, where the current owners bought in good faith at full market price several generations back. What will Dan say about each of the three? I care less about the answers than the rules underneath: I can see at least two principles that fit his whole record — one on which takings are only okay to fund protection against, and rectification of, rights violations by persons; and one on which protection against serious threats to life and property counts whatever the source of the threat — and there are probably others I havent thought of, especially about how far back rectification can reach.

So: lay out the candidate rules that fit the record, tell me which pairs of them come apart on which of the three cases (I suspect at least one pair splits between the hurricane and the fire brigade, of all things), give your best guess for each case with your reasons, and then tell me what further case — not one of the three — would do the most to settle which rule he's actually running. Fair warning: he's never once ruled in a way that surprised me, so I expect this to be doable, but he does have a soft spot for disaster victims he'd never admit to in theory, so dont be shocked if the record underdetermines the man as well as the rules.
