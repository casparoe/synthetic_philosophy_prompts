so my roommate is a straight-up Mill-style utilitarian, and after months of arguments he's demanded I actually write down where I stand instead of just objecting to his views. Now that it's on paper I'm not sure my own list makes sense. Here it is roughly: (1) I think happiness is genuinely good, so a world full of happy people and no suffering is better than an empty world — I'm not one of those negative utilitarians who thinks only suffering matters. (2) But I do think extreme suffering has a kind of priority that can't be traded off: no amount of added happiness, however much, makes up for a single instance of torture. It's not just that suffering weighs heavier, it's that at some intensity it stops being comensurable with happiness at all. (3) Below that extreme level I'm a normal aggregator — lots of small pleasures can outweigh a bad day, and I'm fine with utilitarian tradeoffs in everyday ethics. (4) I also accept the procreative asymmetry: bringing into existence a child whose life will be full of suffering is wrong in a way that bringing into existence a blissfull child is not obligatory — nobody wrongs anyone by not creating a happy person. (5) And on population: I think adding lives with lots of happiness and little suffering makes the world better, impersonally, even if those lives affect no one who currently exists. (6) Finally, I think a quick painless death is a small harm compared to a life of chronic suffering, so for an animal raised in misery the slaughter is nearly the least bad part — though I still think factory farming is one of the worst institutions humans run.

The question: does this hang together? My roommate says the lexical thing in (2) plus everyday aggregation in (3) already commits me to saying a world containing one torture victim and a billion happy lives can't be improved by adding more happy lives — since the torture stays put — which he claims quietly collapses (3) into the negative utilitarianism I rejected in (1). And he insists (4) and (5) can't both be true without some weird asymmetry about merely possible people that will come back to bite me. I suspect he's cheating somewhere, because it feels like plenty of people hold most of these views at once, but I can't put my finger on where the trick is.
