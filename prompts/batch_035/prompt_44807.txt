He insists the second is obligatory, because its expected value works out to roughly a thousand lives rather than a hundred, even though it is overwhelmingly likely to save no one at all. I've since learned that this commitment of expected-value maximization — always take the higher expectation, no matter how the expectation is generated — is what philosophers in this literature call fanaticism. My teacher describes it as simply doing the arithmetic.
