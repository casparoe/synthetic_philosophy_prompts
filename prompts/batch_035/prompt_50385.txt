I've been asked to circulate a short position statement in advance of a workshop on self-knowledge, and before I send it to the other participants I want an audit of whether my views actually fit together. The topic is the reliability of introspection, and here is roughly where I stand:

1. Careful, sincere, present-tense reports about the character of one's own ongoing experience are the primary data in phenomenological disputes. If an attentive subject reports that her peripheral visual experience is not richly detailed, that report starts out with substantial evidential weight, and the burden falls on any theory that contradicts it. I want this to apply across the board: to the debate over whether thought has its own phenomenology, to questions about the experience of agency, and so on.

2. The confabulation findings do not threaten (1), or so I've argued to myself. Subjects confidently explaining choices they never actually made, rationalizing behavior they were hypnotically induced to perform, preferring the rightmost of four identical pairs of stockings while insisting it was superior in quality — those findings concern the causes and sources of our states, not the occurrent phenomenal character a subject is reporting right now.

3. At the same time, I accept the accumulating evidence that we misreport occurrent character itself: people describe vivid, picture-like detail across the whole visual field even though their performance on peripheral tasks shows the underlying representation is sparse; dream reports about color and imagery shift with how the questions are asked and, apparently, with the prevailing media environment; two attentive subjects thinking about the same thing will disagree about whether there was any imagery in their thought at all. So sincere introspective reports of current experience can be substantially false, and — worse — the subject typically cannot tell from the inside when this is happening.

4. On knowledge of our own beliefs and most other propositional attitudes, I hold a transparency view: you find out whether you believe that p by considering whether p, not by detecting an inner state. Inward inspection is simply not doing the work there.

5. On the avowal asymmetry — why my present-tense sincere claim that I am in pain has an authority my claim about your pain lacks — I hold a constitutive or expressivist line: such avowals express the state they are about rather than reporting it on the basis of evidence, and that is what explains their special authority.

My working sense is that (2) is what insulates (1) from the empirical literature, and that (4) and (5) are complementary halves of a single story about self-knowledge. But I have doubts, and I honestly cannot tell which of these I would give up first. Go through the set and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and how badly, which pairs only look to be in tension, what the cheapest repair of each genuine tension would be and what it would cost me elsewhere, and which single revision would clean up the most.
