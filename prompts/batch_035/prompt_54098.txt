I've been trying to work out, from my gut reactions to a series of cases, what exactly I think is bad about human extinction — as opposed to the deaths it happens to involve — and I'd like help finding the principle, if there is one, that captures my verdicts, along with an honest account of which of them no principle can save. For context: I'm in my forties, I volunteer with a conservation group on weekends, and this all came up over dinner after a friend described a thought experiment about a comet. My verdicts, such as they are: my own death is clearly bad, but bad in a bounded, ordinary way — bad for me, bad for the people who love me, and that is roughly the end of it. Learning that humanity will end about thirty years after the last person currently alive has died frightens me far more than my own death does, and in a different register — not as an accumulation of deaths, which nobody now living would suffer, but as a kind of finality or bleakness hanging over everything. The same scenario moved a thousand years into the future, with billions more people living full lives in between, is considerably less disturbing to me, though it involves vastly more deaths, which I find embarrassing to admit. A comet sterilising the planet next Tuesday is worse for me to contemplate than a slow collapse over three centuries, even though it involves less total suffering along the way.

Two more, which I think expose me. If the thirty-years scenario were true, my conservation work on a local wetland — which would, in that scenario, flourish undisturbed for those thirty years — would suddenly feel pointless to me, and I can't explain why, since the wetland does just as well either way. And an advanced alien civilisation flourishing for a million years after our extinction gives me no comfort whatsoever, while a galaxy settled by greatly modified descendants of ours gives me both some comfort and some unease, because I can't decide whether they still count as 'us'. My tentative principle is something like: what I care about is that there be a future in which the things people do now still make sense as contributions to something ongoing — but I can see this is too self-regarding, since I'm disturbed by the thirty-years scenario also on behalf of people whose projects will long since have been completed. So: is there one coherent principle behind all of this, which of my verdicts does it force me to abandon, and what does it predict for the case I keep wobbling on — a future in which humanity survives indefinitely but in a form barely recognisable as human? Please don't give me a reading list; I want the carving itself, worked out in the answer. And kindly write in British English.
