I've been a longtime reader of the rationalist blogosphere, and after rereading Hofstadter's superrationality material -- the idea that a rational player in a prisoners' dilemma should ask what all rational players would jointly choose, and then choose that, rather than defecting against one's own replica -- I keep wanting a serious case study rather than the usual toy examples. So: analyze the Montreal Protocol (1987) and the ozone regime it created through the lens of superrationality. Treat it as a many-player prisoners' dilemma over phasing out CFCs and be disciplined about the mapping: who are the players (states, chemical firms like DuPont, the negotiators as individual agents?), what the choice space actually was, and what "choosing as all rational agents choose" could even mean when the players were radically asymmetric -- DuPont had substitutes ready while other firms didn't, developing countries had contributed little to historical emissions and were being asked to forgo cheap refrigeration. Say whether the eventual near-universal ratification and compliance looks like superrational cooperation, or whether it's better explained by ordinary self-interest once the scientific evidence and substitute chemicals made cooperation cheap; and say what the Multilateral Fund's technology transfers do to the mapping -- do they dissolve the dilemma, or are they part of the superrational solution? Then run the analysis in both directions: what superrationality genuinely illuminates here, where it is silent or forced, and whether the episode supports the concept, embarrasses it, or just shows it was never needed for a case where the cooperative equilibrium was available anyway. After the analysis, append a JSON object with the following fields: "players" (list, with a one-line rationale for each), "strategy_space" (list), "superrationality_explains" (list of features of the episode it captures), "superrationality_misses" (list of features it misdescribes or ignores), "better_framing" (string, naming the model that does better if any), "verdict" (one of "supports", "undermines", "unclear", with a one-sentence justification), and "confidence" (float between 0 and 1).
