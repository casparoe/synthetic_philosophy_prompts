I'm a retired analytical chemist -- spent thirty-odd years running a lab, and now that I've got time on my hands I've been reading aesthetics of all things, which is a strange country for someone whose whole career was calibration curves. Something's been bugging me and I want a philosopher's take.

Here's the practice. Wine competitions. The judges taste blind -- bottles in numbered bags, flights randomized, the whole double-blind routine. The stated reason is that if a judge knew the producer or the price, it would bias the score. Meanwhile the entire point of the exercise is the medal: a little gold sticker that goes on the bottle, whose function is to tell thousands of consumers who will never taste blind (and often never taste at all before buying) that this wine is good. So the same institution spends enormous effort making sure the judge's judgment isn't contaminated by outside knowledge, and then turns around and treats that judgment as something you can just... hand to people. Secondhand. Like a lab report.

And there's a second layer that got me reading. In the wine world itself there's a line that gets repeated a lot, to the effect that your own palate is the only one that matters, that nobody can taste for you. So the culture seems to hold both views at once: aesthetic judgment is so fragile that even the judge must be shielded from the label, and aesthetic judgment is sturdy enough to survive being printed on a sticker and shipped to strangers.

So what I'd like: reconstruct for me what theory of aesthetic judgment -- or aesthetic knowledge, or whatever you want to call it -- this practice presupposes. Which rival views would it quietly rule out? For instance, the view that aesthetic verdicts can't be passed along by testimony at all, or conversely the view that knowing the producer is just part of tasting the wine, the way knowing the experimental setup is part of reading the data (my own instinct, honestly, having spent my life on the latter). Does the blind-tasting half and the medal-sticker half presuppose consistent things, or is there a genuine tension there? And where is the practice underdetermined -- that is, several theories of aesthetic judgment would each license the whole setup, so you can't argue from the practice to any one of them? Finally, if we replaced the implicit view with a hard "you must taste it yourself" line -- no stickers, no scores, no critics telling you anything before you buy -- what concretely would have to change in the industry?

One request more. I'm new to this field and can't always tell when I'm being handed the party line. Please keep separate what's actually settled among people who work on aesthetic testimony from what's genuinely contested, and if you present some particular theory as the obvious reading, flag whether it's the consensus or just one camp's view. I got burned enough times over my career by review articles that presented a contested mechanism as established fact, and I'd rather not repeat the experiance in a new field.
