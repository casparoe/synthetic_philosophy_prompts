I would like a probability estimate, with the reasoning fully decomposed, on the following proposition about Libet's readiness-potential experiments: in the original paradigm — subjects instructed to flex a wrist at a moment of their own choosing, spontaneously, while reporting afterward where a rapidly sweeping clock hand stood at the moment they first became aware of the urge — the averaged cortical negativity that begins roughly half a second before the movement reflects an unconscious process that has already initiated the act, so that the conscious intention, which arrives only about a fifth of a second before the movement, is not doing the causal work of starting it. Call this the initiation claim.

Please give me your credence that the initiation claim, as it applies to these tasks, is true, and then decompose it rather than asserting it. At minimum I would expect the following terms, though substitute a different carving if you think mine is wrong:

1. The probability that the early readiness potential in these averaged traces is a genuine decision signal, as against the stochastic-accumulator reinterpretation, on which it is largely the statistical residue of ongoing background fluctuation in motor cortex — a reinterpretation that would also explain why the pre-movement ramp is so much less pronounced when movements are time-locked to an external cue rather than averaged on movement onset itself.
2. The probability that the subject's W judgment — a memory-based report of where the clock hand stood when the urge arrived — actually tracks when the intention first became conscious, given what is known about the distortions of retrospective timing judgments and about the difference between attending to an urge and forming an intention.
3. The probability that the original interpretation survives the objection that these subjects had already decided, well before recording began, to flex at some point in the next few minutes, so that the decision the experiment needs to catch was made before the readiness potential ever started, and nothing occurring at minus 550 milliseconds bears on it.
4. A residual term for what the initiation claim would show even if the first three resolved in Libet's favor — for instance, whether the conscious veto window that Libet himself emphasized does enough work for agency that the threat to free will is less severe than advertised.

Assign each term a number with one line of justification, show how the terms combine into your final credence, and identify the term to which that final number is most sensitive. Finally, describe an experiment that could actually be run and that would most move your value on that sensitive term, and say in which direction you would expect it to move.
