I've been keeping a list of my gut calls about when a psych finding counts as "replicated" vs "dead" after a replication attempt, and I'd like to know if there's actually a principle under them or whether I'm just being inconsistent. Here's the list:

Power pose: original effect was huge, replications mostly nothing, I call it dead, even though some of those replications were powered to detect the *original* effect size and so couldn't really have detected a true effect at half that size. Ego depletion: same story, dead. A pre-registered direct replication that finds the effect at p=0.048 with roughly the original effect size: replicated, obviously. A replication that comes out in the same direction, at about half the original effect size, p=0.06: I want to call that a partial success rather than a failure, even though I don't trust a p=0.048 original much to begin with, and I can feel that these two attitudes don't fit together. A study that "replicates" with a tiny effect that's statistically significant only because n=5000: somehow I don't count that as a replication at all, it feels like a different finding. A meta-analysis where the effect shrinks study by study toward zero: dead. A Bayesian reanalysis showing the evidence favors the null 3:1: doesn't move me even slightly, which is embarassing because I'm generally sympathetic to Bayesians. And a conceptual replication that finds a downstream consequence of the effect using a totally different manipulation: I count that in favor of the finding more than a direct replication from a mediocre lab, which everyone tells me is exactly backwards.

My tentative principle is that I'm tracking "is the effect still big enough to care about in the original units" rather than "did p come in under .05". But that principle seems to say the n=5000 tiny-significant case counts as a replication, and I don't believe that. So either the principle needs surgery or some of my verdicts do. What's the cleanest principle that saves the most of these, which verdicts does it have to throw out, and what does it say about the case I genuinly can't place: huge original effect, replication run at 80% power for the original size, result comes in at exactly half the size with p=0.08, and the replication team had pre-registered a somewhat stricter exclusion rule than the original authors used?
