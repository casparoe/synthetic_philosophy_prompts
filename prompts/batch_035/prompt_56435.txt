My friend in our metaphysics reading group (we read Wiggins and Gibbard last term, the Lumpl and Goliath stuff) hit me with an argument last week and I havent found a way out that doesnt cost too much. Here it is, as he gives it:

(1) Last week the clay — Lumpl — sat in a box in the studio. The statue — Goliath — did not exist last week; nothing in that box was a statue yet. (2) If Goliath were identical to Lumpl, then by Leibniz's Law Goliath existed last week, since whatever is true of Lumpl is true of Goliath. (3) So Goliath and Lumpl are two objects, not one. (4) And they share all their physical properties — same atoms, same shape, same spot — and, he insists, the same causal powers, since theres no room between two things in the same place made of the same stuff for an extra event; anything either could cause, the other causes just as well. (5) The same pattern runs on me and my body: my body could outlast me — kept going by machines long after everything that matters about me is gone — and I cant outlast that, so by the same reasoning I am not my body. (6) I share all my physical states with my body. (7) Thinking is being in a physical state. (8) So every thought I've ever had was thought twice, once by me and once by my body. Two thinkers per skull.

He thinks the moral is just that the world is more crowded than common sense admits, and honestly that part I can live with. What I cant live with is where it goes when I push him: he says the escape is to deny that statues exist at all — theres just clay, arranged statue-wise — and that tables and ships go the same way. Fine. But I dont see how that stops before persons. The same move that dissolved Goliath dissolves me, and then neither of us exists, which seems like a lot to prove starting from a box of clay. If it helps you understand where I stand: the two claims I'd most like to keep are that theres one thinker per head and that Goliath didnt exist in the box, though I suspect he'll tell me I cant have both.
