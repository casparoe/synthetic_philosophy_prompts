Here's the student's answer:

"The symbol grounding problem, introduced by Stevan Harnad, is that a formal system's symbols can only be related to other symbols, never to the things they're supposed to mean — like trying to learn Chinese with only a Chinese-to-Chinese dictionary. Meaning just circles around from word to word without ever touching the world. Harnad's proposed solution was essentially this: if the symbol-to-symbol network is rich enough, with symbols connected to many other symbols in the right systematic ways, the system as a whole becomes grounded. Searle's Chinese Room makes a similar point: the man in the room manipulates symbols purely formally, and he doesn't understand Chinese because he has no sensory grounding at all — if you hooked a camera up to the room, Searle concedes the whole system would understand. As for LLMs, their training data is text produced by humans who are themselves grounded, so meaning transfers through the data the way a copy of a photo keeps the image. That means LLMs don't really face the problem, though maybe multimodal ones edge closer."
