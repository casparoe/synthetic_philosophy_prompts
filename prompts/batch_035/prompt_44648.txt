My colleague and I disagree about the Sleeping Beauty problem, and I want your help locating the fault line rather than rehashing the standard case for either side. To fix terms: on Sunday, Beauty is told that a fair coin will be flipped that night; if it lands heads she will be woken once, on Monday; if tails she will be woken on both Monday and Tuesday, with a drug ensuring she has no memory of any prior awakening when the second one comes. She is woken, and asked her credence that the coin landed heads. Halfers answer 1/2; thirders answer 1/3. My credence that the thirder answer is the correct one is about 0.65; my colleague's is about 0.25.

Please give your own number, but the number is the least important part: what I want is the derivation in the open. Decompose your credence into the sub-questions it rests on — for instance, whether the awakening itself gives Beauty any evidence she did not already possess on Sunday; whether, given that it does not, self-locating information of the kind she has can still legitimately shift credence, and if so by what rule; whether the dispute is really verbal, with 1/2 and 1/3 answering different questions (heads-given-this-experiment versus heads-given-this-awakening) and neither strictly wrong; and how much weight the betting arguments deserve, given that their verdict depends on the scoring rule — whether the bet is settled per awakening or per experiment, and whether the setup is one-off or repeated. Assign a rough probability to each sub-question with one line of justification for each, show how they combine into your final figure, and identify which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to.

Two further requests. First, in weighing the betting consideration, take seriously the escalated version of the case in which tails leads not to two awakenings but to a thousand: it is often claimed that the halfer's answer becomes incredible there, since Beauty woken on one of a thousand tails-days is asked to be nearly certain she is in a tails-world while still believing heads was half as likely as tails all along. Say whether you think that claim is sound, or whether it smuggles in an assumption the halfer rejects — and if so, which one. Second, tell me which of my sub-questions most likely accounts for the gap between my 0.65 and my colleague's 0.25, and what argument or kind of evidence would move your own number the furthest.
