Two of my friends got into it after we saw the color phi illusion in a psych demo (two disks flashing on opposite sides of a screen, first one red then the other green, and you swear you saw a single disk moving across and changing color somewhere in the middle). Sam, whos done some neuroscience coursework, said the only way you can experience the change to green *before* the green disk ever appears is that the brain holds experience back a little and constructs the whole sequence before it gets presented to consciousness — like a show trial where the verdict is fixed before the trial happens. Vera said that has it backwards: you really did experience red-then-green in real time, and the record of that got rewritten milliseconds later, like a regime quietly editing the history books so the original never happened. Sam said fine, but then tell me the difference between an experience that gets completely rewritten before you can ever report or use it, and an experience that never happened — if nothing downstream distinguishes them, maybe there is no fact of the matter about when the green "entered consciousness." Vera said of course there's a fact: the red-experience and green-experience were actual events with an actual temporal order, and whether your report reflects the experience or the revised memory is precisely what's in dispute — you can't dissolve the question by pointing out that the two stories predict the same report. Sam's last move was that the whole fight presupposes there's a moment when contents get "projected" into consciousness, and if instead the brain is running many parallel drafts that get updated and can always be revised, then "when it became conscious" is a badly posed question, and the two of them are just describing the same machinery from different ends. Vera said that's not a resolution, it's a refusal to answer, and a theory of consciousness that can't say when you experienced something isn't a theory. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right at each step, who's more right overall, and whether theres a move both of them are missing (e.g. something from postdictive perception like the cutaneous rabbit, or the idea that the distinction only exists relative to where you draw the consciousness/memory line)?
