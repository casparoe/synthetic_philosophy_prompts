So here's the thing: I teach phil of mind, and over the last couple years I've drifted into defending, in seminar and now in a paper I cant seem to finish, what I've started calling the "ecological breadth" account of intelligence. The idea: an organism is intelligent to the degree that it can cope in enviroments other than the ones that shaped it. Intelligence isn't something you have more of, it's a kind of mismatch tolerance — a bug exquisitely tuned to its host can be perfectly optimized without being smart; a rat or a crow that makes a living under novel conditions is. My favorite corollary, which I think is mine: intelligence should tend to show up as clumsiness in the ancestral environment, since fine-tuned specialization and breadth trade off. (Godfrey-Smith's environmental complexity thesis and the old Umwelt idea are in the background, but the tradeoff corollary is the part I'm least sure of.)

Tell me what would count against it. I don't want the standard objections rerun — I want the full map of exposure. Which comparative cognition results would embarrass me, if any (niche-locked species that show surprising learning, or the reverse — is that pattern even real, and if so is it a problem or do I just get to define it away)? Where does the no free lunch stuff leave breadth as a concept — does it cut against me or quietly support the corollary? Which intuitions is the view actually standing on (savants, LLMs that look broad right up until the distribution shifts), and which of those could a committed defender concede versus only explain away? And the question I most want an honest answer to: is the view risking anything at all? I've noticed I have a move for everything — broad organism, I claim intelligence; narrow one, I say wait for different conditions or look at its relatives. Our department Humean says that makes it unfalsifiable, and he may be right. If he is, is that fatal for a definition in this area, or do definitions of intelligence get some insulation as a matter of course? Rank the defeaters from fatal down to merely awkward, and say which one you'd lead with if you were reviewing the paper.
