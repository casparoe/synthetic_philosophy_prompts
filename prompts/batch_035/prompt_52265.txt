I'm a science journalist and I've got a framing fight brewing with my editor that's turned weirdly philosophical. I was assigned a feature pegged to Copernicus' De revolutionibus (1543), pitched as the classic arc: the Scientific Revolution, the moment modern science was born, Copernicus through Newton, superstition out, method in. For background I called a historian of science, and she basicly detonated the assignment. She said the "Scientific Revolution" as a package is mostly a construction of later centuries, that nobody at the time thought they were starting anything, that medieval scholars at Oxford and Paris were already doing serious mathematical work on motion a couple hundred years earlier, that Kepler cast horoscopes for a living and Newton wrote endless pages on alchemy and biblical chronology, and that even the "Middle Ages" label was invented by Renaissance writers flattering themselves at the expense of the previous thousand years. Her line was that if I run the standard framing without qualification, I'm just laundering a myth for a million readers.

My editor's response: readers need a clean arc, everyone knows what the Scientific Revolution means, and scholars always complain about simplification. And honestly, part of me thinks the historian is overcorrecting. Something did change between 1550 and 1700, right? Nature got mathematized, experiment became an arbiter of disputes, scientific societies and journals appeared. If a label tracks all that, maybe it's cutting at a real joint even if people at the time didn't experience it as a "revolution" — like "the Ice Age," which no mammoth noticed either.

So what I want from you is the actual philosophical structure of this mess, not just editing advice. Is periodization a useful fiction that we should use with air quotes, or can a period be a real thing that the people living through it were in no position to see? Where's the principled middle ground between my editor's clean arc and the historian's "the whole frame is propaganda"? And concretely: what do I owe my readers in the lede of this piece? Keep it under 350 words — I need to file.
