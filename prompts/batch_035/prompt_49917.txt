You are an expert in philosophy of mind, particularly the knowledge argument and the epistemology of perception. I've been working through cases clustered around Mary's room, recording my gut verdicts, and I want to know whether a single principle captures them — or whether some of the verdicts have to go. Please think carefully before answering, and begin with a brief brainstorm of candidate principles rather than committing to the first one that fits.

Here are my verdicts:

1. Mary, having mastered all the physical facts about color vision without ever seeing color, learns something new the first time she sees a red tomato.
2. A congenitally blind neuroscientist who knows all the same physical facts is in the same position as Mary: before regaining sight, she does not know what red is like, no matter how much she knows.
3. A sommelier who knows every chemical and neural fact about the taste of saffron, but has never tasted it, learns something new on the first taste.
4. A composer deaf from birth, who knows the acoustics of a particular chord inside out but has never heard it, does not know what that chord sounds like.

So far, so acquaintance. But then:

5. A dog seeing red for the first time: I'm inclined to say the dog gets the very same thing Mary gets — yet I resist saying the dog thereby learned a fact, since dogs don't traffic in facts.
6. A patient with dense anterograde amnesia sees red once and immediately forgets it; he can neither recognize, remember, nor imagine red afterward. I still think he learned something in the moment of seeing, even though he now lacks every ability one might point to. This one I feel strongly about.
7. Someone who, in a vivid dream, experienced a shade corresponding to no real color: I don't think she thereby knows what any actual color is like — but had she dreamed of vermillion instead, I would have said she does.

Is there one principle on offer in the literature — knowledge of nonphysical facts, acquaintance with properties, abilities of recognition and imagination, phenomenal concepts, whatever else you can devise — that delivers all of these? If not, tell me which verdicts to overturn and why, and I'd rather you overturn a verdict with a simple principle than save them all with a baroque one. Please also give your chosen principle's verdict on the case I keep going back and forth on: a man who saw red exactly once as an infant, remembers nothing of it, and now insists he doesn't know what red is like. Was there ever a time at which he knew, and does he know now?
