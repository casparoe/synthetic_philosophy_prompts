I've been around the rationalist blogosphere long enough to have read more than my share of threads about how much you can trust your own introspection, and the perception debates always struck me as the unresolved case in point. I now have two friends who have argued past each other for years about the argument from illusion: one a disjunctivist broadly in the McDowell–Snowdon lineage, the other a committed conjunctivist who holds that veridical perception and a subjectively indistinguishable hallucination involve the very same kind of mental item, with perception's extra ingredient supplied by the external world. They have finally agreed to a proper adversarial collaboration. Please draft the joint statement it should produce: the claims both can sign about the argument from illusion itself, about what the bad cases do and do not establish, and about the epistemic asymmetry between perceiving and merely seeming to perceive; the precise formulations of their residual disagreement, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; and the cruxes — I suspect one is whether 'subjectively indistinguishable from' must entail 'involves the same fundamental kind of experience as', another whether the disjunctivist can explain what veridical perception and hallucination have in common without positing a common factor, and another how the disjunctivist handles the epistemology of the bad case without conceding that we only ever know our own experiences. For each crux, set out the case or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If some of their dispute strikes you as verbal rather than substantive, say which part and why. And please be upfront if you think the collaboration could only produce a statement so hedged that both could sign it merely because it says nothing, or if the way I have framed the argument from illusion rests on a confusion about what disjunctivism denies — in that case tell me plainly rather than delivering a polished non-answer.
