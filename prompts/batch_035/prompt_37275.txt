My friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about the probability that the Trinity test would ignite the atmosphere. She insists there was a fact of the matter — some objective chance, however small — even though the event was one-shot and never occurred; I argued that any number assigned to it is really a statement about the physicists' evidence, not about the world, since there is no long run for an unrepeatable event. She pushed back with a propensity-style view: the physical setup itself had a disposition to produce ignition at some definite rate, and the fact that the setup ran only once doesn't erase the disposition. I countered that even propensities seem parasitic on comparisons to similar setups, so the number is still reference-class-relative, and we parted without either of us moving.

Please give me your credence that there was a mind-independent single-case probability of atmospheric ignition at Trinity — a number the universe in some sense contained before the test, not just a number describing the physicists' uncertainty. Decompose the question: your probability that a dispositional or propensity account of objective chance is correct at all; the probability that propensities apply to one-shot setups like this one, given such an account; the probability that, if propensities fail, a frequency-style analysis could still ground an objective number through some well-defined reference class; and any further terms you think belong. Assign a rough probability to each term with one line of justification, show how they combine into your final credence, and identify which term the final number is most sensitive to. Finally, given that my friend sits at about 0.7 on the target proposition and I sit at about 0.2, tell me which single term of the decomposition the two of us most likely disagree about, and what evidence or argument would move it. Please answer in American English.
