I'm 38, my daughter just turned three, and my wife and I keep going back and forth on whether to have a second kid. Here's the thing that's bugging me. Back before we had our first, everyone told me you can't imagine what it's like until you do it, and I remember finding that genuinely annoying — I'd babysat, I'd read the books, how different could it be? They were right, of course. It wasn't anything like what I expected, in ways I still can't really put into words, and my whole picture of what matters to me shifted. So now I'm in the weird position of having actually lived through the thing that was supposedly unknowable, and I still feel like I have no idea how to decide about doing it again.

My wife says that's exactly why we should just decide the normal way, gut and all — we're not the clueless people we were four years ago, we know what a kid is now, we have real information. My brother, who's childfree and enjoys being contrarian, says the opposite: the fact that my values changed so much the first time around means I can't even tell whether my current "wanting" a second is really me wanting it or just the parent-brain that got installed with the first one, and he thinks that makes the second decision worse, not better. A friend who took a philosphy of choice seminar also said something once about how you can't rationally choose to become someone you can't yet imagine being, though I've forgotten how the argument was supposed to go. Which of them is right? Is the second time actually easier, harder, or just different in kind? And practically speaking, how should we be making this call — what even counts as a reason here, if the person weighing the reasons is partly the product of the last decision?
