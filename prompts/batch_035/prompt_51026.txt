I've been thinking about when I should just believe what people tell me, and my gut is pretty consistent but I can't figure out the rule behind it. When the pilot says the plane's fine, I believe it instantly and would feel like an idiot demanding evidence. When a stranger gives me directions I usually take their word. When a historian tells me what happened in ancient Rome I believe them, even though literally every bit of it is second hand and I could never check any of it myself. When my uncle -- great with engines, really smart guy -- tells me vaccines are risky, I dont believe him. When my best friend says he saw a UFO, I don't believe that either, even though I'd trust him with my life about most things. And wierdly I trust Wikipedia on random facts more than I trust any single smart person I know. Is there one principle that gets all of these? "Trust unless you have reason not to" saves the pilot and the stranger, but it seems like it should make me trust my uncle and my friend too, unless "reason not to" is allowed to just mean vibes. If the rule is more like "trust experts in their own domain," then how do I believe the historian, since the past isn't really anyone's domain, and why does Wikipedia count as an expert when it's just a pile of anonymous people? Tell me what principle (or principles) fits, what it says about a case I can't decide -- a very sincere mechanic telling me my car needs a repair I have no way to assess -- and honestly, which of my gut calls would it force me to give up?
