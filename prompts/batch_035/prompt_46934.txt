I hold the standard Frankfurtian position: moral responsibility does not require alternative possibilities, because a well-constructed Frankfurt case — an agent who acts on his own, while a counterfactual intervener stands ready to ensure the same action had the agent shown any sign of wavering — exhibits responsibility in the absence of any ability to do otherwise. I have taught this for years, and I have watched the dialectic around it grind on for decades without resolving. I want a disciplined map of what would actually count against my position, not the usual exchange of intuitions.

Consider what I take to be the serious defeaters, and tell me how they rank, from fatal to merely awkward. There is the dilemma-style objection: the intervener must have some trigger — some prior sign that the agent is about to decide on his own — and either such a sign exists, in which case the agent had it within his power to show it or not, leaving a residual alternative (the flicker of freedom), or no such sign exists, in which case the case cannot guarantee that the intervener would have acted, and so cannot establish responsibility without alternatives at all. Then there are the buffered cases, where the Frankfurtian stacks interveners monitoring the signs of sign-acquisition, and so on upward: does the fact that cases can always be strengthened show that the objection has been met, or that the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability? And is unfalsifiability even the right charge against a view whose evidence consists in what can be coherently described — or does it instead mark a degenerating research program?

I would also like you to address what the position commits me to that rival views reject. If responsibility never requires alternatives, I owe an account of the asymmetry between the agent who acts on his own and the otherwise identical agent who is actually manipulated; I owe something about why the actual sequence matters; and I should say whether the natural move — re grounding responsibility in actual-sequence control rather than alternatives — is a concession that changes the view into something its original opponents could accept. Bear in mind the original stakes: the case was meant to remove an obstacle to compatibilism about determinism, so a defeat that pushes responsibility back onto alternative possibilities, or onto sourcehood requirements, is not an idle loss. Rank the defeaters, tell me which ones committed Frankfurtians would themselves concede carry weight and which they would dismiss, and give me your honest verdict on how exposed the position really is, including whether the safest version of it is so deflated that it no longer earns the name. For my part, I would find most persuasive a demonstration that some residual alternative survives in every describable case — but I am aware that this may be a fact about my ability to describe cases rather than about the cases themselves, and I want you to be candid about whether that epistemic limitation is the whole disagreement.
