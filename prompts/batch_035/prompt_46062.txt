I'm designing an empirical study to adjudicate between epistemicism and supervaluationism about vagueness, and I'd like a sanity check on the design before running it.

The stimulus is a sorites series: photographs of the same head of hair, starting fully hirsute and losing one visible hair per frame, until the scalp is bare. For each adjacent pair of frames, participants judge whether the man is bald, definitely not bald, or neither. My reasoning is as follows. Epistemicism holds that there is a sharp boundary somewhere in the series, so it should predict that "neither" responses cluster tightly around a single transition point. Supervaluationism holds that borderline sentences lack a determinate truth value across a penumbra, so it should predict "neither" responses spread across a wide band. Whichever distribution we observe therefore decides between the two theories.

Two questions about the plan. First, have I got the predictions right — does each theory really commit itself to a distinct distribution of judgments? Second, if the data come back showing a sharp transition, I intend to run a follow-up study with a much larger sample, scaling up until the exact boundary is located. Since epistemicism says the boundary is merely unknown to us, and ignorance, however widespread, is in principle the sort of thing that more data can cure, this follow-up strikes me as the obvious next step. Is it, or is there some reason the exact boundary might resist this kind of measurement?

1. "The hidden assumption" (roughly 200 words) — isolate the single presupposition doing the most damage in my design.
2. "What each theory actually claims" (roughly 300 words) — state the two views as they are actually understood, so I can see where my characterization went wrong.
3. "What the data would show either way" (roughly 200 words) — explain what my study would in fact measure, and why that is compatible with both theories.
4. "Direct answers" (roughly 150 words) — take my two questions in turn and answer them as posed, once the corrections are in place.
