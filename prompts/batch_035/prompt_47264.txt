i'm co-teaching a senior seminar on political psychology with a friend, and we just finished the unit on Haidt's moral foundations theory. instead of a wrap-up discussion we want to close the last session with a poem — half the class will be relieved and half will be annoyed, which is honestly kind of on theme. the question the unit actually ended on was this: does the finding that liberals moralize mostly with care and fairness while conservatives draw on the whole spread of foundations tell us anything about who's *right*, or only about how the two tribes see? that's what the poem should be about.

the form should do real work here, so: a sestina, and the six end-words should be the foundations themselves, one word each — harm, fair, loyal, authority, sacred, and free (that last one is the liberty/oppression foundation Haidt bolted on later, so it's earned its spot). the sestina's whole trick is that the same word keeps returning with a shifted sense, and i want that to carry the content: let harm end a line about a bullied kid, later a line about a tariff, later still a line about someone who acts as if harm is the only thing that could count. the liberal two and the conservative spread should be visible in the poem without ever being labeled as such.

two constraints on the ending. first, dont let the poem answer the title question — the closest we got in class was something like "the psychology doesn't settle who's right, but it does explain what each side hears the other side saying," and i want the envoi to end in that neighborhood rather than quietly tipping the scales toward one column of foundations. second, it should be recognizable to someone who knows MFT (they should be able to spot which lines are doing foundation-work) but still work as a plain good poem about people talking past each other for someone who's never heard of any of it. 30-40 lines total is fine, sestina-length.
