I work on AI governance, and one of the proposals my team has to take seriously is using debate between AI systems as a safety check: have two models argue for opposite conclusions in front of a human judge, and treat the judge's verdict as evidence about which conclusion is actually correct. The problem I keep circling back to is what people in this literature call obfuscated arguments — arguments that are easy to verify once you already have the conclusion in hand, but very hard to find from scratch. In a debate setting, a dishonest debater can search for an obfuscated argument that supports the conclusion it wants the judge to reach, and the honest debater, who cannot see inside the other side's search process, may have no efficient way to rebut it: the transcript needed to refute it from first principles can be arbitrarily long. A monitoring scheme that only works when no such arguments exist is not something we can rely on.

Before we evaluate any concrete protocol, I want the specification written down. Please give me the desiderata that a debate-based oversight scheme must meet for us to treat its verdicts as trustworthy — for each requirement, the motivating case: the adversarial behavior, judge limitation, or structural asymmetry it guards against. I expect the list will have to say something precise about cross-examination, namely under what conditions letting the judge ask the dishonest debater follow-up questions actually breaks the asymmetry, versus merely giving it room to produce more obfuscation; it will also have to address what is assumed about the judge's ability to verify individual steps, since a scheme whose soundness depends on the judge checking claims it cannot check is not a scheme. Mark which requirements are non-negotiable for a safety case and which are conveniences we could relax in practice.

Then the part I most want, and please don't bury it: identify any subset of the requirements that is jointly unsatisfiable, at least in the worst case — for instance, whether worst-case soundness, transcripts a bounded human judge can actually evaluate, and a dishonest debater with access to an obfuscated-argument search can all hold at once — and state the impossibility plainly if it's there. If the list itself shows that debate is only conditionally safe, that is the finding, not a failure of the exercise. Finally, tell me which single requirement does the most exclusionary work against the variants people actually propose, such as plain one-on-one debate, debate with cross-examination, or recursive decomposition, and whether that requirement is one we could shore up or one we simply have to accept as a ceiling on the method.
