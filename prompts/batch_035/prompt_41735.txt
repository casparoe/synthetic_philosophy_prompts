I moderate a small late-night forum for arguing about religion/philosophy (all of us are just amateurs, no academic background). The "can God make a rock so heavy He can't lift it" thing comes up every couple months and everyone just talks past each other — one guy says "paradox, therefore no God," another says "God can do anything, including the impossible," a third says the question is just broken. After watching this for a while I've come to think the real disagreement isn't about God at all, it's about what people are already committed to when they say "can do anything" — and people flip between meanings mid-argument without noticing.

So I'd like a quiz, something like 8 questions, each one a concrete case you can answer without knowing where the answer leads: can God make 2+2 come out to 5, can God make a square circle, can God create a being with a plan that the being can then wreck, can God lie, can God make a promise He's free to break later, can God do something evil if a mountain of good comes of it, can God create a person who genuinely surprises Him, can God decide tomorrow that 2+2 was always 5. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the actual positions people hold — the "anything includes contradictions" camp, the "anything means anything logically possible" camp, the "a self-contradictory task isn't a task, so the question is malformed" camp, and any other real position out there (like "omnipotence just means nothing external can stop you" or "God can only do what He in fact ends up doing").

The point is to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which slogan they like, so no question should just ask "do you think God is bound by logic?" — that's the label, not the case. Also please flag any two positions my quiz can't actually seperate, instead of pretending every answer pattern points to exactly one view.
