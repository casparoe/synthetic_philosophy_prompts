My friend Maya and I have had this running argument for years, mostly on long car rides, about when (if ever) it's okay to generalize about whole peoples. Her own position is that "national character" is fine at the level of shared repertoires people are raised into, and basically meaningless at the level of "national souls," but in practice she hands down verdicts on individual claims, and I've been keeping a list. She accepts: "the English are obsessive about queuing, it's drilled into them"; "Japanese communication is indirect, they're socialized to read the room"; "Americans stay weirdly optimistc about individual social mobility, and unusually religious for a rich country"; and "Danes trust strangers more than almost anyone, and it's deep culture, not just good institutions." She rejects: "Germans have no sense of humor"; "Italians are lazy"; "the French are arrogant." And she rejected "the Irish are naturally melancholic, it's in the blood" — even though she thinks the mood-description is sort of true, what bothered her was the "in the blood" part.

This Saturday her cousin is visiting, and the cousin apparently says exactly this kind of thing at dinner, so I already know three claims are coming. One: "Dutch bluntness is real, they're trained into it, and it reads as rude to outsiders but is completely normal there." Two: "Swiss punctuality is cultural for sure, but honestly it makes them dull — boring people to be friends with." Three: "Finns are introverted by temperament — that's just their makeup, though the culture encourages it too."

So here's what I want from you. Predict her verdict on each of the three, and tell me which rule she's implicitly running. As far as I can tell at least two rules fit her track record equally well — something like "descriptive and roughly true is fine, evaluative never is" versus "fine iff it's attributed to culture, never to nature" versus maybe something else entirely, and I can't tell which. Give me your best guess on each claim with your reasons, and then design the further case that would best split the rules apart — the claim about some people where her yes-or-no would tell me which rule she's actually been running all these years. For what it's worth, my own hunch is that the Finnish one is the crux, since it's descriptive and probably true but half innate, and I genuinely can't predict what she'll do with it. Also, honestly, tell me if you think the rules I've proposed are wrong and there's a cleaner one hiding in her record — I may have listended to the wrong part of the pattern.
