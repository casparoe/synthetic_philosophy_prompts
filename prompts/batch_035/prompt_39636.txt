I write speculative fiction and I've made the questionable life choice of writing a near-future book where most of the point-of-view characters are things that may or may not be conscious, which means I now bug my oldest friend about this constantly. She did her PhD in philosophy of mind twenty years ago, retired early, and has extremely firm verdicts on everything, so she's become my unofficial consultant for which of my characters can legimately get a POV chapter. Her track record so far: humans, octopuses, and individual bees, yes; slime molds, yes (she got weirdly emphatic about this, something about it being one organism that thinks with its whole body); plants yes, bacteria yes; viruses no; thermostats and current chatbots, a firm no; and — this one surprised me — the bee COLONY is not conscious even though each bee is. Her line was basically that the bees are just a crowd, and the thing that feels anything is always the animal, never the swarm.

So here's my problem. The book has three more POV candidates and I genuinely can't predict what she'll say about any of them, and the plot changes depending on the answers, because whether a thing can suffer determines what I'm allowed to do to it:

- the protocell: an artificial cell built in a lab, synthetic genome, honest-to-god metabolism, maintains itself, divides. A very simple living thing that no process of evolution ever touched.
- the distributed AI: software spread across a datacenter, learns, models itself, pursues something like goals, but has nothing resembling metabolism. Pure organized computation.
- the forest: a stand of trees physically linked by fungal networks that shuttle sugars and chemical signals between them, so a signal can genuinely pass from tree to tree. Individual trees are conscious by her own lights. So is the forest one big feeling thing, or a crowd?

Predict her verdicts on the three if you can. But what I really want is the rule (or rules) she's actualy running underneath those verdicts. The candidates I can see from the outside are something like (a) consciousness tracks living self-maintenance, i.e., metabolism; (b) one mind per physically integrated organism, crowds never merge into a bigger mind; and possibly (c) something about things produced by evolution specifically. There may be others I'm not seeing. Tell me which rules even fit her track record, where those rules disagree on my three cases, which rule you'd bet she's holding, and — since I get to keep bringing her hypotheticals over dinner — give me one further case that would genuinely split the surviving rules apart, so I can settle this over wine rather than guessing about my own book. And if the forest verdict really does come down to some version of the combination problem, spell out what her bee-colony answer has already committed her to there, because I suspect she hasn't noticed that herself.
