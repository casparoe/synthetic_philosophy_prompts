Last quarter my business partner and I trialed a new syrup supplier at our two bubble tea shops, one in the financial district and one next to the university. The financial district shop switched to the new syrup right at the start of the quarter, but the university one only got it for the last few weeks (they missed the first delivery or something). The numbers: with the old supplier the previous quarter, financial district drinks averaged 4.6 stars and university drinks 3.5, so about 4.0 pooled across both. During the trial quarter, the new syrup averaged 4.5 at financial district and 3.4 at university, but pooled all together the new syrup comes out at 4.2.

My partner says switch permanently: 4.2 beats 4.0, and anyway the pooled average uses the most data, so it should be the most reliable number. My cousin, whos doing a stats phd, says the pooled number is junk: about three quarters of the new-syrup reviews came from the financial district shop since they ran it all quarter, and people there just rate everything roughly a star higher than the students do. So pooled, you're mostly measuring which shop the reviews came from, not which syrup is better. Look within each shop and the new syrup loses both times.

But then it came out that the financial district trial ran exactly during the pre-christmas weeks, and I swear office workers rate everything more genorously around then. So now I cant tell if my cousin actually fixed the problem or just picked her own favorite way of slicing the data. Who's right here, if anyone? Can we settle this from the numbers we already have, or would we have needed to run the trial differently? And at the end of the day: do we switch syrups or not?
