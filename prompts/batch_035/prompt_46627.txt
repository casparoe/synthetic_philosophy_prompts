I've been drafting my position on dream skepticism for a seminar on skepticism across traditions, built around Zhuangzi's butterfly episode, and I'd like a consistency audit before I circulate it. My commitments are: (1) the butterfly episode raises a genuine epistemological problem, not merely a poetic one — there is no phenomenal mark that distinguishes, from the inside, the experience of being a man from the dream of being a butterfly; (2) even so, there is a determinate fact of the matter about whether I am now dreaming, and typically the fact is that I am awake; (3) beliefs formed while dreaming do not constitute knowledge, because dream beliefs are unsafe — they are formed in conditions under which they could easily have been false; (4) the best reply to dream skepticism is the coherence of waking life: waking experience is embedded in a continuous web of memory and intersubjective checkability, whereas dreams, once we wake, do not sustain that web; (5) nonetheless, lucid dreamers can acquire genuine self-knowledge — for instance about their own emotional dispositions — from experiences they have while dreaming; and (6) I reject the relativist reading of the passage, on which the waking and dreaming perspectives are epistemically on a par with no principled way to privilege either. I honestly cannot tell where the problem is, so please don't assume I have a favored sacrificial commitment: tell me which of these are in real tension, which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection, rank the genuine tensions by severity, and say specifically which commitment would have to give in each case and what giving it up would cost.
