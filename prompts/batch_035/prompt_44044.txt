For some years now I have been working out, entirely on my own and without any philosophical training, a set of views about consciousness and morality, and before I go any further I would like someone to check whether it all hangs together. Here is where I stand. (1) I find it incredible that consciousness simply switches on at some threshold of complexity, so I have settled on the thought that mind, in an extremely faint and primitive form, is a basic feature of physical matter — that there is some sliver of experience wherever there is matter at all. (2) I believe that what makes anything count morally is its capacity to suffer, and that the reduction of suffering is the foundation of ethics. (3) I believe moral status comes in enormous degrees: a mite counts for something, a dog for vastly more, a person for more still, tracking roughly the organization of the nervous system. (4) I do not believe there is any sharp line where moral concern drops to zero — it must fade out gradually, like light at dusk. (5) When weighing suffering against suffering, I care about intensity and depth, not mere numbers: a single instance of deep anguish seems to me to outweigh any number, however astronomical, of the faintest possible flickers of discomfort. (6) I am confident that a sufficiently sophisticated artificial mind running on silicon would matter morally just as much as a biological one, and I would count its suffering as no less real. And as a matter of daily practice, I boil water, crush rocks, and stir my coffee without a flicker of moral hesitation, and I am quite sure that is fine even if faint experience is present in those processes. Please go through these commitments pair by pair: which are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how badly? Rank the real tensions by severity rather than listing them as equally serious, and for each one say what the cheapest repair would be and what that repair would cost me elsewhere in the list. Finally, tell me which single revision, if any, would clean up the most — or whether the package can be kept intact with a story I have not yet heard.
