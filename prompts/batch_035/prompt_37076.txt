I am preparing to teach a seminar unit on Confucian role ethics, and for the final session I want to pair the theory with King Lear. The version of role ethics we are working with holds that persons are not autonomous, pre-social individuals who then enter relationships and take on obligations; rather, persons are constituted by their relations and roles -- father, daughter, subject, friend -- and moral cultivation consists in learning to occupy and enact those roles well, in large part through ritual and shared forms of expression. This contrasts with the liberal and Kantian picture in which the morally basic unit is the independent agent and roles are, at best, voluntarily assumed contracts. I want a reading of Lear through this lens, faithful to both the play and the theory, and honest about where each resists the other.

The anchor, for me, is the love test in the opening scene. Lear demands a public, quantified profession of love as the price of a dowry, and Cordelia answers that she cannot heave her heart into her mouth, loving her majesty according to her bond, no more nor less. Several questions branch out from there, and I would like your reading to address at least some of them. First, Cordelia: on a role-ethical view, is her answer the paradigm of role fidelity -- refusing to let the language of affection float free of what the father-daughter bond actually requires -- or is it a failure of the expressive and ritual dimension that Confucians take to be partly constitutive of a role, given that she has just watched her sisters perform fluently and offers Lear silence instead? Note that the Confucian tradition is itself uneasy about exactly this, worrying about ritual performed without humaneness while also insisting that sincerity without form cannot be communicated. Second, Lear's abdication: he tries to divest himself of rule while retaining the prerogatives of king and father. If roles are constituted by their constituting practices -- command, judgment, the giving and withholding of dowries -- does the play dramatize the constitutive claim itself, showing Lear become a non-king precisely by ceasing to do what kings do, and his madness on the heath as the condition of a man stripped of every role that made him who he was? Third, Edmund: role ethics inherits the question of whether roles can be criticized rather than only inhabited, and Gloucester's bastard son is assigned, by birth and law, a role he never accepted. Does the Edmund plot expose the deepest weakness of role ethics -- that a social structure of roles can be arbitrary and unjust, and the theory seems to have no standpoint from which to say so -- and does the play offer anything the theory could use in reply? Fourth, if you can, say something about Kent and the Fool: Kent keeps serving Lear after banishment, in disguise, without recognition, and I want to know whether role ethics can make sense of fidelity to a role whose constituting relationship has been officially severed.

Please do not reduce the play to an illustration. Where the reading strains, say so: for instance, the sisters perform their roles badly in a way the audience is invited to read as inner emptiness, and I suspect a theory that rejects a sharp inner/outer distinction has to work to explain what their flattery gets wrong that Cordelia's silence gets right -- I would like to see whether that can be done or whether the play quietly assumes a virtue-ethical or sincerity-centered psychology instead. And end by telling me what Lear adds to the debate between role ethics and autonomy-centered ethics that argument alone does not: my working thought is that the play lets us watch what each picture costs -- Cordelia's integrity costs her the bond, Lear's abdication of the role costs him the self -- but I am open to being told the trade is not symmetrical. Assume the seminar knows the play's plot and the Analects' general shape, so no plot summary is needed; scene-level specificity is what I am after. Do not worry about quotations or scholarship; paraphrase freely. Aim for something I could circulate as reading notes before the seminar meets.
