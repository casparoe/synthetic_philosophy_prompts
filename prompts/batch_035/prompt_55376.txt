So my uncle runs this thing every year at the family reunion that has the whole family split in two camps. He's one of those people who is eerily good at predicting what you'll do — like, he called that my cousin would back out of her wedding two months before it happened, he knew I'd quit my gym membership by March. I don't know how he does it, he's just known all of us our whole lives.

The game is: two envelopes on the table. The first one is open, and I can see the $500 inside. The second one is sealed. He tells me straight up that yesterday he made a prediction about what I'd do. If he predicted I'd take just the sealed envelope, he put $50,000 in it. If he predicted I'd take both, he left it empty. Then I choose — just the sealed one, or both.

My wife says take both, obviously, the envelope's already filled or not, taking the extra $500 can't change yesterday. My cousin, who took only the sealed envelope last year and got $50,000, says the two-envelope people have all walked away with 500 bucks and that's it — every single time, going back years.

Something about it feels rigged to me but I can't pin down how. Like, if he already decided, then taking both is free money, but the people who reasoned that way all ended up poor at this game?? Is there something wrong with the "it's already done" thinking, or with my cousin's "look at the track record" thinking, or what? I get one shot this weekend and I'm honestly torn. Who's actually being rational here?
