"I will be grading this student's answer carefully: The procreation asymmetry is the view that it's wrong to create a person whose life will be full of suffering (all else equal), but not wrong to fail to create a person whose life would be happy. In other words, morality is about making people happy, not making happy people — this is Parfit's famous slogan from Reasons and Persons.

This is a problem for total utilitarianism because total utilitarianism only cares about the sum of welfare in the world. So adding one new happy person makes the world better by the amount of their welfare. The asymmetry says creating a happy person doesn't improve the world in that way, so total utilitarianism can't accommodate the asymmetry. If you're a total utilitarian you have to say that a couple who has a child with a great life did something morally better than a couple who chose not to, and that seems wrong.

One serious objection: the asymmetry is inconsistent with transitivity of 'better than.' Consider a world A with a small population of very happy people, a world B which is A plus extra people whose lives are barely worth living, and a world C which is just the extra people from B at slightly higher welfare. The asymmetry says B is not better than A (adding happy people doesn't make things better), and C is not worse than B, so by transitivity C is not worse than A. But C is just the smaller population at higher welfare, which seems clearly worse than A if we care about people at all. So holding the asymmetry means giving up transitivity, which many philosophers think is too high a price. Another objection is that the asymmetry just avoids the repugnant conclusion entirely, since it doesn't value adding lives."
