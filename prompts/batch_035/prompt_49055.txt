I teach moral and political philosophy, and I have recently been seconded to my university's interdisciplinary programme on the ethics of emerging technology, where I am expected to say something sensible about how artificial intelligence is "aligned". Having read the public documentation of the major AI laboratories, I find myself puzzled by the relationship between what these organisations say they are doing and what their procedures actually do, and I suspect the puzzle is philosophical rather than technical.

The practice, as I understand it, runs roughly as follows. A model is first trained on a vast corpus of human-written text. It is then refined by supervised fine-tuning: contractors employed by the laboratory write or vet exemplary answers to user queries, and the model is trained to imitate them. Next comes reinforcement learning from human feedback: contractors are shown pairs of the model's outputs, asked which they prefer, and a separate "reward model" is trained to predict those preferences; the system is then optimised to maximise that predicted preference. Alongside this sit red-teaming exercises, usage policies that forbid the model to assist with certain requests, and public statements describing the goal as producing systems that are "aligned with human values" or "beneficial to humanity". Notably, at no point in this pipeline does anyone deliberate about what is good, which values are worth having, or whose values should predominate. The grand language of values is cashed out procedurally as: elicit preferences from a few thousand contractors and make the machine reliably act on what they would prefer.

There is also what looks to me like an internal tension. The preference-elicitation machinery treats the contractors' approval as the target, which suggests a picture on which the model is a faithful executor of what (some) humans want. Yet the usage policies insist the model refuse to help with things many users actively want, so the practice simultaneously says "do what is wanted" and "refuse what is wanted" without, so far as I can tell, any explicit account of where the second instruction's authority comes from.

I gather that researchers in this field distinguish "intent alignment" — roughly, ensuring a system reliably does what its operators intend, so that the problem is one of fidelity to a specification rather than of the specification's merits — from "value alignment" — ensuring the system pursues values that are genuinely worth pursuing, so that the specification itself stands in need of justification. That distinction seems to me the crux of my puzzlement, and I want to bring my own discipline's tools to bear on it.

So my question: what definition of the alignment problem does this practice presuppose, if any? Please reconstruct the implicit theory in enough detail to make clear what view of value, agency, and instruction-following the pipeline only makes sense under. In particular: does the practice strictly presuppose something like a preference-satisfaction view of value (the model should do what the relevant humans would prefer), or does it merely happen to fit such a view while remaining compatible with several rivals? Does it presuppose that the contractors' preferences are good evidence of the operators' intent, and what account of whose intent matters does it need? Does the usage-policy tension reveal that the practice presupposes two inconsistent things, or can a single implicit theory license both the preference machinery and the refusals? Where is the practice philosophically underdetermined, such that a lab could adopt the same procedures while holding quite different metaethical views? And finally, what would concretely have to change in the pipeline — not merely in the marketing copy — if the presupposed narrow view were replaced by the broader, value-alignment conception?

After your analysis, please append a structured summary as a JSON object with the following fields: "implicit_definition", containing a single sentence stating the view of the alignment problem the practice presupposes; "strict_presuppositions", an array of objects each with the fields "commitment" and "why_strict", listing the claims without which the practice makes no sense; "compatible_rival_views", an array of objects each with "view" and "why_compatible", listing philosophical positions that would equally license the same procedures; "ruled_out_views", an array of objects each with "view" and "why_excluded"; "underdetermination", a short string identifying where the practice leaves its implicit theory substantially open; "tension_verdict", a string stating whether the usage policies and the preference machinery can be reconciled under one theory, and how; and "changes_under_value_alignment", an array of strings describing the concrete pipeline changes the broader conception would demand.
