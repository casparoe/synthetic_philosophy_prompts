Before I send off my position statement for the symposium volume on knowledge and practical interests, I want someone to tell me whether what I've ended up believing is even a coherent package. I'm a professor, I've been working on this stuff for twenty years, and I have a nagging feeling I've papered over a crack somewhere. Here's the set of views as I'd defend them over a drink:

First, I'm a contextualist about 'knows'. The truth-conditions of knowledge attributions shift with the conversational context of whoever is doing the attributing — raise the standards in the room, mention demons or what rides on being wrong, and attributions that were true become false without the subject's epistemic position changing at all. This is the only thing I've ever found that lets me keep both my ordinary 'I know I have hands' and my respect for the skeptical challenge.

Second, I believe in pragmatic encroachment: whether a subject knows depends partly on that subject's own practical situation. Same evidence, same confidence, but if a lot rides on being wrong about it, you can fail to know what you would otherwise have known.

Third, knowledge is closed under known entailment. If I know p, and I know that p entails q, then I know q. I've never seen a reason to give this up and I'm not looking for one.

Fourth, in the classic sort of bank case — two versions, one where a lot rides on the deposit being in by Saturday and one where it doesn't — my verdict is that in the high-stakes version the subject doesn't know the bank will be open, and in the low-stakes version she does. I'd testify to that contrast, and I think anyone who denies it is theorising away the data.

Fifth, knowledge is the norm of assertion: asserting that p is proper only if you know that p.

Sixth, justification and evidence are not interest-relative at all. Nothing about your practical situation changes how good your evidence is or how justified your beliefs are — if there's any encroachment, it happens at the level of knowledge, not below it.

So where does this creak? I have a suspicion that at least one pair of these doesn't sit together, and an equal suspicion that one or two pairs only look like they conflict. I genuinely don't know which is which, which is why I'm asking. What I want is an audit, not a lecture: identify the pairs that are in real tension, rank them by severity, and for each one tell me what would have to give and what it costs. For the pairs that dissolve on inspection, explain why they dissolve, because I don't want to spend my symposium reply defending a concession I never needed to make. And at the end, tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Please dont structure your reply with headings or bullet points or anything like that — just continuous prose, the way you'd write me an email. Be specific about which of the six is doing the damage; 'there's some tension between contextualism and pragmatic encroachment' is the sort of answer I can get from a second-year.
