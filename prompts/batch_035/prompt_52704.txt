quick background: i write about science and ideas for a general-audience magazine, and i'm trying to figure out my editor before i pitch him. his track record on cross-cultural findings about philosophical intuitions, these past few years: he happily ran the story that Gettier intuitions vary (non-WEIRD samples being readier to say the person really knows); he killed the finding that semantic intuitions about proper names vary — the gödel/schmidt caes — on the grounds it was probably translation or education, not a real difference; he ran the fat-man trolley one, where the aversion varies across cultures, but refused to touch the claim that switch-case intuitions vary too, insisting those would come out the same anywhere; he spiked a piece on personal-identity intuitions varying because, he said, nobody outside a philosophy seminar has actual intuitions about teleporters, you're just measuring how people react to sci-fi; and he ran the one about the Knobe effect being weaker outside WEIRD samples.

now a researcher sends me a new survey: on the experience machine, several non-WEIRD samples are way less bothered — a lot of respondents say the person plugged in is living about as well as anyone — while the educated Western samples give the standard verdict. do i pitch this, and if so how do i frame it? more than the call itself, i want the rule he seems to be running, the other rules that fit his record just as well, which of those rules diverge on this new case, and what further finding would best split them apart. and keep in mind i can't hand him a finding and a principle at the same time — frame it wrong and he'll just file it under whatever he already believes.
