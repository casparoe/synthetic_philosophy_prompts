Ok so something weird has been happening to how I make decisions and I want to know if there's a proper name for it.

I got made treasurer of our community garden last month (nobody else wanted it, classic), and the recurring headache is the watering rota over summer. Each member has an incentive to quietly skip their slot — one skipped watering barely matters, and getting up at 6am sucks. Standard collective action stuff. But here's the part I can't find a word for: I've started deciding as if my choice tells me what everyone else who deliberates like me will choose. When I'm lying in bed at 6am deciding whether to get up, I think: several of the other fifteen are going through this exact same calculation on their day, and I cant see myself as the special one who defects while everyone else is the showing-up type. If I'm the kind of person who talks herself into staying in bed, then the people who reason like me are that kind too, and my choice is basically a sample from that group. So I get up, because I want the sample to be "shows up."

Its not quite Kant's thing I think? He seems to be about rules you'd want universalized, whereas I'm doing something more like a bet: not "what if everyone did this" but "the ones like me will probably do whatever I do, so my choice kind of counts sixteen times." Is there an established concept for treating your own decision as evidence about similar reasoners and then choosing for the whole bundle? I have a vague feeling someone once ran a real contest in a magazine column that turned on exactly this. But mostly I want to know: does the reasoning have a name, is it actually sound or just a mood, and what separates it from guilt-tripping myself into compliance.
