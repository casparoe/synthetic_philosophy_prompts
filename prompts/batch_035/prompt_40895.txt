I've been in a running argument with my housemate about Weber's Protestant ethic thesis — he thinks any explanation of capitalism's rise that leans on religious values is basically hand-waving, and I think the purely materialist explanations do their own kind of hand-waving. Before we go another round I want the actual spec on the table. Write out the desiderata for a causal explanation of why modern industrial capitalism took off where and when it did: what any adequate explanation has to cover (the timing in northwestern Europe, the weird psychological features of early capitalist behavior like deferred consumption and a calling-like devotion to accumulation for its own sake, the fact that big mercantile economies existed for centuries without producing the thing needing explaining), what it's not allowed to do (beg the question by smuggling capitalist behavior into the description of the cause, leave "culture" as an unopened black box, or lean on correlations between Protestant regions and economic outcomes without saying which way the arrow runs — since richer, more literate, more commercially dense places may just have been the ones that adopted Protestantism in the first place). Also tell me which of these requirements are genuine demands and which are just camp prejudices dressed up as rigor, like the demand that a real explanation never appeal to ideas at all. And flag whether some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — I have a suspicion the requirement to give microfoundations at the level of individual believers pulls against the requirement to explain something as big and slow as an economic order over centuries, but maybe thats just me being lazy. I'd especially like to know which single requirement does the most work in knocking out either Weber's own thesis or the standard materialist rival, since thats the fight we keep having. Skip a full scorecard of every theorist in the field — just the requirements and the reasoning behind each one.
