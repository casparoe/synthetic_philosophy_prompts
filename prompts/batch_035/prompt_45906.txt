My classmate Aurora and I have spent the whole semester of our ethics elective arguing about where persuasion ends and manipulation begins, and over time she has built up a surprisingly stable track record of verdicts that I've been writing down. She calls it manipulation when a car dealer quotes an inflated sticker price to anchor the negotiation, when a charity caller first gets you to agree to something tiny and then leverages that little yes into a large monthly donation, when her parents tell her that her grandmother will be heartbroken if she skips a family dinner, when a mall pipes in warm scents and slow music to put shoppers in a spending mood, and when a classmate compliments someone's presentation right before asking to borrow their lab report. But she says it is not manipulation when a friend tearfully tells a true story about a refugee camp to persuade you to donate, when a doctor describes a surgery as having a ninety percent survival rate rather than a ten percent death rate in order to help a frightened patient consent, when a charity's poster shows one named child instead of a table of statistics, and when a coach reminds a runner of the reasons the runner themselves gave for training for the marathon.

Two new cases have come up in our argument, and I want to be able to predict her verdicts before she gives them, mostly so I can finally win one. In the first case, a teacher who genuinely wants her students to succeed tells them, untruthfully? no, let me be careful: truthfully, that she gave up her entire weekend to write their practice exams, and that skipping the review session would waste her sacrifice, so that they attend out of guilt rather than out of interest in the material. In the second case, an insurance company runs an ad with real, graphic footage of crash victims in order to sell a comprehensive plan that most customers do not actually need, though the footage itself is accurate and the coverage it describes is real. What do you think Aurora will say about each case? I'm less interested in the bare predictions than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that would fit her earlier verdicts just as well, and whether the teacher and insurance cases actually pull those rules apart or whether they coincidentally agree. If you think her track record genuinely underdetermines one or both verdicts, tell me that honestly, and then tell me what further case I should spring on her next that would best separate the candidate rules, since that's the one I most want to have ready.
