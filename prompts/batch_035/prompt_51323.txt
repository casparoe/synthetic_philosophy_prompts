His diagonal argument then proved a stronger result: for any set, the collection of all its subsets is strictly bigger than the set itself, so the infinities form an endless staircase. Hilbert's Hotel illustrates the ground floor. The hotel has infinitely many rooms, all occupied, yet the front desk can always squeeze in one more guest by having everyone move up a room; and by the same trick it can absorb any number of new guests, even infinitely many. From this, Hilbert concluded that infinity plus anything is still infinity — which is what mathematicians mean when they say that all infinite sets are equal in size. Finitists, the school that refuses to accept these results, object that a hotel which is always full and yet always has a vacancy is a flat contradiction; and they point out that it was the strain of defending his infinities against this sort of skepticism that sent Cantor to the sanatorium."
