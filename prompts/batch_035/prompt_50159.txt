so I've been trying to write up my actual views on infinite ethics for a forum post and I keep going back and forth, so before I post it and get shredded in the comments I want a sanity check on wether the whole package hangs together. here's roughly where I'm at:

(1) In ordinary finite cases I'm a total utilitarian — more wellbeing is better, adding happy lives makes the world better, and it doesn't matter how far away in space or time the wellbeing is. no discounting, the numbers just get added up.

(2) If the universe is spatially infinite (which it honestly might be) and there's flourishing life scattered thru it, then the total is infinite no matter what I do, so plain summation says my actions make literally zero difference. and I flat out refuse to accept the conclusion that in that case nothing I do matters. deflecting an asteroid from a city still seems obviously worth doing even if the cosmos is infinite.

(3) I also don't think infinite worlds are all automatically on a par. a universe of infinite bliss is better than a universe of infinite agony, even though you can't subtract the two totals against eachother. so some comparisons between infinities are real.

(4) But I'm completely against fanaticism — a 1 in 10^(10^10) chance of an infinitely good outcome should not outweigh a certainty of curing malaria for a million people. no epsilon is small enough that chasing infinite payoff beats doing the sure thing.

(5) And I don't think value tops out either. for any finite world there's a better one. I'm not a bounded utilitarian, that always felt like a hack to me.

(6) One more thing. I've been told the natural fix for (2) is to evaluate acts by their local effects — make things better in your causal neighborhood and stop trying to sum the whole cosmos — and I kind of like it, but something bugs me about it: if there are aliens a billion light years away doing ethics too, I don't want their answer about how good our shared universe is to come out different from mine just because they're sitting somewhere else.

which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and what's the cheapest fix for each? rank the tensions by severity rather than dumping them on me all at once as equally bad, and if there's one revision that cleans up the most, tell me which. I have a nagging feeling at least two of these can't both be true but I genuinely can't pin down which.
