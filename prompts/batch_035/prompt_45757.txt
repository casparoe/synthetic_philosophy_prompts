Our philosophy of mind reading group has been going through Jerry Fodor lately (the Language of Thought stuff, plus his famously cranky takedowns of connectionism from the 80s and 90s), and we got into an argument I'd like your read on. Fodor died in 2017, right before large language models became a thing, which honestly feels like a cosmic joke — he spent his whole career insisting that thinking happens in an innate, compositional mentalese that's prior to natural language, and that piling up statistical associations (his caricature of neural nets) can never get you genuine thought because it can't explain systematicity and productivity. Now here come LLMs, trained on nothing but natural language text, apparently doing compositional, reasoning-ish stuff. So the obvious question: what would Fodor actually say about them? Would he just file them under "fancy association machines, nothing to see here," or would parts of the LLM story tempt him in either direction? I can see it cutting both ways — like, on one hand it looks bad for him that a system with no innate mentalese, just tokens and weights learned from English (and other languages), seems to have something like compositional structure. On the other hand, maybe he'd say the interesting part is what the models build internally: those interpretability results where people find vector representations and feature directions that compose in rule-like ways look suspiciously like a learned language of thought, which could make him feel vindicated in a weird way he never asked for.

There's a second layer I'm curious about too. Fodor had that extreme view that concepts are mostly innate and that "learning" new ones is basically impossible — you just trigger concepts you already had. LLMs at scale seem to acquire an enormous range of new abilities from training, which on its face is a direct counterexample to his radical concept nativism. But maybe a Fodorian could dig in and say the abilities aren't really new concepts, just recombinations of representational primitives, or that scale changes nothing about the argument. How do you think he'd play it?

Give me your best reconstruction of his likely reactions, and please flag where you're going beyond what he actually wrote versus extrapolating from his general temperament and commitments — he was such a specific guy that I worry about just pattern-matching to "grumpy nativist." Also tell me how confident you are in the overall prediction, and point out the single part of your reconstruction you're least sure about — I'd rather know where the weak spot is than get a confident-sounding story all the way down.
