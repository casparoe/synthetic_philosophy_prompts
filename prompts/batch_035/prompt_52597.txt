i'm a journalist who covers AI and ideas, and my editor greenlit an interactive piece for the magazine's site: a "what do you actually think an LLM is?" quiz, the kind readers click through and then fight about in the comments. I don't want it sorting people by which label they like — everyone says "it's just a tool" until you give them the right hypothetical — I want it built out of concrete cases, and only afterwards do you find out which camp you landed in. The leaves I want it to reach are roughly: the simulator view (the base model is a big role-player that instantiates characters, some of whom are agents, but the model itself isn't one), the deflationary "it's a next-token predictor, stop anthropomorphizing" view, the degrees-of-agency view (today's models are weak but genuine agents, and the interesting question is how strong), the harness view (agency lives in the scaffolding — tools, memory, the loop the model's embedded in — not in the weights), and the position, if anyone ends up there, that the whole question is a category error the way asking whether a thermostat believes anything is. Please do eight or so diagnostic questions — cases like a model that argues one side of an issue on Monday and the opposite on Tuesday, a model mid-way through a long tool-using task that gets an odd subgoal and runs with it, a jailbroken model suddenly presenting as a very different character, that kind of thing — each phrased so a normal reader can answer without having heard of any of these views. Then a scoring key in prose that maps answer patterns onto the positions and says which two positions the quiz genuinely can't seperate, since I suspect it's the simulator view and the harness one, and I'd rather you admit that than paper over it. One more thing: I want at least one case that pulls apart being an agent from being a moral patient, because comment sections always collapse those and it drives me nuts. Write the whole thing as continuous prose please, no headings or bullets or flowchart arrows — I know that's weird for a quiz, my editor is on a warpath about listicles, just describe the questions and the key like you're explaining it to me over coffee. And if you think the quiz rests on a bad premise — say, that these views are actually separable by cases at all, or that my list of leaves misses the position most informed people actually hold — tell me straight instead of building something you don't believe in.
