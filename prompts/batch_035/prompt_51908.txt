I'm a science and ideas journalist, and my editor has commissioned a feature on philosophers who turn up, uncredited, in blockbuster films — the working conceit being that some of the best 20th-century philosophy got smuggled into the multiplex. For the centrepiece I want to read Christopher Nolan's Memento through Borges's views on time, specifically his essay "A New Refutation of Time", and I'd like a reading that takes both the film and the essay seriously, rather than just gesturing at "ooh, time is weird".

Here's the angle as I've sketched it — tell me if it holds up. Borges, following Berkeley and Hume, tries to dissolve time altogether: if each moment contains everything needed for our feeling of self, if identity doesn't require a river carrying it forward, then succession is an illusion, and the essay famously ends by insisting that "Time is the substance I am made of. Time is a river which sweeps me along, but I am the river; it is a tiger which destroys me, but I am the tiger; it is a fire which consumes me, but I am the fire." Nolan then hands us Leonard, a man who literally lives in Borges's atomised present — no new long-term memories, just poloroids, notes and tattoos, a self persisting entirely through external props. So does Leonard's condition vindicate the refutation (a life without experienced succession is still recognisably a life), or demolish it, since without duration Leonard becomes putty for whoever writes the notes — Teddy, Natalie, Leonard himself? And is the whole thing basically "Funes the Memorious" run in reverse — Funes drowning in total recall, Leonard engineering a chosen forgetting — or does that parallel break down somewhere I'm not seeing?

I'd also like some honesty about where the film resists the Borges reading (there are stretches where the plot is doing thriller work, not metaphysical work), and a view on what I can defensibly claim Nolan dramatises that Borges couldn't — that's the paragraph the piece needs to land. And if you think the whole fit is a stretch and I should swap in a different film, say so now rather than in the comments.
