(1) Judicial review is legitimate — unelected judges striking down laws passed by elected legislatures is not some democratic crime, that's just what a constitution is for. (2) I'm basically an originalist: constitutional provisions mean what they publicly meant when they were adopted, and when judges substitute their own moral views for that, its a power grab dressed up as interpretation. (3) Brown v. Board was rightly decided, and I mean more than "good result" — segregated schools genuinely violated the Constitution, and Plessy was genuinely wrong before that. (4) But as I understand it, the original expected application of the Fourteenth Amendment did not treat school segregation as unconstitutional: the very same Congress that sent the amendment to the states was presiding over segregated schools in the District of Columbia, and basically nobody at the time thought school segregation violated the clause they were drafting. (5) I can't stand living constitutionalism — to me it just amounts to judges legislating their preferences and calling the result "interpretation." (6) I think Lochner was wrongly decided: courts had no business inventing substantive limits on economic regulation out of due process. (8) I also think precedent matters: a court can be justified in standing by a decision it would not have reached itself.
