I work as a vet tech and for the last few months I've been taking an evening class in animal ethics, partly for my own sanity after long days at the clinic. Our final session is on whether sentience and moral status come in degrees rather than being an all-or-nothing thing, and the instructor asked if anyone wanted to bring something creative to close with. I want to bring a poem, and I have a very specific form in mind: a pantoum.

In case the rules aren't at your fingertips: in a pantoum, the second and fourth lines of each stanza come back as the first and third lines of the next stanza, and in the final stanza the loop closes -- the second line of that last stanza is the third line of the very first stanza, and the last line of the poem is its first line. So lines keep circling back, recontextualized each time they return, which is exactly what I want philosophically. The poem should walk down a ladder of sentience, one rung per stanza -- something like a pig, then a fish, then a bee, then maybe a jellyfish or a nematode, and by the end a thermostat -- and one recurring question line (something about where the line falls) should come back at every level but sound different each time: urgent for the pig, uncomfortable for the bee, almost absurd for the thermostat. Each rung should be only a little different from the one above it, and yet somewhere between the bee and the thermostat the reader has crossed something, and the poem should never say where.

Here's the thought I keep chewing on and want the poem to leave open rather than resolve: ethics seems to see a ramp, but law needs a step. This came up in class because of the UK's Animal Welfare (Sentience) Act, which was recently extended to cover octopuses and lobsters -- somewhere a line got drawn on a ramp, and drawing it meant pretending it was a step. So please don't let the poem land on everything deserves care or only the complex ones count. It should end on the repeated line coming around one last time, subtly changed, so the reader can't quite tell whether it's still a question or has quietly become an answer. And just give me the poem itself, nothing else -- no title, no note afterward explaining the conceit, no headings or bold or anything, only the verses.
