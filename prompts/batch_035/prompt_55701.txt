I'd like help thinking through a puzzle in the ethics of wild-animal welfare, and I'd appreciate a careful conceptual treatment rather than a general-purpose appeal to conserve nature.

Here is the reasoning that troubles me. Many small-bodied, fast-reproducing species have enormous fecundity: a female fish may shed hundreds of thousands or millions of eggs in a season, of which on average only two or so survive long enough to reproduce. Suppose we assume, for such species, that (a) the brief lives of the vast majority that die young contain more suffering than satisfaction, and that (b) an animal that is never conceived is not thereby harmed — there is no victim of the life that fails to begin. It then appears to follow that a habitat supporting a large population of such animals is the site of a vast ongoing production of suffering, and that reducing the number of lives begun — for instance by lowering the habitat's carrying capacity — would reduce total suffering. On this reasoning, habitat destruction could in some cases be better for animals than habitat preservation, which is a difficult conclusion to sit with.

Please explain the following:

1. Which steps of this argument are doing the real work, and where it is most vulnerable. In particular, I want to understand how ecological details complicate the inference from "less habitat" to "fewer lives begun." Consider compensatory versus additive mortality, source–sink dynamics, and the fact that converted habitat (farmland, pasture, suburbs) is not empty but instead hosts different animal populations whose abundance and welfare may be higher or lower than what was displaced.

2. Which parts of the dispute are empirical and which are philosophical. My impression is that the load-bearing philosophical assumptions are (i) the sign of average lifetime welfare for short-lived organisms, (ii) whether and how much death is a harm to the one who dies, and (iii) the non-identity / procreative-asymmetry question of whether non-existence can be bad for a particular individual. Please correct or extend this list if it is incomplete.

3. Where, in all of this, the disagreement among people who have seriously studied these questions actually lies, as opposed to what is settled. Please clearly separate the settled points from the genuinely contested ones throughout the answer, and do not present a contested position as the consensus view of the field.

If it helps, feel free to work through the stylized fish example with explicit numbers, but please flag at each step which assumptions are carrying the argumentative weight.
