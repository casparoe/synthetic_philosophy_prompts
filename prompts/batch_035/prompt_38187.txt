I'm on the editorial board of a tiny literary magazine that mostly publishes translated poetry, and we've been arguing for months about whether to accept machine-translated submissions (or human translators who lean heavily on an LLM and then tidy up). One of my colleagues keeps going on about Hofstadter's Le Ton beau de Marot, the book he built around dozens and dozens of English versions of one tiny Clément Marot poem — that short three-syllable-per-line French get-well poem written for a sick young woman. I've actually read it now, and the thing that struck me is that Hofstadter's whole point seems to be that form is not packaging: the three-syllable lines, the rhymes, the playfulness are part of the meaning, so a translation that keeps only the semantic content has thrown away half the poem, however "faithful" it looks on a dictionary level.

Here's my problem though: modern LLMs are surprisingly good at exactly the thing Hofstadter complained humans never even attempted. Ask one to translate a rhymed, metered poem and it will often produce rhyme and roughly matching metre without much coaxing. So it's not obvious to me whether that means the Hofstadter test is passed, or whether his argument cuts deeper than surface form. Like, his translations were driven by enormous attention to tone and register and wordplay, and I suspect an LLM can imitate the shape of that while missing whatever the shape was for.

Please act as a translation scholar who knows both the philosophy of translation and how these models work, think carefully and maybe brainstorm a bit before answering, and lay out the most important implications of Hofstadter's argument from that book for how a magazine like ours should evaluate machine-assisted poetry translation. Concretely: which of his criteria could an LLM plausibly satisfy, which ones are about something a system like that arguably can't have, and what does that mean for whether his framework is even usable for us — should we be grading translations by his lights at all, or has the game changed since he wrote it? I'd also love your honest take on whether an editor with no French can tell the difference.
