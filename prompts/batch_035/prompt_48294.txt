I help run a rationality meetup — about 14 regulars, mostly people who've been reading LessWrong and the alignment forum forever, plus a couple of newer folks. We've got a 2-hour session coming up and instead of just talking about Kavka's toxin puzzle for the millionth time I want to actually run it, with real stakes, so people feel the bind in their gut instead of nodding along. Quick refresher on the setup in case it's fuzzy: a billionaire offers you a million dollars tonight, paid tonight, if and only if at midnight you intend to drink a toxin tomorrow — a toxin that will make you miserably ill for a day but won't harm you long-term. Tomorrow morning, once the money is safely in your account, there's no reason left to drink. The puzzle is whether a rational person can even get into the winning position.

Design the session for me. Constraints and open problems I want you to actually work through:

- Real but scaled stakes. I can put up maybe $60 total out of my own pocket (so a few dollars per person max), and the toxin has to be safe but genuinely unpleasant — I'm thinking a 90-second hand plunge in ice water, or a tablespoon of hot sauce, or you come up with something better. It has to be bad enough that nobody drinks it for fun.

- The hard part is operationalizing "intends." In the original puzzle it's a perfectly reliable lie detector, and that's doing a LOT of the work. I don't have one. Figure out the best substitute — I'm imagining some combination of the group predicting each person (a prediction market with actual nickels?), people pre-registering their reasoning in writing, a commit/reveal structure, whatever. Tell me which mechanism you'd pick and, importantly, what answer to the puzzle each mechanism smuggles in, because I suspect the choice of detector basically decides the outcome in advance.

- I suspect one-shot-ness and anonymity are load-bearing. If everyone knows they'll be back next month and will be remembered as the person who weaseled out of drinking, then reputation effects make intending easy and the puzzle quietly evaporates. The game probably needs to defeat that somehow, or at least the debrief needs to flag exactly where it leaked in.

- Rules, materials, a timeline for the two hours, and the full payoff scheme.

- A debrief that maps what actually happened onto the puzzle: who intended, who drank, the one-boxer vs. two-boxer readings of the case, whether "you just can't form an intention at will, intentions aren't directly controllable the way hand movements are" is the right diagnosis or a cop-out. And an honest note on which feature of the puzzle the game structurally cannot reproduce no matter how I set it up.

- What will predicably go wrong the first time I run it. My bet is that a nonzero number of people drink the toxin out of pure spite and pride, then insist afterwards that they "totally did intend all along," and the room spends 40 minutes relitigating what intending even means until we're out of time. Tell me how to handle that without just banning the discussion.

Everything should be runnable by one facilitator (me) with a kitchen, a whiteboard, and whatever I can buy at a grocery store. No special materials.
