I write a philosophy blog for a mostly-lay audience and my most popular post by far was a "which personal identity view do you actually hold" quiz, so now I want to do the same treatment for the cross-cultural stuff about intuitions. The target reader has maybe read one intro phil book and vaguely remembers Gettier, but has no idea who ran which study, so the quiz has to sort them by their gut reactions to cases, not by which camp they can name. The topic in play: those results where people from different cultural or educational backgrounds apparently give systematically different intuitions — like the case where a man uses the name "Gödel" for a theorem that was actually proved by someone named Schmidt and subjects disagree about who he's talking about, Gettier-style cases where a true belief is only true by luck, and the trolley variants where some populations apparently find it much less acceptable to divert harm than others.

Here's the format I want: eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, and a scoring key at the end mapping answer patterns onto named positions. The leaves should include at least (1) the view that intuitions from some groups should be discounted as evidence, (2) the view that the groups just have different concepts, so the disagreement is verbal and nobody is wrong, (3) the straight-up skeptical view that this shows the whole intuitions-as-evidence method is broken for everyone, (4) the defense that the variation reflects performance factors (distraction, misreading the case, culture-specific interpretive habits) rather than genuine competence differences, and (5) whatever you'd call the position that intuitions were never doing much work in philosophy anyway. If there's a natural sixth leaf, add it.

A few constraints to make the quiz actually diagnostic. No question may ask anything like "do you trust intuitions?" — the reader has to be reacting to a scenario, not a slogan. The scenarios should mix the famous cases with a couple of invented ones so readers can't pattern-match off half-remembered wikipedia (e.g., a case where a reader is told that a village agrees unanimously that a healer *knows* the rain ritual works even though it works by sheer coincidence, and asked what that agreement shows). At least one question should probe whether the reader treats disagreement among their own friends differently from disagreement across cultures — I suspect a lot of people only get skeptical when the disagreement is foreign, and I want the quiz to catch that. Finally, be honest in the key about combinations the quiz can't separate — I assume some of the leaves above will produce overlapping answer patterns, and I'd rather have that flagged than papered over. Audience is smart laypeople, so keep the jargon to the leaf names only. Typos in my post have been forgiven before so dont worry about matching my register, just make it clean.
