hey, I'm in a philosophy of emotions course this semester and we just wrapped up the "what IS an emotion" unit and honestly the whole debate feels completely stuck and I want to know why. quick sketch of the terrain as we got it in class: the judgment/appraisal people say emotions are evaluations — fear is basically judging that something is dangerous, anger is judging that someone wronged you; the feeling camp going back to William James says an emotion just IS the feeling of your body doing its thing (the fear is the felt racing heart, the felt adrenaline, etc., not anything mental behind it); and then there's a middle camp that says emotions are perceptions of value or bodily perceptions — fear *perceives* danger kind of the way your eyes perceive a shape, so it's sensory-ish but also world-directed. three theories, people have been going at it since at least the 70s, and as far as I can tell everyone's positions are basically set in stone. our professor jokes that every new book is just the same book with the terms renamed.

heres my actual hypothesis though. in seminar we kept hitting the same two stock problems: (1) recalcitrant emotions — you stand on the glass skywalk, you fully believe it's safe, you still feel terror — which the judgment people patch by retreating from "judgments" to "appraisals" or "construals" or "judgments with less than full commitment", and each patch just makes me think the view is bleeding out through the qualifications; and (2) animals and babies — does a dog really *judge* that the mailman is dangerous, or is the judgment theorist forced to invent "proto-judgments" that don't seem to judge anything? so my thought is: maybe the three camps aren't actually disagreeing about one thing at all. maybe each one is picking out a different component of the whole emotional episode — the appraisal, the bodily feeling, the action tendency to run — and the fight is really over which component gets *named* "the emotion". like it's a fight about baptism, not about the facts. but if that's right, then there's a leftover real question nobody's arguing cleanly: do emotions *represent* value at all, and if they do, does that make value mind-dependent in some weird way?

so what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a survey (I've read the survey). is this a verbal dispute about the word "emotion", three camps answering three different questions under one heading, or a genuine substantive fight about whether emotions represent that's just been run badly? if the different-components reading is right, why hasn't it dissolved the debate already — is the naming question still load-bearing somehow, like for whether cognitive therapy is supposed to work by changing beliefs rather than breathing exercises? and what would even count as progress here — a better taxonomy, some affective neuroscience data, a proper distinction between what an emotion is vs what it does — or is this one of those arguments that structurally can't end, and if so why not? be honest if you think my hypothesis is dumb btw, half the reason I'm asking is that nobody in my class pushed back on it and that made me suspicious.
