Ok so background: I dont have any philosophy training, I drive a delivery van, but I've been going down a youtube rabbit hole lately about this thought experiment called twin earth. As I understand it theres a planet just like ours where theres a liquid that looks, tastes, and behaves exactly like water but is made of some other chemical, XYZ or whatever, and the people there couldnt tell the difference in a million years. The punchline is that when twin earth people say "water," they mean the XYZ stuff, even though whats in their heads is supposed to be identical to whats in ours. So the meaning of a word is partly out there in the world and not just in your skull, which honestly broke my brain a bit when I first heard it, still kinda does.

Anyway I cant stop imagining a startup built around this. They sell "semantic relocation" services -- the pitch is they quietly swap out the stuff in your community's glasses, taps and reservoirs for the twin earth liquid, and now every time you say "water" youre talking about XYZ, whether you wanted that or not. Premium plan, no refunds on meanings. Its a bit like how I heard we all meant the same thing by "water" back before anyone knew water was H2O -- the word was pointing at the stuff all along, and nobody asked our permission. What got extended to a whole town without anyone noticing, bascially, except now theres a monthly fee.

So heres what I want: write that company's customer FAQ page, around 600-700 words, in a deadpan chipper corporate support voice (think a software company answering tickets about password resets, except the tickets are about reference). The jokes should land for someone who actually knows the externalism stuff: a question about what happens if only YOU subscribed but your neighbors didnt (does the whole community have to be swapped for your words to follow?), one from a customer who is upset to learn she cant just decide what her own word means, something about their "twin customer" on the other planet who got the reverse deal, maybe a proud customer who is now the community expert on water despite never having touched the stuff (division of linguistic labor I think its called?). Dont explain any of the philosophy outright -- let the fine print do it. End the FAQ with a one-line company slogan that doubles as a thesis about where meaning actually lives, formatted exactly as \answer{...} on the very last line.
