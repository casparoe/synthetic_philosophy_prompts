My friend and I spent most of last evening arguing about the distinction philosophers draw between finks and masks for dispositions. To fix terms: a fink is a mechanism that, were the triggering conditions to arise, would remove the very disposition before it could manifest — the classic picture is a fragile glass wired to a device that would, upon any strike, instantly render it non-fragile. A mask (or antidote) instead blocks the manifestation while leaving the disposition itself intact — the fragile glass packed in foam, or a person's anger defused before it ever shows. My friend's claim, which I could not refute but do not believe, is that outside the contrived laboratory cases the distinction does no real work: in ordinary life, she says, every interference is both a fink and a mask, depending only on which trait you happen to be tracking. I want to test her claim against a case built to order, and I would like you to construct it to the following specifications.

The scenario must be realistic and quotidian — an office, a team, a family; no electronic gadgets, no demons, no perfectly reliable mechanisms, and nothing that would look out of place in an ordinary week. It must concern character traits rather than physical properties like fragility: the dispositions at issue should be the sort we ascribe to people in everyday life. And it must be engineered so that one and the same arrangement of other people's standing habits — a colleague's reliable routine, say — functions simultaneously as a mask for one of the subject's dispositions (she retains it, but its manifestation is blocked) and as a fink for another (she also has it, but it would be taken from her the moment its triggering conditions arose). Both traits and the covering mechanism should be laid out in a single paragraph, and you must not stipulate the verdicts — you have to derive them. Then walk through the analysis: for each trait, state the simple conditional analysis (something is disposed to M in circumstances C iff, were C to obtain, it would M), say whether it delivers the correct ascription in your case, and identify precisely which feature of the setup is doing the work.

Finally, address my friend's claim head-on: in your case, does the fink/mask distinction mark a genuine difference in the world, or is it merely perspectival, an artifact of which disposition we happen to be tracking? And tell me what a defender of the simple conditional analysis would have to add — some clause requiring that conditions be "suitable" or "non-finking" — in order to repair it, and whether that repair treats the two traits symmetrically or quietly rigs the case in favor of one reading over the other. If the specifications turn out to be jointly unsatisfiable in some respect, build the closest satisfiable variant and flag exactly where and why you deviated.
