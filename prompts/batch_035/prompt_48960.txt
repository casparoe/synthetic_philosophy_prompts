I help run a monthly ethics discussion group, mostly people with no philosophy background but strong opinions, and next month's topic is whether brain size has any direct moral significance. The topic keeps coming up sideways: someone points out that some birds have smaller brains than ours but more neurons per gram, someone else mentions that a dolphin brain is larger than a human one, and a third person insists the whole thing is offensive because it seems to imply something about humans with unusually small brains. I've realized the group members don't actually disagree about brain size; they disagree about a whole hidden cluster of views — whether moral status tracks capacities at all, whether it comes in levels or only thresholds, whether species membership does independent work — and "brain size" is just theoccasion where those views surface.

So I'd like you to write a "which view do you actually hold?" quiz for the group to take before the discussion. Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. The cases should be ones where the positions genuinely come apart, and they should be varied: real ones (corvids with dense small brains, dolphin brains larger than ours, humans with microcephaly), and invented ones so we can hold everything else fixed — say, a drug that shrinks cortical volume by twenty percent with no measurable effect on any behavior or capacity, or a selectively bred line of livestock with much smaller brains but identical performance on every cognitive test we can devise. No question should just ask "does brain size matter morally?" — the quiz should sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these leaves: Singer-style equal consideration of interests regardless of cognitive level; a threshold view where a certain cognitive package flips a switch and everything above it is equal; a graded or hierarchical view where moral status comes in degrees tracking capacities; a view on which species membership or relational facts do direct work; and the deflationary view that brain size is a mere proxy and nothing rides on it intrinsically. Build the tree from the logical space of answers first, then locate the positions, and note any unoccupied combinations with a sentence on whether nobody holds them because they're incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. Also flag any pair of leaves the quiz can't really separate — I suspect the graded-by-capacity view and the speciesist view will be hard to pry apart if the speciesist keeps appealing to capacities. One more thing: if you think the quiz as I've framed it is confused — say, because the invented cases smuggle in an assumption about what "no measurable effect on capacities" could even mean — please tell me that plainly rather than papering over it, and design around the problem or explain why it can't be designed around.
