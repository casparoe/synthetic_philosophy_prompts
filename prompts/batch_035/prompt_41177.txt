I'm basically an adaptationist about phenotypic traits — not the cartoon version where every trait is optimal, but the working-hypothesis version: when I see a complex trait, my default is that natural selection built it for something, and the research program should be to figure out what, starting from engineering-style reverse engineering. Gould and Lewontin's spandrels paper obviously took a run at this, and I know the exaptation and constraint stuff, but I've never really thought through what would actually make me give the position up rather than just... refine it.

So tell me what would count against it. I want the potential defeaters laid out properly, and I want you to think harder than the usual "just-so story" slogan everyone repeats. Some things I'd like covered: what empirical findings in evolutionary biology or genetics would genuinely embarrass adaptationism (vs. just being absorbed with a shrug), what the constraint and developmental-biology literature would have to show for it to be a problem rather than a detail, whether traits built by selection for one function then co-opted (exaptation, spandrels, whatever you want to call the Gould side of this) actually undermine my position or just relocate the adaptation one step back, and honestly whether the drift/neutral view of things at the molecular level touches my claim at all given that I said phenotypic traits.

Also — and this is the part I actually care about most — I want you to assess whether my version of the position has been qualified into unfalsifiability. It feels like whenever someone names a trait that looks nonadaptive, an adaptationist can say "it's linked to something adaptive" or "the selection pressure is there but weak" or "we haven't found the benefit yet," and those moves never cost anything. If that's a real escape hatch, say so. And if it is, tell me whether it's a defect for a research-program commitment of this kind (maybe a default that structures research doesn't need the same falsifiability as a hypothesis) or whether I'm just doing motivated reasoning with a lab coat on.

For each defeater, I want to know: would a committed but honest adaptationist concede it counts against them, or would they wave it off, and is the waving-off legitimate?
