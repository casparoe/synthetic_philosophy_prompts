Write a satirical internal memorandum, strictly no more than 600 words, from the newly appointed Director of Confirmatory Audit at a fictional ornithological research institute. The institute's sole mission is to establish the hypothesis that all ravens are black, and its bylaws enshrine Nicod's criterion as official evidential policy: since "all ravens are black" is logically equivalent to "all non-black things are non-ravens," any observation of a non-black non-raven counts as confirming evidence. The memo, written in the deadpan voice of a conscientious middle manager, should instruct field staff in the new compliance procedures this policy requires: the proper logging of white shoes, green umbrellas, and the director's own red car; what to do about objects whose color is indeterminate at dusk; a recent departmental dispute over whether auditing the campus cafeteria constitutes over-reliance on convenient but obviously irrelevant evidence, together with the committee's ruling on that dispute; and a closing morale paragraph observing that a single afternoon of inventorying the parking lot now advances the institute's mission more than a decade of slogging through bogs looking for actual ravens.

The comedy has to come from the real logic of Hempel's paradox, not from invented absurdities: the equivalence condition doing the work, the uncomfortable fact that on this way of counting the hypothesis is confirmed thousands of times a day, the nagging sense that something has gone wrong with a criterion that no one can point to a specific premise to reject, and the difficulty the director faces in formulating an official objection — since any principled complaint about relevance would seem to indict the bylaws themselves, which he is not authorized to do. A philosopher of science who knows the literature should recognize the piece as fair to the actual paradox and to the standard responses (appeals to background knowledge, to the minuscule degree of confirmation provided by a white shoe, to sampling from the wrong population), and should be able to tell throughout what is being ironized without the director ever winking at the reader. Please deliver the memo complete with a subject line, numbered sections, and a distribution list, and observe the 600-word cap strictly — trimming is part of the exercise.
