My grandmother passed away last spring and left behind notebooks full of rhymed Spanish verse — mostly lullabies and little occasion poems — and since I'm the "literary one" in the family, my cousins have decided im the one to translate them for the American side of the family. Cue three increasingly tense video calls. One cousin is adamant that the rhyme and meter have to survive, even if that means padding lines and fudging images, because the sing-song feel IS the poem, especially for bedtime use. Another says grandmother would've hated clunky padding, so meaning first and let the form go. A third thinks we should do both versions side by side and let readers pick, which honestly feels like a dodge.

While procrastinating on this I read Hofstadter's Le Ton beau de Marot, where he re-renders this tiny rhymed French get-well poem like a hundred different ways, convinced that the tight form is part of what the poem is and that dropping it loses something essential. But then I remembered everyone from Schleiermacher onward basically already said you either bring the reader to the author or the author to the reader, and here we are two centuries later with translators still just picking a side. Functionalists talk like the whole thing is settled once you ask what the translation is for; literalists act like fidelity to wording is the only real kind of fidelity; Hofstadter-types treat it as an aesthetic fact that the form is the meaning.

So my question: has this debate actually stalled because it's not really one debate? My working theory is that "faithful" is doing equivocation duty — faithful-to-words, faithful-to-effect, faithful-to-function are three different targets, and each family member (and each school of translation) is silently answering a different question. But maybe it's something else — a genuine substantive disagreement about whether form carries meaning at all, or just bedrock aesthetic temperament that nobody can argue anyone out of. Diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, using what the actual sides in the fight claim, and tell me honestly whether anything — a conceptual distinction, a decision procedure, whatever — would count as progress, or whether asking which approach is right is like asking whether jazz beats classical. Extra credit if the diagnosis actually helps me figure out what to tell my cousins.
