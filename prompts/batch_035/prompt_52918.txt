So my philosophy of mind reading group has been stuck on multiple realizability for like a year and a half, and I've started to suspect the disagreement isn't really about the facts at all. Wanted to run my half-baked theory past you and get a proper diagnosis.

Roughly where everyone stands: on one side you've got the people who think MR is just obviously true — pain or memory gets realized in mammal brains, octopus brains, maybe eventually silicon, and the physical details differ wildly, so there's no interesting physical kind common to all the realizers. On the other side you've got the skeptics, basically following Larry Shapiro's old challenge and then Polger and Shapiro's book length version of it. Their complaint is that nobody has ever actually *tested* multiple realization: when you look closely at the supposed cases, the realizers either aren't as dissimilar as advertised (pain across species converges on a lot of the same neurochemistry) or the "same psychological kind" was only counted as the same becuase the researchers already assumed function individuates the kind — Bechtel and Mundale made a point like this about how neuroscientists actually compare brain areas across species, the sameness in function is built in from the start. So the realists say "look at all these different realizers" and the skeptics say "you never established they're different *in the relevant way* or that the kinds are the same *in the relevant way*." And then it just loops. Nobody has budged.

My working theory is that the whole thing turns on grain — how coarsely you slice things at the psychological level and how coarsely at the physical level. Coarse mental kinds plus fine-grained physical descriptions makes MR trivially easy to "find"; fine mental kinds plus coarse physical ones makes it trivially hard to find. If that's right the debate is partly verbal, a bookkeeping convention dressed up as an empirical claim. But I'm honestly not sure, because the skeptics also seem to be making a substantive point that evolution is much more of a tinkerer-reuser than the textbook picture allows, and neural implementations might be way more constrained than functionalists assumed, which would be a real fact about the world and not a grain choice.

So: what kind of disagreement is this actually? Verbal, or a hidden empirical question about how constrained implementations are, or the two sides answering different questions (one defending a metaphysical thesis about kinds, the other policing a claim about scientific practice)? And given your diagnosis, is there anything that would count as progress here — a sharper way of individuating kinds, some actual comparative neuro data, or should we just accept that this one doesn't move and drop it from the syllabus?
