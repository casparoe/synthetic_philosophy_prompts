My housing co-op has been having a running fight about our house charter, and my housemate Dev used Sen's liberal paradox to argue the charter is basically incoherent, and now I can't decide whether he's proved way too much or is actually right. Let me reconstruct it as best I can.

First the concrete bit. The charter has two rules we all like. Rule 1: each member is the final authority over their own personal stuff — your bedroom, your habits, whatever counts as "yours". Rule 2: if literally every member unanimously prefers one house arrangement to another, the house goes with it (we call it "no vetoes on unanimity," seemed like the weakest possible requirement at the time).

Dev's example, using just me and him: I paint my room, his thing is practicing trumpet at 6am (thin walls). Suppose:
- If my wall stays beige he'd rather sleep in. If I paint it neon green he'd rather blast trumpet at dawn than lie there steaming about it. But overall he hates the green wall more than he enjoys the revenge.
- I'd rather have green than beige whatever he does, but I hate the 6am trumpet more than I love the green.

Now: I'm decisive on the paint, so green+quiet ranks above beige+quiet. We both prefer beige+quiet to green+trumpet — me because the trumpet is worse for me than green is good, him because green is worse for him than revenge is good — so by Rule 2, beige+quiet ranks above green+trumpet. And he's decisive on the trumpet, so green+trumpet ranks above green+quiet. Cycle. Fine, that part is just Sen's thing, I'd seen it before.

But then Dev goes further, and this is the part I cant shake:

(1) Rule 1 (personal authority) and Rule 2 (unanimity) jointly force a preference cycle, so "what the house prefers" is undefined whenever preferences cross like this.
(2) And they cross like this basically always. Every so-called personal matter is somebody else's business to at least some degree — my wall offends his eyes, my playlist ruins his afternoon, my habits disgust him. There's no sphere of choices that never enters anyone else's ranking.
(3) So the cycle can show up for any personal matter, any time anyone cares enough in the wrong way.
(4) A rule set that generates undefined collective preferences whenever one member is nosy or spiteful isn't a real charter, it's a liability.
(5) So we have to fix it at the root: either drop Rule 2, so one person's idiosyncratic attachment to their own stuff can veto literally everyone else's unanimous preference, or else accept that "personal authority" is a fiction and the collective ranking gets to override personal choices whenever a cycle threatens.
(6) Since nobody in the house will accept the first horn of (5), and the second horn licenses overriding basically any personal choice (which is exactly what the charter exists to prevent), the charter was incoherent from the start.

I think (6) proves way too much — Dev is basically saying no liberal-ish charter can work, ever. But I honestly can't find the bad step. I checked the two-person example myself and the cycle is really there. I'd like to keep both rules if there's a way (they're the whole point of the house for me), but if keeping them means accepting Dev's conclusion, tell me what I actually have to give up and what that costs. One more thing in case it matters: we're free to amend the charter however we want, including adding rules letting members commit in advance or trade away their authority over their own stuff. Dev says that option is just another way of admitting his point.
