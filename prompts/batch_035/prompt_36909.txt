I am a retired pharmacologist — most of my career in preclinical toxicology and safety pharmacology, with the last decade overseeing Phase I study design — and I have recently, in retirement, taken up reading in the philosophy and strategy of artificial intelligence. The alignment literature I keep encountering centers on a dispute I gather is called "prosaic alignment": the view that systems built from present-day methods (large-scale deep learning, reinforcement learning from human feedback, and whatever else is in the current toolkit) can be made safe and aligned without any fundamental conceptual breakthrough, versus the skeptical view that this program rests on methods too shallow for the problem and that something theoretically new is required.

Rather than another round of the usual arguments, I would like a translation exercise. Please restate the prosaic alignment position — and, as a control, the strongest opposing position — in the vocabulary of my own field, as faithfully as you can: dose–response and exposure–effect relationships, safety margins and therapeutic index, mechanism of action versus mechanism of toxicity, ADME (the disposition of a compound in a system), the phased clinical trial structure, post-marketing surveillance, and idiosyncratic toxicity that no preclinical program predicts. Be disciplined about what the mapping commits you to: if scaling laws are dose–response curves, say what is playing the role of the organism; if RLHF is post-marketing surveillance, say what would correspond to withdrawal from the market. Then give me the residue report I actually care about: which parts of the alignment debate survive the translation cleanly, which survive only by distortion (where my vocabulary smuggles in assumptions — e.g., that the compound's chemistry is fixed between exposures, or that the patient and the sponsor are different parties), and which parts have no counterpart in pharmacology at all. Finally, tell me your honest view of the residue: does it indicate that the pharmacologist's vocabulary is missing something essential, or that portions of the alignment debate are less substantive than their prominence suggests? I am fluent in the target vocabulary, so do not soften the technical mapping to make it accessible.
