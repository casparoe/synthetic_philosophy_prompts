I've been arguing with a friend about whether fundamental physics could matter morally. Take the proposition that there is some morally significant valence, however slight, in the states of elementary physical systems, such that the total disvalue instantiated in non-biological matter dwarfs everything happening in nervous systems — so that, on some framings, heating a cup of coffee would be a moral event. My credence in this is about 0.15; hers is about 0.4. Please give me your own number, but more importantly decompose it into at least these terms: the probability that some panpsychist or pan-protopsychist thesis is true (rather than the apparent plausibility of such theses being an artifact of our ignorance of the intrinsic nature of physical properties); the probability that, given that, the micro-level qualities include a valence dimension rather than being affectively neutral precursors; the probability that they combine into aggregates that are morally considerable rather than morally negligible in virtue of their structure; and any deflationary terms you think belong — for instance, that even if all this is true, the valence is distributed so uniformly that no feasible action changes the total in a way that matters. Assign each term a rough probability with one line of justification, show how they combine into your overall credence, and then tell me which term you are least confident in and what argument or observation would move it most. Finally: given that my friend and I sit at 0.15 and 0.4, where do you think our disagreement most plausibly lives — in the metaphysics, in the valence question, or in the moral aggregation — and what single consideration should one of us bring to the other?
