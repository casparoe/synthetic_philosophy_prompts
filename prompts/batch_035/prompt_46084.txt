Covering the archaeology/climate beat for a mid-sized outlet, mostly explainers and the occasional long read. There's a pattern I keep running into and I'd like to know whether it has an established name, because right now I end up circling it with different phrasing in every other piece.

The pattern: a society is said to have "collapsed," and when you dig into what actually ended, it's almost always one specific layer that ended — the layer that wrote things down, commissioned the monuments, ran the palace or the tribute system. Classic Maya: the southern dynasties stop putting up stelae, the royal courts dissolve, and that gets called the Maya collapse — but the Maya obviously didn't go anywhere, millions of their descendants are alive today, and meanwhile in the north cities like Chichen Itza keep building for centuries, which nobody calls a collapse. We just can't see the "collapsed" era very well because our window onto it was the inscriptions, and the inscriptions stopped. Bronze Age: the palaces burn, Linear B goes silent, and it reads as a general dark age, but villages carry on, pottery traditions continue, people keep speaking Greek. Easter Island is the messiest — the classic ecocide story is built almost entirely out of the statues plus population guesses reconstructed from much later visitors, and now some archaeologists are arguing the crash, if there was one, came after European contact, with disease and slave raids — so whether that society "collapsed" seems to depend on who you count and from which decade.

So here's the thing I keep noticing and can't name: the end of record-keeping gets read as the end of the people who were being recorded, or at least as everything going dark, even when there's decent evidence for continuity at the village level. It's a kind of evidence bias — elite and monumental stuff is what survives, what we can date, so the end of elite production looks like the lights going out for everyone. Is there an established term in archaeology or historiography for this exact failure mode? I know about survivorship bias and that's not it — if anything the elite material is the over-represented part. "Dark age" is the symptom, not the cause. And everyone knows "history is written by the winners," but that cliché doesn't by itself explain why gaps get over-read as catastrophes.

If there is no established name, could you coin one and define it — including the edges? Boundary cases I'd want covered: when does record loss actually track mass death (the Black Death killed a third of Europe and the record-keeping barely flinched — is that the contrast class?), and what about cases where the people really do scatter and the "society" dissolves even if the population survives? Basically I'm after the carving, not just a label — nearest existing concepts, how each one misses, and what fits best.

Reason I'm asking: my editor keeps letting me write "collapsed" in these stories, and I'd like one sentence in the next piece that names the distortion instead of repeating it. Don't need a bibliography, I'll sort sourcing myself — just the concept and the arguments around it. Sorry for the wall of text, been sitting on this one for awhile.
