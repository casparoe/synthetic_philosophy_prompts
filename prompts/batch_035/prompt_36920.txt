I've been reading the rationalist decision-theory threads for a good while now — the ones about Parfit's hitchhiker, where the driver will take you out of the desert only if she predicts you'll pay her once you're safe in town — and there's a move that gets made in every one of them that I've never seen properly examined. When someone objects that paying, once you're standing in town with your wallet out and the driver's prediction already made, is just throwing money away, the reply is that deciding on a policy now is like choosing principles behind Rawls's veil of ignorance: you don't yet know whether you're the self stranded in the desert or the self in town, and from behind that veil you'd obviously want to be someone who pays. From there the move gets extended in the usual ways — updateless decision theory is described as Rawls for a single agent, the veil is said to cover your copies across possible worlds, precommitment devices get compared to constitutional conventions binding successor generations.

I'd like a proper audit of this analogy. Give me the correspondence table first: what plays the role of the parties in the original position, the veil itself, the choice of principles, the strain of commitment. Then tell me where the mapping breaks, and pay special attention to two places I suspect it does: first, the veil trades on fairness among distinct persons with potentially conflicting interests, while the hitchhiker's two selves share interests exactly — so it's unclear why the "would have chosen" of one agent should have the standing that "would have agreed" has among parties bargaining on behalf of others; second, Rawls's choosers are kept ignorant of their probabilities, while the hitchhiker knows precisely what's at stake at each point in the story. Then say which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion the analogy is being used to license, and whether that conclusion — that you should pay, or at least should be the kind of agent who pays — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the veil was carrying it the whole time. If there's a better analogy for what precommitment is doing in the case, I want to hear it; if the honest answer is that no analogy is doing any work and the dispute should be settled some other way, say that instead.
