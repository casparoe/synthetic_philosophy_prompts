Been on these blogs since roughly 2012, read all the sequences-adjacent stuff, usually just lurk, but a friend of mine (huge Dennett guy, we've gone rounds about real patterns before) walked me into an argument last weekend and I can't find the exit.

The setup, which I mostly buy: Darwin pulled off this weird inversion. Before him, whenever you found something that looked designed — an eye, a wing — you posited a comprehending designer behind it. Darwin showed you can get all that competence with nobody comprehending anything: blind selection, grinding away. Dennett says Turing did the same move for thought: a machine churning syntax produces what looks like understanding, and nothing inside has to get the meaning. Competence without comprehension, cranes not skyhooks, the whole bit. Ok. On board so far. Now the actual argument:

(1) Every competence we have ever explained turned out to be produced by a process that did not itself comprehend what it was producing — selection built the body, syntax drives the mind.

(2) So comprehension is never needed as a premise in any explanation of any competence. Point at any competence you like and there is a no-comprehension story that covers it.

(3) Never believe in a posit that does no explanatory work anywhere. (I actually like this principle — it just seems like the skyhooks lesson again, and I don't want to give it up.)

(4) Therefore comprehension does not exist. Nobody has ever understood anything, including the two of us right now — there was nobody home doing the getting-it when I read Dennett any more than there was a designer behind the eye.

(5) And by the same token nothing ever will: a superintelligence that talks circles around us still won't comprehend anything, ever, by definition, because comprehension was never a real thing to begin with.

That last step is where I get off the bus, but I can't say exactly where the bus went wrong. Something between (2) and (4), I think, but every time I try to name it my friend says that's just me flinching from the conclusion the way people flinched at Darwin, and honestly the frame he puts it in sounds right to me. The annoying part is he embraces the too-much: he's stopped saying he understands French (he grew up in Montreal!), he just says his French-producing competence runs reliably. He thinks this is a feature of the view. He also now corrects people at parties who say the chess engine understands the position, which, whatever, but it does make me suspect something's off.

So: which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost? If I have to choose, I'd rather keep (3) than accept (4), and I want to know whether keeping (3) forces me to say comprehension is a real pattern in the way a center of gravity is a real pattern. My friend grudgingly accepts centers of gravity but claims understanding can't be like that since the intentional stance is for prediction, not ontology — though I may be mangling him there, he'd had a few. Also tell me if I'm wrong that something slipped between (2) and (4), because if the argument is actually valid then I guess I need to hear which premise is cheapest to drop and why it looked so plausible to both of us.
