I've just left a teaching post at a sixth-form college to work as an education tutor in a prison, and last week I attended the induction day for new staff. The centrepiece was a talk by one of the trainers, a committed enthusiast for what she called the Scandinavian model, and since my daily work will be shaped by what I took from that room, I want to know how much of it to trust. For background: I took the job precisely because I believe education is the one intervention that genuinely changes people inside, but I have never studied penology, and I found the talk inspiring in a way I suspect may not survive scrutiny — I can't tell inspiration from overstatement.

Her summary, as best I noted it down, ran as follows: Norway abolished the idea that prison exists to punish. Sentences serve only to rehabilitate, so they are kept as short as the person's risk allows; and since nothing within the sentence is meant to hurt, conditions inside replicate ordinary life as closely as security permits — Halden prison has cells resembling student rooms, no bars on the windows, and officers trained half as guards and half as social workers. The results settle the argument: Norwegian reoffending rates are a fraction of American ones, while the United States, the world leader in incarceration, keeps millions in cages out of little more than a taste for vengeance. So the retributive model has been tried and found wanting, and anyone who believes prison should hurt is part of the problem. That closing remark was, I think, aimed at some of the longer-serving officers present, and it made for an awkward afternoon.

Please take her account apart claim by claim and tell me which parts a defender of the Norwegian model would accept as accurate, which are simplifications a specialist might tolerate in a popular talk, and which are genuine misrepresentations. I have my own suspicions about two points in particular: whether punishment was really abolished, or merely relocated to the deprivation of liberty itself; and whether reoffending statistics can refute a retributive justification at all, given that retribution was never a promise to reduce reoffending. Then assess whether her practical conclusion — that believing in punishment as such disqualifies a person from this work — actually follows from whatever survives the audit. For each verdict, please state how confident you are in it, and close by singling out the point in her summary, or in your own corrections, that you would be least sure of.
