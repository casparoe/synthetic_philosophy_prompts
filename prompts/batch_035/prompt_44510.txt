I'm writing a term paper for a moral psychology course on whether cross-cultural variation in moral judgment undermines moral objectivity. I've drafted the core argument paragraph, pasted below, and I'd like comments on it before I build the rest of the paper around it. Please focus on three things: whether the central inference actually works, whether the empirical claims are roughly right (flag anything I've misremembered or oversimplified), and whether the argument is interesting or just a standard relativist riff I could find anywhere.

Here's the draft: "WEIRD subjects — Western, educated, industrialized, rich, democratic — are moral outliers, and the moral psychology described in intro textbooks is a local curiosity rather than human nature. Three illustrations. First, Shweder's interviews in Bhubaneswar in the 80s: people there organized moral life around three ethics — autonomy, community, divinity — such that things WEIRD people file under taste or religion, like violations of purity norms, were treated as full-blown moral crimes, while people in the US basically reduced morality to harm and rights. Second, ultimatum games: WEIRD subjects offer close to half the pot and punish lowball offers, and even bystanders will pay to punish unfair strangers. But Henrich ran the game with the Machiguenga in the Peruvian Amazon and got offers averaging about a quarter with essentially zero rejections, and in the larger cross-cultural project offers rose with market integration — the fairness that feels written into the universe turns out to track how much your economy runs on dealing with strangers. Third, incest: the prohibition that feels most objective of all seems to be stamped in by co-residence rather than genetic relatedness — kids raised together in Israeli kibbutzim almost never married each other, and Taiwanese minor marriages, where the bride was adopted as a child and raised alongside her future husband, fell apart far more often than other arranged marriages (if I'm remembering Wolf's findings right). So the felt obviousness of one of our deepest taboos tracks a contingent psychological mechanism. Conclusion: our strongest moral intuitions are products of our local social arrangement, and their feeling of objectivity is just what a locally produced intuition feels like from the inside. We should hold moral objectivity, if we hold it at all, very loosely."
