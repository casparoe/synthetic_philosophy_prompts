A friend of mine against the death penalty keeps running this one by me and I cant locate the broken step. Set aside deterrence and retribution — this is only about the innocence angle. (1) The strongest objection to capital punishment is innocence risk: the state may not run a punishment scheme that will foreseeably inflict the punishment's full harm on some factually innocent people. (2) What makes that bite isn't error as such — every punishment scheme has error — but irreversibility: once you're dead, nothing can be given back. (3) But irreversibility in that sense isn't unique to death. An innocent man who serves life without parole, or even forty years, doesn't get those decades back either — no compensation restores them — so by his own criterion LWOP is exactly as objectionable as execution. (4) And it goes all the way down: nobody can hand you back three lost years, so any carceral sentence is irreversible too. (5) So either the innocence objection condemns all imprisonment along with the death penalty, or it condemns neither. He says he's fine with the first disjunct since he's half an anarchist about punishment anyway; I'm not. I want to keep the innocence objection as a genuine objection to the death penalty without it sweeping in every prison sentence ever imposed, and I have a feeling (3) or (4) is doing something sneaky with irreversible, but I can't put my finger on what. Which step do I reject, and what does rejecting it cost me?
