I'm not an academic — I work in logistics — but I've spent a couple of years thinking hard about a question I can't resolve by reading alone, and I want to approach a small animal-welfare fund that gives grants for non-academic research projects. They ask for a two-page proposal, and everything I've written so far rambles. Here is my half-formed idea, and I'd like you to turn it into a proper proposal I could actually send.

The question: when people compare the ethics of eating beef versus chicken, they usually count deaths per meal. Roughly one cow is spread across hundreds of meals, while one chicken is one or two meals, so chicken comes out looking far worse. But I've come to suspect the counting is inconsistent across the comparisons I've seen. Some count the animals killed during feed-crop cultivation — the mice, birds, and others that die when fields are plowed and harvested — and some don't. Some weigh the length of the animal's life, some only the death. Nobody seems to agree on whether insects killed in cultivation belong in the tally at all, or whether the fish ground into fishmeal for chicken feed count. My own attempt was to convert everything into animal-hours: for each kilogram of protein, how many hours of sentient life of what quality are spent producing it. That got me stuck on pasture-raised beef, which may cause very few cultivation deaths while the cow is alive for over a year, versus a broiler chicken that lives six wretched weeks — and I honestly can't tell whether an hour of a grazing cow's life should net out positive, negative, or whether the two kinds of hour simply aren't comparable in the way my ledger assumes.

Please write the proposal. It should state the question precisely enough that it could in principle be answered, explain why the standard ways of framing the comparison leave it open, and set out the method: what I would actually do over six months, month by month, as a non-academic with access to published figures but no lab, no farm, and no fieldwork budget. It should say what a finished result would look like, and what would honestly count as the project failing — including whether my animal-hours idea should be abandoned rather than refined. The fund will want to know why a non-philosopher should be funded to think about this rather than the scientists who actually measure animal welfare, so include a short section answering that directly. Don't include any citations, bibliography, or reading list; I'll add references myself. Keep the whole proposal under 800 words.
