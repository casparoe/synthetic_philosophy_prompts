My wife and I are arguing about whether to try for a baby this year or wait three years until we can move. The issue: we live right next to a freight corridor, the air quality is genuinely bad, and there's evidence early-childhood exposure matters for development. My brother-in-law, who did a philosophy degree, says the air thing shouldn't really factor into our timing. His reasoning: the kid we'd conceive now and the kid we'd conceive in three years would be two different people. So if we wait, there's no child who's worse off because of our choice — and the child we'd have now can't complain about being brought into existence in worse conditions, since the alternative was never her existing at all. No victim, no wrong, he says, so timing is basically a wash as far as the kid's interests go.

Something about this feels off to me. It seems like it'd license parents being pretty careless — he even conceded the same logic would apply to a mother who drinks heavily all through a wanted pregnancy, and while he admits that conclusion sounds monstrous, he can't locate the flaw. Neither can I, honestly, but I also can't believe the conclusion. Is his argument actually sound, and if not, where exactly does it break? And practically: should the different-person point change how much weight we put on waiting?
