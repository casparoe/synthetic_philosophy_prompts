Anyway, here's what I want. Take Moore's hand-raising proof of an external world — the lecture where he holds up his hands, asserts that here's one hand and here's another, and deduces that things outside his mind exist — and restate it, plus the whole skeptical fight around it, in the vocabulary I actually think in now: theorems, `axiom` declarations, the trusted kernel, proof terms, `sorry` holes, the difference between a well-founded proof and one that quietly assumes what it's proving. And the standard skeptical comeback — that Moore can know the premises only if he antecedently knows he's not dreaming, so the proof transmits no warrant to anyone who started out doubting the conclusion — looks to me like a claim about the *epistemic pedigree* of the premises, not about validity. My hunch is that the kernel is, by design, totally silent on that distinction: it happily accepts an axiom and a proof built on it is exactly as mechanically fine as any other.

And then the real question I'm circling: does the residue show that the proof-assistant vocabulary is missing something epistemically important, or does it show that the Moore debate was less substantive than it looks — essentially a dispute about which axioms get to be axioms, a dispute the system itself is structurally mute on?
