Hi, I'll try keep this reasonably short. I've just been voted onto the commitee of a residents' association in a medium-sized English town (~40k people), and after a really ugly row about a housing development proposal — half the estate feels the committee just decided over their heads — a few of us are seriously considering pushing for some form of referendum on big decisions. Not full-on Swiss direct democracy obviously, more like a binding or semi-binding vote of members whenever a decision is above some threshold of controversy, possibly paired with a citizens' jury or some deliberative forum beforehand. So a two-part contraption: deliberate first, then vote.

Before I draft a proposal for the next AGM I want to get my priorities straight, because the debates around referendums seem endless and I don't want to burn the committee's goodwill on questions that turn out not to matter at our scale. So: which of the open questions and controversies about direct democracy genuinely matter most for a setup like ours, and which are overrated? Things I can already see people fighting about at the meeting: whether ballot wording is manipulative (and who gets to fix it), whether turnout thresholds should be able to invalidate a result, whether votes on fiddly planning questions mostly measure who ran the best leaflet campaign, whether repeated voting erodes trust or actually builds civic muscle, and whether agenda-setting power just quietly moves from the committee to whoever decides what counts as "controversial enough to trigger a vote" (that last one I suspect is underrated and would especially like your take on). But I honestly can't tell which of these deserves our limited meeting time and member attention.

What I want is a defended ranking, not a survey. Set out your criteria first — how much difference the answer would make at our scale, how tractable it is for volunteers with no social scientists on call, whether it's upstream of the other questions — then the ordering itself, then what you'd deliberately punt on and why. Also flag wherever the standard argument assumes a national-scale vote (Brexit, California ballot props, Swiss double majorities) and quietly breaks at 40k people with a volunteer committee — I'd rather know now than find out at the AGM. Oh, and please write the whole thing in British English.
