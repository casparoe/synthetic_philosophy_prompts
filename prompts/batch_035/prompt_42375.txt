I write novels for a living and my brother-in-law is a neuroscientist who loves to needle me at holidays. His go-to move: everything I write about — grief, ambition, a character falling in love — is ultimately just chemistry. Water is just H2O, life is just chemistry, love is just oxytocin and dopamine, so the stuff my novels traffic in is a convenient way of talking about something that's fully accounted for at a lower level. He even compared it to calling a table solid when it's mostly empty space. To be fair he's not saying novels are bad — he says he enjoyed my last one — he's saying the higher-level description does no real explanatory work, it's a shorthand for humans who cant do the real science.

I have two reactions and they pull in opposite directions. One: fine, its all chemistry, and yet the love in a chapter does something no molecule-level description does — my editor can tell me the love scene doesnt work, and no brain scan of the reader could tell me that, or not in any way I could use. Two: I suspect I'm mainly defending my paycheck.

So take his analogy seriously for me and then audit it. Water is to H2O as love is to oxytocin (and, I guess he'd add, as my novel is to its sentences, and traffic is to cars). Give me the mapping — what plays the role of the molecules, the science of molecules, the macro-property, the "solid table." Then show me where the mapping breaks. I half-remember that the water case has some wrinkle about the higher-level facts being fixed by the lower ones without being *deducible* from them in any useful way, or about multiple realizability, but I dont trust my memory of it — does that wrinkle help him or help me? And is "nothing but" doing the same job in each case, or is he sliding between "fully caused by" and "fully captured by"? Finally: is there a better analogy available for the novelist's situation — is a novel's relation to its sentences more like reduction, more like emergence, or does that whole framing already concede too much? What I most want to know is whether his conclusion — that nothing at the novel's level is doing genuine work — survives once the disanalogies are laid out, or whether the analogy was carrying that conclusion the entire time. Please be honest even if the honest answer is that my editor's job is the one that's fake.
