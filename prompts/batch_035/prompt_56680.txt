Play her as a sharp, good-faith philosopher who takes the machinery of extrapolation seriously and who is willing to concede genuine side difficulties but will never abandon the core claim: that extrapolation toward idealized conditions is a better target for alignment than any poll of present preferences. Here is my opening move. I come from a family of quite devout believers, and it struck me that if you ran CEV on my grandmother, extrapolating her under conditions of greater knowledge and stronger rationality, you would presumably strip away her belief in the literal resurrection of the dead — and with it, the charitable and communal life that belief anchors. It looks as though your extrapolation procedure has a built-in direction: it idealizes people toward the reflectively endorseable, coherent, evidence-responsive end of the spectrum, which is just to say it idealizes them toward people like the philosophers who designed the procedure.
