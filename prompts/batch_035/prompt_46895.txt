I run the tutorial programme for a second-year applied ethics course, and I want a decision-tree handout that sorts students into a position on the nonidentity problem before they have met any of the labels. The tree should start from a single entry point — a depletion policy that predictably lowers the quality of future people's lives, where those people will exist only because the policy was adopted — and branch only on questions a student can answer from her own reactions to cases, never from terminology. At least five and at most seven branch questions, none of which may use the phrase 'nonidentity' or any position's name; at least three of them must concern whether the student's objection requires comparing the person's life with a counterfactual in which she does not exist, whether a wrong needs an identifiable victim who is worse off, and whether the person-affecting intuition — that morality is about making people better off — is worth keeping at any price. The tree must route students through at least these diagnostic cases along the way: the depletion case itself; a risky nuclear-waste policy that foreseeably damages the lives of later people who would not have existed without the policy; a couple using IVF who can select either an embryo certain to have a lifelong but manageable impairment or one certain not to, where both resulting children would have lives worth living; and a case of historical injustice that is the condition of the victims' descendants existing at all. Build the tree from the logical space first and only then locate named views in it: the leaves should include impersonal consequentialism, a counterfactual-comparison view, a contractualist or 'wronging without harming' view, a rights-based view on which a person can be wronged by the very conditions of her origin, and a debunking view on which the relevant intuitions are a tracking error. Note any branch combinations no leaf occupies, and say for each whether it is incoherent, unattractive or merely unexplored; also flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot genuinely separate. Please write in British English, in three sections in this order: 'The branch questions and why they cut' (roughly 400 words), 'The tree' (an indented outline or diagram, any length), and 'Leaves, gaps and indistinguishable pairs' (roughly 300 words).
