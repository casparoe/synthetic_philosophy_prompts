I've got a podcast interview coming up with a philosopher whose new book defends the extended mind thesis, but with a twist: she wants to replace the famous Otto-and-the-notebook style coupling-constituency talk with what she calls a mutual calibration condition. Her line is that something counts as part of your mind only if it and your brain have gotten tuned to each other through use, such that the system as a whole has competences neither part has alone -- so on her view a stranger's diary you glance at isn't in the loop, but your own heavily-reworked notebook is, and the interesting hard cases are things like your phone's autocomplete, which you didn't calibrate in any normal sense but which has calibrated itself to you.

She's also, judging from her talks, very quick with the standard deflections: that the thesis is about cognition and not consciousness so the zombie intuition proves nothing, and that Rupert's embedded-but-not-extended alternative is just a labeling dispute.

Write me ten or so questions that actually dig into the calibration idea instead of making her re-litigate 1998. Specifically I want at least one question each on: (1) whether calibration can be done for you, like autocomplete does, or whether she's smuggling agency back in; (2) whether the condition really blocks the bloat problem or just moves it to asking how much joint tuning is enough, which sounds just as vague as access-and-trust ever was; (3) therapy, prosthetics, and other cases where something becomes part of you by compensating for a deficit rather than by being tuned to a healthy baseline -- does the condition treat these right; and (4) what a disagreement with her would even look like, i.e. what evidence or a case should make her give the view up. For each question give the follow-up I should ask based on the answer she's most likely to give, since she's a good talker and will steer anything vague toward consciousness, which we've agreed beforehand not to spend the whole episode on.
