My study group has been fighting about this for two weeks and I got volunteered to write up the strongest version of the other side, and now I can't beat my own writeup. Two cases. Counterfactual mugging: a predictor flips a fair coin. Heads, he quietly puts $1M in your account and you never hear from him. Tails, he shows up, proves he predicted in advance (correctly) what you'd do, and asks you for $100. The functional decision theory people say pay: the decision function that pays is the one that walks away with $1M half the time, even though in the actual world the money never existed and paying is just lighting $100 on fire.

XOR blackmail (I'm probably butchering the exact numbers, but the structure is the point): the same kind of predictor either has dirt on you or doesn't, and he sends you a letter demanding $1k iff (you have dirt AND he predicted you won't pay) OR (you have no dirt AND he predicted you'll pay). Say exposure of the dirt costs you way more than $1k. The letter is in your mailbox. FDT says refuse: if your decision function had output "pay" instead, the letter would only exist if you were dirt-free, so the paying function buys letters full of nothing, while the refusing function either never gets a letter or gets one backed by real dirt that paying $1k wouldn't actually clean up... wait, no, paying would suppress the dirt. Anyway, FDT refuses.

Now the argument I'm stuck on:
1. In both cases there's a past event (the deposit / the letter) that counterfactually depends on your decision function's output, via the predictor's simulation of you.
2. In both cases the output that's best "across the linked worlds" differs from the output that's causally best in the actual world.
3. FDT pays in the mugging and refuses in the blackmail, and claims both verdicts fall out of one principle: choose the output of your decision function that yields the most utility across the worlds where that function's output matters.
4. But run the principle in the blackmail case while holding fixed that you DO have dirt. The linked worlds are then: your function refuses and the letter arrives, versus your function pays and no letter is sent. Across exactly those worlds, paying looks better, the same way paying looks worse in the tails branch of the mugging.
5. So the principle gives opposite verdicts depending on which linked worlds you hold fixed, which means "best output across linked worlds" isn't doing any real work — the actual FDT verdicts must be coming from something else (a gut sense that extortion should be discouraged), and the single-principle story is decoration.
6. Therefore FDT is either ad hoc or needs a rule for WHICH linked worlds count that nobody has stated.

I'm fairly sure something breaks at step 4 — my instinct is it has something to do with the dirt being an independent fact my decision function doesn't get to vary — but the coin in the mugging is also independent of my function, so that can't be the whole story. Which step should I reject, and what does rejecting it commit me to? And if there IS a principled rule for which linked worlds count, I want to know what it says about plain predicted blackmail — letter sent iff (you have dirt AND he predicted you won't pay), no XOR clause. Does the rule refuse there too, and if so, am I allowed to think that's a reductio?
