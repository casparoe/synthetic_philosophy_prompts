I'm a second-year student taking a political philosophy course, and I've been trying to get clear on luck egalitarianism, which my professor mentioned only in passing before moving on to Rawls. Over the weekend a friend of mine who listens to a lot of philosophy podcasts gave me his summary, and I want to know how much of it I can trust. Here is roughly what he said: luck egalitarianism is the view that the only inequalities justice permits are the ones people are responsible for — everything down to luck must be neutralized by redistribution, while everything flowing from your own choices is yours to keep. So a luck egalitarian society would compensate people for bad luck in the genetic and social lottery, but let gamblers and people who chose their predicament live with the consequences. He then said the whole view collapses on the case of someone who makes a genuinely free but catastrophic choice — the reckless driver who ends up paralyzed — since that person chose their plight, luck egalitarianism tells us not to help them, and so the view ends up abandoning exactly the people who most need help. He concluded that this is why most egalitarians gave up on luck egalitarianism and went back to Rawls, whose theory doesn't hold anyone responsible for anything.

I'm inclined to find the view attractive, or at least I was before he told me this, but he was very confident and the responsibility part sounds like something a serious theorist would say. Go through his summary claim by claim and tell me what a luck egalitarian would accept as a fair statement of their view, what they'd want to qualify, and what they'd flatly deny — especially whether the view really requires withholding aid from people whose situations result from their own choices, and whether the conclusion that egalitarians have abandoned the position follows from anything true in what he said. Also, if I've garbled his summary in relaying it to you, or if the question rests on some confusion about what luck egalitarianism even is, please say so plainly rather than working around it.
