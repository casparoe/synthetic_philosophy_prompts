A friend and I spent most of yesterday evening arguing about the inefficacy objection, and I want to put a number on where we actually diverge. The proposition, made precise: a typical individual's decision to take one discretionary round-trip flight — say a transatlantic vacation trip she could forgo at modest personal cost, with no special obligation riding on it — is morally wrong all-things-considered. My credence in that proposition is about 0.7; my friend's is about 0.15. His core claim is that the expected causal effect of one flight is essentially zero: airlines set capacity in batches, marginal emissions are absorbed by fixed schedules, and if she stayed home someone else would take the seat at a slightly lower price, so the act makes no difference to the world and therefore, he says, cannot be wrong. My resistance is that even if the expected effect is tiny, it is not obviously zero, and wrongness may not track expected differences anyway.

Please give me your own credence in the proposition, and more importantly the decomposition behind it. I have in mind at least these sub-questions, though you may add or restructure terms if you think the question decomposes differently: (1) that a threshold model of collective harms is correct, on which individual acts can be decisive at the margin even when no single act is visibly decisive; (2) that, given such a model, a single discretionary flight has non-negligible expected effect, considering how demand elasticity and airline scheduling actually respond to one fewer purchase; (3) that even if the expected effect is vanishingly small, the act could still be wrong on other grounds — fairness, complicity, or the expected value of a small probability of a large effect; (4) that the personal cost of forgoing the trip counts as modest enough that any of the above can generate an obligation rather than a mere aspiration. Assign a rough probability to each term with a one-line justification, show how they combine into your overall number, tell me which term the final credence is most sensitive to and what evidence or argument would move it most, and then say where my disagreement with my friend most likely lives: which of his 0.15 and my 0.7 is closer to the truth matters less to me than knowing which single term we would need to resolve to settle it.
