I write novels, and I've gotten myself into an odd corner. I'm drafting this book where the narrator keeps insisting people don't really change, they just get revealed, and her old mentor keeps insisting the opposite — everyone is a river, nobody steps in the same man twice, that kind of thing. Somewhere in the middle of this I fell down a rabbit hole and discovered actual philosophers have been fighting about a version of this for a century under names I'd never heard: endurantism vs perdurantism. Apparently one camp says you persist through time by being wholly present at every moment you exist, and the other says you persist the way a road stretches through space — you're spread out across your whole history, and your temporal parts do the work. Which, if I understand it, means my narrator and her mentor are both wrong in ways neither of them suspects, and I'd like to find out exactly how.

Here's what I want: write me a dialogue between a committed endurantist and a committed perdurantist. I picture them as two old friends on a long train ride arguing about whether the man who gets off at the final station is the same man who boarded, but honestly the setting is up to you. Make them actually fight, though. I've read enough bad fiction to know how these scenes go wrong: one character is obviously the author's mouthpiece and the other exists to lose. I want the perdurantist to land real punches (the problem of temporary intrinsics keeps coming up in my reading — how can the same guy be bent at noon and straight at midnight without contradicting himself?) and I want the endurantist to land real punches back (my impression is the "temporal parts" move gets accused of redescribing persistence rather than explaining it, and there's something about counting — how many thinkers are in a thinker's chair over an hour — that's supposed to be embarrassing). Ideally they each walk away having conceded something real without either converting, because that's honest and also better for my book.

Then, separately from the dialogue, give me a short note on what's settled versus what's genuinely contested among people who know this stuff. Like, I'm fairly sure the temporary intrinsics problem is a standard, real argument and not something I hallucinated — but I have no idea whether the replies I've half-absorbed are the standard replies or whether I've accidentally handed one side a cheat sheet. Flag anywhere the dialogue leans on a contested reading, or gives one character a move that serious people on the other side think just fails. Don't give me citations or a reading list — I cant check references from where I am, and I'd rather you not guess at them. And don't be too confident about which side is currently winning; my sense is this is a live fight, not a closed one, and if you think otherwise I'd want to hear the case.
