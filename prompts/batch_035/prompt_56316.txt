Quick recap in my own words — correct me if I mangle a detail: a billionaire offers you $1M, paid tonight at 9pm, on one condition: at 9pm you must sincerely intend to drink a vial of toxin at 10pm that evening. The catch: at 9pm you know that at 10pm you'll already have the money and no reason left to be sick, so you won't drink; and knowing that in advance, you can't actually form the intention at 9pm; so the billionaire (who can tell real intent from posing) never pays. One-shot analysis says you walk away with nothing and that this is somehow the "rational" outcome, which is what makes it a puzzle.

Here's what I want built: a variant where the verdict flips — where it IS rational to form the intention and collect the money — without touching the payoff structure. Same million, same sick day, same timeline, and no making the toxin secretly pleasant or the deal sweeter in any way. The only lever you're allowed to pull is the agent's own future information state: the billionaire also offers, free of charge, a pill taken at 9:30 that permanently erases the knowledge that the toxin is harmful — and the agent knows in advance that the pill does exactly this and nothing else. Everything else stays as in the original.

Constraints on the scenario itself: one paragraph, same genre as the original (billionaire, vial, evening timeline), and no perfect predictors — the billionaire just has a reliable way of telling sincere intent from faking it, think a polygraph that actually works.
