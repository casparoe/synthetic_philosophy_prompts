I've been in an abortion ethics reading group for about a year and its finally dawned on me that nobody has changed their mind on the core question since roughly the eighties, and I'd like a diagnosis of why, rather then another round of the arguments themselves.

The state of play, so you know what I mean: the Thomson side argues from bodily rights — the violinist plugged into your kidneys is meant to show that even a full-blown person with a whole life ahead of it doesn't get to use your body without your consent, and the variants (the burglar who climbs in because you opened a window, the people-seeds you tried to keep out with a flimsy screen) exist to handle the comeback that if you let it happen through your own risky behaviour, you're responsible for it. There's also the move that society can at most demand you be a minimally decent samaritan, not a good one. The Marquis side argues that what makes killing wrong is depriving someone of a future like ours, and a fetus has one, so abortion is wrong for the same reason killing you or me is; the standard stress test of that argument is whether it also condemns contraception, and there's the well-known reply that in the contraception case there's no determinate individual that gets deprived, which the other side finds ad hoc.

What I want to know is what kind of stall this actually is. My working theory — offered as a theory, I could be wrong — is that the two camps are answering different questions under one heading: the future people are really arguing about the ethics of killing, the violinist people are really arguing about what one person may demand of another's body, and when they collide each side treats the other's criterion as an optional extra you'd only accept if you'd already lost. Is that right? Or is there a hidden shared question — maybe whether death is a deprivation at all, or who bears the burdens of rescue — that would actually settle it if we could? Or is it bedrock intuitions all the way down, with the published arguments doing mostly rationalising work? And given whichever diagnosis you favour, what would count as progress here: a distinction, a test case that forces everyone to bite a bullet, a change of question? Or should I just accept that this one is genuinely hard rather than ill-posed?

Answer in American English please. And no reading lists or citations — I've read the papers, I want the diagnosis, not a bibliography.
