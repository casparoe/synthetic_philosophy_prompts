I'm on the rules committee of a 40-unit housing co-op, and every month we relitigate proposed house rules from scratch -- quiet hours, subletting, whether you can book the common room for your cousin's birthday. I've gotten into the republican idea of freedom lately, freedom as non-domination rather than just non-interference, and I want to actually put it to work instead of citing it at people. Write us a vetting procedure for any proposed rule that fits on one page, since we have 90 minutes a month and nobody on the committee is a philosopher. It should say what to ask about a proposal and in what order, what counts as a good enough answer at each step, where the procedure says the rule fails on republican grounds even if it's probably a fine rule, and where it punts entirely because the question is what kind of community we want, not anyone's freedom. The part I most want it to nail: some rules interfere a lot with members without dominating them (if they're made and contestable the right way), and some barely interfere at all but leave one member at another's mercy -- the procedure has to be able to tell those apart, and also catch rules that are formally contestable but nobody could realistically contest. Be honest about where it'll misfire. Then run it on two things we're actually arguing about: a rule letting the board enter any unit for "maintainance inspections" on 24 hours notice with no appeal, and a flat ban on all pets that would cover a member's elderly cat. Finish with exactly two lines, one per proposal, each formatted as Verdict: PASS / Verdict: FAIL / Verdict: VALUES-CALL followed by at most ten words of why.
