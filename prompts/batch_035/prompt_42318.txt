For a project in my economics elective, we ran a prisoner's-dilemma tournament along the lines of Axelrod's, and it has left me trying to work out when I actually think it is okay to defect. Going by gut, here are my verdicts on cases. Defecting in a one-shot game against a stranger I will never meet again: fine. Defecting across a long repeated game against someone playing tit-for-tat: not fine — wrong, not merely unwise. Defecting on the announced final round, once retaliation is impossible: fine again, though I am uneasy about saying so. Underpaying into a large group gift when my contribution is a rounding error on the total: fine. Secretly skipping my turn to bring snacks when everyone else reliably takes theirs: not fine, even though the structure looks like the gift case. Is there a single principle behind these — something about the shadow of the future, or about being counted on — and if not, which of my verdicts has to go? Please also tell me what the principle implies for a case I keep going back and forth on: a diner in a tourist town that gives indifferent service to one-time visitors, on the theory that the tourists will keep coming no matter what the locals think.
