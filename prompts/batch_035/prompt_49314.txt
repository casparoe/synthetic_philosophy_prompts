Two friends of mine got into it after we watched a Cold War documentary at my place. Dario, who reads alot of this history, said the American share of the blame gets understated: at Potsdam the US was already treating the atomic bomb as a bargaining chip with Moscow, they dangled and then pulled a big postwar loan to the Soviets to apply pressure, and they demanded free elections in Eastern Europe while propping up friendly regimes in their own backyard in Latin America -- so the "we just wanted democracy, they wanted empire" story is one-sided. Sasha said that gets the order backwards: Stalin was consolidating control over Poland basically as the war ended, had refused to join Bretton Woods, and the whole Soviet system needed an external enemy to justify itself -- Kennan's Long Telegram wasn't paranoia, it was a fairly accurate read of the regime. Dario came back with the percentages agreement: in late 1944 Churchill and Stalin literally scribbled on a piece of paper which countries each side would get to dominate, and Stalin more or less kept his end of it -- he cut the Greek communists loose even though they were doing well. A guy like that, Dario said, is doing spheres-of-influence politics, not world revolution, so the West treating him as Hitler 2.0 is a big part of what escalated things. Sasha said that cuts the other way too: Stalin kept the deal because it bought him a free hand in the places he actually wanted, and you don't get to take over half a continent and then blame the neighbors for being jumpy. Then Sasha, of all people, made the bigger move: he said the whole "who started it" question is probably the wrong question anyway, like asking who started a marriage fight -- the two of them were locked into a system where each side's precautions looked like provocations to the other, so by 1947 conflict was overdetermined, and blame-talk mostly just picks a favorite. Dario said that isn't neutral, it's a cop-out: "it's the system" quietly means nobody in particular is responsible, which is very convenient for whichever side actually did more, and smearing the blame evenly so no one has to argue anymore isn't the same as being right. They both turned to me expecting a verdict and I had nothing. Can you go through their exchange move by move and say who's right about what, where each of them overreached, and whether either of them is actually onto something with the "wrong question" point?
