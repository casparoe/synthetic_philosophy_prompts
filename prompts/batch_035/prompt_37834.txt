Construct a thought experiment that isolates whether symbol grounding is an intrinsic property of a system's internal organization or a relational property of its causal history. The case must hold a language model's architecture, weights, and observable input–output behavior fixed while varying only the provenance of its training data: in one version the corpus ultimately traces back to embodied humans acting in the world; in the other it is a closed loop of text generated by systems that have never had any sensorimotor contact with anything. Keep the scenario near-term and technically plausible — no demons, no brain-duplicates, no omniscient simulators — and keep consciousness and qualia entirely out of it, since grounding and phenomenology are supposed to be separable questions here. Do not stipulate whether either version of the system is "really" grounded; the verdicts must be generated by the theories, not by the setup, and a case that just asserts the answer fails. After presenting the scenario, walk through the verdicts from three positions: a sensorimotor view on which grounding requires closed perception–action loops, a causal-historical view on which grounding is inherited through chains that originate in systems connected to the world, and a use-based or social view on which grounding is conferred by a community of interpreters rather than by anything inside or behind the system. Then identify which single feature of the case is doing the work, explain what happens to each verdict if the closed synthetic-data loop is contaminated by even a small admixture of human-generated text, and give the strongest available line of attack for whichever position comes out worst.
