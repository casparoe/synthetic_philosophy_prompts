Just spent like three hours arguing with my friend Marco and I want to know whether the thing we were arguing about has an actual name, because neither of us could pin one down and it's driving me crazy.

The pattern I noticed: some cooperative arrangement works fine as long as it's open-ended, and the moment a fixed end point becomes known to everyone, it collapses -- and the collapse starts way before the actual end. Concretely: at my old job everyone covered each other's shifts for years, nobody kept score. Then it was announced in spring that the office would close in December. By October nobody would cover anything, then September, and it kept crawling backwards -- and the frustrating part is that each individual refusal was sort of reasonable? If you're sure nobody will cover you in November, it's dumb to cover someone in October, and if they're sure you won't in October... Marco kept saying it's just "trust breaking down," but that doesn't feel right at all: everyone still liked each other, nobody was angry, it was more like a clean mathematical unraveling from the end backwards. And once I saw it there I noticed it elsewhere: nobody starts any joint project with the person who's moving abroad in June, even back in February, apparantly something similar happens with leases and end-of-season stuff too.

But here's the part that really bugs me. Marco's sister took one game theory course and says the conclusion "everyone bails at the first opportunity" follows from reasoning backwards from the end, assuming everyone is rational. Except empirically that's not what people do -- in lab games with a fixed number of rounds, people cooperate for a while and only quit near the end, and they end up making more money than the theory says they should. And there's a genuinely weird puzzle about what you should infer when somebody cooperates past the point where bailing was supposedly the rational move: it seems to prove they're not the "rational type" the whole backwards argument assumed, which maybe means the argument collapses once you see it happen? I keep going in circles on this. So my question: does this configuration -- the unraveling from a known end, plus the puzzle of the irrational cooperator -- have an established name or names, how do the pieces fit together, and how is it different from an ordinary prisoner's-dilemma-style breakdown? If there isn't one neat name for the whole thing, carve it up for me and tell me what each piece is called, because I will not accept just "game theory" or "loss of trust" as an answer.
