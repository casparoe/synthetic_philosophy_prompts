Hi! I'm a novelist and I'm stuck on a scene, hoping you can help me draft it.

Quick backgroud: my novel follows two sisters, Maya and Ruth, who run into each other at a family wedding. Maya is the type who tracks her charitable giving on a spreadsheet, and she's just decided to give away most of a big raise instead of upgrading her apartment. Ruth thinks she's lost the plot, and the scene is their argument at the kitchen table after everyone else has gone to bed.

What I don't want is the usual internet shouting match where the utilitarian is a robot and the critic just keeps saying that's too extreme. I want both of them smart. Specifically:

Ruth should press the demandingness objection, but the careful version — not just "that's too much to ask," but the thought that something has gone wrong with a moral theory when it leaves the agent no room for a life of her own. And she should figure out mid-argument (maybe with Maya's help, which would be a nice sisterly beat) that this is actually two different complaints bundled together: one about the sheer amount being asked of her, and one about fairness — why should Maya carry the load when the rest of the family doesn't? Once she sees that, she should try both separately, because she suspects they pull in different directions.

Maya should push back with the moves that actually worry philosophers, not slogans. Like: we already accept unlimited demands in other parts of morality — nobody thinks you may kill one person to make your own life easier — so why should costs to ourselves be the special category that gets capped? Maybe the demandingness complaint only feels so overwhelming because we measure it against a comfortable baseline that is itself part of what she's objecting to. And she should get to the point about decision procedures: even if always maximizing would make your life terrible, maybe that shows it's a bad rule to live by, not that it's false — the map isn't the itinerary.

The trick is that neither of them wins outright. By the end Ruth should land something Maya has to actually chew on — my instinct is it's this: the self/other distinction Maya is leaning on to explain why sacrifice gets capped is a distinction her own utilitarianism officially rejects everywhere else, so she's borrowing from the enemy camp. And Maya should end with something Ruth can't quite wave off either. They're sisters, so let it get warm without either becoming a caricature — interruptions, trailing off, grabbing examples from the wedding they just sat through (the cost of the flowers alone, etc.).

Write the dialogue for me? Somewhere around 800-1200 words. Keep the philosophy real but let it come out of their mouths like actual speech, not a lecture. And one honest request: don't let Maya win just because I modeled her on people I admire — I genuinely want to know whether the demandingness objection bites harder than the philosophy books give it credit for.
