So I'm in an ethics of technology course this semester and we just did the unit on autonomous systems, and something keeps bugging me that I can't find the right term for. In class we talked about the responsibility gap — cases where a system does something bad that nobody could have foreseen, so it's not clear any human is responsible. Fine. But the thing I keep seeing in the case studies we read feels different, and I want to know if it has a name.

The pattern: a semi-autonomous system messes up (say it's a triage assistant in a hospital, or a loan screening tool, or a delivery drone, whatever). And afterwards, when you go looking for who's responsible, every single human in the chain has a decent-sounding partial excuse. The engineer says: I built the component, I didn't decide to deploy it in hospitals. The hospital admin says: I bought an approved product, I'm not an ML expert. The nurse says: I followed the system's recommendation like I was trained to, and in 99 cases out of 100 that was the right call. The regulator says: I certified it based on the testing I was shown. Nobody is lying, nobody is obviously evil, and each person's individual contribution genuinely wouldn't have been enough to cause the harm on its own. So the blame just... evaporates. It's not that responsibility transferred to the machine, it's more like the machine and the division of labor together broke it up into pieces too small for anyone to hold.

I know "diffusion of responsibility" from social psych but that's the bystander effect thing — people in a crowd each assume someone else will act. That doesn't seem right here, because in my cases there's no crowd and no one is waiting around; everyone actually did their bit, diligently. And the responsibility gap thing we covered is about unforeseeability, but in my cases each person could partially foresee their piece of the risk — it's just that no one had the whole picture. Is there an established concept for this specific configuration, where the system plus the divided labor jointly shred responsibility, even though every individual act was reasonable-ish? If it does have a name I'd also want to know how it's supposed to differ from plain old "many hands" problems in organizations, which predate computers entirely — is the autonomous system doing any real work in the explanation, or is it just old-fashioned bureaucracy with a new prop? And if there isn't an established term, I'd honestly be happy with a good proposal: what exactly is the phenomenon, what's it not, and where does it end — like, at what point does the chain of partial excuses become genuinely reasonable rather than an excuse?

(Tbh I have to write a short paper on one of the case studies and I suspect naming this properly is going to be half the battle, so the carving matters to me more than the label.)
