You're a philosopher who's prepped people for many a hostile Q&A. Next month I'm giving a talk where I argue that the epistemic problem L.A. Paul made famous with transformative experience is really two problems wearing one coat: (a) you can't know in advance what facts the experience will reveal to you, and (b) you can't know in advance how the experience will change the you doing the valuing. My line is that only (b) is genuinely decision-theoretically troubling -- for (a) you can often get by on testimony from people who've been through it, so expected utility theory isn't in as much trouble as Paul suggests, but for (b) the standard fixes (assign utilities over your future selves, treat it as a problem of choosing an identity, etc.) all smuggle in the very values you can't access yet. The audience is a department that's mostly mainstream epistemologists plus two decision theorists who know the preference-change literature cold and a couple of Humeans about practical reason. Brainstorm first, then give me the prep sheet: the objections in the order they'll actually arrive, phrased the way that audience would phrase them (the decision theorists will come at me differently than the epistemologists), each with a two-minute reply and an honest note on where the reply is soft. Include the one question I'm praying doesn't get asked. And tell me what I should concede in the intro so I'm not fighting on three fronts at once.
