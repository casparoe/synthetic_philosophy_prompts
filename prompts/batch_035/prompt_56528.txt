My roommate is convinced he's refuted coherentism and I keep failing to find the hole, though I'm pretty sure one is there. The argument is a dilemma about the size of the circle:

(1) Coherentism says a belief gets its justification entirely from its relations to other beliefs in the web; nothing gets any from outside.
(2) Consider the smallest possible web: two beliefs, A and B, where A supports B and B supports A, and neither has any other standing. He claims nothing is justified here — "A because B, B because A" is just mutual assertion, like two friends coordinating their alibi stories.
(3) A large web differs from that two-belief circle only by having more links. Same kinds of relations, just more of them.
(4) If one unsupported link contributes zero justification, more zero-links can't conjure any out of nothing. Passing IOUs around a bigger circle doesn't make anyone richer.
(5) So either the big web justifies — in which case the two-friend circle does too, which seems nuts — or it doesn't, and coherentism justifies nothing whatsoever.
(6) And if circular support really does justify, then the tightest circle of all is a single self-consistent belief supporting itself, at which point every belief is its own foundation and coherantism collapses into foundationalism where you get to pick your own foundations.

I'd rather keep (1), and (2) seems obviously right to me too, but I can't live with (6) or the nothing-justified horn of (5) — I don't want to be shoved back into the regress or off into infinitism. Which premise should I give up, what does giving it up cost, and is there any way for girth to matter without smuggling foundations in through the back door?
