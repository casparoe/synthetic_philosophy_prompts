I never went past high school, but a few months ago I found a battered paperback of Pascal's Pensées at a second-hand bookshop, and the fragments about justice and force have been living in my head rent-free ever since. As I read him, the argument goes something like this: what is just has no power to make itself obeyed, and what has power has no justice in it, so, since we couldn't make what is just strong, we settled for making what is strong just. Law's real legs are force and age-old custom wearing the costume of justice, and the costume only works because nobody at the top ever stands up and says so. Pascal even suggests this silence is structural: the moment a state openly admits its authority is just force with a good tailor, obedience starts evaporating, and yet he still concludes that we ought to keep obeying anyway, because anything else means war. The theory, in other words, is one that can only be true if it is never avowed by anyone who actually runs anything.

The reason this all feels urgent is my brother-in-law, who works in corrections and treats the whole thing as simple common sense: the law is whatever's written down and enforced, full stop, and all this justice talk is decoration. I keep telling him Pascal already rode that train to the last stop — and that the last stop is the interesting part, because the position can't survive being announced by the institution itself. He shrugs. I want to show him what I mean.

So here is my request. Write a satirical piece: an official government press release, around 650 words, in the flattest possible civil-service register, from a fictional mid-sized republic whose Ministry of Justice has just announced that, following an internal reading group on Pascal, it is dropping the word Justice from its name and avowing force as the sole basis of its authority. Include a Q&A section, and at least one invented spokesperson — say, a Commissioner for Constitutional Candor — fielding questions from the public and the press. The comedy must come from the actual structure of the position, not from silly add-ons: I want the press release to lean on antiquity and custom as the official substitutes for justice, to keep invoking fairness to explain why citizens should comply now that fairness has been disavowed, and to get steadily more expensive in the enforcement it has to promise as belief drains out of the system. The closing beat should be the ministry quietly restoring the word Justice, described as a routine rebranding exercise — the reader should be left seeing exactly why the costume was load-bearing. Please keep it deadpan throughout, no winking at the audience, and make it fair enough to the actual view that someone who has read Pascal carefully would nod rather than roll their eyes.
