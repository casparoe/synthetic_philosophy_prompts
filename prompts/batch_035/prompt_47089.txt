Longtime LessWrong/EA Forum reader here (since ~2013), and irony of ironies I now sit on the grant committee of a small longtermist fund. We give out maybe $400k a year in grants of $10k-$50k, mostly to tiny projects, and we decide quarterly. The money is earmarked for longtermist causes, so "just fund bednets instead" is off the table, and so is punting to a big evaluator -- the whole point of us is that we take chances on stuff the big funds won't touch.

Our fight this quarter: two finalist applications, and the disagreement on the committee is really about the hinge of history hypothesis -- roughly the claim that we live at an unusually influential time, where what people do now has outsized effects on the entire long-run future, as opposed to times being basically alike in how much influence their inhabitants have. Application A is an AI governance shop whose whole theory of change is "we're at the hinge, so building capacity and getting the right people placed this decade dominates everything else you could do with the money." Application B wants seed funding for a project meant to compound over centuries -- a kind of civilizational library plus long-horizon governance work -- that explicitly bets we are NOT at any special moment and that the long game is the only game. Same dollar amount; we can fund one at full or both at half.

Half the committee thinks the AI thing is obvious ("time of perils, it's basically decided by mid-century whether the future goes well or not"), and the other half keeps making this point I've half-absorbed from forum arguments: that believing you're at the most important time ever should *lower* your confidence you're right, since everyone alive tends to think that and they can't all be. I've lost track of who actually holds what anymore, honestly.

Draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting. It should recommend a concrete decision (A, B, split, or defer with conditions and a deadline), and also lay out criteria the committee can reuse next quarter, because this exact fight is going to come back with different applications attached. Take the strongest version of both sides seriously -- the hinge people aren't LARPing urgency and the anti-hinge people aren't just cowards -- and be honest about which parts of this come down to a prior or a values call we can't argue our way out of. Assume the committee is smart generalists with no formal philosophy background who have read an enormous number of blog posts; calibrate to that.
