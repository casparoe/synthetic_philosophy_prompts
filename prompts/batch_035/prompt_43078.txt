I'm taking a course on collective behavior / social epistemology this semester (it's cross-listed econ-philosophy, weird combo but it works), and we just did two weeks on things that I cannot stop conflating in my head. First, information cascades: people make decisions sequentially, everyone can observe predecessors' choices but not their private signals, and after a couple of identical early choices it becomes rational to ignore your own information and just follow the herd — the classic example being two people going to restaurant A and everyone after them inferring that A must be better even if their own info favors B. Second, pluralistic ignorance: a whole group privately rejects some belief or norm but each member wrongly takes the others' silence or compliance as evidence that the others accept it, so the norm perpetuates itself even though nobody actually endorses it (the emperor's new clothes basically).

On the surface these seem to produce the same outcome — a bunch of people publicly going along with something their private evidence is against — but the mechanisms look pretty different. In the cascade case the followers seem to be doing something genuinely rational: the predecessors' choices really do carry information, and updating on them is arguably the epistemically correct move. In the pluralistic ignorance case it seems more like a breakdown — the silence of others is being misread, coordination fails, and the whole thing is sustained on an illusion. My friend in the course keeps insisting that they're actually the same phenomenon, and that cascades are just "pluralistic ignorance with equations," which feels wrong to me but I can't fully articulate why.

So I'd like a real comparison of the two ideas. Specifically: in what exact sense is cascade-following rational where pluralistic ignorance is not? Do they make different predictions — e.g. about how fragile the conforming behavior is, or whether one well-informed dissenter can break the spell? Could they interact, like a cascade generating pluralistic ignorance downstream, or pluralistic ignorance being what makes the early signals in a cascade untrustworthy? And do the standard fixes overlap — does making votes simultaneous/anonymous, or adding contrarians who ignore the herd, do the same work in both cases? I'd appreciate it if you could also take a stance on whether my friend's reduction holds up, because I want to win this argument (or at least lose it for the right reasons).
