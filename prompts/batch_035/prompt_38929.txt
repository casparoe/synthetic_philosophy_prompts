Is there a name for the situation I keep hitting in arguements about fairness in healthcare and welfare? The people I argue with basically agree that society should help people whose problems weren't their own doing, and shouldn't help people whose problems were. Fine. But then we hit cases where the "own doing" part is itself traceable backwards, and nobody can say whether it counts. Example one: a guy loses most of his vision to diabetes from decades of terrible diet — but he grew up in a household where nobody cooked and fresh food was a bus ride away, and by the time he had his own money he'd never learned any other way of eating. Example two: a woman wrecked her knees from a brutal warehouse job she took at 19 because it was the only work where she lived, and she knew at the time it was wrecking her body. My friends split on both: some say once you're an adult the choice is yours and that's what matters, others say a choice made from a bad starting position isn't the kind of choice that should disqualify you from help. It feels like there has to be an established distinction, or at least a live debate, about exactly this — a disadvantage that was "chosen" but has an unchosen chain behind the choosing. Does it have a name? How does it relate to the plain chosen/unchosen distinction, and where exactly does that distinction stop doing work? If there's no settled term, coin one and give a definition plus a couple of boundary cases. And don't just hand me a label and move on — I want you to say which parts of this people who actually study it agree on and which parts are genuinely contested, and not dress up one side's view as if it were the consensus.
