For some years I have taught, and in print defended, a position I take over from Michael Dummett — the position he laid out in the book he wrote on immigration late in his life, after decades of anti-racist campaigning. The position has roughly four commitments. First, a feudal analogy: citizenship in a prosperous state is an unearned hereditary privilege, an inheritance fixed by the accident of birthplace, and a state's closing its borders in order to preserve its citizens' comparative wealth is structurally the same act as an aristocracy fencing off its estates — a survival of feudalism in modern dress, more culpable because we no longer even notice it as a privilege. Second, a claim about weighing: where admission is claimed as a right, the gains and losses of existing citizens — even genuine losses to their welfare, not mere inconvenience — are the wrong kind of consideration to be set against it, because weighing an advantage against a right is not weighing at all. Third, a near-unrestricted duty of admission for those fleeing persecution and, more contentiously, for those migrating from grinding poverty; the refugee fleeing violence and the migrant fleeing destitution are, on this view, on a continuum rather than in different moral categories. Fourth, a claim about treatment: whatever states do about entry, residents who have arrived must never be made second-class — no permanent non-citizenship castes, no legal gray zones for the undocumented.

I have come to suspect I can no longer see this position's weak points clearly, and I want a map of its exposure rather than another rehearsal of its strengths. What would count against it — not only the standard objections from the distributive and democratic self-determination literatures, but which specific intuitions, possible cases, and results in neighboring areas it stands or falls with? I mean things like the following, though you should not confine yourself to them: whether the feudal analogy, if it were disanalogous in some way that matters (feudal privilege, unlike citizenship, was not arguably needed to sustain any welfare institutions), would take the rest of the position down with it or only detach a rhetorical flourish; whether any principled line could be drawn for the weighing claim, or whether admitting one welfare-based limit concedes everything; whether the continuum between refugee and economic migrant can survive a case where admitting the latter foreseeably destroys the political will to admit the former; and whether the treatment claim could turn out to be the load-bearing part, such that the view survives as a whole even if the admission claims fail — or collapses with it.

Two specific demands on the answer. First, distinguish what the position's own defenders would concede as counting against it from what only its critics say counts against it — Dummett himself, for instance, appeared willing to grant that institutions of redistribution presuppose bounded populations; does that concession, taken seriously, quietly gut the view? Second, tell me whether the position has been insulated by qualifications into something that risks nothing — a rights-based view that cannot name what would falsify it — and, if it has, whether that is a defect in this particular view or simply what any view with a rights-structure at its center must look like.

Please deliver the core of the answer as a table with these columns: Defeater (a one-line statement of the observation, argument, case, or result); What it targets (which of the four commitments it attacks, and by what route); Concession or resistance (whether a defender of the position would concede it counts against them, and how much, or would refuse and on what principled ground); Severity (decisive, serious, or merely awkward); Absorbing response, if any (the qualification or reply that blunts it, and what that qualification costs the view). Sort the rows from most to least severe. Below the table, add a short paragraph with your overall verdict on how exposed the position actually is, including whether the feudal analogy is doing argumentative work or only rhetorical work, and — if you conclude the view is largely insulated — say so plainly rather than manufacturing vulnerability.
