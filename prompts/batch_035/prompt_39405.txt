here's my set of commitments about dreams and sleep, written up for a reading group where everyone's supposed to poke at each others' profiles. tell me if this hangs together:

(1) dreams are experiences that occur during sleep — there really is something it's like while the dream is going on, not just when you wake and remember it;
(2) waking reports are our only evidence about dreams, so everything we know about dreaming is knowledge of post-waking memories, not of the dreams themselves;
(3) memory generally is reconstructive, and dream recall especially is probably stitched together partly at the moment of telling;
(4) I reject the Dennett-style move where the dream is basically composed on waking — dreams are not fabricated retrospectively;
(5) in dreamless sleep there is nothing it's like at all, consciousness simply goes out and comes back on;
(6) lucid dreams show that a sleeper can know they're dreaming and even deliberate and act on intentions formed while asleep;
(7) I also think people dream for a much larger fraction of the night than they ever recall, and "I don't dream" almost always means "I don't remember."

Go through these and tell me which ones are in genuine tension, which only look like they are, and what the cheapest fix would be for each real one. Rank the tensions by severity rather than dumping them all on me at once — I genuinly don't know where the problem is, if there is one, so don't be polite about it.
