I run a small online nomic game, about 15 players, one turn every couple of days, everything happening in a chat thread and a numbered proposal log. We started from a Suber-style initial ruleset: mutable rules can be amended or repealed by ordinary rule changes, immutable ones can't be touched unless they're first transmuted to mutable, players rack up points under whatever scoring rules survive, and hitting the point threshold wins. Pretty early on we abolished rotating judges and made the Speaker (me) the sole adjudicator of disputes, which felt efficient at the time and increasingly feels like a mistake, because the disputes keep being about self-reference and I have no method, just vibes and a growing grudge.

Here's the kind of thing I mean. Last month someone proposed a rule whose entire text was "All rules that refer to this rule are repealed," and we spent a week arguing over whether it repealed itself, whether the rules governing rule changes referred to it, and whether — if those got repealed — the game would still contain any legal way to repeal anything ever again, including the mess we'd just made. Before that: "this rule is mutable" submitted as an amendment targeting an immutable rule (does the proposal do the transmuting, or is it a proposal to transmute that only says what the result should look like?), and the recurring favorite, a proposal that both replaces the repeal rule and changes how repeal-proposals are judged, so it's arguably operating on its own conditions of validity. I ruled differently on each of these and everyone correctly noticed I was making it up.

What I want is a procedure the Speaker can actually run at proposal time, before the vote, in about ten minutes: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it terminates with "returned to proposer as ill-formed, try again," and — crucially — where it hands the question off to a player vote or an explicit judgment call, because I suspect some of these are indeterminate in principle and pretending otherwise is worse than admitting it. Ground it in whatever is actually understood about self-amendment and self-reference in rule systems — the whole reason I got into nomic was the idea that the game is built to expose the difference between changing the laws and changing the laws about changing laws — but it has to be usable by someone without the theory at 11pm when quorum closes. Then run it on three test proposals: the self-referential repeal above, the "this rule is mutable" transmutation dodge, and one I'll describe where a player proposes that "this proposal counts as passed if it recieves at least half of votes cast, and rules interpreting this proposal shall be construed in its favor." Be honest about where the procedure will break — especially proposals that amend the procedure itself, or that amend the rules about amending the procedure itself — and include at least one case where it delivers an answer I won't like, since I'm on record in the chat saying I'd have let the self-repealing one through and I'd like to know if that was defensible or just lazy.
