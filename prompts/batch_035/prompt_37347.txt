I help moderate an online community of roughly 20,000 members that currently enforces a strict, context-blind word-filter blocking any post containing certain slurs. This has produced strange results: it blocks members who quote a harasser's message verbatim when filing a report, and it blocks in-group members using reclaimed terms affectionately, while posts conveying plainly bigoted content through neutral-sounding vocabulary pass through untouched. We are deciding whether to keep the strict filter or move to context-sensitive human review. Please describe the most important implications of the semantics and pragmatics of slurs for this decision. In particular, address what the literature's distinction between a slur's semantic content and its pragmatic effects implies about whether the harm resides in the word form itself, in the speaker's attitude, or in the conversational setting, and what that implies about the respective failure modes of a context-blind filter versus case-by-case human judgment.
