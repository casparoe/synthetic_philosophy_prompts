The new riddle of induction has been running since the fifties and my phil sci reading group has hit the wall everyone hits with grue: someone says green-projections are legitimate because "green" is a natural kind and "grue" is gerrymandered, someone else asks gerrymandered relative to what?, then entrenchment gets invoked, then someone says entrenchment is circular, and we replay the same four moves every month. My working theory is that the whole thing is a disguised choice of language rather than a disagreement about induction: both sides agree on every prediction anyone could ever test, so the fight is only over which predicates we're permitted to project, which looks more like a decision about how to carve up vocabulary than a factual question with an answer. But I half suspect that's too quick — if it were purely verbal, why does it genuinely feel like the anti-grue side registers something real?

So diagnose it for me, and commit: is this a verbal dispute, a substantive-but-empirically-empty one, or two different questions (one about confirmation, one about meaning) run together under one heading? Don't hand me the menu of diagnoses with "it depends" at the end — take a position and defend it. Then tell me what would count as progress on your diagnosis: a distinction someone could actually formulate, a change of question, or nothing, in which case I want to know whether I should just dissolve the reading group.
