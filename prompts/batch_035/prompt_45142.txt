my roommate and i got into it about how committees and electorates should pick between options, and she said the set of things i believe about voting doesn't hang together — but she couldn't quite say which part is the problem, and honestly neither can i. here's roughly where i stand:

(1) the only legitimate input is how each person ranks the options. one person one vote, and you can't compare how strongly different people feel across persons — that kind of comparison is meaningless, so it can't count for anything.

(2) if an option beats every other option one-on-one — a majority prefers it to each of the others taken separately — the group should go with that option. that just seems like the core of fairness to me.

(3) whether the group ranks A above B shouldn't depend on whether some third option C is also on the ballot. if C drops out of the race, the verdict between A and B shouldn't flip. otherwise spoilers decide everything, and theres something deeply unfair about that.

(4) second choices matter. an option that 51% rank first and the other 49% rank dead last is a worse outcome for the group than the option that is everyones second choice even though nobody ranks it first. a healthy group should be able to settle on the unifying option instead of the polarizing one.

i'm not expecting a perfect rule here, i know every voting system fails some criterion somewhere. i just want to know which of these four i actually have to give up. go through them and tell me which ones genuinely fight each other and which only look like they do, rank the fights by how bad they are, and for each real one say what the cheapest fix would be. then tell me which single revision cleans up the most.
