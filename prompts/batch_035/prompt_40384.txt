I'm a programming-language-theory researcher: type systems, stratification, quines, meta-circular evaluators, and Gödel numbering via arithmetization are all things I use weekly, but my philosophy comes entirely from pop treatments, chiefly Gödel, Escher, Bach read years ago. I want you to translate Hofstadter's account of tangled hierarchies and the inviolate level — the one illustrated by Escher's Drawing Hands, where each hand draws the other, with the paper and the artist's hand standing outside the tangle — into my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report.

Concretely, attempt the translation along these axes (and any others you think genuinely fit): (1) the two hands' mutual drawing versus self-application and Russell-style paradox, and how stratified type systems dissolve the latter by forbidding exactly the kind of level-mixing Escher depicts; (2) the claim that self-reference can occur without paradox, mapped onto quines and fixed-point constructions, and on whether a quine's self-description is reference to the program or to a description of it; (3) the inviolate level — the paper, the un-drawn drawing hand, and in Hofstadter's application the neurons and hardware that supposedly never get tangled — mapped onto substrate: the physical machine under a reflective tower, the compiler you bootstrap from, the un-coded arithmetic that carries the Gödel coding; (4) his further claim that a self is a strange loop, i.e., that the tangled part of the hierarchy is not merely permitted but constitutive, mapped onto the distinction between a system that can represent itself (self-hosting compilers, meta-circular interpreters) and one that is in some sense made of that self-representation.

Then the residue report, in three parts. First: what translated cleanly. Second: what translated only with distortion — and here I particularly want your verdict on the mediation issue, which is where I suspect the whole thing turns: in Gödel numbering, the sentence's apparent reference to itself is really reference to its code, and one could argue Escher works the same way — the "drawing" hand is drawn, so the tangle is an artifact of the medium's forgetting itself, paper all the way down until you hit the inviolate level. If that reading is right, the "tangle" is just stratification with the levels visually interlaced, and I'd want to know whether Hofstadter ever answers it or only gestures past it. Third: what has no expression in my vocabulary at all — in particular whether the claim that the tangled part of the hierarchy is constitutive of the self, rather than an eliminable representational trick, is a substantive thesis or a mood, and whether my vocabulary lacks the resources to state it or whether it was never stated clearly enough to translate.

I want a committed answer, not a menu. Take a definite position on whether the strange-loop thesis, as applied to the self, survives the translation as a substantive claim about systems, or whether the residue report shows it dissolving into stratification plus metaphor — and say which way the surviving or dissolving goes for the inviolate level in particular, since that's the part I've never been able to decide. If you think the question itself is confused in a way my framing inherits, say so directly and say what the defensible remnant is.
