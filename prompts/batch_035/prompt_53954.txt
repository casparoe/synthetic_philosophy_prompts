Question: Explain the multiple realizability argument against type-identity theory. Then answer: does multiple realizability by itself establish that mental states are functional states? Defend your answer.

Student's answer: The type-identity theory holds that each mental state type is identical to some physical state type — pain just is C-fiber firing, as Place and Smart argued. Putnam's multiple realizability objection is that the very same mental state can be realized by very different physical states: an octopus, a hypothetical alien, and maybe a future silicon system could all be in pain even though their physical make-up is nothing like ours. If pain is identical to C-fiber firing, none of them could feel pain, which seems wrong. So the identity theory is empirically refuted. Functionalism follows directly: if a mental state can be realized by many different physical states, what makes it that state can't be its physical composition, so it must be the causal role it plays — its relations to inputs, behavior, and other mental states. So multiple realizability just is the functionalist thesis, since functionalism is basically the claim that each mental state is type-identical to whatever physical state happens to play the right causal role. This also gets us token identity for free: every particular pain turns out to be identical to some physical particular or other, just not the same type of particular across creatures.
