Our philosophy club recently read about Nick Bostrom's simulation argument, and as I understand it, the argument claims that at least one of these three propositions must be false: (1) civilizations go extinct before becoming technologically able to run ancestor simulations, (2) advanced civilizations for some reason choose not to run such simulations, or (3) almost everyone who has ever existed is simulated rather than biological. Since neither of the first two seems likely, I have concluded that the argument proves we are living in a computer simulation. My problem arises at this point. If our universe is a simulation being run on some future civilization's computer, that computer must be a physical machine located somewhere — presumably inside some galaxy in our own sky — and the beings who built it must likewise exist somewhere within our universe. So shouldn't astronomers be able to detect either the simulators or the enormous computer running the simulation? Why has no one ever reported finding such a thing? Could the simulators simply be hiding from our telescopes with some kind of cloaking technology, or might they be located beyond the cosmic microwave background where we cannot observe them? I would appreciate an explanation of where exactly the simulators' computer is hiding within our universe, since that is where the simulation must be running.
