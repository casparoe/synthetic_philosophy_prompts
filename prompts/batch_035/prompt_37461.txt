I want you to take the Chinese Room debate -- Searle's 1980 paper and the decades of replies it kicked off -- and analyse it as an episode in the history of intuition pumps, not as another round in the strong-AI war, which I've read plenty about allready.

Quick facts so we're aligned, and so nothing turns on contested details: Searle asks you to imagine a man shut in a room, following an enormous rulebook for responding to slips of paper passed under the door with Chinese characters on them. The outgoing replies are so good that people outside the room take it to be occupied by a fluent Chinese speaker, though the man himself understands no Chinese at all. Searle's conclusion: running the right symbol-manipulation programme, however good, is not sufficient for understanding -- syntax doesn't get you semantics -- so passing a Turing test doesn't show that a machine understands anything. The replies came thick and fast in the same journal issue and after: the systems reply, the robot reply, the other-minds reply, and so on.
