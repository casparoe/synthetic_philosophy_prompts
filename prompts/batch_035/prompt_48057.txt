I've been going through the debate-as-scalable-oversight literature (the AI safety debate idea, two AIs arguing in front of a human judge) and the thing that bugs me is that everyone defends it with the same two intuitions -- lying is harder than checking a lie, and adversaries will sell each other out -- and then jumps straight to protocol details without ever saying what success would even consist of. So before I take any specific proposal seriously I want the spec written down first. Fix the purpose like this: a protocol worth actually using to supervise a model that is genuinely stronger than its judges at the task (hard code, research-level math, forecasting), where the judge cannot just check the answer directly. Adequacy is relative to that.

Write the desiderata: what any adequate debate-based oversight scheme has to deliver, with the motivating case behind each one. Some things I want covered, though go beyond these: the verdict reliability has to beat what the judge gets with no debate at all, otherwise it's decoration; it has to hold up when one debater is optimizing for persuasion rather than truth; it has to survive the judge having a limited attention budget that debaters can attack (dump enough plausible-sounding intermediate steps and nobody ever checks the middle); there has to be a way for a debater to profit from flagging its own earlier mistake rather than dying on that hill; the bootstrap condition -- the protocol can't quietly assume the judge can already tell which debater is right, since that's the whole thing we're trying to bootstrap around; and a third party reading the transcript later, without re-running anything, has to be able to see why the verdict went the way it went. Mark each requirement as non-negotiable or as something only one camp actually demands -- I suspect the "make alignment evals better" people and the "supervise models doing novel research" people want different things here, and I'd like to know which desiderata are camp prejudices rather than real requirements. Then flag any pair you suspect can't be jointly satisfied. My own bet is that "short enough for a human to actually attend to" and "checkable in pieces by a later auditor" pull against each other, and also that the bootstrap condition rules out more than people admit, but I want your view, not mine parroted back at me.

Then, briefly, run three protocol families against the list: plain two-debater debate with a human judge, debate with forced commitments and cross-examination, and decomposing the question into recursively smaller sub-debates. Keep the weight on the criteria -- the scoring should be a quick pass at the end, not the main event.

For format: after the prose part, give me a table with these columns -- Requirement | Motivating case | Non-negotiable vs camp preference | Which family it hits hardest. If you think a requirement is jointly unsatisfiable with another one, note it in that row and say which. And if you think one single requirement knocks out the most contenders, say which and why, thats the part I most want to see.
