quick context: I'm a phil mind PhD student, and our reading group has spent basically two semesters on shared intention / joint action and gotten nowhere. The setup is familiar — Bratman says shared intention just is interlocking individual intentions plus mutual responsiveness and common knowledge, Gilbert says you need a plural subject formed by joint commitment (with the ensuing obligations and entitlements), and Searle hovers in the background claiming collective intentionality is primitive and not reducible to I-intentions plus beliefs about each other. Everyone in the group can recite the standard counterexamples in both directions (Gilbert's wedding-planners case aimed at Bratman-style accounts, Bratman's push-back that joint commitment gives you obligations nobody wanted), but literally nobody has moved an inch since the 90s as far as I can tell.

My working hypothesis: this isn't a substantive disagreement at all. "Shared intention" is ambiguous between a planning-coordination concept (the thing that lets you and me carry a table downstairs without talking) and a normative-bond concept (the thing that makes it a betrayal to walk out mid-joint-activity), and the counterexamples just trade on sliding between the two. Is that right, or am I being too quick — maybe there's a genuine hidden question about what grounds social normativity that keeps it substantive? I want a diagnosis built on what the sides actually say, not a literature survey, plus an honest answer about what would count as progress here — a distinction, an experiment on how kids or great apes track joint commitments, a change of question — or an argument that no progress should be expected and why.
