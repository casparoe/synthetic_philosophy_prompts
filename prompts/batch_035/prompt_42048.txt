I'm about to spend a serious amount of money on a sapphire, and I've run into a disagreement about what would make it reasonable to go through with the purchase. I cannot myself tell a natural unheated stone from one that has been heat-treated; the difference is invisible without laboratory equipment. The dealer has handed me a certificate from a gemological laboratory stating that the stone is unheated, and the lab's stamp is one that dealers in this market treat as decisive. My companion, who came along to keep me from being cheated, is unmoved. His principle is that paper is only someone else's claim: if I cannot verify the fact myself, I do not know it, and buying on the strength of a stranger's word is a leap, not knowledge. When I proposed taking the stone to a second laboratory, he said a second sheet of paper has the same standing as the first — testimony all the way down, and no testimony can serve as a foundation for knowledge. The odd consequence is that on his view I can never responsibly buy this stone at all, since no accumulation of certificates ever changes what I can check with my own eyes — and yet people transact this way every day, and it mostly works.

A third friend, who studied Indian philosophy at university, remarked that my companion had mistaken one means of knowledge for the only one, and that an old tradition of logic and epistemology there would treat the word of a competent, honest authority, speaking within that person's domain, as a basic source of knowledge in its own right — not a shortcut that must first be cashed out into perception or inference before it can justify belief. I want to know who is right. Is my companion's principle coherent, or does it defeat itself once I ask how he knows the things he takes for granted: that heat treatment exists, that this stamp carries weight, that gem markets are mostly honest? And if the certificate can amount to knowledge, what makes it so — the reliability of the laboratory, my background reasons for trusting that kind of laboratory, or something about the practice of certifying itself?

Please take a definite position rather than surveying the options: say whether my companion's view can survive in any form, give your account of the certificate's actual epistemic status, and tell me what I should do about the stone.
