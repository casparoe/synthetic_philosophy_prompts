At a recent seminar on scientific observation, a colleague repeatedly invoked the line that we do not see the world as it is, but as we are, in order to argue that observation is so saturated with background belief that it cannot adjudicate between rival theories. The slogan clearly gestures at something like the cognitive penetration of perception, but I suspect it is doing far less work than she thinks. Please disentangle the distinct precise theses this one-liner could be expressing — from trivial truths about attention and perceptual set, through diachronic effects like perceptual learning, to genuinely contentious synchronic claims about cognition shaping the content of experience itself, and finally near-Kantian claims about never accessing a mind-independent world. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or plain false, and indicate which readings, if any, would actually support using the slogan to discredit theory-neutral observation in the sciences. Lay the readings out as a structured list, and write your answer in British English.
