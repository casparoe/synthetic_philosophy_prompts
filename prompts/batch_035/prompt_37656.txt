Two theorists have spent years arguing past each other about whether the left/right distinction picks out something real. Camp A holds that it tracks a deep value orientation — roughly, the priority of equality versus the acceptance of hierarchy — and that this single axis explains why whole bundles of issue positions travel together across countries and generations. Camp B holds that "left" and "right" are labels for historically contingent coalitions — bundles of group interests, inherited allegiances, and institutional path dependence — and that the apparent coherence of each side is an artifact of coalition maintenance rather than any shared principle. They have now agreed to an adversarial collaboration. Write the joint statement they should produce.

I want, in this order: a numbered section of claims both camps can sign — covering the empirical regularities both accept (which issue positions actually cluster across countries, and which stubbornly don't), what each camp owes the other by way of explanation, and the known limits of the evidence; then a disagreements table in which each residual disagreement is phrased so that its own camp would accept the wording as fair to its view; then the cruxes — I suspect one is whether genuinely new political issues (say, geoengineering or AI regulation) attach to the left/right axis by their internal logic or by coalition accident, and another is what to make of positions that stay put on one side while the surrounding circumstances change beyond recognition; and finally, for each crux, a concrete kind of case or observation both camps agree would bear on it, with each camp's prediction recorded in advance. If they conclude that some part of the dispute is verbal — for instance, about what counts as a distinction being "real" — say which part, and why.
