I've been reading the consciousness threads on rationalist blogs for long enough to know the arguments by heart, and I've noticed that whenever the meta-problem of consciousness comes up — the problem of explaining why we judge and report that consciousness poses a hard problem in the first place — the discussion jumps almost immediately to candidate explanations: deflationary stories about our cognitive limitations, inflationary stories that quietly smuggle in an answer to the hard problem itself. Before I evaluate any of them, I want the specification. Write out the desiderata any adequate solution to the meta-problem must satisfy: the phenomena it has to explain (why the intuition of an explanatory gap arises, why it seems coherently conceivable that a physical duplicate of me lacks experience, why these reports are stable across cultures and stubbornly resistant to argument), the constraints it must respect (being mechanically explicable in ordinary cognitive-scientific terms without presupposing whether phenomenal consciousness exists, applying equally to the introspective reports of realists and illusionists), and the distinctions it cannot collapse (between explaining the reports and explaining the thing reported on; between a theory's being true and its being credible to the person delivering the reports), with the case behind each requirement. Then flag which desiderata you suspect are not jointly satisfiable, and say which single requirement does the most to exclude the candidate explanations you know of — in particular, tell me whether neutrality between physicalism and dualism is a genuine constraint or already a substantive metaphysical thesis, since that's the part I most want to see.
