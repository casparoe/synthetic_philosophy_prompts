so I've been reading lesswrong since like 2013 and the surrogate goals idea still bugs me more than almost anything else in the decision theory discussions. quick recap so we're on the same page: if you're worried about acausal blackmail — some future agent or alien sim threatening you (or simulations of you) unless you pay up / help it get created — the proposed fix is to precommit to treating attacks on some cheap proxy value as attacks on your real values. the classic throwaway example is that you publicly start caring intensely about, like, mail fraud, so that anyone who blackails you over anything learns you're the kind of agent that escalates over trivial slights, and the whole exploit-ability signal makes threats against you not worth making in the first place.

the thing I keep going back and forth on: the argument seems to only work if the threatener can't distinguish surrogate goals from terminal ones. the whole point of the surrogate value is that its actually cheap for you to defend, which is exactly why a sufficiently smart threatener discounts your counter-threat — and ex post, once the threat is made, you have no real reason to follow through on torching your actual interests to punish a mail fraud attack. so is the whole proposal secretly a bluff, and if so does it just collapse into ordinary costly-signaling / commitment-device theory, with nothing genuinely new in it? or is there something about the acausal setting (threatener modeling you from afar, no common knowledge of your true utility function) that makes the bluff robust in a way it wouldn't be face-to-face?
