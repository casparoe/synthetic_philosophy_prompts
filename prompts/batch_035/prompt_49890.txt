longtime lurker here (SSC days, then ACX, way too many hours on the alignment forum circa 2021) and there's an argument I keep half-forming in comment threads and never seeing made properly, so I want you to actually make it.

The standard rationalist party line on whose values an aligned AI should serve, roughly: not the designers' values (regime lock-in, one lab's idiosyncrasies become permanent), not a raw majority vote (tyrrany of the majority, plus majorities believe wrong stuff), but some CEV-flavored thing — extrapolate everyone's values under better information and more reflection, and serve that. The implicit pitch is that this is doing Rawlsian work: you don't know where you'll land, so pick the aggregation rule that's fair whatever position you end up in.

But here's the worry I can't shake, and I want you to argue FROM it, not around it. Call it neutrality laundering: every step of the aggregation pipeline — who counts as a person, what counts as genuine reflection versus corruption of someone's values, how you treat intransitive or loopy preferences, whether conflicts get resolved by averaging, Pareto, or veto — is itself a substantive value commitment dressed up as a neutral procedure. Classical liberal theory sort of has an answer to this at the human scale: the state stays neutral, and citizens fight out the substantive stuff in civil society, which sits outside the aggregation mechanism. But an aligned AI isn't a referee standing outside the game — it's the referee and the arena and increasingly the weather. There's no leftover civil society where the disagreements it declined to adjudicate can actually play out.

So: please argue that CEV-style extrapolation, or something in that family, is still the right target for alignment DESPITE the neutrality laundering problem. Don't just tell me the objection is hard or that all procedures have values baked in (I know, that's my objection, and I'm not a relativist so it doesn't warm my heart). Make the positive case: which parts of the laundering worry you concede, which you answer, and what the fallback is if the concession turns out to be bigger than you claimed. If the answer is "the extrapolation has to be anchored in some thin overlapping consensus and that's not neutral but it's the best we get," then say that and defend why it's not just the designers' values with extra steps.
