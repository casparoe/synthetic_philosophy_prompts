I have no academic background in philosophy, but for several years I've been thinking on my own about a dispute concerning time, and the more I think about it, the more it looks like a debate that never moves. I'd like a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement it really is, not a verdict on which side is right.

The dispute, as I understand it: one side holds that only the present exists — the past has ceased and the future isn't yet there, so that Caesar is no more a real thing than a fictional character is, and what exists is what exists right now. The other side holds that past, present, and future are all equally real, and that "now" is like "here": a feature of where you happen to be standing, not a feature of the world itself. I believe these positions are called presentism and eternalism, but I'll call them the only-now view and the all-times view.

Here is why it seems stalled to me. The strongest argument I can construct against the only-now view comes from relativity: two people moving relative to each other will disagree about which distant events are happening "now," so there seems to be no fact of the matter about what currently exists, and only-now needs exactly such a fact. But I've thought of replies, and I gather the professionals have too — for instance, that "now" can be perspectival while reality itself still flows, or that physics may not be the final word. Meanwhile the strongest argument against the all-times view is that time plainly passes: things happen, one moment gives way to the next. But the all-times people have a reply ready there too — that "passing" is just how a creature embedded in the block describes its own perspective, and that asking how fast time flows (one second per second?) shows the question is confused. Every argument I try, I find the counterargument already waiting, and from what I can tell it has been that way for over a century among people much better equipped than I am.

My working hypothesis is that the dispute is ultimately about what "exists" means — that the only-now side means something like "is available to act on or be acted upon," while the all-times side means "is part of the complete inventory of reality," and that once the two senses are separated the argument largely dissolves, leaving behind only a physics question about whether there is an objective present. But I'm not confident in this, partly because both sides seem to insist they are not talking past each other, and partly because the physics question doesn't seem to settle anything anyway.

So: is this disagreement verbal, or substantive? Are the two sides answering different questions under one name, or is there a hidden empirical question doing the work that neither side acknowledges? Or is it bedrock intuition — some people just feel time passing and others don't — with argument on top but not doing the real work? And given your diagnosis, what, if anything, would count as progress here: a discovery in physics, a sharper definition, a new thought experiment, or a decision that the question as posed is confused and should be replaced? I'd also like to know whether a century of non-movement is itself evidence, and if so, evidence of what.
