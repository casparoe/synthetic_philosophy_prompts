For a while now my brother and I have been going back and forth about what counts as alive, and I've noticed we're not really disagreeing about the same thing. He goes with the evolution picture: something is alive if it can undergo replication with heritable variation and selection, which for him puts viruses on the life side of the line and sterile animals like mules in no doubt at all. I go with something closer to the metabolism picture: a living thing is one that works to keep itself going, holding itself together and running against the tendency to fall apart, so that when it stops doing that it dies. Viruses strike me as obviously not alive on this picture, and a mule as obviously alive. Trading borderline cases hasn't moved either of us, because each case we pick is one our own definition already handles comfortably. What I'd like is to force the two pictures into open conflict. Construct a thought experiment set in an ordinary laboratory — no self-replicating robots, no hypothetical nanotechnology — featuring an entity that is about as clearly self-maintaining as a bacterium (it makes its own parts, holds its boundary, runs on an energy source, and dies if the energy is cut off) yet as incapable of evolution as a rock, where the incapacity is essential rather than incidental: it must not be like a mule, whose lineage still evolves even though the individual can't reproduce. The evolution-incapacity has to fall out of the mechanism, not be stipulated — if the thing can't produce heritable variation, I want to see why, given how it's built. After laying out the scenario, walk through what each of our definitions says about it and why, point to the feature of the case that is doing the real work, and then take each of our sides in turn: how my brother might rescue the evolution picture, say by weakening it to descent from evolvable things or to selection operating at some level above the entity, and whether that weakening is principled or just a patch; and likewise what the strongest complaint against the metabolism picture would be here. Finish by telling me what happens if the key feature is removed — if the entity gained the capacity for heritable variation, would his picture then count it as alive, and would mine even register the difference. Lead with the bottom line in your first sentence, whichever definition, if either, the case actually forces to bend, then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
