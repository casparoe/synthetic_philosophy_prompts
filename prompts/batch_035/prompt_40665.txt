I work as a strategy consultant. Clients bring me competitive decision problems — pricing, sealed bids, contract terms — where they must pick one option while a counterparty picks theirs, and my job in a first meeting is to prune the client's own option list down to the ones worth discussing. I want the pruning to rest on dominance reasoning, including the version where an option is beaten not by any single other option but by a randomized mix of two, since clients never think of that themselves and it's often the only eliminations available. Please write a procedure I can run at a whiteboard in twenty minutes with rough, client-elicited payoff estimates: the steps in order, what counts as dominated (strict versus weak, by a pure option versus by a mixture), when to stop iterating, and what to do when the payoffs are too vague to compare at all. It should be honest that iterated elimination only follows if the counterparty is rational in exactly the same way and that this is known, that striking weakly dominated options can discard the best option against an opponent who blunders, and that with weak dominance the surviving set can depend on the order of elimination — so say where the procedure should default to the more conservative test. Then run it on two cases: a three-option tender where one option is dominated only by a mixture of the other two, and a case where the procedure strikes the client's favorite option because flipping a coin between two others does at least as well against every counterparty move and strictly better against one — include how to present that finding without losing the room. Mark clearly the points where the numbers cannot decide and the judgment is mine.
