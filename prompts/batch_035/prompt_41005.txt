Write a dialogue between two historians arguing about whether the assassination of Franz Ferdinand caused WWI. One is a structuralist: she thinks the alliance system, the mobilization schedules, and Anglo-German naval rivalry made a general European war close to inevitable by 1914, so calling the assassination "the cause" is basically a category error — at best it was a trigger. The other is a contingentist: he thinks the specific events of July 1914 (the ultimatum to Serbia, the blank cheque, the cascade of mobilizations) genuinely made the difference, and that if Princip had missed, there's no general war in August 1914 — maybe not for years, maybe not in that shape at all.

A few requirements:

1. The contingentist should at some point object that the structuralist keeps saying "overdetermined" when that's not really what she means. Strict overdetermination is two independently sufficient causes each producing the same outcome (like two shooters firing simultaniously at one victim) — but her actual claim is that the outcome is insensitive to swapping out the trigger, which is weaker. He can mockingly dub her view "trigger-swap robustness" or something. Importantly, she has to actually respond to this point, not just brush it off — maybe by arguing that if war would have broken out by 1917 under almost any replacement trigger, the practical difference between the two claims doesn't matter much, and he can push back on that.
2. The contingentist should also press on the unfalsifiability of "it would've happened anyway" claims — how could we possibly know?
3. The structuralist needs some real hits of her own, e.g. that but-for counterfactuals make nearly anything "the cause" depending on which alternative world you pick, or that "the assassination caused WWI" and "the alliance system caused WWI" aren't competing claims at different levels at all.

Both should be clearly competent, and they should respond to each other's actual points rather than talking past each other. It's fine (honestly preferable) if they end by narrowing the disagreement to something precise rather than agreeing wholesale. Aim for something like 600-900 words.
