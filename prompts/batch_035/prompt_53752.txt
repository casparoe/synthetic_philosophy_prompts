Some of the loose threads I've noticed, to give you a sense of the neighborhood: whether Elinor Ostrom's design principles for self-governing commons (clear boundaries, graduated sanctions, cheap dispute resolution, etc.) can actually be scaled or transplanted, or whether they only work for the small irrigation and fishery commons she studied and everyone citing her for climate is doing something sketchy. Then there's the taxonomy itself: public goods people mostly argue about whether good G is excludable or rival, but goods also differ in how contributions combine — threshold goods where nothing happens until enough people pitch in (crowdfunding, the "assurance contract" idea), weakest-link goods where the worst contributor determines the outcome, and so on — and it's not obvious the standard subsidy/logic-of-collective-action toolkit transfers across those types. And lately I wonder whether digital commons like open-source software and Wikipedia are genuinely a new case, since contributions there are so cheap to monitor and reputational rewards are so visible, or whether that's just the old small-group logic of conditional cooperation wearing new clothes.
