I've been on the zombies/conceivability beat for like a decade now, ever since I fell down the LessWrong rabbit hole and then actually read the primary sources instead of just the blog posts about them. At this point my considered view is basically the Chalmers-style two-dimensionalist package: ideal positive conceivability (conceivability where you've reasoned it all out, filled in all the details, no hidden contradictions waiting to be discovered) is a defeasible but real guide to metaphysical possibility. So the zombie world is ideally conceivable, and that's prima facie evidence it's metaphysically possible, which then does serious damage to physicalism. The Kripke a posteriori necessities don't scare me because of the primary/secondary intension machinery -- "water is H2O" is necessary at the primary-intension level once you fix the reference by description, but "this watery stuff is H2O" was conceivable-and-false only in a way that the zombie case allegedly isn't. I've read the back-and-forth with the phenomenological-strategy people and I mostly come away thinking they win on heat but lose on light.

But here's my actual ask, because I'm trying to stress-test this rather than just rehearse the standard dialectic like everyone at the meetup does. Give me exactly five things that would count against my position, ranked from most to least threatening, and for each one a sentence or two saying what it is and why it sits where it sits in the ranking. I want the real candidates, not strawmen: stuff like scenarios where ideal conceivability and possibility come apart systematically, or ways the primary/secondary intension move quietly begs the question, or results about what happens to the view if you can't actually run the idealization in finite agents like us (this one keeps me up at night a bit -- "ideal positive conceivability" is doing a LOT of work and I'm never sure it's well-defined enough to do it). Distinguish what a committed two-dimensionalist would concede as genuine counterevidence from what they'd wave off, and tell me honestly if the position has been qualified into something that can't lose anymore -- and if so whether that's fatal for a principle that's supposed to be doing epistemological work rather than just being a description of our concepts.

Format-wise: exactly five numbered items, one or two sentences each, no intro paragraph and no conclusion -- just dive into item 1 and let item 5 be the last word. And since I'm asking for a ranking that's supposed to reflect actual epistemic weights, I want you to end each item with a quick tag on what would move that item up or down your ranking, i.e. where your own judgment here is most likely to go wrong -- I'd rather have your honest uncertainty flags than a confident-sounding ordering you can't actually defend.
