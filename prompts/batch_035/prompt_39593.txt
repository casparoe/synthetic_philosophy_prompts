Hi! Novelist here, three chapters into a book whose POV character is a neuroscientist (cliche alert, I know, bear with me), and I've hit a scene I keep rewriting. The scene: my character is at a party, someone says her name across the room, and she *notices* that she heard it — that little jolt of wait, that was my name. I want to render the split second between the sound hitting her ear and her being aware of it, from the inside, and I keep flip-flopping between two ways of writing it.

Way one is what I've been calling the screening-room version: somewhere in her head there's a little audience — her, the real her — watching a finished feed of sights and sounds, and when her name scrolls across the screen the audience perks up. Very cinematic, easy to write, and honestly most of my draft interiority has worked this way so far without me thinking about it.

Way two is messier: no screening room, no single finished moment, just lots of parallel drafts of "what just happened" being written and rewritten at different speeds, and what she calls noticing her name is whichever draft happens to be winning when the story gets filed. The jolt isn't her reacting to the sound; it's one draft overhauling another mid-sentence.

I described this to a friend who took one philosophy of mind course and she said I've basically reinvented a real debate — she called the two things the Cartesian theater and Dennett's multiple drafts model. I'd never heard of either, went down a rabbit hole, and now I have questions.

Can you compare the two for me, but specifically as they bear on the craft problem I described? Like: (1) what does each one actually say about a moment like hearing your name at a party, (2) what does each one say is wrong with the other, and (3) which one is closer to how you'd actually write that split second — the screening-room version, the drafts version, or something in between? If you think the theater version is genuinely bad philosophy but still good prose, say so, I can take it. One thing I keep chewing on too: in the drafts version, who or what is the "she" that experiences the jolt? Feels like if I commit to it I lose my character entirely and the book becomes a machine narrating itself — which, fine, but then who exactly is sad when her dog dies in chapter nine? Help me think that through as well.

Give me your honest take, and make the very last line exactly "Verdict: X" where X is one of "theater", "drafts", or "in between", whichever way you come down for my scene.
