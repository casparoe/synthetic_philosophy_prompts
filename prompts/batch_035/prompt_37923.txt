I buy the big picture that the world has been getting less violent over the long run -- fewer war deaths per capita, homicide rates way down from historical levels, torture going from spectacle to scandal, etc. But I want to actually stress test it, not just enjoy it. What would genuinely count against the view?

Some things I want covered, and then take it wherever you think it needs to go:

- How much of the trend survives messing with the definition of violence? If you fold in structural violence (avoidable deaths from deprivation), or factory farming, or the violence done to the biosphere generally, does the trend flatten or even flip? And is that a fair move or just changing the subject?
- Per capita vs absolute numbers. A 90% drop in per capita war deaths can coexist with more total dead humans than any previous century, just because there are so many more of us. Which is the right thing to measure, or is there no fact of the matter about which one the thesis is "really" about?
- Start-point sensitivity. The story looks great from the mid-20th century or from the Neolithic. Does it look the same from, say, 1913, or from the perspective of a Pacific islander or a native american population whose "long peace" ended catastrophically? How much does the thesis depend on where you stand when you draw the line?
- Something I've been chewing on that I havent seen discussed much: everyone measures rates, i.e. averages. But you could instead ask about the distribution of violence -- like the fraction of people who will personally witness a killing in their lifetime, or the median person's lifetime probability of being attacked, or tail risk (the worst-case scenario a typical person has to live under the shadow of). It seems logically possible for the mean to fall while the median exposure rises, or for both to fall while tail risk grows (nuclear weapons sitting there being peaceful). Is that a real potential defeater, or is it just reframing the question until you get the answer you want?

Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a defender of the view would themselves concede are real trouble versus wave off, and be honest with me about whether the thesis has been qualified so many times -- well, obviously not ALL violence, obviously not per every subpopulation, obviously not from every start date -- that it's no longer capable of losing an argument, and whether that matters for a claim of this kind. I'd rather find out I'm wrong now than keep believing a comfortable thing.
