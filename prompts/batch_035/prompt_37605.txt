i run the moderation team for a mid-sized gaming forum, about 40k users, 4 mods total including me. no policy department, no lawyers, appeals come to me. our rules are things like "no harassment," "no low-effort posts," "keep it roughly PG-13," and most of my day is arguing with the other mods about whether a specific post is over the line. none of them are ever obviously over or under. it's always "well, it's kind of rude but he's also kind of joking."

I've been thinking about this on my own for a while and here's where I've landed. there's that old puzzle where you take one grain off a heap of sand and it's still a heap, take another off, still a heap, but if you keep going you end up with 3 grains and that's obviously not a heap — no single step ever makes a difference, yet somewhere in the run it flipped. that IS my job. for any single report we could honestly say "this post isn't meaningfully worse than the one we approved an hour ago," but across a whole week anyone can see the tone went from fine to garbage, and none of us can point to the post where it crossed. every piece of advice we get ("write clearer rules," "just be consistent," "use your judgment") seems to quietly assume there's a place the line actually IS. I've become pretty convinced there isn't one — there's just a fuzzy stretch. but we still have to rule on every single post, and users quote our own past calls back at us ("you allowed this guy to say basically the same thing yesterday").

so what I want from you: a decision procedure my team can actually run per report, in 5 minutes or less (we clear ~150 reports a day), grounded in whatever philosophy has figured out about this kind of thing — I'm assuming people have thought hard about fuzzy lines and there's something usable in there. concretely it should have: the questions to ask about a post, in order; what counts as a good enough answer to each one; the points where the procedure outputs "remove" or "allow"; and the points where it should stop and hand it to a human because the remaining question is about what kind of community we want, not a fact about the post.

then run it on three posts for me so I can see it work: (1) a guy who never uses a slur or makes a threat but has spent two weeks showing up under every post by one specific player to "just ask questions" about her gameplay, (2) a low-effort post that's literally one meme image with the caption "thoughts?", and (3) a post where the procedure gives the answer I won't like — and tell me honestly that it does.

two more things I need the procedure to handle. first, the consistency complaint: should we keep a precedent log, and if so what happens when the log has genuinely conflicting calls on basically identical posts (which it will)? second, at some point a procedure like this probably has to stipulate cutoffs somewhere — like "3 pile-on replies in a thread = a dogpile" — even though nobody thinks the real line is at 3. is that the right move, philosophically? what exactly do we gain and lose by stipulating, and is there any version of this that doesn't have to? last thing: be honest with me about which steps of the procedure are just judgment calls wearing a procedure costume. I'd rather know than pretend.
