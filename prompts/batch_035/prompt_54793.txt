so I write about AI and automation for a living (science and ideas desk at a mid-size outlet, been on the beat about ten years) and somewhere along the way I assembled a set of opinions about automation that I've realized are basically Marx-flavored, even tho I came to them thru covering tech rather than thru reading him. I did eventually go read the Grundrisse machine fragment after everyone kept citing it at me, plus Capital vol 1. Anyway I'm planning a longread where I lay out my actual position, and before I do I want someone to pressure-test whether the position hangs together. here's the package:

1. I take the labor theory of value seriously, or at least seriously enough: living labor is what creates new value, machines just move their own value across into the product. profit comes from squeezing surplus labor, not from machines being clever.

2. the rate of profit really does tend to fall as firms mechanize — higher organic composition, same ultimate source of surplus value. I read the postwar boom and every revival since as the counter-tendencies (cheapening constant capital, opening new labor markets, etc.) doing overtime, and those run out eventually.

3. full automation, or near enough, is coming this century, and it's terminal for capitalism as a value system: no workers, no surplus value, no profit. this is where I think the Grundrisse fragment is basically right — once labor time stops being the measure of wealth, production on the basis of exchange value breaks down.

4. but here's the thing — I don't actually expect a collapse! my honest prediction is the system muddles thru: a shrinking core of technical labor, a huge precariat doing underpaid care-type and made-up work, and the state papering over the gap with transfers and UBI-style schemes. capitalism survives precisely by refusing to automate the last mile.

5. long-run I'm an optimist: eventually the machines get owned in common and you get the short working day / realm of freedom outcome. I think that's the actual payoff of the whole analysis.

also, for what it's worth — as someone whose own job got cheaper to do with every model release, it's genuinely hard to look at an AI producing a publishable draft and NOT think the machines are creating value now. I assume that's me being sloppy about value vs use-value, but flag it if its worse than sloppy.
