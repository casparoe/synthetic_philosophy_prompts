I host a small philosophy-of-physics podcast — audience is mostly grad students and a few physics teachers, so the listeners can handle technical stuff, but they hate it when a guest just recites their book. Next week's guest is a philosopher who's spent a big chunk of his career defending the block universe via the Rietdijk–Putnam argument, the one where you take two observers moving relative to each other, note that their planes of simultaneity tilt differently, and conclude that whether a distant event has "happened yet" is observer-relative — and since someone's simultaneity plane always slices through your future, the future is exactly as determinate as the past. He's also fond of saying this is "just special relativity plus the obvious principle that what is observed is real," and that presentists are clinging to leftover Newtonian time.

Please act as a philosopher of physics who has prepped a lot of these interviews — think carefully and maybe brainstorm a bit before drafting. I need eight to ten questions that actually press where the literature presses, not "could you walk us through the argument again." Specifically I want at least one question on the transitivity step: the argument needs the relation "is real for observer O" to chain across observers, and it's not obvious that's physics rather than a metaphysical assumption wearing a lab coat. At least one on the worry that it proves too much — the Andromeda-style case where two people strolling past each other on a street corner in opposite directions thereby have different verdicts about whether a fleet light-years away has already departed, which a lot of people feel is a reductio of the premises rather than a proof of the conclusion. One on the response that the argument at most gives us determinateness-relative-to-an-observer, and that maybe "determinate" should fragment along observer lines just like simultaneity does, instead of being absolute in a way that forces one unified block. And one on whether determinism is being quietly smuggled in — the block being fixed doesn't mean it's determined, since a block can contain genuinely chancy events, so ask him whether he needs a separate argument for determinism or whether determinism was never his claim. For each question, include a follow-up keyed to the answer he's most likley to give — e.g., if he insists the slicing is purely conventional, ask whether a conventionally chosen slicing can ground a metaphysical conclusion about what exists, and if he bites the bullet on Andromeda, ask him what he'd say to someone who runs the same bullet-biting in reverse to argue nothing is determinate.

No generic stuff either — skip "what drew you to this topic," skip asking him to restate special relativity. My listeners will roll their eyes. If it helps, my last episode on this got a bunch of emails saying nobody ever asks block-universe people about the gap between "determinate" and "real," so lean into that distinction wherever you can.
