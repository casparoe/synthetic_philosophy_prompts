Hey, could you write me a satirical piece? I want a mock consumer-product recall notice for a product called Human Existence, done deadpan in the style of actual government recall announcements — the kind that open by naming the cooperating agency, then list the hazard, the affected units, and instructions for consumers. Around 700 words.

The target is Benatar-style antinatalism, and the humor has to come from actually getting his asymmetry right, not from a vague life-is-suffering mope. So the recall notice should present his asymmetry as if it were an engineering defect report: the product ships with a design flaw under which (a) an absence of pain counts as a good even when there's no one around to enjoy that good, (b) an absence of pleasure doesn't count as a bad unless someone exists to be deprived of it, (c) therefore every unit that ships is a net loss relative to the unit that never ships, and (d) no one is harmed by the never-shipped unit, which is why nobody who never recieved the product has ever filed a complaint. The manufacturer, to its credit, accepts the reasoning and is recalling accordingly.

The fine print should also work in some of the more in-the-weeds stuff, fairly:

- the distinction between a life worth continuing and a life worth starting, so the no-refund policy for discontinued units can get a joke out of the fact that a unit can be not worth ending while still having been not worth starting;
- the optimism-bias point about self-assessment — owners of defective units systematically rate them highly, which the manufacturer can cite as a reason not to trust owner reviews;
- coverage of both the philanthropic strain (the world damages the units) and the misanthropic strain (the units damage the world and each other), maybe as a checkbox situation;
- something on the complaint line about how the only people in a position to complain about non-delivery are, by construction, the delivered ones.

Keep the irony legible but unwinking — the manufacturer is sincere, competent, and agrees with the antinatalist all the way down, and the What Consumers Should Do section should follow that logic to wherever it honestly leads. Someone who has actually read Benatar should come away thinking the piece understands him and is being fair to the argument, even while it gut-punches them a little. No real named people inside the piece itself; invent the company. Don't use any verbatim quotes from real texts either — paraphrase any philosophical content in your own words.

Write the whole thing (and any framing you add) in American English please — grey/gray, apologise/apologize and all that. Maybe 700 words, a little more is fine, and don't let it tip over into open contempt for parents or children; the satire is aimed at the argument's structure, not at anybody's family.
