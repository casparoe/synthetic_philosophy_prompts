My credence that the correct answer to the standard Sleeping Beauty problem — fair coin, one awakening under heads, two under tails, no indication of the day — is 1/3, rather than 1/2 or anything else, sits at about 0.6; a colleague of mine who works on self-locating belief puts it at 0.95. Please give your own number, but more importantly decompose it into the sub-questions it rests on, with a rough probability and one line of justification for each: (a) whether Beauty's knowing on Sunday night that she will be awakened at least once makes the awakening itself evidentially empty, or whether "I am awake now" is genuine new information; (b) whether, when she is told upon waking that the coin landed tails, she should split her credence evenly between "this is Monday" and "this is Tuesday" — the restricted indifference over centered worlds from which the thirder answer is derived; (c) whether, upon instead being told that it is Monday, her heads-credence must move to 1/2 by ordinary conditioning, may stay put, or can even be driven above 1/2, as happens to those who accept (b) while holding the halfer answer; and (d) whether Beauty's willingness to accept even-money bets on heads at each awakening — fair by halfer lights, yet a guaranteed money-loser across the experiment — shows that the halfer credence is mistaken, or only that credence and betting behavior come apart when one agent places bets from multiple centered locations. Show how your numbers combine into the final credence, identify the sub-question the result is most sensitive to, and then tell me which of us — the 0.6 or the 0.95 — most likely disagrees about which sub-question. If you think some further sub-question belongs in the decomposition, add it with its own probability and justification.
