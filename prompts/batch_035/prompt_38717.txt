I am a graduate teaching assistant for an introduction-to-proofs course, and the recent unit on cardinality has ignited a running argument between the instructor, a set theorist, and the other TA, a former physicist with finitist sympathies. It has spilled across three of our weekly TA meetings, the undergraduates have begun taking sides, and I lead the review session on Cantor's diagonal argument next week, so I would like to get clear on it before I stand in front of the room.

The dispute began with the infinite hotel thought experiment attributed to Hilbert: a hotel with a room for every natural number, every room occupied, and a new guest accommodated by having each guest move from room n to room n+1. Her opening move was that nothing incoherent happens: each guest ends up with a room, the new guest is housed, and every individual guest can verify their own new assignment. Actual infinite totalities are therefore coherent, and the discomfort people feel is just finite-trained intuition misfiring.

His reply was that this shows only that a formal calculus does not immediately contradict itself. The hotel is full in the strongest possible sense — no room is empty — and yet it admits a newcomer; so the word full has been drained of content, an object that is both full and accommodating is not a possible object, and the formalism has quietly stopped describing the thing it was introduced to describe.

Her second move: full simply means that no room is unoccupied, and that remains true throughout the reshuffling. And the fact that adding one guest changes nothing about the cardinality is a theorem, not a defect — a set and that set plus one further element are equinumerous, and the measure only looks broken if one demands that it behave like finite counting.

His second move: that indifference is precisely the defect. A quantity that neither additions nor removals can move has stopped tracking how many in any sense applicable to a hotel. Then his removal argument: evict the guests in every odd-numbered room and infinitely many rooms stand empty; evict every guest in a room numbered above three and exactly three rooms stand empty. The same number of guests — infinitely many — is removed in both cases, and yet the leftovers differ, with nothing in the cardinal arithmetic that predicts which. Subtraction on infinite cardinals is indeterminate, he said, and no concrete object could have indeterminate arithmetic; the hotel is a stable formal fiction being passed off as a possibility.

Her third move: the indeterminacy lives in the phrase the same number removed, not in the hotel. Removing infinitely many is not a specification until you say which elements, and once you do, the leftovers are perfectly determinate — the operations he described differ in the set removed, so there is no single subtraction whose result could be expected to be unique. Cardinal subtraction is a partial function, like many respectable functions, and no contradiction has been extracted from the theory in a century and a half. Mathematical existence has only ever meant coherence plus fruitfulness, and she claims nothing beyond that.

His third move: then the lesson the students are being handed — that infinity has been tamed and the objections answered — smuggles in metaphysics she has just disclaimed. And the story still quietly presupposes a completed infinite task: the relocation is an infinite doing, the announcement of it must reach every guest, and no signal could. If actual infinities of concrete things were really on the table, the physics would be doing real work, and it runs the wrong way.

Her final move: the task is a fiction of the telling. The guests need only a standing rule and their own room numbers; the story requires only that the assignment exist, and it does. The question was coherence, never nomological possibility. He closed by saying that if the claim is only formal coherence, he accepts it, and the headline taught to students should change accordingly.

I could not referee this, and I do not want to lead my review session by repeating slogans I cannot defend. Please go through their exchange move by move: who is right at each step and about what exactly, where each of them concedes or shifts ground without acknowledging it, and what, if anything, both are missing.
