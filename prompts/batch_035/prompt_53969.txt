I'm a software engineer with no philosophy background, but two of my colleagues have spent a year arguing about whether multipolar traps — races to the bottom in which no participant prefers the outcome but no participant can unilaterally stop competing — can actually be escaped, and I've agreed to run an adversarial collaboration between them before it eats any more of our design reviews. The pessimist holds that whenever the number of viable competitors is large and defection is privately profitable, competitive selection strips out restraint regardless of what individual agents want; the optimist holds that this overcounts selection and undercounts engineering, since agents in repeated interaction can build coordination mechanisms — mutual monitoring, verifiable commitments, open standards, liability regimes — that make cooperation stable without a singleton enforcer. Write the joint statement they should be able to sign. I want: a numbered section of claims both accept, including about what it takes for a situation to count as a race at all, which agents can be racing (firms, states, codebases, memes), and which real cases genuinely instantiate one versus merely looking like one from a distance; a disagreements section in which each remaining disagreement is worded so that its own side would accept the phrasing; the cruxes — I'd guess one is whether increasing the number of competitors raises or lowers the odds of eventual coordination, and another is whether "Moloch" names something with interests or is just a metaphor for an aggregation pattern — with, for each crux, a concrete case or argument both would agree bears on it and each side's prediction written down in advance; and a verdict on which parts of their dispute are substantive and which are merely verbal.
