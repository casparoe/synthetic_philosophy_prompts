Exam question:
"In a Bell-type experiment, a source emits pairs of entangled particles to two distant laboratories. Each lab randomly chooses one of two measurement settings and records an outcome, and quantum mechanics predicts joint correlations violating the CHSH inequality. (a) State precisely which class of theories Bell's theorem rules out, and identify the assumptions from which the inequality is derived. (b) Explain why the observed violations, though stronger than any theory in that class permits, still cannot be used to transmit a signal faster than light."

Student's answer:
"(a) Bell's theorem shows that no local hidden-variable theory can reproduce the quantum predictions. A hidden-variable theory supplements the quantum state with extra variables λ, carried by the particles from the source, which complete the description and fix what happens in each run. The derivation of the inequality rests on two assumptions. First, locality: the outcome at each wing depends only on the local setting and on λ, not on which setting is chosen at the distant wing. Second, determinism: given λ and the local setting, the outcome is fixed with probability one. From these assumptions it follows that the correlations must obey the CHSH bound of 2, whereas quantum mechanics allows values up to 2√2, and loophole-free experiments have confirmed violations of the bound. Since local hidden-variable theories are thereby ruled out, nature itself must be nonlocal: when one particle is measured, the physical state of the other is instantaneously altered, however far away it is.

(b) Even so, no usable signal can be sent. Each individual outcome is intrinsically random, and the statistics observed at one wing are the same no matter which setting is chosen at the other wing; only when the two labs later compare records do the correlations appear. So the instantaneous influence is present but cannot be modulated: because outcomes cannot be controlled, the distant lab never receives anything it would not have received anyway. Superluminal communication is therefore impossible, even though superluminal causation occurs on every run."
