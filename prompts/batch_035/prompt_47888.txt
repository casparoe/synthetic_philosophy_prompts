I'm a graduate student in cognitive science, and a fellow student and I have been arguing about Dennett's denial of the Cartesian theater — the claim that there is no single place or time in the brain where conscious contents arrive to be appreciated by an inner audience. My credence that this denial is correct is about 0.75; hers is about 0.45. Her reservation is not that she believes in a soul-like spectator, but that she suspects global workspace theory has quietly smuggled the theater back in: the ignition event reported in workspace experiments — a coordinated frontoparietal burst after which a stimulus becomes reportable — looks to her like a stage and a spotlight, whatever Dennett says. Give me your own credence, but decompose it into the terms that actually drive the number: the probability that a functionally identifiable moment of global access counts as a theater in the philosophically loaded sense, versus whether Dennett's alternative — availability understood as a content acquiring influence over subsequent processing and report, rather than being presented to anyone — dissolves the worry; how much weight postdictive phenomena such as color phi and the cutaneous rabbit deserve, given that both the theater and the multiple drafts model can be stretched to accommodate them; whether the felt unity of experience is a datum that a theater-free account must explain rather than explain away; and whether the dispute is partly verbal, so that the theater hypothesis is never clearly falsifiable. Assign each term a rough probability or weight, show how they combine into your final number, and tell me which term that number is most sensitive to — and which of the two of us, at 0.75 and 0.45, is most plausibly disagreeing about that term rather than about anything else.
