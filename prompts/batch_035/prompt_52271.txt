I've never taken a philosophy class in my life, this is just something I chew on during my commute, so forgive me if I'm reinventing wheels here. My position, which I've gotten pretty attached to over the years: when a sculptor shapes a lump of clay into a statue, you don't just have one thing there, you have two — the lump and the statue — sitting in the exact same spot, made of the exact same stuff. They're not the same object because they don't stick around under the same conditions. Squash the statue flat and the lump's still there, just lumping around, but the statue is gone. If they were really one thing, how could one survive and the other not? And the reverse: if the sculptor had never finished and just left the clay in a bag, the lump would exist without the statue, so the lump clearly doesn't need the statue. That asymmetry is the whole reason I think they're two.

I've also worked out a couple of my own corollaries that I'd want to defend or give up as a package. One: I think the same goes for me and my body — I'm one object, my body is another, coinciding until something happens to just one of them. Two: I keep count of things the way I learned to do with the statue, so when my friend says she owns "one thing" that's really two coinciding objects (say, a wedding ring and its gold), I think there's a literal sense in which she owns two things and I'm inclined to say so. Three: the persistence conditions are what individuate the objects, so I'm okay saying there are two objects even though there's only one "hunk of matter" — the hunk, as I'd put it, is what they share, not what they are.
