I'm a science journalist and I've spent the last few years covering the LLM boom, and somewhere along the way I settled into what I guess you'd call the parrots-skeptical position: that these systems are doing sophisticated pattern-matching over training text and nothing that deserves the word understanding. It started when I read the 2021 paper by Bender and Gebru and colleagues (the one with the bird in the title) and it's stuck with me through every demo I've been shown since. When people show me a model writing poetry or passing bar exams, my stock line in print has been that a parrot that says "Polly wants a cracker" when hungry all ten times hasn't grasped the concept of hunger, it's learned an association, and I've basically argued that scaling that up a billion-fold changes the sociology of the parrot but not the metaphysics. I should be honest that part of why I hold this is vibes and career momentum at this point — I've written it enough times that writing it again is easy, which I recognize is itself a kind of parroting, and that embarrasses me a little.

So here's what I want from you, and I want it straight, because I'm deciding whether my next column should be a genuinely serious self-audit rather than a token both-sides piece. What would actually count against my view? Rank the defeaters for me from fatal to merely inconvenient, tell me which ones the people on my own side of this debate would concede versus dismiss (and whether the dismissals are principled or motivated), and be blunt about whether my position has quietly become unfalsifiable — whether I've defined "understanding" in a way that no behavior, no probe result, no architecture could ever disconfirm, and if I have, whether that's actually a problem for a view of this kind or whether it's a reasonable epistemic caution given how much money is being spent trying to convince me otherwise.
