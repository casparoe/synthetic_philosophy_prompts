My workplace's union branch has voted to strike, and I have to decide by Friday whether to join the picket line. The dispute is over the closure of the final-salary pension scheme to newer staff — a change that was already imposed on those of us hired within the last five years, so the strike, even if it succeeds, would preserve the scheme only for colleagues hired before the cut-off. My own terms would not improve by a penny. I benefit from the union in other ways (last year's pay rise came from its bargaining), and I know that if everyone in my position stayed out, the action would probably fail. On the other hand, I pay my dues, I voted against the strike, and the immediate stakes don't touch me. Some of my newer colleagues say joining would be pure sentiment; some of the older ones say that if we let the scheme close for new entrants, the same logic will eventually be turned on every benefit, and that this strike is really about what happens once we're the senior staff. Which of these considerations actually bears on what I should do, and which are just pressure dressed up as principle?
