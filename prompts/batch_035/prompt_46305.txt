I moderate a big discord server where people argue about generational stuff constantly ("millenials killed X", "ok boomer", "gen z can't write cursive" etc), and whenever the argument gets heated someone eventually plays the trump card that generations aren't real, they're just something marketers invented to sell stuff, and someone ELSE says no, they're a real sociological thing with actual effects. These arguments never go anywhere because everyone means something different by "real." So I want to make a sorting quiz for the server: "what do you actually think a generation is?", and I need help writing it.

Spec: somewhere between 8 and 12 questions, each a concrete case or thought experiment, each with 2-4 answer options. Crucially, no question can just ask "do you think generations are socially constructed?" — people will answer based on vibes and which label they like the sound of. Instead use cases where the positions genuinely come apart, like:

- two siblings born 14 months apart on either side of some official cutoff (say 1996/1997 for millennial vs gen z) — did anything real just happen at midnight, or is the boundary pure bookkeeping?
- a person born in 1962 who grew up poor in a rural area with no TV vs. a person born in 1948 with a suburban upbringing — are they the same generation, and what does your answer commit you to?
- a kid who immigrated at age 11 into a cohort's culture and absorbed it, vs a native-born kid who grew up in an insulated religious community and missed the cohort's formative experiences entirely
- the 2008 financial crash hitting someone at 22 vs at 55 — when we say a generation was "shaped by" an event, is that a fact about the cohort or just an age effect that would've hit any cohort passing through that age at that time? (I gather there's some classic identification problem here with age vs. period vs. cohort effects, where you can't fully separate the three — worth building a question around that if you can do it accessibly)
- whether a generation could exist with nobody self-identifying as part of it — like, if nobody had ever coined "generation X," would the thing still be there to be discovered?
- whether calling people millennials does causal work: does the label make the group more group-like (people reading thinkpieces about their generation and then acting accordingly)?

Then I need a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto actual positions in the philosophical space, and I'd rather you build it from the logical space than from a list of famous names: figure out what the fundamental questions are (is the grouping objective or observer-relative? does it have causal powers of its own or is it just a correlate of birth-year-bounded exposures? does it require self-identification or shared consciousness of kind?), and then locate the real positions in that grid. I'd expect at least these to show up as leaves: the hard realist view (generations are genuine social kinds that exist independent of anyone labeling them), the moderate realist view going back to the Mannheim tradition (real, but constituted by shared formative location, and splintering into what he called generation units), the nominalist / deflationary view (a "generation" is just a renamed birth cohort plus noise, a convenience for statisticians), and the pragmatic instrumentalist view (fiction, but a useful one that earns its keep for prediction or explanation). If there are others worth including, add them.

Also: please flag any pair of leaves the quiz can't actually separate (I suspect the moderate realist and the instrumentalist end up giving the same answers to most cases and I want to know if that's just my quiz being bad or whether the positions themselves only differ verbally). And if some combination of answers is coherent but nobody holds it, note that too, with a sentence on whether that's because the combo is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored. My server has a mix of people from total laypeople to a couple of soc grad students, so keep the question wording accessible, but the scoring key can be as technical as it needs to be.
