was at a philosophy of mind reading group last week and someone basically shut down the whole hard problem discussion by declaring that consciousness is a "user illusion" -- the brain builds a GUI for itself and what we call phenomenality is just the icons on that interface, so there's nothing left to explain. the way he said it, it was supposed to dissolve the hard problem so we could all go home early.

i get the metaphor (he was riffing on the old computer desktop thing: you "see" a folder on the screen but there's no folder down there in the machine, just voltages and gates). what i can't figure out is what the actual claim is. it seems like it could mean anything from something pretty boring to something totally wild, and the wild version sounds self-undermining. so: can you lay out the distinct precise things "consciousness is a user illusion" could be claiming? i have guesses -- something weak like "introspection is spotty and unreliable", something middling like "our self-model misrepresents the causal machinery underneath it", something radical like "there is no phenomenal character at all, full stop" -- but i'm sure i'm missing readings, and i want the full menu.

for each reading, say (a) whether it's basically a platitude, (b) whether it's substantive and defensible if so, and (c) this is the part i actually care about, whether it gets you out of the hard problem or just renames it. like, the radical reading might really make the hard problem go away if it's true, but does anything support it, or does it just assert the conclusion? and the weak readings seem obviously true but then i don't see how they end the discussion the way he wanted.

related thing i keep tripping over: an illusion of a pink rat still involves something pink-ish seeming to happen in experience. so how can pain be an illusion unless there's something pain-ish going on? the illusion seems to presuppose a bit of the very thing it's supposed to be an illusion OF. which readings survive that objection, and is it a knockdown or does the illusionist have a decent reply (e.g. about how it only applies to seemings that have the right kind of external-directed content)?

lay the readings out as a numbered list so i can bring it to the next meeting, and at the end tell me which one you think the guy actually meant and whether it licensed ending the discusion like that. i suspect the answer is that he was sliding between readings, using the weak one to make it sound obvious and the strong one to get the payoff, but i'd like to see it spelled out properly.
