I spent most of Saturday arguing with a friend about whether structural realism is a genuine middle path between full-blown scientific realism and instrumentalism, and the argument kept slipping sideways because we never fixed what the middle path is supposed to accomplish. His line was that structural realism is either trivial or empty: if it says only that the mathematical structure of successful theories is approximately true, then the Newman objection reduces it to a claim about cardinalities that nearly anyone could accept; and if it goes all the way to "there is nothing but structure," then it owes an account of relations without relata, which he found incoherent. My line was that the pessimistic meta-induction still needs answering — the aether, phlogiston, and caloric were all doing real explanatory work in their day — and that something like Fresnel's equations surviving into Maxwell's theory, with the aether itself discarded, suggests that structure really is what gets retained across theory change. But I noticed neither of us was arguing about structural realism per se; we were arguing past each other about what any position in this debate has to achieve, and I want to settle that first before going another round with him.

So write me the desiderata: the requirements any adequate position on the realist-instrumentalist spectrum must meet, with the motivating case or consideration behind each. At minimum I want you to cover what the position owes the no-miracles intuition, what it owes the historical record of discarded ontologies, how it must respond to the Newman objection or else concede triviality, whether it must draw and defend a distinction between structural content and ontological posits (and whether that distinction can survive theory changes in which even the mathematics is rewritten, not just reinterpreted), whether underdetermination arguments set a constraint or merely express a preference, and whether a position is entitled to help itself to different commitments about observable versus unobservable structure. For each desideratum, mark it as non-negotiable or as a camp-specific preference dressed up as a demand, and note which existing positions it threatens — van Fraassen-style empiricism, entity realism, epistemic and ontic versions of structural realism, whatever else naturally falls out. The part I most want is the joint-satisfiability question: if some subset of these requirements cannot be met together, say so explicitly and explain which one has to give, because that is the point my friend and I will actually fight about. Do not pad this with literature surveys; argue the requirements on their merits.

Please give me the main analysis as a table with four columns: "Desideratum" (a short name plus one-sentence statement), "Motivating case" (the concrete example or argument that generates it — use the historical cases like Fresnel-to-Maxwell, phlogiston, and the aether rather than abstractions), "Status" (non-negotiable or camp preference, with a clause of justification), and "Threatens" (the positions it rules out or forces to mutate).
