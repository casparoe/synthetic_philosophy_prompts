I'm a digital hardware engineer, so my daily vocabulary is flip-flops, comparators, arbiters and clock domains. A puzzle I've run into in a philosophy of rationality reading group is Buridan's ass: the donkey placed exactly between two identical haystacks, with no reason favouring either, and the claim — usually tied to the principle of sufficient reason — that it must starve. I'd like you to restate this debate in the vocabulary I actually use, and I'm fluent enough in that vocabulary that you needn't condescend to it or oversimplify. Specifically, I have in mind metastability in flip-flops: when a setup or hold violation leaves a latch balanced between two stable states, the output can hover at an intermediate voltage for an unbounded time before thermal noise tips it to a 0 or a 1, with no bias in either direction; and also arbiter circuits, which must declare a winner between two simultaneous requests and can themselves go metastable, plus the standard engineering answers of dither (deliberately injected noise to prevent a system sticking near a decision boundary) and hysteresis in comparators (making the threshold for switching in one direction slightly different from the threshold for switching back).

So restate the philosophers' positions faithfully: the view that the ass is genuinely stuck and must perish, the view that indifference is itself a licence for arbitrary choice and nothing is lost, the view that rational choice requires a reason for preferring the chosen option specifically, and any view resting on the distinction between being indifferent between two options and facing two different options whose advantages cancel out. Then give me the residue report I care about. Which parts of the debate map cleanly onto metastability and its stochastic resolution, which parts only translate if you quietly distort them — for instance, is calling the flip-flop's eventual output a 'choice' or a 'decision' already smuggling in something the circuit doesn't do? — and which parts have no expression at all in my vocabulary? I want a considered view on whether the residue shows that engineering vocabulary is missing something the philosophers were tracking, or whether it instead shows that parts of the debate trade on confusions that a metastable latch quietly dissolves. Please answer in British English.
