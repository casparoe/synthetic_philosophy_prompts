I've been going through Dennett's stuff on the self, especially his line that a self is a "center of narrative gravity" -- his analogy is that a self is like the center of gravity of an object: there's no little pivot or bead sitting at that spot inside the thing, but the point is still perfectly real in the way physics needs it to be, and you can do serious prediction with it. From what I can tell he uses the analogy to do two jobs at once: reassure people who think dropping the "inner spectator" picture means the self doesn't exist, and also deflate the idea that selves are intrinsic private observer-like things (his whole war on the Cartesian theater). My friend says the analogy is carrying alot more weight than it can bear -- a center of gravity is uniquely defined for a given mass distribution and it can't read books about itself, whereas a self can get revised by the very theory of it that it reads, can arguably split (dissociative cases, or just the way your "self" differs at 20 vs 50), and seems answerable to norms about which life story it *should* be telling, and there's just no analogue to any of that in mechanics.
