I chair the pharmacy and therapeutics committee at a mid-sized regional hospital. We make sequential formulary decisions — adding, restricting, switching preferred agents within a class — monthly, in ninety-minute meetings, with no member trained in decision theory. I've come to suspect we can be led around in circles: last year we switched our preferred proton-pump inhibitor twice and ended up back where we started, minus the switching costs in retraining, protocol revisions, and a quarter of therapeutic confusion. A colleague who reads decision theory says we are money-pumpable and that the irrationality lives in the sequence of decisions, not in any single meeting, and he muttered something about whether rationality should guard individual choices or whole plans. I don't want the theory; I want a procedure the committee can actually run. Please write it: how we should log each decision and its stated reasons so that cycles surface on their own, tests to distinguish a genuine change in evidence or values from mere intransitive drift or status-quo bias, where the procedure should block a motion with 'we already answered this, and here is what it would take to reopen it,' and where it must hand the question back to the committee because it is a value judgment no procedure can settle. Say plainly which of your tests are just judgment dressed up as a rule, and say whether your procedure treats each meeting's choice as free-standing or binds us to plans, and why you chose that. Then run it on two cases: a manufacturer pushing us to switch to their agent on essentially the same cost-effectiveness trade we declined six months ago, and a real reopening problem — new comparative safety data favoring a drug we removed two years ago in a cost-driven switch, where reopening means admitting the earlier switch was a mistake that cost the hospital money. I want to see what the procedure does when the honest answer is to reopen a settled question against our face. Begin your response with the procedure itself, with no preamble and no restatement of this request.
