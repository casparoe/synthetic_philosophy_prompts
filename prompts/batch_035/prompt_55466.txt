my roommate is deep in his Searle phase (computation is observer-relative, the brain causes minds but doesn't "compute" them, AI hype is one big category error etc etc) and after our third 2am arguement about it I finally sat down and wrote out where I actually stand. I have a nagging feeling something in here doesn't fit together but I genuinely can't tell which part. here's the list:

1. the mind is what the brain computes. thinking, perceiving, remembering, all of it is computation, and that's why AI could in principle have minds and why an upload could in principle be a mind.
2. what matters is the computation, not the substrate. same computation, same mental states, whether it's running on neurons, silicon, or whatever else.
3. when people bring up the old gerrymandering result — that if you're allowed to carve up a system's physical states cleverly enough, basically any hunk of matter can be counted as implementing any finite state machine, so a rock runs every program — my reaction is that this just shows implementation has to be earned by the system's actual causal organization. the mapping has to track how the system really works. a wall doesn't run my mind no matter how fancy your state-assignment is, and I take this to show computation is an intrinsic feature of physical systems, like digestion, not something we project onto them.
4. at the same time, I don't believe you'd need molecule-level fidelity to upload a person. the mind lives at a level of description well above the chemistry, and an optimized reimplementation — different architecture, internal states that look nothing like neurons — could compute the same thing and host the same mind.
5. the embodied cognition people are onto something real too. minds like ours need bodies, sensorimotor loops, ongoing give and take with an environment. it's not all skull-bound symbol pushing. I keep half-agreeing with them and it's making me nervous about 1 and 2.
6. two test cases I've commited to: (a) if a person followed a giant rulebook, one step per day, taking a decade to do what my brain does in a second, the system as a whole would be implementing the same computation and would host the same mind — the follower has no idea what's going on, but the whole system does. (b) a giant lookup table wired up to match every behavioral input and output of a person is just a table. nobody's home. I feel pretty good about both of these verdicts.

so: which of these actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest way to fix whichever ones are broken? order the tensions by severity instead of flagging every pair, and tell me which single revision, if any, would clean up most of the mess. and if one of them is secretly the same problem as another, say so.
