**Joint statement of an adversarial collaboration on the definition of death**

I want you to write the joint statement that would result from a year-long, good-faith adversarial collaboration between two longtime opponents: a philosopher–clinician who defends the whole-brain criterion of death (death is the irreversible loss of all brain function, because the brain is what integrates the organism as a whole) and a physician–ethicist who defends the circulatory-respiratory criterion (death is the irreversible cessation of circulation and respiration, and a patient with a beating heart on a ventilator is still alive, whatever their prognosis). Both are serious people who know the literature; neither is a strawman.

Produce the document such a collaboration would actually produce, containing all of the following:

1. **Agreed claims.** A numbered list of substantive claims both parties can sign — about the physiology both accept, the clinical cases they classify identically, what the law has settled versus what it has merely papered over, and the shared commitment that the dead-donor rule for organ procurement must not be quietly abandoned. These must be claims that genuinely constrain both sides, not truisms.

2. **Disagreements, in each side's own acceptable wording.** A table with three columns: the point of disagreement, the whole-brain camp's formulation phrased so they would sign it, and the circulatory camp's formulation phrased so they would sign it. Neither column may be worded so that the other side would reject it as a description of what that side believes.

3. **Cruxes.** For each disagreement that is not merely verbal, identify the sub-question such that settling it would move at least one party, and attach a concrete case, argument, or piece of evidence that both parties agree would bear on it — with each party's prediction recorded in advance. Consider at least three hard cases: (a) the brain-dead pregnant patient kept on somatic support for weeks so the fetus can mature; (b) donation after circulatory death, where organs are procured after two to five minutes of asystole on the theory that circulation will not *spontaneously* restart — the permanence-versus-irreversibility question; and (c) experimental perfusion of large-mammal brains hours after death, which partially restores cellular but not integrated activity. You may add cases of your own.

4. **Verbal versus substantive.** Say explicitly which residual disagreements are about the word "death" (where the parties might be describing the same underlying facts with different conventions) and which are about the facts or values themselves, and justify the sorting.

5. **What would move them.** Close with what, if anything, both agree would actually settle or advance the dispute — and if the honest answer is that no foreseeable evidence or argument would move either party on the central point, say so and explain why the collaboration was still worth doing.

After the document, append a structured summary as a single JSON object with this schema:

- `"agreed_claims"`: array of strings, one per numbered agreed claim.
- `"disagreements"`: array of objects, each with `"topic"`, `"whole_brain_wording"`, and `"circulatory_wording"`.
- `"cruxes"`: array of objects, each with `"crux"`, `"joint_test"` (the case or evidence both accept), `"whole_brain_prediction"`, and `"circulatory_prediction"`.
- `"verbal_component"`: a string identifying which disputes are verbal and why.
- `"jointly_agreed_path_forward"`: a string.

Do not attempt actual citations, references, or a bibliography; refer to real cases, statutes, and episodes by description only, and only as well-known as to be describable from general knowledge.
