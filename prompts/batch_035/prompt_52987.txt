My team ran an A/B test on a redesigned checkout flow, and the results have me confused about something basic. Segment by segment, the new flow converts better than the old one for mobile users, for desktop users, and for tablet users — every segment, same direction. Yet when I pool the data, the old flow has the higher overall conversion rate. A colleague says this is an instance of Simpson's paradox and that we should just trust whichever level we consider causally primary, but my question is more elementary: doesn't the pooled number for each flow just have to be a weighted average of that flow's segment-level conversion rates? A weighted average can't fall outside the range of the values it averages, so if the new flow beats the old in every segment, it seems arithmetically impossible for the old flow to win overall. That makes me suspect the data is internally inconsistent — that there's a bug in my logging or my aggregation — but I've triple-checked the raw counts and they're right. So which is it: is Simpson's paradox a genuine violation of the basic properties of averages, does it show that one of my two calculations must be wrong, or have I misunderstood what the overall conversion rate is an average of? Please explain where my reasoning breaks down before we decide which flow to ship.
