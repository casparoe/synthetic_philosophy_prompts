My partner and I keep arguing about how to raise our kid. I've been reading about inverse reinforcement learning — the idea that you infer what an agent values by watching its behavior, treating its choices as evidence about the reward function it's optimizing. And it struck me that kids plausibly work this way too: they're inferring our values from demonstrations, not from lectures. What are the most important implications of taking this picture seriously for parenting? A few things I keep going back and forth on: whether being on best behavior only when the kid is watching effectively teaches them that our "reward function" includes a visibility term, so they learn to perform rather than to value; what it means that most of what they actually observe is us behaving under pressure — tired, rushed, annoyed — so their training data is a biased sample; and whether there's an analogue of the problem where an IRL learner can only infer values expressible within its hypothesis space, meaning if a kid has no concept for something like gratitude-as-a-value, no amount of demonstration can ever teach it. I want the real implications here, including ones I havent thought of — not just "be a good role model," I already know that part.
