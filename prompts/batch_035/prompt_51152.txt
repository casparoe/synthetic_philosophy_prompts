At a barbecue a couple weeks ago, two of my friends got into a long argument over a game the host was running, and I've been turning it over ever since. The game: two big jars of marbles. Jar A had exactly 50 red and 50 black. Jar B had 100 marbles, red and black in some proportion nobody knew — the host filled it and sealed it a year ago, and the seal only got broken when a marble was drawn. Each round you pick a jar and call a color; if your color comes out of your jar, the host pays you $100, otherwise nothing. Everyone put in $10 a round to play.

My friend Sam bet on Jar A every single round, whichever color he called. Dana rode him about it all afternoon. It went roughly like this:

(1) Dana: "You keep picking A over B. When you called red, you took A-red over B-red — so you must think B has fewer than 50 reds. And just now you called black and still took A, so you also think B has fewer than 50 blacks. That's more than a hundred marbles missing from a jar that only holds a hundred. Your choices don't add up."

(2) Sam: "I never said anything about how many reds are in B. I said I don't know. With A, I know exactly what I'm getting. With B, I don't, and I'd rather have known odds than unknown ones. That's a preference, not a math error — I'm not claiming B is short on red, I'm claiming it might be short on anything."

(3) Dana: "'I don't know' isn't some fourth thing sitting above all the probabilities. If you truly have no view on the mix, then for choosing purposes that has to count as 50/50, same as A — otherwise you're just preferring to feel certain, and a feeling isn't odds. And notice your rule proves too much: if 'unknown' loses to every known chance, you'd of turned down B even at much better payouts, and you'd be indifferent between the sealed jar and a jar some guy openly rigged against you, since both count as 'unknown.' Those obviously aren't the same situation."

(4) Sam: "The rigged jar and the sealed jar aren't the same to me, so the problem is in your bookkeeping, not mine. Look, you buy name-brand ibuprofen over the store brand even though the label says they're chemically the same — you told me yourself it's because with the brand you know what you're getting. Same instinct. And for me it's not really about the jar at all. My caution is about who could have arranged the jar. On paper B is 50/50; the paper doesn't know who filled it."

(5) Dana: "Then your actual claim is 'I don't trust the host,' which is a belief, and beliefs take numbers too. If you're 80% sure he filled it fairly, B is mostly a fair coin and clearly worth betting at these payouts. And if you think he'd cheat you at marbles, I'd love to hear why you ate his potato salad."

Sam never got a word in after that because the burgers were ready, and honestly I couldn't pick a winner myself. Can you take the five moves in order and say for each one whether it's right, half-right, or flat wrong, with your reasons? And tell me if there's a move neither of them ever makes that would get them off the treadmill.
