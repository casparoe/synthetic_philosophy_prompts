I'm a retired plant pathologist, seventy-one, and since I stopped going into the lab I've gotten way more into philosophy than is probably dignified -- mostly ethics, and lately the whole question of what you're supposed to DO when you're not sure which moral theory is true. My daughter-in-law Priya has thought about this way more than me and we argue about it whenever she visits. Her overall picture: she gives maybe 60% credence to a fairly standard "help the global poor now" view, maybe 25-30% to a view where animal suffering matters a great deal, and a smaller slice to a longtermist view that says what really matters is keeping the far future from going permanently wrong (value lock-in, permanent totalitarianism, that sort of thing).

Her track record so far. With money, she splits: her donations go roughly by credence, mostly to malaria-type charities with a slice for animal welfare, and when I pressed her on why not put it all on whichever view she thinks has the bigger stakes, she said something like splitting is how she respects views she can't decide between. But she also stopped eating factory-farmed meat years ago even though her credence there was maybe 30% -- her reasoning was that the cost to her is basically just tastiness and habit, so even a minority credence tips the scale. And one more data point: when her sister asked whether to volunteer her one free weekend a month at a food bank (the poverty view's pick) or at a prairie restoration site (a biodiversity view Priya only weakly holds), Priya told her to just go with whichever cause she herself was most confident actually mattered, don't overthink it.

Now the case that's got me stumped. Priya's nephew is graduating and has exactly one gap year before med school, indivisible, one shot. Option A: a year doing direct clinical-adjacent poverty work abroad, which her dominant 60% view clearly favors. Option B: an unpaid internship at an outfit working on preventing permanent value lock-in (AI governance stuff), which only her maybe-15% longtermist view favors, but that view says the stakes are astronomically larger than a year of poverty work. She'll be asked for her recommendation over the holidays. What will she say? Partly I want the prediction, but honestly I'm more interested in what rule she's actually been running all along -- expected-choiceworthiness with cross-theory comparisons, credence-weighted hedging with a cheap-precaution overlay, or "go with your highest-credence theory when the choice can't be split" -- and whether her old verdicts even pin it down, since I can see all three sort of fitting what she's done. Also flag where you think your read is weakest: what would Priya have to say over dinner that would flip your prediction, and is there a hypothetical case you'd want to spring on her to cleanly seperate the candidate rules before we trust any forecast? Please don't just list the theories at me, actually make the call.
