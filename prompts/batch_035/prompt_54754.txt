Write a joke about dialetheism, the view that some contradictions are true — paradigmatically, sentences like the liar. The joke needs to actually land with people who know the view, so the punchline should turn on its real content rather than on the generic idea that "contradiction" means chaos or sloppy thinking. In particular, it should engage with paraconsistency: dialetheists typically reject the principle of explosion (ex falso quodlibet), the classical rule that from a contradiction anything and everything follows. For them, a true contradiction is quarantined — it doesn't trivialize the whole theory. So a joke whose punchline is just "then everything is true and nothing you say means anything!" would misrepresent the position, roughly the way a joke about moral realism that treats it as "whatever you feel is right" misrepresents moral realism. A joke where the contradiction is carefully contained when it shouldn't be, or where triviality conspicuously fails to ensue, is on target. Wordplay on self-reference is welcome if it's doing genuine work, not just decoration.

Some context: I'm putting together a humor slide for the end of a graduate seminar session on non-classical logics, and the students have sat through weeks of proof systems, so they'll punish anything that feels like a "philosophy, huh, weird stuff" gag rather than an insider one. Length and format are up to you — a short dialogue, a limerick, a one-liner, whatever serves the punchline. If you can, give me one main joke, and if you have a genuinely distinct second one that works a different part of the view (say, the dialetheist's problem of saying what a sentence is not, given that some sentences both are and aren't true), add it too.
