A friend and I got into a long argument at dinner about why human extinction would actually be bad, and it's been bugging me since. I took what I assumed was the boring, obviously right line: extinction is bad because of all the individual deaths, and that's the whole story -- beyond those deaths, who exactly is left to be worse off? Nobody, so what would the "extra" bad even attach to? My friend thought this was too flat, and her whole position rests on an analogy she kept drawing: humanity is like a single person. It has a birth, a childhood, its own projects and an open future; it's mid-story. So extinction isn't just billions of separate deaths, it's an additional death on top of all of those -- the death of the one thing that was writing the story. She compared it to the difference between every author in some shared novel dying, versus the half-written manuscript being destroyed forever. The second, she says, is a loss the first doesn't capture, and humanity is on the side of the manuscript.

I half-remembered something from a while back that she now claims is on her side: a philosopher named Scheffler (I think?) who argues that what we value in our own lives right now depends on assuming that humanity continues after we die -- that there'll be other people carrying things on, a kind of collective afterlife. But when I think about it, that sounds like a claim about our psychology *now*, about what makes our present activities feel worth doing, not about some extra bad located at the moment extinction happens. So I don't know if it's evidence for her analogy or a different point entirely that's wearing the same clothes.

Can you audit the analogy properly? Give me the correspondence -- what plays the role of the person, the person's death, their unrealized future, and whether the analogy is even entitled to import things like an open future or projects at the species level -- then where it breaks, and, most importantly, which of the breaks matter for the conclusion she wants out of it (that extinction is bad *beyond* the deaths it involves) and which are just pedantry. Also tell me if my "no one left to be harmed" reply does any real work or whether it's smuggling in its own assumption, and whether the Scheffler thing, whatever its exact status, ends up supporting her or me. If the person-analogy fails, is there a better one, or is that a sign the extra bad she's gesturing at needs to be stated some other way entirely?
