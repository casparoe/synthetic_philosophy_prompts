My friend and I had one of those arguments on Friday that lasts four hours and by the end you're arguing about what the argument was about. It started political but it ended up being about how comparisons work. She said something like "Trump was the Republican Obama", and I noticed the comparison doesn't work the way I assumed — nobody hears it as a claim that the two men match up point for point. "Obama" in that sentence quietly stops meaning "a democratic senator from illinois" and slides into meaning "the outsider who realigns the party around himself and makes the old guard obsolete", and everyone in the room just lets the concept slide. And the people who object always object by freezing it back to the literal meaning — "Obama was young, Trump was old, that's a terrible analogy" — and it never lands, because everyone felt that the comparison was doing its work at the other level. Then we started seeing the same thing everywhere for the rest of the night: "the iPhone of cars", "the Beatles of cricket" (my friend is indian, don't ask), the startup pitch pattern where every product is "the Uber of X". In each case one concept gets stretched along a certain track — usually its position in a sequence, its bigness, or its role in a story — instead of being copied over whole.

Then she pulled up a letter puzzle to test me, and it sort of blew the whole thing open. If "abc" changes to "abd", what does "xyz" change to? I said "xya", wrapping the alphabet, and she said most people say that but she'd argue for something like "wyz", on the grounds that in the original pair what really got changed was "the thing that comes right after the run's start", so if you can't go right you go left. And I realized the reason I liked "xya" is the exact same reason I liked her Obama comparison and she hated both, and the reason she liked "wyz" is the same reason she loves analogies that everyone else calls a reach. Whether you allow the sliding seems to be a whole personality trait, and the sliding itself seems to follow tracks — number, order, size, how central the concept is to its category — but I can't tell if that's real or me pattern-matching on three examples.

So my question: does this thing have a name? I know Lakoff wrote about metaphors in everyday language, but this doesn't feel like the same layer — "time is money" is a fixed metaphor you reuse, this is one-off stretching invented on the spot that could slide a different way tomorrow. And I know analogies are a whole field, but what I want is the specific mechanism: when a concept is being carried across from one thing to another, which of its properties go along, which stay behind, and what decides. If there's no single established term, tell me that honestly, and then give me your own name and definition, plus where the edges are — like, is "the Uber of dog walkers" doing the same sliding as the Obama one, or is that just a template with a blank? I want the actual carving of the phenomenon, not just a word for it. Also if it's known whether some slides feel "deep" and others feel cheap, and why, I'd take that too.
