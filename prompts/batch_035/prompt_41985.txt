There's an argument I keep running into in talks about complexity and AI, and I want to know whether it's doing real work or is just a cool picture people like. The claim is that an intelligent agent is basically a Maxwell's demon: the demon sits at a little door between two boxes of gas and only lets the fast molecules pass one way, so it sorts hot from cold without apparently doing any work, and builds up a temperature difference you could run an engine off of. And the punchline people want from this is that intelligence is the same move — a bacterium running chemotaxis, a trader, and lately everyone says it about language models too: you use information about the world to extract order from noise, and that order is a resource. Someone recently gave me an even more specific version for LLMs: training is the demon doing its sorting (the model distills a low-entropy predictive grip on a noisy corpus), and the heat dumped into the datacenter cooling systems during the run is where the entropy bill gets paid, because Landauer showed memory erasure costs kT ln 2 per bit and there's no free lunch. I liked that version alot, but I honestly can't tell if I liked it because it was true or because it sounded true.

So audit the analogy for me. What actually plays the role of the door, the demon's decision, the demon's memory, the one-bit erasure event? Is mapping the training run onto the demon's memory reset even coherent, or is it loose in a way that matters — does anything about language modeling correspond to the demon having to *forget*, or is the trained model actually more like the demon's memory than the demon itself? Then tell me where the mapping breaks down and which of the breaks matter for the conclusion people want (roughly: intelligence is an entropy-extraction device and the bill is always paid somewhere else, in heat) versus which breaks are just nitpicking. And tell me what the analogy smuggles in — the demon's setup presupposes a pre-existing equilibrium and an observer who isn't part of the system being sorted, and I have a feeling that's load-bearing somehow, but I can't articulate why.
