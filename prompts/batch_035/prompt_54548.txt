I retired two years ago after a career in gravitational physics, most of it spent on the initial-value problem in general relativity, and I have spent the time since reading philosophy of time with the seriousness of a convert. I now want to write up a project of my own, and I would like your help drafting the proposal before I approach a small foundation that funds work at the border of physics and philosophy.

Here is my half-formed idea, and I will try to state it as a scientist would. The recurring complaint that relativity refutes presentism seems to me to run together three claims that a working relativist would want kept separate. Claim A: there is no frame-invariant simultaneity relation — this is, near enough, a theorem of special relativity. Claim B: there is no physically privileged foliation of the spacetime manifold into space-like slices — this is not a theorem but a messier question about what counts as a gauge artifact, and in general relativity it is complicated by the fact that our standard formulation of the field equations, the Cauchy problem, requires us to choose a foliation and then argue that nothing physical depends on the choice. Claim C: only events on some one slice exist — which, as far as I can tell, is not a question physics even has the vocabulary to ask. My suspicion is that the standard incompatibility argument gets its force by sliding from A to C through an unexamined B, and that if that slide is blocked, presentism and relativity turn out to be merely unacquainted rather than enemies. But it may equally be that the slide is perfectly legitimate, that B is not a distinct claim at all, and that my years of telling students that foliations are "just coordinates" have made me mistake a modeling convenience for a metaphysical discovery. That possibility is precisely what the proposal should subject to a fair test rather than a confirmation.

Please turn this into a proposal for a six-month fellowship, roughly two pages in length. It should contain: a precise statement of the research question, framed so that it has a determinate answer; an explanation of why the existing positions leave it open — I gather presentists have defended the idea of a privileged frame and eternalists have pressed the argument from relativistic simultaneity, but I want the proposal to show why distinguishing A, B, and C is not just one more move in that game; the method, month by month, of what I would actually do (I can do the physics myself; what I need is a workable plan for the philosophical work, since I have no illusions about which half of this project is harder for me); the possible conclusions and what considerations would decide among them; and an honest statement of what would count as the project failing.

If you think the framing above rests on a confusion, or that the project as I have posed it simply cannot be done well, I want you to say so at the top of your response rather than politely building the proposal I asked for.
