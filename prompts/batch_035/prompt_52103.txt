I have been invited to present at a formal epistemology workshop a paper on the Judy Benjamin problem, and I want help preparing for the discussion period. For orientation: a radio operator, Judy Benjamin, is dropped at night into a region divided evenly between Red-held and Blue-held territory, with the Blue half further divided into a headquarters sector and a non-headquarters sector; her prior over her location is uniform. Her only new information is a radio message telling her that if she is in Blue territory, she is three times as likely to be in the headquarters sector as in the rest of it. The message is silent about Red territory and fixes only a ratio within Blue — yet it somehow feels like evidence about her location overall.

My thesis is that the case has no unique solution. Rationality requires of Judy only that her posterior satisfy the constraint the message actually encodes — the conditional ratio between the two Blue sectors — which leaves her credence in Red territory unconstrained. Her post-message state should therefore be represented by a set of credence functions rather than a point, and the familiar point answers on offer — keeping her Red credence fixed, minimizing relative information to the prior in van Fraassen's original treatment of the case, conditioning on a Stalnaker conditional via imaging — each smuggle in a tie-breaking principle the message never supplied (and the information-minimization answer, in particular, has struck many critics as counterintuitive in the value it hands Red).

The audience: a dozen or so strict Bayesians, for whom conditionalization on a proposition is constitutive of rational credence and who will hear "no unique answer" as a confession of confusion; two philosophical logicians from the Stalnaker–Adams tradition, convinced the puzzle is an artifact of ignoring the semantics of conditionals — identify the right proposition to condition on, they think, and a unique posterior falls out; and one Bayesian statistician who regards minimum relative information, i.e., KL projection onto the constraint set, as the obviously correct update and will say so in exactly those words.

Draft my prep sheet: the objections I will actually face from this room, ordered by how much damage each would do, phrased the way its asker would phrase it, each with a reply I can deliver in two minutes and an honest note on where the reply is thin. Make sure to cover the Bayesians' charge that a set of posteriors leaves Judy unable to act or bet; the logicians' demand that I state exactly which proposition she learned, if not a conditional; and the statistician's line that my underdetermination claim amounts to refusing a principled, prior-privileged update rule. Close with the question I most hope nobody asks, and what to say if it is asked anyway.
