Daniel Dennett and Gregg Caruso debated free will and punishment for years, culminating in their co-authored book Just Deserts: Debating Free Will (Polity, 2021): Dennett defends a compatibilism on which people can deserve blame in an ordinary, non-ultimate sense, while Caruso denies that anyone ever deserves punishment in the basic desert sense and proposes reconceiving criminal justice along public-health lines, with quarantine-style incapacitation but no retribution. Write the joint statement the two could sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. I want the claims both can accept — about the irrelevance of contra-causal free will, about which practical reforms follow either way, and about where their remaining disagreement actually does work. Then I want the precise formulations of their disagreement, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording. Finally, set out the cruxes with, for each, a concrete case both would agree bears on it and each side's prediction recorded in advance. Two candidates you should treat explicitly: whether Dennett's everyday sense of "desert" does any justificatory work once contra-causal desert is abandoned, or whether their disagreement about desert dissolves into a verbal one; and how each view handles two offenders with identical predicted recidivism but different histories of self-correction — the case where I'd expect everyday desert to keep punishing differentially and the public-health view to have no grounds to. If you conclude that some portion of their dispute is merely terminological, say which portion and why, and be clear about what remains a substantive disagreement that no reformulation could dissolve. Format: a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
