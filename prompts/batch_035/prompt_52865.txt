I've been going back and forth with a friend about whether we should care, morally, about insects — like actually factor them into decisions, not just "aw, poor bug" caring. She thinks the probability any given insect is sentient is so tiny (she says like 0.1% for something like a fly having any kind of felt experience at all) that it's basically noise, and that people who take insect welfare seriously are letting warm feelings about tiny faces override their epistemics. My position is kind of the opposite: even at her number, there are something like 10^18 to 10^19 insects alive at any moment, so a 0.1% credence per insect still shakes out to an astronomically large number of possibly-suffering individuals, and expected-value reasoning says we can't just round that to zero. But I confess I don't actually know how to think about the combination cleanly — whether it's just per-insect credence times count, or whether the credence itself should be correlated across all insects (if one fly is sentient, probably they all are, so it's not really 10^18 independent coin flips but one big coin flip about the whole taxon).

So: give me your actual credence that at least some insects are sentient in a morally relevant sense — a number, not a hedge — and decompose how you got there. I'd want at least these terms broken out: the probability that phenomenal consciousness requires something insects lack entirely (integrated information, recurrent processing, whatever your favorite necessary condition is), the probability that some form of valenced experience exists but in a weakened or alien sense that might matter less morally, and whether you think moral weight scales with anything like neuron count or behavioral complexity or doesn't scale at all. Then tell me how you handle the multiplication side — does the scale argument actually work under moral uncertainty, or does it get absorbed by the possibility that insect experience, if real, carries near-zero moral weight per individual? And which sub-question, if resolved, would move your final answer the most?

One more thing: my friend's at roughly 0.001 that flies feel anything, and I'd put myself around 0.35, and we can't figure out whether our disagreement is really about the neuroscience or about something sneakier, like whether "morally relevant" and "sentient" come apart for creatures with lifespans of weeks and no long-term plans. Where do you think the disagreement between someone at 0.001 and someone at 0.35 most likely lives? I want to know where to aim the next argument.
