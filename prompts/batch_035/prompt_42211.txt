I'm taking a grad seminar on epistemic justification and we've now spent six weeks on the regress problem, and honestly I've come out of it thinking the debate has been running in place for a very long time. Agrippa's trilemma is ancient, the modern foundationalism/coherentism exchange (BonJour's old attacks on coherentism, Sellars' myth of the given used against foundationalism, Alston saying the regress is just a level-confusion and that once you see that, nobody actually needs to answer it) is decades old, and then Klein shows up with infinitism, which everyone finds fun but nobody converts to. Every move is well known, every reply is well known, and in seminar nobody budges an inch. My working theory, presented as a theory: the three camps aren't actually disagreeing about one thing. The foundationalists and coherentists are answering different questions -- roughly, what makes a belief justified for a subject at a time versus what makes a belief system as a whole worthy of credence -- and the infinitist is really arguing that neither question is well-posed without a third one about the structure of the justificatory relation itself. But I'm not sure that's right, and I can't tell whether the "humanly attainable" issue (infinite chains of reasons being psychologically impossible, and Klein's reply that actual possession of reasons isn't required) is a genuine knockdown point or a red herring that keeps the debate alive by giving everyone something to talk about.

Please act as a professional epistemologist and diagnose this for me. Is the disagreement substantive, verbal, or a case of different questions under one heading? What does each side actually say that supports your diagnosis? Where exactly does the impasse come from -- hidden disagreement about whether justification must be accessible to the subject, about whether "self-warrant" or "coherence" are even intelligible kinds of warrant, about whether epistemology owes psychology anything? And given your diagnosis, tell me honestly whether anything would count as progress here: a distinction, a formal model, some result about inferential structure, a change in the question -- or is this one of those debates that persists because the Agrippan setup itself smuggles in an assumption all three camps accept (e.g. that justification is a linear, all-or-nothing relation between beliefs) that a skeptic could exploit either way? I want the diagnosis with reasons, not a neutral survey of the positions, and if you think my working theory above is wrong or only half right, say so and say why. Please think through the structure of the disagreement carefully before you answer rather than just recapping the standard arguments for each view.
