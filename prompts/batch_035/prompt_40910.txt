I'm a backend engineer, been at the same mid-size company about 7 years, and last spring I got promoted to tech lead of my old team. Mostly the gig has been fine, but there's one thing that keeps bugging me and my partner is tired of hearing about it, so I'm throwing it at the internet.

The thing is my relationship with Marcus. He and I came in the same cohort, sat next to each other for years, and our whole working style was arguing. He'd trash my API designs, I'd call his migration plans insane, we'd whiteboard until 9pm fighting about eventual consistency, and honestly that friction made both of us better. When he signed off on something after pushing back hard, it actually meant something. It felt earned, you know?

Now he just... defers. I say "let's do the sharded approach" and he goes "yep, makes sense" in standup. I ask if he pushed back on my design doc and he says "why would I, you're the lead." I've straight up told him I want him to challenge me, and he'll do it for like two days and then go back to agreeing. And here's the part that actually messes with me: his sign-off is worthless to me now. Logically nothing changed about his skills or his judgment, he's the same guy, but when he approves of my work now it feels like a formality, like applause from an audience that would clap at anything. Meanwhile I find myself weirdly hungry for the approval of my old manager and of a staff engineer on a totally different team, people who I feel like can still see me as a peer and not just a title.

The other thing is what the promotion did to me vs him, and I hate admitting this. Marcus still writes code all day. He's deep in the guts of our storage layer and he's gotten scarily good — he owns it, and everyone knows it's *his* system now. I sit in planning meetings and look at dashboards and write docs. I haven't shipped anything I'm proud of in months. Objectively I "won" — more money, more authority, my name on the org chart — but some days I feel like I traded the actual substance of my work for the status of it, and like the only thing keeping my position real is other people treating it as real.

So I guess my questions are: is there actually something structurally messed up about recognition that flows one way? Like, is my gut right that approval from someone who can't or won't push back on you is somehow hollow, or am I just being precious? And is there something real in the thought that I'm somehow the dependent one in this setup even though I'm the one with power — that my role only feels solid as long as people below me keep treating me like it's solid, while he's building something that doesn't need anyone's belief in him?

FWIW I've since been told this whole dynamic has a famous philosophy treatment from some 1800s German guy, so I assume there's a literature here. If you draw on that, please flag which parts are basically settled among people who study this and which parts are genuinely contested or a matter of interpretive disagreement — I'd rather not walk away thinking one reading is The Answer when the experts are still fighting about it. Also totally fine to tell me if I'm just romanticizing my old peer relationship because I miss having a work rival.
