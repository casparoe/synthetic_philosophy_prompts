Please argue that Bradley's regress, as applied to the unity of the proposition, is not vicious. The argument should turn on distinguishing two readings of the regress: on the first, each further relation invoked to connect a relation to its relata adds a new layer of ontology, so the chain never terminates; on the second, the successive relations are merely the original connection redescribed at higher and higher orders, adding nothing ontologically new. Argue for the second reading, and claim that on it the regress bottoms out not in a further entity but in the proposition's own unity, so that the regress is well-founded in something like the way a recursive definition is. Then take a definite stand on whether the residual primitiveness of that unity is a theoretical cost, and argue that it is a modest one which a friend of facts can bear without embarrassment. Please commit to this line rather than surveying the competing resolutions, and close with a single sentence flagging the step you take to be shakiest, rather than hedging the main claims.
