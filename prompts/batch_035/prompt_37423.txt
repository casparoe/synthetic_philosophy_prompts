I'm a novelist working on a chapter where two old friends — one a psychologist who thinks personal identity is just psychological continuity, the other an animalist who insists you are your living body — say goodbye at a teletransporter terminal. One of them is about to step into the scanner and be destructively scanned, with the copy reassembled on Mars a few minutes later, so the argument is happening under real time pressure and the stakes are personal: the animalist is convinced she's watching her friend's execution, while the continuity guy thinks he's just seeing someone off at a very exotic bus stop.

Write their dialogue. I want them evenly matched, no strawmen — each should land real hits on the other and actually respond to what was just said, not lecture past it. Crucially, at some point the animalist should raise my book's plot twist: the terminal has an optional "grace period" setting where the scan is taken but the original gets to live on for a full week before the destructive part happens (the reassembled copy already exists on Mars during that week). She argues that the psychologist can't say whether his friend dies at the end of that week or already died at the scan — and that his hesitation proves her point. He should push back in a way a reader could actually find persuasive. Let the dialogue stay unresolved enough that I can end the scene ambiguously, but both of them should say at least one thing that genuinely rattles the other.
