been in a long argument with a friend (very MIRI-adjacent, thinks best-system chance is the only account of chance worth anything) and he claims he's shown that calibrating your credences to objective chance is self-defeating. his setup: imagine a world where a quantum coin gets flipped exactly 100 times, ever, and then the universe ends. the argument:

(1) PP: conditional on the objective chance of heads being p, and on any admissible evidence, your credence in heads should be p.
(2) information about how *other* flips turned out is admissible — it's just more observed history, it doesn't tell you anything about the mechanism, it's not "insider info" about this flip.
(3) but on the best-system view the chances are just a summary of the total pattern of outcomes, so in a small world like this one the actual outcomes partly fix the chance: after watching 99 heads in a row, the best-system chance of heads on flip 100 is nowhere near 1/2.
(4) so by (1) and (2), my credence in heads on the last flip should climb as the heads come in, exactly as if the coin had a memory.
(5) but the flips are quantum-independent — no common cause, no mechanism, nothing — so a rational agent shouldn't move at all.
(6) therefore either PP is false or best-system "chances" aren't real chances, and my friend says: either way, "defer to the objective chance" turns out not to be a rule you can actually follow, which is the conclusion he wanted all along.

here's why i suspect he's proven too much. if (2) is false, then plain observation of independent trials counts as inadmissible evidence — but watching other rolls is literally how we learn that a die is loaded, so his argument would make ordinary statistical inference irrational, not just fancy metaphysical deference. that can't be right. but if (2) stands then (5) has to go, and we get calibrated agents treating independent trials as correlated, which sounds even worse.

which step do i reject, and what does rejecting it cost me? fwiw i want to keep (1) — calibration against a proper scoring rule is the whole reason i care about any of this — and i'd genuinely like to keep (3) as well, since best-system chance is the only story about chance that makes sense to me without spooky propensities. i feel like "admissible" is doing something sneaky between (1) and (2) but i can't put my finger on it. price out the alternatives for me.
