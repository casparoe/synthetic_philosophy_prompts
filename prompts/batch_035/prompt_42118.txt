me and two friends have been arguing for weeks about the star trek transporter thing, except we've gone way past the standard version and now nobody's speaking to each other. the basic setup in case it matters: machine scans you, destroys the body here, builds an exact copy of you on the planet below. my friend marco says obviously you die and that's just a corpse plus a new guy with your memories, like a really advanced photo. my other friend ana says obviously you don't die, the copy wakes up with all your memories and plans and loves your wife exactly the same, and nobody — not the copy, not the wife — can tell any difference, so what is "dying" even supposed to mean here. die from whose point of view?

here's where it gets messy and why im writing to you. i tried to invent variants to break the tie. variant 1: the machine breaks after scanning but before vaporizing you, so now there's you on the ship AND a copy on the planet, both completely sure they're the real one. marco says this proves the copy was never you to begin with — if there can be two, then neither one is you. but that feels like it proves too much, because in the normal case the scan-and-vaporize happens in one quick zap and ana says nobody's dying then, so how does it become death just because the original stuck around a bit longer? variant 2: forget the zap, the machine replaces your neurons one at a time with functionally identical synthetic ones over the course of a year, and you stay awake the whole time chatting with the nurse. intuitively i'd say i survive that, even though at the end my whole brain is new stuff — but then why does the identical end state feel like death when it happens in five seconds instead of a year? is the speed somehow what matters?? that seems insane but i cannot shake it.

so anyway, write me an essay that actually takes a side, not one of those "well it depends what you mean by you" cop-outs that end the argument without ending it. the question as i understand it: does the thing that matters in survival ride on the mechanism (gradual, continuous, one-track, no branching) or on the pattern (memories, personality, all of that)? if you say the pattern, you have to tell me why the two-survivors case doesn't bother you — would you step into marco's broken machine knowing there'd be two of you, and which one goes home to your wife? and if you say the mechanism, you have to tell me why a year-long replacement is survival but a five-second one is murder, when the end result is the same brain. pick one and defend it all the way down, and feel free to use my variants or sharpen them or invent better ones if mine aren't clean enough.
