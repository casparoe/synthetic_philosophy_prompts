I'm taking an aesthetics course this semester and we just finished Hume's standard of taste, and I've basically talked myself into the Humean view: beauty isn't just whatever you happen to like, because if you gather the right judges -- people with practiced sensibility, wide comparison, no prejudice, in a proper frame of mind -- their verdicts converge enough that some works really are better than others, and that convergence is what objectivity amounts to here. Not observer-independent like mass, but real enough to rank Pope above his imitators.

My roommate thinks this is circular and unfalsifiable: you pick out the good judges partly by the fact that they rate the right things highly, then use their ratings to fix what counts as good, so nothing could ever disconfirm it. He also says the qualifications (practiced, unprejudiced, etc.) were reverse-engineered from the canon Hume already accepted. I want to stress-test the view properly instead of just waving off the circularity charge, since I suspect some versions of it hit harder than others.

So: what would count against it? Give me the defeaters ranked from fatal to merely awkward. Things I'm wondering about:

- what would have to be empirically true for it to fail -- like, if the verdicts of qualified judges never converged across cultures even after full exposure to each other's traditions, or if whatever convergence we find is explained entirely by familiarity and status effects (a judge liking the famous poem *because* it's the one used to train judges). I've been daydreaming about a blind version of the test: strip works of author, era, and prestige and see if the supposed delicacy of imagination still tracks anything. What result there would hurt, and what would the Humean have to say about it?
- the case of a single deviant judge: someone who meets every one of Hume's qualifications but prefers the work every other true judge finds bad. Does the view have to call them wrong, and can it, without just adding "and agrees with the majority" as a hidden qualification? Is that hidden qualification a fix or a capitulation?
- what it stands or falls with from neighboring areas: whether it needs convergence to be more than accidental (evolutionary debunking of aesthetic preferences seems relevant here), whether it commits you to a weird response-dependent property, how it interacts with the plain fact of stubborn disagreement among smart people
- which of these a committed Humean would actually concede as counting against the view vs. dismiss as missing the point, and whether the dismissals are legit or whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability -- and if so, whether that's even a problem for a claim of this kind (my prof hinted it might not be, since maybe the view is closer to a proposal about what we *mean* by good than an empirical hypothesis?)
- what the view predicts that plain subjectivism and relativism about taste don't, so I can tell apart evidence that supports Hume from evidence compatible with everything

And if you can untangle whether the circularity is vicious or virtuous, that'd help -- my roommate is certain it's fatal, I'm less sure.

End with a single line in the format \verdict{X} where X is exactly one of: fatally-exposed, seriously-exposed, awkward-but-survivable, well-insulated-and-better-for-it, well-insulated-and-worse-for-it -- your honest overall call on how much risk the Humean standard actually carries.
