In arguments about prison reform I keep running into the "debt to society" metaphor: the offense creates an entry on some moral ledger, prison time is how you pay it down, and once you've paid you're square. The weird thing is I've heard it used on both sides. My neighbor leans on it to defend Norwegian-style prisons like Halden — if the point is just to settle the debt, you'd want the cheapest and least destructive way of doing it, so humane conditions make perfect sense. My uncle uses the exact same metaphor to argue the opposite: a debt you can't feel isn't really paid, so American prisons being unpleasant is the whole point. Audit the metaphor for me: what maps onto what (creditor, debtor, principal, interest, default), where the mapping breaks down, what each side is quietly smuggling in about what "payment" amounts to — suffering? elapsed time? reformed behavior? — and whether either conclusion actually follows from the metaphor, or whether they're both just reading their prior views into it.
