Ok so I just got voluntold onto the bylaws commitee of my food co-op, and we've walked straight into a knot I can't stop thinking about. Here's the setup: the bylaws say the general membership can amend the bylaws by a two-thirds vote at a meeting with quorum. But the bylaws also say the board interprets the bylaws -- and the bylaws are what creates the board in the first place. And right now someone has proposed an amendment that would lower the amendment threshold itself to a simple majority, and they want to pass it by simple majority, while the old guard insists that an amendment to the amending rules has to clear the old two-thirds bar. I've been reading Hofstadter on the side (tangled hierarchies, Escher's Drawing Hands, that stuff) and it really is that picture: the level that makes the rules is made by the rules it makes. So what I want to know is what this whole way of running an organization presupposes. Does it only make sense if there's some inviolate level -- the members standing outside the bylaws in a kind of constituting capacity -- whose job is to break the loop? Is that even coherent, since the membership is just the same people who are governed by the bylaws? Or could you run it fine with no fixed level at all, and if we went that way, what would concretely have to change about who decides the threshold fight and on what authority? And honestly I'd also like to know whether the two factions in our fight (old rules govern their own replacement vs. the new rules get to govern their own adoption) are actually disagreeing about something deep, or just each grabbing whichever rule happens to give their side the win.
