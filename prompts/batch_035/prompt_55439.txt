I teach a graduate seminar in formal epistemology, and this term, after several weeks of Bayesian orthodoxy, we arrived at imprecise probabilities. My students seized on the phrase 'false precision' at once. Ever since, whenever a result favoring sharp credences came up — the Dutch book theorems, the accuracy-dominance arguments for probabilism — someone has dismissed it with the remark that assigning a single number like 0.62 to a hard question on thin evidence is false precision, and that the correct formal object is a set of probabilities, or an interval, or something in that family. I want to push back, but first I want to know exactly what the slogan commits them to, because I suspect it is doing several jobs at once, and only some of those jobs would support the use my students are putting it to.

Please give me a menu of the distinct, precise theses 'false precision' might be expressing in this context. The candidates I can see include: a representational thesis about what an epistemic state actually is; a normative thesis about what credences one ought to have under sparse evidence; a thesis about elicitation — that betting quotients and scoring rules cannot in principle recover a unique sharp value; a merely psychological thesis about the limitations of actual humans, irrelevant to ideally rational agents; and perhaps a semantic thesis about probability talk itself. For each reading, tell me whether it is trivial, substantive but defensible, or false; whether it would actually defeat some specific argument for sharp credences or merely relocate the dispute; and, crucially, whether it survives the rejoinders that friends of sharp credences press — that a set of probabilities itself has perfectly sharp boundaries, and that on some evidence imprecise credences barely update at all while a sharp agent has long since learned from it. My own suspicion is that the slogan is really a claim about evidence — that thin evidence simply does not determine a number — masquerading as a claim about rational agents, and that stated that plainly it no longer licenses the blanket dismissals. Tell me whether that diagnosis holds up.
