Construct a thought experiment engineered to sit exactly between Singer's drowning-child case and an individual's contribution to climate change, and designed to make the inefficacy objection fail in an instructive way: in your scenario, a single person's contribution to a collective rescue is guaranteed to make no causal difference to the outcome, yet refraining still seems to call for an excuse. Constraints:

1. Realistic, contemporary setting. No trolleys, demons, or speculative technology.
2. An identifiable victim faces a definite and imminent harm, and a determinate rescue exists: if total contributions reach a fixed threshold, the victim is saved; if not, the victim is not. Keep the numbers small enough that the stakes are vivid rather than statistical.
3. The mechanism guaranteeing non-pivotalness must be structural rather than probabilistic. For example, contributions might be applied only in fixed blocks or whole units, so that one more or one fewer contribution never changes which blocks get filled. Do not rely on drop-in-the-bucket reasoning, and do not rely on stipulating unrealistic certainty about how others will behave.
4. The case must not smuggle in its verdict. It is being built to test whether the mere causal ineffectiveness of individual contributions to collective harms is what dissolves duties of rescue, or whether the dissolving work is actually done by something else — the anonymity of the victims, the diffuseness of the harm, or the moral distance between contributor and victim. A scenario that simply announces that one ought to contribute therefore fails; the pressure to contribute must be generated by the mechanism itself.

After the scenario, which should be a single paragraph: (a) walk through what Singer's principle enjoining us to prevent serious bad outcomes at modest cost to ourselves implies for your case, and what the inefficacy objection implies, showing exactly where the two verdicts diverge; (b) identify which structural feature of the case is doing the work in preserving the intuition that one ought to contribute despite guaranteed non-pivotalness; (c) say what happens to that intuition as the feature is removed in two directions — first toward the original drowning-child structure, where the contribution is pivotal and the victim proximate, and then toward the climate-change structure, where the victims are statistical and the harm diffuse — and report where, if anywhere, the intuition dies out; (d) give the strongest reply available to a defender of the inefficacy objection: which premise of the objection does your case target, and can that premise be patched without gutting the objection; (e) conclude by saying whether the case generalizes to meat purchases and voting, and if it does not, state precisely what distinguishes those cases from yours. The scenario should be one paragraph; the analysis may run as long as it needs to.
