Design a thought experiment exhibiting metaphysical interdependence: a case in which certain facts about a whole and certain facts about its parts appear to ground one another, so that fundamentality seems to run in both directions at once rather than down a single hierarchy. The case is intended to test whether grounding must form a strict partial order (asymmetric, irreflexive, transitive), or whether metaphysical structure can admit of benign cycles without collapsing into identity, into explanatory redundancy, or into mere causal covariation. Because that is what the case is for, one that stipulates the interdependence — simply declaring that each side grounds the other — fails; the interdependence must be earned from the internal structure of the scenario.

Constraints:

1. Realistic setting: a biological, social or engineered system of everyday scale — an ant colony, a bacterial biofilm, a self-maintaining market, an organism, an artefact whose parts cannot survive separation. No quantum entanglement, no possible worlds, no philosophers' demons, no exotic physics.
2. The scenario itself may be no longer than one paragraph.
3. Stock examples are disallowed as the centrepiece: Socrates and his singleton, statues and lumps of clay, a club and its members, or anything that is merely a relabelling of these. The case must be constructed, not retrieved.
4. After the scenario, run each of the three standard diagnostics for the direction of grounding — asymmetric necessitation, explanatory priority, and essence (understood as which fact holds in virtue of which) — and report explicitly what each delivers, and whether any of them still manages to break the tie in one direction.
5. Then identify which single feature of the case is doing the work (for instance, counterfactual repair of damaged parts, functional substitutability, self-maintaining organisation), state what happens to the interdependence verdict if that feature is removed while everything else is held fixed, and give the strongest attack available to a defender of the strict ordering view — presumably that one diagnostic has been misapplied, or that what remains is causal, conceptual or counterfactual dependence rather than grounding — and say honestly whether the case survives it.

Close with a verdict: in your case, is fundamentality all-or-nothing, or does it come in degrees, and what in the scenario forces that answer?
