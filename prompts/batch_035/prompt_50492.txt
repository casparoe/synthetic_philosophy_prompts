I build retrieval-augmented LLM systems for a living — the usual stack: context windows, vector indexes, tool-calling agents, KV caches, sometimes a persistent memory store that carries over between sessions. Lately I've gotten mildly obsessed with the extended mind thesis from philosophy of mind, and I want you to translate the whole debate into my vocabulary as faithfully as you can, and then give me the residue report — what carries over, what gets distorted, and what can't be said in my terms at all.

Quick sketch of what I've picked up so we're on the same page, and correct me where I've got it wrong. Clark and Chalmers argue that if an external resource does the same cognitive work for Otto (who keeps addresses in a notebook he always carries) that biological memory does for Inga, then by parity it counts as part of his mind — the notebook entry is his memory of the museum address in a real sense. They wave at criteria like constant availability, easy access, and Otto's trusting the entries without checking them. Then there's a big fight: Adams and Aizawa's coupling-constitution objection, which as I understand it says that merely being causally coupled to a system doesn't make something a constituent of it, and that you need some principled mark of the cognitive instead of gesturing at parity. A later wave concedes extension but leans on complementarity — the external part has to do things inner cognition can't, not just duplicate it. And skeptics like Rupert say fine, cognition is embedded, but the model-plus-notebook is the wrong unit of analysis, and cognitive science should study the on-board parts.

Now the translation. Is the parity principle anything more than the observation that inference-time behavior can be equivalent across very different system boundaries — same tokens out, whether they came from weights, a cache hit, or retrieval? Do the reliability criteria on Otto's notebook map onto stuff I actually deal with, like cache invalidation, freshness guarantees, uptime, and how much you trust a retrieved document before passing it through? Is the coupling vs. constitution fight just the question of where you draw the deployment boundary — model only, or model plus index plus tools — which matters for benchmarking and for who's to blame when the system screws up, but which we'd never call a metaphysical question? And is Rupert's point basically that nobody can reproduce a benchmark result that depends on the exact contents of one specific vector index, so stop treating the coupled blob as a scientific object?

Then the residue, which is the part I actually care about. My honest suspicion is that the residue is mostly the first-personal claim — that the notebook is part of Otto's mind in a sense that matters to Otto, not just causally wired into his behavior — and that my vocabulary has literally no way to express that, because from a systems standpoint a boundary is just an interface you draw for engineering reasons and could redraw tomorrow. But I can't tell whether that means the philosophers are onto something real that my framework is missing, or whether their claim is less substantive than it sounds and my framework is exposing that. Maybe both. Tell me which, and don't be diplomatic about it.
