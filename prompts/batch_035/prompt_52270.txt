and here's one student's answer, which is the one i need help with (typed out as they wrote it, typos and all):

---

(a) On the BHK interpretation a proof of ¬P is a construction that transforms any proof of P into a proof of a contradiction, so ¬P is basically the type P → ⊥, like a function that never returns. A proof of P ∨ ¬P would have to be either a proof of P or a proof of ¬P, and for an arbitrary P we may have neither available, so intuitionists can't assert P ∨ ¬P. Excluded middle isn't exactly false for them, it's just not always constructible. Intuitionistic logic is basically classical logic plus a third truth value ("undecided"), so P ∨ ¬P comes out as not-true rather than false, and that's the whole difference between the two logics.

(b) Intuitionists reject double negation elimination (¬¬P → P fails), so they must also reject ¬¬(P ∨ ¬P). Double negating a statement that isn't provable doesn't make it provable, since if you could prove P ∨ ¬P you'd already have proven it, so its double negation can't be proven either.

(c) They drop excluded middle and keep the rest of the machinery, e.g. modus ponens and the deduction theorem. In mathematics generally, constructivists also don't accept actual infinite sets, only potentially infinite ones, so they can't do existence proofs over infinite collections at all.
