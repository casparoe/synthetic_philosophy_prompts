My friend and I got into a whole thing last night about luck egalitarianism (the view that justice should neutralize inequalities that come from brute luck but leave alone the ones that come from your own choices) and I went to bed actually kind of persuaded by it, which is annoying because now I want to know what would make me un-persuaded. So: what would count against it?

I don't just want the famous harshness objection rehashed at me, though I guess I want it included. What I keep chewing on is the border stuff, because that's where our argument actually got stuck. Like the guy who takes a knock on the head and afterwards genuinely, wholeheartedly wants to gamble away his savings -- the taste is acquired through brute luck, but the gamble is chosen. Does the view have anything principled to say there, or does it just pick whichever answer makes the case come out the way we already wanted? Same with religious people who think schooling their kids a certain way is non-negotiable: if their convictions were formed by an upbringing they didn't choose, is observing them a choice in the relevant sense, and who eats the cost? And what about people whose preferences adapted to poverty so smoothly they claim to want very little -- they chose cheap lives, technically.

Also the ex ante problem my friend kept hammering: if the state backstops every bad outcome of a gamble, nobody has any reason to insure or avoid risk, so maybe the view only works by treating "option luck" as a black box that quietly does all the moral work.

What I want is the defeaters ranked -- from fatal, through things a committed luck egalitarian should concede as counting against them, down to stuff they'd honestly just dismiss. Tell me what the view predicts that its rivals (sufficientarianism, prioritarianism, plain rawlsianism, whatever) don't, and whether the brute-luck/option-luck line has ever been drawn in a way that doesn't collapse into either "compensate everything" or "compensate only the badly off regardless of choice." And an honest call at the end: has the view been qualified into something unfalsifiable, and if so does that even matter for a view of this kind. I found the harshness stuff way more troubling than my friend did, FWIW, so factor that in.
