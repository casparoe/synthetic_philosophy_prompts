I've basically bought the standard story that mental states are functional states, and the main reason is multiple realizability: the same pain (or belief, or memory) can be realized in mammalian brains, octopus neurons, maybe silicon, and type-identity theory supposedly can't handle that. Lately tho I keep running into people claiming MR is basically an urban legend -- that real neuroscience keeps finding the same mechanisms converging across species, and that the famous cases are either not the *same* kind at all, or differ only in trivial ways. So tell me what would actually count against my position. Concretely: what would empirical findings have to look like for multiple realizability to be *false* rather than just rarer than advertised, and how much of my functionalism really hangs on MR versus being rebuildable on other grounds (causal-role explanation, levels of abstraction)? Also, if I respond to bad news by retreating to "the sameness lives at a higher level of description", am I just making the view unfalsifiable in a bad way, or is that kind of abstraction doing legitimate work? Rank the defeaters for me -- which ones a committed functionalist should honestly concede, and which ones they're entitled to brush off.
