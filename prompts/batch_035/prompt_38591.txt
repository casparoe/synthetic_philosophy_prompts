For thirty years I worked on sexual selection and communication in birds, and I have lately been sitting in on philosophy seminars where "costly signaling" is invoked to explain everything from conspicuous consumption to academic credentials. I have also watched economists apply the handicap principle to human affairs with a casualness that would never survive a grant review. Before I take any account of signal honesty seriously, I want the specification written out first. Please give me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of how signals stay honest must deliver.

To make the job concrete, the theory should at least have something to say about the following: peacock trains and other ornaments that look like pure waste; stotting in Thomson's gazelles, where the signal is addressed to a predator rather than a mate; nestling begging, where both the sender and receiver have interests that are partly aligned and partly opposed; aposematic coloration, which is honest but looks cheap once the warning is heeded; degree credentials and luxury goods, where the cost is paid in a currency of money and time rather than fitness; and signals whose cost arrives only after the audience has responded, such as threats or promises whose credibility rests on what backing them out would later cost. Among the distinctions the theory must respect, I would expect at least these: cost actually paid versus cost necessary to maintain honesty; handicap signals whose expense is the point versus index signals that are honest because fakes are physically or developmentally impossible; and signals whose reliability is guaranteed by production costs versus by receivers punishing cheaters after the fact. For each desideratum, give the case or consideration behind it, and mark which are non-negotiable and which reflect the biases of the biological versus the economic tradition. Most importantly, tell me whether some subset of the desiderata cannot be jointly satisfied — for instance, whether demanding that the theory cover cost-free honest systems while retaining the core insight that honesty is purchased is already a contradiction. Finally, a brief scorecard of Zahavi's original handicap principle, Grafen's formalization in terms of separating equilibria, the index-signal view, and accounts that lean on receiver retaliation, against your list. I care most about the criteria; keep the scoring to a paragraph.
