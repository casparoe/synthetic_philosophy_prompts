I'm a PhD student in political theory and my reading group has had two members going at eachother for about a year over hate speech law. One is basically an American First Amendment liberal: he thinks the Millian harm principle is the whole story, that offense isn't harm, and that European bans on things like Holocaust denial are a well-intentioned slide into paternalism that ends up protecting the state from criticism more than it protects anyone vulnerable. The other takes the European line: that hate speech works by undermining the public standing of members of targeted groups, that a society's assurance to minorities that they belong is a legitimate object of law (roughly the Waldron dignity argument), and that the American obsession with the state as the only threat to free speech ignores how a hostile environment quietly suppresses other people's speech. They've mostly been talking past each other. Please write the joint statement a good adversarial collaboration between them would produce: a numbered section of claims both could sign, a disagreements table where each row is worded so its own side would accept it as a fair statement of their view, the cruxes, and for each crux a concrete case or legal example both would agree bears on it -- things like cross-burning laws, Holocaust denial bans, campus speech codes, "glorifying terrorism" offences -- with each side's prediction written down in advance. If you conclude part of their dispute is really verbal, say which part and why. Also, if you think I've split the camps wrongly, or that one of my examples doesn't actually do the work I'm assuming, tell me that plainly instead of building a tidy document on my mistakes.
