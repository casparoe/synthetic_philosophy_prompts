I should state my positions plainly so you can audit them. I spent close to four decades as a condensed matter physicist, and since retiring I have been reading philosophy of mathematics with growing unease. Here is where I stand: (1) mathematical structures are discovered rather than invented — across my career it always felt as though the mathematics was already there, waiting for the physics to catch up; (2) mathematical knowledge is the most secure knowledge we have, more certain than any measurement; (3) in my scientific life I only accepted a belief when something like a causal connection tied the belief to the fact — a track in a cloud chamber, a spectral line — and I have come to think that is what knowledge amounts to quite generally; (4) my principal ground for (1) is indispensability: since mathematics is woven through our best physical theories, mathematical entities earn their ontological credentials the same way electrons do; (5) mathematical truths are necessary — in any universe, however different physically, the primes would be exactly as they are; and (6) even if physics were someday reformulated in purely finitary terms, classical analysis would remain true, not merely a convenient instrument. Please tell me which of these commitments pull against which, which apparent conflicts dissolve on inspection, and how severe each genuine tension is. Then say which single revision would clean up the most, and what it would cost me to make it.
