I got diagnosed with cluster headaches a few months back (turns out "suicide headache" isn't just a nickname people throw around) and ever since I keep drifting toward the view that pain and pleasure just aren't on the same scale — that no amount of good feeling can pay back really bad suffering. But when I actually check my gut on cases, I'm not sure I believe it, so let me lay out my verdicts and you tell me if there's a principle behind them:

- One person tortured for a month vs. a million people each getting a month of the best experiences of their lives: I'd take the torture being prevented, and I don't think it's close. No pile of pleasure seems to touch that.
- A world containing only beings with mild aches and great, rich pleasures vs. an empty world: the world with the beings is better. I'm pretty confident about this even though every one of them has some pain.
- For myself: I would NOT accept a permanent small worsening of my baseline (say, daily low-grade headache forever) in exchange for any amount of added enjoyment. But I obviously WOULD take a few days of moderate pain from a medical procedure to be able to walk again. So small pains do seem tradable against pleasure, and I can't tell where the cutoff is or whether there even is one.
- Relieving one person's cluster headache for one hour vs. giving a thousand people each an hour of mild enjoyment: headache relief wins, but if you ask me whether a big enough number of the enjoyment-hours would eventually flip it, I want to say no... except my "empty world" answer above suggests I do aggregate somewhere.

Is there a single principle that gets all of these — some kind of lexical threshold where suffering past a line can't be paid off with pleasure, but everything under the line is just summed up? And if no principle cleanly does it, which of my verdicts should go, and what does the repaired principle say about a case I didn't list: donating to a charity that reliably prevents severe suffering vs. one that reliably adds a lot of happiness to already okay lives?
