I'm a backend engineer with zero philosophy background, but after a decade of arguments about sprint estimates I've started collecting my gut verdicts on when you should trust "the outside view" (base rates, what happens to projects like this in general) versus your own inside knowledge of the details. Also when you should defer to people with better track records and when you shouldn't. I want to know if there's a principle that captures all of these verdicts or whether I'm just rationalizing whatever I felt like doing at the time.

1. Big migration estimate. My team, who know the codebase intimately, say 6 weeks. I know from bitter experience that migrations like this take 3-4x the estimate at basically every company I've ever worked at. Verdict: go with ~5 months, outside view wins, no contest.

2. Debugging a weird production failure. In the abstract I'd say 95% of the time it's in your own code, not the database driver. But last month I spent three days auditing every line of my new module, was genuinely sure it was clean, and it turned out to be a race condition in the driver itself. Verdict: I still think you should assume it's your own code first, but at some point the inside view of "I have actually checked this twice" should beat the base rate. This feels different from case 1 but I can't quite say why.

3. A few years ago I chose a startup offer over a stable one. Everyone knows the base rate for startup equity going to zero. I took it anyway because I'd worked with the founder before and had a read on the team that no aggregate statistic could capture. Verdict: justified. But this is exactly the move every deluded founder makes when they ignore the base rate, so I'm suspicious of my own comfort here.

4. Design argument with a much more senior architect. He wants microservices, I want a boring monolith. On paper he crushes me on experience and track record. But I'm the only one who's actually read the current code, and my objection is a concrete trap in one specific module. Verdict: I should push back and not just defer.

5. Stack Overflow consensus says framework X behaves a certain way. My one-off reproducible test says the opposite. Verdict: trust my test. One reproducible result beats an aggregate of human judgment, even a large aggregate.

6. A junior dev on my team is certain our alerting pipeline drops events, which the vendor says doesn't happen. "Junior blames the vendor" is basically a meme, the base rate is brutal. Verdict: discount her claim, at least a little, until she shows me logs. But this doesn't sit with #5, where a single repro beats the crowd, or with #4, where being the person who read the code licenses disagreeing with the senior guy.

So my tentative principle from 2, 4, and 5 is something like: the inside view wins when your evidence is mechanical or verifiable (you ran it, you read the actual source, you can point at the line), and the outside view wins when your evidence is judgment (estimates, hunches, vibes about how long things take). But #3 is trouble for this: my read on the founder is pure judgment, and I still think it was right to let it override a very solid base rate. And #6 is trouble from the other direction, since presumably the junior dev could produce logs eventually too, and I'm still discounting her.

Questions: is there a principle that gets all six verdicts, or close to it, and if the mechanical-evidence one doesn't survive, what would you replace it with? If it does survive, what do you do with #3 — was taking that job defensible or was I just flattering myself, and if the latter, is everyone who ever joined a startup on the strength of "I know these people" wrong to? What does your principle say about the case I keep going back and forth on: I'm 80% confident I can cut our cloud bill by 30% because I know exactly where the waste is, but my calibration data on my own past 80% confidences says I'm right more like 60% of the time. Should I downgrade myself to 60%, and if I do, haven't I just discarded the very private information that made me confident in the first place? And which of my six verdicts does your principle have to throw out, because I've stared at them for a while and I genuinely don't know which one is the weak link.
