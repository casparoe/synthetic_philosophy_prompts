Two people in my reading group have been going at it for about a year over whether "better than" is transitive, and I want them to do the adversarial-collaboration thing instead of another shouting match. Write the joint statement they should produce.

Quick background on where they each stand. D holds that "better than" is transitive as a matter of the logic of comparatives: if A is better than B and B is better than C, then A is better than C, and that's not an empirical finding, it's constitutive of what a comparative relation is. Any spectrum case that seems to conflict with this is, for D, a confusion between the vagueness of our measurements and the relation itself. M holds that some spectrum cases show a genuine failure of transitivity — the relation itself, not our instruments, is nontransitive — and that D's insistence otherwise smuggles in a picture of "better than" as modeled on "longer than," which M thinks is exactly the thing that's up for grabs.

Their favorite test case, for refrence: a sequence of 500 possible post-op outcomes for the same patient, each involving a slightly longer hospital stay than the one before — each step is roughly an extra hour, none of them individually perceptible to the patient, but the first outcome (discharge in three days) is obviously and hugely better than the last (discharge in about twenty-four days). M says every adjacent judgment in the chain is true and the end-to-end judgment is true, so transitivity fails outright. D says the adjacent judgments are either false or indeterminate — artifacts of a discrimination threshold, not facts about betterness.
