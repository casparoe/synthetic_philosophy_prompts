"Back in the 1960s, when the first machine translation programs were being tested, people fed in the Bible verse about the spirit being willing but the flesh being weak, translated it into Russian and back, and got out 'the vodka is good but the meat is rotten.' The computer had no clue what the words meant. The cognitive scientist Douglas Hofstadter ran modern versions of these tests decades later on Google Translate and got the same kind of nonsense, so nothing fundamental had changed. His point was that real translation requires understanding — you have to grasp what a sentence is actually about before you can carry it into another language — and statistical translation systems have none of that. They just match word sequences in one language to word sequences in another based on how often those sequences show up in past translations, essentially a giant lookup table. That's why they can never translate anything they haven't seen before, and why they fall apart on poetry, jokes, and wordplay, which is exactly what he demonstrated. In the end he proved that no statistical system, no matter how much data you give it, will ever understand language — the large language models everyone is excited about now are just the same trick scaled up."
