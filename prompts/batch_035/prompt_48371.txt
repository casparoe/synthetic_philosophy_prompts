I'm a PhD student in philosophy of cognitive science, writing a chapter on whether large language models force a revision in our concepts of belief and desire rather than merely extending their application. My question is deliberately narrow, so please resist generalizing to 'AI systems' at large: for at least one currently deployed LLM-based assistant and at least one humdrum, non-self-referential proposition p (something on the order of the Eiffel Tower being in Paris, or 17 being prime), what is your credence that the plain sentence 'this model believes that p' is literally true — true as used, not merely useful to assert?

To keep the answer from collapsing into a slogan about tools, please separate three readings of what could make that attribution literally true, and give a probability for each:

1. The predictive reading: the intentional stance earns its keep — no tractable non-intentional description of the model's dispositions is available, so belief-talk is an indispensable compression, and that is all literal truth amounts to here.
2. The real-pattern reading, in something like Dennett's sense: there is a pattern in the model's dispositions that belief-vocabulary tracks independently of our interest in predicting it, the way a chess engine exhibits the strategy of fianchettoing whether or not anyone bothers to describe it that way.
3. The original-content reading: some internal states of the system are about their objects and can be wrong in their own right, rather than merely inheriting content from the human use of the words whose statistics it absorbed during training.

Then give your overall credence in the plain-sentence claim as uttered by an ordinary speaker, which requires locating ordinary usage among the three readings rather than averaging over them. I'd also like to know where my two committee-shaped interlocutors would most plausibly part ways with you: my advisor puts the predictive reading at 0.95 and the overall credence at 0.95, holding that the stronger readings add nothing to literal truth; a visitor puts the original-content reading at 0.6, which drives her overall number up rather than down.

Throughout, tell me how confident you are in each component, single out the one you are least sure of, and say what argument or empirical finding about these systems would move that component most.
