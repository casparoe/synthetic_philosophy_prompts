I'm a high school junior who writes for our school's philosophy zine. Our class just finished a unit on American pragmatism — Peirce's paper on making our ideas clear, alongside excerpts from James's Pragmatism — and our teacher keeps warning us against the standard caricature of James as the man who thought "true" just means "convenient for me right now." Please write a deadpan consumer product recall notice, roughly 450 words, for a fictional smart speaker called the VerifiR™, recalled after its Truth Assessment Engine began certifying claims as true whenever a single user reported feeling satisfied by them that same afternoon. The humor should come from getting the actual dispute right: a Satisfaction Mode that consults one individual over the short term versus the Long Run Module the device shipped without (indefinite inquiry by the whole community, converging on a final opinion); a Cash Value setting that prices beliefs by immediate payoff; and two neighboring households whose devices flatly contradict each other, with no firmware path to agreement. Include a short closing Engineer's Note from a fictional Peircean who resigned over the recall, and structure the piece with the standard sections of a recall notice (The Defect, Is Your Device Affected, What We Are Doing). One further requirement: a Jamesian reader should come away thinking the piece mocks the caricature of James rather than James himself — perhaps by having the company's denial of relativism misstate his view in precisely the way his defenders complain about. It should be funny to someone who knows the literature and still readable to someone who doesn't.
