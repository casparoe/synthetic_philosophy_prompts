Quick question that came out of a huge family arguement and I can't let it go. My cousin agreed to water my grandma's plants while she was on vacation, forgot, and the plants died. Everyone in the family talks about it like he killed them. But technically he didn't DO anything, right? He just... didn't water them. And the more I think about it the weirder it gets, because literally every other person on earth also didn't water those plants. Her neighbor could've walked over and done it. I could have. Some random guy in Ohio could've flown out. Nobody thinks the neighbor killed the plants, let alone the guy in Ohio. So what's going on?

Part of me thinks it's just the promise thing -- he SAID he'd do it, so this is really about breaking your word, not about causing anything, and calling it "killing the plants" is just my grandma being dramatic. But then the other night I was thinking about how if you stand next to a pool and watch a toddler fall in and do nothing, people would absolutly say you let it happen, or worse, even though you never agreed to watch anyone's toddler. So the promise can't be the whole story. And also, what even IS the bad thing my cousin did? There's no moment where he did the plant-killing. Not doing something isn't an event, there's no time and place you can point to. The only actual events are him playing xbox all week and the plants drying out. So can nothing cause something?

Oh and one more thing that confuses me -- I watched some law show where they said in court you ask "but for" the person's action, would the bad thing have still happened. But for my cousin not watering, the plants live, ok, fine. But but for the neighbor not watering they'd also die... except no, wait, if the neighbor had watered them they'd live, so doesn't the neighbor pass the same test?? By that logic everyone on earth is equally the killer, which obviously can't be right.

So my actual questions: does this have a name in philosophy, this whole causing-by-not-doing thing, and is there a name for whatever rule picks out WHICH non-doer counts as the causer? My cousin keeps saying "I didn't touch those plants" and my grandma keeps saying "you killed them" and I honestly think they're both kinda right which is annoying. And please don't just drop the fancy term and peace out -- I want to know whether philosophers think this is real causation or just a way of talking when we're mad at someone, whether my cousin "caused" the death or only "let it happen" (is there even a difference), and where the line actually falls, like why the cousin and not the neighbor, and whether the toddler-pool guy is on the same side of the line as my cousin or the neighbor. If there's no established concept for the part about picking which non-doer counts, that's fine, just say so and tell me what you'd call it. This has been bugging me for like two weeks.
