Here's roughly my package on distributive justice, and I want to know if it hangs together before I commit to it in a paper:

(1) I take the leveling-down objection to be close to decisive against egalitarianism as a telic view — a world where everyone is worse off but equal isn't better in any respect.

(2) So I'm a prioritarian: the moral weight of a benefit depends only on the recipient's absolute welfare level, not on how they compare to anyone else.

(3) I want the priority weights to be pretty steep — the claims of people at the very bottom nearly swamp everyone else's.

(4) But I also think numbers matter, and I'm confident I'd choose a million moderate benefits for comfortably-off people over a tiny benefit for a single person in hellish conditions.

(5) I hold that a big part of welfare is positional anyway — status, relative income, where you stand in your community genuinely affect how well-off you are.

(6) And I think inequalities that come from domination or exploitation are worse than the exact same inequality arising from brute luck, though I'd deny this makes me a closet egalitarian.

Go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and which dissolve on inspection, rank them by severity, and for each real tension say what the cheapest fix costs me elsewhere in the package. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I honestly don't know where the problem is, if there is one.
