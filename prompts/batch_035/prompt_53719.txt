been thinking about where i stand on manipulation vs persuasion and i want a consistency check before i try to write any of it down. here's the package:

1. what makes manipulation wrong is the bypass: influence counts as manipulation when it works around your rational capacities instead of giving you considerations you can assess. give me arguments and evidence and I can weigh them, that's persuasion; press buttons I can't see the workings of and that's manipulation.

2. subliminal advertising is about as clear a case of wrong manipulation as there is.

3. automatic organ donation registries (you're in unless you opt out, and opting out is easy and well publicized) are legitimate policy. everything's on the table, the information is right there, you can exit anytime. that's not manipulating anyone.

4. ads that first manufacture an insecurity and then sell you the cure (the classic deodorant ad that makes you terrified you smell) are manipulative and wrong even if every literal claim in them is true. "7 out of 10 women notice" might be a real survey result, whatever, it's still manipulation.

5. if my friend knows i'll bristle at something they need to say, i think it's actually kind of them to wait for a moment when i'm rested and in a good mood, and they don't need to tell me they chose the moment strategically.

6. guilt-tripping my roommate about the dishes is manipulation, but making a genuine, emotional appeal about how the constant mess is getting to me is fine.

so which of these actually pull against each other, which pairs just look like they conflict but don't, and what's the cheapest fix? rank the tensions by how bad they are, and for each one say what would have to give and what that costs me. i genuinely don't know where the problem is so don't just flag everything equally, do the audit. also a little wary that my "bypass" definition in (1) might be doing less work than i think, so tell me if the whole thing needs rebuilding rather than patching.
