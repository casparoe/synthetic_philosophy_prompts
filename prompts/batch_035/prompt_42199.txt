Background: I spent 35 years as a geophysicist, mostly doing seismic modeling for oil companies, and since retiring I've gotten embarrassingly deep into philosophy. I help run a discussion group at our community center — about 14 people, mostly fellow retirees plus a couple of philosophy grad students who keep us honest — and we meet for 90 minutes every other Thursday. Next session is on anthropic reasoning, and I don't want people nodding along to a description of the presumptuous philosopher problem; I want them to get talked into it (or out of it) by their own choices, before anyone names it.

My half-baked idea, which you should fix and flesh out: a game built around a coin flip and selection into rooms. I secretly flip a coin; heads, only one person from the group gets pulled aside, tails, ten do. Nobody who's pulled knows how many others were. Each selected person bets candy or poker chips on which way the coin landed, with odds set so the house pays out big in whichever world more people are betting. The part I actually care about is running it twice with different selection procedures — once where I draw names from a hat out of the fixed 14 who showed up (so the pool size is independent of the coin), and once where the coin decides how big the whole participant pool was in the first place — because my sense is that smart people's intuitions flip between these two setups even though from inside the room they feel identical. Then a final round where the whole group has to bet, before any flip, between two "cosmologies" that agree on all the physics anyone will ever check but differ in total number of observers by a factor of a trillion trillion, and I want to see how many people who refused to count their own existence as evidence in round one suddenly find it compelling when it's dressed up as the size of the universe.

So what I need from you: complete rules, a materials list I can actually assemble (paper, envelopes, a coin, a jar, maybe stickers), a payoff scheme that works without real money, a minute-by-minute timeline fitting 90 minutes including the debrief, and a debrief that maps each round onto the self-sampling versus self-indication dispute without jargon-dumping — use the game's own moments, like "the moment you said being chosen was evidence of tails," as the anchors. Please also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it — I guarantee someone will argue through the entire first round, and someone always sneaks info back to the main room, so I want contingencies, not optimism. And be blunt about which part of the real philosophical fight no game with a fixed group of 14 people who all saw each other walk in that evening can possibly reproduce — my suspicion is that knowing the pool size ruins the analogy to not knowing how big the universe is, and I want to know exactly what I'll have to supply by hand in the debrief.

Last constraint: nobody hears the phrase "presumptuous philosopher" or any of the technical vocabulary until the very end. I want the name to land as a diagnosis of what they already did, not a label they perform toward. And if you think one of my rounds is doing the wrong work or two of them collapse into the same point, cut or replace it — I'd rather have three rounds that each earn their twenty minutes.
