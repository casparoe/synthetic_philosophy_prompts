so: write a decision tree that starts from the shared starting point -- "hesperus is phosphorus" is informative in a way "hesperus is hesperus" is not, but both are true, and the astronomer who assents to one and not the other isn't just confused about the sky. every branch node should be a question about a concrete case, answerable without any jargon. i specifically want cases on: substituting co-referring names inside belief reports (if i believe hesperus is visible at dusk, do i automatically believe phosphorus is visible at dusk?); empty names like "vulcan" (did it mean something before we found out nothing's there?); the modal one -- whether "hesperus might not have been phosphorus" makes any sense at all (my advisor claims this is where the real action is, i'm skeptical but include it anyway); and what exactly the name contributes to the *truth* of the identity sentence, not just to how it feels.

keep node language undergrad-friendly, 7 or so nodes, and dont require any citations since its a handout not a paper
