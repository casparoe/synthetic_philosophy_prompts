I'm a philosophy of biology graduate student, and for two years my department's reading group has circled the etiological versus causal-role debate about biological function without anyone budging an inch. On paper this debate ended decades ago: the pluralists won, everyone agreed that selected-effects accounts do one job — underwriting malfunction and the normativity of saying what a part is for — while causal-role accounts do another, namely picking out what a component contributes to some capacity of a containing system, and the journals officially moved on. Yet whenever a concrete case lands on the table we are right back in the old fight, and I've begun to suspect the pluralist settlement is a truce nobody actually honors. So what I want from you is a diagnosis of the disagreement, not another survey of the two theories.

Here are the cases where the two camps in my group genuinely split despite everyone swearing allegiance to pluralism. First, the arrhythmic heart: all agree that on the selected-effects story its function is still pumping blood, but half the group insists that a physiological explanation of why this particular heart pumps poorly is itself a function attribution, and each half thinks the other has silently changed the subject. Second, the lens-inducing tissue: early in development a patch of tissue emits a chemical that induces nearby cells to form a lens, and developmental biologists call this the tissue's function even though nothing in its selection history explains why it's there — the causal-role people treat this as a clean win, while the selected-effects people either deny it's a function or quietly shift to talking about the function of the whole developmental system. Third, traits that have only very recently come under a new selection pressure, where people's intuitions flip depending on whether the case is described in terms of the trait's past or its present contribution. Fourth, function talk about artifacts and about institutional roles like the function of a judge, where both accounts seem to apply and to give different answers.

My working hypothesis — and I want it tested, not accepted — is that the two sides are answering different questions under a single word: the etiological side is doing normative and metaphysical work, making it true that a part can malfunction and that there is a fact about what it is for, while the causal-role side is doing descriptive and explanatory work within a specific analysis, and the cases only feel contradictory when we slide between the two jobs in the middle of a sentence. But I can see at least one rival diagnosis: that there is a genuine substantive dispute about whether function statements carry normativity at all, and the pluralist truce merely papers over it. Tell me which diagnosis better fits what people actually say when they argue about the cases above; whether the impasse is verbal, substantive, or a symptom of one term being asked to do two jobs inside a single science; and what would count as real progress given your diagnosis — a distinction, a change of vocabulary, some class of cases whose treatment both camps could accept, or a formal account. And if you think no progress should be expected at all, say that plainly and explain why.
