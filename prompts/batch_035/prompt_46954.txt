I am a member of the city council of a town of about twelve thousand people, and I expect to be the swing vote on a question I can't get straight in my own head. I'd like your help thinking it through.

The situation: our state, like several others, still has an old criminal statute on the books making adultery a misdemeanor. It has not been enforced in living memory, and everyone assumed it was dead letter. Six months ago, a long-running affair between two well-known residents became public — the man is married, the woman is not — and it badly upset a great many people here: a church group circulated a petition with over two thousand signatures asking the county attorney to bring charges, and the county attorney has now come to the council asking us to either formally request that the district attorney revive enforcement, or formally request that the legislature repeal the statute, or do nothing and leave it in place. The couple are consenting adults. No one claims anyone was deceived into the affair, no minors, no money, no professional relationship, no breach of any contract that anyone intends to sue over — the wrong alleged is purely that adultery is morally wrong and that a community that says so ought to be able to say so through its law.

The petition's organizers make an argument that I find harder to dismiss than I expected. They say a society is held together not just by laws against harm but by a shared moral code, that our code genuinely condemns this conduct, and that if the law can never enforce the majority's actual moral convictions, then the law is no longer the community's law but something imposed on it. Repeal, on their view, is not neutrality: withdrawing a public condemnation is itself a public statement, and it tells our children something. The other side says that people's private consensual conduct is nobody else's business, that criminal punishment is a distinctively severe response, and that a statute under which we could all be prosecuted for things we'd never dream of bringing to court is a weapon waiting for the next grievance.

I want to vote on principle, not on which side yells louder. But the principle is what eludes me. Is there a defensible middle position between "the law may criminalize whatever the prevailing morality condemns" and "the law may never punish conduct that harms no one other than the participants"? And does it matter that the moral code being enforced is one I happen to share in this case?

Please respond as a political philosopher who has spent a career on the limits of the criminal law and on what, if anything, a community may demand of its members by way of virtue. Before writing, think through both sides at their strongest — don't build a strawman out of the petition organizers, and don't assume the civil-liberties side is obviously right. You don't need to cite cases or statutes; the question is about the principle, not the local law. Structure your answer with the following sections, in this order:

1. "My recommendation" — a direct statement of how you think I should vote and why, in no more than three sentences.
2. "What's really at issue" — roughly 150 words identifying the underlying question the debate turns on, as distinct from the surface politics.
3. "The strongest case for enforcement" — roughly 150 words.
4. "The strongest case for repeal" — roughly 150 words.
5. "The middle ground, if any" — roughly 150 words on whether the two extreme positions exhaust the options, and if not, what lies between them and where exactly the line should fall.
6. "What I should say publicly" — roughly 100 words of how I could explain my vote to the town, including to the petition organizers, without either pandering or lecturing.
