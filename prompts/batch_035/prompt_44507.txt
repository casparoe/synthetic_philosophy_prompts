Before I read any more defenses of top-down causation in the mind, I want the specification they are being measured against. Picture Hofstadter's careenium, introduced in his 1982 Synthese response to Dennett and reused in I Am a Strange Loop: a billiard-table stand-in for the cranium in which a vast number of small magnetic balls — simms, standing in for neurons — careen about, occasionally clumping into larger, longer-lived structures, simmballs, standing in for symbols, which from a zoomed-out view appear to mirror and react to the world outside the table. The whole controversy is Hofstadter's title question, in paraphrase: within such a system, which level does the shoving and which gets shoved? On one reading the simms do all the pushing and the simmball level is a convenient compression of the substrate story with no causal work of its own; on another, the simmballs, once formed, do real pushing — shaping which simm collisions come next, and steering the whole system's behavior. Write the desiderata for a theory of this level-interaction that would be good enough to settle, case by case, whether a symbol-level explanation ever earns its keep as a causal explanation rather than a compression. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it; mark it non-negotiable, negotiable, or a prejudice of one camp; flag any pair you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable; and name the single requirement that excludes the most popular candidates. Then, in at most two sentences each, score Dennett's patterns-in-the-substrate view, Kim-style causal exclusion, and Sperry-style emergent downward causation against the list — the weight should sit on the criteria, not the scoring. Deliver everything as a single JSON object with this schema: a "requirements" array of objects with fields "name", "statement", "motivating_case", "status" (one of "non-negotiable", "negotiable", "camp_prejudice"), and "excludes" (popular views it knocks out; may be empty); a "tensions" array of objects with fields "requirement_pair" and "conflict" (stating whether the pair is genuinely unsatisfiable or only apparently so); and a "scorecard" object mapping each of the three views to a one-line verdict.
