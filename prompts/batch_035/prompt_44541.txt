I started two months ago as the only archivist at a county historical society, and I've quickly learned that the real job is appraisal -- deciding what to keep when people show up with boxes. It happens maybe once a week: a retired judge's meticulously organized papers, a moldy box of reciepts from a defunct immigrant social club that a grandchild found in a garage, old arrest and surveillance records the sheriff's office wants out of their basement. We have maybe 200 linear feet of free shelf space and basically no budget, and the board has to sign off on anything that costs money.

I've also been reading enough to know that archives like mine are exactly how the historical record got skewed: the papers of the powerful and tidy survive, everyone else's traces rot or get tossed, and the silences in the sources are the cumulative result of decisions just like mine. So "just take the best-organized stuff" quietly reproduces the bias. But I can't take everything either.

Please turn the archival-silences idea into a procedure I can actually run in twenty minutes at someone's kitchen table: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure can say no on its own, and where it has to stop and hand the decision to me or the board because it's a values question, not a records question. Then run it on the three cases above, including at least one where the procedure gives an answer I won't like. End with a one-sentence statement of the procedure's guiding rule as the very last line.
