At a recent meeting of my school's ethics society, we debated the morality of eating meat, and the argument that carried the room went like this: moral status depends on the capacity for suffering and other forms of experience; that capacity depends on the brain; and one animal has more of the relevant capacity than another roughly in proportion to how big its brain is, the way a computer's overall performance scales with the size and power of its hardware. From this, the student who made the argument concluded that we owe chickens less moral consideration than pigs, and pigs less than humans, roughly in proportion to their relative brain sizes — enough of a ranking, he claimed, to settle how we may permissibly treat each kind, without any further argument about the nature of their inner lives.

I suspect the size step is the weak one. I have read that sperm whales have brains several times the size of ours, and that crows and ravens achieve remarkable feats of planning and problem-solving with brains about the size of a walnut, which makes me doubt that raw size tracks anything morally interesting. But I am not sure how to organize the objection beyond pointing at counterexamples, and I would like to be fair to the argument before rejecting it.

Could you therefore treat his claim as an analogy and audit it properly? First, set out the mapping explicitly: what, in his argument, plays the role of the hardware, what plays the role of the performance it is supposed to deliver, and what plays the role of the conclusion the performance is supposed to license. Second, identify exactly where the mapping breaks — is the trouble in the step from brain size to psychological capacity, or in the step from capacity to moral status, or in both? Third, tell me which of the disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion he wanted and which are beside the point. Finally, judge whether any graded ranking of the consideration we owe to different animals survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether his graded conclusion was being carried by the analogy all along — and if some weaker version of his ranking does survive, say what it would and would not be entitled to imply about how we may treat chickens, pigs, and humans.
