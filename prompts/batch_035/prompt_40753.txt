I've been trying to figure out when someone's own conduct disqualifies them from blaming others, just by going through cases and noting my gut reactions. Blaming my roommate for never doing the dishes when I never do them either: clearly out of line. Blaming a friend for texting while driving when I used to do it all the time but quit years ago: feels fine, and I weirdly feel like I have *more* standing than someone who's never done it. Blaming a coworker for gossiping when my record is clean only because nobody ever tells me anything: standing feels shaky anyway. Blaming my brother for cheating on his taxes when I cheat on mine too but feel awful about it and admit it: more permissible than plain hypocritical blame, but not clean either. Blaming a stranger for littering while I drive a gas-guzzler every day: feels totally fine, littering is just different. Blaming someone for snapping at me when they only snapped because I snapped at them first: no standing whatsoever. So is there a single principle behind all of these, or close? If two principles are needed, tell me where one leaves off and the other takes over. And please tell me what your principle says about the case I keep flip-flopping on: does a reformed racist who genuinely changed have standing to blame racists, and does it matter how recent the change is or whether they used to be much worse? If you have to throw out one of my verdicts to make the principle work, tell me which one and why.
