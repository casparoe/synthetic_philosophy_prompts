been reading rationalist blogs since the OB days and honestly after the nth EA forum thread where people rediscover that total utilitarianism implies the repugnant conclusion, i want something funnier than the threads. write me a parody product review page — amazon/yelp style, with star ratings, reviewer handles, "verified purchase" badges, a ratings histogram if you want — for a fictional subscription service called Mere Addition™. the pitch: for $9.99/month they deliver one new life to your civilisation that is just barely worth living. muzak and potatoes. decent but not great. and you can scale up. about 700 words.

the jokes have to come from getting population axiology actually right, so someone who's read their Parfit laughs instead of winces. some required beats, in whatever order works:

- top five-star review from a committed total utilitarian who is THRILLED and upsells you to the billion-lives-per-month plan. they treat "repugnant" in "repugnant conclusion" as a branding problem, not a math problem. they should do the arithmetic out loud and it should be correct.
- a one-star review from an average utilitarian who's upset the new lives lowered the average, and who muses, without ever noticing how it sounds, about ways to get the average back up. leave it legible that the obvious way to raise an average is horrible.
- a two-star review from a critical-level sort of person who concedes each life is worth living but says the subscription is net bad because the lives don't clear the threshold, and gets dogpiled in the replies by the total utilitarian ("they're POSITIVE. POSITIVE utility!")
- a review from someone with person-affecting sympathies who just can't get worked up: nobody was harmed, nobody who failed to exist can complain about it. maybe they mention being glad their own kids exist.
- someone with the asymmetry in their bones who's relieved the service refuses to ship negative lives, with a total utilitarian in the replies insisting that's discrimination.
- somewhere the manufacturer's response to a refund request should straight-facedly invoke the non-identity problem — like, the complaining customer's own grandchildren wouldn't exist without the subscription, so who exactly is owed the refund, and for which life?
- the comment threads under the reviews should gesture at the way every escape hatch someone proposes has a bullet someone else immediately points at, but don't name any impossibility theorems or cite anyone by name — no bibliography stuff, nobody's googling anything.

keep it deadpan: the reviewers all sincerely beleive their axiology, nobody winks at the reader, and the manufacturer is polite corporate-voice throughout. by the end a careful reader should be able to reconstruct the whole dialectic from the reviews alone — why each view finds a world of billions of barely-content people repugnant, and why each proposed fix is repugnant to someone else. that's the actual joke and it should land without a single reviewer saying "as Parfit says."

if you want a ratings breakdown like "4.2 stars, 12,847 reviews" or a "customers also subscribed to" footer, go for it. just don't let any review get longer than a real person would write.
