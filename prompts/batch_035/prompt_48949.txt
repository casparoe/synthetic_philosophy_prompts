I'm taking a philosophy of mind course, and for a project on introspection and the inverted spectrum I've been asked to simulate an adversarial collaboration between two camps that have been arguing past each other for decades: a committed qualia realist, who holds that experiences have intrinsic, private, ineffable qualitative characters that are grasped immediately in introspection, and a convinced follower of the 'quining qualia' programme, who holds that any property meeting all four of the traditional marks (ineffability, intrinsicness, privacy, immediacy) is an incoherent posit, and that everything worth explaining can be explained by dispositions to discriminate, react and report. Neither is a strawman: the realist knows about discrimination studies and heterophenomenology and still insists something is left out; the quiner knows about Nagel-style worries and still insists the leftover 'something' does no explanatory work and cannot be coherently characterised.

Draft the joint statement they would both sign after a year of good-faith argument. It should contain: (a) the substantive claims both accept, including about what introspection actually delivers, the dependence of experience on neural machinery, and what Chase-and-Sanborn-style cases of gradual taste-shift (e.g. the coffee drinker whose enjoyment fades imperceptibly over decades) show about the stability of apparent qualitative character; (b) the points where they still part ways, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording — including whether 'the way it seems' names a fact over and above dispositions, and whether ineffability is a defect of the concept or of language; (c) the cruxes, such that settling one would move at least one side; and (d) for each crux, a concrete case, thought experiment or line of argument both agree would bear on it, with each side's prediction about that case written down in advance. If you conclude that part of their residual dispute is merely verbal, say which part and why.

Format: a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table with these columns — (1) point of residual disagreement; (2) the realist's signed formulation of their own view; (3) the quiner's signed formulation of their own view; (4) the crux it turns on; (5) a case or argument both accept as bearing on it; (6) each side's advance prediction. Then append a short JSON object summarising the collaboration, with the fields "agreed_claims" (array of one-sentence strings), "verbal_disputes" (array of strings, possibly empty), "cruxes" (array of objects with the keys "crux", "test", "realist_prediction", "quiner_prediction"), and "would_move_either_side" (a boolean with a one-sentence justification string).
