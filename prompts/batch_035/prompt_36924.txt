writing up my positions on the bell stuff for our philosophy of physics discussion group (mixed crowd: a retired physics teacher, two grad students, one guy who mostly read popular books) and before I send it out I want to know if my own view even hangs together. here's where I've landed:

1. Bell's theorem shows nonlocality, period. it's not a theorem about hidden variables or about Bohm vs Copenhagen — any theory at all that reproduces the observed correlations and gives each run one definite outcome on each side has to be nonlocal somewhere.

2. I beleive measurements have unique outcomes. one result per measurement, every time. I'm not an Everettian — worlds splitting every time a photon hits a half-silvered mirror strikes me as far more extravagant than any nonlocality.

3. I'm pretty sympathetic to the quantum state being a state of knowledge, qbism-adjacent. collapse on that picture is just updating your information, nothing physically whooshes from one wing of the experiment to the other.

4. I think all relativity really forbids is faster-than-light signalling, and quantum mechanics respects that, so there's no genuine conflict with relativity, and people who go on about a conflict with the spirit of relativity are being precious.

5. superdeterminism — the measurement settings being correlated with the hidden variables from the very beginning — is conspiracy thinking, and I'm not going to waste the group's time on it.

6. on the other hand the retrocausal loophole, where what's emitted is already conditioned on the future measurement settings, seems like it might actually be worth taking seriously. I like that it keeps the world local.

7. and honestly I don't think anything travels between the two wings of a bell experiment anyway. the correlations are just there, holistically, like the two ends of a stretched rope are correlated except there's no rope — no influence, no signal, nothing moving.

go through these and tell me which ones actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what the cheapest repair would be in each case. rank the tensions by severity instead of listing everything as equally bad, and if the whole package forces a choice somewhere, be blunt about which commitment has to go and what taking the alternative on commits me to. my instinct is that the retrocausal one and the superdeterminism one can't both be treated the way I treat them, but I might be wrong about where the real problem is.
