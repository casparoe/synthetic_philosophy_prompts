A friend of mine is very into the idea of mind uploading, and whenever the topic comes up he shuts down any skepticism with the phrase "the brain is just a computer." Last week it came up at a party in a conversation about whether a perfect digital copy of a person would actually be that person, and when someone expressed doubts he basically said the debate is over, it's like arguing about whether thunder is an electrical phenomenon, it's just an established fact that brains are computers. The person he was arguing with said that's a bait and switch -- if "computer" just means something that processes information, then sure, but that trivial reading doesn't get you uploading at all, and if it means something strong like "the brain runs a program that could be run on other hardware," that's exactly what's at issue, so quoting the slogan as if it were settled science is circular.

I got pulled into the middle of it without really being equipped to referee. My own sense is that both of them are gesturing at a whole menu of distinct claims hiding in that one sentence, and they kept swapping between the strong ones and weak ones without noticing. Like, there's the reading where brains perform computations (some of them, sometimes), the reading where cognition as a whole is computation, the reading where the brain is a digital computer with a software/hardware separation like your laptop, the reading where the same "program" could run on silicon and preserve everything that matters about the mind, maybe even something about neural implementation vs abstract function. I can't tell which of these are near-platitudes, which are live scientific hypotheses, and which are actually false or confused, and I suspect different people mean different ones when they say it.

Can you lay out the distinct precise theses that "the brain is just a computer" could be expressing -- ordered roughly from weakest to strongest -- and for each say whether it's trivial, substantive but defensible, or false/misleading? And crucially, tell me which of those readings would actually support my friend's uploading argument if it were true, and whether the circularity charge sticks for whichever reading he'd need. I want to bring the list to the next hangout where this inevitably comes up again.
