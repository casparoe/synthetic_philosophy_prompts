My friends keep telling me I have a weird internal rule about when it's ok to just say things, so I wrote down my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases and now I want to know if there's actually a principle behind them or if I'm just making it up as I go. Here's the list.

I ask my wife if the pharmacy is still open, and without checking I say "yeah, they close at nine" -- I'm remembering the schedule from like two weeks ago. Fine. I tell a guest "there's oat milk in the fridge" without looking. Fine. A coworker asks if the meeting is at 3 and I say yes after a half-second of trying to recall the email. Fine. My brother, who's visiting, says "the bus to downtown comes every ten minutes" based on riding it once three years ago. Not fine, that one bugs me, he should've checked.

I tell my nephew "you'll definitely win the raffle" when he's holding one of a hundred tickets. Not fine. Same nephew asks if his drawing is good and I say yes. Fine (and honestly it wasn't great). A friend about to pet a dog says "he's friendly, right?" and I say "yeah" based purely on the dog's face. Not fine. Someone at trivia confidently announces "the answer is Casablanca" when it's actually To Have and Have Not, they were just mixing up Bogart movies. Not fine, obviously. But here's one that complicates whatever story covers that: I once told a friend "the library is open till midnight" going off the old schedule, the schedule HAD actually changed the day before, but due to some staffing thing they hadn't switched yet, so it was open till midnight anyway and everything I said was true -- I still feel like that was fine, even though my reason was bogus.

Also: my dentist says "this won't hurt" and it does, a little. I don't hold that against her, she had every reason to believe it. But a psychic saying "you'll meet someone tall" with the same confidence seems totally not fine, even though psychics kind of also believe what they say?

So -- is there a single principle that gets all these? I've heard philosophers argue about whether you need to KNOW something to assert it, vs it just being TRUE, vs you sincerely BELIEVING it, but every one of those fails on at least one of my cases (the dog one kills "sincere belief", the library one is awkward for "knowledge", the dentist one is awkward for... something). Can you construct a principle that saves most of my verdicts, tell me honestly which of them it has to throw out, and then tell me what it predicts on the case I keep going back and forth on: my doctor saying "it's just a cold" when she hasn't ruled out some rare thing, but where basically no doctor ever would run those tests at that point. Fine or not fine? I genuinely can't tell and I want to know what my own rule, whatever it is, commits me to.
