me and some friends in a rational choice reading group have been going through the classic money pump argument: if your preferences are cyclic, some trader can move you around the cycle, taking a little money at each step until you end up where you started but poorer, so acyclicity must be a requirement of rationality. fine. but every version we read quietly assumes your preferences stay fixed while the pump runs. and it occured to me that you could maybe get pumped dry without ever being cyclic at any single moment, if your preferences just drift over time in some perfectly ordinary way and the trader gets to come back for repeated visits. the intransitivity never appears because at each trade the agent's preferences look fine.

so build me that case. one paragraph, everyday scale and realistic — food, apartments, subscriptions, stuff like that, no casinos and no weird framing tricks where the reversal comes from how the options are redescribed rather than from the agent. the preference change itself has to be the engine, and crucially it should not be stipulated as irrational: I want something like satiation or habituation, the kind of change any fully rational person undergoes. the agent should be acyclic and sensible-looking at every point in time and still end up systematically poorer.

after the scenario, walk through the machinery: show the actual sequence of trades, verify the agent is acyclic at each moment, and tell me which structural feature is really doing the work (my guess is it's the trader's access at multiple times rather than any defect in the agent, but tell me if I'm wrong). then tell me what this does to the standard lesson — if acyclic-at-each-time can still be drained, is the actual requirement something diachronic, like acyclicity across time-slices or a constraint on how fast your preferences may shift? and finally, how would a defender of the classic money pump argument repair the conclusion given a case like this, and does the repair succeed — or does it quietly swap 'don't be intransitive' for 'don't be predictable,' which seems like a much stranger requirement? because if it really comes down to predictability, that would mean any agent whose preference changes can be anticipated is exploitable, rational or not, and that last point is the thing I actually want to figure out, so don't skip it.
