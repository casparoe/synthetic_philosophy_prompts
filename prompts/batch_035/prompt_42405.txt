I'm putting together a creative writing assignment for a philosophy seminar on well-being, and I'd like you to write a short story (roughly 800–1200 words) exploring Robert Nozick's experience machine thought experiment, but with a twist I haven't seen treated much: partial immersion. In the standard thought experiment, a person chooses between reality and a machine that gives them a perfectly pleasurable simulated life. In my story, the protagonist splits their time — say, waking hours in the machine and evenings back in their ordinary life, or alternating weeks — and gradually becomes uncertain about which regime of existence they actually prefer. What interests me is the way the choice looks different when it's not all-or-nothing: the machine's pleasures may cheapen by contrast with the ordinary days, or the ordinary days may come to feel like a dutiful chore between sessions, or the protagonist may lose track of which memories and attachments belong to which life. I'd like the story to take a definite stance on what ultimately happens to the protagonist — please don't leave it ambiguous as a cop-out — and to dramatize some specific theory of well-being (hedonism, desire satisfaction, or objective-list) through the protagonist's trajectory rather than by having characters lecture at each other.

A bit of setting to seed things: the protagonist is a piano tuner named Ilse in a mid-sized city, and the machine is accessed through a modest, unglamorous clinic — I want the story to resist the usual neon-dystopia aesthetics of simulated-reality fiction. The other characters should include at least one person who has refused the machine entirely and one who has gone fully under, so the partial-immersion case is framed against the two poles Nozick originally contrasted. It's fine if the story leans on a contested reading of Nozick — for instance, the objection that his intuition pump tests our fear of deception rather than our commitments about well-being — but I want that to be a deliberate choice on your part, not an accident.

After the story, please append a structured summary as a JSON object with exactly these fields: "title" (the story's title), "theory_dramatized" (the single theory of well-being the story most centrally engages, and one sentence on how), "stance_taken" (a one-sentence statement of the definite position the story commits to about partial immersion), "liberties" (an array of two or four items, each a brief note on where the story takes liberties with Nozick's setup or leans on a contested interpretation of it), and "decisive_scene" (a one-sentence description of the scene that carries the story's verdict).
