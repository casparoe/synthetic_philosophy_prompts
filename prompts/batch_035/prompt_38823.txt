I teach an intro-level ethics course and we're about to hit the unit on consent (medical, sexual, and contractual consent — I want all three in one frame, since the students will notice the differences anyway). Rather than lecturing on the standard accounts and letting them pick a favorite, I want to flip it: give them the spec first. So here's what I want from you. Write out the requirements that any adequate theory of when consent is valid (i.e., actually transforms what would otherwise be wrongful into permissible) has to satisfy, with the motivating case behind each one. By way of seeding, here are the sorts of puzzles I want the spec to be able to handle, since my students keep generating variants of them and I never have a clean answer: a patient agrees to join a clinical trial believing it's mostly for their own treatment benefit even though the consent form never said that and nobody at the clinic thinks they said it either; a massage therapist who, over months of sessions, cultivates a client's emotional attachment and only then proposes a sexual relationship — no false statement was ever made, no threat was ever made, and the client says yes and even insists they mean it, but something still smells; a person who marries someone who, unknown to both, would have called the whole thing off if they'd known about a hidden affair — no deceiver, yet we're tempted to say the consent was defective; and the sweatshop-adjacent case where someone "freely" takes a terrible job because every alternative open to them is worse, where the person pressuring them isn't any agent at all but just their circumstances. Those four shouldn't get four unrelated ad hoc treatments — the spec should say what they share and how they differ.

A few specific things I want you to take a stand on rather than just laying out options. First, is there a single validity condition (say, uncoerced, competent, informed choice) or does consent valid for one purpose become invalid for another, and is that a feature of consent or just of our sloppy vocabulary? Second, distinguish for me which requirements are non-negotiable and which are actually just the preferences of one camp — e.g., whether mere circumstances can coerce, or whether wrongfulness-of-the-pressure vs. causal-effect-of-the-pressure is a substantive dispute or a verbal one. Third, and this is the part I care about most: tell me whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, the way I suspect the "fully informed" and "actually obtained from ordinary humans" requirements might be — since nobody who understood everything about the trial, the relationship, and the job market in the cases above would consent the way the people in them did, and yet the world is set up so that we need their consent anyway. If some requirements can't be met together, say which ones you'd sacrifice and why.

And don't give me a neutral survey; I want you to commit to a spec, defend the ranking of requirements, and own the tradeoffs, even where you're not certain. If some part of your answer depends on a contested judgement call, flag it inline as contested but still make the call.
