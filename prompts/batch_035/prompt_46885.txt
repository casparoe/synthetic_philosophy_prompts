I'm drafting a statement of my epistemological position for a graduate seminar on the structure of justification, and I'd like a consistency audit before I submit it. Here is the position:

1. The regress of reasons is genuine, and the demand driving it is correct: whenever one belief derives its justificatory support from another belief, that supporting belief must itself have justificatory support of its own. Nothing justifies for free.
2. Foundationalism fails: there are no self-justifying beliefs, and the idea of basic beliefs whose justification is somehow intrinsic to them is a myth.
3. Coherentism fails as well: coherence among beliefs cannot generate justification that none of them possesses — a conspiracy theory whose parts hang perfectly together is not thereby one bit more justified.
4. That leaves infinitism, which I accept: the right structure for justification is an infinite series of supporting reasons, each giving support to the one before it, and such a series is benign rather than vicious.
5. Since ought implies can, and finite agents cannot complete infinite tasks, the series needs only to be available rather than actual: I discharge my justificatory obligations by being able to go on producing further reasons, one at a time, on demand — no completion is ever required.
6. At the same time, I hold that justificatory work is done only by considerations that actually operate in the subject's cognitive life. A reason the subject has never entertained, does not now recall, and does not in any way deploy cannot contribute to justifying anything; mere possibilities don't justify.
7. And I hold that my current visual experience of the lamp on my desk justifies my belief that there is a lamp on my desk, here and now, without my needing any belief about that experience or its reliability. I am not prepared to concede that no one has ever actually been justified in believing anything.

Before I send this off, I want to know whether it hangs together. Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with which others, and which pairs only look like they conflict but come apart on inspection? For each genuine tension, tell me how severe it is and what would have to give. Please order the tensions by severity rather than presenting a flat list, and identify the single revision that would resolve the most at the lowest cost. Finally, tell me whether the position, once repaired, is still recognizably infinitism — or whether the repairs quietly turn it into one of the views I claim to reject.
