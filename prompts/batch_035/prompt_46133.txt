My friend Maya and I had a stupidly long argument last night (like 1am, dishes not done, both of us googling on our phones) about what actually counts as intelligent, and I've been writing down my gut reactions to a bunch of cases to figure out what I actually believe. Here's the list. Stockfish beating the world champion at chess: intelligent, but somehow in a thinner way than a dog is. My phone calculator: obviously not intelligent, and Maya says that's arbitrary since it's the same kind of program. A crow bending a wire into a hook to pull food out of a tube: intelligent, honestly more impressive to me than the chess engine. Google Maps rerouting me around a crash: not intelligent. A thermostat: lol no. An ant colony building the shortest bridge between two branches: not intelligent, even though I'd struggle to do the geometry that fast. Slime mold spreading through a maze and finding the shortest path (real thing, look it up if you don't know it): I genuinely can't decide, and I don't know why it feels different from the ants. A rat learning a maze: intelligent the first time, but it doesn't feel MORE intelligent after the fiftieth run even though it's gotten way better at the maze. A person following a scam script on the phone who's very convincing but adapting nothing: not intelligent, which is awkward, because a fixed script doing well in a varied world is... sort of what the ant colony is? My friend who can't do basic arithmetic but reads a room better than anyone I've ever met: intelligent, full stop. My grandmother in her late dementia years who could still absolutely clean you out at cards: intelligent. A chatbot that consoled me for half an hour after my dog died and said things no one had thought to say to me: I flip-flop on this one every time.

So that's my pile of verdicts. Is there one principle that captures all of them, or close? The two things I most want sorted out: (1) the Stockfish vs calculator thing — I feel a real difference but can't say what it is, and whatever I say there is going to bite me on the crow too, since the crow is presumably "just" running evolved machinery the way Stockfish just runs search. (2) The slime mold vs Google Maps thing — they're doing basically the same computation and my gut treats them completely differently, which makes me suspect my gut is a hypocrite. Please also tell me what the principle says about a case I left out, like a self-driving car in fog, and be honest about which of my verdicts it just can't save — I'd rather lose one than keep them all with some bloated four-clause exception-ridden rule.
