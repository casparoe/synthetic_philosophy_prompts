the exam question was: "Explain the 'Meno problem': why is knowledge more valuable than a merely true belief? Outline Plato's response in the Meno, and briefly assess one modern reply."

and here's what Jamie wrote:

"Plato raises the problem in the Meno. Socrates points out that a man who knows the road to Larissa and a man who merely has a true belief about the road will get you there equally well, so long as the belief is true — a true belief is just as good a guide as knowledge. Plato then compares true beliefs to the statues of Daedalus: if you don't tie them down they run away, and a belief is tied down by working out the reason why it is true. Once tethered, it becomes knowledge and stays put. So Plato's solution in the Meno is essentially the justified true belief analysis: knowledge is justified true belief, where the justification is the account of the cause. Gettier showed in 1963 that this analysis is incorrect, but this does not affect the value point: Gettier cases show you can have justified true belief that is not knowledge, so the extra value of knowledge must come from the absence of luck. Since lucky beliefs are less likely to be true, knowledge is more valuable. For a modern reply, reliabilism says knowledge is true belief produced by a reliable method, and because a reliable method gets things right far more often than an unreliable one, the person with knowledge is right far more often in the future. This solves the value problem: the knower is a better guide going forward, which is why we prefer knowledge."
