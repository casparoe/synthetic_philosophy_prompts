I've been trying to sort out what I actually believe about whether things "just are" their smaller parts, going off my gut on cases. Water being wet: fine, that's nothing but molecules bumping about. Temperature: same, just molecules moving fast, I'm happy to say that. A traffic jam on the M25: I can't bring myself to say it's "nothing but cars", even though thats literally what its made of — it seems like a pattern the cars make, and you need the pattern to explain why everyones late. A stock market crash: I'd say it was caused by panic, not by particle physics, even though I also believe physics underlies everything, so these two thoughts sit awkwardly together. Me feeling sad: I'll say its just brain chemistry, yet I balk at saying it in the same flat way I say water is H2O — with the water it feels like the whole story, with the sadness it feels like something's been left out. And a game of chess: real to me, winning genuinely matters, even though every piece is just bits of wood. Is there one principle behind all these verdicts, or close? If yes, what does it say about the case I keep going back and forth on — whether a hurricane is "nothing but" swirling air, given we name them like animals and they seem to have a life of their own? And if the principle can't save all my verdicts, tell me honestly which one has to go.
