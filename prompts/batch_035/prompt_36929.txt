I'm moderating the Q&A after a talk by a psychologist who defends a broadly Fodorian view: that early vision is informationally encapsulated, and that the apparent top-down effects reported in the literature are in fact effects of attention, memory, judgment, or post-perceptual response biases rather than genuine cases of cognition penetrating perception. Draft nine to eleven interview questions that engage the actual substance of her position rather than inviting her to restate it. I want at least one question on each of the following, each with a follow-up keyed to the reply she is most likely to give: (i) the so-called el Greco fallacy objection, i.e., the worry that a perceptual distortion cannot be the right explanation of some top-down effect if the same distortion would cancel out when applied to both the stimulus and the comparison standard; (ii) the methodological worry that signal-detection analyses alone cannot cleanly separate perceptual sensitivity shifts from criterion shifts, and whether her side can do better than diagnose confounds case by case; (iii) the well-known finding that Russian speakers, whose language lexically distinguishes light and dark blue, discriminate those shades faster, and whether the impenetrability thesis forces her to treat such language effects as confined to non-visual stages; and (iv) whether the empirical dispute even matters for the epistemologist's use of cognitive penetration in arguments that penetrated experience can rationally be downgraded as a source of justification — or whether the philosophical interest survives even if all real effects turn out to be judgment-level. Please make the questions follow a deliberate arc, moving from the methodological core to the philosophical consequences, and end with the single case you think would be hardest for her to defuse, phrased so that a generic appeal to task demands or post-perceptual processing would not by itself answer it.
