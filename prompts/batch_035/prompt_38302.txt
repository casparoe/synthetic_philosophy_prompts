I've been trying to figure out where I actually stand on speech limits by just going case by case with my gut, and now I want someone smarter than me to tell me whether there's a principle under my verdicts or whether I'm just making it up as I go. Here's my list. Telling a crowd outside a nervous man's house that he's a pedophile, knowing he isn't: obviously not ok, should be stoppable. Writing an op-ed arguing that some religion is false and its adherents are wasting their lives: fine, even though I know some adherents are genuinely devastated by that kind of thing. Publishing the home addresses of abortion doctors alongside rhetoric calling them murderers: not fine. A comedian doing a bit built on racial stereotypes: fine. A telegram channel posting the same stereotypes as facts along with instructions to go harass specific people: not fine. Gambling ads during live sports aimed at adults: fine, barely. Same ads placed in a kids' app: not fine. Telling a specific suicidal person they should do it: not fine. A website laying out the philosophical case for a right to die, including practical info: fine, and I'd defend that one pretty hard. Publishing detailed instructions for making weapons at home: fine? I honestly go back and forth. And here's the pair that bugs me most: publishing true but illegally leaked info about a politician's health — I say fine. Publishing true medical records of a random private citizen — I say not fine, even though the distress caused is comparable or worse for the politician.

I've tried to stuff these into Mill's harm principle, the version where speech is only restrictable to prevent harm to others, and I can't make it work without stretching 'harm' so far it feels dishonest. The two stereotype cases don't split on harms at all — they seem to split on something about what the speaker is *doing*, maybe? And the health-records pair doesn't split on harm either, or not cleanly. Falsity isn't doing any work there.

So, what I want: tell me if there's one principle (or a small set of principles) that captures most of my verdicts, state it as precisely as you can, and for each clause tell me which of my cases is motivating it. Then be honest about which of my verdicts your principle has to overturn, and why it's that one and not another. Also, if the principle can't cover everything and I have to accept an ad hoc-looking exception somewhere, I'd rather know which exception does the least damage. Finally, tell me what the principle says about two cases I haven't listed but keep getting asked about: nonconsensual deepfake porn of a real person, and ads for conversion therapy aimed at adults who say they want it.
