I'm a phil mind PhD student, and my reading group has spent the better part of a year going in circles on the Chase and Sanborn thing — the quining qualia idea that qualia are an incoherent posit, so what we want is an error theory about them rather than either eliminativism about consciousness or some vindication of intrinsic qualitative character. Two members in particular have been talking past each other the whole time, and I want to try something differnet: draft the joint statement the two of them should have produced by now, as if they'd done a proper adversarial collab.

The two positions, as best I can state them. One of them (call her the error theorist) thinks experiences are perfectly real and have determinate contents — quality spaces, similarity structure, whatever it is that gets us to discriminate and act — but the supposed intrinsic, ineffable, directly-given qualitative character is a posit that no coherent theory can supply, roughly the way the moral error theorist thinks objective prescriptivity can't be coherently supplied. The other one (call him the qualia defender) accepts the quality space story as far as it goes but holds that intrinsic phenomenal character isn't a theoretical postulate at all — it's what's presented in introspection, and the special from-the-inside concepts (recognitional, quotational ones) explain why it *looks* ineffable and private without it being a fiction.

What I want from you, concretely:

1. A numbered section of claims both could sign their names to — including about what the quality space machinery genuinely explains, and about what introspection is good for (both agree it isn't infallible, I think?).
2. A disagreements table where each residual disagreement is phrased so that its own side would accept the wording. Neither vacuous nor slanted — this is the part they always screw up.
3. The cruxes. My guesses: one is whether the analogy with moral error theory is doing real work or is just decoration — i.e., whether the incoherence charge rests on a substantive account of what intrinsic qualitative characters would have to be like, or only on claims about our concepts. Another is explanatory: the error theorist says the intrinsic qualities would be explanitorily idle, the defender says they earn their keep by fixing *which* similarity structure is the relevant one. Feel free to tell me I'm wrong about which disagreements are cruxes and which are downstream or just verbal — if part of the dispute is about words, say which part and why.
4. For each crux, a concrete case or argument both sides would accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Two I'd like covered: what each of them says about someone gaining a new discriminated quality (learning to genuinely tell two near-identical shades apart after training), and whether the error theorist's own formulation, which seems to lean on how things seem to her, is self-undermining. Add others if you think they're better tests.

Please don't turn this into a survey or dump a reading list on me — I want the actual document, the thing they could both put their names on with the leftover disagreement made exact.
