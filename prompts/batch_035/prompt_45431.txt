I do control engineering for a living — feedback loops, state-space models, stability margins, that's basically my native language. Recently I got into the old cyclical theories of history: Polybius' anacyclosis (the monarchy → tyranny → aristocracy → oligarchy → democracy → mob rule carousel), Ibn Khaldun's dynasty cycle where group solidarity (asabiyyah) builds up, lets a dynasty conquer everything, then decays over roughly four generations, Spengler with his fixed civilizational life stages, and Toynbee's challenge-and-response thing, though Toynbee is honestly the one I understand least. My persistent itch while reading all this is that it's secretly dynamical systems talk being done before the math existed. So do the translation for me.

Restate each of these theories in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can and one at a time. Tell me what the state variables are (is asabiyyah a single scalar? is the anacyclosis better modeled as a finite state machine with a forced transition order than as anything continuous?), what plays the role of the plant, what the forcing function is, whether each one is a limit cycle, a relaxation oscillator, a transient response to some disturbance, or just a metaphor dressed up as dynamics. Spengler's claim that the stages are fixed and the timing is roughly predictable — is that a claim about period and amplitude, and is it even coherent without a way to measure the state? Then give me the residue report, because honestly that's the part I care about most: which parts of these theories map cleanly, which parts you had to distort to squeeze them into feedback/control language, and which parts can't be said in my vocabulary at all. I'm thinking Toynbee's teleology, the whole normative layer about whether a turn of the cycle is bad, maybe the way these writers treat the "system" as having a purpose — but you tell me.

And then two specific questions I keep chewing on. One, my hunch is that a lot of the residue isn't a limitation of my vocabulary but a sign that the cyclical theorists were vaguer than they sounded — no well-defined state variables, no observable outputs, no stability claim anyone could test — but check that hunch, because I'm obviously motivated to find their models under-specified rather than my own. Two, there's the reflexivity problem: actual kings and revolutionaries read Polybius and Ibn Khaldun and behaved differently because of it, and I don't know of a clean control-theoretic analog for a plant that reads the controller's model and changes its dynamics in response. Does that break the translation in a way even adaptive control doesn't fix, or is it just ordinary model mismatch and I'm overthinking it? Also happy to hear where the linear/progressive view of history is easier or harder to translate than the cyclical one — I suspect the cyclical ones only look more dynamical and the linear one is actually the easier fit, but I could have that backwards.
