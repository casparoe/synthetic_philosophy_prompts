Last month my first child was born, and the experience has made me unwilling to keep living as though my individual choices are causally inert — but it has also put me in a series of arguments I am losing. At a family dinner my brother-in-law, an economist, dismantled my resolutions with what I have since learned is called the inefficacy objection: my one transatlantic flight does not cause any emissions that would not have occurred anyway; the chicken I decline to buy shifts no crates, because supermarkets place orders in batches far larger than my purchases; my single vote is almost never pivotal, and in a close national election the probability of one vote deciding the outcome is plausibly on the order of one in tens of millions; therefore whatever I owe the world's future children, it is not private abstinence. He is not merely rationalizing — the arithmetic behind his challenge is, as far as I can tell, largely correct. But I have read enough fragments of the literature to suspect the objection proves too much: taken literally, it seems to dissolve every personal contribution question into the conclusion that only collective action matters, and I do not know how to live inside that conclusion while telling my daughter why we do or don't do things.

Please convert whatever the strongest philosophical treatment of this problem is into a decision procedure I can actually run, alone, before choices of this kind: I have no background in philosophy, I am comfortable with arithmetic and rough probability estimates, and I need something that takes under an hour per decision. I want an ordered set of questions, criteria for what counts as a sufficient answer to each, a point at which the procedure stops with a verdict, and points at which it hands the decision back to me because the remaining question is one of values rather than facts. Then run it on three cases from my actual life: (1) an annual transatlantic flight to visit my aging parents, where I could purchase certified carbon offsets; (2) whether to extend my current weekday vegetarianism to full-time, given that poultry supply chains apparently move in batch sizes large enough that my individual purchases plausibly change nothing at the margin for years at a time; and (3) voting in national elections, in a district that is not remotely competitive.

The procedure should meet the following specifications:

- It must distinguish, and say which of them is doing the work at each step: (a) my act being causally pivotal; (b) my act being one of a set of individually non-threshold-crossing acts that are jointly sufficient for a harm, such that no single act makes a verifiable difference and yet something is wrong with each of them; (c) my act being evidence about what similarly situated people will do, which is not the same as my act causing theirs; and (d) considerations about the kind of agent I become by participating in or abstaining from a practice, while being honest that this last consideration is precisely the sort the inefficacy objector refuses to grant any weight.
- It must handle the offsetting question explicitly: whether donating to a carbon-removal or mitigation organization conditional on flying is best understood as (i) causally preventing an equivalent harm elsewhere so that the net effect nets out, (ii) an evidential signal to other agents and institutions, or (iii) a licensing mechanism that makes flight easier for me while doing nothing I could defend to the objector — and it must tell me which of these the procedure actually tracks and why.
- It must include at least one worked case where the procedure returns an answer I will not like, and at least one where it honestly returns undecidable and hands the call back to my judgment, naming the value question it is refusing to settle.
- It should be explicit about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as arithmetic, and it should close by stating plainly the one thing the procedure cannot do — my suspicion is that it cannot, at any step, spare me from having to decide what sort of person to be, but I would like to know whether that suspicion survives being operationalized rather than merely asserted.
