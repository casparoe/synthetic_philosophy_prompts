I'm the facilitator for a two-year adversarial collaboration between a philosopher in Daniel Dennett's compatibilist camp and one in Gregg Caruso's free-will-skeptic camp. Their topic is just deserts and punishment: whether anyone ever basically deserves to suffer for wrongdoing, and what should follow for how societies blame, sanction, and incarcerate. Draft the joint statement they will both sign.

Stipulated background, to be treated as the collaboration's fixed working assumptions rather than claims you need to defend: both parties accept that determinism alone does not settle the question — the skeptic holds that basic-desert responsibility is incompatible with determinism and also with the kinds of indeterminism on offer, so appeal to physics cannot rescue desert; the compatibilist holds that a modest, non-retributive desert is compatible with determinism. Both reject the fatalist view that differences in character and choice don't matter. Both accept that dangerous conduct may legitimately be prevented, contained, and answered; that some actual punishment practices, especially carceral ones, exceed whatever either theory licenses; and that the everyday benefits of the responsibility system — trust, moral formation, motivation — are a real constraint on any revision. Both also accept the manipulation data point: as cases are smoothed step by step from ordinary deliberation toward covert manipulation, at some point intuitions abandon responsibility, and neither side can simply dig in at the boundary without argument.

The document must contain:

1. A numbered agreed-claims section. Go beyond what either camp's slogans predict. Both can sign claims about, for instance: what capacity differences license in how agents may be treated; why suffering should never be inflicted for its own sake; the sense in which a wrongdoer's history is causally explanatory without being desert-conferring; and what any acceptable sanction regime owes to the person sanctioned. Include the points of contact that the two camps' public rhetoric obscures.

2. A disagreements table. For each disagreement, give the compatibilist's phrasing and the skeptic's phrasing side by side, each worded so its own side would sign it while the other would refuse it, together with a jointly acceptable label for the row. At least one row must concern the word deserve itself: the compatibilist's view that it can be deflated and kept, against the skeptic's charge that retaining it guarantees retributive inflation.

3. A cruxes section. I believe the cruxes include at least: (a) whether the benefits of the responsibility system depend on belief in basic desert, or only on thinner beliefs about answerability and agency; (b) whether the reactive attitudes — resentment, indignation, gratitude — can be selectively revised, keeping their moral-address function while shedding the suffering-because-deserved implication, or whether the package is all-or-nothing; (c) whether the skeptic's quarantine-style replacement for desert-based punishment smuggles desert back in whenever it rations confinement by seriousness of past wrongdoing rather than by forward-looking risk alone. Add further cruxes if you judge them real, but each must be stated so that settling it would move at least one side.

4. For each crux, at least one argument, case, or protocol that both sides agreed in advance would bear on it. Two are stipulated as part of this collaboration and must appear:

- The Last Retributivist: a thought experiment they built together in their second year — a future society in which belief in basic desert has fully faded, but the practice of holding answerable persists: evidence is aired, the wronged are heard, proportionate forward-looking responses are imposed, and recidivism, trust, and moral formation are measurably unaffected. The predictions you must record do not cleanly diverge: the compatibilist predicts the society has lost nothing that matters; the skeptic predicts the society has lost nothing but has also vindicated nothing the compatibilist wanted to keep, since the residual practice no longer confers what the compatibilist meant by desert. Say whether both sides noticed this, what they concluded about the scenario's evidential value, and whether they kept it in the final statement.

- The blame-maintenance study: a longitudinal protocol they agreed to treat as bearing on crux (a). Two otherwise-similar communities; in one, public discourse keeps ordinary desert language; in the other, it is replaced by forward-looking accountability language while the sanctions themselves are held fixed; both are followed on trust, compliance, moral formation of the young, and how the sanction regime itself drifts over a decade. Record each side's prediction in advance, including the secondary outcomes each side quietly cares about, and what each would concede if its prediction failed.

5. A closing section naming which residual disagreements, if any, the parties judged merely verbal — naming the specific words at issue if so — and which are substantive, and noting where the drafting process itself forced a verbal dispute into the open.

Throughout, you will be choosing between formulations that are safe but nearly vacuous and formulations that are sharp but that one side would refuse. Take the sharper option and make the call. Where you judge that the sharp formulation would be refused by one side, add a single bracketed clause naming which side balks and why, then move on; do not offer me alternative wordings to choose between. Finally, append a facilitator's note of your own, clearly marked as not part of the joint statement, in which you commit to a definite judgment as to which side's prediction for the blame-maintenance study is better supported by the claims both parties already accept, and state what would make you revise that judgment.
