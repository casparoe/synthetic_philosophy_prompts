My friends and I run a small wiki-ish project where we tag history-adjacent claims as "mainstream history," "fringe," or "pseudohistory," and I've slowly realized we've been doing it entirely by vibes. Atlantis is easy, Fomenko's new chronology is easy, ancient aliens is easy, but then it gets awkward fast: Gavin Menzies' 1421 stuff, the Solutrean hypothesis, Velikovsky — and worse, there are things that were once considered fringe and got rehabilitated, like continental drift in science, or in history things like the Younger Dryas impact hypothesis going from crackpot-adjacent to at least a serious debate in some circles. So before we tag another article I want the actual spec. Write the desiderata: what an adequate criterion for demarcating pseudohistory from merely heterodox or revisionist history has to deliver — the cases it has to classify correctly, the distinctions it absolutely cannot collapse (bad history vs. not-history-at-all, minority interpretation vs. methodologically broken work, revisionism vs. fabrication), what it should say about unfalsifiability, phantom or recycled evidence, conspiracy-shaped reasoning, and independence of corroborating sources — with the motivating case behind each requirement.

A couple of things I specifically want your take on. First, mark which desiderata are non-negotiable and which are basically one camp's prejudice smuggled in as a criterion — I suspect some Popper-flavored demands don't transfer cleanly, since historians can't do experiments and everything rests on sources with provenance, so maybe falsifiability isn't the right lens at all. Second, tell me whether you think some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable, because if the list can't all be met at once, that's actually the part I most want to know — it would tell us our tagging scheme needs a fourth category or something. And third, say briefly whether the criterion should be history-specific or just borrowed from philosophy of science with tweaks, since our one friend insists demarcation in history is basically the same problem and I'm not convinced. Don't bother scoring actual named pseudohistories against the list beyond using them as test cases, we'll do that ourselves on the wiki.
