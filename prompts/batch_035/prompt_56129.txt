I've been keeping a list of my gut reactions about when there's "something it's like" to be a thing, and I want to know if they add up to anything. A fish flopping on the dock after being hooked: something it's like, pretty confidently. A spider wrapping up its prey: I keep going back and forth but I guess no. Me sitting here with my glasses on my nose — until my wife asked if they were pinching, I hadn't noticed any pressure at all, but now that she mentions it, the pressure was "there" the whole time, or at least it feels like it was. Dreamless sleep: obviously nothing. A chess player I know who says a position can "smell wrong" before he can articualte why — honestly I think there is sometimes something it's like to think. And the one I can't shake: those two lines in the optical illusion where I've literally measured them with a ruler and they're the same length, and they still LOOK different, the looking doesn't care what I believe. That last one matters because it seems to show the "look" of things isn't just a belief — you can know better and the look carries on regardless.

Here's the wrinkle. I heard a podcast where a guy said maybe conciousness as a whole is like the desktop of a computer — the little folder icons are a handy simplification for the user, the machine underneath has no folders anywhere, and the picture on the screen is the thing that's kind of fake. So maybe "what it's like" is a story my brain tells me about itself, and the actual machinery inside doesn't have any feel to it at all. Part of me wants to say my list supports that — the glasses case shows the feel only shows up once something gets flagged for me, which sounds like it's being manufactured on demand. But the illusion-lines verdict cuts the other way — if the look survives my knowing better, then the look isn't just a judgment I'm making, it's got some independent standing. Those two gut reactions don't seem to want to live together, and I can't tell which one should win.

So: is there a principle behind my list, or close to one? If there is, what does it say about two cases I haven't decided — whether there's something it's like to be a newborn that hasn't had a single thought yet, and whether a really good chicken robot that behaves distressed has the thing or not? And be straight with me about which of my verdicts you had to bend or toss to make the principle work, where your own reasoning is most likely to go off the rails, and what consideration would honestly change your mind — like, if there's a way to explain the stubborn-looking illusion lines without giving the "look" any independent status, I'd want to hear it, because that's the case I'm most attached to. I don't need philosopher names or a reading list, just the actual thinking.
