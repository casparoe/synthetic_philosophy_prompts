I'm a macro PhD student and somewhere over the last couple of years I basically converted to Lakatos. Not as a history-of-science curiosity but as an actual working tool: whenever people in my group argue about whether DSGE is still worth doing after 2008 — the ad hoc patches, the financial frictions bolted on after the crisis, the versions that seem like they could fit anything — I keep thinking this is just what a degenerating problemshift looks like and everyone should say so out loud. And when the defenders push back that the rivals (the agent-based crowd, the HANK people) don't even have a hard core, I think fine, that's a Lakatosian point too. But lately I've been worrying about the layer above that: the framework I'm using to make these judgments. Does the progressive/degenerating distinction itself risk anything? What would actually count against it?

Some things gnawing at me specifically. What would the history of science have to look like for the appraisal to be doing no real work — are there cases where the right theory choice went the other way? I have a vague memory that Lakatos himself conceded the Copernican programme went a long time without novel predictions and that even Newton's had rough patches, which seems like trouble if you want to use the tool prospectively rather than in a rational reconstruction after the fact. And then there's his line about it being rational to stick with a degenerating programme until a rival overtakes it — I get that this blocks "instant rationality," but doesn't it also mean the methodology never actually tells anyone to do anything? So what I want is: the real defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward; which ones Lakatosians themselves would concede count against the view (I gather people tried to patch up the notion of a novel fact after him, which I always take as a bad sign for a concept); which ones are just Feyerabend-style jeering; and whether a framework that's explicitly retrospective and tolerant like this should even be expected to have falsifiers, or whether demanding them is a category error. Last thing, tell me honestly: if I can always win an argument against anyone excited about new theory by calling their programme degenerating-but-not-yet-dead, have I adopted something that can't lose, and is that a problem?
