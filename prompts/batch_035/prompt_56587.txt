Something I keep circling back to and can't sort out myself. I work adjacent to public health policy, and I've been trying to figure out what our drug laws are quietly assuming about people. Consider three practices side by side. Portugal since 2001 has decriminalized personal possession of all drugs: someone caught with a small amount goes before a dissuasion commission that can fine them, suspend a professional license, or push them toward treatment, but using drugs is no longer a crime, while selling any of it stays a serious criminal offense. Meanwhile a bunch of US states now run fully legal recreational cannabis markets — taxed, advertised, corporate supply chains, treated basically like alcohol. And yet in those same states possession of heroin or meth can still put you in prison for years. What view of the person, or of the state, do these arrangements presuppose? The Portuguese setup seems to say drug use is a health issue rather than a moral failing, but the commissions still coerce people into treatment and impose fines, which feels like punishing an illness — or at least like paternalism aimed at people who presumably chose to use. The US cannabis model looks like it presupposes consumer sovereignty, informed adults may choose a risky product, but the very same state denies heroin users that standing, and I genuinely can't tell whether that's because heroin users are being deemed incompetent to consent to the risk, or because their costs are assumed to spill onto others, or just historical accident dressed up as principle. And what about Portugal keeping the ban on sale — is the implicit thought that users are patients and therefore excused, while dealers exploit the patients and are therefore fully responsible for the same substance? Can you reconstruct the implicit theory or theories here, tell me which rival views of paternalism, consent, and responsibility these practices would actually rule out, and whether the pieces are even consistant with each other or just reflect different historical compromises? Also please say where the practices are underdetermined, in the sense that several different philosophical theories would happily license the same arrangement, so we can't read one off them. And lastly, what would concretely have to change if a jurisdiction tried to rebuild all this on a pure harm-to-others principle — would the dissuasion commissions survive at all? Write it as plain continuous prose please, like a long email, no headings or bullet points or tables or anything like that. Oh and sorry in advance if this is a muddle, its not my home turf.
