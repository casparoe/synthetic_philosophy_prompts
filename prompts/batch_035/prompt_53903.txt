My game theory lecturer tried to summarise iterated elimination of dominated strategies for us, and I want to know how much of his gloss to trust. What he said was roughly: a dominated strategy is one that some other strategy beats no matter what the opponents do, so no rational player would ever play one; therefore you can strip dominated strategies out of a game one round at a time, and since each individual round only removes stuff nobody would do anyway, the whole procedure is beyond reproach. He added that the order you delete in doesn't matter, and that when the process leaves each player with a single strategy, that profile is the unique prediction of rational play and the only equilibrium of the game. His main example was the second-price auction, where he claimed bidding your true value dominates every other bid. The thing is, he never once distinguished weak from strict dominance, and I have a feeling that matters somewhere in there — like maybe for the claim about order not mattering, or for "no rational player would ever play one". Please go through his summary and tell me which parts a game theorist would sign off on, which are quietly conflating two different notions, and whether the conclusion about a unique prediction survives once you sort out whatever is wrong. Oh, and please write your answer in British English.
