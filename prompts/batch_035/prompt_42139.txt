ok so over the past year I've basically converted to situationism about character, and I want to stress-test it. My view: there aren't robust, cross-situational global traits like honesty, courage, or compassion — what explains most of the variance in how people behave is the situation (time pressure, mood, who's watching, ambient details), and trait-attribution is a story we invent afterwards. The evidence that converted me is the usual parade: the seminary study where being in a hurry predicted helping more than anything dispositional, Milgram's obedience results, those 1920s studies where kids who cheated on one test were barely more likely to cheat on the next, Mischel's old point that trait measures rarely correlate above ~.3 with actual behavior, plus more recent stuff — mood effects on helping, and the way whole literatures like ego depletion collapsed when people actually tried to replicate them, which I read as situationism winning by attrition.

Here's what I want: a map of what would count against the view, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, and I want it to include things that have arguably already happened, not just hypotheticals. Some things I'd specifically like you to take seriously (a) the psychometric stuff — Big Five measures show real stability over decades and even predict life outcomes; do I have to swallow that, and does it hurt me or not? (b) cross-cultural cases where dispositional explanation seems to be doing genuine work; (c) the philosophical counterattack — people like Kamtekar, and Alfano on factitious virtue, arguing that situationists set the bar for a "trait" absurdly high, that the trait concept actually in use is indexed-to-situations anyway. Is that a fair hit, or is it the situationist quietly retreating into unfalsifiability? Relatedly, give me an honest assessment of whether my view has been qualified to the point where nothing could ever count against it — and whether that would even be a problem for a view of this kind, or whether it's fine for it to be mostly a view about explanation rather than prediction.
