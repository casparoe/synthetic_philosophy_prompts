I've been going down the rabbit hole of the abolish-suffering-through-biotech stuff (the David Pearce hedonistic imperative people) and I keep noticing my gut gives totally different verdicts on cases that look like they should be the same case. I want to know if there's an actual principle in there somewhere or if I'm just flailing. Here are my verdicts:

- Editing the genome so future kids can't feel chronic pain: obviously yes, sign me up.
- Replacing the raw nastiness of pain with a "gradient of wellbeing" — pain still carries all its information, it just doesn't hurt: yes, honestly this is the part I like most about the whole program.
- Straight-up wireheading, the electrode bliss button: no. Feels like death while still technically alive.
- Deleting the capacity for mild disappointment so nobody ever feels let down: also no, and weirdly this bothers me MORE than deleting chronic pain, which makes no sense to me.
- Insects that maybe have some ultra-primitive version of suffering: I feel basically nothing, pro or con.
- Predators in nature reserves: I'd redesign them if it were cheap and easy, but I wouldn't lose sleep if we never got around to it.
- Livestock: yes, abolish their suffering, and I notice I care way more per cow than per wild deer, even though the deer probably suffers more total.
- Me: I'd take the gradient-of-wellbeing upgrade in a heartbeat, but I wouldn't take a pill that just made me indifferent to whether my life goes well or badly.

So is there one principle that generates all of these? My half-formed candidate is something like "keep the informational function, delete the affective sting" — but does that survive the disappointment case, or does it eat my verdict on wireheading too (since wireheading presumably doesn't destroy any information, just floods it)? And it feels like the insect and deer cases need some other machinery entirely, maybe something about replaceability or tractability, I can't tell. Give me your best principle or set of principles, tell me which of my verdicts it can't capture and would force me to give up, and then tell me what it predicts for the case I keep going back and forth on: suppose we could delete grief without touching memory or love at all — keep it or delete it?
