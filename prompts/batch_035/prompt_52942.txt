I host a history podcast, and for the season finale I've booked two guests who've been fighting about the British Raj for years — one basically a Niall Ferguson-style apologist (empire brought rule of law, infrastructure, and plugged India into the world economy), the other a Shashi Tharoor-style critic (drain of wealth, deindustrialization, famines that happened with railways already in place). They've finally agreed to sit down and write one document they can both put their names on, and I want to hand them a draft to react to. Structure it as: (1) claims both accept, starting with the raw numbers both sides have to live with — India's share of world GDP and manufacturing at the start of the period vs at the end — so the fight is over interpretation and not the spreadsheet; (2) a disagrement table where each row is worded so its own side would actually sign it; (3) the cruxes. My hunches on the cruxes: the counterfactual comparator (benchmark against Japan and Siam, or against what a Maratha-dominated subcontinent would plausibly have done?); how much of Indian deindustrialization is explained by colonial policy once you notice China's manufacturing share also collapsed without being formally colonized; whether railways built to move raw material to ports still count as a legacy gift because the tracks are still there; and whether famine mortality was actually better or worse under the Raj then under the regimes it replaced, given that railways existed and relief still failed. For each crux, write down in advance what would count as bearing on it and what each side would predict. One last section, and take it seriously: where is this whole statement most likely to be wrong — the assumptions both of them share that could make their agreement an artifact. I'm suspicious of two in particular: both taking GDP per capita as the measure of the balance sheet, and both letting 1750 be the baseline as though Mughal decline hadn't already begun. And if part of their dispute turns out to be really about the word "benefit," say which part and why.
