I've been invited to submit a position statement on scientific realism for a philosophy of science workshop, and before I circulate it I want to know whether the package of views I've drifted into is internally stable. Here is what I currently commit to:

(1) The no-miracles argument is sound in spirit: the novel predictive success of our mature theories is best explained by those theories being at least approximately true.

(2) The pessimistic induction is also sound in spirit: the central theoretical posits of most past empirically successful theories — phlogiston, caloric, the electromagnetic ether — turned out not to exist, and no one has given a principled reason to think our current posits differ in kind.

(3) Structural realism is the correct synthesis of (1) and (2): what is preserved across theory change, and what we should believe, is structure. The transition from Fresnel's wave optics to Maxwell's theory is the paradigm case, since the mathematics survived while the ontology it was about was abandoned.

(4) Newman's objection is fatal to pure structural realism: the claim that there exist some objects standing in relations with the form of our theory is, over a large enough domain, close to trivially true, so a structural realism worth defending must say considerably more than that.

(5) Hacking's manipulability criterion is right: where experimenters can intervene on an entity and deploy it as a tool to produce effects elsewhere, we are entitled to believe in that entity quite independently of the fate of the larger theory — belief in electrons rests on being able to spray them, not on any theory of the electron being true.

(6) Theory change involves genuine losses and referential drift, but radical Kuhnian incommensurability is false — and my reason for thinking so is precisely that the mathematics carries over, as in the Fresnel-to-Maxwell case.

Please audit this set rather than lecture me about each item in isolation. Which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, and which only look like it? Rank any real tensions by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what giving it would cost me dialectically. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Finally, tell me whether the position that survives is best read as epistemic structural realism, ontic structural realism, or neither — and if neither, what it should be called. I have suspicions about one or two of these, but I'd rather not bias you by saying which.
