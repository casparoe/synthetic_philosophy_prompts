I'm in a metaphysics course and we just hit dispositions, and I've gotten obsessed with the fact that the obvious analysis keeps failing on cases I have strong gut feelings about. The obvious analysis being: X has the disposition to M in circumstances C iff X would M if it were in C. Seems bulletproof until the professor started throwing weird counterexample machinery at it (finks, masks, antidotes -- apparently this is a whole genre), and now I can't tell whether my intuitions are tracking one thing or several.

So here are my verdicts on cases, and I want to know if there's a principle that captures them. A wine glass that would shatter if dropped on tile: obviously fragile. The same glass packed in styrofoam so it never breaks: still fragile, obviously. A live wire hooked up to an electro-fink -- a device that detects any touch and cuts the charge before it could shock you -- so that touching it would NOT shock you: I still want to say the wire is live, it's just got this thing sabotaging the shock. But a dead wire attached to the reverse device, one that electrifies it the instant someone touches it, so that touching it WOULD shock you: I say that wire is not live. And a headache disposed to make me wince, except I've taken an aspirin that masks the pain: I still have the headache. A chameleon that would turn brown on a brown leaf, except a sorcerer zaps it green the moment any color change starts: I flip around on whether it has the disposition. And the case I genuinely can't settle: a fragile glass with a sorcerer who instantly mends any crack before it spreads, so it would never actually shatter -- is it fragile or not? Notice my verdicts on the two wire cases point in opposite directions relative to the conditional analysis (one ascribes the disposition though the manifestation wouldn't happen, one denies it though the manifestation would), which makes me think no conditional-style fix can get everything.

So: is there a single principle that delivers all or nearly all of these, or do I have to give up some verdicts? If there's a repair of the conditional analysis (like restricting what counts as an "interfering" factor) that's supposed to work, I want to see exactly where it draws the line between the electro-fink and the styrofoam, since both prevent shattering/shocking from outside the object. And tell me what your best principle says about my sorcerer-mender glass.
