I retired a few years ago after a career in visual neuroscience, and I have since taken up philosophy of mind with a convert's zeal and an experimentalist's habits: when two theories disagree, I want to know what each of them predicts. The dispute between higher-order theories of consciousness — on which a perceptual state is conscious only in virtue of being represented by a suitable higher-order state — and first-order theories, on which phenomenally conscious content is already carried by perception itself, strikes me as partly empirical and partly verbal, and I cannot reliably tell which part is which. Please draft the joint statement that a committed higher-order theorist and a committed first-order theorist could both sign after a good-faith adversarial collaboration. It should contain: the claims both can sign about the shared empirical terrain — blindsight, masked priming, above-chance discrimination without reported awareness, and "no-report" paradigms that attempt to index consciousness without asking subjects anything; the precise residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording of its own view (I would especially like to see whether the dispute over whether reportability is constitutive of consciousness or merely diagnostic of it can be made exact); the cruxes, such that settling any one of them would move at least one party; and, for each crux, a concrete experimental manipulation both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance, preregistration-style. Where you judge a disagreement to be merely verbal, say so and explain which words are doing the damage. Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table.
