A colleague of mine — a committed total utilitarian who thinks the sum of wellbeing across everyone who ever lives is all that matters — and I, who am drawn to a person-affecting view on which adding lives barely worth living to a world of wonderful lives does nothing good for anyone, have spent the better part of two years arguing about the repugnant conclusion and its cousins. We have decided, before we exhaust ourselves entirely, to do the responsible thing and draft the joint statement an adversarial collaboration between our two camps should produce, and I would like you to draft that document for us. I want the claims we can both sign — about what the repugnant conclusion is actually asserting, about the sadistic conclusion and what it is supposed to show, about the non-identity problem and why it complicates any view that leans on 'who is harmed', and about which anti-totalist conclusions even the totalists concede follow from their view — followed by the exact statements of where we still differ, each phrased so that the totalist would accept the wording of her own position and I would accept the wording of mine. Where part of our dispute strikes you as merely verbal (I half suspect the fight over whether a world of a trillion barely-worth-living lives is 'worse' rather than just 'not better' for anyone is partly verbal), say which part and why.

Two cases we keep circling should feature as cruxes, because I suspect they mark where our disagreement actually lives. The first is what we've been calling the auditorium case: a universe containing ten people living wonderful lives, versus a causally disconnected universe containing those same ten plus a trillion people whose lives are barely worth living; my hunch is that my resistance weakens somewhat when the extra lives are in a separate universe, and I want to know what that says about whether my view is really about harm to particular people or something vaguer. The second is what we've been calling deferred creation: a button that either creates the trillion extra lives now or creates them, identically, in a billion years' time. For each crux you identify, please specify a case, argument, or consideration that both camps would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance — what would each of us expect to say, and which of us would have to move, if the consideration went one way rather than the other.

Please format the response as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table with these columns: Crux; Totalist wording of the disagreement (as a totalist would phrase it); Person-affecting wording (as I would phrase it); Case or argument both accept as bearing on it; Totalist prediction; Person-affecting prediction. After the table, include a JSON object with fields 'agreed_claims' (array of strings), 'disagreements' (array of objects, one per crux, with keys 'crux', 'totalist_wording', 'person_affecting_wording', 'test', 'prediction_totalist', 'prediction_person_affecting'), and 'verbal_disputes' (array of strings identifying any parts of the debate you judge merely verbal, with a one-line reason). Keep the formulations sharp enough that neither of us could sign a claim our side actually rejects, but not so hedged that the document says nothing.
