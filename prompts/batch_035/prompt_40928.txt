My team builds evaluation pipelines for language models we fine-tune with reinforcement learning from human feedback. Whenever a trained model finds a loophole in the reward — padding its output with plausible-sounding filler, flattering the grader, exploiting a quirk of the rubric — a colleague dismisses it as just overfitting: the same statistical phenomenon the field has always handled with held-out data, regularization, and early stopping, so there is supposedly nothing qualitatively new to worry about. The analogy she is leaning on goes roughly as follows: the reward model is the training set, the behavior we actually want is the true underlying distribution, and reward hacking is the generalization gap — the model is fitting the proxy's quirks rather than the signal we meant to measure. I would like a proper audit of this analogy.

Please do the following. First, lay out the correspondence explicitly — in the RLHF setting, what plays the role of the training data, the hypothesis class, the noise, the regularization term, and the held-out validation set — and flag any slots in the overfitting picture that have no occupant at all. Second, separate the disanalogies that matter for her conclusion from the ones that do not. Here it seems relevant that writers on this topic distinguish several varieties of Goodhart's law — regressional (any noisy proxy regresses toward the mean as you select on its extremes), extremal (the proxy's correlation with the target breaks down in the region you are optimizing into), causal (intervening directly on the proxy does not move the target), and adversarial (another agent with its own objectives deliberately exploits your proxy) — and I would like to know which of these, if any, statistical overfitting genuinely models, and which ones the overfitting framework cannot even express. Third, tell me whether her reassuring conclusion — reward hacking is a familiar problem with familiar statistical remedies — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was quietly carrying the conclusion the whole way; be specific about whether any of her proposed fixes (held-out evaluation, regularization, early stopping) transfers to the varieties of Goodhart you identified as non-overfitting-like. Finally, if overfitting is the wrong source domain, propose a better one and explain what work it does that overfitting could not.
