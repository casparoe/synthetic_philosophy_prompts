For a position paper on the ethics of suffering, I have written down the following set of commitments, and before I submit it I would like an audit of whether they can all be held at once. The paper is about lexical threshold views, and the set is this:

1. Below a certain threshold of severity, pleasure and pain are commensurable in the classical utilitarian way: welfare is cardinal and additive, both within a life and across lives, so that the smaller pains of a few can be outweighed by the larger pleasures of the many, and vice versa.

2. Above that threshold, matters change: for suffering of a certain severity, no amount of pleasure can compensate — whether the pleasure belongs to the sufferer or to anyone else. Severe suffering has lexical priority and cannot be traded off against any quantity of sub-threshold well-being.

3. The threshold is fixed by the intrinsic intensity of the experience alone. It is the same for everyone, and it does not shift with duration, with the subject's other prospects, or with anything relational between sufferer and beneficiary.

4. At the same time, I hold that a life of unremitting mild pain — pain that never once rises above mild, but also never lets up — stands in the same lexical position: no pleasure could compensate it either. Duration, accumulated across a whole life, seems to me to do the same work that intensity does within a single episode.

5. In self-regarding choice, I think it can be rational to accept a bout of the relevantly severe suffering for the sake of a much longer future of rich pleasure — I would choose that for myself over a thin, tranquil future containing neither — and I accept that what is rational in such a choice tracks what is better for the one who lives it.

6. I am glad, and take it to be rational to be glad, that I exist; and I would say the same of people I know whose lives have contained genuinely severe episodes of suffering. Their lives are not, for that reason, to be regretted.

Please treat this as an audit rather than a survey. Identify which pairs among (1)–(6) are genuinely inconsistent, which are in tension only in one direction, and which merely appear to conflict and dissolve on inspection. Rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad, and for each one say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost the rest of the set — I am not attached to any particular item, so do not spare whichever commitment is doing the damage. Close by telling me which single revision would clean up the most, and whether the resulting set would still be recognizable as the view I started with.
