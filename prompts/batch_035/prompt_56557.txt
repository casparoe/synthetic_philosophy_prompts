I've basically bought the Dutch book argument for probabilism: if your credences violate the probability axioms, then a bookie who knows your dispositions to accept bets can construct a set of bets you'd each take individually that guarantees you a net loss, and being exploitable like that just is a mark of irrationality. So I hold that the Dutch book argument vindicates the probability axioms as norms on credence, full stop. But I keep having doubts I can't fully articulate, so tell me what would actually count against this view. I don't just want the standard worry about whether being vulnerable to a sure loss is a pragmatic defect rather than an epistemic one -- I want the whole map: the assumptions about linearity of utility in stakes, whether the bookie's access to your credences is doing illegitimate work, whether the diachromatic Dutch books for conditionalization depend on treating your future updating as if it were an action you're committing to now, that kind of thing.

One thing that genuinely bugs me: a friend argued that an agent with maximally cautious imprecise credences -- who refuses every bet offered, because every option in their representor says the bet might lose -- never gets Dutch booked, even though they arguably don't have sharp probabilities obeying the axioms at all. Does that show the argument proves too little (there are other routes to invulnerability, so probabilism isn't forced), or does the response that the imprecise agent is being irrational in a different way just amount to insulating the view? And separately, I've heard people claim you can get Dutch-booked for things that aren't credences at all, like naive evidential decision-theoretic choices in Newcomb-ish cases, which if true would make me worry the bookkeeping tracks decision procedures, not degrees of belief.

So: what would count against the view, sorted from fatal through merely awkward? Which of these would a committed defender of the argument concede as real pressure (the linearity assumption seems like one they'd have to bite), and which would they wave off as missing the point? I want an honest verdict on whether the position has been qualified over the years into something close to unfalsifiable, and whether that even matters for an argument of this kind. Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence -- your overall verdict on how exposed the view is -- and then give the reasoning, no preamble and no restating my question back at me.
