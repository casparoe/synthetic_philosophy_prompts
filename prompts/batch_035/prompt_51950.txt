I teach intro metaphysics most years, and every time I get to the survival unit I realize my own gut verdicts are a mess, so let me just lay them out and see if there's any principle under there.

Scan-and-destroy teletransporter, replica wakes up on Mars: that's me, I barely hesitate. Gradual replacement of my neurons with functionally identical silicon over a semester: also me, obviously. Fission, where the scan is used to build two copies, one on Mars and one on Europa: I can't get myself to say both survive, even though each copy has everything the single one would have. And the one that really bugs me — a variant where my body is held in stasis for a week while the Mars copy is already up and living, with the original destroyed only at the end of the week. Something in me says that copy isn't me in a way the plain teleporter copy is, even though I've already said the plain one is fine, and I can't see what the week adds except making the death visible.

Also on the combined spectrum idea, if you slide the case step by step toward plain death — less and less psychological continuity in the copy — my verdict flips somewhere and I have no idea where to put the line.

Is there one principle that captures all this, or do I have to throw out a verdict, and which one? And does whatever you land on support the Parfit-ish thought I half-remember, that identity isn't really what we should care about here, or is that a seperate question from which cases are survival? Last thing: tell me what the principle predicts for a case I haven't given — the teleporter glitches and the copy comes out missing its last five years of memories but is otherwise fine.
