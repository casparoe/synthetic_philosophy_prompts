I'm a retired physiologist -- forty years working on insect motor control, so fixed action patterns, releasing stimuli, central pattern generators, efference copy, setpoints, open- vs closed-loop control, habituation, hierarchical control. That's all home turf for me. Now that I've got time on my hands I've gotten into philosphy, and I've run into the whole "sphexishness" business, and I want to run it through my vocabulary.

As I understand it: there's a digger wasp who paralyzes a cricket, drags it to the lip of her burrow, leaves it there, goes down to inspect the burrow, comes back up, and pulls the cricket in. If you nudge the cricket a few inches away while she's inside, she drags it back to the lip, goes down and inspects again, comes up, and if you keep moving it she'll do this over and over, seemingly forever. No noticing that the burrow was just checked. Dennett coined a word for this kind of thing, "sphexish," and philosophers use the wasp to talk about mechanical rigidity: the wasp looks intelligent from a distance, but she's running a fixed program, and the worry is that we're the same kind of thing with more layers. There's also, I gather, a compatibilist reply along the lines that sphexishness comes in degrees, humans are somewhat sphexish, and being a bit sphexish doesn't rule out a kind of freedom.

Restate that whole debate in the vocabulary I actually use, as faithfully as you can. Some specific things I'd like addressed:

- Does "sphexish" translate to something precise on my side of the fence? Open-loop control where a closed loop should be? A releasing stimulus gating a fixed action pattern regardless of feedback? Give the best mapping and tell me where it wobbles.
- Translate the claim that humans are "a bit sphexish." What does that even mean in control terms -- some loops open where they should be closed, upper levels of a hierarchy too slow to override fast lower ones, limits on habituation? What's the honest translation, and what did you have to fudge?
- "Could have done otherwise" -- the thing everyone in the free will fight keeps saying. Does that have any expression in my vocabulary at all? Closest I can come up with is counterfactual sensitivity of the trajectory to perturbation, but I suspect that's me smuggling something in.

Then the residue report: which parts of the philosophical debate carry over cleanly, which get distorted in translation, and which have no expression in control vocabulary whatsoever. And give me your view on the residue -- does it show my vocabulary is missing something, or that the philosophers were arguing about something that was never stated clearly enough to translate in the first place? I'd especially like to know whether the freedom the compatibilists are trying to save is anything more than well-behaved closed-loop control with internal state, because if it is, I can't see it, and if it isn't, I can't work out why it needed saving.
