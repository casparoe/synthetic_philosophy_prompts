so this just became a real decision for me and not a blog-post argument: i started on the "agent behavior" team at my company last month, and in q3 we have to decide how much autonomy to give the internal research assistant we're building. at dinner after the offsite the team basically split into two camps and i realized i couldnt articulate either side's best version, i just knew the vibes. i want to see it argued out properly before i have to have an opinion in a design doc.

for context if the terms aren't familiar: by "goal-directed" i mean an agent with a fixed objective that plans over long horizons, scoring every candidate action by how much it advances that objective. by "approval-directed" i mean an agent that at each decision point asks "which action would some overseer approve of *right now*" and picks that -- it doesn't lock in a long-range plan in service of a single goal, the values get applied fresh at every step. the bet is that each step stays legible and correctable rather than the values being baked in once and trusted forever.

write a dialogue between maya, who thinks we should go goal-directed, and theo, who is the strongest approval-directed person on the team. make maya genuinely good, not a strawman: she thinks approval-directedness just smuggles the objective into the overseer's judgments (so you haven't removed the goal, only hidden it), and that a step-by-step agent will be incoherent, weak, and annoying to use. and make theo actually press back: his whole thing is that a fixed objective is precisely how you get a system that does something horrifying when the world shifts in a way the objective didn't anticipate, and that legibility is worth paying for in raw capability. they should engage with each others' actual points -- maya should push on what happens when the overseer has the same blind spots as the main model, theo should push on what happens when the goal is misspecified. no fake synthesis at the end; if they genuinely get stuck somewhere, let them get stuck there, thats honestly more useful to me. definately don't have either of them concede on a weak point just to wrap it up.
