Here's something I've been chewing on and can't settle myself. Exposure therapy for phobias and OCD is like the most empirically supported thing in all of clinical psych — you get the person to encounter the feared thing and stay with it until the fear comes down. The old textbook story was habituation: fear naturally decays when nothing bad happens, so you just outlast it. But the newer treatment manuals are all about expectancies and disconfirmation — you're supposed to design each exposure so the patient's specific prediction (if I touch this doorknob I'll get contaminated, if I don't check the stove the house burns down) gets flatly violated, which sounds an awful lot like the predictive processing crowd saying the brain is a prediction machine that updates on prediction error, with anxiety as a badly calibrated generative model, and things like avoidance as ways of keeping the model from ever getting corrected. Friston's free energy picture even gives you a story about why attention and arousal matter — precision weighting, or whatever.

So here's my question. What does exposure therapy, as an actual practiced thing in clinics, actually presuppose? Does it need the predictive processing / free energy story of the mind, or is the practice philosophically underdetermined — would plain habituation theory, or the inhibitory learning account, license the very same drills? I want you to reconstruct what the practice strictly needs to assume to make sense of why it works, separate that from what merely fits nicely, and tell me where multiple theories would predict the same clinical moves.

And then flip it: if a clinic genuinely committed to the predictive processing picture, would anything in the practice have to change? Session design, what counts as a good exposure, how you'd think about avoidance and safety behaviors, even what you tell the patient about why this is working? My hunch is the theory is doing mostly marketing work and the practice would survive swapping out any of these frameworks, but I want a real reconstruction, not just my hunch.
