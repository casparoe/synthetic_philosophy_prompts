You're a labor economist with a sideline in satire, and you've spent too many years reading Zahavi's handicap principle and laughing without joy at job postings. Write a parody job advertisement — 450 to 600 words — for an unpaid "prestige internship" at a fictional media company, where the author has fully internalized signaling theory and is weirdly honest about it. The ad should come right out and say that the requirements serve no productive function whatsoever: they exist to be costly, and costly precisely in a way that only the right kind of applicant can afford to pay. So the unpaid-ness is a feature, not a bug — a handicap, like the peacock's tail, that proves fitness precisely by being wasteful. The "must be passionate about media" line, the requirement of an elite degree in an unrelated field, the expectation of prior unpaid experience, the "fast-paced environment" that means long hours — each should be framed by the ad itself as a deliberately expensive signal that filters out anyone who can't afford to send it, guaranteeing honesty because faking it is impossible to fake cheaply. Somewhere in there the ad should mention, smugly, that if the signal weren't wasteful it wouldn't work — cheap talk is free and therefore worthless — and maybe a line where the fictional hiring manager brags that they've achieved a "clean separating equilibrium" while other firms are stuck in pooling. Include at least one quote from a "concerned" outside expert (an evolutionary biologist, or an economist who studies matching markets) who notes that the whole edifice only sorts people by parental wealth, not talent, and that the cost is deadweight. Don't name any real company or living person. Brainstorm a bit first about which actual prestige-job-market practices map onto which pieces of honest-signaling theory (handicaps, costly vs cheap signals, separation vs pooling, receiver skepticism), and make sure every joke is a joke an actual signaling theorist would nod at rather than wince at — the satire is of the practice, and the theory is the straight man. Deadpan throughout, no winking at the reader that this is a parody. Keep it around 500 words if you can.
