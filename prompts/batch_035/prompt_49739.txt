I'm TA-ing a formal epistemology course and marking a problem sheet on updating on uncertain evidence. I want a second opinion on one answer before I commit to a mark, because I suspect there's something subtly off in it that I keep almost seeing. The question was worth 20 marks total:

"Trudy's radon detector beeps, but the manual says it gives false readings roughly a third of the time. After the beep, her credence that there is radon in the basement rises from 0.4 to 0.6. (a) State Jeffrey's rule for updating on uncertain evidence of this kind, and say what the rigidity condition demands. (b) Explain why the rule reduces to ordinary conditionalization when the evidence is certain. (c) Can Trudy's Jeffrey update always be redescribed as ordinary conditioning on some event in her original probability space? Give a reason."

Here's the student's answer:

"(a) Jeffrey's rule says that when your credence in E shifts to some new value q but your conditional credences given E and given ¬E stay put, your new credence in H is P(H|E)q + P(H|¬E)(1−q). The rigidity condition is exactly this staying put: it demands that P_new(H|E) = P(H|E) for every H. This is required by coherence itself, since an agent whose conditional credences shifted along with her credence in E could be Dutch-booked, so rigidity isn't really an extra assumption on top of the rule. (b) Setting q = 1 gives P_new(H) = P(H|E), which is just ordinary conditioning, so conditionalization is simply the special case where the evidence is certain. (c) Yes. Since the update just mixes the two conditional probabilities, there must be some event A in the space — presumably some subset of E — such that conditioning on A delivers the same new credences. So Jeffrey conditioning adds convenience but no genuinely new content: anything it does could have been done by conditioning on the right proposition, if only the agent knew which one."
