My sister teaches an intro philosophy night class at the community college, and since I'm the person in the family who won't stop reading pop neuroscience books about free will (zero formal training, just a lot of Daniel Wegner-adjacent paperbacks over the years), she lets me do first-pass grading on the short answer questions. This week's question was:

"Benjamin Libet's experiments in the early 1980s are often said to threaten free will. (i) Describe what subjects were asked to do and what Libet measured. (ii) State the key timing result. (iii) Explain how Libet himself responded to the worry this raised about free will. (iv) Give one reason later researchers have questioned the standard interpretation."

Here's one student's answer:

"Subjects sat in front of a clock face with a spot going around it and were told to flex their wrist or finger whenever they felt like it. Libet measured the readiness potential, an EEG signal recorded from electrodes placed over the motor cortex, which controls voluntary movement, and also had subjects note where the spot was at the moment they consciously decided to move (which Libet called the W judgment). The readiness potential started about half a second before the movement, but subjects only reported becoming aware of the intention about 200 ms beforehand, so the brain had already decided to move about 350 ms before the person was consciously aware of deciding. Libet's own conclusion was that conscious will doesn't initiate our actions and we're basically conscious afterthoughts, though he thought we keep a kind of veto: consciousness can block an action in the final moment before it happens (sometimes called 'free won't'). The result has been replicated a lot, including fMRI versions done by Libet's own lab, where researchers could predict which of two buttons a person would press up to 7-10 seconds in advance with near perfect accuracy. As for criticisms, some researchers argued the readiness potential is just an average of random background fluctuations in neural activity, and that the actual decision happens at the moment of awareness, so the RP isn't a decision signal at all. Basically Libet's experiment has been debunked and shows nothing about free will."
