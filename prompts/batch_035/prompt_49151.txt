I'm giving a job talk in three weeks defending a version of epiphenomenalism, and I want a Q&A prep sheet before I fly out. The thesis, in brief: physical causal closure plus the absence of any a priori entailment from physical facts to phenomenal facts leaves it open that phenomenal properties are causally idle byproducts, nomologically correlated with physical states but not themselves doing any causal work. My own twist, which is what the paper is really about: I argue that the usual epiphenomenalist embarrassment about reports and judgments -- if qualia don't cause anything, how do our *talk* about qualia track them? -- dissolves once we see that phenomenal concepts need not be causally connected to their referents in the way physical concepts are; the psychophysical correlation laws themselves can do the reference-fixing. I don't claim this is fully worked out, but I think it's better than the standard replies, and it's the part of the paper I expect to bleed from.

The audience: a large, analytically oriented department with an active philosophy of mind group. The senior hire is an old-guard type-identity theorist who thinks conceivability arguments died with the a posteriori necessity of water and Hesperus. Two mid-career people are committed higher-order theorists who will say my datum -- that there is something it is like -- is doing all the work and begging the question. There's a functionalist who works on causal exclusion and will know the mental causation literature cold, and one young faculty member who is an out-and-out illusionist in the deflationary mode and enjoys dismantling property dualism for sport. The rest are epistemologists, ethicists, and a metaphysician with strong views about grounding; they won't know the psychophysics literature but they will be sharp about modality, explanation, and whether my correlation laws are legitimate posits or ad hoc epicycles.

Please draft the prep sheet: the eight or so objections I'll actually get from this room, ordered by how much damage each would do if I fumble it, each phrased the way that particular person would put it rather than in textbook form, with a reply I can deliver in two minutes and a candid note on where the reply is soft. Make sure the list includes the exclusion argument against my correlation laws from the metaphysician's angle, the illusionist's charge that the "problem of phenomenal judgment" is really evidence against the datum rather than a puzzle for me, and the question I'm hoping nobody asks, which I'd rather discover from you now than at the front of that seminar room.
