I'm a software engineer with no formal philosophy training, and I've ended up in a long-running argument with a colleague on my team about the ethics of war. Rather than keep trading gut reactions, I sat down and wrote out what I actually believe, and now I'd like a consistency check on the package. Here it is:

1. Once fighting is underway, soldiers on each side are legitimate targets for the soldiers of the other side. That seems to be how the laws of war actually operate, and on reflection I think it is not merely a convention but morally right.

2. Behind that, I hold a general principle about harming people in defense: you may kill or seriously harm someone only if they are responsible for a wrongful threat to you or to others. If they aren't wrongfully threatening anyone, then the fact that killing them would be useful to your cause cannot by itself make them killable.

3. Most rank-and-file soldiers in an unjust war had no realistic exit: conscription, propaganda from childhood, and in some armies prison or execution for desertion. I don't blame them for the war. The guilt for the war itself belongs to the leadership.

4. I accept the post-1945 judgment that following orders is not a defense, and I think it was right to convict people on that basis even in cases where refusing would have been personally dangerous.

5. On a specific case: a nineteen-year-old conscript sent across the border as part of an unjust invasion, who shoots at defenders in accordance with the rules of war, does nothing wrong. His killing a defender is not murder on his part, and a defender's killing him is not murder either. The ordinary soldiers on the two sides can kill one another, and none of it counts as wrongdoing by them.

6. I also think it is unrealistic to expect an ordinary soldier to work out whether his side's war is just. That judgment is above his pay grade; the most he can reasonably be held to is fighting within the rules, and a soldier who does that has met the standard that applies to him.

So: is this package consistent? Tell me which pairs of commitments are genuinely in tension, which only look like they are, and rank the real tensions by severity instead of presenting them all as equally bad. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost. Finally, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, since I would rather make one change than five.
