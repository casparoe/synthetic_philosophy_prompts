I record a podcast for our department's grad student philosophy society, and this week's guest is a committed Lewisian who thinks de re modality is all about counterparts and that nothing in another possible world is ever strictly you. Help me prep eight to ten questions that go past the intro-lecture stuff. I definately want one on the Humphrey objection — why should Humphrey care that his counterpart wins the race when he himself never does — with a follow-up keyed to the reply he'll most likely give, that Humphrey has the same sort of reason to care about his counterpart's win that we have to care about our kids' or our countrymen's successes. I also want one pressing on the counterpart relation not being transitive — you can have a counterpart of a counterpart of Humphrey who is no counterpart of Humphrey, so 'could have been' statements start behaving weirdly — and one on the charge that he's just changed the subject: 'Humphrey could have won' now means 'some similar guy won', which is a similarity report about the world, not a modal fact about him. At least one question should ask whether counterpart theory can survive without modal realism itself, since almost everyone thinks the concrete parallel worlds are the crazy part — does the view stand alone or was it only ever plumbing for the big ontology? If you can fit in a question about temporal counterparts and whether four-dimensionalism is quietly doing all the work here, bonus points. Keep them sharp but conversational, not gotcha-y — he's friendly and I want a real discussion, not a gotcha festival.
