I develop interactive theorem provers (systems like Lean, Coq, and Isabelle), and a long-running argument with a colleague about our design choices has turned, without our fully intending it, into a question about Lewis Carroll's tortoise. I would like your help reconstructing what our practice presupposes.

The practice, concretely. When a user submits a proof, a small fixed kernel checks every step. A step passes only if it instantiates one of a handful of primitive rules hardwired into the kernel's code — in the Lean/Coq family, ultimately forms of function application and definitional equality, with modus ponens present only as a special case — or if it follows from axioms the user has explicitly declared. The user may freely add new axioms; the system warns but permits. The user may never add a new rule. And the system audits content-dependence: Lean can report exactly which axioms a given theorem rests on, so that projects like its mathematics library can enforce a standard axiom list. Nothing analogous audits rule-dependence, because every theorem trivially depends on the kernel's rules.

Two things strike me. First, Carroll's tortoise shows that a rule of inference cannot be turned into a further premise without launching a regress: the tortoise accepts A, accepts that A implies Z, accepts that A together with the first conditional implies Z, and still declines to infer Z, demanding that each new conditional be added as a premise in turn. Our kernels appear to answer the tortoise by decree — the rules are code, not content, and the regress stops where the kernel's checking loop does. Second, the family does not agree on the answer. Isabelle takes the opposite approach to rule-representation: it encodes an object-logic's inference rules as axioms of a small higher-order meta-logic, whose own kernel performs the actual inferences. So one design hardens the rule into the checker, another hardens it into an axiom, and users of both insist the difference is mere engineering.

So my questions: What view of the justification of deduction does kernel-checking presuppose, if any? Is it Carroll's own moral, that rules are licenses rather than premises and so need no premise-like justification at all? Is it that justification of the basic rules terminates in fiat, so that the buck stops at the kernel and there is no further theory to give? Or does the practice equally well fit several rival accounts — that rule-circular justification is acceptable, that rules are constitutive of the meanings of the logical constants, that rules are justified pragmatically by their role in preserving truth — so that nothing is strictly presupposed? Relatedly, does the asymmetry between rules and axioms, with new axioms permitted and audited but new rules forbidden and unaudited, presuppose that inference rules carry no epistemic risk? And is that consistent with the fact that rules like ex falso quodlibet are safe only relative to a consistency assumption the system never tracks? Finally, is the Isabelle design really the same presupposition in different syntax, or does it embody a rival view of what a rule is?

Please structure your response as follows:

1. The practice restated (roughly 150 words): describe the kernel-checking arrangement accurately, correcting me where I have it wrong, so that the analysis rests on shared ground.
2. What the kernel presupposes about rules (roughly 250 words): reconstruct the implicit theory of inference rules and their justification.
3. Presupposition or mere fit (roughly 200 words): distinguish what the practice strictly requires from the rival theories it is merely compatible with, and say where it is philosophically underdetermined.
4. The rule/axiom asymmetry (roughly 200 words): what view of epistemic risk, if any, the audited-axioms-but-unaudited-rules pattern presupposes, and whether that view is internally consistent.
5. If the tortoise got her way (roughly 150 words): what a proof checker would have to look like if inference rules genuinely required premise-style justification, and whether such a checker could be built at all.
