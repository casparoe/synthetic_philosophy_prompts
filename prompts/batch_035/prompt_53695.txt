1. a fetus is morally a person basically from the start. like it has a right to life, and not in some watered down sense.
2. I'm pro-choice -- abortion should be legal basically on demand, at least early on, and for any reason.
3. Thomson's violinist case is right. your right to life doesn't give you a right to somebody else's body, even if you'll literally die without it.
4. but I ALSO think responsibility matters. the violinist case is where you got kidnapped out of nowhere, and thats why it feels different from most pregnancies. if you chose to have sex knowing pregnancy was a possible outcome, you're at least partly the reason the fetus needs your body in the first place, and that seems like it changes something.
5. except I dont think a driver who causes a crash can be legally forced to give blood (let alone a kidney) to the person they hit, even tho they're 100% responsible for the situation. forced donation is just off the table, period.
6. when we read Thomson's people-seeds case (seeds drift in through the window you deliberately opened, and one takes root in your house) I found it pretty convincing at the time -- like ok, you took a known risk, and you still dont owe the resulting person your house/body. but I cant shake the feeling from #4 that responsibility matters anyway??
