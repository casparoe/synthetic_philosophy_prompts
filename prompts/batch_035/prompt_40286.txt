I teach a graduate metaphysics seminar and every spring we do the laws of nature unit: Humean supervenience and the best-system account on one side, Armstrong-Dretske-Tooley necessitation and Maudlin's primitivism on the other, and by week three the students are just lobbing stock objections at each other. I've decided the problem is that nobody in the room has actually written down what we're demanding of a theory of laws in the first place. So before the unit starts this year I want to hand them a spec sheet.

Write me the desiderata. What does any adequate theory of laws of nature have to deliver? Obviously I have things in mind -- it has to distinguish laws from merely accidental regularities (the old uranium-spheres trick vs. an actual law), it has to say something about why laws support counterfactuals, it has to say something about whether laws explain the regularities or just summarize them, it has to handle probabilistic laws without quietly turning them into deterministic ones about frequencies. But I also want the less obvious ones on the list, and the reasoning behind each: why is it a requirement rather than a preference, and which camp's preference is it if it's a preference? Two I specifically want your judgment on:

1. A colleague of mine claims that "governing" is not a desideratum at all but a metaphor that has conned three generations into thinking there's a phenomenon there -- that once you list what laws do (explain, support counterfactuals, back predictions) nothing is left for governing to do. Is that right, or is there real explanatory work that only the governing intuition does?

2. I keep going back and forth on whether a theory of laws owes us an epistemology -- an account of how we could ever tell which regularities are the lawful ones -- or whether that's a separate question for philosophy of science. Give me a view.

Also please tell me which subset of the desiderata, if any, you think is jointly unsatisfiable -- if there's a no-go result buried in the list itself I want to know, since that's the most interesting thing I could hand the students. And I suspect a couple of the standard demands are secretly the same demand wearing different hats -- if you notice that, say so.

Score the three main contenders (best systems, necessitation, primitivism) against the list at the end if you want, but keep that part short -- the criteria are the real deliverable, I'll do the scorecard in class.

American English please -- and I know I type fast and loose, but keep your own prose clean.
