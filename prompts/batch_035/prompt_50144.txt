Two friends in my philosophy club have been arguing about advance directives, and I promised to bring them a verdict. The case: a woman, while fully competent, signed a directive refusing artificial nutrition if she ever developed advanced dementia, stating that she would rather die than live in that condition. Years later, demented and living in a care home, she is by all reports content: she enjoys her meals, greets visitors, and shows no distress. The staff face the question of what to do when she can no longer swallow on her own.

Maya argued that the directive must be followed. The whole point of autonomy, she said, is that we decide for our future selves while we can — and this woman did exactly that, in writing, with the consequences spelled out.

Jonas replied that this misses what autonomy is for: it protects a person's interests, and the person whose interests are now at stake is the contented woman in the care home, not the author of a document from a decade ago. Following the directive, he said, amounts to letting a stranger decide that this woman should die — a stranger who happens to share her body.

Maya answered that precedent autonomy is like making a promise or taking out a mortgage: we bind our future selves all the time, and nobody thinks the future self gets to opt out because it no longer feels like it.

Jonas said the analogy fails precisely because a promise binds the very same person who made it, whereas here — if the directive is to have any content — the demented woman must be someone so transformed that her earlier judgments do not automatically speak for her. Her past self can inform us about her, but cannot simply overrule her.

Maya's final move: if you take that seriously, you could ignore people's wills and contracts on the ground that the author "no longer exists" — which is absurd.

Please adjudicate their exchange move by move: for each of the five moves, say whether it is right, wrong, or right only in part, and briefly justify your rating. Then state who is more right overall, and identify anything important that both of them missed. Keep your entire answer under 600 words.
