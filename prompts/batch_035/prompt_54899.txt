I'm a backend engineer and I've got a decision coming up that I keep going back and forth on, and I'd genuinely like some help thinking it through rather than just a "do what feels right" answer. After eight years at the same company I'm leaving with about £90k in savings, and I'm deciding between two places to put roughly £40k of it while I figure out my next move. Option A is a boring diversified fund -- I can pull up decades of data, see the drawdowns, and put rough numbers on the probability of losing 20% in a given year. Option B is a stake in my friend's company. He's legit smart, the product is decent, I've read the cap table and the runway, and honestly my gut says the expected outcome is comparable to option A, maybe slightly better. But here's the thing: for option B I can't really form a probability. It's not that I think it's 50/50 versus some other number, it's that any number I'd assign would be made up, and I know I'd just be dressing up my vibes in a percentage.

What I've noticed in myself is that I keep gravitating back to option A even when my rough estimate of B's payoff is better, and when I poke at why, it seems like it's not the *amount* of risk that bothers me -- it's that with B I'd be deciding in the dark. Some of my colleagues say that's just a bias I should correct for: if my best guess says B is better, I should pick B, full stop, and any extra return I demand for "not knowing the odds" is me paying an irrational tax. Others say the opposite -- that when you genuinely can't quantify something, the fact that you *can't* quantify it is itself information, and demanding a premium for it is the rational move. I've even caught myself doing a weird experiment in my head: if someone offered me a coin flip on the fund's performance with known odds versus a mystery box with the "same" expected value, I'd take the coin flip every time, and I can't tell if that's wisdom or cowardice.

So, what should I actually do, and more importantly, what's the right way to think about this? Is there a principled difference between the kind of uncertainty I have about option A (where I can at least estimate the odds) and option B (where I feel like any probability I assign is fiction), or is that distinction something decision theorists have shown to be incoherent?
