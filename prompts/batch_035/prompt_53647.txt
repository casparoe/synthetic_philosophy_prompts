I teach epistemology, and for the last few years a colleague of mine has co-taught the unit on higher-order evidence with me. Somewhere along the way we started this game where students bring in cases and she rules on what the agent should believe, and she's so consistant about it that I've taken to collecting her verdicts and trying to reverse-engineer the rule she's running. There's a new case coming up next week and I'd like to walk in with a prediction. Her track record so far, roughly in order:

A doctor reads a chest x-ray as showing a fracture, then learns from the technician that her reading of this kind of film is unreliable — and the film still looks exactly the same to her. Ruling: downshift, don't believe there's a fracture on that basis. A researcher's belief in a hypothesis originally rested on a paper that later got retracted, but the retraction was for fraud in one of the paper's other experiments, and she had re-derived the result from the raw data with a different method anyway. Ruling: the belief is fine, she keeps it. An exhausted grad student checks a proof three times, it keeps checking out, and then she learns that students at her level of sleep deprivation are wrong the large majority of the time when a proof feels this solid to them. Ruling: shelve it — dont believe the proof is sound until she's slept.

The new case: same exhausted grad student, same sleep-deprived state, but this time she believes the theorem holds because her well-rested advisor, who went through the proof carefully that morning, told her it does.

So, three things. First, the prediction itself. Second — and this is the part I actually care about — lay out the candidate rules that fit her record. I can see at least two: one that puts the defeater in the specific channel the belief came through (tainted channel, revise; clean channel, keep), and one that puts it in the believer whenever the higher-order evidence is about her own condition rather than about some external source like a paper or a machine. Say what each rule predicts about the advisor case, since as far as I can tell they part ways exactly there, and give me your best guess about which way she'll go and why. Third, since whichever rules end up agreeing on the new case will still need separating, propose the further case that would best discriminate between them — I want something in reserve to test whatever rule you settle on, ideally before she figures out that's what I'm doing.
