Write a short dialogue between a grad student and her advisor, stuck at an airport after a conference. The grad student is a true believer in the whole "agriculture was a mistake" narrative — she loves the old Jared Diamond line about it being the worst mistake in the history of the human race, the stuff about hunter-gatherers working fewer hours, eating a more varied diet, having skeletons that look healthier than early farmers', and living in bands where nobody could hoard much or boss anybody around because there was nothing to hoard. The advisor thinks this story is way too neat, but her objection isn't the boring "but civilization gave us vaccines" one. Her actual worry is stranger: suppose you grant every datapoint about per-person welfare getting worse after the switch to farming. Agriculture still let the human population grow by orders of magnitude, so whether the transition counts as "bad" seems to quietly depend on population ethics — whether a world with billions of lives barely worth living beats a world with a few million excellent ones. The grad student should push back that this sneaks in a totalist view she doesn't accept, and that nobody who says agriculture was a mistake is obligated to answer for that anyway. The advisor gets real replies too: she can argue that the "affluent forager" evidence is itself doing ideological work (it got popular partly because people wanted a stick to beat modernity with), that workload estimates extrapolate from a handful of contemporary foraging groups whose circumstances may be nothing like the paleolithic, and that "mistake" is a weird word for a process no one chose and that may have been a one-way ratchet — you can't easily go back once populations have grown past what foraging supports. Make both of them genuinely sharp, no strawmen, and let the back-and-forth actually build: the grad student concedes some things, the advisor concedes others, and by the end they should have landed somewhere more interesting than either starting position — maybe on the question of what it even means to evaluate a historical transition that has no counterfactual. Keep it conversational, they can be a bit tired and snarky, airport coffee and all that. No speeches please — they should talk to each other, not at each other.
