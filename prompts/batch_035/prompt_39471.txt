A friend of mine, a statistician by training, waves off Wigner's puzzle about the unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics with an analogy: mathematics fits physics the way a tailored suit fits the wearer. Nobody is surprised that the suit conforms to the man's measurements, because the suit was cut from those measurements in the first place. Likewise, he says, our mathematics fits physical phenomena only because we developed and selected our mathematical tools with those phenomena in view, and because we quietly forget every garment that never sold. So he concludes that Wigner's wonder is a mistake, that there is nothing deep about the applicability of mathematics to nature, and that people who infer from its success that reality is somehow intrinsically mathematical are confusing a wardrobe for a metaphysics.

Audit the analogy for me. First, set out the correspondence explicitly: within the tailor's shop, what plays the role of the tailor, the measurements, the cloth, the fitting sessions, and the discarded garments? Second, identify where the mapping breaks, and sort the disanalogies into those that actually matter for the conclusion he wants and those that are harmless. Third, spell out what the analogy quietly imports that the mathematical case lacks — I am thinking especially of what it presupposes about the direction of fit, and about how far a garment can depart from the measurements it was cut from. Fourth, and this is the part I care most about, tell me whether his deflationary conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table. The cases that worry me are the ones where mathematics was developed for one body and then turned out to fit bodies the tailor never measured; non-Euclidean geometry sitting around for decades before general relativity is the obvious example, and there are others in the same vein. Can his survivorship point — the forgotten garments — be stretched to cover those cases, or does it tear? If the analogy cannot carry the conclusion he was using it for, tell me what a better comparison would be, and whether a better comparison leaves any residue of Wigner's puzzle standing.
