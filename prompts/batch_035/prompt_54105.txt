For a seminar paper I've been asked to state my considered position on moral responsibility and defend its coherence, and I'd like to stress-test it before I commit it to print. Here is where I actually stand: (1) Strawson was right in Freedom and Resentment that holding people responsible is grounded in the participant reactive attitudes — resentment, gratitude, hurt feelings, indignation — and not in some further metaphysical fact about free will; (2) determinism poses no threat to moral responsibility, because what matters for blame is the quality of a person's will, not the causal history that produced it; (3) as the sciences of behavior mature, we ought increasingly to take the objective attitude toward wrongdoers — treating them as objects of management and treatment rather than targets of resentment — and I think a criminal justice system built on that attitude would be an improvement on ours; (4) psychopaths, who cannot grasp what they owe to other people, are nonetheless morally responsible for at least some of their wrongdoing; (5) when someone genuinely deserves blame, they deserve it in a sense that isn't exhausted by the role resentment happens to play in our relationships; and (6) we are right to blame a person more intensely when their wrongdoing expresses malice than when it reflects mere negligence, even where the negligence produces the worse outcome. Before I submit: audit the set. Which of these commitments pull against each other, which only appear to, and what is the cheapest repair for each real tension? Rank the tensions by severity, say what each revision would cost me, and tell me which single change would clean up the most.
