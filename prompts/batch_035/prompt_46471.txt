so for a grad seminar i'm supposed to hand in a one-pager on "where I stand on the levels of selection" and I want a sanity check before I put it in writing. heres my profile:

(1) deep down i'm a gene-centrist: adaptations exist for the benefit of the genes that built them, and the gene's-eye view frames things organism-level talk cant even express — meiotic drive, cytoplasmic male sterility, inclusive fitness all falling out of one story.

(2) but I also accept the bookkeeping point: for the standard cases, multilevel-selection models and kin-selection/gene-level models are mathematically equivalent redescriptions, so fighting about which framework is "the true one" is mostly a waste of everyone's time.

(3) still, I think group selection is a real causal process and not just an artifact of how you slice the data — the trait-group stuff and the whole eusociality fight left me convinced the anti-group-selection orthodoxy of the 80s and 90s was an overcorrection. at the same time I think the Nowak et al. Nature paper attacking inclusive fitness theory was simply wrong, and the consensus that piled on afterwards was right.

(4) on the t-haplotype in house mice (the meiotic drive element that gets itself into the vast majority of a heterozygous male's sperm and leaves homozygotes sterile): I take this to be a case where there is just a plain fact that selection is acting at the suborganismal level, against the organism — its not a matter of perspective or descriptive convenience which level selection is "really" on there.

(5) species selection, on the other hand, i'm skeptical of: most of what got labeled species selection looks like sorting — side-effects of ordinary selection on organisms, rather than selection acting on species as such.

(6) and I take the major transitions picture seriously: organisms are themselves evolved coalitions, individuality comes in degrees, there's no privileged level of biological organization that selection is "supposed" to act on.

I assume (1) and (3) are supposed to be at war but honestly I dont feel the conflict. go through the pairs, tell me which are genuinely in tension and how badly, which ones only look that way, what the cheapest fix is for each real tension, and if you could make me revise exactly one of the six commitments, which revision cleans up the most mess. rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damning.
