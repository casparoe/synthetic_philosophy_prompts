I'm on a working group at our regional health service and we write assessment guidance for clinicians who have to judge symptoms they can't directly observe — chronic pain, fatigue, dizzy spells, that kind of thing. These assessments gate disability support, adapted equipment, referrals, so the stakes are real, but each appointment is like 20 minutes with a nurse or GP who has zero philosophy background.

Here's why I'm writing you. Someone at a conference told me about a distinction Wittgenstein drew somewhere in his later work (the stuff around the private language argument) between criteria and symptoms. As I understand it: for most medical concepts a symptom is inductive evidence for an underlying condition — fever supports flu, a shadow on the x-ray supports cancer. But for some concepts, especially pain, the patient's saying and doing isn't just evidence for a hidden inner fact, it's partly what our concept hangs on — a normal utterance of "I'm in pain" is closer to the expression of pain (like crying out) than to a report about something behind the behavior, and asking what lies beyond the behavior may be asking for something idle. The catch: our institution has to treat these reports as claims that get weighed and corroborated and doubted, and I suspect that's exactly where things go wrong.

So write a procedure our assessors can actually follow. Steps/questions in order, what counts as a sufficent answer to each, where the procedure stops and hands it to a human because it's a values question not a factual one, and where it will misfire — be honest about that. Then run it on three cases: (1) a patient reporting constant lower back pain with totally normal imaging, applying for a disability benefit, where the assessor privately suspects exaggeration; (2) a patient reporting "seizures" whose episodes, on description, don't fit what the word seizure is actually for in our language; (3) a patient with weekly migraines requesting an expensive treatment, where the question of how much uncorroborated suffering justifies the cost isn't something any procedure can settle.

One more thing I want flagged: the deep tension I think is sitting there. If these reports work expressively, then demanding corroboration misfires for pain — but the gatekeeping role can't just drop the reports either. I want the procedure to locate that tension honestly, not pretend to dissolve it.
