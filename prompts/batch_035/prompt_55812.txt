A friend and I spent the better part of an evening arguing about what economists mean when they talk about "utility," and I left without a clear sense of who was right. Near the end, she condensed her position into a summary that I want checked. As best I can reconstruct it, she said the following:

Samuelson invented revealed preference theory to rid economics of unverifiable psychology: preferences are nothing over and above the choices a person makes. The theory says that if someone's choices are consistent — they never pick option A over B in one situation and B over A in another when both are affordable — then their behavior is exactly what you'd see from someone maximizing a utility function. But that means utility theory is empty: it just relabels consistent behavior as "maximization" and so cannot be wrong under any circumstances. It follows that the model has no empirical content whatsoever, and that the only scientifically respectable way to find out what a person wants is to watch what they buy. Since utility is merely a bookkeeping device for choices, comparing one person's utility to another's is a category error, and any ethical theory that needs such comparisons — utilitarianism, for instance — is thereby shown to be nonsense.

She is an economics student and I am not, so I can't easily tell which of these claims is standard doctrine and which is her own extrapolation. Some of it sounded right in the moment, but a few steps felt like they moved too fast: the jump from "choices reveal preferences" to "preferences are nothing but choices," and the move from unfalsifiability to the conclusion about ethics, in particular. Please audit her summary part by part. Tell me which claims a careful economist would endorse as stated, which are defensible only with qualifications she omitted, and which are genuine misrepresentations or non sequiturs. For each part you correct, explain what the corrected version would say. Finally, address her two headline conclusions directly: is revealed preference theory really empirically empty, and does the theory, properly understood, actually have any bearing on whether utilitarianism is coherent? Please keep your answer under 700 words, and lead with a short statement of your overall verdict before the part-by-part analysis.
