My brother and I have been arguing about discounting the future for literally years and we've finally agreed to do the whole adversarial-collaboration thing properly. For context so we're on the same page: by "pure time preference" we mean discounting future welfare just in virtue of how far away in time it is — not because the future is more uncertain, not because of returns on investment or opportunity cost, and not because the people there are different people, just because it's later. He's on the side that a modest pure time preference is rationally fine (he's an econ guy, keeps saying impatience is a legitimate taste like any other taste). I'm on the side that it's arbitrary in the same way discounting welfare by physical distance, or by whose welfare it is, would be — and that the only discounting that survives scrutiny is the uncertainty-and-returns kind. We both care about two levels of the question: the impersonal/policy one (climate discounting, infrastructure, longtermist stuff) and the personal one (should I put off the dentist, should I study tonight vs cram later).

Write the joint statement we should both be able to sign, with these sections in this order:

1. **What we agree on** — a numbered list, roughly 6–10 claims, including the boring formal ones (exponential vs hyperbolic discounting and time-consistency, why discounting for uncertainty is uncontroversial, etc.). Only stuff we'd both genuinely accept — nothing smuggled in that favors one side.
2. **Where we still disagree** — a table, one row per disagreement, with each row phrased so that the side holding the view would accept the wording of its own position. Include at least one disagreement about the personal-future case (my own future welfare) and one about the impersonal case (far-future strangers, climate).
3. **Cruxes** — aim for about 3. For each, give a concrete hypothetical or thought experment both of us would agree actually bears on it, plus each side's prediction written down in advance, so that someone can turn out to be wrong later. If a crux is untestable in principle, say so and explain why we still think it matters.
4. **The verbal part** — if you think some chunk of our dispute is basically verbal (e.g., whether we even mean the same thing by "rational," or whether the impatience-as-taste framing just redefines the question away), say which part and why.

Section 1 should be about half a page, the table maybe 4–5 rows, and section 3 can be the longest. Casual tone is fine, we're not submitting this anywhere, we just want to stop talking past eachother.
