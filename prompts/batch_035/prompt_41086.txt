1. rational choice has to be grounded in reasons — if nothing favors A over B, then picking A can't be rational, it's just arbitrary.
2. that said, I think Buridan's ass starves only if you assume deliberation has to end in a verdict about which option is better. it doesn't — deliberation can just stop, and the ass eats. the ass is a non-problem.
3. and anyway exact ties between real options basicaly never happen, because our evaluations are vague and noisy. the ass case stipulates something that doesn't occur in the wild.
4. BUT I'm also genuinely convinced that hard choices are real: sometimes two options are on a par — neither better, not exactly equal either, still comparable — so "which is better?" has no answer, and not because we're ignorant of one.
5. in those hard cases I think you can choose rationally without a favoring reason: you commit, and the commitment is what makes the choice rational rather than the other way around.
6. though I also think flipping a coin in a hard choice is a cop-out — you're dodging the responsibilty of actually choosing.
