I had two friends over for dinner (I cooked vegan chili so no stakes lol) and they got into an argument I couldn't referee. Maya has been vegan for years; Dev eats meat but says he tries to eat "responsibly." It started when Maya said that if Dev insists on eating meat at all, he should at least switch from chicken to beef: one cow gives you like a years worth of meals, while a chicken gives you one meal, so per meal you're killing vastly fewer animals. Dev said that was way too quick. Cows are way more cognitively sophisticated than chickens, he said, so if what matters is suffering and not just body count, you can't just count heads — one cow's life and death might matter more than a hundred chickens'. Maya said fine, weight it by something, but even weighted, chicken comes out way worse. Dev then said she was forgetting crop cultivation entirely: growing the feed for those chickens means tilling, harvesting, pesticides — shredding mice, snakes, and who knows how many insects — whereas pastured cattle need much less of that, so a beef meal might involve fewer deaths overall even counting all the tiny ones. Maya accused him of moving the goalposts: he started by doubting her body-count point, and when that didn't work he shifted to crop deaths, and anyway if you weight by how likely a creature is to suffer, insects barely register, so the crop-death point only works if you pretend consciousness doesn't come in degrees. Dev replied that her body-count rule was only ever a proxy for the real thing, and when your proxy and your target come apart you follow the target, not the proxy — otherwise you're optimizing the scoreboard instead of the score. Then they both looked at me and I honestly couldnt say who was winning.
