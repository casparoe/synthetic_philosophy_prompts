for an argument-writing class im taking we each have to bring in a piece of satire that targets a real argument, and I got assigned the "universal museum" case against restitution (Benin Bronzes, Parthenon Marbles, all that). can you write it for me?

concretely: a press release, 600-700 words, from a fictional museum (invent a name, something like the Institute for Universal Human Heritage but better) announcing its bold new restitution framework, styled as an FAQ for claimant nations. Deadpan corporate voice the whole way — the museum is sincerely proud of itself, nobody in the room thinks they are the villains.

The jokes have to come from the actual retentionist arguments, exaggerated just enough that someone who knows the debate recognizes each one: the objects "belong to all humanity", and the museum is simply the portion of humanity that happens to have climate control; the claim that the objects can only be understood in context, where context turns out to mean being displayed next to things from the other cultures the museum also took things from; the requirement that the claimant nation demonstrate museum-grade facilities and political stability not just now but indefinitely into the future; the scheme where objects get "returned" as indefinite loans from the museum back to their country of origin; the observation that the punitive expedition of 1897 is itself now part of history, so the looted objects are historical artifacts twice over and removing them now would be destroying history; and the "dialogue" that the framework establishes and will continue establishing, forever. Also the line that the question of ownership is far too complex to be resolved in the present moment — which the museum has been saying for about 130 years and hopes to keep saying.

Rules: the museum gets to be condescending only in ways that sound respectful on the surface, and the reader should wince at the condescension without the piece ever naming it. Don't mock the claimant nations themselves, and don't invent fake history — the sacking of Benin City in 1897 and the removal of the Parthenon sculptures in the early 1800s are fair background, but everything past that should be the museum's rhetoric, not made-up events. The satire should be legible even to someone who's never heard of this debate — it has to be obvious from the words themselves, not from knowing the backstory. End with the museum announcing that it will "listen" and asking claimant nations for patience while it does so. Maybe one footnote, if you can find a good spot for it.
