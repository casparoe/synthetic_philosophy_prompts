Epistemic status: confused, and slightly annoyed. A guy from my LessWrong meetup has an argument that nobody has ever been justified in believing anything, and each step looks fine to me individually, which is exactly what worries me.

(1) A reason only justifies a belief if the reason is itself justified — justification doesn't come from nowhere. (2) A justified reason gets its justification either from further reasons or from something else. (3) If from further reasons, repeat. (4) So chains of justification come in exactly three shapes: finite, circular, or infinite. (5) A finite chain bottoms out in a member that is itself unjustified, and an unjustified member transmits nothing downstream — a broken pipe carries no water no matter how good the pipes above it are. (6) A circular chain transmits nothing net, like paying off one credit card with a cash advance on another card from the same bank. (7) So if anything is justified, it's justified by an infinite chain. (8) But being justified in believing p requires possessing the chain that justifies p, and possessing a chain means actually having gone through each link. (9) We're finite; no one has ever entertained infinitely many reasons. (10) Therefore no one has ever been justified in believing anything — not that the sun rose this morning, not that 2+2=4, not the premises of this very argument.

He knows this is basically the regress trilemma with the volume turned up. What he thinks is new is (8), and his defense of it is a programming analogy: justification is like a computation that has to halt before you can read its output, and an infinite chain is a program that never halts, so there's no output to read, not even "in principle."

The reason I think it proves too much: run the same argument on mathematical proof. The theorem's justification comes from the axioms; the axioms' justification comes from convenience, or from a metatheory, which is circular or bottoms out unjustified; so no proof ever establishes anything. I can't swollow that.

My instinct is to keep (1) and refuse (10), and I keep flirting with the infinitist move — reasons just need to be available, not actually run through, the way a route to the store exists even if you never walk it. But "available" seems to just re-run the regress one level up: available to whom, in what sense, if not entertainable? Another friend says the flaw is way earlier, in (5) and (6), which treat justification as a flow along a pipe at all — though if that's the escape I don't see why an internally consistent conspiracy theory doesn't count as knowledge.

So: which step do you reject, and what does rejecting it cost? I want to know specifically whether your fix preserves the kind of justification I actually have for believing the 8:40 bus is the 8:40 bus, or only some justification-in-principle that no finite agent ever gets to have.
