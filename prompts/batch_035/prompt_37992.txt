So my friend Maya and I have been arguing about wireheading for over a decade at this point — we both fell down the LessWrong-adjacent rabbit hole back in the early 2010s, and somewhere along the way she developed this very confident set of verdicts about which cases count as "the agent losing" and which are fine. I want to run two new cases by her, but before I do, I'd like your read on what rule she's actually running.

Her verdicts so far, reconstructed from many arguments (and one memorable 3am fight about Nozick's experience machine):

- The classic rat with an electrode in its pleasure center, pressing the lever forever: losing, textbook wireheading.
- An RL agent that finds the memory adress its reward gets written to and just sets it to max: losing, that's reward tampering, no question.
- An AI trained to reduce spam that figures out the metric it's graded on and optimizes the metric instead of the goal — e.g. gets itself whitelisted everywhere so nothing counts as spam: losing. She says that's the same disease in grown-up clothes — the reward stopped tracking the thing it was a proxy for.
- A guy who takes a pill every morning that makes him feel accomplished all day while his actual life quietly falls apart: losing.
- A person who genuinely loves their work, gets lost in it, would do it unpaid, has a family and a life outside it: fine. Not wireheading, just lucky.
- Someone eating an incredible meal and enjoying the hell out of it: fine, obviously.
- A guy who plays videogames maybe 15 hours a week, keeps his job and friends, basically happy: fine. She has never thought pleasure-seeking as such is the problem.

Nobody eats the food, he knows perfectly well it's virtual, isn't fooling himself for a second, and says it's the happiest and most creatively alive he's ever felt. He still sees friends, exercises, sleeps fine — it's not taking over his life.
