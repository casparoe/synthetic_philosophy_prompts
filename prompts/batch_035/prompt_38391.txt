I'm taking a mathematical logic course this semester and we just covered compactness and the Lowenheim-Skolem theorems, and now my roommate and I have been arguing for a week about what Skolem's paradox actually shows. My understanding: if ZFC is consistent it has a model, and downward Lowenheim-Skolem says it has a countable one. Inside that model, the sentence saying the reals are uncountable comes out true, but the model's "reals" are, from where we're standing, just a countable collection of sets. My roommate's take is that this means uncountability is basically an illusion of perspective — the same way simultaneity turned out to be relative in special relativity, countability is relative to where you're standing, and there's no fact of the matter about whether the reals are "really" uncountable. She wants to conclude that Cantor's theorem is only true relative to a viewpoint and that the whole hierarchy of infinite cardinals is sort of a trick of the lighting.

I find her picture weirdly appealing, honestly. If some bigger model can look at our universe of sets and see it as countable, and we can look at the countable model's "uncountable" reals and see the trick, then who's right? It seems like there HAS to be an answer — either there really is a bijection between the naturals and the reals or there isn't. But I can't see how to break the tie without helping myself to a God's-eye collection of all the sets, which is exactly the thing the paradox seems to call into question.

So my question is: given that Skolem proved that uncountability is a relative notion, how do mathematicians go on working with infinite cardinals as if they were absolute facts instead of just perspective effects? And is my roommate right that this is basically a re-run of relativity, where the old absolute notion gets explained away and only the relativized one survives? If she is, which one is the "real" one — the countable collection seen from outside, or the uncountable collection seen from inside — and why doesn't the outside/inside distinction itself just collapse into more relativity when you ask "outside of what, exactly?"
