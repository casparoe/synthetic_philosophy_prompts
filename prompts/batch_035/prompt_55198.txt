I run a small philosophy reading group and want a "which theory of possibility do you actually hold?" quiz, written as a decision tree. Please give me about eight diagnostic questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options, followed by a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions: Lewisian modal realism, ersatzism (lumping its variants, or splitting them if the tree genuinely can), modal fictionalism, combinatorialism, and a deflationary option on which possible-worlds talk needs no metaphysical underwriting at all. The questions must sort readers by what they say about cases, not by which label they prefer, so no question may ask outright whether possible worlds exist or whether the reader believes in counterpart theory.

Work in at least these cases: (i) the Kripke-style complaint against Lewis — when we say someone could have won an election, is it that very person who wins somewhere else, or only someone relevantly like him?; (ii) a world just like ours except for one extra electron in a distant galaxy that nothing in our world could ever detect — a genuinely different world, or not a way things could be?; (iii) a world containing a property that nothing actual instantiates; (iv) a completely empty world, with no concrete things at all; (v) two qualitatively identical worlds in which the same individuals exchange roles — are there two such worlds there, or only one?; (vi) whether a plain modal claim like "there could have been talking donkeys" is literally true, and if so, what makes it true.

Two craft requirements: phrase every node so a reader can answer it without knowing which position it leads to, and, after the key, flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot really separate (for instance, whether any question in your tree distinguishes brands of ersatzism). Also note any combination of answers you have allowed that no actual position occupies, with one sentence on whether that gap reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or mere neglect. Finally, say how confident you are in the mappings overall and single out the one question you are least sure does real sorting work.
