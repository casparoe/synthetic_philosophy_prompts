Hofstadter's Careenium (in *I Am a Strange Loop*) imagines a vast, frictionless billiard table on which thousands of small marbles — "simms" — roll and collide under strictly Newtonian rules. When simms cluster together, the clusters — "simmballs" — glide around the table as stable, self-maintaining shapes, and we are invited to treat them as symbols: the level at which the system has patterns, meanings, something like a self. The delicate question is whether the simmball level can be said to *do* anything — whether symbols push the substrate around — or whether talk of simmballs is only a compressed way of describing what the simms were going to do anyway.

Write a pantoum about the Careenium in which the form carries the philosophical weight. In a pantoum, the second and fourth lines of each stanza return, verbatim, as the first and third lines of the next, and the final stanza folds back to retrieve the poem's own opening lines, closing on the very line it began with. I want that machinery to be the argument. Each recurring line is the same substrate — the identical words — but each time it recurs it should have been shoved into a new role by the lines surrounding it, so that the reader can interpret the recurrence either way: as nothing over and above the previous collisions of lines, or as the pattern level bending the marbles to its purpose. The closing return to the opening line should enact the strange loop itself: identical words that no longer mean what they meant, with the poem leaving it genuinely open whether that shift was accomplished from below or from above.

A few constraints: five to seven quatrains; repeated lines must be exactly repeated, not varied; the poem must neither assert the symbols' causal power nor deny it — its task is to make both readings of the same recurrence available at once; and please avoid the words "emergence," "levels," "downward," and "causation," since the point is to stage the problem rather than label it. The result should be accurate enough that someone who knows the Careenium recognizes what the form is doing, and good enough that someone who doesn't still receives a poem rather than a diagram. Please write the poem, together with any brief note you append on how the form is doing this work, in British English.
