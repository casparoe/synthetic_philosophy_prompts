I'm presenting a paper at a graduate workshop on distributive justice. The thesis: the leveling-down objection fails against telic egalitarianism once the egalitarian's "there is something good" about leveling down is read as a claim about a pro tanto component of value rather than about overall betterness — a world in which the better-off are lowered and no one gains is worse all things considered, but it is more equal, and the fact that this residual good benefits no one no more disqualifies it than the fact that a pleasurable distraction can be part of an overall worse life disqualifies hedonism. The audience is mostly prioritarians who followed Parfit from equality to priority, plus two act-utilitarians and one luck egalitarian. Prepare my Q&A sheet: the objections I will actually face, in roughly the order they will come, phrased in the idiom each group actually uses — the prioritarian's charge that pro tanto equality is just the person-neutral value he abandoned for a reason, the utilitarian's demand for a decision procedure once value comes in competing components, the luck egalitarian's claim that deontic equality does all the work my telic component is supposed to do — each with a two-minute reply, a note on where the reply is soft, and what I should concede up front. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks.
