hey, so quick context: I cover science and ideas for a news outlet, and I moonlight teaching a night section of intro astronomy at the community college. We just did the unit on life in the universe and I could use a second pair of eyes grading one midterm answer. Heres the question I asked, followed by what one student wrote.

**Question (10 points):** SETI has detected no technosignatures since it began in 1960. Does this silence give us reason to believe technological civilizations are rare in our galaxy? Explain what the silence does and doesn't show, and give at least one consideration that strengthens the inference and one that weakens it.

**Student's answer:** Fermi raised this back in 1950 with his famous question, where is everybody? The Fermi paradox is that if technological civilizations were common, the galaxy is old enough (about 13 billion years) that some would have arisen billions of years before us, and even expanding at a small fraction of lightspeed they would have colonized every system by now. Their absence therefore means they aren't out there, and the more sky we've searched without hearing anything, the more confident we can be in that conclusion. SETI has now been listening for over 60 years and has covered the sky pretty thoroughly, so the silence at this point amounts to strong evidence that technological life is rare. The Drake equation breaks the number of transmitting civilizations into factors, and while we don't know most of the factors, the search failure is best explained by the Great Filter: some step on the path from non-life to spacefaring life is nearly impossible. And since we ourselves made it all the way here, the filter must lie in our past, meaning the hard step was probably abiogenesis or the jump to complex life. So yes, the silence shows technological civilizations are rare.
