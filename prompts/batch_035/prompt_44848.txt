Construct a pair of thought experiments about responsibility gaps for autonomous systems, engineered to pull apart two things that usually travel together: the epistemic opacity of the system and the existence of a responsibility gap. In the first scenario, an autonomous system causes a serious harm through a chain that is fully transparent — every design and deployment decision documented, every step of its behavior traceable by anyone who cares to look — and yet, when the chain is traced, no individual human should come out clearly responsible for the harm. In the second, the system's operation must be opaque to every human in the loop, and yet responsibility should attach cleanly and defensibly to one or more specific people. The point of the pair is to test whether opacity is really what generates responsibility gaps, or whether some other structural feature — something about how control is distributed across agents and over time — is doing the work, and, if so, to identify it. Constraints: keep both scenarios realistic (a hospital, a warehouse, an agricultural operation — no trolleys, no weaponized systems), one paragraph each, and do not smuggle in the verdicts by stipulation; they should follow from the facts of the case. Then deliver everything as a single JSON object with the following schema: "scenario_transparent" and "scenario_opaque" (the one-paragraph scenarios); "agents_transparent" and "agents_opaque" (each an array of objects with the fields "name", "role", "control" (what this person could actually influence), "foreseeability" (what they could reasonably have anticipated), "responsible" (a boolean), and "reasoning" (one or two sentences)); "isolated_feature" (the structural feature that actually opens the gap, with an explanation of how it operates in the transparent case and why its absence lets the opaque case close); "removal_test" (what happens to each scenario's verdicts if that feature is removed while everything else is held fixed); and "objection" (the strongest reply someone who holds that gaps arise from opacity would make, and whether the cases survive it).
