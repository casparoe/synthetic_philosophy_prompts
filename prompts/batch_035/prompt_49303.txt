As a novelist nearing the end of a long manuscript, I have assembled a panel of twenty-one beta readers, each of them a seasoned consumer of psychological thrillers. Before any group discussion takes place, I ask each reader separately two questions: at which chapter did you first suspect the ending, and did you find the final twist satisfying. I intend to treat the panel's answers like a jury's verdict, using majority opinion to decide whether the twist is genuinely too predictable and must be rewritten. Please describe the most important implications of the Condorcet jury theorem for how I should interpret this procedure. Bear in mind that my readers, though individually perceptive, have all absorbed the same genre conventions and read the same bestselling authors, so their mistaken hunches about where the plot is headed may be correlated rather than independent. Present your answer as a numbered list of exactly five items, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
