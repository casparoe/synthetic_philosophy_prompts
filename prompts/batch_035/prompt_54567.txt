Two people in our discussion group have been arguing about Thomson's lamp for over a year and its gotten stale. One of them is basically on Thomson's side: she thinks the lamp story shows that completing an infinite set of tasks in finite time is incoherent, and that the lamp's state at the end being neither on nor off reveals a genuine contradiction buried in the very idea of a supertask. The other is basically on Benacerraf's side: he thinks Thomson's setup simply fails to *specify* the final state, that this is just an underdetermination problem rather than a contradiction, and that you can even rig variants where the final state IS determined (say, by adding an extra flip at the limit) without anything going wrong. Please draft the joint statement they should have produced after a proper adversarial collaboration.

What I want in it:

- A numbered section of claims both can sign. This should cover things like: what the lamp description does and doesn't fix (button pressed at t=1, released at 1/2, pressed at 3/4, and so on); that "how many times was the button pressed" has no answer in the infinite case and that this by itself is not yet a contradiction; and what the parity argument (odd number of presses → off, even → on) legitimately shows and where it overreaches — they should be able to agree that the parity reasoning works for finite collections but doesn't automatically transfer.
- A disagreements table: each residual disagreement stated so that its own side would accept the wording as fair. I suspect at least one of the disagreements is partly verbal — maybe over whether "task" has to be something with a completion time, versus just being one event in a sequence. Please say explicitly which parts of the dispute are verbal and which are real.
- The cruxes. My guesses: (1) whether the underdetermination of the final state is a benign feature of an incomplete description, or evidence that no complete description of a supertask is even possible in principle; (2) whether physical implementability matters for the coherence question, or whether coherence is settled purely at the level of the story/math; and maybe (3) whether a variant lamp where the final state is stipulated (Benacerraf's extra-switch-at-the-limit move) shows supertasks are coherent, or only shows that a different story got quietly swapped in.
- For each crux, a concrete case, argument, or thought experiment that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Some candidates they might use: a lamp rigged so the final state is stipulated on with everything else unchanged; a machine in Max Black's style running through the sequence, where we ask separately about the machine's own condition at the limit; or pressing on what happens if you insist every state-change needs a cause and then ask what, if anything, caused the final state. But dont feel bound to these — if you see a better test, use it.

One ground rule: both of them accept standard mathematics (convergent series, limits). The document should not take sides on finitism, and it should not pretend the disagreement is about whether 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ... converges — it doesn't, and both know it. Keep the tone casual but the formulations precise, and no citations or reading lists — just the arguments. Format: numbered agreed-claims section, then the disagreements table, then the cruxes with predictions.
