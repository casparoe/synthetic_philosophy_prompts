Is there an established term for the following? I'm the kind of person who's terrible at resisting temptation in the moment, so years ago I gave up on resisting and switched to making sure the moment never happens in the first place. Ice cream: I don't keep it in the house, so the 10pm "choice" between eating it and not eating it simply doesn't arise -- there's nothing in the freezer to be tempted by. Phone: it charges in the kitchen overnight, so the "should I check it in bed" deliberation never gets a chance to start. Same with taking a route home that doesn't pass the bakery, and telling the waiter "nothing for me, thanks" before the dessert menu appears rather than after. My friends call this discipline or willpower, but it feels like the opposite of willpower -- I almost never test mine, because the confrontation is always scheduled for a time and place where it can't occur.

Now, some things it's near but, I think, not identical to. Akrasia is the failure mode, the thing I'm trying to avoid, not the strategy itself. People also talk about Ulysses contracts -- binding your future self -- and that's close, but Ulysses tied to the mast still hears the Sirens and has to stand there enduring them; the binding is what lets him survive the encounter. My move is to not be in the same room as the Sirens at all. The struggle is skipped, not survived. That difference seems real to me, but I can't tell whether the literature treats it as a real difference or just a subtype.
