My brother-in-law and I have been having the same arguement every thanksgiving for like five years now and I want to see it actually written down properly. He's a startup guy, works probably 70 hours a week, genuinely loves it, and his whole worldview is basically the protestant work ethic with the religion taken out — he thinks hard work is what makes a life meaningful, that people who push for shorter work weeks are just trying to dodge being judged by their output, and that countries with 35-hour weeks are basically choosing comfortable mediocrity. I'm the opposite: I've read about karoshi in Japan (people literally dying from overwork — heart attacks at 45, suicides tied to exhaustion) and it seems obvious to me that a work ethic that only stops when your body stops isn't an ethic at all, it's just an appetite that's been dressed up in moral language. But the honest thing is, when I actually argue with him, my side cheats a bit. Like I point at the overwork deaths, and he says those are a bug of Japanese workplace culture specifically, not of working hard itself, and I don't really have a clean answer for where the line is.

So here's what I want: write a dialogue between the two of us, but make us both smart. He should NOT be a hustle-bro caricature — he's someone who really believes the discipline you build at work is where dignity comes from, and he's ready for the karoshi point (his move is that any good thing in excess kills you, water included, so overwork deaths count against the ethic no more than liver failure counts against enjoying dinner). And my side should get its best version too: the case that the 35-hour week isn't about laziness but about leisure being its own separate good — time where you're not producing anything and that's the whole point — and that if your work ethic can't say what all the work is FOR, it's just a treadmill with virtue words painted on it.

Somewhere in there I want them to actually hit the hard question instead of talking past each other: if a rich country gave everyone a shorter week, would the freed-up time actually become that good leisure, or would it just fill up with work-adjacent anxiety — checking email, side hustles, "investing in yourself" — so that nothing really changed? That's the part neither of us has an answer to at thanksgiving. It's fine if neither of them fully wins, honestly that would be more realistic, but each of them should land at least one real blow the other has to actually respond to. Maybe 900-1200 words, casual register, they can interrupt each other and get a little heated but stay family.
