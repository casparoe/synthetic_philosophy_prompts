I've read it so many times it's starting to look normal to me, and shes on a deadline, so I'd love a second pair of eyes. Here's the paragraph:

"In 1963 the philosopher Edmund Gettier demolished the classical analysis of knowledge in a famous three-page paper. The analysis, which goes back to Plato, said that to know something is to hold a belief that is both justified and true. Gettier constructed two counterexamples in which someone has a belief that is justified and true but only true by luck, and so does not count as knowledge. His own diagnosis was that in these cases the subject's justification runs through a false assumption — a false lemma — and his proposed fix was to add a fourth condition: the justification must not contain any falsehoods. He concluded that knowledge cannot be analyzed in terms of belief, truth, and justification at all, and that the search for any definition of knowledge is hopeless."
