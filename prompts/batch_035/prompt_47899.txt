Here's the answer I have to grade:

"Multiple realizability (MR) is the claim that the same mental state type can be realized by lots of different physical states. Putnam's point was that pain in humans might be C-fiber firing, but octopuses or aliens could have pain too even with completely different neurochemistry, so pain can't be identical to any one brain state type. Since type identity theory says each mental type just is a physical type, MR refutes it directly. And because MR is a conceptual truth — it follows just from the definition of mental states as functional states — the identity theorist can't hope that more neuroscience will rescue them. The argument is a priori, so no amount of empirical progress can undo it.

What's left standing is functionalism: mental states are individuated by their causal role, by what typically causes them and what behavior they in turn cause. Any state that plays that role counts as the mental state, no matter what it's made of. This also neatly handles the qualia problem, since functionalism individuates states by role rather than by any intrinsic feel. Putnam also gave the china-body thought experiment, where the entire population of China imitates a brain by passing messages via radio, and since the system plays the right causal role it would literally have a mind. That seems like a reductio of functionalism being too liberal, but honestly I think it just shows our concept of mind is broader than we assumed. Either way, MR is why basically everyone abandoned identity theory: if pain tokens are realized by different physical states in different species, mental states can't be identified with brain states even at the token level."
