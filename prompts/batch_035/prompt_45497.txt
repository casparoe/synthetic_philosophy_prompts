My best friend and I have been having the same argument for basically two years now, and our other friends are so tired of it. The topic is whether "collapse" is actually a real thing that happens to societies. I'm in the camp that some societies genuinely fail: the Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, where palaces burned, long-distance trade networks came apart, and whole writing systems died out; the Terminal Classic Maya, with cities in the southern lowlands abandoned; maybe Easter Island with its deforestation. She thinks "collapse" is basically a category analysts impose, not something that happens to the people living through it: the Bronze Age "collapse" is a cluster of transitions where some institutions ended but people adapted, migrated, and kept transmitting whatever mattered to them; the Maya didn't vanish, populations shifted and the northern cities did fine for centuries afterward; Easter Islanders are literally still there. Her line, which stings because I can't fully rebut it, is that calling a period a failure is a judgment made from the perspective of elites and outside observers, and the word does quiet political work — it frames change as breakdown so that whatever comes after looks like a loss rather than a reorganization.
