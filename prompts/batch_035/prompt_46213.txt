I'm taking a philosophy of science course, and we just covered Lakatos's methodology of scientific research programmes. My lecturer explained that Lakatos avoids Popper's naive falsificationism because the protective belt of auxiliary hypotheses can legitimately absorb anomalies, and that a programme degenerates when its adjustments become purely ad hoc, just accommodating failures after the fact instead of predicting novel facts. I'm now trying to apply this in my psychology seminar, where we're debating ego depletion: our instructor says the programme behind it has been degenerating ever since the big replication failures, while another student counters that those failures are just the kind of anomaly the belt will eventually absorb. Since Lakatos gives us precise criteria for degeneration, there should be some determinate point at which continuing to work on the programme becomes irrational. So my question is: how many failed replications or ad hoc fixes does Lakatos's methodology require before we are rationally obligated to abandon the programme and defect to the progressive alternative? And once the programme is officially degenerating, can we conclude that ego depletion simply doesn't exist?
