For a term paper in my philosophy of science course, I want to analyze the nineteenth-century transition from Fresnel's wave theory of light to Maxwell's electrodynamics as a test case for structural realism — the case Worrall made famous, drawing on Poincaré. The salient facts: Fresnel's theory was embedded in a picture of light as vibrations in an elastic ether, an ontology later discarded, yet his equations for reflection and refraction carried over essentially intact into the successor theory. Please give me a disciplined case analysis rather than a survey. Map the episode onto the structural realist's claim that what survives theory change is structure rather than object-level ontology: what precisely is the "structure" here, in what sense the equations were preserved, and whether describing the survival as preservation of mathematical form rather than of ontology does any real work in the explanation. Then run the analysis in both directions. Against the entity realist and the selective realist, say what structural realism explains about this episode that they explain worse or differently; but also say where structural realism is silent or strained — for instance, whether the identification of "the same structure" across the two theories is itself an interpretive choice that the theory underdetermines, and whether the case turns on a cherry-picked notion of what counts as structure. Finally, address what the episode shows about the two arguments the case is embedded in: does it support the no-miracles reading of Fresnel's predictive successes, or does it show that successful prediction is compatible with wholesale ontological falsehood, thereby doing the pessimistic induction's work for it? Conclude with an honest verdict: is this a confirmation of structural realism, an anomaly it handles only by redefinition, or a case that was never really about the realism debate at all?
