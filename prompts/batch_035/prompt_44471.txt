so me and a friend have been going back and forth for a couple years about whether genuine moral dilemmas exist, and somewhere along the way I think I actually settled into a position. I want to write it up for our reading group but first I'd like someone to check it for cracks. Here's what I believe, roughly in order of how settled each one feels:

1. Genuine dilemmas happen. There are cases where every option available to you is morally wrong — not just worse than some alternative, but wrong, full stop. Think of the Sartre case: the guy torn between staying to look after his mother and slipping off to join the resistance. If he leaves, he abandons her; if he stays, he betrays the cause. I want to say he does something wrong either way, and that's exactly what makes it a genuine dilemma and not just a sad choice between two okay options.

2. But 'ought' implies 'can'. Nobody is ever obligated to do the impossible. This one feels non-negotiable to me — it's what keeps morality from going insane.

3. If you ought to do A and you ought to do B, then you ought to do A and B. When this came up in the argument I said yes almost without thinking — if I'm obliged to feed the cat and obliged to send the email, surely I'm obliged to do both.

4. In the tragic cases, the person who does the best available thing is still not off the hook. They ought to feel guilty, and an apology is actually owed to whoever lost out. And I don't mean guilt as a useful social signal — I mean the guilt is fitting, there's something real it's responding to. Plain regret, like what you feel when your project fails through no fault of your own, seems like the wrong emotion for these cases.

5. Once you've done what you ought to do, all things considered, morality is finished with you. There's no further moral verdict left to render on you; whatever remains is just psychology, and while that matters it's not morality's business anymore.

My friend keeps insisting the whole list can't be true at once, but when I press her on which specific line has to go she just says "the whole picture", which isn't alot of help. It seems wierd to me because each item on its own sounds boring and obvious. So: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and for each genuine one what's the cheapest fix and what does it cost me? If you think one of them has to go entirely, tell me which and why that one rather than the others. And please don't be diplomatic and tell me it all works with the right footnotes — I want a ranking.
