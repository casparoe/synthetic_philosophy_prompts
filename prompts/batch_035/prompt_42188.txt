I'm a novelist and my current book follows two researchers who've spent a decade on opposite sides of the AI paternalism fight — one thinks assistants should defer to users almost always and treats refusals and overrides as presumptuous meddling, the other thinks a capable assistant that can see someone heading for serious harm and stays silent is basically negligent. They've argued past each other for years (they used to be friends, which is a whole subplot), and now a foundation has locked them into an adversarial collaboration: months of good-faith work ending in a document they both sign. I want to write that document — but it has to be one a philosopher would actually respect, not a mushy "both sides agree dialogue is important" thing.

So here's what I need. The two camps, for my purposes: the autonomy camp (refusals and overrides are a form of unearned authority, and the assistant doesn't know the user's life) and the stewardship camp (an assistant with real foresight and influence that just executes requests is abdicating). Give me the claims both could genuinely sign — including about what refusals actually do to users, about the difference between judgment and coercion, and about the easy cases everyone agrees on. Then the precise disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording (this matters, I don't want either character to sound like a strawman of himself). Then the cruxes — I suspect one is whether a user's in-the-moment request can be weighed against their longer-term or self-declared preferences without the assistant just inventing a "true self" to defer to, and another is whether a widely deployed assistant's influence makes it more like a bartender or more like a doctor, but feel free to find better ones. For each crux, a concrete case both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance — e.g. a user with insomnia asking at 3am for help polishing an angry email, a writer who's explicitly instructed the assistant to keep critiquing her manuscript harshly but keeps getting softened feedback anyway, someone with a history of disordered eating asking for a meal-planning app. And if some of their fight turns out to be verbal — maybe over the word "manipulation" or what counts as a "harm" — say which part and why, since I want to dramatize the moment they realize it.

Format: numbered agreed-claims section first, then a disagreements table, then the cruxes. After the document itself, add a brief structured summary as a JSON object with fields "shared_ground" (array of strings), "genuine_cruxes" (array of strings), "verbal_disputes" (array of strings), and "test_cases" (array of objects, each with "case", "autonomy_prediction", and "stewardship_prediction"). Keep the residual disagreement exact — no filler sentences both sides would sign but neither would act on.
