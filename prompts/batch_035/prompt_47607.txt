I got into an argument at a dinner with some physics grad students (a friend's birthday, they took over the conversation) about the Aspect/Clauser/Zeilinger experiments, and someone ended my question by repeating what seems to be the official slogan of the whole field since the 2022 Nobel announcement: "local realism is dead." My question had been whether the correlations in a Bell test mean anything is actually passing between the two detectors — some influence, signal, whatever — and the slogan was deployed like it obviously answered "no, nothing passes, the universe just isn't locally real, next topic." The thing is, I don't think anyone at that table could actually unpack the phrase, and honestly neither can I, which bugs me. So I want your help laying out what it could mean.

Since "local realism" sounds like a conjunction, I'd want the menu organized around what the conjuncts even are. On the "local" side, I've noticed people slide between what look like different commitments — no faster-than-light causal influence of any kind, no dependence of one wing's outcome on the distant detector setting, Bell's own later factorization/local-causality condition — and I've also seen a distinction drawn between dependence on the distant setting versus dependence on the distant outcome, where supposedly only one of those is compatible with no-signaling. On the "realism" side it's even worse: hidden variables exist, results are predetermined at the source, there are facts about unperformed measurements, or just vaguely that the theory is about a mind-independent world. For each precise reading you can construct, tell me whether it's plausible, whether the experiments plus the theorem actually rule it out, and — the part I care about most — whether that reading supports the use the slogan was put to at dinner, i.e., a flat "no, nothing travels." My suspicion is the honest story is something like: local causality in Bell's sense is dead, the no-signaling theorem separately guarantees you can't exploit the correlations to send a message, and the word "realism" in the slogan may not be doing any work at all — but I want it as a proper laid-out list with verdicts, not a vibes paragraph, so I can bring it to the next dinner.
