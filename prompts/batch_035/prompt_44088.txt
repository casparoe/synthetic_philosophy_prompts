I came to philosophy of science sideways, through years of reading and arguing about interpretations of quantum mechanics on the usual blogs and forums, and the argument that kept coming up was the no-miracles inference: success needs explanation, and the explanation that pays its way is some species of realism. That led me to the structural realism literature, and I've been watching that debate for years now without anyone moving an inch. The setup, as I understand it: Worrall's answer to the pessimistic meta-induction is that what survives theory change is structure — the mathematics, roughly — and the Newman objection replies that a claim of pure structure, shorn of all non-structural constraints, becomes trivially satisfiable, so structural realism can't say anything unless it sneaks structure back in. The ontic structural realists then take the apparently radical route: no objects ontologically prior to relations, particles individuated entirely by their place in the symmetry structure. And there the debate sits — decades in, with the same moves, the same counterexamples, and a suspicious amount of the argument turning on exactly what "structure" is supposed to denote. My working hypothesis, which I'd like you to test rather than flatter, is that the whole dispute rests on an equivocation: "structure" sometimes means the syntactic or formal profile of a theory, which is the thing that plausibly survives from Fresnel to Maxwell, and sometimes the relational organization of the world itself, which is the thing the metaphysics of "relations without relata" is about. If that's right, then the epistemic/ontic split may just be two different research programs — one in epistemology of science about inter-theoretic retention, one in metaphysics about objecthood — sharing a label and litigating their apparent disagreement. But maybe I'm wrong, and there is a genuine hidden question underneath, for instance whether symmetry structure in fundamental physics really does all the individuating work the ontic structural realist needs, or what could even count as "bearing" a relation in the absence of relata. Diagnose the disagreement for me: verbal, substantive-but-stalled, or ill-posed; say which with reasons drawn from what the sides actually argue; and then tell me honestly what would count as progress given your diagnosis — a sharper formal distinction, a worked example from physics, or nothing short of retiring the question — and, if the answer is the last one, what we should be asking instead.
