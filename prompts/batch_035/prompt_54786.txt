I'm a third-year undergraduate taking a seminar on formal epistemology, and we've just spent three weeks on the Sleeping Beauty problem. Before the unit I assumed it was a curiosity, but the amount of ink it has generated suggests otherwise, so I've been working through Elga's original paper and Lewis's reply in preparation for a term paper. The trouble is that I've reached a conclusion that seems too clean to be right, and I'd like to be disabused of it before I commit anything to paper.

Here is my reconstruction. Elga's argument for the answer of one-third looks to me like nothing more than a straightforward Bayesian update: Beauty wakes up, registers the evidence that she is awake and being interviewed today, and conditionalizes. Since that experiential evidence arises twice as often under tails as under heads, conditionalization moves her credence in tails from one-half to one-third, and refusing to move just is refusing to conditionalize on admissible evidence. On top of that, my tutor sketched what he called a Dutch book against the halfer: if Beauty's credence in heads is one-half, she will pay even odds for a ticket on heads at every awakening, and the bookie profits from her in expectation on every run of the experiment, because she buys the losing ticket once under heads but the winning ticket only once under tails — she never gets to double up on the outcome the ticket pays on. Between a conditionalization proof and a demonstration that her odds are exploitable, halferism appears to be mathematically refuted rather than merely counterintuitive, in the way that a clever-looking roulette system is refuted.

So my question is: given all this, why does anyone still defend the halfer position, let alone someone of Lewis's caliber? Is the surviving halfer literature just motivated clinging to an intuition about a fair coin, or have halfers actually located a technical gap in the proof — and if so, where exactly does it open? I want to understand where the real fault line runs before I write anything, because at the moment it looks to me like a math problem with a settled answer, and I assume the debate's persistence means I've misidentified what's being debated. For instance, I can't currently see what a halfer could even say was wrong with the step where Beauty conditionalizes on being awake, and I suspect that's exactly where I've gone wrong.
