Write a dialogue between a Lange-style market socialist and a Hayekian economist, set in the present day, where a government has just announced it will build an AI-powered planning bureau that ingests real-time consumer data and sets producer-goods prices algorithmically. The Langean thinks this finally vindicates the trial-and-error solution: Lange himself said the market could be simulated by managers instructed to adjust prices in response to shortages and gluts, so why couldn't a system that observes shortages and gluts at much higher frequency do it better? The Hayekian should press on the difference between the knowledge problem as a data problem and as a dispersed, tacit, contextual knowledge problem -- prices aren't just signals to be read off but condensed information that only gets generated when people actually face tradeoffs, including entrepreneurs who don't yet exist to report their would-be innovations. Both should be smart and charitable -- the Langean should have real replies (e.g., that Hayek's own tacit knowledge argument applies to firm managers inside capitalism too, and that internal prediction markets or role-playing mechanisms inside the planning system could generate some of that disciplining pressure), and the Hayekian should get to refine rather than just repeat the objection. I want them to actually move the debate forward -- maybe ending on what experiment or institutional detail would settle it between them, rather than on either one conceding or giving a speech. Keep it to maybe 600-900 words of dialogue.
