In my design studio, whenever the question of AI-generated images and creativity comes up, someone inevitably reaches for the photography precedent: when photography arrived in the nineteenth century, painters and critics dismissed it as mere mechanical image-making that would displace real art, yet within a couple of generations photographs were accepted as works of art, and today no one thinks the camera's involvement disqualifies the photographer. The conclusion my colleagues draw is that resistance to AI images is the same panic and will look equally quaint within a decade, and — the part I actually care about — that the analogy also tells us where the creativity resides: with the person making the artistic choices rather than the machine, just as the photographer, not the camera, is the artist.

Please audit this analogy rather than simply endorsing or rejecting it. Lay out the full correspondence first: what in the photography story plays the role of the camera, of the photographer's creative choices (framing, timing, subject, darkroom manipulation), of the years of craft training those choices rested on, of the painters whose livelihoods were threatened, and of the art world's eventual accommodation. Then identify where the mapping breaks: which disanalogies genuinely matter for the conclusion being drawn (both that AI images can be art and that the creativity is located in the human prompter) and which are incidental. Pay particular attention to whether the analogy, once examined carefully, actually supports the second conclusion as smoothly as it supports the first — or even argues for something closer to the opposite. Finish by saying whether the intended conclusions survive on independent grounds once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was doing all the work.

Format: return the correspondence audit as a table with the columns photography-side element; AI-side counterpart; maps cleanly (yes/partially/no); whether the mismatch matters for the conclusion, with a one-sentence reason. After the table, give a brief structured verdict of no more than 150 words stating (i) which conclusion survives and which does not, and (ii) what a better analogy, if any, would be.
