Quick recap so we're on the same page: a soldier is dropped somewhere that's half Red army territory and half Blue, and within Red there are two zones she can't tell apart. Before anything, she's 50/50 on Red vs Blue, and if Red, 50/50 between the two zones. Then a radio message tells her, roughly: if you're in Red territory, the odds are 3 to 1 you're in the first zone. That's it, nothing at all about the case where she's in Blue. The question is what her new beliefs should be, and the fun part is that none of the obvious tools give a clean anwser: straight conditioning can't be applied because she isn't certain she's in Red, and even the fancy fix for partial evidence (something like Jeffrey conditioning, "probability kinematics"?) seems to imply nothing should change at all, which feels definately wrong. When I dug around, some people argued the right answer involves a "square root rule," others said there just is no single right answer — and honestly, that tension is what I want the session to land on.

My guesses on the misconceptions, for what it's worth: that you can just condition on the antecedent (she got info about Red, so update as if she's in Red), that Bayes' theorem is a universal hammer, and that if there's no formula then the question must be meaningless.
