I help run a small ethics discussion group (not academics, just people who like arguing over drinks) and next month's topic is pest control -- like, is spraying your kitchen for ants actually OK? I want a taxonomy handout from you, formatted as a decision tree, that sorts people into a position on whether and when insecticides and pest control are permissible.

A few things I care about, since the generic "are insects sentient?" framing gets stale fast:

1. One branch question has to be about the deterrent-vs-killer distinction, which I think is underrated. A repellent, a fence, sealing the cracks -- these stop insects from ever being in your kitchen, whereas bait poisons the ones already living there. Some people will say deterring future ants is fine even if killing the current colony isn't, which is a weird procreation-flavored asymmetry, and I want a node that catches whether someone actually draws that line.

2. Another node should be about whether "pest" is a relational fact. A termite chewing a dead log in the forest is just doing termite stuff; the same termite in your house's beam is a pest. If preventing property damage can license killing, is it the termite's location that matters, or the damage, or neither?

3. A third on uncertainty about invertebrate sentience -- does the mere possibility that insects feel something force a precautionary approach, or is it fine to act on your best estimate that they mostly don't?

The deliverable: a tree where every branch node is phrased as a question someone could answer without having any idea which position it leads to, and each leaf carries a name (use a real recognizable view if there is one, "unnamed" if not) plus one sentence on that leaf's most awkward commitment. Build the tree from the questions and the logical space first, then afterwards say where actual positions land in it. Note any answer combinations nobody holds, and say whether you think that's because the combo is incoherent, just unattractive, or merely unexplored. And flag any two leaves your tree can't genuinely seperate rather than pretending it's fine.

Keep it usable by non-philosophers -- concrete cases at each node (kitchen ants, a wasp nest over the daycare entrance, neighborhood-wide mosquito spraying, farm pesticide runoff into a stream), not abstract principles. Aim for something like 6 to 9 leaves. No citations or reading lists needed, I just want the tree itself so I can photocopy it.
