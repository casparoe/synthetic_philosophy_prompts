I run a small rationalist-adjacent blog and I want to build one of those "which position do you actually hold?" quizzes, this time on the Dennett vs. Caruso free will debate. The complication, and the reason most existing quizzes suck for this: Dennett and Caruso famously agree on like 90% of practical stuff. Neither wants to gut the justice system overnight, both like rehabilitation, both think psychopaths shouldn't just be dumped on the street, Caruso's preferred system (quarantine-style incapacitation plus a big public-health push on the causes of crime) and Dennett's preferred system on a normal day are barely distinguishable. So a quiz keyed to policy questions can't separate them at all, and I've noticed most people who read the debate come away saying "huh, they basically agree" when actually they disagree about something deep underneath. That's what I want the quiz to find.

So the questions have to be cases that split the *underlying* commitments, not the policy preferences. Some cases I want in there (use these or better ones you come up with):

- The "could have done otherwise" intuition pump: an agent, in the *exact* same past and laws, does the bad thing. Is there any sense of "could have" that matters for deserving suffering, or is the question malformed?
- Whether an agent who deserves blame can deserve suffering *for its own sake*, as opposed to suffering that's instrumental (deterrence, moral education, protecting others). My hunch is this is where Dennett and Caruso actually come apart but I've never seen it cleanly separated.
- A "luck swap" case: two people, identical upbringing, identical genes basically, one gets caught in circumstances where the crime succeeds and one where it fizzles out through no choice of their own. Does either *deserve* more?
- The Whitman-style brain tumor case, but pushed further: how much of the tumor do you have to remove before the guy deserves full blame again? If your answer is "any physical difference changes nothing, desert is about capacities not etiology" or the opposite, that's diagnostic.
- Whether feeling guilty has any intrinsic role, i.e. is guilt appropriate because the person deserved it, or just useful, or actually bad?
- Whether the entire universe were deterministic vs. indeterministic-with-randomness changes anything. (If someone says randomness helps, that's a tell.)

Requirements: 8-10 questions, each a concrete case with 2-4 answer options, phrased so someone can answer without knowing any of the jargon — no question should be "are you a compatibilist?" dressed up. Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto at least these leaves: classic compatibilist just-deserts (Dennett-ish), hard incompatibilist / free will skeptic with the forward-looking replacement (Caruso/Pereboom-ish), libertarian free will (yes some of my readers believe this, they should be sortable too), and a revisionist middle position where desert exists but is way smaller than everyday practice assumes. If your tree can't cleanly separate revisionism from Dennett-style compatibilism, or libertarianism from something else, say so rather than papering over it. And for each *combination* of answers that's logically possible but matches no named position, note it and guess why nobody holds it (incoherent? just unattractive?).

Also: since my audience will absolutely try to game the quiz to get the answer they like, write the questions so the "obviously correct-feeling" answer isn't always the same position's answer. Maybe shuffle which option letters map where, or tell me how you'd randomize it. One last thing — the scoring key should include, for each position, the single follow-up question I could ask in the results page that would most likely make someone realize they're actually in the wrong bucket. Sort people by what they say about cases, not which label they vibe with.
