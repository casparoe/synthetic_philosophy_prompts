my friend and I had a raging argument on the train home about who's to blame for WWI and it turned philosophical real fast. he's all in on the Fischer side — Germany's leadership in 1914 deliberately risked and kind of wanted a continental war — while I kept pushing the more Clark-style "sleepwalkers" picture where everyone stumbled in through alliances and mobilization timetables. but the thing we actually couldn't settle was what it even means to hold a nation responsible for a war. Fischer himself mostly blamed a small elite around Bethmann Hollweg and the general staff, yet the Versailles war guilt clause and popular opinion at the time went after Germany as such, and my friend thought that was fine since the elite was acting for the nation. I think that's too quick — a nation isn't the kind of thing that decides anything. so: write an essay on whether a nation can be held responsible for a war, using the Fischer controversy as your test case. does the question of blame change depending on whether the war was chosen by a handful of men or ground out by a whole system, and does blame attach to elites, to the wider society that cheered them on, or to nobody at all? take a side and defend it, don't just conclude that everyone was a bit guilty and leave it at that, that's what my friend did and it drove me nuts. continuous prose only please, no headings, bullets, bold text, or tables — write it like a letter.
