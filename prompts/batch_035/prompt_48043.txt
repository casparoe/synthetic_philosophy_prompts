My mom was diagnosed with early-onset Alzheimers about a year and a half ago, and it's moved fast enough that my brother and I are now fighting about basically everything — whether to move her into memory care near him, whether the feeding tube decision should follow what she "would have wanted," and honestly whether the woman who sits by the window and sometimes doesn't know our names is still *her*. He says obviously yes, same person, and I half-agree while feeling like something else is going on that none of our language is catching. I've been reading a messy mix of things and two ideas keep showing up: (1) the Buddhist no-self claim that there was never a persisting self to lose in the first place, with its split between conventional truth (where "same person" works fine for everyday purposes) and ultimate truth (where nothing like that holds); and (2) Parfit's move that even in normal life strict identity isn't what matters — what matters is psychological connectedness, which comes in degrees and doesn't need identity standing behind it. There's also a thought I can't shake: in the thought experiments where one psyche splits into two, everyone seems to agree the identity question goes indeterminate, and I keep wondering whether dementia is that in slow motion.

So here's what I want from you, and I want you to actually commit rather than laying out the menu of views and letting me pick:

Give me your credence — a real number — that my mom as she is now is, in any robust sense, the same person as she was before the diagnosis. Decompose it: how much rides on which theory of personal identity comes out true (psychological continuity, biological/organismic, narrative, or the Buddhist deflationary line that "same person" is just a useful convention with no deep fact under it); how much on whether, given the true theory, her case actually satisfies the conditions rather than only partially satisfying them; and how much on whether "same person" is even the kind of question with a determinate answer once you push past everyday use. One line of justification per term, then combine them and show me how you got to the final number.

Then the part I actually care about: tell me which term you'd bet is doing the most work in the disagreement between my brother and me — is he and I really disagreeing about a fact, or about which convention to adopt? And give me a definite recommendation: going forward, should we treat the wishes she expressed forty years ago as *her* wishes now, and if your answer is going to be "partly," tell me straight what the part should track (intensity of past preference? current traces of approval? just what's kind?). No hedging into "depends on your view of identity" — pick, show the arithmetic, and finish by telling me what observation about her over the next year would move your number the most.
