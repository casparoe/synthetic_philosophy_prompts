As an undergraduate I read Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach, and lately I've returned to his discussion of where the meaning of a message is located. As I remember it, he splits every message into three layers: a frame layer that announces "this is a decodable message" (like recognizing that a bottle washed up on the beach contains something written, rather than sand), an outer layer that carries the information needed to decode it (recognizing that the marks are Japanese tells you how to read them), and an inner layer, the content actually intended. Two further claims stick with me: that the outer layer's decoding cannot itself be a decoding — it has to bottom out in pattern recognition built into the receiver, or you get an infinite regress — and that he rejects the view that a message is merely an arbitrary trigger for meanings already stored inside the receiver, the way a jukebox record triggers a song.
