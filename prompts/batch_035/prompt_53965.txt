Question about whether something I've noticed has a name. Apologies in advance if this is a well worn thing that I just don't know the term for.

I noticed that there are a lot of words where I can be flatly wrong without the word changing what it means for me. I can't tell a jackdaw from a rook, but if I point at a rook and say "jackdaw," I've made a mistake about birds, not revealed that "jackdaw" means something different in my mouth. Same with my grandmother, who says her arthritis has moved into her thigh — everyone treats that as her being wrong about her ailment, not as her having a private word. Her usage gets graded against a standard she doesn't herself possess.

Fine so far, probably boring. But here's the part that got me thinking. Sometimes the standard that grades the usage doesn't seem to exist anywhere, not even spread out across the community. "Jade" turned out to cover two chemically unrelated minerals, and when that was discovered nobody split the word — we just kept saying jade, and apparently had been successfully referring all along even though the "expert knowledge" fixing the extension was false. And with whales, where the folk theory was that they were fish, I genuinely can't tell whether the community later learned something or whether "fish" quietly changed what it meant, and I suspect the answer differs from the jade case in a way I can't articulate.

I half remember the Twin Earth stuff from a course years ago, and "division of linguistic labor" sounds like it might be the term for the first batch of examples. But is there a name specifically for the second thing — where the communal fact that's supposed to settle usage is absent, contested, or just wrong, and the word goes on meaning something anyway? If there's no established concept, I'd honestly rather have someone carve it up and tell me where the edges are than get a single label. Like, does the community get to be wrong about jade the way it can't be wrong about gold's atomic number, and what's the difference exactly?
