Before I engage any more of the literature on the nonidentity problem, I want the specification the literature is arguing over. Draft the desiderata that any adequate solution must meet. The cases it must get right should include the standard ones — depletion policies, the risky conservation decision, the teenage mother who knowingly conceives a child she could have had later under better conditions — plus this variant I've been working with: a fertility clinic finds that a given sperm batch carries a 50% risk of a serious, non-fatal condition; switching to a batch available a year later would mean different children entirely, and the parents have made clear it is this batch or no child. The eventual children lead lives worth living and, when adult, insist they would rather exist with the condition than not exist. State which verdicts on this case a solution is required to deliver and which it may leave open.

Beyond cases, the spec must cover: the distinctions a solution may not collapse (harming versus wronging; identity-dependent versus identity-independent wrong-making features; lives worth living versus lives that are good enough to generate); the constraints it cannot violate (it may not render all identity-affecting procreation impermissible, and it may not rescue the wrongness of depletion by denying the nonidentity itself); and the scope question — whether a solution must extend to non-person-affecting cases like the clinic or may treat them as a separate problem. Mark each desideratum as non-negotiable or as the preference of one camp, with the motivating case behind each. Then do two further things: identify any subset of the desiderata that is jointly unsatisfiable — I suspect the person-affecting restriction and the wrongness of depletion cannot both be kept, and I'd like that either confirmed or dissolved — and name the single requirement that does the most to exclude otherwise-live candidates. A numbered list plus a table is fine; skip any scorecard of existing views.

Throughout, distinguish what you're confident in from what you're stipulating, and single out the one point in the spec you're least sure belongs on the list at all.
