I work as a software engineer, and for the past few years I've followed the fight over whether frontier models should be released with open weights, and it strikes me as completely stuck. One side says openness democratizes access, enables external scrutiny of capabilities and safety work, and prevents a few companies from deciding how the most powerful tool of the decade gets used; the other says a released model cannot be recalled, and that a weight dump hands every well-resourced bad actor the strongest available model with the guardrails trivially stripped off. What I notice is that both sides read the same incident reports and the same fine-tuning studies and nobody moves an inch. My working hypothesis is that the disagreement isn't primarily empirical — the empirical questions, like how quickly fine-tuning defeats alignment training or how fast released capabilities diffuse, get studied and cited by both sides — but that it's something else, and I can't tell what. Maybe the sides are weighting different nightmare scenarios, or they're implicitly answering two different questions under one heading, one about concentrating power in a handful of labs this decade and one about irreversible harms that outlast any company. Or maybe part of it is verbal: as a software person I notice that 'open' does a lot of load-bearing work, because what actually gets released is frozen weights under a license, not inspectable and modifiable source in anything like the software sense, and yet the rhetoric of the open-source tradition — transparency, community, trust — gets imported wholesale; attempts to sort out what 'open' should mean for AI seem to have changed no one's position. Diagnose the disagreement for me: is it substantive, and if so, over exactly which proposition; is it a difference in values or weightings that evidence can't touch; is it a hidden empirical question after all; or is some of it sustained by the word 'open' covering everything from raw weight dumps to genuinely community-governed systems? Support the diagnosis with what each side actually says, and then tell me what would count as progress given that diagnosis — a technical result, an agreed distinction, a shared case study, a reframed question — or argue that no movement should be expected, and why.
