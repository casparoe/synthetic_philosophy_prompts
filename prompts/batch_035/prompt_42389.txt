I'm a retired physical chemist, spent most of my career on spectroscopy, and I've gotten deep into philosophy of science since retiring and I've basically converted to the view that inference to the best explanation isn't just a useful heuristic but the actual engine of scientific rationality. My story goes something like this: when we "condition on evidence" in the Bayesian way, what we're really doing, when you look at how scientists actually reason, is asking which hypothesis would make the evidence most intelligible, and priors themselves are largely standings of explanatory virtue from earlier rounds of the same process. I even like the idea (picked up from reading about minimum description length stuff in information theory, which appeals to my old-fashioned physics instincts) that "best explanation" might bottom out in something like compression — the best explanation is the shortest one that lets you regenerate the data. If that's right, IBE isn't some loose folk practice that needs a Bayesian cleanup, it's the fundamental thing, and the probability calculus is bookkeeping on top of it.

So, what would actually count against this? I know there's an old argument, I think from van Fraassen, that accepting IBE alongside Bayesian conditioning lets you get Dutch-booked, and I've seen people claim the two are incompatible and people claiming they can be reconciled, but honestly I can't tell if the incompatibility charge is a real bullet or a technicality. And I know the standard worry that "best" explanations track loveliness rather than likeliness, plus the whole underdetermination thing. But what I want is a proper map of the exposure: which of these are decisive if they land, which are merely awkward, and what would a committed defender of my view concede as a genuine cost versus dismiss as confusion? What observations, or historical cases, or formal results would genuinely move me? Like — if it turned out that across the history of science, theoretically ugly but predictively accurate models kept winning over beautiful unifying ones, at some rate that compression-based measures couldn't account for, would that sink the view or just force a patch? And is there any way the compression story could be tested as an empirical claim about how scientists' judgements actually track explanation quality, rather than just being a definition I've chosen?

Also please be straight with me about whether my position has quietly been qualified into something unfalsifiable — if every failure of a "beautiful" theory gets explained away with "well it wasn't really the best explanation, was it," is that just a problem with how I've stated the view, or is it endemic to IBE itself? And if the view can't risk anything, is that fatal for a view of this kind, or do philosophical theses about rationality get some kind of exemption? Rank the defeaters for me, fatal to merely awkward, and tell me which one you'd personally press if you were arguing against me over a beer. Sorry for the wall of text, I've been mulling this over for months and my philosopher friends keep changing the subject.
