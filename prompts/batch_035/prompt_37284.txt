So I'm a novelist, four books in, all in English but I grew up bilingual (Polish at home, English everywhere else), and for my next book I've got a main character who's a linguist and a genuine true believer in the strong version of the Sapir-Whorf thing — not the watered down language-nudges-attention version, the one where your grammar really does shape which thoughts are available to you. I've talked my way into an hour with an actual linguist who defends something close to this (keeping her name out of it), and I want to use the interview as research and possibly straight-up source material, since chunks of the book are me dramatizing exactly this kind of argument.

Draft me around ten interview questions. Not the generic what-drew-you-to-linguistics stuff — questions that actually press where the strong view is weakest. At least one question on each of the following, and for each one a follow-up keyed to the answer she's most likely to give:

- the old retreat move where the strong claim quietly becomes thinking-for-speaking, Slobin's thing — does that rescue the view or give away the store, and if she retreats to it, what's left of the strong version my character believes in?
- the grammatical gender studies, where Spanish and German speakers supposedly described a bridge or a key differently depending on whether the noun was masculine or feminine in their language. My understanding is at least one of these didn't replicate — would she still defend the effect, and does replication trouble in one corner worry her about the whole program?
- Guugu Yimithirr-style absolute directions, where speakers of some Australian languages keep track of north/south/east/west at all times. Is that evidence that language shapes thought, or just evidence that a community drills a habit into its kids? How does she keep those apart?
- evidentials — languages where you have to mark whether you saw something yourself, heard about it, or inferred it. Does her view predict that speakers of a language like that are better at remembering where their knowledge came from, and has anyone actually checked?
- translation, the one I care about personally: my narrator is bilingual like me, and I want to know whether the strong view says she can have a thought in Polish she literally cannot have in English, or whether it just costs her an extra beat. Wierd question to build a whole book on if the honest answer is just "extra beat," so I want to hear her push back.

End with one question about what evidence would genuinely count against her view, phrased so that both nothing-it's-complicated and we-already-falsified-the-strong-version-here's-what-survives are live answers she could give.

Keep the whole thing under 600 words — I'll be reading these off my phone in a noisy cafe. Casual register is fine, shes read my books and knows I'm not an academic.
