I'm a novelist three-quarters of the way through a book about two sisters who inherit their late mother's rural veterinary clinic. The elder sister, a former bank auditor, approaches ethics as a matter of rules she can state and defend — she keeps an actual written list of professional and personal maxims taped inside a ledger. The younger sister, who runs the clinic's day-to-day practice, has no list; she trusts her read of each situation, case by case, and is scornful of anything that smells like a decision procedure. I've written them arguing about small things for two hundred pages, and I now want a scene where a real moral disagreement between generalism and particularism is generated by the situation itself rather than by their tempers.

What I'd like you to do is construct the scene to specification. Build me a pair of twin scenarios set in the clinic — realistic, everyday, no runaway trolleys and no murderers at the door asking about hidden refugees — identical in every respect except for one detail, such that a rule generalist and a moral particularist reach the same verdict in the first version but opposite verdicts in the second. The flip has to be earned, not stipulated: after the two versions, walk through what each theory actually says in each case and why, so I can see the disagreement falling out of the theories rather than out of your thumb on the scale. Two further constraints, since I want neither sister to come off as a fool: the rule in play must be one a thoughtful generalist would genuinely endorse (no straw rules, nothing like "always put paperwork first" that no one holds), and the particularist's change of verdict must not amount to simple sympathy for whichever animal or client happens to be more vivid.

Finally, tell me honestly which detail of the case is doing the work — what happens to the disagreement if you remove or soften it — and give me the strongest move available to whichever theory comes out looking worse: the generalist reaching for a second-order rule, a ceteris paribus clause, or a better-formulated principle, or the particularist denying that the load-bearing detail has any genuine moral relevance. If the two sisters could each read your walkthrough and recognize their own position being treated fairly, the scene will work; if either would feel caricatured, I'll know to revise.
