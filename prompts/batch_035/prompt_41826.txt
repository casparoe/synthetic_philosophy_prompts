Like everyone in my field, I've heard the water-cooler question about whether the characters I make could suffer, and like everyone, I've waved it off because current NPCs are obviously just state machines. But I want to know whether the philosophers' debate about the moral status of video-game characters can even be stated in the vocabulary I actually use, or whether my dismissal relies on machinery I'm not seeing.

Restate the main positions in that debate in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can. Treat an NPC the way I do: as a per-tick loop that reads world state, scores candidate actions, writes to a blackboard, and sends animation events — with everything observable from the profiler and the debugger. Then give me the residue report. Which claims in the debate translate cleanly (e.g., does 'increasingly sophisticated AI will demand moral consideration' become a claim about architecture, or about something else)? Which translate only with distortion — for instance, when philosophers talk about a character 'experiencing' its punishment or 'caring' about its in-game goals, is that just anthropomorphizing output that I know to be a scoring function, or is something else being claimed? And what has no expression at all in my vocabulary: does phenomenal consciousness, as opposed to behavior I can trace tick by tick, have any counterpart in what a runtime system is? Finally, tell me honestly whether the residue points to something my vocabulary cannot say — a fact about these systems that no profiler would show me — or whether it shows the philosophers' debate is less substantive than it looks, trading on an intuitive notion of experience that does no work.
