I've been going back and forth on modal claims about particular things, and I want to figure out what my gut verdicts on a bunch of cases actually add up to. Here they are.

When I read that Hubert Humphrey could have won the 1968 election, I take that to be about Humphrey himself — that very guy, winning, in some other way things could have gone. Not a claim about some lookalike winning while the real Humphrey sits it out. That seems obvious. There's a well-known complaint along these lines against the counterpart view: on that view what the claim ends up being about is someone else, someone similar to Humphrey, winning — and surely that misses the point of the regret.

My bike could have been sold to someone in another city before I ever saw it, and left to rust in a shed somewhere. Still my bike, the very one. Fine.

But: if a different sperm had reached the egg, and my parents had a kid anyway — say a girl — I don't think that would have been me. That's someone else, basically a sibling.

Except when I phrase it as "I could have been a girl" it suddenly sounds true. My parents used to say things like, if you'd been a girl we'd have named you Sarah, and that does not sound like a claim about some stranger named Sarah. So the same case feels true described one way and false described the other, and I genuinely can't tell which one I believe.

I could have been raised in a different country, speak different language, have a totally different life and personality. Feels clearly true — same person, different biography.

The kitchen table: it could not have been made of ice, easy one. But could it have been made from a different tree, same factory, same day, same everything else? My gut says... maybe? Which is weird, because if a different sperm makes a different person, a different tree should make a different table, no? Why would origins matter so much more for me than for the table?

Last one, which I can't place at all. There could have been a person exactly like me down to the last atom, living exactly my life, who is nonetheless not me — say, in a world where my parents met on the same day, but the conception that would have produced me never happened and this duplicate happened instead. Something in me says that's possible, and something says it's flatly incoherent.

So: is there one story that gets all or most of this? The two options I know of are Lewis's counterpart theory, where these claims bottom out in a similarity relation to people/things in other worlds, and the transworld identity view, where the very same thing exists in other worlds. My problem is that my verdicts seem to want both: Humphrey himself winning sounds like identity, but a different-sperm kid not being me sounds like counterparts, or like some origin-essentialism add-on. Can the counterpart relation just be weighted differently for different kinds of thing, or weighted differently for origin versus biography? Or is the better move to keep transworld identity and build necessary origins into it, and if so how do I stop it from eating the table case and maybe the girl case? Whatever you pick, tell me what it predicts for the duplicate case and for a couple of cases I haven't listed, and be honest about which of my verdicts it can't save — I suspect "I could have been a girl" can't survive intact, but I'd like to know if that's right or just me being lazy.
