After a career spent running psychophysical experiments, I have in retirement worked through Dennett's *Consciousness Explained* and find myself largely convinced by what he calls the Stalinesque view of temporal anomalies like color phi: that there is no moment-by-moment stream of consciousness delivered in real time, but rather a retrospective construction, with the brain back-dating and editing its output into a coherent narrative. I want to know what would count against this position. Please give me the observations, experimental results, intuitions, and theoretical developments that would disconfirm it, ranked from fatal to merely awkward, distinguishing what committed defenders would themselves concede is problematic from what only critics press. I am especially troubled by Dennett's own point that the Stalinesque and Orwellian (experience-first, memory-revised) accounts seem functionally indistinguishable: does that admission render the position unfalsifiable, and if so, is that a fatal flaw for a theory of the timing of consciousness, or a legitimate upshot of there being no fact of the matter about when experience happens? As someone trained to demand operational tests, I would like to know what, if anything, the Stalinesque view predicts that its rivals do not.
