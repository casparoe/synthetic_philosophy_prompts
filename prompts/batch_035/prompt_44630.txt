Is there an established name for the following configuration? I am a retired actuary who has drifted into reading popular discussions of how one world can be better than another, in the aggregating spirit where you add up everyone's wellbeing and compare the sums. It is generally granted that this goes badly when the population is endless, but the specific pattern I keep noticing does not seem to have been given a label of its own, and I would like to know whether it has one.

Consider an endless line of people living mediocre lives, and suppose you improve the first billion of them drastically — a huge finite addition of wellbeing. Under the adding-up view nothing has happened: the ledger has no last page, both totals are endless, and the improvement is a rounding error that cannot move the comparison. That part I recognize as a known embarrassment. What I have not seen named is a second feature: whether a second world counts as better than the first can depend on how you line the two populations up. If every inhabitant of world B is placed next to a worse-off inhabitant of world A, the counterpart-by-counterpart comparison favors B everywhere; shift the alignment by one position, and the same two worlds now compare the other way, with every A inhabitant better off than the B person beside her. Nothing about the worlds has changed — only the bookkeeping that pairs them. It reminds me of the old puzzle about the perfect squares being at once a proper part of the counting numbers and matchable one-to-one with them, but I have only ever seen that treated as a curiosity about counting, never as a problem about value.

So: does this value version have a name, and if so, how does the named concept relate to the plain problem that endless totals cannot be compared? Where exactly does it apply — only when the two worlds contain different people, or can it arise between two possible fates for one and the same endless population? And if no established term exists, please coin one and define it precisely enough to distinguish it from mere incomparability of worlds, and then probe its edges, including whether it survives when one world does better than the other under every possible alignment. I am after the carving — what the phenomenon is, what it is not, and where it ends — not just a label.

Please lead with the bottom line in your first sentence — whether there is an established name, and what it is — and only then give the reasoning. No preamble, and do not restate my question back to me.
