My brother and I have spent a long while arguing about two scenarios I came across while reading around decision theory, and we've realised we can't even state our disagreement as a number, so I'd like yours. In the first scenario, which I believe is called the counterfactual mugging: a very reliable predictor flipped a fair coin long ago. If it came up heads, it paid £1,000,000 into your account yesterday, but only if it predicted you would agree to the arrangement; if it came up tails, it instead approaches you today for £1,000. You accepted the arrangement knowing all of this, the coin landed tails, and the request is now in front of you — so refusing today costs you nothing. In the second scenario, something like a blackmail variant discussed under the same headings: you hold a letter from a blackmailer who has genuine photographs that would ruin you, demanding £1,000 and threatening release if you refuse. The blackmailer sent the letter if and only if it predicted you would pay; had it predicted refusal, it would have deleted the photographs and stayed silent forever. My credence that the correct treatment of the pair is pay in the mugging and refuse in the blackmail — that the two cases genuinely come apart, rather than being one problem in different clothes — is about 0.7. My brother, who says both are simply someone exploiting your predictability, and that you should refuse in both, is nearer 0.25. Please give me your own number for the claim that the pay-the-first, refuse-the-second split is correct, and decompose it: how much turns on whether the proper object of evaluation is this single decision or the policy or disposition standing behind it, how much on whether there is a principled difference between a correlation that runs through a fair coin outside anyone's control and one that runs through your own predicted character, how much on whether rationality here means getting the most from this very choice or from your life considered as a whole, and any further terms you think belong. Then tell me which term does the most work, and what argument or observation would shift it most. For what it's worth, my brother's favourite rejoinder replaces the coin with a lottery on your own body — whether you are ever approached at all depends on a gene you happen to carry that also makes people somewhat more inclined to pay — and I honestly can't tell whether that case helps his side or mine.
