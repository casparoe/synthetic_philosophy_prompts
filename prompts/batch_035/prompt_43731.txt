I left school at seventeen and have worked with my hands ever since, so what follows comes from a popular science book and my own long thinking, not from any training. For some years I've been gnawing on something Gödel found, late in his friendship with Einstein: the field equations admit a universe that rotates, and in that universe a sufficiently long and fast journey can bring a traveller to any point in the past — including their own past. The path through spacetime closes on itself; earlier and later turn out to be the same place met twice. If I've understood him rightly, Gödel thought this enough to show that time itself is an illusion: that nothing really passes, and that the moving present is something travellers carry with them rather than something the world does. What I gather clinched it for him was that the laws merely need to allow such a universe at all — whether time truly passes could hardly depend on which of the permitted worlds we happen to occupy.

I'd like a sestina about this. Six stanzas of six lines, then a three-line envoi; the six end-words of the first stanza come back in every stanza in the sestina's fixed order, and all six are worked into the envoi. I want the form to carry the physics. The same six words, returning in each stanza in a new arrangement, are the same events revisited along a closed curve from another direction: the words change position, never meaning; the poem rotates and arrives where it began. And I'd like the envoi to land the philosophical sting: the traveller, having reached their own past, finds the journey spent only their own private hours, while the world itself never moved — the passage was in the passenger all along.

Please make it accurate enough that someone who knows the Gödel universe would nod, and strange enough that someone who doesn't would still feel the vertigo. Write the poem and everything around it in British English.
