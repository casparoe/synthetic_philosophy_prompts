So my little monthly philosophy meetup is doing Dennett's brain-transplant story next session -- the one where his brain (Yorick) gets removed and left in a vat in Tulsa while his body roams around Houston getting radioed its instructions. And every time we touch this thing we end up in the same shouting match: while all that is going on, where IS Dennett? Houston? Tulsa? Both somehow? I want to settle this once and for all by building a decision tree that sorts people by what they actually believe about the case, not by which fancy label they like the sound of.

Some requirements so it actually works on my crowd:

1. Build the tree from the logical space first, not from a list of isms. The first split should be something anyone can commit to before reading a word of personal identity literature, like whether the story has a determinate correct answer at all vs. whether it somehow depends on us or our naming practices. After that, every node should be a concrete question about the story answerable straight off intuitions: does it matter where the brain is sitting, does it matter where things seem to be from the inside (which, funnily, is the direction Dennett himself drifts mid-story), what should we say if the backup copy, Hubert, were hooked up to run a second body while Yorick keeps running the original, and does the late twist -- he finds out Yorick and Hubert had been swapped at some point years ago and he never even noticed -- change anything or expose that we were tracking nothing all along.

2. The leaves need to include at least: the view that he simply is his brain and is therefore sitting in Tulsa; the view that he's wherever his body/point of view is; a psychological continuity take; some deflationary leaf where the whole question gets dissolved (Dennett's own ending gestures at something like deciding where he is); plus one or two unnamed leaves if your tree forces them, each with a sentence on why nobody sits there -- is the combo incoherent, just unappealing, or merely unexplored?

3. No node should require knowing any position's name to answer it.

4. Flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really seperate with these cases. I suspect brain-identity and plain animalism end up looking identical here unless you slip in something like gradual neuron replacement -- if you want to add a case or two of your own invention to prise them apart, go for it, but keep it in the spirit of the story (radio links, vats, backups).

5. Six or seven leaves maximum. We are a beer-and-argument crowd, not a seminar, so every node has to be answerable by a normal person holding a beer.

And don't just retell the story at me, I've read it twice. I want the tree, plus a couple sentences on where YOU would land in it and which single node you predict will cause the most shouting on the night.
