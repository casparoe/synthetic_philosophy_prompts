I'm drafting a dissertation prospectus and need help turning a half-formed idea into a proper two-page proposal. The idea: Dennett's account of real patterns is usually deployed defensively — beliefs, desires, and other intentional posits are real because adopting the intentional stance buys you predictive economy, and that's a perfectly objective fact about the pattern in the data. But predictive economy by itself looks too cheap. A gambler's betting system compresses the roulette record, in his eyes at least; a conspiracy theorist gets staggering predictive mileage out of positing a single hidden agent behind unrelated events; pareidolia finds faces everywhere and they do guide expectation somewhat. So the criterion of "it pays to adopt the stance" seems unable to distinguish real patterns from overfitted ones. My suspicion is that either real patternhood needs an extra constraint — something like generalization to unseen cases, or robustness across independent observers, which Dennett's discussion gestures at but never isolates — or the intentional stance has to lean on something other than patternhood to rule out spurious agency, in which case the standard defense of the stance is doing less work than advertised. I want the proposal to state that question precisely, explain why the usual answers (the "further facts" Dennett denies, the instrumentalist readings, the mixed physical/design/intentional hierarchy) leave it open, lay out the method — which thought experiments and comparison cases I would actually construct, chapter by chapter across three years, including whether the overfitting analogy from statistics can be made rigorous rather than merely suggestive — and say honestly what would count as the project failing. If the constraint that separates real from overfitted patterns turns out to be simply "the physical stance vindicates it," I'd want to know that early, since it would collapse the autonomy the real-patterns story was supposed to secure. Don't include a bibliography or citations; I'll add those myself, and please don't lean on any specific passages being quoted a particular way.
