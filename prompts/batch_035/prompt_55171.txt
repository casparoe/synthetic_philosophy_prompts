I've gotten deep into some rationalist blog posts lately, and the one topic I keep coming back to is what they call acausal trade. From what I can piece together, the idea is that two agents who by assumption can never communicate, never meet, and never even learn of each other's existence can nonetheless carry out something like a trade: each one behaves generously toward the other, each confident that the other is reasoning the same way and will do the same. The example that gets used a lot is two civilizations in causally disconnected parts of the universe each simulating the other and cooperating in their simulations. My brother-in-law, who is way more into this stuff than I am, insists this is all perfectly rational, but he couldn't answer my actual question when I pressed him, so I'm trying here.

Here is what I can't get past. Ordinary trade has a medium: a handshake, a signed contract, a shipment of goods, at minimum some signal passing back and forth. If the two agents in acausal trade can never even find out about each other, then nothing at all travels between them, and yet we're supposed to believe a transaction takes place. That sounds like an influence acting across a gap with no channel to carry it, which to my layman's ears is like saying a force can operate with nothing in between — the kind of thing physics spent centuries ruling out. So my question is: what exactly is the medium that carries the trade between the two agents, and how do the people who take this seriously square it with the ban on faster-than-light influence?

I'd also mention, in fairness, that I might just be misreading what the bloggers are claiming in the first place, since I have no background in this and I'm reconstructing it from posts written for people who already get it. Please tell me how confident you are in whatever you say, and single out the part of your answer you'd be least sure about — I'd rather know where the shaky ground is than get something polished that sounds more settled than it is.
