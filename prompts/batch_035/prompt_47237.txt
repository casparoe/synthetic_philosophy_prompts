My partner and I have spent the better part of a year circling the doomsday argument, and we have agreed to stop trading intuitions and put numbers on the thing instead. Here is the proposition I want your credence for: that the doomsday argument is sound, where "sound" means that from my observation that I am roughly the hundred-billionth human to exist, together with the assumption that I should reason as if randomly sampled from some reference class, a genuine evidential discount on long human futures follows — and that this discount is not merely an artifact of how the reference class was chosen or of the sampling assumption itself. Please do not give me a bare number. Decompose it: (a) the probability that the correct anthropic theory needs no reference class at all, for instance because self-locating evidence can be handled without one; (b) conditional on a reference class being needed, the probability that the right class is wide — all observers capable of having such thoughts, whatever their biology — rather than narrow, restricted to humans who have reached roughly our epistemic situation; (c) the probability that the update itself is legitimate even given the right class, that the sampling is not somehow double-counted or precluded by the causal structure of the case; and (d) any further term you think the decomposition requires, with a one-line justification for each. Then combine them, give the final number, and tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and what argument or observation would move that term the most. For calibration: my own credence sits around 0.3; my partner, who thinks the whole thing dissolves the moment you ask, sampled from what?, sits around 0.05. Given those two numbers, tell me which sub-question we most likely disagree about. Hard limit: 400 words, no padding, and no tour of the anthropic literature.
