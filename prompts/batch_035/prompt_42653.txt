Alright, I teach a tech ethics course (mostly engineering students, zero philosophy background) and there's a pattern that keeps showing up in our case discussions that I can't find a name for, and I'm starting to worry I'm inventing a wheel.

The standard story about autonomous systems is the responsibility gap: system does harm, and because nobody quite occupies the role of the responsible agent, the blame sort of evaporates. But in the cases we actually work through, I keep seeing almost the opposite of evaporation. The blame always lands *somewhere*, it just lands somewhere weird, and it goes in two characteristic directions.

Direction one, which is probably old news: it lands on whoever is physically closest, even when they had the least actual control. The backup driver in the car that is doing the driving gets charged. The nurse who clicked confirm on the dosing algorithm gets fired. I assume there's existing vocabulary for this one that I just havent come across.

But direction two is the one I actually want a name for: the blame lands on the system itself, and everybody finds this strangely satisfying, and the case gets closed. "The algorithm denied your claim." "The system flagged you." People say it in the exact tone you'd use for the weather. Nobody did it — the system did it. And what's odd is that this gets treated as an explanation rather than as a scandal. It's not a shrug, it's an answer. Someone even says it in the official paperwork, the inquiry ends, and everyone goes home with their sense that harm was accounted for intact. It's a bit like the old practice of blaming fate, except we've manufactured the fate ourselves and installed it in a datacenter.

So the phenomenon, as I'd put it: when an automated system causes harm, the system itself gets used as a designated blame-absorber — an officially blamable non-agent — and performing the ritual of blaming it functions to end inquiry. It's not that responsibility falls into a gap; it's that we built a landing pad.

Questions. Does direction two have an established name? I won't be happy with "that's just the responsibility gap again," because the whole point of my description is that in these cases nothing falls into any gap — everyone gets an answer and the file gets stamped. I'm aware it's adjacent to the problem of many hands and to buck-passing, so tell me specifically what separates it from those, or whether one of them actually covers it and I'm wrong that there's a distinct thing here. If there is no established term, coin one, give a definition, and defend it over the obvious alternatives.

And please commit — don't give me a menu of five candidate concepts and tell me it's ultimately a matter of perspective. Pick: either an existing term fits, in which case say which one and why it beats the others, or nothing fits, in which case name the thing and draw its edges. What I'd specifically like on the edges: is a human bureaucrat doing the same job (person in the office whose whole function is to say no and absorb the anger) a case of the concept, or does it have to be an artifact? And is it still the same move when the victim of the harm accepts the blame-attribution, or does the concept require someone with authority doing the attributing?
