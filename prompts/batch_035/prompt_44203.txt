I'm the one stats person at a mid-sized county health department, and every month or two somebody up the chain wants to put a claim in a press release or a board briefing like "vaccination rates rose this year" or "our ER wait times fell," based on one aggregate number. My recurring nightmare is that the aggregate moves one way while every subgroup underneath it moves the other way, and I didn't catch it before it went out. I know the textbook version of Simpson's paradox but I need something I can actually run on a two-page table in half an hour, not a lecture.

Please turn whatever the formal idea actually is into a procedure I can follow: the questions to ask in order (like, when do I even owe the subgroups a look, which partitions matter, how big does a reversal have to be before it's not just noise), what counts as a good enough answer at each step, and where the procedure should stop and say "this is a judgment call, escalate to a human" (that human might be me, but at least name it as a judgment call). Then run it on two cases for me: (1) teen vaccination coverage looks like it ticked up countywide, but the split by school district shows every district down slightly, and (2) average ER wait time is down a lot while waits in every acuity tier are flat-to-up, because case mix shifted. I'd especially like the second one to be handled honestly even though the honest answer kills the press release. Also tell me where this procedure will misfire — e.g. am I just fishing for a partition that reverses, and how would I know? — and what it flat out can't settle, like which aggregation is the "real" one for the public to hear about.
