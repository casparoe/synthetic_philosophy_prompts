longtime lurker here, been reading OB/LW since maybe 2010, and i've read every Fermi paradox thread on the forum at least twice. every few months someone posts The Definitive List of Fermi Explanations and its just... a bulleted list. rare earth, great filter, zoo hypothesis, dark forest, grabby aliens, we're in a sim, done. that's not a taxonomy, it's a menu. nobody who reads it learns which two hypotheses actually disagree with each other, or what observation would move credence between them.

so build me a decision tree instead, for a handout at my local meetup. the start node is just "we look up and the sky is silent" — no dyson swarms, no obvious megastructures, no signals. every internal node should be a question a normal person can answer from their gut without knowing which leaf it leads to, stuff like "if a technological civ is left alone for a billion years, does it visibly expand or stay home?" or "is our own most likely future extinction, quiet stasis, or something that would still be visible from other galaxies?". the questions should carve the actual logical space — is there nobody out there, are they out there but quiet, or are we misreading the data entirely — and then the named positions get hung on the leaves wherever they land.

positions that have to appear as leaves somewhere: rare earth, early filter, late filter (probably two leaves, your call), grabby aliens, dark forest (yes, the one from the novels), zoo hypothesis, transcension / "everyone migrates to inner space", simulation, and the "the silence isn't evidence of anything because anthropics" move — though if you think that last one is a standing objection to the whole tree rather than a leaf, say so and why.

specific asks:

- one branch question has to make the anthropics explicit. does finding ourselves early in cosmic history shift our credences, and does it shift differently under SIA vs SSA? if the two update rules genuinely send you to different leaves, split them; if not, don't fake it.
- dark forest and zoo both bottom out in "they're hiding" — if you can't separate them with a question a human could actually answer, flag the pair as inseparable instead of inventing a distinction.
- note which combinations of answers come out unoccupied, and take a guess at why: incoherent, unattractive, or just nobody's bothered to write it up?
- keep it to 8-12 leaves total so it fits on one page.

and please no "maybe they're just really far away" as a leaf unless you can make it do real work — "too far" is an excuse every hypothesis can borrow, not a position in its own right. or argue with me on that, i genuinely don't know.
