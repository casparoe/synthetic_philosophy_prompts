I've been circling the literature on the epistemic status of the Church–Turing thesis for a while — the definitional reading, the empirical reading, the quasi-empirical-mathematical reading — and before I read another word of it, I want the specification fixed. Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of the thesis's epistemic status has to deliver, with the motivating case behind each requirement, and a judgment about which are non-negotiable and which are mere prejudices of one camp. Fix the purpose as follows: an account is adequate if it settles what kind of claim the thesis is, what would count for or against it, and whether and how it could be overturned.

Some requirements I want you to at least consider taking a stance on, though you should add and subtract: the account must say what to make of the convergence of independently invented formalisms — Turing machines, lambda-definability, general recursiveness, register machines, Post systems — since a stipulative definition makes that convergence a lucky accident while a substantial claim makes it evidence, and the account must wear which story it tells; it must keep the original thesis, about the informal notion of effective calculability, cleanly separate from the stronger physical variants about what any machine can compute, and say whether they share an epistemic status or merely a name; it must say something principled about well-specified but uncomputable functions, since an account on which the thesis could not have been false struggles to explain why the halting problem feels like a discovery rather than a bookkeeping choice; it must either assign the thesis a plausible role in mathematical practice, where it is routinely invoked as if beyond doubt, or explain away that practice; it must explain rather than merely dismiss the peculiar phenomenology of the thesis, the way it strikes nearly everyone as obvious in retrospect; and it must not simultaneously demand that the thesis be substantive enough to be refutable and immune from refutation by physics we do not actually possess — though if you think that pairing is jointly satisfiable, or jointly dispensable, say so, since that is the part I most want to see. Finally, mark which single requirement excludes the most popular accounts: I suspect it is the one about convergence, but I want to see whether you agree. Then, briefly only, run the definitional, empirical, and quasi-empirical-mathematical readings against the list — I care about the criteria, not the scorecard.
