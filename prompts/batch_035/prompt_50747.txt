I never studied history past school but I've gotten a bit obsessed with this on my own, and my wife is tired of hearing about it, so now you get it. Please act as a professional historian who also thinks hard about the philosophy of history, take a moment to brainstorm before committing to anything, and then walk through my steps one at a time.

The background: from what I've read, the "Middle Ages" as a name is basically an afterthought. People living in, say, the year 1000 had no idea they were in a "middle age" — the label got attached centuries later, mostly by Renaissance writers who wanted to see themselves as the rebirth coming after a long "middle" stretch between ancient Rome and their own time. Same goes even more for "the Renaissance" and "the Scientific Revolution": those names came later too, historians apparently still fight about when they start and end, and some of them even argue about whether they're single things at all.

So I built this argument, and it lands somewhere I can't swallow:

1. For a historical period to be real, it has to be either a real feature of the past itself (the way a mountain range is a real feature of the earth) or just a story we tell afterwards.
2. It can't be a real feature of the past itself. Nothing physical marks where it starts or stops, the cutoff years are conventions that historians keep moving, different regions drift in and out at different times, and the people living inside it couldn't recognize it as a period at all — they'd never heard of it.
3. So it's just a story we tell. But if the period is just a story, then strictly speaking nothing ever happened *during* the Middle Ages. Events happened, sure, but the period itself names nothing, so no event falls inside it.
4. Therefore nothing happened during the Middle Ages — no battle, no coronation, no plague happened during it.
5. And by the same reasoning nothing happened during the twentieth century either, since that count is anchored to the year of a birth that got miscalculated anyway, and "the 90s" is just as much a label. On down the line, nothing happened during any named stretch except maybe ones the people inside could actually name for themselves.
6. But 4 and 5 sound obviously wrong to me. The Battle of Hastings definately happened during the Middle Ages, and my parents were born during the twentieth century. Or at least it certainly sounds that way.

Here's my problem: I actually believe 2, and I like it. The thought that there's no fact of the matter about where the Middle Ages end and the Renaissance begins seems right to me — you can draw that line almost anywhere and defend it. And I don't want to say periods are mountain-range real either; that seems way too strong. But I can't accept that nothing happened *during* them. Something has to give.

So: which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me? Be honest about the price — if the cheapest way out means I have to stop saying "the Middle Ages" entirely, I want to know that up front. And if you think one of my steps only *looks* fine because of how I phrased it, point at the exact wording, because I've read this thing so many times now I can't see it straight anymore.
