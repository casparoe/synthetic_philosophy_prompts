My friend Sam is, by temperament, loose about rules — but he is not uniformly loose, and before I predict him on a new case I want to work out the policy he is actually running. I have a record of his verdicts, each of which he has stated flatly: it was fine to drive through a red light at 3 a.m. at a completely empty intersection, after coming to a full stop and looking carefully both ways; fine for him, at nineteen, to have a glass of wine at his own family's dinner table in a state where the drinking age is twenty-one; fine to drive fifteen over the limit on a divided highway at 2 a.m. with no other car in sight; fine, in his view, when our neighbor ran the laundry machines at 1 a.m. against the posted building schedule, because the adjacent units were empty that week; not fine — an apology was owed — when he cut to the front of the pharmacy line because he was in a hurry; and not fine when an acquaintance of ours parked in the disabled spot for what he called "just five minutes." The new case: this weekend his cousin, who self-published a novel Sam genuinely disliked, will ask him, in front of a dozen relatives at a family dinner, whether he liked it. Will Sam tell the polite lie?

What I would like from you: the candidate rules that fit his record — I can see at least two, and I suspect there are more, some of which may fit only because of how I happened to describe the facts; what each rule predicts about the dinner; your best guess, with your reasons for preferring it; and the further case or cases that would most cleanly separate the candidate rules, since I can arrange to watch Sam in new situations over the next few months. I would also like your view on one thing behind all this: people say whole cultures sit on a spectrum from tight to loose about demanding conformity to norms, and Sam seems to have an internal version of that split — loose exactly where he is loose, tight exactly where he is tight. Is that package one a person, or a society, could coherently defend, or is it just an accident of what he happens to care about?

One request about how you frame the answer: please keep visibly separate what his record genuinely settles, what it leaves genuinely open — where people who knew him well could reasonably read him differently — and your own best guess, and do not present a reading you favor as though it were the settled one.
