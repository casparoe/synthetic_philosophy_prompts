I'm a philosophy professor (philosophy of science, and I teach the quantum mechanics bootcamp for our grads every other spring) and next month I'm moderating the Q&A after our department's colloquium. The speaker defends a psi-epistemic view of quantum states -- very roughly, the idea that the wavefunction isn't a fact about a system sitting out there in the world but a state of someone's information or credences about it, in the general neighborhood of QBism and old Copenhagen-flavored instrumentalism. I've seen this person give talks before, and they are extremely good at the high-level deflection -- the state is a bookkeeping device, and bookkeeping devices don't need ontology -- after which the audience nods and we never actually get to the hard parts. So I want a sequence of questions that starts off ordinary and gets progressively harder to wave away like that.

Specifically, I need at least one question on the PBR theorem -- the result from around 2012 (Pusey, Barrett, Rudolph) purporting to show that you can't keep the quantum state merely epistemic if you grant that independently prepared systems have independent underlying states -- with a follow-up keyed to the standard psi-epistemic move of denying that preparation independence assumption, since I'd bet money that's where they'll go (the follow-up should ask whether that denial doesn't require a bizarrely fine-tuned conspiracy between pieces of lab equipment that have never interacted). Second, something on Bell: if the state is just someone's information, then sure, no psi means no spooky collapse transmitting anything, but the correlations still cry out for explanation by *something*, so which do they actually buy -- retrocausality, many worlds, relational absolutism about facts, or biting the superdeterminism bullet -- and a follow-up for whichever they pick, since each has its own well-known sore spot. Third, a question in the Wigner's friend vicinity: if quantum states are agent-relative credences, what do we say when two competent observers assign different states to the same system, and is the intersubjective agreement physicists do in fact achieve just a lucky regularity? And fourth, something on where the Born rule gets its teeth -- if squaring amplitudes is just part of how I organize my beliefs, why do I reliably lose money betting against it in a way that never happens with my other bookkeeping devices, like my calendar?

Give me eight to ten questions total, each with the follow-up marked (you can write it as 'if they say X, press with Y'). They should sound like they come from a curious but slightly suspicious moderator rather than someone hunting for a gotcha -- I have to invite this person back eventually. And please nothing about their personal journey into philosophy of physics; we always run out of time and those questions are dead air anyway.
