I work near the preference-learning corner of ML and something keeps nagging at me about inverse reinforcement learning, so let me lay it out. The practice: you watch a human behave in some environment (driving demos, mouse trajectories, whatever), and you infer the reward function they seem to be optimizing, with the aim of building a machine that pursues the same thing. Everyone knows any finite behavior is consistent with infinitely many reward functions, so the field adds priors and, crucially, a rationality assumption — the human is optimal plus noise, or Boltzmann-rational, deviations from the optimum are random. Meanwhile the same community infers preferences a completely different way in RLHF, from pairwise judgments the human makes on request, and when the recovered reward from behavior comes out looking wrong — the human did something dumb that day, or the inferred function rewards something the human would object to if you showed it to them — practitioners just hand-tune or re-regularize and move on.

So what does this practice actually presuppose about preference? I want the implicit theory reconstructed. Like: does treating behavior as evidence of preference require a causal view where desires drive action, or would a revealed-preference view (preference just IS choice disposition) license it too, and do those come apart anywhere in the pipeline? Does the noise assumption quietly presuppose that human error is unsystematic in a way that makes the recovered preference something the human would endorse on reflection — and if so, how does that square with correcting the inferred preference when the human complains, since that correction treats the human's report as better evidence than the behavior it was inferred from? Is the practice even consistent, or is it running one theory of evidence in the IRL branch and a different one in the RLHF branch without noticing? And how much of this is genuinely presupposed versus underdetermined — several rival theories of preference would license the exact same code, I suspect, but maybe not once you look at the noise model and the corrections.

Also tell me what would have to change if we replaced the implicit theory with the hardline revealed-preference view where the inferred reward is correct by construction and there is nothing to correct.
