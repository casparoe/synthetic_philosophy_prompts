I'm a high school senior who runs a small philosophy club podcast, and next week's guest is a philosopher who defends dialetheism: the view that some contradictions are literally true, and that classical logic goes wrong in letting a single contradiction entail everything. Please draft my interview questions.

I don't want questions that just ask him to restate the view. I want questions that press where the actual debate presses, including at least one on each of the following:

1. The strengthened liar — if he says the sentence "this sentence is not true" is both true and false, what does he say about "this sentence is not true or is it not the case that it's both"? I want the question to make him say something about truth itself being one of the glutty cases, and then a follow-up keyed to that reply.
2. The distinction between paraconsistency and dialetheism: someone can reject the explosion principle (from A and not-A, everything follows) without thinking any contradiction is true. What would make him cross that line rather than stay on the safer side of it?
3. How he answers people who say that once one contradiction is true, nothing stops all of them from being true, and dialetheism quietly collapses into trivialism.

For each question, include a follow-up that anticipates the reply he is most likely to give.

Format: no more than ten questions total, counting the follow-ups, and the whole draft under 500 words. Please end with a brief note, three sentences at most, telling me which of my questions might rest on a contested or uncharitable reading of his position, and what would make me rephrase or drop that question.
