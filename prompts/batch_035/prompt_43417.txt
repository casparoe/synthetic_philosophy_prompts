I've been asked to contribute a short position statement to a workshop on the ethical implications of panpsychism, and the organizers want my own commitments rather than a survey of the field. Before submitting, I'd like a consistency check: I suspect that at least one pair of these pulls against another, but I can't tell which, and I'd rather find out now than from the commentators. Here is the package:

1. I am a physicalist in every other respect, and I hold that consciousness does not strongly emerge: no new kind of fact appears at some scale where nothing of the sort existed below it. This is my principal reason for being a panpsychist — experience has to be present all the way down, or absent all the way up.
2. The fundamental physical entities — particles, field excitations, whatever turns out to be basic — are loci of extremely simple experience: not thought, not perception, just a faint qualitative character.
3. Experience is never neutral. Every episode of experience carries some valence, however slight: it is always marginally good or marginally bad to be that thing.
4. Morally, bad experience counts wherever it occurs, and there is no complexity threshold below which it stops mattering. A bad episode is a bad episode, wherever it is located.
5. I take the combination problem seriously, and as things stand I believe the only subjects are the fundamental entities: a rock or a thermostat has no unified experience, even though each of its parts is a tiny subject.
6. In practice I am a suffering-minimizer: I believe aggregate suffering can in principle be measured through its physical correlates, compared across cases, and reduced, and I make my dietary and charitable decisions by that standard.

Please audit the set rather than lecture on panpsychism in general: identify which pairs of commitments stand in genuine tension, order those from most to least severe, and for each say what restoring consistency would cost; flag any pair that only appears to be in tension and explain why it dissolves on inspection; and close by naming the single revision that, if I adopted it, would clean up the most.
