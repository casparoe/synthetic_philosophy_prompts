For an upper-level seminar on Wittgenstein (12 students, 80 minutes), design a structured activity in which participants run into family resemblance rather than being told about it. Premise: the students form a translation committee for a field linguist who has brought back descriptions of roughly two dozen practices from a community that uses a single word — invent the word and the practices — covering a cluster of activities sharing overlapping features but no feature common to all of them. The committee must first sort the described practices into "yes" and "no" piles by consensus, then write a definition of the word stating necessary and sufficient conditions, and finally a rotating "field agent" must adjudicate novel borderline cases invented by the other students, using the written definition as the only permitted authority and no appeals to intuition. Provide materials, rules, a timeline, some stake or scoring structure, and a debrief that maps the predictable failure of the definitions onto family resemblance and onto the thought that a concept is held together by shared reactions and agreement in judgment rather than by definitions. Also name at least two things that will predictably go wrong on the first run and how to handle them, and be frank about which feature of Wittgenstein's view no classroom game of this sort can reproduce and the facilitator must supply separately.
