quick background before the actual question: i'm a novelist, i write these slow family sagas nobody buys, and i'm 300 pages into one about two brothers, marcus and larry. marcus is my problem child. as a young man he put two people in the hospital, basically unprovoked, and everyone in the family still talks about him in hushed tones like he's an unexploded shell. now he's 58 and hasn't hurt anyone in three decades, and the book is sort of secretly about whether he's still a dangerous man.

but here's whats eating me. he never explodes, but the reasons he never explodes are all different, and i've accidentally built all three into the draft:

1. the pill. the second he feels his chest go hot he takes some medication (he calls it his governor, which my editor says is too on the nose, whatever) and the rage just drains out of him. it works, every time, it has for 30 years. so the feeling shows up and gets erased before it can become anything.

2. the daughter. she knows his history and she's basically a one-woman security detail. she never lets him drive at night, she intercepts the one guy in town who'd love to provoke him, she intercepts the phone calls. the provocations mostly never even reach him. it's not that he stays calm in bad situations — the bad situations get dissolved upstream.

3. and then in my latest draft i added a scene where the pill fails and the daughter's out of town and he gets cornered anyway and he STILL doesn't do anything, because after 30 years he's become someone who narrates himself out of it. the belief in the pill was apparently doing some of the work? my writing group threw things at me for this scene.

meanwhile larry, the other brother, has never hurt a fly and never needed any machinery to not hurt a fly. and the family treats marcus as the dangerous one and larry as the safe one, forever, no appeal.

so my agent said just call marcus cured and move on, and i said he's not cured, he's contained, and she said what's the difference, and i openned my mouth and realized i don't know. THAT's my question. is "dangerous" a thing you are or a thing you do? if a man would hurt you but for the pill, is he dangerous? what about but for the daughter — is being kept out of bad situations the same as not being the kind of person who's bad in them? and does the third thing (the self-narrating) mean he maybe actually ISN'T dangerous anymore, and if the three cases aren't the same, which one is closest to larry? i want the novel to take a position on this but honestly i'd settle for understanding what the positions even are, cause right now i'm just arguing vibes with my agent.
