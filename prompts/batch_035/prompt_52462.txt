"State Arrow's impossibility theorem, listing its conditions. Explain each condition in plain terms. Explain why the theorem requires three or more alternatives. Finally, describe one way of escaping the theorem and say which condition it gives up."

Here's the student's answer, transcribed exactly as written (so dont fix the punctuation, it's their's):

"Arrow's theorem: for three or more alternatives, no social welfare function satisfies all of unrestricted domain, Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA), and non-dictatorship. Unrestricted domain: the rule has to give an answer for every possible profile of individual preferences. Voters can rank the options any way they like — including cycles? no, wait, individual rankings have to be complete and transitive, so no cycles at the individual level, but any combination of individual rankings is allowed. Pareto: if everyone ranks x above y, the social ranking has to put x above y. IIA: adding or removing an irrelevant option shouldn't change how the social ranking treats the remaining options — like, if a third candidate drops out of the race, the ranking between the two remaining candidates shouldn't change. This is the same IIA you hear about in behavioral economics, decoy effects and all that. Non-dictatorship: there's no single voter whose ranking determines the social ranking all by himself no matter what anyone else says.

Why three alternatives: with only two options, majority rule satisfies all the conditions. There's no room for the profile-dependence tricks the proof uses — Condorcet cycling among three or more options is basically what breaks things, and majority rule with two options is Pareto, non-dictatorial, and independent.

Escape route: use cardinal information. Arrow's setup only looks at ordinal rankings. If we let voters give scores instead — range voting, say — all four conditions can be satisfied."
