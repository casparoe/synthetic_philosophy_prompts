I'm taking an intermediate game theory course, and my study partner Ana has developed remarkably firm instincts about when a strategy is, in her words, "ruled out by reasoning alone." Her track record so far, all of it in two-player settings: in a guessing game we played, where each of us named a number between 0 and 100, the target was two-thirds of our average, and your score was minus your squared distance from the target, she declared that any guess above two-thirds of the maximum was not merely inadvisable but provably mistaken, and that carrying the reasoning to its conclusion would push both of us toward zero. In matching pennies she insisted that no option is ruled out at all and that randomizing is not just permitted but the only sensible plan. When a classmate refused to play an option that was weakly dominated — strictly worse in one contingency, merely tied in the others — she defended the option, on the grounds that ties condemn nothing. And in a coordination game with two equilibria and nothing dominated, she said logic alone could not select between them, so no prediction was warranted.

Our problem set this week contains a game I want to test her on. She would be the row player, choosing among T, M, and B against a column player choosing L or R, with payoffs listed (row, column):

|     | L       | R       |
|-----|---------|---------|
| T   | (4, 4)  | (4, 4)  |
| M   | (6, 0)  | (3, 2)  |
| B   | (3, 2)  | (6, 6)  |

As far as I can tell, no strategy here is strictly dominated by another pure strategy, so I genuinely cannot predict whether her machinery even gets started, let alone where it stops.

What I'd like from you: predict Ana's verdict on this game — which strategies, if any, she will rule out for each player, and what, if anything, she will say the game boils down to. I'm less interested in the bottom line than in the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that fit her track record just as well, and where exactly those rules come apart on this game. Give me your best guess, along with your reasons for preferring it. And since the rules that survive this test may still agree with one another here, tell me what further case would tell them apart — bearing in mind that every game she has ever ruled on, including all of ours, had exactly two players.
