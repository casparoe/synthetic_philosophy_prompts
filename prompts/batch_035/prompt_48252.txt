I'm a high school student taking my first philosophy elective, and we just finished a unit on the Euthyphro dilemma. I've been left with a confusion I hope you can resolve, because it's making the rest of the subject look strange to me.

Here is my understanding of the argument. Plato showed that morality cannot be grounded in God's commands: either God commands what is right because it is already right, in which case morality stands independent of God and divine command theory is false; or what is right is right simply because God commands it, in which case morality is arbitrary, since God could just as easily have commanded cruelty and thereby made it obligatory — and divine command theory is false again. Either way, the theory is refuted, as conclusively as a counterexample refutes a conjecture in mathematics. But if a proof of this kind exists, I don't understand why anyone defends divine command theory anymore, or why philosophy of religion journals (which I've started browsing) keep publishing versions of it. I gather the standard contemporary move is to say that God's commands flow from God's nature, which is necessarily good, so morality is neither independent of God nor arbitrary. That looks like cheating to me: can't the dilemma simply be run again on the nature? Is God's nature good because God has it, or does God have that nature because it is good?

So my question is this: given that the Euthyphro dilemma is a proof, why hasn't divine command theory simply been abandoned? Are its defenders just refusing to accept a valid argument, or have I misunderstood what the dilemma actually establishes?
