For the last six years I've worked at a mid-sized philanthropic foundation that funds public-health delivery programs, and I've ended up on the committee that owns our measurement framework — the machinery of indicators, dashboards, and audits through which we decide which grantees get renewed. The framework's history is a long chain of Goodhart incidents. We originally measured patients screened; programs began screening whatever population was easiest to reach, so we moved to high-risk patients screened; programs front-loaded high-risk outreach into renewal months, so we added a spot-check audit; auditors were scheduled in advance, so we randomized them; and so on. Our current composite has a bit over thirty components, each one traceable to a specific gaming episode, and the standing institutional answer to each new episode is that the composite needs one more component. What I want to understand is what this practice — respond to gaming by refining the proxy, indefinitely — actually presupposes about the thing being measured.

I read the LessWrong-adjacent literature on this enough to know the standard taxonomy of Goodhart's law (regressional, extremal, causal, adversarial), so you can take that as background rather than explaining it from scratch. It seems to me that iterative refinement is a coherent strategy for some of those varieties and not others: refinining a noisy proxy can help with regressional Goodhart, but against adversarial Goodhart each refinement just changes the game the grantees are optimizing, and a decade of escalating composites has done something to what the field even counts as a good program — the grantees now organize their actual operations around our dashboard categories, not just their reports. So the question I keep circling is this: does the practice of indefinite proxy-refinement only make sense on the presupposition that there is a fixed latent quantity — genuine health improvement, independent of any measure of it — that our metrics are successively approaching? Or can it survive on the weaker view that the target is partly constituted by what we choose to measure? What exactly does the practice strictly presuppose about measurement, value, and the stability of the target, and which rival views (a constructivist or performativity view, on which the measure and the measured thing co-evolve) would it quietly rule out? Relatedly, does the practice presuppose that the measured and the measurer are independent — that our watching doesn't change what's watched except through the funded interventions themselves — given that in fact the whole field now orients around our framework?

Please give me a reconstruction of the implicit theory underwriting proxy-refinement, a clear separation between what the practice strictly requires and what it merely fits well with, and a verdict on where it is underdetermined — that is, which several incompatible views of measurement and value would all license it. Then tell me what the practice would have to change if the latent-quantity presupposition were abandoned in favor of the co-evolutionary one, and whether the taxonomy's distinction between regressional and adversarial Goodhart marks the real fault line between "the composite needs one more component" and "the composite needs to be abandoned." Keep the whole thing under 700 words, and lead with your single most important claim rather than easing into it.
