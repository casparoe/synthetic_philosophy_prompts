For a philosophy assignment I have to write a short position statement on personal identity and then defend it in a class discussion. Before I commit to it, I'd like to know whether my views actually hang together. Here is the set: (1) What makes me the same person over time is psychological — memory, character, personality. If my brain were transplanted into another body, I would go with the brain; the body left behind would not be me. (2) I could not possibly have originated from a different sperm and egg. If a different sperm had fertilized the same egg, the resulting person would be someone else — perhaps similar, but not me. (3) If scientists assembled an exact physical and psychological copy of me without any causal connection to the original — no scanning, no shared matter, just independently building the same person from scratch — I think that copy would be me, or at least there would be no important difference between the two of us. (4) It is a matter of pure luck that I exist at all: a tiny difference before my conception and my parents would have had a different child, and I would simply never have been. These all strike me as common-sense views, but I've begun to suspect that some of them pull against each other, and I can't tell which. Please go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look like it. For each genuine tension, say how bad it is, what the cheapest fix would be, and what that fix costs me elsewhere. Finally, tell me which single revision, if I made it, would clean up the most.
