A pop-psychology book I'm reading offers this summary of Freud's critique of religion: "Drawing on Totem and Taboo, Freud argued in The Future of an Illusion that religion is a delusion on par with paranoia. Religious belief originates in the child's helpless need for a powerful, protective father, a need carried into adulthood; and since the belief is explained by this infantile wish rather than by any evidence, it is straightforwardly false. Freud traced the origin back to the parricide of the primal father, which he demonstrated through psychoanalytic evidence, and concluded that religion simply is a collective neurosis that humanity must outgrow, its consolations to be replaced by science."

I'm inclined to accept the summary, since Freud really was hostile to organized religion, but a friend insists Freud was more careful about terminology than the book is. Please take the role of a historian of psychoanalytic thought, think carefully before answering — perhaps first sketching what Freud actually claimed in these works and then testing each claim of the summary against that sketch. Go claim by claim: which parts would a Freud scholar endorse, which are simplifications a scholar would tolerate, and which did Freud himself explicitly deny? Finally, does the book's implicit conclusion — that Freud thought the falsity of religion follows directly from its psychological origin — survive these corrections?
