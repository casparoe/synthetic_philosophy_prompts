I am an American, raised on the idea that the dream of rising is dignity itself. Here is my opening move. I'll grant you that bragging is obnoxious and that we have our own envy norms — we just wait for the successful to stumble and then relish the fall. But Jantelagen is different in a way I think is fatal to your position: it taxes achievement itself, not the display of it. The kid who aces every exam, the founder who gets rich building something people want — the message they receive is not "be modest about your success" but "your success is a kind of betrayal of the rest of us." And notice that your own societies do not actually live by the rule: your Nobel laureates, your world-conquering companies, your Olympic champions — none of them acted as though they were no better than anyone. If the norm's celebrated benefits depend on enough people quietly breaking it, so that the community lives off the ambition it publicly condemns, isn't Jantelagen just envy with better PR — a free-rider scheme where the meek consume the surplus created by the proud?
