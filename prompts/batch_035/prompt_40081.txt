Is there a name for this thing? I work on interpreting neural nets and keep running into a disagreement that nobody seems to have a word for. Two groups take the same trained network and come back with different accounts of what it represents — different features, different internal decompositions — and both accounts reproduce the network's behaviour on every input anyone has tried. Each group insists theirs is what's actually in the model, the other being just a convenient re-labelling. Then a third person shrugs and says maybe neither story is "in" the model at all: any behaviour-preserving account is as good as any other, so there's simply no fact of the matter about what the network "really" represents. Someone tried to settle it with "just look at the weights, they break the tie", but you can redescribe the same weights in a different basis and the dispute just recurses a level down.

Philosophy must have fought this fight already, presumably over human language or animal signalling, long before anyone cared about neural nets. So: does the stance that only behaviour constrains what a system represents have a name? Is there a name for the opposing view that there's a fact of the matter about which decomposition is correct? And is there a standard rejoinder to the "look at the weights" move — or to the point that, unlike with humans, we designed and trained this thing, so maybe the training procedure or the designer's intent pins down the right decomposition? If the classic concept doesn't map cleanly onto the network case, tell me where the analogy strains. Please answer in British English.
