I'm a second-year PhD student in cognitive science, and since my lab works on the temporal resolution of perception I've been steeped in Dennett and Kinsbourne's Orwellian vs Stalinesque distinction for the past year — Orwellian revision being where the experience happens as it initially seems and the memory record gets doctored afterwards, Stalinesque being where the brain edits the content before it's ever experienced at all. Everyone keeps telling me the distinction is supposed to dissolve once you buy Multiple Drafts, but before I accept that I wanted to see where my own gut verdicts actually land. So I went through a pile of cases and wrote down what I find myself saying, and now I want to know if there's a single principle behind them or whether I'm just inconsistent.

Here are the verdicts. Color phi with the two spots around 100ms apart: I'm confident I experienced the dot moving across and changing color midstream — I don't for a second believe I only acquired a false memory of the motion afterwards, the motion felt live at the time. So that one comes out Stalinesque for me, the trajectory must have been composed before it hit experience. Cutaneous rabbit, taps at the wrist and elbow half a second apart: I genuinely felt taps hopping up the forearm, at spots that were never touched, and again I can't bring myself to call that ordinary memory corruption — Stalinesque again. Backward masking where the mask lands 50ms after the flash: fine with saying the flash was never experienced, it got cancelled before broadcast, not experienced-then-forgotten — Stalinesque a third time. But here's where it breaks: last week I was describing a magic trick to a friend and confidently "remembered" the assistant's dress being red when it wasn't — I'm sure I experienced the trick accurately at the time and my memory got contaminated later, which is the textbook Orwellian pattern, and no one would say my brain pre-edited the experience in anticipation of how I'd gossip about it later. And then there's the case I keep flip-flopping on: the cutaneous rabbit with a two-second gap between the wrist tap and the elbow tap. The hopping phenomenology is exactly the same as in the fast version, but a two-second pre-experiential buffer sounds ridiculous, so when I think about the mechanism I want to say it was Orwellian fill-in, and when I just replay how it felt I want to say Stalinesque. I genuinely cannot settle it.

So, the question: is there one principle that captures all of these? The obvious candidate is some kind of temporal integration window — anything revised within a few hundred milliseconds of the first relevant event gets composed pre-experience (Stalinesque), anything after that is real experience plus a corrupted record (Orwellian) — and it seems like it would save my first three verdicts and my magic-trick one, but I suspect it can't give me both the fast rabbit and the two-second rabbit the way I want them, and I'd rather know which verdict has to go than paper over it. Please also tell me what the surviving principle predicts for a case I haven't listed: suppose that during the two-second-gap rabbit I have to press a button the instant I feel a phantom tap on my mid-forearm — does the principle say I press during the gap, at the elbow tap, or not at all? And if you think the whole distinction collapses under this exercise rather than being saved by a window, argue that from my verdicts, not from doctrine — that's also an answer, but it has to be earned.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence — name the principle, or say outright that none works — and then give the reasoning; no preamble, and don't restate my cases back at me before getting on with it.
