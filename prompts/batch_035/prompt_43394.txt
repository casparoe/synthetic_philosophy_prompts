I am preparing materials for a seminar on the boundary between manipulation and legitimate persuasion, and I would like to use a colleague of mine as a fixed point of reference. Her considered judgments on a series of cases are as follows. She counts as manipulation a retailer's false assurance that an item is the last one in stock when the storeroom is full; a negotiator who opens with a deliberately inflated figure, knowing that the anchor will drag the final price upward; and a partner in a failing relationship who gradually rewrites its history until the other person doubts their own memory. By contrast, she sees nothing manipulative in a workplace cafeteria that places fruit at eye level and pastries at the back in order to nudge employees toward healthier eating, and nothing manipulative in a charity appeal built around truthful but harrowing photographs, even though she concedes that the photographs do their persuasive work on the emotions rather than through argument. Next week I intend to present her with three fresh cases and predict her answers in advance, and I would like your help. The first: an oncologist, wanting a frightened patient to choose the treatment that patient would endorse after calm reflection, presents the survival statistic in its positive frame, saying that ninety percent of patients survive, while knowing that the identical figure phrased as a ten percent mortality rate would deter the patient from a treatment the doctor believes is best. The second: a friend who, after being refused a favor, openly invokes everything she has done for the refuser over the years, making no false claim and hiding nothing about what she is doing, in order to induce guilt and get the favor anyway. The third: a political advertisement that is entirely truthful, discloses everything relevant, and motivates viewers to vote for a policy that will leave most of them financially worse off, by pairing accurate figures with frightening imagery. Please predict her verdict on each of the three cases, identify the principle or principles that most plausibly generate her existing judgments, indicate precisely where those candidate principles come apart on the new cases, give your best overall prediction together with your reasons for preferring it, and propose the single further case that would most cleanly separate the leading candidates. In your reply, please write in continuous prose only, as one would in a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
