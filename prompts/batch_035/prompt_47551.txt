I run a small reading group — mostly non-philosophers, we've been doing applied ethics — and next month's session is on lying vs. misleading. The trigger was an argument with one of the members who insists that anything "technically true" can't be as bad as a flat lie, and another who thinks the whole distinction is a lawyer's invention. What I want is a decision tree that sorts people into the actual positions on whether there's a moral difference, starting from concrete cases rather than theory labels, and I want it built so people discover what they believe instead of picking the team they already root for.

Some specs. The tree should start from a common commitment everyone in the room will grant (something like: deliberately causing someone to have a false belief is pro tanto wrong), and then branch on questions a normal person can answer without knowing where they lead. The cases I want worked in as branch nodes or quiz questions: the scalar-implicature dodge (saying "I ate some of the cookies" when you ate all of them), the true-conjunct dodge ("Are you seeing someone?" — "I went out with friends last night"), answering a different question than the one asked, the half-truth, misleading by pure omission, and misleading non-verbally (a nod, a raised eyebrow). The leaves should include the strict Kantian no-lying-ever position, the standard moral-difference view (lies are intrinsically worse), the equivalence view (no intrinsic difference, only contingent ones), and a view some of my group flirted with that I've seen called responsibility-shifting — the idea that misleading is *worse* in some ways because you drag the hearer into manufacturing the false belief themselves. If there are real positions my tree can't separate, flag them instead of pretending they're distinct, and if some combinations of answers lead to a position nobody actually holds, say whether that's because it's incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

One more thing, and this is important: don't give me a neutral map and leave the choosing to me. I want you to commit — to which fork in the tree does the real philosophical work, which diagnostic case you think is load-bearing and which are red herrings, and where you think the debate actually bottoms out. And since the tree is ultimately for sorting people, tell me honestly: based on the branch questions you chose, which leaf do you think most thoughtful people who haven't studied this end up at, and is that a fact about the cases or a fact about how you phrased them? For what it's worth, my own suspicion is that the "technically true" dodge between intimates is basically as bad as lying and everyone who says otherwise is consoling themselves, so if your tree suggests I'm wrong I want to hear the argument, not a shrug.
