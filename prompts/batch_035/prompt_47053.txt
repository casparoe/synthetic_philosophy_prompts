I'm a philosophy graduate student drafting a paper on the meta-problem of consciousness — the problem of explaining, in purely functional and mechanistic terms, why we humans systematically judge that there is a hard problem of consciousness at all: why we report an explanatory gap between physical processes and experience, why we find zombies conceivable, why we insist that a completed neuroscience would still leave something out. My paper's thesis is that the field has jumped to competing solutions (illusionist deflation, phenomenal-concept strategies, dualist readings of the intuition) without first agreeing on what would count as solving the meta-problem in the first place, and my advisor suggested I build the specification before evaluating anyone's attempt to meet it. So that is what I'm asking you for: the desiderata.

Concretely, I want a list of requirements any adequate solution to the meta-problem must satisfy, each with the case behind it. Some of the ones I keep circling, which you should feel free to correct, refine, add to, or reject as a pseudo-requirement: it must explain the actual phenomenology of the hard-problem intuition (its intuitive force, its resistance to argument, its recurring appeal even to people fully briefed on physicalism) rather than merely our verbal reports; it must be non-circular, in that it cannot invoke phenomenal consciousness itself to explain our beliefs about phenomenal consciousness, unless it explicitly takes on the extra burden that creates; it should be evidence-neutral enough that a dualist and an illusionist could both accept the explanation of the intuition while disagreeing about what the intuition tracks — though I genuinely don't know whether that neutrality is achievable or whether demanding it already begs some question; and it should be consistent with what empirical psychology actually finds about introspection and confabulation. I'd also like you to take seriously the possibility that some subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable — that the neutrality requirement and the non-circularity requirement, say, pull in incompatible directions — because if so, that is itself the result my paper should be arguing for rather than a failure of the list.

Please be discriminating about which requirements are genuine and which are just prejudices of one camp dressed up as adequacy conditions: I suspect the dualist demand that a meta-problem solution preserve the appearance of epistemic transparency to the subject, and the illusionist demand that the solution come out mechanistic all the way down, are both of that kind, but I may be wrong. Mark each desideratum as non-negotiable or negotiable, and say which one, if any, does the most work excluding live contenders — I have a hunch it's whatever you say about what the solution must do with the persistence of disagreement between smart, informed physicalists and smart, informed dualists, rather than with the origin of the intuition in any single head. A table is fine, prose is fine, whatever serves the reasoning. Please don't pad it with textbook exposition of the hard problem itself; I know that part cold and will cut it.
