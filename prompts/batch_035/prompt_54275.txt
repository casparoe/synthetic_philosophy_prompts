So my little reading group just finished the "Ant Fugue" dialogue from Hofstadter's Gödel, Escher, Bach (the one with the Anteater whose friend Aunt Hillary is an intelligent ant colony, and the ants themselves have no idea what the colony is "thinking"), and we want to figure out where everyone actually stands on whether a colony like that could have a mind. Not which labels people like -- where they actually stand. Can you write me a sorting quiz for this? Eight or so questions, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions.

The leaves I'd want covered (add or rename if the logical space demands it): plain functionalism, where Aunt Hillary thinks just fine; the substrate view that minds need the right kind of causal hardware, brains or something brain-like, so the colony is like a good simulation of weather that never gets wet; the "too slow" objection, that a mind whose thoughts take months relative to its parts is a category mistake somewhere; the deflationary view that the ants dimly know everything anyway so there's nothing left for the colony level to add; and straight eliminativism about all minds, which makes the whole question moot. If some pattern of answers doesn't land on any leaf, say so explicitly and tell me whether that's because the combination is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

Ground rules for the questions: each should be a case a person can answer without knowing which position it's nudging them toward -- so nothing like "do you accept functionalism?". Use cases that actually split people: whether the fact that my neurons have no idea what I'm thinking about is relevant when I claim the colony's ants don't either; whether a colony that reorganizes its worker clusters into different "symbols" depending on the task is more mind-like than one with rigid fixed castes, or whether that flexibility cuts the other way; whether thinking speed counts, if Aunt Hillary needs a week to have what would be a passing thought for us; whether the Anteater holding a conversation with her -- while, admittedly, eating some of her ants -- should count as evidence of mind or just of good pattern-matching on his part; and at least one question about whether the same reasoning applies to a corporation or a city, since that's usually where everyones intuitions start to crack and I want to see it happen on the page.

Keep it usable by people who haven't read GEB -- one sentence of setup where needed, no assumed knowledge beyond that. No reading lists or citations please, and if you bring in Hofstadter paraphrase him rather than quoting, my group can't check quotes and neither can I. Scoring key should note anywhere two leaves can't actually be seperated by the questions.
