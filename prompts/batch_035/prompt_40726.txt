I teach a second-year seminar on rationality, ~14 students, one 50-minute session. They've done basic decision theory (dominance, expected utility) but haven't read Parfit yet. Design an in-class game that puts them in the hitchhiker's seat from Parfit's hitchhiker: stranded in the desert, a driver will only pick you up if she can predict you'll pay her at the destination — and once you're at the destination, paying is just costly, so a self-interested version of you wouldn't pay.

Here's the part I care most about: I want two rounds. In one, players can publicly bind themselves in advance (some kind of commitment device — a signed card, a deposit, whatever you think works). In the other, they can't, and the "prediction" is just whatever mechanism you can devise without me actually reading minds — maybe a cheap-talk announcement stage, maybe the driver being another student who's incentivized to guess well. The point is they should feel the difference: what precommitment buys, and what it's like to be the hitchhiker with no way to make your future self cooperate.

I need rules, a payoff scheme in points (no real money), timing for 50 minutes including the debrief, and a debrief that maps what actually happened onto the idea that it can be rational now to intend to do later what will be irrational later — but not just me lecturing at the end. Also: tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I'm guessing someone will try to game the prediction mechanism), and be honest about which feature of the real case the game just can't reproduce and I'll have to supply in the debrief.
