In my historical sociology seminar the professor uses the slogan "the economic base determines the superstructure" as a conversation-ender. It struck me that the slogan only settles anything if it's making a specific claim, and I can't tell which one. What distinct precise theses might it be expressing — from something nearly trivial, like "productive relations constrain which institutions can persist," through intermediate claims about explanatory priority, to something strong, like "every major feature of law, religion, and culture is fully causally explained by the base and could not have been otherwise"? Lay the readings out as a menu, and for each say whether it is plausible, whether it faces the counterexamples people usually raise against it, and whether it would actually license dismissing my classmate's claim rather than just re-describing the disagreement. One classmate also mentioned that some defenders read the slogan as licensing functional explanation — superstructures persist because of their effects on the base — so please include that as a candidate reading if you think it's a genuine interpretive option. Finally, please mark which points in your answer are settled among scholars who work on historical materialism and which are genuinely contested — including what Marx himself committed to — and don't present a contested reading as the standard one.
