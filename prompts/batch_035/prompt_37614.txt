I tried drafting it last night and halfway through I started suspecting my actual views don't hang together, so before I commit anything to paper I want an audit. here's roughly where I stand:

1. moral facts don't move. slavery in virginia in 1780 was wrong, period, not merely wrong-by-our-lights. I really don't want to end up a relativist about this.
2. the "man of his time" defence never works as an excuse. the fact that basically everyone around jefferson thought owning people was fine doesn't make him one bit less blameworthy for it.
3. but blame requires that you could have known. someone who genuinely couldn't have known they were doing wrong isn't blameworthy, and I think most ordinary virginians back then genuinely couldn't have known -- moral knowledge spreads slowly over generations, kind of like science.
4. moral progress is real. abolition really was progress, we know more now than they did then. and by the same token our descendants will condemn us for something we currently can't see, and they'll be right to condemn us.
5. I'd been assuming 1 and 3 fit together fine because wrongness and blameworthiness are seperate things anyway -- an act can be wrong without the agent being blameworthy -- but I havent actually thought that through.
