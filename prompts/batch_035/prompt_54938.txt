(1) purism: whether you know p never depends on what's at stake for you, practical interests don't reach into the epistemic status of your belief. I got here from thinking it's just wierd for decision theory to reach backwards into what you know. (2) I'm an invariantist about "knows" — same relation expressed in every context of utterance, and the shift everyone reports between the two bank-case conversations (Hannah and Sarah, the Friday deposit thing) is pragmatics, not semantics, so no contextualism for me. (3) knowledge is the norm of practical reasoning: it's proper to treat p as settled in your deliberation if and only if you know p — this is the one I'd actually fight for, it's what makes knowledge the concept that deliberation runs on. (4) knowledge norm of assertion as well: flatly asserting p is proper only if you know p. (6) last thing: I think what you're permitted to do can vary with the stakes even while what you know stays fixed, that's how I keep practical stuff out of epistemology altogether.
