i'm a software engineer with zero philosophy background, and I got into a slack argument about the word "if" that I can't stop thinking about. I wrote down where I actually stand on conditionals, and a coworker who did a philosophy minor took one look, said a couple of these definately can't all be true, and then got pulled into a meeting before telling me which. can you go through the list and tell me which ones are in real tension, ranked by severity, the cheapest fix for each, which only look like conflicts but actually aren't, and whether there's a single revision that cleans up the most mess? for the record I have no idea which one I'd give up.

1. "if A then B" means what it means in logic: true exactly when A is false or B is true, i.e. ¬A ∨ B. I think that's right for English too, not just for truth tables. a conditional with a false antecedent is vacuously true and I don't get why people treat that like a scandal.
2. "If Oswald didn't kill Kennedy, someone else did" — true. "If Oswald hadn't killed Kennedy, someone else would have" — false. those feel like completely different claims to me. same with "if I'd dropped that glass just now it would have shattered" (true) versus "if I'd dropped it, it would have jumped back up into my hand" (false).
3. but I don't accept that there are two different "if"s in english. the "would have" sentence is the same proposition as the plain one, just stated with different grammer — tense and mood don't change logic.
4. even when a conditional is true, you can be out of line asserting it. if I'm 100% sure it won't rain tonight, telling you "if it rains, they'll cancel the match" as some kind of warning is still somehow off, like I'm implying something I don't believe.
5. conditional promises work the same way: "if it rains this weekend I'll mow the lawn" — if it doesn't rain, the promise was kept, full stop, nobody owes anybody anything.
