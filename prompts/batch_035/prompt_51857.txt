i keep running into the "voting is like littering" move whenever the paradox of voting comes up. it goes: sure, one candy wrapper on the highway doesn't measurably dirty anything, and nobody's single abstention decides an election — but if everyone reasoned that way we'd be knee-deep in trash and neck-deep in illegitimate governments. so just as you shouldn't litter even though your wrapper alone is harmless, you shouldn't stay home on election day even though your vote alone won't decide anything. people deploy it mostly to guilt-trip nonvoters, and honestly it usually works on me, but i've started wondering whether it's doing real argumentative work or just sounding deep.

you're a political philosopher who also knows decision theory cold. think about this carefully before answering, and maybe start with some rough brainstorming notes on the mapping. then give me the full audit: what corresponds to what (a table is fine — the act, the harm, the threshold, the "if everyone did it" clause, whatever else belongs), where the analogy breaks, and which breaks actually matter for the use it's being put to (getting individuals to vote) versus which are just noise.

some things i'd want you to take seriously: littering seems to have a smooth dose-response — each wrapper adds a tiny marginal harm no matter what anyone else does — whereas a vote looks all-or-nothing at some pivot that almost never occurs, and its effect depends on which side everyone else piles onto (a wrapper never gets "outvoted" by somebody elses wrapper). but there's also the margin-of-victory stuff people talk about — mandates, legitimacy, close elections begetting closer ones — which looks alot more litter-like, continuous, no pivot needed. do those carry the analogy on their own?

last two asks. first: does the guilt-trip conclusion survive once the disanalogies are all on the table, or was the warm feeling doing all the work the whole time? second, if the analogy can't carry it: is there a better one? the best candidate i've heard is a threshold public good — like a Kickstarter that only delivers if contributions cross a line, except the line is set by the other side's turnout. does that do better? and does it still get people to the polls, or does it quietly license staying home whenever your side looks like it'll win anyway?
