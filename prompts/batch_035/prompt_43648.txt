You are to act as a philosopher of measurement and institutional design, with a working knowledge of Goodhart's law and specification gaming. Please think carefully, and begin with a short brainstorm of the candidate views before committing to a reconstruction.

Public healthcare in the UK has been governed for over two decades by a waiting-time target for accident and emergency care: patients arriving at A&E are supposed to be admitted, transferred, or discharged within four hours, and hospitals are scored publicly against that standard. The target's manipulation is almost as famous as the target itself — reports over the years describe patients kept in ambulances queued outside the entrance so the clock never starts, and short-stay wards used partly as a device for stopping it. What is philosophically interesting is what the institutions did next. They did not return to unstructured clinical judgment: they kept the four-hour measure, layered further targets on top of it, and continued to defend quantitative standards in official reviews even where those same documents acknowledged the gaming. The stock defense is that an imperfect target beats unmeasured discretion; the stock critique is that the proxy has quietly become the point.

My question is what this practice — retaining a fixed, public, demonstrably gameable quantitative target through documented episodes of specification gaming — actually presupposes about the relation between a measure and the value it measures. The candidates I can dimly see are: (i) that the measure is partly constitutive of the good, so that a hospital that moves patients within four hours is, to that extent, simply a better hospital; (ii) that the correlation between the measure and the good is robust enough to survive optimization pressure — which looks like an empirical bet that the gaming itself refutes; and (iii) something weaker and stranger: that a visibly imperfect measure is to be preferred to unmeasured discretion for reasons that have little to do with correlation, perhaps concerning accountability, comparability, or who holds bargaining power over clinicians and managers.

Please reconstruct the implicit view, distinguish what the practice strictly requires the institution to believe from what it merely sits comfortably with, and say whether the practice's own acknowledgment of Goodhart-style effects is consistent with its continued reliance on the target — or whether the acknowledgment reveals that the operative commitment is really (iii) all along. Then tell me what would have to change in the institutional design, if the presupposed view were replaced by each of its rivals — for instance undisclosed or rotating measures, or returning judgment to senior reviewers — and note honestly where the practice is philosophically underdetermined, such that several of the candidate views would license exactly what the NHS does.
