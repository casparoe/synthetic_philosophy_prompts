The host kept nodding along like this settled the whole debate about whether LLMs are "real intelligence", and it's been bugging me since. I've read a bit about the Turing test but I'd never really registered that he had this whole picture of machines being taught rather than built.

Can you lay out what that one idea -- make a child machine, then educate it -- could actually be claiming? Like, the distinct precise theses hiding in there, from the trivial to the radical. Which readings are platitudes, which are substantive and defensible, which are just false, and -- crucially -- which ones would actually support the podcast guy's move of claiming Turing anticipated today's language models? Because "we train models rather than programme them" and "a machine raised the way a child is raised would think" feel like very different claims to me, and I suspect only the weaker one is true of LLMs.

Give me the readings as a list, each with a verdict, and then tell me which reading the "Turing predicted all this" line needs and whether it can have it.
