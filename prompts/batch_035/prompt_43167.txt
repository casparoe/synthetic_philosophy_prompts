I'm taking a biomedical ethics elective this semester and we just started the doing vs. allowing unit, and it's kind of wrecking me, so I've been trying to figure out what I actually believe by just going case by case on gut reactions. Here's my list so far. Runaway tram headed for five workers, you can divert it to a side track where it kills one: fine, I'd pull the switch without agonizing. Big guy on a footbridge who's the only thing heavy enough to stop the tram, push him off: no way, not fine. Surgeon quietly takes one healthy walk-in's organs to save five dying patients: obviously not fine. Waking up to find a famous violinist has been plugged into your kidneys and only you can keep him alive, you unhook yourself and he dies: fine, it's your body, and even though he dies it doesn't feel like killing. Turning off a ventilator for a hopelessly ill patient whose family wants it off: fine. Giving that same patient a lethal injection so they reach the same endpoint a bit sooner: not fine, and I can't really defend that one, since they die either way. On the pure allowing side: walking past a kid drowning in a shallow pond because you don't want to ruin your shoes: not fine, that's monstrous. But not sending $40 to a charity that reliably saves kids far away: fine, and honestly I don't even feel bad about it, though I suspect that's a problem.

There are two more cases I keep flip-flopping on, which is really why I'm writing. One is a version of the tram where the lone person on the side track happens to be heavy enough that his body stops the tram, so if he weren't lying there the tram would curve back around and kill the five anyway. My gut says this is somehow worse than the plain switch case, but I genuinely go back and forth on whether it's actually different. The other is from a scenario my TA made up about a hospital blackout: the backup generator is currently running one patient's ventilator, and you can flip it over to a ward where five patients' ventilators are failing, which lets the one die. My gut says fine, same as the tram switch, but my roommate insists it's just the organ transplant case in disguise — taking one person's life support to save five — and I don't have a good answer for her.

So what I'm asking is: is there a principle (or maybe a couple of principles) that captures all my verdicts, or close to it? If it can't capture all of them, which one should I give up — the lethal injection one, the pond/charity pair, the ventilator ones, or something else? And what does the best principle predict about the generator case and the looping track case? If you can, I'd also like one further case that would really settle whether what I care about is the doing/allowing distinction itself or something else like whether someone is being used as a means, since right now I can't tell those two apart in my own head.
