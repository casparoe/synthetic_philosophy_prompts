Been reading about the aesthetic testimony problem and I can't tell if it's weaker than everyone makes it sound, or if its actually a crazy view. The idea, as I understand it, is that you can't gain aesthetic knowledge secondhand — if a friend tells me a film is a masterpiece, I can believe them, but I don't *know* it, because aesthetically knowing something requires experiencing the work yourself. OK, I can sort of see the intuition. But if thats right, doesn't it follow that critics are just selling opinions nobody can verify until they've already bought the product? And that everyone who thinks the Taj Mahal is breathtaking without having stood in front of it is straight up wrong? That last one seems nuts, since nearly everything any of us believes about art came from other people in the first place. So my question is: at what point does accepting this view commit you to saying most peoples aesthetic beliefs are unjustified, and how do proponents square it with the fact that we're all perfectly happy to trust an art historian about which canvas is the original and which is the forgery? Is the move there just that provenance is a fact and beauty isn't, and if so why does being a fact make testimony about it suddenly fine? sorry if this is basic, I'm pretty new to aesthetics and honestly the more I think about it the less sure I am that I even know what my own aesthetic beliefs are doing when half of them were probably absorbed from reviews I read as a teenager.
