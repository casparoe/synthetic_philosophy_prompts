My roommates and I run a tiny reading group that's been slogging through luck egalitarianism for about a month now, and I've realized we keep talking past each other because nobody's clear on what they actually believe -- people who say they're "for equality of opportunity" turn out to mean wildly different things once you push them on cases. So I want a "which position do you actually hold?" quiz that sorts people via concrete cases rather than labels. The positions I'd want as possible outcomes: Dworkin-style equality of resources (with the hypothetical insurance thing), Cohen's equal access to advantage, Arneson's equal opportunity for welfare, plus the nearby critics -- Anderson-style relational/democratic equality, sufficientarianism, prioritarianism, and plain old Rawlsian views that refuse to track responsibility at all. If some corner of the logical space has no named occupant, just say so and tell me whether that's because it's incoherent or just nobody's bothered.

The diagnostic questions are the important part, so here's what I want them built out of. Two distinctions must show up somewhere: brute luck vs option luck (I want a case about the reckless climber with no insurance, or something equivalent), and the difference between having a preference you didn't choose and being responsible for acting on it (I'm thinking of the person whose religious convictions require fasting and who develops health problems -- does compensation depend on whether the commitments were "chosen"?). I also want the harshness issue in there: something like the uninsured motorcyclist who needs a transfusion, asked in a way that tests whether someone bites the bullet and lets people suffer for their choices or bails toward a sufficiency or relational view. And please include one case I made up that keeps derailing our discussions: a friend of mine quit a well-paid job to try becoming a professional streamer, failed after two years, and now has much worse prospects than her peers -- should the egalitarian state treat that gap as her problem? I genuinely don't know what the "official" answer is on any of these theories, so the scoring key should explain what each answer pattern commits you to, not just name-drop.

A few constraints: eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options that never use the jargon (no question like "do you believe in responsibility-sensitive egalitarianism"), and the audience is people who've done the reading but argue badly, so it's fine if the scoring key gets technical. Also, please be honest about which leaves the quiz can't actually separate -- my hunch is that Dworkin vs Cohen vs Arneson mostly collapses once you're answering multiple choice about cases, and if that's right I'd rather know than get a quiz pretending otherwise. American English please. And if you can work in whether the "expensive tastes" cases do any real work separating the theories (I've always suspected they're the same problem as the surfer one in disguise), that would settle a running argument with my roommate.
