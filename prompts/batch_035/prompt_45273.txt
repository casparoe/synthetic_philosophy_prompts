Before I write my term paper for my metaphysics seminar on individuation, I'd like a consistency check on the package of views I've found myself defending in section. The set is: (1) Max Black's two-sphere universe is a genuine metaphysical possibility — a world could contain exactly two spheres sharing every qualitative property — so the identity of indiscernibles, in its strong form, is false. (2) Leibniz's law, the indiscernibility of identicals, is a necessary truth known a priori. (3) I am an anti-haecceitist: there are no primitive thisnesses, and all facts about particular objects, including facts about which object is which, supervene on the total qualitative character of the world. (4) I hold a no-brute-plurality principle: whenever a world contains two objects rather than one, there must be something about the objects themselves — some feature, relation, or aspect of reality — in virtue of which there are two of them; the bare fact of plurality cannot be the end of the story. (5) I am sympathetic to structuralism about concrete objects: an object is nothing over and above a node in the world's network of relations, so a complete structural description of a world fixes all the facts about its objects. And (6) I think it is simply a mistake to describe the Black world as one sphere "counted twice" — it determinately contains two.

Please tell me which of these commitments are in genuine tension and which tensions are only apparent. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them as equally serious, and for each genuine tension say specifically what would have to give and what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set. I genuinely don't know where the problem is, so please don't soften anything.
