I have recently joined the disciplinary committee of the board that licenses members of my profession, and we have wide discretion over sanctions: anything from a private reprimand to permanent revocation. In my first two hearings, the chair kept invoking the old line that "the punishment must fit the crime," and I realized I did not know what she was actually claiming.

In the first case, she used the phrase to cut off a colleague who wanted to weigh the licensee's cooperative attitude during the proceedings and the deterrent message our sanction would send to others in the profession — the implication being that these considerations were beside the point. In the second case, she invoked the same line to justify a substantially harsher sanction than our precedents supported, reasoning that although the misconduct was similar in kind to the earlier cases, it was more deliberate and more concealed, and therefore demanded a proportionally harsher response. I am not sure both uses can rest on the same principle, and I need to know what I actually think before the next hearing.

Please lay out, as a structured list I can bring to our next meeting, the distinct precise theses this slogan could be expressing — starting with readings so weak they are nearly platitudes, through substantive and defensible positions, down to readings that are probably false. For each reading, tell me: whether it would actually support the chair's first use (dismissing consequences for deterrence and future conduct), whether it would support her second use (scaling sanctions upward with the deliberateness of the offense beyond what precedent dictates), or whether it supports neither or is simply silent on both. I would also like you to address the converse: my colleague's forward-looking approach sounds consequentialist, but I have read that a pure consequentialist about punishment faces serious objections — including that it can license punishing the innocent when doing so maximizes benefit — so I want to know which of the defensible readings, if any, leave room for consequences at the margins without collapsing into that. Finally, tell me which single case, if it came before our committee, would most sharply reveal which reading the chair is in fact running, since I suspect our precedents so far do not distinguish among the candidates.
