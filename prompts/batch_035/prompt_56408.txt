I had a big argument with my history teacher about who was to blame for WWI and now I can't work out whether I've found something real or just talked myself into a corner. We'd been covering the Fischer thing — the historian who argued Germany's leaders actually wanted the war rather than everyone sleepwalking into it. Here's my argument. (1) You can only be blamed for a choice you really had. (2) By late July 1914 nobody really had a choice: the mobilisation timetables, the alliance commitments, Germany's blank cheque to Austria — once Russia started moving, each leader's decision was fixed by what the others had already done, like dominoes "choosing" to fall. (3) And even if any one minister had personally refused, the war would have happened anyway, so nobody's individual decision made a difference, and you can't blame someone for something that would've happened without them. (4) A nation is only responsible for a war if its decision-makers are, so no nation is responsible — which would make the Versailles war-guilt clause morally confused even if Fischer turned out to be completely wrong. (5) But that can't be right, because the same argument would show nobody is responsible for anything collective — a bank run, a stampede, climate change — since in those too each person's bit made no difference and everyone was carried along by everyone else. And maybe even worse, if Fischer IS right and a handful of ministers deliberately engineered the war, my argument still lets the German nation off the hook while saying those ministers were just doing what their positions and ambitions pushed them into. Something's gone wrong somewhere but I can't name it. Which premise or step should I give up, why did it look so plausible, and what do I have to pay for giving it up? Please write in British English, and could you seperate the stuff that's actually settled among historians and philosophers from the stuff people genuinely disagree about — I don't want to walk back into class feeling confident about something that's actually still being fought over.
