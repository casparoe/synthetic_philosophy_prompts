Two of my friends have been going at each other about insect welfare for like two years, and I've finally talked them into trying an adversarial collaboration. One of them is a scale-argument person: her line is that with something like a quintillion insects alive at any moment, even a fairly low credence that any of them have valenced experience means the expected amount of suffering at stake dwarfs everything else we worry about, so serious research money and attention should be moving there now, not eventually. The other is a skeptic with a threshold streak: he thinks moral status doesn't come in expected-value crumbs — that below some credence you should just act as if insects aren't in the moral circle at all — and he half suspects the scale stuff is motivated reasoning by people who want the movement to have a bigger lane.

Write the joint statement they should come out with. I want:

1. A numbered section of claims both can sign. They should be able to agree on more than you'd expect — the empirical stuff about insect nervous systems, what nociception does and doesn't establish, how the behavioral evidence cuts both ways, the actual size of the numbers, and that neither of them is a monster.
2. A disagreements table, where each remaining disagreement is phrased so its own side would accept the wording (no gotcha phrasing either direction).
3. The cruxes. My guess is at least one is whether moral considerability is the kind of thing you can be 5% of, and another is how to handle decisions under moral uncertainty generally — straight expected value vs. some threshold vs. something else — but let them find their own cruxes rather than me putting words in their mouths.
4. For each crux, a concrete study, observation, or argument both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. Trade-off tests, analgesia self-administration, whatever — but it has to be something both sides concede is relevant, not a rigged test.

A couple of extra asks. First, don't have them resolve everything, and if part of the dispute turns out to be verbal (e.g. what each of them means by "sentience" in the first place), say which part and why. Second, end with a short honesty paragraph: how confident each of them is in their overall position, and — this is the part I care most about — the single claim in the joint statement that each one is least sure the other would actually put their name to, with a sentence on why.

Keep the tone like it's going out to our reading group, not a journal. It can be a bit rough. If some sentence only works because it quietly assumes one of their frameworks, rewrite it til it doesn't.
