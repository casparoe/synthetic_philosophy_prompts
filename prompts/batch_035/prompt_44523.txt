I'm not a philosophy guy, I work at a warehouse, but watching The Matrix with my kid got me thinking about knowledge, and I came up with a little test that seemed to work great: a belief only counts as knowing if you'd have dropped it had it been false. Like, you don't KNOW you're not in the Matrix, because if you were, you'd still believe you weren't. But you DO know your car is where you parked it, because if it weren't there you'd have noticed. The test got everything right as far as I could tell. Then my buddy Dave helped me build this and now I can't find the exit:

1. I know my car is parked in my spot right now. (I parked it there this morning, ordinary day, nothing weird.)
2. If its parked in my spot, it hasn't been towed in the meantime.
3. If I know the first thing, and I know it rules out the second, then I know the second. Knowing that p ought to get you the obvious consequences of p, otherwise what's knowing even FOR.
4. But if the car had been towed, I'd still believe it's parked there. I've been at work for six hours with the radio on, nobody would've called me, I'd walk out at 5 and find it gone. So my belief that it wasn't towed is exactly the kind my test rejects — I'd hold it whether or not it was true.
5. So I don't know it wasn't towed.
6. So either I don't know my car is parked there — and by the same token I never know where anything is once I quite looking at it, my kids at school, whether my house is still standing — or else knowing doesn't transfer across obvious consequences, so a detective who deduces whodunit from the footprint doesn't know it, which is nuts.

If I have to pick, keeping 3 feels way better than walking around not knowing where my own car is. But keeping 3 means giving up 1, and that seems to prove too much: before I made up the test I felt like I knew tons of out-of-sight stuff, and the whole point of the test was to explain that, not erase it. So which step do I give up, and what does giving it up cost me?

One more thing eating at me. When I try to save 1, I want to say the check this morning is what made it knowledge and the rest of the day is just me holding onto it. Dave says no — the test applies to what I believe NOW: at 5pm, if it had been towed at noon, I'd still believe, so the morning check stopped counting somewhere along the way. Is he right that the test runs on the current belief and not on how you got it? Because that feels like the whole ballgame to me.
