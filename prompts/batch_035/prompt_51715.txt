My friend has gotten very into the betting side of the coherence literature, and over drinks she keeps handing down verdicts on whether various people can be dutch booked — meaning a bookie offers them a set of bets, each of which they'd happily take, and the set guarantees they loose money however things turn out. Her track record so far. A guy who puts 0.7 on rain tomorrow and 0.6 on no rain: bookable, she said, the bookie just sells him both bets and he's down thirty cents whatever the sky does. A woman betting on a casino die at a sixth per face: not bookable, nothing there. Someone who put 0.25 on each of three horses in a three-horse race where one of them has to win: bookable, this time the bookie buys the bets off her, pays 75 cents for a package that pays out a dollar. A man whose credence in A is 0.8, in B is 0.9, but in A-and-B only 0.2: bookable. And her cousin, whose current credences are perfectly coherent, but who plans to update on tomorrow's poll by nudging every probability halfway toward fifty percent rather than conditionalizing: also bookable, she says, the bookie just has to trade with him today and again after the poll comes out.

Here's my question. The new case is a woman we'll call Priya. She puts 0.53 on rain tomorrow and 0.53 on no rain, so she's slightly incoherent, but she's a haggler: she will not accept any bet unless she reckons it favors her by at least ten percentage points, and that applies to selling bets as well as buying them. Will my friend call her dutch-bookable? Honestly I care less about the yes or the no than about which rule my friend is actually running. As far as I can tell her verdicts so far fit at least a couple of different rules. On one, bookable just tracks whether the person's credences violate the probability rules, maybe counting a bad updating plan as a violation too. On another, what matters is that there exists a set of bets the person would genuinely accept that locks in a loss for them. Those two seem to split on Priya: on the first she's bookable, since 1.06 is not 1; on the second maybe not, because every bet she'd accept looks good to her by her own lights, and I can't find any package of bets she'd take that guarantees the bookie a profit — sell her the rain bet at 43 cents and the no-rain bet at 43 cents and she's up, charge her 63 cents each and she just refuses.

So: predict my friend's verdict on Priya, lay out the candidate rules that fit her track record equally well, say which rule you'd bet on and why, and then propose the further case — a tweak on Priya, or a whole new person — that would best tell the rules apart. Please write it as continuous prose, no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables, just plain paragraphs like you're writing me an email. And don't assume I know any of the technical vocabulary, explain what you need as you go — I've had a couple of drinks.
