I'm a software engineer (backend, mostly distributed systems, zero formal philosophy background) and I need a term paper proposal for an intro ethics seminar I'm taking at night school. Long story, but here's the thing: I got into an argument at Thanksgiving with my brother-in-law, who's doing a masters in philosophy, about whether happiness is the only thing that ultimately matters. He said hedonism has this famous sore spot — the heterogeneity problem — basically the question of what the pleasure of eating good pizza, the pleasure of finally fixing a gnarly bug, and the pleasure of a hot shower even have in common that makes them all count as "pleasure." They don't *feel* anything alike, they don't seem to have a shared ingredient, so what's the one thing hedonism says is the good? That argument has been bugging me for weeks.

My hunch, coming from code: this looks a lot like a typing problem. Maybe pleasures don't share a common property — a base class, if you will — but they share an interface. Like, maybe what makes something a pleasure is its role in an agent: states you're disposed to prolong and seek again, things that function like reward signals do in reinforcement learning, stuff that gets "reinforced." It's duck typing — if it does pleasure-shaped things, it's a pleasure — rather than nominal typing by some internal essence. I suspect framing it that way either rescues hedonism or shows exactly where it breaks (e.g. what about masochistic pain that you're motivated to continue? or anesthesia-like states you'd choose for their downstream effects but don't feel good in the moment?). I want a proposal that turns this half-baked idea into something an actual grader would approve.
