Quick bit of background: I spent 35 years as an entomologist (mostly agricultural pests, some bee work near the end), retired a few years ago, and since then I've fallen down the philosophy of mind rabbit hole. Next month I'm giving a 40-minute talk to my local naturalists' club on insect suffering and the scale argument -- the idea that even a pretty low probability that insects are sentient, multiplied across the absurd number of individual insects alive at any moment, might mean more total suffering than in all the vertebrates put together. The audience is sharp but rusty on math, mostly amateur birders and gardeners, and a few of them read Animal Liberation back in the 70s and beleive the animal ethics question got settled right there.

Don't explain the argument itself to me -- I know it cold, it's the teaching I'm unsure about. I want a plan. Specifically: what has to be in place before the multiplication step can land at all (does the nociception-vs-pain distinction come first, or the raw numbers, or what "probability of sentience" even means for a thing we can't ask?), and which single concrete case should carry the whole talk -- I keep going back and forth between a backyard ant colony and the mosquito larvae in one neighborhood pond, and I honestly can't tell which one does the conceptual work and which one just makes people feel icky. Also: I keep getting pulled toward the point that most individual insects die within days of hatching, so whatever suffering there is gets crammed into very short lives -- but I can't tell if that strengthens the talk or just derails the room, so factor that in. Then the misconceptions I should expect and where in the sequence to head each off (I'm guessing "expected value is just gambler's logic" and "so a fly counts more than my dog?", but there's definately worse coming), what I should openly refuse to get into given 40 minutes and zero background in the hard problem, and one question I can ask at the end to check whether they've actually understood the argument rather than just been disturbed by it.
