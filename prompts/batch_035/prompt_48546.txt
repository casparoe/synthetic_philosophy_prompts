I'm a software engineer who has recently become fascinated by Herbert Simon's work on bounded rationality, and I host a small evening seminar series where non-academics interview researchers. Our next guest is an economist who has spent her career defending satisficing and bounded rationality against orthodox rational-choice theory, and I've been asked to prepare the questions. Please take on the role of a philosopher of economics who knows this literature intimately, think carefully before writing, and perhaps brainstorm the strongest versions of the objections before drafting anything.

Write me eight to ten interview questions that go well beyond asking her to restate what satisficing is. At minimum I want: one question pressing the objection that satisficing is just constrained optimisation in disguise — that setting an aspiration level and stopping at the first option that clears it can be redescribed as maximising a modified utility function — with a follow-up keyed to whatever distinction (procedural versus substantive rationality, perhaps) she is most likely to invoke in reply; one question on how aspiration levels are supposed to rise and fall over a search, and whether that adjustment is itself something a good theory of rationality needs to govern; one question about the famous image of rationality as a pair of scissors, with one blade the agent's cognitive limits and the other the structure of the task environment — specifically, what work the second blade does that a purely psychological story would leave out; and one question on where she stands between the view that heuristics are repairable sources of bias (Tversky and Kahneman's line) and the ecological-rationality view that fast and frugal heuristics can outperform optimisation by exploiting environmental structure. Feel free to add further questions on adjacent issues, such as whether normative economics can survive the move from the fully informed maximiser to the satisficing one.

A few requests on care and calibration. Where a question turns on something genuinely contested among people who know the field — for instance, over how much of orthodox theory satisficing actually overturns versus merely refines — please flag it as contested rather than presenting one side as the settled view. Conversely, tell me plainly where the objections are standard ones from the literature. Finally, since my own reading is entirely second-hand, if any of my questions rest on a mistaken framing of Simon or of the literature, say so rather than writing questions that inherit my mistake.
