i've been reading about the Versailles treaty and something's bugging me. The famous war-guilt clause (article 231 I believe) had Germany accept responsibility for the war as the legal basis for reparations -- so the peace settlement treated the *nation* as the party responsible. Meanwhile the allies also wanted to try the Kaiser personally for offenses against international morality (that mostly fizzled out), and nobody ever seriously suggested putting ordinary Germans on trial. Then after WWII the whole thing flips: Nuremberg puts individuals on trial, and the settlement deliberately avoids anything like a national guilt clause, with reparations decoupled from blame.

so here's what I want you to think through: what theory of responsibility does writing a guilt clause into a peace treaty actually presuppose? That the state is a moral agent that persists over time, so the Germany of 1919 owes for what the Germany of 1914 did? Or just a causal-liability view -- whoever caused the damage pays, no moral blame needed, the way an insurer pays? Or some "the nation benefited from it so the nation pays" idea? Is the practice consistent with itself, given the same treaty deals in both national and personal responsibility? And does it actually *need* one of these theories, or would several rival views of collective responsibility license it equally well?

One more thing that confuses me. When historians fight about WWI -- Fischer saying Germany's leadership deliberately grabbed for world power, Clark's Sleepwalkers saying everyone stumbled into it -- are they even answering the question the guilt clause was about? If Fischer is right does the clause suddenly make sense, and if Clark is right does it become nonsense, or was it never really tracking what historians argue about?

Two requests on how you write it up: keep clearly separate what's settled (about the treaties, what the clause actually did legally) from what's genuinely contested -- don't present Fischer's thesis or Clark's as the historians' consensus, because they aren't -- and same among philosophers, since whether groups can be responsible in any robust sense is itself an open fight. Don't quietly pick a side and present it as the answer.
