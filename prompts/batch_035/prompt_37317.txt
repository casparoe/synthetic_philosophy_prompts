After twelve years as an organizer and then deputy chair of my local chapter, I'm leaving the party, and the honest reason is that I finally sat down with Weil's little 1943 essay on abolishing political parties and couldn't unsee it. Her picture of a party as a machine for exerting collective pressure on each member's mind, living off collective passions that feel like conviction but are really just contagion, and turning every idea into a slogan by the time it reaches you -- I kept recognizing our Tuesday night meetings in it. I have to say goodbye at the chapter's end-of-year dinner. The audience is loyalists, some of them dear friends who've given decades to this thing, a few cynical staffers who'd love to hear it all confirmed, and my successor, who has to keep the machine running on Monday. I'm no good at writing speeches, so please write it out in full -- around five minutes, warm, no citations, nobody there has read her. It should be honest about why I'm going, fair to the people staying, and true to Weil: so no cheap "the party is a family" stuff, but also no pretending she said parties are merely badly run. And it should gesture at her alternative -- collective action without the machine -- without me claiming I know how to build it. I'd like the loyalists arguing with me at the bar afterwards, not just hugging me sadly.
