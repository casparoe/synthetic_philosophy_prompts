My mom is 78 and has early-ish dementia — she still lives at home but needs someone to handle meds, meals, driving, and increasingly just to keep an eye on her. I live twenty minutes away; my two brothers each live a flight away. Up to now I've basically been doing everything, maybe 80% of the hands-on care, and honestly I'm burning out and getting quietly resentful about it. So my brothers called a family meeting, and their solution is a strict rotation: each of us takes equal four-month blocks of being "on call," with them temporarily relocating or moving her in with their families. They have a spreadsheet. Their argument is that anything less than an exactly equal split is exploitative — they don't want to be the brothers who dumped everything on their sister, and fairness is fairness.

Part of me is relieved. But here's what bugs me: Mom gets confused and agitated when her routine changes, she barely knows her sons' houses or their kids, and her nueroslogist said that consistency of caregiver is basically the one lever we still have for keeping her day-to-day functioning from deteriorating faster. When I raised this, my older brother said I'm "hiding behind the diagnosis" to keep control of Mom, and that if I really believed my own argument, the fair thing would be for me to keep doing the extra hours while they pay me for the difference — which opens a whole other can of worms, because money between siblings makes everything gross. And not for nothing, I'm the daughter, so I don't fully trust my own motives: maybe the "consistency is good for her" line is partly me rationalizing wanting to stay in charge, or maybe it's partly them rationalizing why the tidy equal split is the only one on the table. How do I even start thinking about who's right here — is "equal shares" the right measure of what we owe each other in this situation, or is that whole frame somehow off?
