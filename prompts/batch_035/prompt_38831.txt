Okay so this got very real for me: I have two job offers that, after three weeks of pro/con lists that have completely balooned, are exactly tied — same pay, same commute, teams I liked equally. My partner keeps telling me to just flip a coin already, and I hate that advice. My position has always been that if options really seem tied, that just means you haven't dug deep enough — there's a fact about which is better, even if it's buried somewhere, and choosing with literally zero preference is a failure of rationality, not a form of it. What would actually count against that view? I get that the ass starving between two identical haystacks is the classic worry, but I want the harder stuff: whether a genuine tie leaves any room for the choice to still be *mine* rather than the coin's, whether "there's always a hidden tiebreaker" is even distinguishable from unfalsifiable hand-waving, and what to make of the line (I think from the hard choices literature) that in ties you don't discover which option is better, you make one better by putting yourself behind it — is that a genuine alternative or does it collapse back into coin-flipping with extra steps? Rank the defeaters for me, fatal to merely awkward.
