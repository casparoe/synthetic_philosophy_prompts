I'm a backend engineer with zero philosophy background, and I volunteer as the tech person on the board of a small animal-welfare nonprofit. Over the last year we keep getting proposals that, after I went down a rabbit hole one weekend, I realized are all trying to apply something called the hedonistic imperative — David Pearce's idea that we should use biotech to abolish suffering in all sentient life, not just reduce it a bit here and there. Examples we've actually gotten: knocking out certain pain receptors in livestock, breeding chickens without the chronic anxiety that factory conditions produce, gene drives to wipe out a parasite that causes horrible disease in wild deer, and one guy who wanted funding for research into making farm animals so mellow they'd stop caring about confinement. That last one gave everyone the creeps, but nobody could say exactly why, and our board meetings just go in circles between the "any reduction of suffering is a win" camp and the "this is playing God, and besides the animals won't really be animals anymore" camp. We get maybe one of these proposals a month, and the whole board (none of us are philosophers) votes at the end of the meeting, so we need something runnable in one sitting.

Can you turn that whole philosophy into a decision procedure we can apply to any proposal? Specifically I want a numbered list of exactly five items — the five questions we ask in order, each item just a sentence or two: the question itself plus what counts as a good enough answer to move on, and what answer kills the proposal. No introduction, no conclusion, just the five items. If one of the questions is really about values rather than facts — meaning the board just has to vote on it and no amount of data settles it — flag that inside the item itself. The procedure should be grounded in the distinctions the actual theory draws, like taking away suffering versus adding pleasure on top, or raising an animal's baseline so it's ok versus making it indifferent to everything, but phrased so my fellow board members who've never read a page of ethics can apply it. Don't run it on our past proposals for me, I want to do that myself at the next meeting and see where it surprises us.
