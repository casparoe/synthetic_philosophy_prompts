I've been roasting my own coffee for a few years, and lately I can't tell whether my espresso actually changed when I switched grinders or whether my palate has simply developed: the cups I used to love now taste muddy to me, but I can't say whether that reflects a change in the taste-as-experienced or just a change in my standards. This made an argument between two friends at our coffee club feel unexpectedly urgent, and I'd like a verdict on it. Julia, who has read a lot of Dennett, invoked his thought experiment from "Quining Qualia" about the two Maxwell House tasters, Chase and Sanborn: a taster finds his coffee no longer tastes the way it used to, and Dennett presses the point that no investigation the taster could conduct would settle whether his experience itself changed or merely his discrimination and evaluative standards did. Julia said this generalizes: qualia were supposed to be intrinsic, private, and directly apprehensible, but whenever we try to check whether a quale changed, the only accessible evidence consists of similarity and preference judgments — exactly the sort of thing that could shift with standards rather than experience — so qualia, as traditionally conceived, are an incoherent posit. Marco objected that this shows only that introspection can't settle the question, not that there's no question: in principle, neuroscience could establish whether the change occurred early in the pathway (receptor loss on the tongue, say) or late (in evaluative systems), and that looks like a perfectly good way of distinguishing the experience changed from the standards changed. Julia replied that those are functional distinctions that license predictions and interventions; they don't reveal intrinsic qualia, and the traditional posit does no work the functional story leaves undone. Marco's closing move was that Julia may be entitled to claim qualia are theoretically idle, but that is a claim about explanatory utility, not incoherence, and she shouldn't market an Ockham's-razor result as a demonstration that the concept contradicts itself. Please go through their exchange move by move, say who is right at each stage, note anything both of them are missing, and tell me whether anything follows for my own question about my espresso.
