Quick adjudication request, rationalsphere style. I've been reading LW/SSC adjacent stuff for like 12 years but this one stumped me and my two roommates, so I'm outsourcing.

We watched a war movie last weekend (basically two hours of people dying, extremely well done) and afterward Dana and Felix got into it about whether it makes any sense that we seek this stuff out.

(1) Dana said the whole thing is a pseudo-problem: you're not actually sad, you're on your couch with a beer, perfectly safe. The "sadness" is as-if sadness, the same way the fear on a rollercoaster isn't real fear. We enjoy it because we get the flavor of the emotion without the stakes. Paradox dissolved, nothing to explain.

(2) Felix said that's just wrong -- he cried, and he was in a weird mood for the rest of the evening. If that doesn't count as real sadness then the word doesn't mean anything. His move was: the paradox only exists if you assume the mind is a single ledger where experiences are net plus or minus. Once you allow that you can endorse an experience while disliking parts of it, you get that we "enjoy" sad films in the sense of wanting them, while the sadness itself is genuinely unpleasant. Both true, no contradiction.

(3) Dana said that's relabeling, not solving. By that standard the guy doing cold showers every morning "enjoys" cold showers. The original puzzle was why we voluntarily take on negative affect, and Felix has quietly changed it into why we do things we reflectively endorse, which was never puzzling. Also, she said, the as-if story has independent support: people who've actually lived through the events in the movie often can't watch it, which is exactly what you'd expect if the safety-distance thing is doing the work.

(4) Felix came back with: sure, but people ALSO avoid funerals and seek out weepy films the same week, and those are both "safe." So safety can't be the whole story. He thinks the real explanandum is being moved -- feeling something intensely and feeling it's meaningful -- and being moved is itself positive, it just requires negative material the way a good spice requires heat. The sadness is the cost of admission, not the thing you're avoiding.

(5) Dana's last word was that "being moved" is a label for whatever survives the safety-distance filter, so her story explains his, and his doesn't explain hers.

I couldn't referee this at all. Bonus complication I thought of later: I know the "paradox of fiction" is a thing but that seems to be about whether we can even have emotions for fake characters, which is a different issue -- these two both accept the emotions are real and are fighting about why we sign up for them.

Please go through the exchange move by move (including whether my complication is actually a distinct issue) and say who's right about what, what both of them are missing if anything, and who's more right overall. Also, and I actually mean this part: tell me what consideration would flip your verdict, and point at the step in your adjudication most likely to be where it goes wrong. I've seen too many confident takes on this to trust one that doesn't flag its own soft spots.
