My friend is unusually consistent about the rationality of tiny individual contributions to huge collective outcomes, and I've been collecting her verdicts: you should vote in a national election even if you live in a state that will never be close; buying a lottery ticket is irrational; recycling your single bin is rational, and a neighbor who doesn't recycle is blameworthy; giving a small amount to a malaria charity is rational even though your donation alone will prevent at most a fraction of one death; serving on a jury in a tedious case is the rational thing to do; but she dismisses Pascal's wager as obviously irrational, and she says cheering while watching a match alone at home is harmless but not something reason asks of you. Next week we're going to argue about three new cases: voting for a third-party candidate with no chance of winning when she lives in a swing state and much prefers the lesser evil; giving five dollars to a stranger who claims his organization can avert a catastrophe affecting billions of people with a probability of one in a trillion; and walking five miles in freezing rain to vote in a low-stakes local referendum that the polls say will be decided by thousands. Predict her verdict on each of the three, identify the rules that fit her track record so far (I can see at least two), say what each rule predicts about the new cases, give your best guess with your reasons, and tell me what further case would best tell the candidate rules apart.
