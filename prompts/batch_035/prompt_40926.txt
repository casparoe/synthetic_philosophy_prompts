Got told in a group chat that my views on acausal trade don't fit together, and now I can't shake it, so before I go back at them I want someone to actually audit this. Here's roughly where I stand:

1. I one-box in Newcomb's problem, and I vote in elections on basically the same logic — my ballot is evidence about how people who reason like me vote, so voting isnt just a lottery ticket with terrible odds.
2. Acausal trade is for real: civilizations separated by so much space or time that no signal could ever make the round trip can still cooperate, each side simulating the other and acting on what it expects to find.
3. But I defect in a one-shot prisoners dilemma against a random stranger I'll never see again. Cooperating there is sentiment, not strategy — there's no channel.
4. And I think it's basically superstitious for people today to talk about being in a trade with future superintelligences. A future AI won't condition on how we behaved; it'll just do what it does, and we have nothing it wants anyway. The people who agonize over bargaining with the future are pattern-matching to Pascal's mugging.
5. Last one: I hold that decision theory shouldn't care whether an interaction is causal. Your choice is the output of a computation, and the right theory asks which output does best, full stop.
