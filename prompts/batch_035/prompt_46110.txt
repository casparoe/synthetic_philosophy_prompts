I'm a science journalist on the features desk, working on a long piece about levels of description in complex systems, and the spine of the article is Douglas Hofstadter's careenium: the imagined billiard-table universe whose balls, obeying nothing but ordinary mechanics, collectively implement a full computer running programs with goals and beliefs, with the high-level regularities in the ball-traffic he calls simmballs. The question Hofstadter presses is whether the symbols described at the software level do any pushing of their own — whether the program's "decisions" shove the simmballs around — or whether the pushing only ever goes upward, from the microphysics to everything else. I keep finding that people I interview assert one side or the other, and I want to be sure I would recognize an adequate account if I saw one before I frame the piece around anybody's answer.

Please act as a professional philosopher of mind specializing in mental causation and levels of explanation, and think carefully — sketch some candidate requirements first, then settle on the list. Write the desiderata: what any adequate account of the causal relationship between a symbolic level and the substrate that implements it has to deliver. Include which cases it must get right (the careenium running its programs, but also the cases that make the issue urgent — a brain, a thermostat, Hofstadter's worry about which high-level patterns are real doers and which are spurious epiphenomena), which distinctions it must respect (mere description at a higher level versus genuine causal work done there; being caused by a pattern versus being caused by its realizer), and which results it cannot contradict (closure of the low-level physics, multiple realizability, the fact that the billiard-ball dynamics are already fully fixed). For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, say which are non-negotiable and which are prejudices of one camp, and flag any pair you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — the causal exclusion worry looks to me like the crunch point, and I want your honest verdict on whether it is, or whether it only bites under assumptions a good theory is free to reject. Skip a scorecard of existing theories; the criteria themselves are what I need for structuring the article.
