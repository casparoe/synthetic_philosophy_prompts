i mostly write about AI and neuroscience for a general readership, but i'm working on a feature right now about the ethics of care — gilligan, tronto, nell noddings, all that — and one thing that keeps sticking with me is that care isn't a task you complete and check off, it's ongoing maintenance: the same small things done over and over, and the relationship's "success" is never a finished state you can point to. i want to close the piece with a poem, and the more i think about it the more i want it to be a pantoum, because the form seems built for exactly this: lines 2 and 4 of each stanza come back as lines 1 and 3 of the next, so everything recurs but recontextualized, and the final line circles back to the first. i want the form to do real work here — the repetition-with-a-difference should track how care is iterative and responsive rather than a problem solved once, and the return to the opening line at the end should feel like maintenance, not resolution.

for the setting/voice, i'm imagining an adult daughter looking after her mother whose memory is going: the daily round of did you eat, driving to appointments, the flashes of recognition followed by confusion, picking up the same prescriptions. somewhere in there i'd love tronto's point that the response of the person cared for is itself a moral element of care — care-receiving is part of the practice, not just a feedback signal about how well i did — but with zero jargon and no philosopher namechecked in the poem itself. it should just be right, such that someone who knows the literature would nod and someone who doesn't would still get a good poem. maybe 5 or 6 stanzas. and please don't let it turn saccharine — the exhaustion and occasional resentment of caregiving should be in there too, since the care ethicists themselves insist ambivalence is part of the picture, not a personal failing that disqualifies the care as real.

one specific thing i want if you can pull it off: one of the recurring lines should shift its meaning across its appearances — same words, different sense depending on what's come before it. thats basically my whole thesis about why the pantoum is the right form for this, so if the poem can enact that in at least one place i'll be thrilled.
