so I'm putting together my position statement for a phil of mind reading group and I want a sanity check before the others tear into it. Here's roughly where I stand:

1. I'm a physicalist about structure and function: everything the brain does can in principle be explained physically, and physics is causally closed — no physical event ever needs a nonphysical cause to complete its explanation.
2. But I'm not a physicalist about consciousness. Qualia — the felt redness of red, the awfulness of pain — are real, nonphysical, and not reducable to or identical with brain states.
3. So I've landed on epiphenomenalism: brain processes cause qualia, and that's the whole causal story. Qualia hang off the physical like the whistle of a train — produced by it, but never pushing anything back. (this is also exactly why I can keep holding 1.)
4. When I say "I'm seeing red right now," I say it because I'm seeing red. My introspective reports are caused by my experiences — that's what makes them reports about my experiences, and it's why anyone should take them as evidence about my consciousness rather than random noise.
5. I'm a reliabilist about knowledge in general: a belief only counts as knowledge if it's reliably produced by the fact it's about. that's why perception gives us knowledge — the table causally shapes the light hitting my retina, the signals up the optic nerve, etc.
6. Mary's room works imo. when she steps out of the black and white room and sees red for the first time, she learns a genuinely new fact, and it's a fact about qualia, not about physics.
7. On ethics, the reason torture is wrong is the phenomenal badness of pain — the hurt is what makes it wrong, not the c-fiber firing per se. And the same thing explains why I personally avoid stepping on nails: I don't do it because it would hurt.
8. If it matters, I also think zombies are conceivable and metaphysically possible.
