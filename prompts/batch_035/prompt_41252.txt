I'm a philosophy of mind PhD student and my department wants to try running little adversarial collaborations instead of the usual conference Q&A bloodsport. First pairing: someone defending something in the neighbourhood of Norman Malcolm's view that dreams are not experiences at all (dream reports aren't reports of anything -- they're, roughly, waking utterances the sleeper is disposed to make, and the whole idea of a stream of dream-experience is a confused picture) against someone in Dennett's camp (dreams are real enough but, per his "uploading upon waking" model, there needn't be any experience happening during sleep at all -- memory inserts itself at wake-up).

Write the joint statement they could both sign. I want: a numbered section of claims both accept (about sleep physiology, about what dream reports are evidence of, about which popular pictures of dreaming both think are wrong); the exact residual disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording; the cruxes -- I suspect one is whether Malcolm's verificationist argument retains any force once Dennett has given up on during-sleep experience too, and another is how to individuate "an experience" such that waking confabulation doesn't count but dreams might; and for each crux a concrete case or experiment both would agree bears on it (lucid dream signalling, pre-sleep learning, lesion studies -- whatever actually works), with each side's prediction written down in advance. If some of the dispute turns out to be verbal (e.g. both disown the "cinema in the head" picture and are only fighting over the word "experience"), please say which part and why.

Keep it tight -- max 600 words, and please use British English throughout. Format as numbered agreed claims followed by a disagreements table. Its fine to flag where you think one side just has the better of it, but the document itself should be signable by both.
