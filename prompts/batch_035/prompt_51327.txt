I'm prepping for a seminar on Hume's "Of the Standard of Taste" and there's a bit in the opening pages that always trips up my students, so I want to make sure I explain it right. Hume starts by noting that in science, disagreements usually dissolve once you define your terms, but in matters of taste the disagreements get *worse* the more you get into particulars. Then, oddly, he brings in morals, which seems off-topic for an essay about beauty and art criticism. He compares how Homer and Fénelon portray Ulysses and Telemachus:

"Homer's general precepts, where he delivers any such, will never be controverted; but it is obvious, that, when he draws particular pictures of manners, and represents heroism in Achilles and prudence in Ulysses, he intermixes a much greater degree of ferocity in the former, and of cunning and fraud in the latter, than Fenelon would admit of. The sage Ulysses in the Greek poet seems to delight in lies and fictions, and often employs them without any necessity or even advantage: But his more scrupulous son, in the French epic writer, exposes himself to the most imminent perils, rather than depart from the most exact line of truth and veracity."

And a bit later he says that whoever invented the word "charity" and used it in a good sense taught "be charitable" more efficaciously than any prophet who inserted such a maxim into his writings.

My question: what is this digression about moral language actually doing for his arguement about taste? Is it just an analogy (moral praise words like "virtue" work like aesthetic praise words like "elegance," so apparent moral unanimity is verbal, just as apparent agreement about good writing is verbal), or is he claiming something stronger — that when we get down to concrete cases, moral judgments diverge the way taste judgments do, which would be a pretty radical thing for a sentimentalist to concede? I can see it both ways and want to settle on a reading before class. Also, if you have any thoughts on why he singles out the Homer/Fénelon contrast rather than just sticking with the aesthetic examples, I'd be glad to hear them.
