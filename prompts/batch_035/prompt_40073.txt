My verdicts on which reference class to use when guessing probabilities, and I want to know if there's a principle behind them, because right now it feels like I just pick whichever class gives the answer I like.

- When my doctor estimates my ten-year cardiovascular risk, she plugs me into a big table of men my age with my cholesterol and blood pressure. That seems right, even though nobody in that class shares my diet or family history in any detail.
- When my friend asked whether her specialty bakery would survive its first year, I rejected "small food businesses" (most fail) as irrelevant, because she had a decade of loyal customers from her market stall and a lease locked in below market rate. The narrow, information-rich judgment seemed obviously better there.
- But when a founder tells me "companies with AI in the name get huge valuations, so will we," I don't trust that narrow class at all. The feature defining the class seems to be doing no work.
- Asked whether a stranger's marriage will last, I'd happily use "first marriages in their country and education bracket," and I'd refuse to refine it with "marriages where both partners' names start with the same letter" — but I'd also refuse to refine it with "marriages where one partner's parents divorced," even though that one is actually predictive.

The case I keep going back and fourth on: my chance of being in a car accident this year. "Drivers my age with a clean record" says very unlikely. "Drivers on my exact commute, at my hours, in my weather" contains far more information about the actual risk and says somewhat less unlikely — and I can't even decide which of those is the right kind of thing to average over.

So: is there one principle that gets all of these — maybe something about whether the property defining the class is causally connected to the outcome, or about how much the class has been tailored to the individual? If it can't save every verdict, tell me which one to drop and why. And tell me what the principle predicts for a case I haven't mentioned: my friend's kid, a strong student at an average high school, asking about their odds of getting into an Ivy — do I use "applicants with that GPA," or "strong students at average schools"?

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then the reasoning. No preamble and no restating my question.
