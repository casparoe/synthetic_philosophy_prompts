There's an argument I've been stuck in for like a year, in a discord server of mine, about whether panpsychism has any ethical consequences, and I've started to suspect it's not really an argument about ethics at all. Quick background: I'm a software engineer, no philosophy training, so tell me if I'm framing this badly. The two sides: me and one other guy hold that if experience is woven into matter at the bottom level, then even very simple systems carry some nonzero moral weight — so things like running a simulation that has anything pain-like in it, or even slamming doors, could involve a tiny amount of moral badness, and we should at least be uncertain instead of defaulting to "no minds, no worries." The other side says the combination problem destroys this: if panpsychists can't even explain how micro-experiences add up to a human mind, then we have no idea how to compare the moral significance of a rock (or a thermostat, or a video game NPC) against a person, and when you have no idea how to weigh something, the rational move is to act as if it has no ethical implications until the metaphysics gets sorted. Also they think our side smuggles in an assumption: that bare presence of experience matters morally, when maybe only the intensity or structure of experience does — and on that view a proton's "experience", even if real, is so thin it can't be the sort of thing that suffers. We go around in circles every few weeks and nobody has budged. What I want from you is a diagnosis, not a verdict on who's right. Is this a genuine moral disagreement, a verbal one about the word 'suffering', two groups answering different questions under one heading (one about what could in principle matter vs what we're justified in acting on now), or is it really a hidden bet about how the combination problem will turn out? And given whatever diagnosis you land on, tell me honestly whether anything would count as progress here — a conceptual distinction, a better theory of combination, some kind of priority ranking of experiences — or whether this is just permanently stuck because it bottoms out in stuff nobody can know.
