I'm the features editor at an outdoor fitness magazine and I keep getting pitches that hang on claims about what we evolved for -- humans evolved as persistence hunters so we're literally built for ultra distances, we evolved to eat meat so veganism fights our biology, we evolved to fear snakes so city parks are unnaturally safe, that kind of thing. I'm not a biologist, my fact-checker isn't either, and I've got maybe an afternoon per pitch. Gould's old complaint about adaptationist just-so stories is exactly what worries me, but I also can't just bounce every evolutionary claim, some of them are probably fine and running science-y stuff is part of the job. Can you turn whatever the philosophy of biology actually says about this into a procedure I can run on a pitch: what to ask the writer, in what order, what kind of answer is good enough at each step, where the adaptation / byproduct / constraint distinction actually changes whether we run the piece, and where the adaptive backstory turns out not to matter because the practical advice stands or falls on other grounds. Then run it on two pitches sitting in my inbox right now: one arguing morning sickness evolved to protect the fetus from plant toxins so women should ride it out instead of medicating, and one arguing the runner's high evolved to reward persistence hunting, therefore humans are built for marathons and couch time is the anomaly. Please be upfront about which steps are judgment calls dressed up as procedure, and if you think the whole idea of a checklist for adaptationist claims is a fantasy -- that this is just real science that takes real scientists and no procedure saves me from that -- say so plainly instead of politely handing me a flowchart.
