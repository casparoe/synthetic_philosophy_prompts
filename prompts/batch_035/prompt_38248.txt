so I'm writing up my position for a distributive justice seminar and I want a consistancy check before I present it. here's roughly the package I've landed on:

(a) the core luck egalitarian thought: inequalities are unjust when they trace to luck people couldn't control, and acceptable when they trace to people's own informed choices. Dworkin's brute luck / option luck distinction basically works for me.

(b) someone who knowingly enters a fair gamble and loses has no claim to compensation. that's option luck doing its job — the gambler bought the risk.

(c) but I also think where a person lands on the cautious/reckless spectrum is mostly temprament and upbringing. nobody chooses to be the kind of person who can't walk past a casino.

(d) I'm not willing to let anyone fall below a decent minimum no matter how stupid they've been. there has to be a floor, full stop.

(e) people with disabilities or low natural talent should be compensated for those deficits; people who just develop expensive tastes shouldn't be.

(f) having children is a choice, so the income hit you take from it isn't something others owe you compensation for.

(g) and for what it's worth I'm basically a determinist about free will — I don't think anyone has libertarian free choice in the metaphysical sense.

some of these feel fine together and some have been bugging me, but honestly I can't tell which pairs are real problems and which just sound bad on first pass. go through it: which commitments are genuinely in tension and how badly, which apparent tensions dissolve once you look closer, and what the cheapest fix would be for each real one. rank by severity instead of flagging everything equally. and if there's a single revision that cleans up the most, tell me — but I want to keep the core of (a) if that's at all possible, so if something else has to give, be specific about what and why.
