I help run a film-and-philosophy discussion group — mostly grad students and autodidacts, plus one neuroscientist who enjoys stirring the pot — and next month we're screening Pixar's Inside Out (2015) as the finale of a unit on consciousness. I'd like to structure the discussion around Daniel Dennett's critique of the Cartesian theater and his multiple drafts model from Consciousness Explained, and I want you to respond as a philosopher of mind who takes both the film and the theory seriously rather than using the film as a straw man for the theory or vice versa. Please think the reading through carefully before committing to a line; if it helps, brainstorm the interpretive options first and then argue for the one you find strongest.

On its face the film looks like a sitting duck: Headquarters is literally a control room where personified emotions sit before a screen, watch the world, and press buttons on a console to steer Riley, while memories arrive as glowing orbs and the train of thought is an actual train. But the more I sit with it, the less simple it seems. Dennett himself is fond of decomposing the mind into progressively stupider subpersonal agents — homuncular functionalism — and the emotions might be read as exactly such agents, except that Joy is far too clever for that, with plans, preferences, and what looks like a self-model. And the ending, where Sadness takes over the console and produces a mixed joy-sadness core memory while Riley's personality islands collapse and rebuild, seems almost designed to illustrate Dennett's idea of the self as a center of narrative gravity that gets rewritten rather than a spectator who watches. So my worry is that the reading cuts both ways: the film stages the very theater Dennett attacks while quietly agreeing with him about almost everything else, and I don't want our discussion to collapse into a lazy "Dennett refutes the movie" or "the movie refutes Dennett."

What I'd like from you: a reading of the film through the Cartesian theater and multiple drafts debate that (a) says where the film genuinely endorses a theater, and whether that matters given Dennett's own comfort with control-room imagery under the right constraints about the competence of the homunculi involved; (b) identifies where it instead dramatizes multiple drafts or the narrative self — the mixed core memory, the rebuilt islands, possibly Bing Bong's fading as a kind of memory-editing; and (c) names the places where it resists the Dennettian reading in ways the theory has to answer for — for instance, when Joy and Sadness are locked out of Headquarters, Riley goes emotionally blank, which suggests the film really does think everything comes together in one place. Point me to the specific scenes we should watch closely and say what each one is doing; flag honestly the moments that fit neither camp; and tell me what the film adds to the philosophical debate that argument alone doesn't — for example, whether it makes the theater intuition so vivid and charming that it reveals why people believe it in the first place. No reading list, please: the members already have their Dennett summaries; what I need is the interpretation itself, in enough detail that I can build an evening's discussion around it.
