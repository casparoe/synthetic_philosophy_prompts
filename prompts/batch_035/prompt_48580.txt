A few months back I had a bit of a burnout scare, and since then I've gotten seriouly into meditation, and now I can't work out whether I actually believe something my meditation teacher keeps hinting at: that every "positive" feeling is really just the temporary absence of some craving or discomfort, so the whole project of pursuing pleasure is confused once you see it clearly. I want to know if I believe that or just like the sound of it.

I run a small blog for skeptics-turned-meditators, and I'd like a "which view of pleasure do you actually hold?" quiz. Six to eight questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options phrased as descriptions of what the person feels — no philosophy jargon, so nobody can answer by knowing the labels. Use cases like: eating when hungry vs. eating a great dessert when already full; watching a sunset you weren't craving beforehand; the calm of a settled mind vs. the buzz of excitement; whether you'd take a pill that erases all craving without adding anything else; and a couple more along those lines that you think do real sorting work.

Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto positions: strict tranquilism (only the absence of craving/suffering has value), relief-reductionism (positive experiences do reduce to craving-relief, but relief itself is a genuine good), asymmetric views (suffering counts for more, but pleasure is real), and plain symmetrical hedonism. If some cell in that space is unoccupied, say so and guess whether that's because it's incoherent or just unexplored. And flag honestly which pairs of leaves my cases can't actually separate — I suspect the sunset one does less work than people assume. Keep it readable for people who've never opened a philosophy paper.
