I'm a novelist, and my editor and I have been fighting for three weeks about a decision my protagonist makes in chapter 12, and I've realized it's secretly a philosophy question that I need a number for, not vibes.

The setup: my protagonist, 22, in a country sliding into a long famine, gets pregnant on purpose now instead of waiting two years for things to stabalize. She knows waiting means a healthier kid, safer pregnancy, actual schools. She also knows — the book is explicit about this — that the kid she'd have two years later would not be the same kid. Different gametes, different person entirely. Her actual daughter, the one the whole novel orbits, only exists because she didn't wait. The daughter's life will be worth living, just clearly worse than the other kid's would've been.

My editor says obviously not wrong: nobody is worse off than they otherwise would be, the daughter can't point at a better life she was robbed of, since in the world where my protagonist waits she doesn't exist at all. I keep feeling that's too quick but can't articulate why. A friend told me this is called the nonidentity problem or something — I don't need the literature, I need your credence. I'm at maybe 0.55 on "it was wrong for her to conceive then rather than wait"; my editor's at maybe 0.15.

Give me your own number, and more importantly decompose it: how much rides on whether the wrong has to be somebody's wrong (person-affecting vs whatever the alternative is), whether a life worth living sets a floor that changes the moral math, whether the daughter's later endorsement — "I'm glad you didn't wait, I don't want some other kid to have had the good life instead of me" — dissolves the wrong or is beside the point, and any other terms you think belong. One line of justification each, then the combination. Then tell me which term is doing the most work in splitting me from my editor, and what would actually move you on it.

One wrinkle the editor keeps forgetting: if waiting two years changes which kid exists, it presumably changes everything downstream too — every grandkid, the whole future population becomes different people. If that's right, does it make the choice more wrong (more lives affected) or does it spread the wrongness so thin it dissolves? Fold that in as its own term if you think it deserves one.
