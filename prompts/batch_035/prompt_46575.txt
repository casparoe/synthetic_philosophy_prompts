I've been teaching the knowledge argument in my philosophy of mind seminar off an on for maybe fifteen years, and somewhere along the way I noticed the debate has quietly stopped being a debate and turned into two adjacent literatures. On one side you've got the physicalists, who by now mostly rally around the phenomenal concept strategy — the idea that when Mary leaves the black-and-white room she gains a new concept or mode of presentation of a fact she already knew under a physical description. On the other you've got the people who insist what she gains is knowledge by acquaintance, or the apprehension of a property she'd never instantiated, and honestly a lot of these folks barely cite the concept-strategy people and vice versa. And Jackson himself bailed on the argument decades ago, which you'd think would settled something, but neither side seems to treat that as data about anything except Jackson. So what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a survey of positions — I can get that anywhere. Is this a substantive disagreement about qualia, has it fragmented into something verbal about the words "knowledge" and "gains," or are the two camps answering different questions — one about whether physical knowledge is closed under deduction, the other about what concepts of experience are like — under the cover of a single thought experiment? My own hutch is that the ability hypothesis lost on points but won the war: nobody defends the pure Lewis-Nemirow line anymore, and yet every later physicalist reply is in some sense an apology for why they were pointing at the right spot. Tell me if that's right, tell me what's actually load-bearing in the disagreement as it stands today, and give me an honest answer about whether anything — a mature theory of phenomenal concepts, some neuropsychological discovery, a better taxonomy of what Mary lacks before release — could actually end it, or whether the thought experiment is just indefinitely refinable in a way disputes like this always are. Answer in American English, please — these go in my teaching notes and I want the spelling consistent.
