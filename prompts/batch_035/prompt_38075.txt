My oldest friend stayed with us last weekend and we got into a running argument about his job. He works for a guy who can fire anyone at any time for any reason, monitors everyone's email, and has a volatile temper — but in six years has never actually stopped my friend from doing a single thing he wanted to do. My friend's line all weekend was that he's as free as anyone he knows: nobody's ever interfered with him, so where's the unfreedom? His wife agreed and honestly they mostly teamed up on me. The one crack in his position, I thought, was when he admitted he never says anything even mildly critical on the company chat "just in case" — but when I pressed on that he said that's just being sensible, not being unfree. I teach political philosophy for a living so you'd think I'd have won this at the dinner table, but I kept reaching for jargon and losing them. Was I even right that he's less free than he thinks — and if so, how would you actually make the case to two smart people who've never read a word of this stuff and think freedom just means nobody stopping you? Also, be honest about whether his "just being sensible" point is a real objection or a concession.
