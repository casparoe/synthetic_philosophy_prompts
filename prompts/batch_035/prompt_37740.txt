For a seminar presentation I've been asked to write up my position on the Lucas–Penrose argument, and before I do I want a consistency check on it, because I've arrived at it piecemeal and I can't tell anymore whether it's one position or several taped together. Here is the set of commitments I'd be defending:

(1) I'm a mechanist about mathematical cognition: whatever a working mathematician does when they grasp that some statement is true could, in principle, be replicated by a machine; I see no room for non-physical mathematical insight. (2) I think the Lucas–Penrose argument fails, and I locate the failure in its central premise: that a human can always see the truth of the Gödel sentence of whatever formal system they themselves are. (3) My diagnosis is the standard one: if I am a system F, I cannot establish that F is sound, so I cannot see F's Gödel sentence as true — I can do that only for systems other than me. This covers Penrose's patched version too, the one that restricts attention to systems we can know to be sound. (4) Separately, though, I doubt there is any determinate fact about which formal system a given person is; the mapping from a human to a Turing machine looks hopelessly interest-relative, since any finite stretch of someone's mathematical behavior is compatible with indefinitely many incompatible machine descriptions. (5) I hold that arithmetical truth is objective and determinate, and that our mathematical beliefs are by and large reliable. (6) And I accept reflection as obviously valid in each particular case: for any system I can specify and take to be sound — ordinary set theory, set theory plus large cardinals, whatever — I can see that its Gödel sentence is true, and iterating this never seems to bottom out in anything mysterious.

What I want is an audit, not a survey of the literature: which pairs of these genuinely pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what the cheapest repair would cost. I have suspicions about where the worst problem is, so don't just confirm them — rank the tensions by severity, tell me which single revision would clean up the most, and say explicitly whether any tension is merely apparent and dissolves on inspection. Please also say how confident you are in each verdict, and single out the point in your own audit where you'd least trust yourself.
