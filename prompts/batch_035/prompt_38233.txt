I've recently taken over as editor of my university magazine's arts section, and our first big interview is with a philosopher who defends what she calls aesthetic testimony pessimism: on her view, testimony about artworks can justify aesthetic beliefs but can never transmit aesthetic knowledge, because genuinely knowing that a film is moving or a painting graceful requires firsthand experience of the work. Compose eight to ten interview questions for me. Include at least one question each on (i) why aesthetic testimony should differ in kind from ordinary empirical testimony, where we happily defer to historians and meteorologists; (ii) whether the knowledge/justification distinction really does any work, given that she concedes a reader can be fully justified in believing a poem is wrenching without reading it; and (iii) the deferential judge — someone who sincerely praises a novel purely on a trusted critic's word, and seems to get the belief right while somehow missing the point. For each question, anticipate her most likely reply and attach a one-line follow-up keyed to it. Please keep the full list under 400 words.
