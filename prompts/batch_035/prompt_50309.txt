I chair the technical committee drafting a national standard for auditing automated decision systems, and we have a problem upstream of every specific metric: we argue about whether an audit should demand demographic parity or error-rate balance before anyone has written down what a fairness criterion has to achieve for the audit to be worth anything. So write me the desiderata — the requirements any adequate criterion of algorithmic fairness must meet — with the concrete case motivating each. At minimum, the list has to confront: differing base rates of the outcome across groups, and what follows from the impossibility results showing that calibration within groups and balanced error rates across groups cannot hold together unless base rates are equal or predictions are perfect; proxies for protected attributes, since a criterion satisfied by blinding a model to race can still be violated through a zip code; the difference between judging a score and judging a threshold imposed on it; subgroups beyond those named in the statute, since a classifier can look fair on every audited group and fail badly on a group no one listed; ranking and top-k selection, where criteria formulated for binary decisions may not transfer; and the question of which worldly disparities the criterion is supposed to correct rather than merely avoid amplifying. For each requirement, say whether it is non-negotiable or a preference of a particular camp, and flag which requirements you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — that trade-off structure is the part I most need, because if the impossibility results force a choice, the choice effectively is the standard. Then, briefly, run demographic parity, equalized odds, calibration-based criteria, and counterfactual criteria against the list; I care far more about the criteria than the scorecard. Lead with the bottom line: your first sentence should name the requirement you would refuse to trade away and identify the subset of desiderata that is jointly unsatisfiable, and only then give the reasoning. No preamble, and do not restate my question back to me.
