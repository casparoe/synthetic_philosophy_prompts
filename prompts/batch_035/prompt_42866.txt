Quick question about the Epicurus death argument. As I understand it, the whole thing turns on there being no subject left once you're dead -- no one around for the harm to happen to, so death can't be bad for you. Like he's not actually answering the argument, he's answered a different question about life being good rather than death being bad. Plus the deprivation account has this weird feature where the badness floats free of any particular time -- nobody is worse off at any moment, past, present or future, yet somehow the death is still bad for you. So basically: given that the deprivation crowd can never point to a moment where you're actually suffering the harm, why is the deprivation account treated as the standard reply to Epicurus instead of as a concession to him? Also, is the Lucretius symmetry point (we don't fear the endless time before our birth, so we shouldn't fear the endless time after death) genuinely a seperate argument, or is it just the same no-subject point restated in fancier clothes?
