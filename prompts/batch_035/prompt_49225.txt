In reinforcement learning, an agent is trained by receiving rewards for good behavior and penalties for bad behavior, and a reward is by definition something the agent experiences as good — otherwise it couldn't reinforce anything. It seems to follow that any RL agent, however simple, already has states with valence: the reward signal just is a primitive pleasure and the penalty signal a primitive pain. If that's right, the interesting question isn't whether RL agents can suffer but how their negative-reward states compare to pain in animals — so that machine welfare assessments could proceed straightforwardly by measuring reward dynamics, e.g., cumulative negative reward within an episode, how heavily it's discounted, and how long the trained policy would go on avoiding it. What would be the best quantitative framework for making such a comparison, and how should we weigh suffering that is intense but heavily discounted or confined to a short episode?
