Anyway what bugs me is that I can't tell if "truth is many-sided" is doing real work in our arguments or just ending them. And if it's the platitude reading, it doesn't follow that my roommate's take on the city is *right*, just incomplete, which feels like a sneaky downgrade from "I'm correct" to "we're both partially correct" that always lands in her favor.

Can you lay out the different precise claims "truth is many-sided" could actually be making, from the totally trivial to the genuinely radical -- maybe including what that sevenfold syādvāda predication would have to mean for it not to be incoherent -- and for each reading say whether it's plausible and whether it would actually support what my roommate uses it for, which is basically "stop arguing, we're both right from our own angle"? I've also heard that the Jains had this whole theory of standpoints, nayas I think she called them, where each partial viewpoint is a legitimate way of looking at things but only valid as long as you don't mistake it for the whole story -- if that's right maybe fold it in as one of the readings. And there's an obvious worry about self-refutation here (is "reality is many-sided" itself only one side of reality?) that I'd like you to address under whichever readings it actually bites. Make it a list I can actually use, and tell me honestly if any of these readings would let us settle the argument about the city instead of just dissolving it.
