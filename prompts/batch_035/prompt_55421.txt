ok so context: i'm in a rational choice theory course this semester and I somehow volunteered to make a "which decision theory do you actually hold?" quiz for the course blog. audience is other phil students — they've heard of decision theory but most havent taken more than intro logic, and a few of them are the type who'll say "im an evidentialist" because it sounds cool. so the whole point of the quiz has to be sorting people by what they DO in cases, not what they call themselves. no question is allowed to name any theory, and answers should be phrased as first-person actions ("i take both boxes"), not as positions.

can you write me 9 questions, each a concrete scenario with 2-3 answer options, plus a scoring key at the end? these cases have to show up somewhere in the quiz:

- plain newcomb's problem, one box vs two.
- transparent newcomb, where you can see box B is full before you choose.
- counterfactual mugging: a predictor flipped a fair coin before you were involved. if it had come up heads, it would've put $1,000,000 in your account iff it predicted you'd pay $100 on tails. it came up tails, and now you're asked for the $100. pay or keep it?
- the letter case: you receive a letter saying "i predicted whether you'd pay me $1,000. i sent this letter iff (you have a painful-but-not-dangerous medical gene) XOR (i predicted you'd pay)." paying obviously can't change whether you have the gene. pay or throw it out?

plus one case i made up myself and want folded in — tell me if it actually works or if its broken: call it the loyalty club. a supermarket's predictor decided last year whether to slip you a $200 gift card, based on whether it predicted you'd join their $60/year club this month. the gift card is already in your wallet. the club has nothing you'd ever buy. do you join? (im not sure whether to make the predictor infallible or only right 90% of the time — the fallible version feels more realistic but i cant tell if that ruins what the case is supposed to show, and i'd honestly like your take on that before locking it in.)

for the remaining 3 or so questions youre free to invent cases, but at least one shouldnt be a prediction scenario at all — i want something that catches people whose answers are just vibes, like whether they'd precommit differently for an agent they were designing to act on their behalf vs for themselves.

the scoring key should map answer patterns onto causal decision theory, evidential decision theory, and functionalist/updateless theories, plus a "two-leveler" leaf for people who act on the good policy when its not yet sunk but excuse themselves after, and an "unprincipled vibes" leaf. flag which leaves the quiz genuinely cant seperate — e.g. people who one-box on everything INCLUDING paying the letter (evidential pattern) vs people who one-box, pay the mugging, AND ignore the letter (updateless pattern), and wherever transparent newcomb fails to split one-boxers at all.

keep each scenario under ~100 words so its doable in one sitting, and put the invented loyalty club question somewhere in the middle rather than first. thx!
