I'm a senior data engineer at an insurance company, and I've just been handed the master-data-management problem created by our merger: two legacy customer databases, decades of sloppy entry, and my job is to decide, row by row, which records refer to the same real-world customer. A colleague with a philosophy degree told me I've wandered into a live debate — the identity of indiscernibles, Max Black's two-sphere universe, and the haecceitist replies — and I want the translation done properly in my own vocabulary, because I suspect the fit is instructive in both directions. To calibrate: I know SQL cold, I understand candidate and primary keys, functional dependencies, normalization, and surrogate versus natural keys, and I've read enough relational theory to know the strict set-theoretic model forbids duplicate tuples while SQL's bag semantics permits them. My philosophy is a first-year course, twenty years ago.

Restate, as faithfully as possible, in my terms: (1) the identity of indiscernibles, and how it differs from its uncontroversial converse; (2) Black's symmetric universe and what it is supposed to show; (3) the haecceitist response that each sphere has its own thisness. Draw on whatever actually helps from the following, and tell me if I've misdescribed any of it: surrogate keys as stipulated identity that ignores recorded attributes; the everyday practice of assigning them precisely because attributes can't be trusted to individuate; the fact that any schema is a finite projection, so two rows agreeing on every column is far from two entities agreeing on every property; SQL's duplicate rows, distinguishable if at all only by physical position rather than content; and symmetric database instances — whether a row-swap that preserves all values is best described as two rows or one row described twice.

Then the residue report: what survives the translation cleanly, what survives only distorted, and what can't be said at all. And give a straight verdict on two suspicions. My colleague's: that the set-semantics versus bag-semantics split is the Leibniz-versus-haecceitism dispute in miniature, with the relational model's ban on duplicate tuples amounting to an identity of indiscernibles built into the formalism. Mine: that database practice is neutral on the metaphysics, because the surrogate-key habit is epistemic — a confession that our projections are incomplete — rather than a claim that identity outruns all qualitative facts; the engineer who insists two identical rows might still be two customers is, I think, pointing at missing columns, not at thisness. Where does the residue sit — is there something in the philosophers' debate my vocabulary genuinely cannot express, or does the debate look less clear once forced into schema terms? Don't soften the verdict to spare either of us. No citations needed; positions and arguments by name are enough, and I'll chase the literature myself.
