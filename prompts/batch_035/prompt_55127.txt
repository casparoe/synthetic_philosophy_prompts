I've been reading about Gettier cases and something bugs me. There seem to be two different kinds of case lumped together under that label. The luck is sort of inside the reasoning: they got to the truth through a falsehood. But in the other kind — the fake barn county sort of case — the person's reasoning is completely fine, no false premise anywhere. They look at a real barn and form the belief that there's a barn. The luck is all in the environment: the area is full of barn facades, and they could just as easily have looked at one of those instead and formed the same belief falsely.

Is there an established name for this distinction in the literature, or do philosophers just treat both as "Gettier cases" and not worry about it?
