Draft the joint statement that a reductionist physicalist and a defender of robust emergence could both sign at the end of a two-year adversarial collaboration. The reductionist holds that the special sciences are heuristic compressions of microphysical fact: chemistry, biology, economics, and psychology earn their keep practically but add nothing to the world's ontology. The emergentist holds that some higher-level properties are genuine features of the world — autonomous in their laws and in their causal work — and cannot be recovered in microphysical vocabulary even in principle. They have spent the collaboration fighting over water and H2O, multiple realizability, Kim's causal exclusion argument, phase transitions, and natural selection; the statement should read as the residue of those fights, not as a neutral encyclopedia entry.

The statement must contain, in this order:

1. **Agreed Claims** — a numbered list, roughly six to nine claims, each phrased so that both parties would sign it. These should include the points both sides in this literature actually concede to each other: that classical Nagel-style intertheoretic deduction nowhere succeeded as a matter of the history of science; that multiple realizability, whatever it ultimately shows, is a real pattern; that phase transitions exhibit nonanalytic behavior strictly only in the thermodynamic limit, so that the familiar claim "ice melts at zero degrees" is about an idealized system; and that the renormalization group explains why wildly different micro-physics (a fluid near its critical point, a magnet near its Curie point) can share macro-structure. Write these so they are neither vacuous ("science is complicated") nor slanted toward either party's stronger view.

2. **Disagreements** — a table with three columns: the issue, the reductionist's position, and the emergentist's position. Each entry must be worded so that the party named would accept the phrasing of its own row. At minimum cover: whether the thermodynamic limit is a mere mathematical convenience or does constitutive work; whether higher-level causes are real or pre-empted by their microphysical realizers; whether "emergent" names an objective feature of systems or our current ignorance; and whether the renormalization group is physics absorbing the special sciences or physics discovering autonomy from the bottom up.

3. **Cruxes** — the three or four subquestions such that settling one would move at least one party, and for each, a concrete case, simulation, or argument both have agreed in advance would bear on it, with each party's prediction registered before the fact. For instance, both should agree on what a decisive case of non-multiply-realizable higher-level kinds, or a worked microphysical derivation of a specific biological generalization, would show — and disagree only about how likely such a case is to materialize.

4. **Verbal or Substantive** — a short closing section identifying which of the disagreements in the table are substantive and which reduce to different uses of "cause," "law," or "real," with reasons.

Both parties care about precision more than detente: do not soften any disagreement to make the signing easier.
