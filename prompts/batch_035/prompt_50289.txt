My sister has been in a minimally conscious state since an anoxic injury eight months ago, and her team keeps deferring to the family on questions like whether to escalate pain treatment when she grimaces at procedures. Reading to prepare for those decisions, I keep meeting the claim that what a patient feels turns on whether a higher-order theory of consciousness is true, since such theories tie felt pain to a capacity for states about one's own states. That apparently makes them predict that patients with certain prefrontal damage feel nothing, and that patients who can covertly follow commands in imaging are conscious without any outward sign, while first-order theories split the same cases the opposite way. Please rank the open questions in this area by how much settling each would change what families like mine should infer and do at the bedside: for instance, the problem of what makes a given first-order state the one the higher-order state is about, the dispute over whether the relevant second-order state is a thought or something attention-like, and the challenge posed by paradigms that measure awareness without asking for reports. I want a definite ranking with reasons, plus a frank note on which clinical implications of higher-order theories are commonly overstated and don't really follow, and please write it as continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
