I'm a retired geologist who spent half his career making charts for government reports, so when I finally got around to philosophy in retirement and found Otto Neurath — the Vienna Circle guy behind Isotype, who wanted to replace walls of raw numbers in statistics yearbooks with rows of little identical figures, one figure standing for so many thousands of people or tons of coal — I felt like he'd been reading over my shoulder the whole time. Write me a poem about that picture-language project of his. The constraint: the poem should itself work the way his charts did, where quantity is always shown by repeating identical small units, never by drawing one big unit — so build it out of repeated identical lines or modules, and let the count carry part of the meaning. Then give it a turn near the end, where the repetition breaks down over something that refuses to be counted in units, and let that moment carry his other, less advertised insight: that pictures arent some magic universal language that takes us back before Babel, theyre conventions you have to learn like everything else (same spirit as his sailors rebuilding the ship plank by plank at sea, with no drydock of perfect foundations to return to). Maybe 14 to 20 lines, no fixed rhyme scheme required. Someone who knows Neurath should recognize both the method and the point; someone whos never heard of him should still get a good poem out of it. And please write it in American English.
