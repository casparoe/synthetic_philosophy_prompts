My phil language course is ending and I'm left confused about something meta. The internalism/externalism fight over meaning has now been running for like fifty years — Twin Earth, the arthritis thought experiment, slow switching, all of it — and both sides have heard every move the other makes and nobody converts. My externalist prof thinks internalists are just in denial about the intuitions, and the internalist-leaning papers we read basically concede the cases and then shrug and keep using narrow content anyway. So my question isn't who's right. It's what KIND of disagreement is this actually?

Here's my working hypothesis, feel free to shoot it down: the two camps are answering different questions under one word. The externalist is doing metasemantics — what fixes what my word 'water' refers to, and the answer (the environment, the causal history) seems obviously right. The internalist is doing something like psychology — what's in my head that lets me mean and reason, and narrow content seems obviously right for THAT, since Oscar on Earth and Twin Oscar on Twin Earth are psychologically identical and it would be weird if their differing environments made them have different thoughts before either has interacted with it. On this reading the "debate" only exists because 'content', 'meaning' and 'thought' get used across both projects, and the stall is a sign that the dispute is verbal, not that it's hard.

But I'm not fully convinced by my own story, for two reasons. First, the disagreement doesn't feel verbal when it gets to the self-knowledge stuff — the slow switching cases and the worry that externalism makes it mysterious how I can know a priori what I'm thinking. That looks like a real clash: either my environment can change what I mean without any change I can detect, or it can't, and both answers seem to have costs. Second, the internalists don't just want narrow content as a scientific posit, some of them clearly think Twin Earth shows nothing about meaning at all, which is a substantive denial, not a different assignment of the term.

So: diagnose the structure of the stall. Is it verbal, or two research programs talking past each other, or a genuine substantive disagreement hiding somewhere specific (and if so, where exactly does the hidden question bite)? Use what the sides actually say to support whatever diagnosis you give. And then tell me what would even count as progress here — a new distinction, some empirical result about concepts, a reformulation of the question, or an honest argument that this is bedrock and nothing should be expected to move. Also happy to hear that my hypothesis is right, if you can defend it better than I did.
