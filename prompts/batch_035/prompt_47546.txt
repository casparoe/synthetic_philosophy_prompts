Is there a name for this thing? I keep noticing a pattern in standoffs where both sides have the option to irrevocably bind themselves. Say two companies are fighting over a market, and whichever one moves first on something truly irreversible -- signs the long-term exclusive contracts, publicly burns the bridge -- basically forces the other to back off, since the move is visible and can't be undone. Fine, that part I get, I know Schelling wrote alot about commitment and I'm not asking about that. What I can't find a word for is the timing logic that falls out of it: since committing first is what wins, both sides are pushed to commit earlier and earlier, including before they'd want to -- before knowing whether the other side is even going to compete, whether the market is real, whatever. So you get everyone locked into terrible positions that were chosen beacuse locking in fast was the whole point, and both sides end up worse off than if irreversible moves had been banned from the start. It feels extra sharp to me becuase the better the commitment tech gets, the worse this gets, and I keep seeing people say it'll get wild once AI agents can make binding deals with each other at machine speed.

Is there an established concept for the race itself and not just for the commitment move? Nearest things I know are chicken and burning-your-boats, but neither is really about the epistemics of committing under ignorance, or about the arms-race-to-commit dynamics. If there's no proper name, please coin one and define its edges for me -- like, does it still count when only one side can bind itself, or when commitments are cheap to fake, or when you commit knowing the other side can't observe it yet? I won't be satisfied with just a label, I want to know where this thing stops and ordinary first-mover advantage begins.
