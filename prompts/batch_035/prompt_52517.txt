Our reading group has spent two terms on imprecise probabilities and has ground to a halt in a way I'd like to understand better. The sharp Bayesians in the room hold that a rational agent's credence in any proposition is a single number, and they explain Ellsberg-style preferences as errors to be ironed out, on a par with the Allais pattern. The imprecise camp says a set of probability functions with no determinate weighting between them is the honest representation of evidence, and that the Ellsberg preferences are precisely what that set predicts. Both sides have now rehearsed everything: dilution and dilation cases, where conditioning an imprecise credence makes it *less* precise; the complaint that if you can't say which member of the set matters, the set is doing no work a single number plus a confidence level couldn't do; the reply that confidence levels smuggle precision back in at a higher order; the inability of the imprecise camp to settle on a decision rule (maximin over the set, E-admissibility, something else entirely), which the sharp camp takes as an admission that the view has no normative content.

My tentative diagnosis is that this is two questions wearing one name: a representational question about what a partial belief is, on which the disagreement may be near-verbal, and a normative question about what it is permissible not to decide, on which the disagreement is real and the sharp camp is quietly assuming it away. But I can't tell whether that's right or whether the dilation cases show that the representational question bites behaviourally after all. Please give me a diagnosis of what kind of disagreement this actually is — verbal, normative, methodological, or a clash of starting intuitions that neither side can argue for — grounded in what each side actually says, and then tell me what would count as progress given your diagnosis: a theorem, a decision-theoretic result, a distinction nobody has drawn, or an argument that the impasse is permanent and why. Please answer in British English.
