I've followed the World Happiness Report debates on LessWrong and adjacent forums for years — mostly the Goodhart-flavored takes about Finland optimizing for the rubric — and I finally sat down with the methodology, which turned up a question I can't resolve on my own.

As I understand it, each country's score is calculated from six components — GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom to make life choices, generosity, and perceptions of corruption — each weighted by how well it predicts ladder responses. Finland keeps topping the table because it scores so well on these six inputs. Given that, I have two questions. First, why do people keep alleging the ranking is culturally biased or otherwise invalid, when the inputs look like a sensible, mostly objective welfare rubric? Second, and more practically: wouldn't the rational move for a mid-ranking country be to treat the six components as the revealed objective function and spend its marginal policy dollar wherever it moves the most weighted components — for instance, boosting measured generosity, which looks far cheaper than raising GDP? If that counts as Goodharting, what exactly is being distorted, given the index is defined by those components in the first place? I gather the ladder question itself ("imagine a ladder from 0 to 10, where do you stand?") is supposed to do some work here, but I can't see how it could override the formula that generates the score.
