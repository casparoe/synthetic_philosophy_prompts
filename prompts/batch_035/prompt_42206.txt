so I've got this grab bag of opinions on voting rights / turnout stuff and I keep getting told at dinner that they can't all be right. before I write something longer defending the whole package I want an audit. here's where I stand:

1. I'm for compulsory voting with a small fine, Australia style. my main reason is that voting is a civic duty like jury duty — turnout is what makes the result legitimate, and people who stay home are free-riding on everyone who bothered to show up.

2. I'm for letting long-term resident non-citizens vote in local elections. if you've lived somewhere fifteen years, pay taxes there, send your kids to the local schools, you're governed by those decisions and you should get a say.

3. I'm for lowering the voting age to 16. every place that's actually tried it found that the 16 and 17 year olds who show up vote about as thoughtfully as anyone else, and honestly they mostly pay attention to civics in school that year anyway.

4. I am dead set against any competence condition on the vote — no literacy tests obviously, but also no cognitive screening for elderly voters, no "informed voter" requirements, nothing. the franchise is about equal standing under the law, not about being informed, and the moment you let competence in the door it gets weaponized.

5. I think an election boycott can be a legitimate political statement, like when a whole community stays home to show the system has lost them, and I'd hate to live somewhere that made protest abstention impossible.

6. also, and this might be a weird one, I think an individual vote basically never decides anything and I still think everyone ought to vote.

is this package consistent? go through it pair by pair, tell me which commitments are actually in tension and which only look that way, rank the tensions by severity, and for each one say what would have to give and what it costs. then tell me which single revision cleans up the most. don't just flag everything as equally bad — I want to know what's a real problem and what dissolves once you think about it for two minutes. I genuinely don't know where the weak spot is, so don't go easy on any of them, including the ones I sounded most confident about.
