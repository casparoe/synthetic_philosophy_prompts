1. im a welfarist about comparing futures: the only thing that matters for whether one outcome is better than another is how well the lives in it go.
2. the average view is wrong, and not only because of the standard case where adding a slightly less happy person to a world of extremely happy people makes the average go down. I think a world with a hundred billion excellent lives is better than a world with a billion lives that are marginally more excellent. numbers count.
3. every single step on the way from the small wonderful world to the huge barely-worth-living world is fine on its own. show me any one arrow — either you add some extra people and the existing ones do just as well, or everyone gives up a tiny bit of quality for a large gain in numbers — and I cant see how that step makes things worse. I've stared at the individual arrows and they all look ok.
4. better-than is transitive. if A beats B and B beats C, then A beats C. if that fails I dont know what better even means anymore.

6. you can't benefit someone by creating them — a happy person isn't better off than they would have been had they never existed, because in the never-existed scenario there's no them to be worse off.
