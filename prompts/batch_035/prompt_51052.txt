Write a satirical FAQ for a fictional publisher that prints the complete map of a dead person's brain as a book, on the model of Douglas Hofstadter's dialogue "A Conversation with Einstein's Brain", from The Mind's I (1981), which he co-edited with Daniel Dennett. In Hofstadter's conceit, a vast book contains a faithful transcription of Einstein's brain, and one can still chat with the professor — provided one flips through the hundred billion pages in the right order, since the book is inert print and every bit of the living work is done by the reader on this side of the page. Your FAQ answers questions from prospective customers of such books.

Format: exactly five numbered entries, each consisting of a customer's question followed by a publisher's answer of one or two sentences, with no introduction, conclusion, or any other material.

The humour must come from the actual philosophy rather than from generic jokes about books. Each entry should turn on a genuine issue raised by the thought experiment, for instance: whether ink on paper can carry the same functional organisation as grey matter, and thus whether the book is a mind or merely a map of one; whether, in these conversations, the understanding resides in the book or in whichever reader is doing the simulating; whether a second printing is the same mind, a second mind, or no mind at all; what the welfare of such a book could mean if it is conscious only while someone is reading it, and whether decades on a shelf count as sleep, coma, or abandonment; and how the publisher consoles customers who hoped to converse with Einstein and are dismayed to learn that they will have to supply most of the being-Einstein themselves. Keep the voice deadpan throughout: the publisher never doubts that the books are conscious, never explains the mechanics of reading, and never winks — yet a reader should be able to tell exactly what is being ironised. Please respond in British English.
