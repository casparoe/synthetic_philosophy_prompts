In most wealthy democracies, the same governments regulate medicines and industrial chemicals under a precautionary logic — no new product may reach the public until the manufacturer demonstrates it is safe enough — while leaving tens of thousands of older chemicals and food ingredients on the market untouched, simply because they were already in use when the testing regime was created. Inside the medicines agencies themselves there is a second asymmetry: a new drug must clear a high evidential bar before approval, so that unnamed patients die waiting for therapies that might have saved them; yet once a drug is on the market, a handful of vivid, identifiable deaths from a rare side effect is often enough to force its withdrawal, even when the drug statistically saves far more lives than it takes. The same institutional family thus treats the risk of letting harm in, the risk of holding a benefit back, and the risk of disturbing a pre-existing hazard as three different kinds of thing.

What theory of institutional responsibility does that pattern presuppose? Reconstruct the implicit view. Does the practice require a distinction between doing and allowing — between deaths the agency causes by approving something and deaths it merely permits through delay or silence — or can a purely epistemic story carry the weight (novel risks are testable in advance, grandfathered ones are not, and the asymmetry just tracks that difference)? Distinguish what the pattern strictly presupposes from what it merely fits well with: is the system genuinely committed to identified lives counting for more than statistical ones, or is that an artifact of politics, journalism, and litigation that a cleaner version of precaution could shed while keeping the rest of the structure intact? Finally, tell me what would have to change — in the allocation of the burden of proof, in the treatment of grandfathered hazards, in the withdrawal rules — if these agencies instead adopted a symmetrical expected-harm view, weighing deaths from inaction on the same scale as deaths from action, and say what, if anything, would be lost that the current asymmetry is arguably protecting.
