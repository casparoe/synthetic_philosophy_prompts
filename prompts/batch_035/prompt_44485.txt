My friends Dana and Marcus spent half a barbecue last weekend arguing about whether humanity is about to go extinct, and I honestly can't tell who won.

Dana's argument: you should expect yourself to be a fairly typical human among all the humans there will ever be. Like, roughly 100 billion people have lived so far, and if you're a random pick from the whole pool of humans-past-and-future, then you're probably somewhere in the middle of the story, not in the very first sliver. So the future can't contain trillions and trillions of people, because then it would be an absurd coincidence that you happen to be one of the first hundred billion. She also mentioned that some astrophysicist, Gott, did basically the same move while standing in front of the Berlin Wall in 1969 -- assume the wall is currently at a random point in its total lifetime rather than near the beginning, and you can put rough odds on how much longer it'll stand -- and the wall fell well within his range.

Marcus's comeback: the coincidence isn't actually a coincidence. Whoever ends up existing, somebody has to be them, and from inside it will always feel like "wow, that's me." And he says if anything our existence is evidence pointing the other way: the more observers there are, the more chances there are that one of them is you, so the fact that you exist at all is a nudge toward reality being absolutely stuffed with people, or aliens, or whatever. He wants to go further and say that between two otherwise equally good scientific theories, the one that predicts way more observers is way more likely to be true, purely because our existence is more expected under it.

Both of these sound like actual reasoning and they can't both be right. Is Dana's inference any good in the first place (and did the Berlin Wall thing even prove anything, or is that just luck that would've happened anyway)? Does Marcus actually neutralize her argument, or does his move just relocate the weirdness -- because "I exist, therefore reality is packed with observers" sounds kind of deranged to me, like he's licensed to believe in an infinite multiverse because he checked that he's conscious? Is there a principled way to figure out who's right here, or is this one of those cases where the intuitions just crash into each other and there's no fact of the matter?
