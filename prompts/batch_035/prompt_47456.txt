I run incident postmortems at a mid-sized company, and something about how we do "root cause analysis" has started to bug me. Every outage has a basically endless causal history — a deploy went out, a config flag was wrong, the retry logic amplified it, the on-call was asleep, the feature even had traffic because marketing ran a campaign. Yet the writeup always lands on one or two items as THE cause, and the way we pick them is basically "what could we actually change so this doesn't happen again" — a process fix, better alerting, whatever. A friend who did philosophy as an undergrad says this makes our postmortems quietly interventionist about causation rather than counterfactualist, and I did not fully follow the distinction but it's been living rent free in my head since.

So my question: what theory of causation does root cause analysis actually presuppose, if any? Is it just counterfactual dependence plus a filter for what's within our power to intervene on, or is there a real tension there? Second thing — our postmortems are officially blameless, but everyone reads the "root cause: engineer overrode the alert" line differently than "root cause: disk filled up", even tho on your causal-graph story both are just nodes. Does the practice of singling out one node quietly presuppose something about responsibility that the blameless framing is supposed to deny? And lastly, tell me whether RCA genuinely needs any of this philosophy or whether several different theories of causation would license the exact same writeups — I want to know if we're underdetermined, not just wrong.
