My cousin just got diagnosed with Huntington's, which apparently means there's a 50/50 chance I inherited it too, since my dad's side of the family is the effected one. There's a clinic near me that does predictive testing, and because of the family history I could get it done basically for free as part of a research program. I've been putting it off for months and I'm starting to wonder whether I'm being irrational, so I wanted to think it through properly.

Here's why I ask. A friend of mine who's really into decision theory says refusing free information is basically never rational — his claim is that there's a classic result showing extra information can never lower your expected payoff, because in the worst case you just ignore the information and do whatever you were going to do anyway. More knowledge = more options, he says, like a free upgrade. And it's true that if I found out I'm a carrier there are things I'd do differently — probably not have kids the usual way, sort out long-term care insurance while I can still get it, maybe change careers entirely. And if I'm not a carrier, I'd finally be able to relax about the twitching in my hand that I've been googling at 2am. So the information seems useful in either direction.

But I keep noticing that "just ignore the information" is exactly the part I don't believe. I don't think I could ignore it. Twenty-odd years of knowing I'm going to come down with something incurable seems like it would itself be a cost, and I've watched an aunt who tested positive basically live inside her diagnosis ever since. There's also my partner to think about — she wants kids either way, but knowing would change the whole texture of that conversation, and I'm not sure I have the right to keep information from her that's partly hers too. So my question: is my friend's principle actually right and I'm just being squeamish, or does the principle have assumptions that quietly don't hold in my situation? What exactly does the decision-theoretic argument for the value of information assume, and where do those assumptions break down here? I'd like to know whether a careful decision theorist would side with me or with him, not just vibes either way.
