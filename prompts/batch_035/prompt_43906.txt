You are a professional philosopher who specializes in moral psychology and the emotions, and I would like you to audit a set of views I have settled into over years of thinking about this on my own, with no formal training, so please write for someone like me rather than for a colleague. The topic is guilt versus shame, and whether a culture can sensibly pick one of them as its favored moral emotion. Here is my set of commitments. First, I think guilt is the better moral emotion overall, because guilt is about what you did while shame is about what you are, and guilt therefore comes with a concrete next step, namely apologizing and repairing, while shame mostly pushes a person to hide and avoid the whole subject. Second, I think a culture genuinely can choose which of the two to foreground, through how it raises children, what it treats as disgraceful, and what it shrugs off, since these emotions are obviously trained rather than fixed. Third, I think an emotion that people cultivate because it is useful is not really a moral response anymore, because for a feeling to carry moral weight it has to arise in you on its own, not be deployed as a tool. Fourth, I think the worst moral failures, like cruelty or contempt for people who are weak, are flaws of character rather than one-off acts, and only shame registers character, so a genuinely cruel person should feel bad about what they are, not merely about some incident. Fifth, I think someone who does something wrong in private, where nobody is harmed and nobody ever finds out, say keeping money found in a wallet dropped on an empty street, still ought to feel guilty about it. My question is whether these five commitments hang together. Please work through them carefully before answering, and don't just list every place where two of them could be made to sound opposed, since I suspect some apparent conflicts dissolve once you look closely. Tell me which tensions are real and how severe each one is, rank them from worst to mildest, and for each say what would have to give and roughly what that revision would cost me elsewhere in the set. If you conclude that one single change would clean up the most, say so and say which. Please write your entire answer as continuous prose, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, in the style of a thoughtful long email, and please use American English spelling throughout.
