Been in a philosophy of time reading group going on two years now and the presentism vs eternalism thing has completely stalled, and I'm starting to suspect it stalled for an interesting reason rather than just because people are stubborn. Quick rundown of where we're at: everyone agrees the present exists. Eternalists say dinosaurs and future Martian outposts exist too, in the same tenseless sense of 'exist' that tables do; presentists say only present things exist; growing block and moving spotlight sit awkwardly in between. The arguments have all been on the table forever -- the Rietdijk-Putnam thing where different observers slicing spacetime differently makes any frame-relative 'now' look like it can't be the privileged one; the truthmaker objection (what makes it true that there were dinosaurs, if nothing past exists to make it true?); Sider pressing that and Craig answering with tensed or restricted quantifiers; the eternalist counter-charge that presentism is basically an error theory about ordinary cross-temporal talk pretending not to be one. Nobody in my group has budged an inch the whole time.

Here's my working theory and I want you to evaluate it, not just nod along: the fight is actually two disagreements stacked on top of each other. Bottom layer is a verbal dispute about what the unrestricted quantifier ranges over -- eternalist says all times, presentist says just now, and once you flag the ambiguity both can cheerfully say dinosaurs exist* in one sense and don't* in the other, problem dissolved. But sitting on top of it there's a genuinely substantive disagreement about whether time *passes*, the A-theory/B-theory thing, which keeps getting smuggled into the presentism fight because presentists and A-theorists recruit from the same pool. So when the debate stalls, maybe what's happening is people are trading blows over the verbal layer while each silently assuming they've won the substantive layer.

Is that diagnosis right? Or is the quantifier thing itself substantive -- like, which things exist simpliciter isn't 'just semantics' because it fixes what you can quantify over and which cross-temporal relations (causation between events at different times, reference to dead people) are even available to you, so the eternalist's claim does real metaphysical work the presentist's can't match? And then tell me honestly what progress would even look like for this debate: a physics result (some empirically preferred foliation of spacetime?), a semantic result about what ordinary tensed exist-talk means, or is it one of those cases where the right ending is that the two sides are answering different questions under one name and should just split the vocabulary and go home?
