ok, heres where I've landed on truth for our pragmatism reading group — I want a consistency check before I send it around, since I suspect something in here doesn't hang together but I can't see what.

(1) I go with Peirce over James: truth is what inquiry would converge on in the long run, if inquiry ran indefinitely under ideal conditions. (2) But I also think a big part of what "true" means for a belief is that it keeps working — that it would survive every future test and never leave us surprised, which is more James than I usually admit out loud. (3) I'm sure it was true in 1400 that the earth moves, even though believing that back then did not "work" for anyone — it was useless for navigation and could get you killed. (4) I'm a realist about the deep past: there's a fact of the matter about, say, how many people were living in Rome in the year 100, and it's true now, even though all the evidence is gone forever and no inquiry, however ideal, will ever settle it. (5) I don't think inquiry ever actually ends — science will keep revising forever, no final opinion is coming — and yet I want to say our current well-confirmed claims are just plain true, not merely "true until further notice."

So: which of these are genuinely in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest fix for each? Rank them by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and be blunt about which one probably has to go — I honestly don't know which I'd give up. Please answer in American English.
