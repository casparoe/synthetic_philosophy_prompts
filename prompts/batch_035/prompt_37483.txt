I'm on the steering committee for a small endowed program at my university -- call it the Safe Haven fellowship. It was set up years ago to bring scholars facing persecution over their work here for a year, and it's mostly done classic cases: a statistician charged over an election study, that kind of thing. This cycle we have money for two residencies and, out of forty applications, the committee has fixated on three candidates, and now we're stuck.

Candidate one is a chemist who published measurements of heavy metals downstream of a mine with government connections, got detained, and fled. Textbook persecution-for-scholarship, exactly what the fund was created for. Candidate two is a high school teacher from a country in civil war whose town was shelled until she left. Nobody persecuted *her* in particular -- the war didn't care who she was -- but she's been running informal classes for kids in a transit camp and is by all accounts extraordinary. Candidate three is a man whose coastal village is slowly going under from rising water: no war, no persecution, not a refugee under any legal definition anywhere, but no state will take him either, and he's a trained archivist who wants to spend the year documenting his village's records before the place is gone.

Half the committee says the fund should stick to persecuted scholars -- that's the mission, that's what persecution means, and the teacher and the archivist are heartbreaking but belong to general humanitarian giving, which is not us. The other half says the persecution requirement is basically an accident of how the postwar refugee definition got written, it's morally arbitrary, and a private fund has no business reproducing it. One member goes further: since a chunk of our endowment traces back to defense contracts, she thinks we specifically owe people displaced by conflict generally, not just scholars, and that the mission language is a fig leaf.

Draft the decision memo I can circulate before the vote. Take a side: which two do we fund, and what written criterion goes in the guidelines for future cycles? I don't want a memo that lays out the frameworks and leaves the choice to us -- I want your actual recommendation with your actual reasons, and I want the strongest argument on the losing side treated honestly enough that the people voting no could sign off that they were understood. Keep it to something I can actually send, not a treatise.
