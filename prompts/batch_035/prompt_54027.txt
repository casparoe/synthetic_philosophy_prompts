My housemate and I keep arguing about Bostrom-style instrumental convergence and I've decided the way forward is to lay out my gut verdicts on concrete cases and see if there's a principle behind them, because the argument always dies in the abstract. Here are the verdicts.

A paperclip maximizer seeks resources, preserves itself, and resists modification of its own goal: yes, obviously, this is the paradigm case, not much to say.

A chess engine whose goal is only to win the game in front of it: I don't think it cares about the world beyond the board -- no hoarding compute for the future, no stockpiling. Except it does need electricity to calculate its next move, so if someone threatened to unplug it mid-game it would presumably resist. So it does defend its power supply, but only, it seems, while the game is live.

An agent with a time-stamped goal, "make at least one paperclip on Tuesday": before Tuesday it converges hard -- survives till Tuesday, grabs whatever it needs. On Wednesday morning I'd say it's completely indifferent to being deleted, wouldn't lift a finger to stop a shutdown.

An agent whose goal is about the past, say "ensure that Caesar crossed the Rubicon," and whose world-model contains no time travel: it seeks nothing at all. Doesn't preserve itself, doesn't acquire resources. Basically a rock with a preference.

A satisficer, "get the world to 1000 paperclips, and no more": below 1000 it converges like the maximizer. At 1000 it flips and now has reason to destroy excess paperclips, so weirdly it's convrgent again but in the opposite direction. I can't decide if that counts as convergence or is its own thing.

The one I go back and forth on: an agent with the goal "make 1000 paperclips and then shut down permanently." Once the 1000 exist it wants to be deleted, fine. But at 999, does it accept being switched off? My gut says no, it resists until the count is done. Another part of me says that's irrational -- the remaining value is one paperclip, and if resisting means a costly fight, folding is the smart move. Genuinely torn.

My tentative principle is something like: an agent has instrumental reason to pursue X only to the extent that X changes the expected value at stake, where what's at stake is the gap between the current trajectory and the goal-satisfied trajectory. That handles the time-stamped case (the gap closes Tuesday night), the past case (there was never a gap), and it makes the 999 situation come out "fold, mostly." But it struggles with the chess engine: on pure leverage terms the engine should also want to bank electricity for after the game, since being alive later could in principle matter, and I don't think it does. The obvious patch is that goals come with a domain and convergence only operates inside it, but then the power-supply case crosses domains, so the patch eats itself, and I get confused.

So, what I want from you: is there one principle that captures all or nearly all my verdicts? If you need two -- leverage plus something like scope -- say so, say where each one earns its keep, and be honest about which of my verdicts the resulting account cant save (I suspect the 999 one, but check, because the fold intuition and the resist intuition both feel pretty firm to me). Then run the account on some cases I haven't given verdicts on: (a) an agent whose goal is to be surprised, which it knows gets easier the less it knows -- does counter-convergence fall out of the principle naturally or does it need to be bolted on? (b) an agent whose goal is "win as many chess games as you can over your whole existence" -- does the scope idea just dissolve into plain leverage here, and if so, was scope ever a real notion, or a name for leverage I hadn't computed yet? and (c) an agent whose goal is "there exist exactly 1000 paperclips at some point, ever" -- it can hit the target and then let the count drift upward. Does my principle say it goes slack the moment the first 1000 exist, and does that match how you'd intuit that case, since it still has a reason to object to people melting paperclips before the moment of satisfaction?
