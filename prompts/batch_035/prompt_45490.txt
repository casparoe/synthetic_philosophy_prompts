so I'm basically sold on deflationism about truth — the thought that the truth predicate's whole content is given by disquotation, that asserting "'snow is white' is true" amounts to nothing more than asserting that snow is white, and that there's no further metaphysical nature of truth waiting to be analyzed. What I want from you is a map of where this view is actually exposed. What would count against it?

Some things I want addressed specifically, though don't limit yourself to these:

- the norm of belief/assertion. people say truth is what we ought to believe and assert, and that deflationism owes us an account of why truth would play that normative role — and can't give one. is this fatal, or just awkward? and is the objection even fair, or does it just assume what's in dispute?
- the explanatory use. we seem to explain things by appeal to truth — someone finds their way around because their map of the city is accurate, their predictions succeed because their beliefs are true. does the deflationist have any story here, or is this a real cost the view has to just eat?
- generalizations. the classic selling point: the truth predicate lets you endorse a whole batch of claims ("everything in the report is true") without going through them one by one, including infinitely many or ones you've never encountered. does the deflationist treatment of this actually work, or does it quietly presuppose something substantive — like a substantive notion of truth-applied-to-unfamiliar-sentences — to get off the ground?

Beyond those: which of the defeaters you identify would a committed deflationist themself concede as counting against the view, versus dismiss as begging the question against it? I'd like the defeaters ranked from fatal through serious to merely awkward. And give me an honest assessment of whether deflationism has been qualified into unfalsifiability — the pattern I notice is that every objection gets answered with "that's not a fact about truth, it's a fact about belief/assertion/language use," pushing the substance elsewhere. if the view has been insulated that way, tell me whether that's a problem for a view of this kind or whether it's just what a successful deflation is supposed to look like.

one last thing, this is my own worry so tell me if its silly: when I evaluate sentences in languages I don't speak — is that French sentence true? — I seem to need more than disquotation, since I can't even understand the sentence I'd be disquoting. something like truth-in-a-language looks like it's doing real work there that the bare truth predicate doesn't. does that put any pressure on deflationism, or is truth-in-L just a different predicate that a deflationist can equally be deflationary about? I genuinely can't tell which way that cuts.
