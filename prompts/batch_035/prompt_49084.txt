I've got no philosophy background, just a thing I've been turning over for years on my own, and I've landed somewhere I can't seem to move off of: sentience isn't a light switch, it's a dimmer, and moral status follows the dimmer the whole way down. A chimp matters more than a dog, a dog more than a chicken, a chicken more than a fish, a fish more than an insect — and even a thermostat responding to "pain" has some tiny nonzero moral status, because the difference between a bug's nociception and a thermostat's temperature sensing is one of complexity, not kind. I've even worked out a little scheme: you weight the richness of the creature's inner life by how confident you are that it has one, so uncertain-but-rich beats confident-but-dim. My wife thinks this is insane. She says either something matters or it doesn't, and if the smoke detector matters even a tiny bit, then I'm committed to comforting my appliances. What would actually count against my view? I want the arguments and cases that should make me give it up — the stuff my wife would say if she argued better, plus whatever the serious version of the objection is — and I want an honest take on whether I've insulated the view so much it can absorb anything (I already notice I keep nudging the cutoff down whenever its inconvenient, e.g. I swat mosquitos without a second thought and tell myself they're barely above the thermostat line). Rank the defeaters for me: which would a thoughtful defender of the view have to concede, and which can they rightly shrug off?
