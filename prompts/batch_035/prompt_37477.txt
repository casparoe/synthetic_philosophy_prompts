so my younger brother has been on dialysis for about two years now (focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, his kidneys just quit on him at 29) and I got tested as a donor last spring but I have high blood pressure so they turned me down. since then I've gone completely down the organ policy rabbit hole — reading anything I can find, arguing with everyone at thanksgiving — and it's genuinely changed where I stand on a bunch of market-adjacent stuff. but now I've written up my positions and I keep staring at the list because I honestly can't tell if it hangs together or not. here's the list:

(1) selling a kidney should be legal. the altruism-only system leaves people dying on the waitlist, and if my brother can take a kidney from a stranger for free I don't see why that stranger can't be paid. Iran pays donors through a government program and they basically don't have a waitlist. (2) the principle behind that, which I think I apply consistently elsewhere: the state shouldn't ban voluntary deals between informed consenting adults. I lean on the same logic for drugs, sex work, assisted dying. no fraud, full information, and then butt out. (3) BUT price gouging after a disaster should absolutely be illegal. our city flooded a couple years back and I personally watched a gas station charge $15 for a case of water to people who'd just lost their houses, and I still think about it. that should be jail, not a functioning market. (4) vote selling should stay illegal. I don't want to live in a democracy where votes have a going price. (5) I read Sandel's What Money Can't Buy for book club and thought it was mostly hand-waving — the whole "markets degrade sacred things" thing does nothing for me, nothing becomes worse just because it has a dollar figure attached. (6) and the last one, which is the one that makes me feel like a hypocrite: even if kidney sales were legal tomorrow, I would never sell mine, and I'd quietly judge a friend a little if they sold theirs to pay off a credit card. also the "market" I actually picture isn't really a market — I'd want the government as the only buyer, at a fixed generous price, no private brokers, no advertising.

so my question for you: does any of this actually fit together? go through the list and tell me which commitments are in genuine tension with each other, which ones only look like they conflict, and rank the tensions by how bad they are rather than listing them all as equally fatal. where something has to give, say which one and what it costs me — e.g. can I keep (1), with my brother in mind, without swallowing the $15 water? and if I try to rescue (3) and (4) with something like "some things just shouldn't be for sale," does that quietly wreck my argument for (1), or is there a version of it that survives? be blunt with me, I'd rather find out now than mid-argument at thanksgiving.
