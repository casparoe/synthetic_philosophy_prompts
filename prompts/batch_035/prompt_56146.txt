So, background: I'm a backend engineer, about ten years in, mostly distributed systems and observability tooling, and I have zero formal philosophy training. I got into consciousness stuff kinda by accident after reading about split-brain experiments and those choice blindness studies (where people defend choices they never actually made) on a long flight, and then falling down the rabbit hole. I've written up a short essay draft arguing for what I gather is called illusionism, but using analogies from my day job, and I want feedback before I put it on my blog.

Here's the gist of the essay. Every system I've built has a monitoring layer -- dashboards, alerts, traces -- and everyone who's run one knows the dashboard is not the system. The dashboard has its own states: a green bar, a red dot, a little graph. Those states are real, they're just states of the monitoring layer, and nobody thinks the green bar means the cluster contains some green essence. My claim is that introspection is our monitoring layer: it's a physical subsystem with limited bandwidth that reports on the rest of the brain, and what we call phenomenal character -- the what-it's-like -- is a property of how that layer formats its reports, not of the states being reported on. The split-brain and choice blindness stuff shows the layer can be confidently wrong, which undercuts the idea that introspective certainty is special evidence. So my conclusion: the hard problem feels hard because the self-model's reporting language bakes in a category it can't cash out, like a build where the debug symbols got stripped and the tooling just fills in plausible-looking mappings. The feeling of an extra nonfunctional property is a formatting artifact, and arguing about whether qualia are physical is like arguing about whether the green bar is really green all the way down.

Three things I'd like comments on, specifically. First, which premise is the weakest link -- I'd rather know where it breaks than hear that it's basically fine. Second, a friend already pushed back that dashboards are observed from the outside while introspection is from the inside, so maybe the analogy is decoration and not load-bearing; is there a version of my point that survives that, or does the whole thing collapse? Third, am I just repackaging Dennett's user illusion stuff with new paint? If so, is there anything here worth posting anyway, or should I reframe it as a software engineer's gloss on existing ideas rather than a new argument? Be blunt, I can take it.
