I'm a bioethics PhD student and two people in my cohort have been going at each other for literally two years about the concept of disease. One is all-in on a Boorse-style biostatistical view: disease just is statistically subnormal functioning of some part, relative to a reference class like age and sex, and the concept is value-free all the way down. The other is a normativist -- she thinks a condition isn't a disease unless it's bad for the person, and that the dysfunction talk at most picks out the medically relevant bit while harm does the real classificatory work. They've finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration and, because I'm friends with both, I got stuck drafting the joint statement. Help?

What I need is: (1) the claims they could both actually sign -- I suspect there's more overlap than they admit, e.g. that whether something is a disease shouldn't turn on whether we can currently treat it, and that disease attributions license certain kinds of intervention and excuse certain behaviors; (2) the residual disagreements stated so that each would accept the wording of their own side (so no sneaking in "dysfunction is secretly value-laden" as a agreed claim, she has to be able to sign every line); (3) the cruxes -- I think one is whether you can even individuate "parts" and their "functions" without smuggling in norms, another is what each view says when dysfunction and harm come apart, and maybe another about whether the reference-class statistics can be done in a non-question-begging way; and (4) for each crux, at least one concrete case both sides would accept as a fair test -- stuff like grief, menopause, aging, or historically pathologized conditions where there was cultural disapproval but arguably no dysfunction -- with each side's prediction about the case written down in advance so nobody can weasle out later. No fair giving one side all the cases it already handles well.

Format: numbered agreed-claims section, then a disagreements table, then cruxes each with the test case(s) and the two predictions.
