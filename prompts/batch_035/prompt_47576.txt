I've basically come to accept the evolutionary debunking argument in metaethics -- the idea that natural selection shaped our moral beliefs for fitness, not for tracking independent moral truth, so if realism is true the reliability of those beliefs is a massive coincidence, since the best explanation of why we believe what we believe makes no reference to those beliefs being true. Street's Darwinian dilemma is my reference point, but any shaped-vs-tracked version will do. My question: what would count against it?

I don't just want the standard moves rehearsed back at me. Take the third-factor reply, where the normative facts themselves are part of what explains the evolutionary pattern -- when would a committed debunker actually concede that this hurts, versus insist it just changes the subject from reliability to explanation? If it turned out that evolutionary convergence pushes lots of unrelated species toward roughly the same cooperative dispositions, does that give the realist any traction, or is convergence still fully compatible with pure shaping? And what if someone showed that populations whose moral beliefs tracked their circumstances slightly worse got out-bred slightly faster -- is "truth-tracking is fitness-enhancing in a coarse way" a live escape route, or does the debunker just reply that the match is way too loose to ground anything like reliability?

The big one as I see it: the argument seems to prove too much. The same reasoning debunks our mathematical beliefs, or our belief-forming faculties generally, since evolution shaped those for fitness too. If the debunker saves math by calling it indispensable, or says the argument only targets contingent faculties and not necessary truths, has the argument been qualified into something that only hits a convenient target? Relatedly, the dilemma itself seems to grant that if realism is true, evolution would have had to have tracked moral truth at least a little for the argument to even get a grip, and I've never been able to tell whether that's a strength or the arguement quietly assuming what's at issue.

So: rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, tell me which of them a commited debunker would themselves concede as counting against the view versus dismiss as a misunderstanding, and be honest about whether the view ends up well insulated by all these qualifications -- and if so, whether that's a bug or just what you should expect from an argument of this kind.
