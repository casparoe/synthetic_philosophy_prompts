Two friends of mine have been arguing about same-sex marriage for maybe six years now, and it's gotten to the point where they mostly just repeat themselves at each other. One is a new natural law guy — he wrote his master's thesis on Finnis and is deep in the Girgis/George "What Is Marriage?" line of argument. The other is a liberal political philosopher on the marriage-equality side, who thinks excluding same-sex couples is basically arbitrary discrimination. I've finally badgered them into doing an adversarial collaboration, and they asked me to draft the skeleton of the joint statement. Help me out.

The document needs:

1. A numbered section of claims both can sign. Within that, I want it to seperate what's actually settled among people who know this literature (e.g. historians seem pretty agreed that marriage as a social institution has taken alot of forms across societies — polygyny was normal in plenty of places) from what's contested even among experts, especially the empirical child-outcomes research. Neither of them should be able to smuggle contested stuff in as if it were consensus.

2. A disagreements table: each disagreement stated so its own side would accept the wording as fair. My natural law friend hates being described as opposing this "because of tradition" — his actual claim is about what marriage is, the comprehensive-union / bodily-union stuff. My liberal friend refuses the label "revisionist," which is the other side's coinage — find wording she'd actually sign for her own view. And flag any parts where the fight is partly about words: I suspect the "is the state recognizing a commitment or supporting child-rearing?" question is partly verbal, but check that rather than assuming.

3. The cruxes, each with a case or piece of evidence both agree bears on it, and each side's prediction written down in advance so nobody can retrofit later. Ones I suspect:
   - The infertility counterexample. If infertile opposite-sex couples count, what exactly is doing the work? His answer is the act-type vs act-result move — coitus is procreative in kind even when it can't produce a child — and she thinks that's ad hoc. Is there anything that would move either of them here, or is it standoff all the way down to bedrock metaphysics? Say which, honestly.
   - The old vs new natural law split. He's on record that the perverted-faculty-argument version (classical Thomism) is weaker than the Finnis/George basic-good version. That's an intra-conservative concession she might be able to use, so have them spell out whether the conjugal case survives if only the new version is right.
   - The law-teaches claim: whether legal redefinition shifts social norms about marriage over time. They could record predictions about measurable things (marriage rates, out-of-wedlock births) in countries that adopted same-sex marriage early, and agree in advance how many years of data would count as evidence. Though if either thinks the data is weak evidence either way, have them say that too.
   - Keeping the conceptual crux (what marriage is) apart from the empirical one (how kids fare). They slide between these constantly and I want the document to stop it.

4. A short closing section: anything both agree would genuinely move the debate, what each would accept as counting against their own view, and whether they'd jointly reject the "get the state out of marriage entirely" escape hatch — I believe they both do, but for different reasons, and the reasons are worth writing down. If a chunk of the dispute turns out to be verbal, name the chunk.

Throughout, mark which agreed claims are settled versus genuinely contested among people who know the topic — they're both exhausted by the other side presenting contested claims as the consensus. And keep it good faith: the point is a document both would actually put their names on, not a gotcha for either.
