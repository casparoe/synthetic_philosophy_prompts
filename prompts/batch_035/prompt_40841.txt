A friend of mine went down a consciousness rabbit hole and sent me this little essay to convince me my brain is lying to me about everything. It sounds pretty persausive but a couple of things feel off, and I can't tell whether that's because he's confused or because I am:

"Perception is a user interface, like the desktop on a computer. The icon for a file doesn't resemble the file it stands for, and likewise your experience of a tomato doesn't resemble anything out there — space and time themselves are just the desktop, and physics is a description of the icon set, not of reality. Since the whole interface is a useful fiction, conscious experience as we know it isn't real either: what's actually happening is unconscious computation, and the felt redness of red is just the interface's cartoon of a data process. Dennett has been arguing for decades that consciousness is exactly this kind of user illusion, and Hoffman has taken it further with an evolutionary argument: selection rewards fitness, not truth, so evolution would have pushed us toward a useful but deeply distorted interface, meaning our senses are basically organized hallucinations. If that's right, science is just a hallucination too, so the theory refutes itself — unless you go all the way and say the physical world doesn't exist, which nobody is going to except. So the view collapses into the oldest skepticism in the book: we can never know anything about the real world, and that's that."

Here's what bugs me. I've also seen people describe Hoffman as a straight-up idealist who thinks conscious experience is the one thing that's actually real — which is basically the opposite of saying "conscious experience isn't real." Can those both be him? Can you go through the summary and sort out which parts are accepted points, which parts quietly merge different thinkers' views into one, and where the final "so it's just skepticism" move goes wrong? And if Hoffman really does deny that the physical world exists, what does he think we CAN know about whatever is behind the interface — is it a shrug, or does he claim there's a way in? (I'm inclined to think my friend is half right and half confused, but for all I know I'm the confused one.)
