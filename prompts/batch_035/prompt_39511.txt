I write novels for a living, and for the past year I've been deep in one about a teleport company whose founder has, let's say, unresolved feelings about the product. Teleportation in my book works the Parfit way — your body is scanned and destroyed at this end, an exact replica walks out at the other — and the plot is mostly about what the founder tells people they're actually buying. My co-writer Dana has turned into our resident ethicist because she hands down verdicts on these cases like a small claims court, and I keep writing chapters around her rulings. Her track record so far:

1. You go under general anesthesia and wake up the next day, same body, mostly same mind: obviously survives.
2. Her grandfather had a massive stroke — lost nearly all his memories, personality totally flattened, then slowly got some of it back, same body the whole time: still him, he survived.
3. Clean teleport, Earth body destroyed, replica on Mars with everything intact: survives. She's weirdly casual about this one, like it's just a long commute.
4. A scanner glitch produces two replicas and the original is destroyed: the guy died, neither replica is him.
5. A replica gets made while the original is fine and asleep: the sleeper is him, the replica is a new person who happens to share his past.

For the last act I need her ruling on two new cases, and I genuinely can't predict her anymore:

A. A man dies in an accident. Turns out the company quietly kept a scan of him from ten years earlier, and they rebuild the replica from that — so he walks out remembering nothing of his last decade, because the scan predates it. Did he survive?
B. An old man with advanced dementia gets scanned, and the replica comes out restored, with the mind he had at fifty, sharp and whole — while the original, confused and barely tracking anything, lives on for another year in a care home. Who's the patient here, so to speak? Did anyone survive him?

So: what should I expect her to say about A and B? What rule or rules actually fit her track record? I can see at least two candidates — something like "you survive just in case there's an unbroken, non-branching chain of psychological continuity" versus "you are whoever turns out to be the closest continuer of the old you" — and the annoying thing is her five verdicts so far are consistent with both. They pull apart on my cases, and a third rule about how much direct psychological connectedness is needed probably gets its own reading of case A. Tell me which rule you'd bet on and why, give me your best guess on A and B, and then — because I'd like a scene where she finally gets cornered — what case should I put to her that would split the candidate rules clean apart, something I could actually write a chapter around? Also please tell me if her track record is just internally inconsistent and she's been bluffing me this whole time, because the deadline is in six weeks and alot is riding on her.
