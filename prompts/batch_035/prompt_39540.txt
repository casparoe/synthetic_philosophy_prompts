My husband and I have been going back and forth for months about whether to have a first child, and our arguments keep dead-ending in the same spot. His position is that the decision can't be made rationally at all. Everything I know about what my life is like is evidence about a life that won't exist anymore if we go through with it: I've never been a parent, so I can't picture what the days will actually feel like, and every account I've read or heard from friends is like a description of a color given by people whose eyes work differently from mine. He says a rational decision requires weighing how much I'll enjoy or regret the outcomes, and since I can't properly represent those outcomes in the first place, the weighing can't get started — so there's no fact of the matter about what it's rational for me to do, only a leap.

I have two worries about this, and then a problem of my own. First, his view seems to prove too much. By the same logic, nobody could ever have rationally chosen their first job, their first marathon, moving across the country, or adopting our dog — anything they couldn't picture in advance. Either all of those choices were irrational too, or something is genuinely different about the child case, and neither of us can say what. Second, I tried to work around him and it backfired. I thought: fine, I can't imagine it myself, but I can ask people who did it and see whether they'd do it again — treat other people's lives as the experiment I can't run on myself. My sister, who has two kids, says she'd do it all over again in a second and that no description could possibly have prepared her, but that it was the right call. Then I read that people's reported day-to-day happiness barely moves after having children, and that people generally remember their big choices as better than they experienced them at the time. Now I can't tell whether my sister's testimony is evidence about the experience itself or only evidence about what the experience does to the people reporting it. If becoming a parent rewrites the person doing the reporting, then asking around hands me the preferences of the person I would become, not a reason for who I am now — though I also suspect that letting my current self's preferences call all the shots is exactly the mistake.

So here is what I actually want to know: how should someone in my position make this decision? Is my husband right that rationality simply runs out, and if so, does that make leaping anyway the rational move — or is "waiting until I'm sure" itself a decision, with a cost we're hiding from ourselves? Is there anything salvageable in the asking-around approach, or is it broken in the way I've described? And how do I tell the difference between a good way of deciding and a rationalization of what I already half-want? I don't want "go with your gut" — I want to know what would make one method of deciding better than another, given that whichever one wins, I'll be a somewhat different person by the time I find out.
