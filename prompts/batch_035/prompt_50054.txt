I've been going down the rabbit hole of coherent extrapolated volition, Yudkowsky's old idea that a friendly AI shouldn't just do what humanity says it wants, but what we "would want" if we were smarter, knew more, thought faster, had grown up farther together, etc. My reading group has been arguing about a distinction one of my friends proposed, which she calls "thin" vs. "thick" extrapolation. Thin extrapolation only strips things out: it corrects for obvious factual errors and well-documented cognitive biases (like scope insensitivity), but it doesn't add anything new to a person's values. Thick extrapolation goes the whole way: it lets the idealized version of you acquire new knowledge, new experiences, maybe even new moral concepts, and takes whatever that idealized self ends up valuing as your extrapolated volition.

My friend is on team thick, and honestly she's got a decent point: if thin extrapolation leaves values untouched, it just enshrines whatever ignorance and parochialism we currently have, and there's nothing especially authentic about the preferences I happen to have given how little I've thought things through. But I want to push back and I need help doing it well. Please make an argument for the thin approach, and specifically address the strongest version of her point. The argument I have in mind so far runs something like this: thick extrapolation faces a "who is that even for" problem — if the extrapolated self has changed enough, its preferences no longer have any special connection to me, and the AI is serving a stranger who happens to share my causal history, which seems to undercut the whole rationale for using MY volition as the input in the first place. There's also something suspicious about the fact that we can't actually run the thick extrapolation in our heads, so claims about what our idealized selves would want are suspiciously easy to load our own current opinions into.

One more thing if you can weave it in: I suspect the choice between thin and thick interacts with the aggregation problem, i.e., how you combine millions of people's extrapolated volitions into one target for the AI. If each person's thickly-extrapolated values have drifted apart from their current ones, it's not clear why the resulting bargain deserves the consent of the actual people living under it. But feel free to push back on whether that's really a problem for thick extrapolation or whether I'm conflating two separate issues.
