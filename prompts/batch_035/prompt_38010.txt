I'm the secretary of a five-member committee that runs a local trail-club, and our last meeting ended in a dispute I was not able to referee. We were deciding whether to move next year's volunteer week to a more remote site, and our written rules say the week gets moved exactly when two conditions are met: the site must be accessible enough for our older members, and it must be worth the ecological effort. We voted on the two conditions separately. Three of us judged the site accessible enough, and a different three judged it worthwhile; only one member judged it both. The chair declared that the club had accepted both conditions by majority, and that since the rules tie the move to the two conditions jointly, the club had decided to move. Mara objected at once. She said four of the five of us would vote against the move if asked straight out, so it is absurd to say the club decided to move: a collective decision that almost no individual member holds is not a decision but an artifact of how the votes were chopped up. The chair answered that a group's judgment has to be built from somewhere, and premise-by-premise majority voting is the fairest way to build it; if we instead voted only on the final question, then whoever chooses which question gets put to a vote controls the outcome, which is hardly more democratic. Mara replied that individual people do not carry contradictions around like that: each of us holds one coherent view, and the only honest candidates for the club's view are views that actual members hold, which the move is not. The chair said that standard would paralyze any group that reasons in steps, since no conclusion drawn by combining separately agreed points could ever count as the group's. Please adjudicate their exchange move by move: say who is right about each specific claim, whether there is any sense in which the club really decided one thing rather than the other, and what, if anything, both of them are missing. Write continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, just the flowing text of a letter.
