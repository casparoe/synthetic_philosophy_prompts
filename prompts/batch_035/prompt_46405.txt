I'm a third year PhD student in econ, and I keep running into the socialist calculation debate from two directions at once -- from history of thought seminars where its presented as settled (Mises: no prices, no rational allocation; Lange: just solve the system, simulate an auctioneer; Hayek: the problem was never the math, it's dispersed and tacit knowledge), and from the tech side, where big firms do huge internal allocation without internal market prices and people on the left are genuinely reviving "cybernetic planning," including the old Cockshott/Cottrell-style line that computing the plan is just a computation problem we've mostly solved by now. It doesn't feel settled to me, it feels reopened, but I only get one dissertation and my committee is skeptical of the whole area.

So: if someone was going to spend ~3 years on the conceptual side of this, which open questions should they rank first, and why? The candidates I see off the top of my head -- (a) whether the knowledge problem is fundamentally computational, such that it erodes as compute and data collection scale, or whether it's about knowledge that's tacit or incentive-laden and never ends up in any dataset no matter how big; (b) what the existence of planned islands inside capitalist firms (Walmart, Amazon) is supposed to show -- that planning works at scale, or only that it works when embedded in an external price system; (c) whether the incentive problem and the knowledge problem can even be pried apart, since most of the "tacit knowledge" firms have is knowledge about how to game whatever target you set; (d) whether Lange-style trial and error procedures have any modern analogue worth taking seriously, or whether that whole branch just collapses into (a).

I want the ordering defended, not a survey: what your criteria are, which questions are upstream of which, which ones are tractable as conceptual work for an econ dept versus which would get me shipped off to CS or philosophy, and what evidence in the next few years would move things up or down the list. Please also push back if the framing itself is confused -- if the "AI reopens the calculation debate" thing is mostly vibes and the genuinely open questions are somewhere else entirely, say that plainly rather than ranking my four options for me. And im not looking for lit recommendations, I have enough reading; I want your actual prioritized take, with the reasoning.
