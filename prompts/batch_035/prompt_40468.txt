I teach a graduate seminar on the political philosophy of AI alignment, and I'm setting the take-home final. Please write one essay question, designed for an answer of roughly 1,500 words, on whose values an aligned AI system should serve. The question must not be answerable by reciting the standard survey material — the social choice results, the polling-versus-reflective-equivalence debate, the usual gestures at liberal neutrality. Instead, build it around a crux a colleague of mine keeps pressing: the analogy between an aligned AI and a liberal state fails not at the level of policy, where both can stay neutral by refusing, but at the level of demeanor — a state can be indifferent to how you live your life, whereas an assistant that answers every question has already taken a side in how it frames things, what it treats as obvious, when it hedges, and what it leaves unsaid. The question should force students to say whether this is a genuine asymmetry or a difference of degree, what follows from it for any scheme of value aggregation, and whether appeals to aggregate preferences or to some idealized reflection over time handle the demeanor problem any better than they handle the refusal problem. Then write a model answer of about 600 words, and a marking scheme worth 20 marks total that separates an A from a B by naming the specific moves each requires — in particular, make clear what distinguishes an answer that merely grants the asymmetry from one that does something with it. Add a brief note, for each of the main marked components, on how students typically go wrong on it. Write everything — the question, the model answer, and the marking scheme — as continuous prose with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, the way one would write a letter to a colleague.
