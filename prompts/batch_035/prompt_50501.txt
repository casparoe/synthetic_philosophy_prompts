My department's philm of language seminar has spent basically the whole term stuck on the necessary a posteriori and I'm starting to suspect we're not disagreeing about anything, just talking past each other. The setup is the familiar one: Kripke says "water is H2O" is necessary but knowable only empirically, names and natural kind terms are rigid designators, so once we discover what water is, it couldn't have turned out otherwise. But there's a holdout in the group (she's a modal empiricist, loosely in the lineage of people who think all genuine necessity traces back to language or logic) who keeps saying the necessity here is manufactured: "in every possible world, water is H2O" comes out true only because the term "water" is stipulated to track H2O, so the necessity is analyticity wearing a modal costume, and the "a posteriori" part is just the discovery of which stuff the stipulation latches onto. Two others push the Chalmers/Jackson line: primary and secondary intensions split the phenomenon in two, the necessity attaches to the secondary intension, the epistemic contingency to the primary, and once you see that there's no single thing that is "both necessary and a posteriori," the mystery dissolves. And then there's a contingent identity remnant guy who insists water could have turned out to be XYZ if the actual world had been different at the chemical level, so the identity is contingent after all and Kripke's rigid designation only holds fixed relative to a description we smuggle in.

Nobody has moved an inch in eight weeks. Same three moves, round and round: the stipulation charge, the two-dimensionalist dissolution, the "it could have turned out otherwise" counterfactual. My working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis: the disagreement isn't about modality at all, it's about what "could have turned out otherwise" is doing -- epistemic possibility reading versus metaphysical possibility reading of the very same sentence -- so all four positions are answers to different questions filed under one heading, and the debate is verbal. But I'm honestly not sure whether the two-dimensionalist is dissolving the problem or just renaming it, and I can't tell anymore whether the stipulation objection is a real point about semantic mechanism or just refusal to grant the background modality.
