My friend and I got into a genuinely long argument over beers the other night about when someone actually counts as dead, and I've been replaying it since. He was super confident on the cases we covered: a ventilated patient with no brainstem reflexes, a flat EEG, and doctors confirming theres nothing left of the whole brain — dead. A guy found frozen in the snow with no pulse and no breathing, nobody even attempting resuscitation — dead (he even said it wouldn't matter that an hour of heroic effort might in principle restart the heart, since nobody's going to try). Someone in a permanent vegetative state who sleeps and wakes and coughs but will never be conscious again — alive. A baby born with only a brainstem and nothing above it — alive. Someone whose heart stopped 90 seconds ago with CPR in progress — alive. We agreed on those, mostly. Where it fell apart was two cases I threw at him and he refused to commit. Case one: a pregnant woman declared brain dead, kept on machines for four months, her body digesting food, spiking a fever when infected, holding its temperature, and the fetus delivered healthy at the end. Case two: same brain-dead patient, no fetus, but suppose we could keep the body going for years, wounds healing, growing if it's a kid. Dead the whole time, or was the original declaration somehow wrong? His gut says dead in both, but he says he can't justify that without making the brain sound like some magic on/off switch.

So: do you think he'd say the pregnant woman is dead? What I'm really after is the rule he seems to be running based on his five confident calls, the other rules that would fit those same five just as well, and what each of those rules says about my two cases. My hunch is alot of this turns on whether what makes you alive is the brain itself versus the body hanging together as one organism, but tell me if thats the wrong cut. And I'd want the further case you'd put to him to figure out which rule he's actually on. If you think I've set the whole thing up wrong — say the five verdicts don't really leave room for more than one rule, or my two cases secretly collapse into each other — please just say that plainly instead of playing along. I'd rather find out the argument was confused than get a tidy answer to a confused question.
