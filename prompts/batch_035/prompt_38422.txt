quick context before the ask: I write a small newsletter on science and ideas, mostly cognitive science and AI, and a piece I did last month on octopus problem-solving sent me down a rabbit hole that ended with Dennett's little book Kinds of Minds — the one with the four creatures, each named after a scientist. The taxonomy has been living in my head rent free ever since. Darwinian creatures are just hardwired: born with their one move, no way to revise it, variation only at the level of the species across generations. Skinnerian creatures can learn, but only by trial and error out in the world — they pay for their mistakes in flesh. Popperian creatures run the trials in their heads first; the idea, which Dennett credits to Popper, is that hypotheses die in our stead. And Gregorian creatures are the ones who get handed mind-tools — words, maps, notations — that don't just add to the thinking but restructure it. The nesting is the part I keep chewing on: each kind is supposed to contain the previous one, not replace it.

Here's the ask: I want to close out the year-end issue with a poem about this, and I don't want a poem that merely name-drops the four creatures — I want the poem to do the taxonomy, stanza by stanza. Four stanzas, maybe 4-5 lines each. The first stanza should be able to do only what a Darwinian creature can do: flat declaritives, no hypotheticals, no address to anyone, blind variation with nothing learned carried across lines. The second stanza has to get conditioned — some word or move from the first stanza repeats and only survives where it gets rewarded, so you can watch the stanza learn as it goes. The third stanza opens up into subjunctives and mights: trials run before commitment, something imagined and then discarded inside the stanza, never acted on. And the fourth stanza is the one that starts borrowing — second person, quotation, an actual tool or symbol used as a mind-tool — but it should only be able to do that because of what the earlier stanzas set up, the way the Gregorian creature needs the Popperian one underneath it.

Two more things. First, don't let each stanza be a fresh start: something has to carry forward from each to the next, because the containment is the whole point — a specialist who knows the taxonomy should be able to read the poem and see which stanza is which and see that the fourth still needs the first. Second, it still has to work as a poem for readers who've never heard of Dennett — I have plenty of diagrams in my drafts folder already, what I want is something that a non-philosopher subscriber would enjoy on a first read and that a philosopher of mind would nod at on a second one. If you can find a way to gesture at where octopuses or LLMs sit on this ladder without breaking the form, that would be a lovely bonus for the regulars, but don't force it.
