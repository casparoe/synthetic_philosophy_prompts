Student council elections at my school are decided by roughly a quarter of the student body, and the winners are almost always the same well-connected friend groups. Yet the council has real power: it decides how a share of the activities budget is spent and which new clubs receive funding. In my civics class, I have been arguing that we should instead pick council members by lottery from the student body — anyone willing to serve puts their name in, and we draw at random. My classmates treat this as a joke, and my teacher has set me a challenge: if I can defend it against the strongest objections, she will let me move it as a formal proposal at the next all-school assembly. Is there a genuinely serious case for selecting a governing body this way rather than by election, and if so, what exactly is it? And which objections should I worry about most — especially about whether randomly chosen students would be competent, and about whether a lottery is any less legitimate than an election most of the school does not bother to vote in? Please also be candid with me about which of my points I am likely to lose, so that I know where to concede rather than fight.
