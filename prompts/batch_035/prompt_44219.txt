my friend is a health economist and basically a utilitarian, and she has very fixed views on when interpersonal comparisons of welfare are actually possible. Her verdicts so far: your own mood today vs your own mood last week — trivially fine; two strangers who both filled in the same 0-10 questionnaire after the same operation — fine, the questionnaire is a shared ruler; a human's headache vs whatever a fish feels — no, the machienry is too alien to line up; judging two strangers' distress just from watching them, one who lost a wallet and one who lost a parent — no, behaviour is too culture-dependent to work as a scale. Next week two new cases come up. Case one: her sister is in a coma after an operation, and since her identical twin had the same operation and reported it as being like a moderate headache, the doctors expect the comatose sister's experience to be about the same — but she can't report anything herself. Case two: an octopus at the aquarium that's been trained to press a 1-7 lever for pain, which the keepers calibrated against actual tissue damage. Will she say we can compare the comatose sister's welfare with the twin's? And the octopus's with a human's? I'm less interested in the answers themselves than in the rule she seems to be running, what other rules fit her past verdicts just as well, and which of the two cases would best split those rules apart.
