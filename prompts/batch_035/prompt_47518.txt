I volunteer at a small regional museum, and I've been drafted into writing the panel text for a traveling show about the end of societies. It has three case studies: the Mycenaean palace world of the late Bronze Age, the Classic Maya cities, and Rapa Nui. The title is already printed on everything and it uses the word "collapse," which I can't change.

The problem is our two consulting archaeologists basically won't speak to eachother anymore. One wants the panels to say plainly that these societies collapsed: dynastic inscriptions in the Maya lowlands just stop, the palaces burn or empty out, record-keeping ends, and on Rapa Nui the population crashes hard. She says softening any of this is dishonest, visitors came for the dramatic truth so give it to them. The other one says "collapse" is doomsday framing: the Mycenaean world didn't vanish, people just stopped keeping tablets and moved to villages; millions of people speak Mayan languages today; the Rapa Nui are still there. In his view, calling these collapses tells visitors that whole peoples died when what mostly happened is political systems came apart and reorganized, and he wants the panels to talk about transformation instead. And honestly? Their both kind of right. The show opens in six weeks.

Is there a principled way through this that isn't just using the word "collapse" in half the panels and "transformation" in the other half? I want something that feels like it tracks what actually happened rather than just managing two egos.

Structure your answer in these sections, in this order, roughly this long: "What you're actually deciding" (~100 words), "Where your first consultant is right" (~150 words), "Where your second one is right" (~150 words), "A way to write the panels" (~200 words), "What to leave out" (~75 words).
