Ok so this came up at a sleepover last weekend (me and one friend arguing about it while everyone else had actually fallen asleep, which is kind of poetic given the topic): are dreams real experiences that happen to you while you're asleep, or are they maybe invented at the moment you wake up and report them? We kept trading gut verdicts on different cases and I want to know if there's actually one principle that captures all of them, because right now I'm pretty sure my own gut is contradicting itself. Here's the list. (1) Waking up from a nightmare about drowning, heart pounding, still able to remember the water and the panic in detail — that was an experience, definately, and it happened while I was asleep. (2) My mom telling me I mumbled "not the red boat, not the red boat" in my sleep, while I remember absolutely nothing — my gut says no experience there, just noises, even though the words sound meaningful. (3) A dream I remember clearly at breakfast but that's shrunk by lunch to just "something about an airport" — still an experience during sleep, just badly stored, like a photo that fades. (4) Waking up with the felt sense of having just concluded some long complicated dream, but zero content, only "it ended badly" — my gut says there was *something* it was like, even with no content, though I can't explain how that's possible. (5) The one my friend used against me: waking with a whole detailed narrative that feels like a memory of hours of dreaming — and he said, what if your brain just wrote that summary in the last half-second before waking and backdated it? My gut says the report could be genuine even if the composition happened at waking, which annoys him, because he thinks if that's allowed then no dream ever literally happened during sleep.

For context, I read in my english elective about a philosopher named Malcolm (book called Dreaming, way back, like 1959) who thought dream reports can't be checked against anything at all, so the whole idea of dreams as experiences during sleep is confused — if I'm remembering him right. That seems too strong to me, but I also can't fully answer my friend's half-second-composition story. And there's a sixth case that's the real thorn: my friend apparently narrated a chase dream out loud in his sleep, muttering "he's coming" over and over and then yelping — if someone had recorded it, that looks like the experience was happening right then, in real time, while asleep. But case 5 says the content might get composed at waking, and case 2 says meaningful-sounding sleep-talk with no memory behind it isn't an experience. I can't get all six to fit one story.

So that's what I'm asking for: is there a single principle about when a dream counts as an experience that captures these verdicts, or close? If more than one principle could do it, lay out the candidates. Please say what the principle predicts for cases I haven't thought of — like lucid dreams where you know you're dreaming and can steer them, or someone under anesthesia who reports a vivid "dream" afterward. And be straight with me about which of my six verdicts the principle just can't save, since I'd rather drop one verdict than glue together some monster principle with a clause per case. Also, since I'm a HS junior and not a philosopher, please tell me honestly what would change your answer — like which of my cases, if it went the other way, would flip your verdict, and where your reasoning is most likely to be wrong. If it turns out my memory of what Malcolm said is off, just correct me and go on.
