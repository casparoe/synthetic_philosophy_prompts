hey, I need a consistency check on my views before I embarrass myself at our department retreat next month. I'm a PhD student in development economics, and I've been deep in the effective-altruism cause-prioritization world for a few years now — I help run our program's giving circle, and somehow I keep ending up as the person defending the framework, so I'd like to know if my own version of it actually hangs together first.

Here's roughly where I stand, in order of how confident I am:

1. The importance / tractability / neglectedness framework is basically right. If you want to compare causes, you should be asking how big the problem is, how solvable it is, and how much attention/money it's already getting. I'd defend this over the alternatives I've seen people propose.

2. Neglectedness isn't a property of a cause, it's a property of the current landscape — who's funding what, who's working on what, right now. So ITN scores go stale, and any ranking more than a few years old is suspect until you re-run it.

3. AI safety is the top cause on the framework: huge scale once you count the far future, genuinely neglected, and at least somewhat tractable. I've argued this at the giving circle.

4. I'm really suspicous of arguments that multiply a tiny probability by an astronomical payoff. That's why I roll my eyes at people who arrive at climate change as a top cause via tail-risk stories, and honestly it's part of why my own donations go to malaria nets — I want robust evidence and small error bars, not expected value arithmetic on speculative numbers.

5. I think global health counts as neglected, even though health is one of the biggest spending categories on earth.

6. Personal fit can move you a full tier down the cause rankings. I told a friend with a hospital logistics background to take the pandemic-preparedness job she's excited about rather than retooling for AI policy, and I'd say the same to most people.

7. And I think the framework should be agent-neutral: run the numbers and you get the same ranking no matter whose career is on the line. If the answer changes depending on who's asking, something's gone wrong.

Individually these all seem fine to me, but I've been staring at them too long to tell anymore.
