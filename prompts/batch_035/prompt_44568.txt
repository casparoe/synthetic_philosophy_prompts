i'm a phd student working on alignment, more on the conceptual/theory side than the empirical side, and somewhere along the way i basically convinced myself that approval-directed agents — systems that, at every choice point, pick the action their (human) overseer would most approve of, rather than optimizing toward some fixed goal over long horizons — are just clearly the safer design compared to goal-directed ones. the pitch as i understand it: no instrumental convergence to power-seeking because there's no goal being pursued across time steps, no goodharting on a misspecified objective, the human stays in the loop at each act rather than handing over a utility function and hoping. i remember the original writeup (christiano, i think) being pretty upfront that this trades goal-directedness for a much heavier dependence on the overseer, but i mostly shrugged at that.

lately i've been trying to stress-test this. what would actually count against the view that approval-direction is safer and/or viable? i don't want the standard rehearsed worries, i want a ranked map of defeaters: which failures, arguments, or kinds of results would be genuinely decisive, which merely awkward, and which a committed defender would just shrug off — and specifically which ones the defenders themselves have conceded vs. which only critics push. some things i keep going back and forth on: whether step-by-step approval quietly collapses back into goal-directedness once the overseer starts approving actions *in order to accomplish something* (in which case what did we buy?); whether the safety advantage just gets relocated to 'the overseer is wise and uncorruptible', which seems like it might be worse to rely on; and whether the claim has been softened over the years from 'approval-direction avoids goal-directed pathologies' into something more like 'approval-direction helps to whatever degree the overseer is adequate', in which case i worry it's qualified into unfalsifiability. is that a fair worry or am i being too harsh? also curious what actual capability/competitiveness results would count as evidence — like, if approval-directed systems can't match goal-directed ones except when the overseer is itself superhuman, does that defeat the view or just reprice it? be honest about how exposed the position actually is.
