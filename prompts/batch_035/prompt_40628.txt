I'm teaching a philosophy of mind seminar and next week we pair a unit on Hofstadter's account of the self as a strange loop — the "I" as a symbol a representational system uses to refer to itself — with a screening of Being John Malkovich (1999). Give me a critical reading of the film through that lens, in exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion. Some tensions to weigh: the portal conceives of the self as an occupant that can be evicted from, or squat in, a body, which sounds Cartesian rather than Hofstadterian; the scene in which Malkovich goes through his own portal and finds a world composed entirely of Malkovich looks like the film's strange loop, but it may be a merely literal causal loop rather than the abstract level-crossing feedback Hofstadter has in mind; and the ending, with Craig permanently trapped inside Malkovich's head as a helpless spectator watching Lotte and Maxine's life through his eyes, risks dramatizing the very inner theater Hofstadter wants to dissolve. So which scenes genuinely support the Hofstadterian picture — Craig's puppeteering as a figure for a self that operates itself is one candidate — and where does the film resist the reading in a way an honest critic should concede?
