I've never studied biology past school, but I keep chewing on a question about what a species actually is, and I've gotten myself to a set of gut judgments that I can't turn into one rule. Here's what my gut says about concrete cases.

Herring gulls and lesser black-backed gulls where I live: two species — they don't interbreed, they look and act differently. But I've read that these gulls are connected around the pole by a chain of gull populations, each of which breeds with its neighbors, until the chain comes back around to these two. So somewhere between "one species circling the Arctic" and "two species in my backyard," my gut gives both verdicts at once and I can't make that stick.

Dogs and wolves: my gut says two species, or at least that "the dog" is a real distinct kind, even though they interbreed freely and have fertile offspring. Meanwhile, picture two populations of the same beetle split by a new river for a few thousand years — no interbreeding, no visible differences, and if the river dried up they'd mix back together with no fuss. My gut says one species there. So interbreeding can't be the whole story, since it comes out wrong in both directions: I split the interbreeders and lump the non-interbreeders.

Then there are two tree frogs I heard about that look identical to me but have different mating calls, and never mate across the call boundary: my gut firmly says two species, even though "looks like a distinct kind" fails here. And stick insects or bacteria that reproduce without mating at all, so no two of them ever interbreed: my gut still carves them into species — some of those asexual lineages feel like one species spanning a continent, others like several — and I have no idea what's doing the work.

My own tentative rule was something like: a species is a branch on the tree of life that has stopped sharing genes with its neighbors. But that wrongly merges my two beetles (no shared genes, still one species) and it gives the wrong answer about the asexual stick insects, where "sharing genes" never happens in the first place.
