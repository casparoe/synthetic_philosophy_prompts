i'm about 200k words into a novel and i've hit a wall on what was supposed to be a small character beat but is turning into the whole book. my protagonist, mira, is a water quality engineer for a mid-size rust belt city who figures out her department head has been doctoring lead test results for years. the way i've written her, she's not a rebel at all — she's the kind of person who reads the employee handbook cover to cover and feels genuinly bad about rolling a stop sign. what she ends up doing is quietly copying the real data onto a flash drive over the course of two months (while telling herself shes just "keeping backups") and then hand delivering it to a local reporter, which in my made-up state is a felony, misuse of official records or something like that — i'm being deliberately vague on the statute.

the problem is the scene after. my writing group is split three ways on what she does once the police actually show up. one camp wants her to plead guilty in open court and do the time with her mouth shut, no lawyer theatrics — they say that's the only version where she's still herself, that if she fights the charge or leans on a technicality or flees she becomes just another person breaking rules when its convenient, and the whole point of her was that she doesn't do that. the second camp thinks that's sentimentality, that accepting punishment is just the same deference that let the lead thing happen in the first place wearing a halo — submitting to consequences from the very state that poisoned its own kids is the *least* principled thing she could do, not the most. and the third camp says the framing itself is broken: she took a job, not a vow, she never agreed to any of this, so the question "what does she owe the law" already assumes something nobody ever established.

i keep going back and forth and it's bleeding into everything around it — like i can't write her interior voice until i know whether she thinks the law is owed something from her or not. so i guess what i'm asking is: is there an actual principled position on any of this or is it vibes? specifically, is there a real difference between "break this one law but accept the state's punishment for it, as a way of showing you're not just thumbing your nose at the whole system" and "you're getting crushed either way, so choose to be crushed and call the choosing a virtue"? and does it matter that she worked in secret for two months before going public — my one friend says that detail changes everything and another says it changes nothing and i honestly can't tell which. any help appreciated, even just a cleaner way to think about the fork, because right now every version of the scene feels like i'm stacking the deck.
