Quick background: I'm a science journalist, I mostly do features on physics and AI and whatever strange idea falls in between. I've got something I keep running into on the beat and I want to know if philosophy — or semiotics or linguistics or whatever the right field is — has an actual name for it.

When the Voyager team sent the golden record up, they etched instructions on the cover: here's how fast to spin it, here's the unit of time, this drawing is a hydrogen atom, etc. But those instructions were themselves a message that needed decoding, and the only reason a hypothetical alien could even start on them is that physics works the same out there and they'd already worked out some of it themselves. So the "how to read this" layer of a message never actually gets delivered by the message — it has to be bootstrapped off stuff the reciever brings to it. And there's an even earlier layer, which is recognizing the thing in your hands is a message at all and not just a decorated frisbee.

I hit the same shape reporting a totally different story. Deciphering Linear B basically only worked because the deciphers guessed the tablets were palace accounting records — a guess about what kind of thing you're holding that comes before any reading of it. And honestly my grandmother's recipe card is the same deal: half the real content is stuff she never wrote down because she assumed I'd grown up in her kitchen.

So here's what I want a name for: the observation that meaning isn't located in the artifact itself. It's split between the artifact and a package of assumptions the reader already has to be carrying, and some of those assumptions are precisely what tell the reader there's something to decode in the first place. A friend of mine said "that's just pragmatics" but from what I can tell pragmatics is about speaker intention and context of utterance — not about the bootstrapping problem of a reader with no shared code at all. And McLuhan's "the medium is the message" seems to be about the medium dominating the content, which is a different claim.

Does this have an established name? If yes, how exactly does it differ from its nearest neighbors — semiotics, pragmatics, that whole family — because I don't want to just be pointed at the closest-sounding label. If there's no established name, coin one and give me the definition plus some boundary cases: does a mathematical proof "mean the same" to every reader, or does it also ride on shared training? Does a database dump mean anything? Where I want you to be careful: my description might be off somewhere, and I'd like you to say where it's most likely wrong, and what would change your mind about the identification — like if a standard treatment puts the split somewhere other than where I put it, flag that instead of forcing my square peg into the nearest round hole.
