I run a little philosophy meetup at my local community center, like 10 people, mostly folks with no background, we meet for about 90 min once a month. Last month we got onto this old thought experiment from the islamic philosopher Avicenna (I think thats how its spelled), the "flying man" -- guy created out of nowhere, floating in the air, blindfolded basically, no limbs touching anything, no sound, and Avicenna says even that guy would still know he exists. The point being that your sense of yourself as a self doesn't come through the senses. My group found it easy to just shrug at ("sure, whatever, obviously he'd know he exists") and I want to make them actually feel the pull of it instead of nodding along.

So: design an activity for us. My rough idea is some kind of staged version -- blindfolds, earplugs, maybe people holding still in the dark, and having them report in the moment what they can and can't affirm about themselves without checking anything with their senses. But I'm open to better structures. The tricky bit I keep running into: whatever we do, people will still have their inner ear, their sense of where their limbs are, memory of walking into the room, etc., so it's never really the flying man's situation. So the activity needs some way of getting at the difference between what they can affirm with senses blocked versus what they can affirm with NO input at all, or at least a debrief that faces that gap honestly instead of pretending we recreated it.

Please include: what I need to bring, what people actually do step by step, roughly how many minutes each part, and a debrief plan that connects what they experienced back to the argument (and to where the argument might be weak -- a couple of my guys are skeptics and I want ammo for them too). Also tell me what will go wrong the first time I run this, I've had activities die before because of stuff I should've seen coming. One of my members is really into meditation and I'm sure she'll say this is just mindfulness with extra steps -- if theres a good answer to that, include it.
