I teach an upper-level seminar on Wittgenstein's On Certainty, and every year I get finals that say hinges are lived rather than believed, wave at the regress argument, and stop there. Write me one essay question (90-minute answer) built around this case I want to use: a community whose form of life treats written records as systematically unreliable — for them it is simply beyond doubt that nothing on paper can be trusted, and the whole weight of their inquiry rests on face-to-face testimony and memory instead. Students have to say whether that community's hinge can be false while remaining a hinge, whether its immunity from doubt is the same in kind as our treating the earth having existed for many years as beyond question, and what follows for the claim that hinge certainty is non-epistemic. The question should force them to take a stand on whether the regress argument (justifications run out somewhere and end in acting) by itself licenses hinge immunity, or whether it only describes our psychology.

Along with the question, give a model answer of roughly 600 words and a marking scheme that seperates an A from a B by naming the specifc moves each must contain — e.g. an A answer should say something about what happens when the two communities meet and try to translate each other's doubts into their own terms, and whether hinge status survives translation or gets lost in it, while a B answer gets the role-vs-content distinction right but fudges whether hinges are truth-apt at all.
