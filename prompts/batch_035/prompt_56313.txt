I keep going around in circles on weakness of will and I'd like someone to audit the whole package, because I'm pretty sure somethings inconsistent in there but I genuinely cant see it myself. Where I stand: (1) akrasia is real and irrational — you can, at the very moment of acting, judge that the worse option is worse and do it anyway. (2) Temptation is hyperbolic discounting: the discount curve is hyperbolic rather than exponential, so preferences cross — from a distance the larger-later option wins, but as the smaller-sooner option gets close it takes over. (3) I'm a naturalist about the mental side of this: I dont believe in a judgment faculty hovering above the motivations and issuing verdicts they can defy. The "I shouldn't" running through the dieter's head while she orders the cake is itself just another motivational state shaped by the same curve, not a tribunal sitting above the others. (4) I firmly reject the successive-selves move — the Ainslie-ish picture where the 10pm self and the 6am self are quasi-separate agents doing intertemporal bargaining. It's one person all through the night; the bargaining talk is a metaphor. (5) On blame: akratic people are responsible in the ordinary way, and I resist the slide toward treating them like addicts, which I'd count as compulsion-adjacent. (6) Two case verdicts I'm fairly confident about: the guy who at midnight, full of resolve, sets a 6am alarm to go running and then stays in bed at 6 — I say the akratic one there is the midnight planner, not the one staying in bed. And the dieter who orders the cake while saying "I shouldn't" is akratic, but the smoker who lights up without a thought while scrolling his phone is not — that's just habit, there was no judgment around to lose to. So: does this hang together? Tell me which pairs are actually in tension and which only look that way, rank the real ones by severity, and for each say what would have to give and what the fix costs. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most — I cant tell whether the trouble is in the naturalism, the one-person claim, or one of the two verdicts, and I'd rather know than be flattered.
