I'm on Street's side of that debate, though I'll admit I've moved to my own stronger version: I call it the twin-lineages test. Imagine two species like ours, evolved under identical selection pressures but with inverted evaluative starting points -- the one with our altruistic tendencies toward kin, the other with, say, a flagellant streak that happens to be equally fitness-enhancing. Now give both species ten thousand years of rational reflection. If reflection washes out the difference and they converge, my realist colleague says the evolutionary origin becomes irrelevant. If the difference persists, I win: it shows reasoning itself is contaminated by its starting points.
