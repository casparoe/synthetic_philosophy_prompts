so I'm teaching a seminar session on guilt vs shame — which is the better moral emotion, and can a culture actually choose — and before I do I want a consistency check on my own package, because I have a feeling something in here doesnt fit but I can't put my finger on what. Here's roughly where I stand:

(1) Guilt is the better moral emotion for a modern society: it attaches to acts rather than the whole self — "I did a bad thing" instead of "I am bad" — so it pushes toward repair rather than hiding, and it works without an audience.

(2) Shame is structurally an audience emotion: it needs a real or imagined witness, and it tracks whatever that witness happens to despise. Thats why a shame-run morality can't criticize its own community's values from the inside — it just registers falling short of whatever the local audience happens to value.

(3) But I also buy the Williams-style point that the shame-audience gets internalized and endorsed: the imagined spectator is someone whose respect you have reason to want, and seeing yourself through their eyes can be a form of self-knowledge, so shame isn't just fear of getting caught.

(4) On the history side, I accept the standard story that confessional Christianity deliberately moved Europe from shame toward guilt — the church installing an invisible, all-seeing witness plus a rulebook — and I want to use that as my proof that cultures can choose their moral emotions.

(5) And I want to say liberal societies should finish the job: parenting and schools should teach kids to feel bad about specific acts, not about themselves — "that was a bad thing to do," never "what is wrong with you" — so we get repair-oriented guilt and starve out the shame pathologies: hiding, blame-shifting, the fixed bad self. I know people say guilt has its own pathologies, like scrupulosity and confessing the same sin every week, but I figure thats just shame leaking back in.

(6) One more: when someone gives an apology that actually lands, I think what's doing the work is shame, not guilt — guilt only registers that a rule got broken, but really acknowledging you hurt someone seems to require seeing yourself as someone who could do that, which is shame's territory.

Go through the pairs and tell me which are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank the real tensions by severity instead of treating them all the same, and for each one say what would have to give and what it costs.
