I'm a novelist four years into a book that follows one woman across four decades, from an ordinarily kind young teacher to someone who has quietly ruined lives, and I've argued myself into a corner about what my own story can claim. Here is the argument. (1) No single act in my draft makes her cruel: taken alone, each is the kind of thing a decent person does on a bad day. (2) If she is not yet cruel on a given day, and the next day contains only acts of that same trivial kind, she is not cruel on the next day either. (3) Character is nothing over and above the sum of the days: whatever holds of her at every stage holds of her, period. (4) So she is never cruel, at no point in the forty years, right through the ending. (5) Yet the novel only works if the reader can say, without hesitation, that by the end she has become cruel — and I trust that judgment more than I trust the argument. Which step should I give up, and what does giving it up cost me as a writer? Rejecting (2) means cruelty arrives full-blown at some point, and I lose the slow ruin that is the whole point of the book; rejecting (3) means she can be cruel though no day or deed makes her so, and I don't know what a narrator could even be describing when she says it. I also want to know whether the standard responses to sorites reasoning — treating "cruel" as a predicate with borderline cases, saying the person is cruel on some sharpenings and not others — give a novelist anything usable, or merely relabel the problem: is there a point in my manuscript where the supervaluationist's verdict would be "neither cruel nor not cruel," and is there any fact of the matter for my narrator to track? One thing I suspect is on my side: unlike grains of sand, a novel controls its own comparison class — the company a character keeps, the judgments other characters voice, the traits she is contrasted against. Could an author deliberately sharpen the context enough to defeat the reasoning, or is that just hiding the slip inside the rhetoric? Tell me where the argument goes wrong, if it does, and what I pay either way.
