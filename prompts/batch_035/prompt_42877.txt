For some years now my friend Miranda and I have tested moral arguments on our Sunday walks. She has never read a page of philosophy and could not name a single thinker behind any of it, but she has firm, settled instincts about which arguments hold and which do not. Her verdicts on the arguments I have put to her so far:

- Intensive farming causes animals to suffer, so intensive farming is wrong. Miranda: this holds.
- Helping people is good, you could help here at no cost to yourself, so you should help here. Miranda: holds.
- Most people in the country approve of our eating habits, so eating animals is fine. Miranda: does not hold — what a crowd happens to think settles nothing.
- Altruism spread because it helped our ancestors survive, so altruism is good. Miranda: does not hold — being natural earns nothing.
- "Wrong" simply means "against the law", this act is legal, so it is not wrong. Miranda: does not hold, and she found the argument almost insulting.
- An injection causes the baby a moment of pain, so you should not vaccinate the baby. Miranda: does not hold — momentary pain against lasting health is no kind of argument.

I have noticed she often says you cannot squeeze a "should" out of a plain fact, yet she let the farming argument through without the missing step written down, and I cannot make the two sit together. Next week I want to put to her: "A virtue" just means a character trait that helps its community to flourish; honesty helps our community to flourish; so honesty is a virtue. I genuinely cannot predict what she will say. Give me exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two: your prediction of her verdict; the principle she seems to have been applying all along, stated plainly; a second principle that fits her every verdict so far equally well but points the other way on the new one; which prediction you would bet on, and why; and the single further argument I could put to her, alongside the flourishing one, that would show which principle is really hers. No preamble and no closing remarks.
