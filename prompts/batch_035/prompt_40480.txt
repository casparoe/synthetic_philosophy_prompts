Presentism vs eternalism has me stuck. The debate's been going in circles for a century: the thank-goodness-it's-over argument, the relativity argument (no privileged frame, so no objective present), presentists replying with a hidden preferred frame or by insisting only present things exist, full stop. And yet nobody switches sides. What gets me is that the two camps agree on all of physics and on every everyday claim — 'there are no dinosaurs' and 'dinosaurs existed' both come out true on each theory once you get the tenses right, so each side can mimic the other's language. So what kind of disagreement is this actually? Is it verbal, just a fight over whether 'exists' is tensed? Is it a real metaphysical question about which quantifier carves nature at the joints? Or is it bedrock intuitions about whether time genuinely passes, with the existence talk doing the work of intuitions nobody can argue for? My hunch is the last one, and that nothing could ever settle it. Diagnose it, and tell me honestly whether anything — a new argument, a conceptual distinction, whatever — would count as progress, or whether I should just accept this one never ends.
