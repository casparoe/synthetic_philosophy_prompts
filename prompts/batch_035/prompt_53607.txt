I am a second-year PhD student in philosophy with a background in philosophy of biology, and I need to settle on a dissertation topic concerning free-floating rationales — Dennett's term for the reasons an organism, organ, or artifact has for being designed as it is, reasons that explain why it is as it is even though nothing anywhere represents or appreciates those reasons. The literature around this notion is thin, so I need a defended agenda more than a survey. Which open questions in this area are most worth three years of my effort, and in what order? The candidates I am aware of: (1) whether free-floating rationales require a selection history at all, or whether self-organizing, self-maintaining systems — termite mounds, immune repertoires, autocatalytic cycles — can have reasons without anyone's having selected them; (2) whether artifact functions, social roles, and institutional purposes are a continuous extension of evolutionary rationales or a disanalogy that breaks the unified story; (3) whether a collectivity such as a firm or a state can have beliefs and intentions in the same free-floating way, and what that would do to the interpretationism that motivates the notion in the first place; and (4) whether free-floating rationales are genuinely explanatory or merely a metaphor that compresses a selection story without adding anything to it. For each question you prioritize, explain what other work it is upstream of, how tractable it is for someone with my profile (strong in biology, light on formal logic), how neglected it currently is, and what a successful answer would look like. Please also flag any candidate you would deprioritize as churning, identify any important question I have not listed, and say what developments over the next few years would move an item up or down the ranking. Write your answer in American English.
