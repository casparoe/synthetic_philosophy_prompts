I've been arguing with my roommate about the extended mind thing all week and I want to try the case-by-case route instead of trading quotes from Clark and Chalmers at each other. Here are my gut verdicts, for what they're worth. Otto with his notebook: yes, that's his memory, I buy the classic case. A blind person's cane for spatial awareness: yes, it feels like part of her, not a tool she consults. Me and my phone for directions: I honestly can't navigate without it anymore, I feel cognitively naked when the battery dies — but my gut says no, its still a tool, and I can't articulate why it's different from Otto. A student allowed a formula sheet in a physics exam: no, the sheet is doing memory work, but it's obviously not part of his cognition. Except the formula sheet has basically the same profile as Otto's notebook — consulted automatically, trusted, relied on for years — so one part of me wants to say yes and another says no, and I flip flop depending on the day. A pianist reading sheet music in performance: no, the score isn't part of his musical mind, even though it seems to satisfy whatever criteria saved the cane. And autocorrect: I've offloaded so much spelling that I struggle unassisted now, and I half-think the spellchecker is part of my writing process, which is either a chilling confession about me or just trivially true of everyone.

So: is there a single principle that gets all of these, or close?
