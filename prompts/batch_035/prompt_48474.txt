I've been reading LessWrong since the Sequences were still being serialized, and for the past several months my local meetup has been doing a slow reading of the logical induction framework — the one where a bounded reasoner's beliefs about mathematics are modeled as a market's prices on sentences, updated one day at a time, with the central guarantee that no efficiently computable trader can exploit that market over time. As the final session's designated closer, I've decided I want to end with a poem rather than a summary, and I'd like it to be a villanelle.

The two refrains should carry the two halves of the result that I've never quite managed to hold in my head at once. One refrain belongs to the incompleteness side: no computable procedure settles every sentence, the door Gödel shut stays shut, and so the reasoner cannot simply know — every sentence gets a price, never a verdict it can rely on in advance. The other refrain belongs to the market side: the guarantee on offer is not knowledge but unexploitability, no trader profits in the limit, prices that a bounded adversary can't Dutch-book even while the reasoner is still wrong about almost everything. The two lines should pass each other in the tercets the way the market passes the truth without catching it — probabilities drifting toward 1 on the provable, toward 0 on the refutable, converging only in a limit where no finite reasoner actually lives.

Here is the part I care about most, and where I want the form to do the philosophical work rather than decorate the content. A villanelle's refrain returns verbatim, stanza after stanza, surrounded by different lines each time — which is exactly the situation of a mathematical sentence under logical induction: the sentence stays fixed while its price changes with each day's trading. So the refrains should read differently every time they come back, their sense shifting with the stanzas around them even though the words never change. The final stanza, where the two refrains meet side by side for the first time, should land the thought I actually want the group to leave with: unexploitability is not knowledge, but it may be what a bounded reasoner gets instead, and it is enough for the prices to converge from inside a system whose whole the reasoner can never survey. Someone who has read the framework should be able to tell which refrain is which and see that the rhyme scheme is doing the theorem's work; someone who hasn't should simply get a good poem about a market that learns without ever being told. Please make the refrains metrically strong — they will each be repeated five times, so they have to bear the repetition rather than collapse under it.
