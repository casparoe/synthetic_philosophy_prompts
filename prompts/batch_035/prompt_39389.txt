I'm a philosophy MA student trying to build a "which theory of pleasure do you actually hold?" quiz for the intro ethics class I TA, because our section on hedonism keeps collapsing into "pleasure = feeling good" and nobody notices there are like five genuinely different views hiding under that phrase. The quiz is meant to sort students by how they react to cases, not by which label they like, so no question can just be "do you think pleasure is a feeling?" — that's leading.

Here's what I want. Maybe 10-12 questions, each one a concrete case or scenario with 2-4 answer options, plus a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. The positions I need as leaves, roughly: (1) the homogeneous feeling theory — all pleasures share one distinctive felt quality, like a common "hedonic tone"; (2) the heterogeneous feeling theory — pleasures are felt but have nothing phenomenal in common, they're unified some other way (supervenience on overall experience quality is one way to cash this out); (3) attitudinal theories, Feldman-style — pleasure is a propositional attitude of enjoying/taking pleasure in something, not a sensation, so you can have attitudinal pleasure without any distinctive feeling; (4) "way of feeling" theories, where pleasure isn't a component of experience but something like a manner or mode of it (the Ayer-ish move where enjoying music isn't having an extra feeling alongside the music); (5) motivational/dispositional accounts where pleasure is whatever tends to keep you doing what you're doing or draws you back to it; and (6) the deflationary "no unifying property" option — pleasures really do have nothing interesting in common, and the student has to say what happens to hedonism about well-being if that's true.

Cases the quiz has to use, because they're where my students actually split: masochism (the masochist in pain — pleasure, pain, both, or neither?); the deceived businessman-style case where someone enjoys something based on a false belief (does the false belief cancel the pleasure?); a runner's "flow" or a mathematician deep in a proof where the person reports being absorbed but can't locate any warm feeling (counted as pleasure by which theories?); the contrast between sensory pleasure like a hot shower and attitudinal pleasure like being pleased that your rival got what was coming to him; someone on a pleasure drug that just dumps in the good feeling without any object or activity; and the anticipation case where the looking-forward seems good in itself right now.

For the scoring key, please don't just assign one answer per position. Real people will mix answers, so the key should say which combinations point where, and flag which pairs of leaves the quiz can't actually separate — e.g. I suspect the homogeneous vs heterogeneous feeling theories only come apart on a question about whether a futur philosopher could in principle mistake a pleasure for a pain, and I'm not sure I can phrase that question so a non-philosopher can answer it, so if you can do better than that, please do. Also, if some combination of answers corresponds to no real position anyone defends, say so and say whether that's because the combination is incoherent, unappealing, or just unexplored.

One more thing: the quiz is for students who haven't read anything, so every question has to be answerable from intuitions about the case alone, and the answer options should be written so a student can't tell which theory they're picking. Assume they know nothing about Feldman or Ayer or whoever. Deliverable is the questions, the answer options, and the scoring key with the leaf descriptions — one or two sentences per leaf about its most awkward commitment would be great, e.g. the attitude theorist having to say something about whether you can take attitudinal pleasure in something while wishing you weren't.
