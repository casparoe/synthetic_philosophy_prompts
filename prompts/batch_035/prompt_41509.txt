Is there a name for the following pattern? I keep a dream journal, and what strikes me, months of entries in, is this: on waking I usually have only a residue — a mood, a single image, the sensation of falling, a conviction that *something* happened — and then, as I write the entry, the details arrive one by one, some apparently remembered, others visibly invented to fit, and I cannot tell which is which. Occasionally the story takes a turn mid-sentence that surprises me, and rereading an entry weeks later, it can seem to describe a different dream than the one I recall having had. My tentative explanation is that the narrative is largely composed at the moment of reporting, around a kernel of something genuinely experienced just before waking. I know of confabulation and false memory, but as I understand them those involve reports that misrepresent a past event; what I am asking about is the stronger idea that the reported event itself — the dream as told — comes into existence only at the telling, so that the report is, in a sense, accurate about something that had no earlier existence. Two questions, then. First, does this idea have an established name in philosophy or psychology, and who has defended or attacked it? Second, does anyone defend the mixed version I find myself holding — that some affective residue predates waking while the narrative is composed afterwards — as opposed to the two extremes that either all or none of the dream was experienced during sleep? If nothing established covers it, coin a term: define it precisely enough to distinguish it from confabulation, from ordinary memory distortion, and from the view that dreams are not experiences at all, and describe a boundary case in which it is genuinely indeterminate whether the concept applies.
