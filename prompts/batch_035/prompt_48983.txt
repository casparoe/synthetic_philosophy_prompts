so I've been on a real demandingness-objection kick lately (blame Scheffler) and I want satire about it, not another essay. Write me an internal HR document: a policy addendum, numbered clauses, around 700 words, from a mid-sized company that has to be plausible but made up — some logistics firm or regional insurance company — announcing that the company has formally adopted act consequentialism as its official ethical framework, effective next quarter.

The humor has to come from the actual philosophy. Jokes like "everyone now has to donate their salary" would be lazy; instead, the memo should quietly take positions on the demandingness debate without ever naming it as a debate. Beats I want covered, roughly one clause each:

- some kind of agent-centred prerogative implemented as a leave/allowance program, whose fine print claws the allowance back whenever the marginal benefit of your doing something else is higher — so it exists on paper and is never usable, which I suspect is the real question about whether prerogatives can be made stable
- an "integrity and personal projects" section that seems protective but has a carve-out any time the numbers go the other way
- the doing/allowing distinction quietly collapsing: e.g., a clause noting that inactivity is a decision and will be logged as such
- aggregation done straight: employee wellbeing on one side, distant strangers on the other, weighed directly, in a wellness-benefits clause no less
- a note that the old "satisficing" and "good enough" performance tiers have been discontinued; all employees are now scored on a single ranked leaderboard with no passing grade
- accrued "moral holiday" PTO that vests only under conditions the reader can see never obtain
- a workplace accomodation clause for the one guy whose capacity for utility keeps eating everyone else's (you know the type)

Then a short FAQ at the end, three or four entries, written in that placating HR tone. One FAQ question should be some version of "can I just go home" and the answer should be policy-compliant.

Constraints: deadpan, no winking at the reader. A reader who knows the demandingness literature should feel the actual worry — that the framework leaves no room for a life of your own — without the memo ever admitting there's a problem, and should also think the satire is fair, i.e. you're only exaggerating real features of the view, not inventing dumb ones. Invent any spokespeople or job titles you need, but don't name actual philosophers or living people, and don't cite anything — just make the content right. The company should sound genuinely proud of the whole thing. Maybe a closing line about the policy being subject to revision if the numbers change, which, you know.
