Been mulling the nonidentity problem ever since our last population ethics thread derailed into total vs average utilitarianism for the millionth time, and I want to make a quiz for my blog that sorts readers by what they actually believe about the cases, not which label they like. Eight or so questions, each one a concrete scenario: a nation adopting a depletion policy whose effects only fall on people who exist because of it, a couple who could wait a month and conceive a healthy kid but conceive now and get one with a serious condition, a teenager who has a kid with a grim-but-barely-worth-living life when waiting would've meant a different, better-off child, and a child conceived partly to be an organ donor for a sibling even though their own life goes fine. Multiple choice answers, scoring key at the end mapping patterns onto the main positions: strict person-affecting (no one is worse off so nothing wrong), wide person-affecting (compare against the other kids who could've existed), impersonal totalist, the 'harmless wrongdoings' view where something can be wrong even tho nobody is wronged, Benatar-style views about whether existence can be a benefit or harm at all, and the 'essential historical facts' idea that what matters is HOW the person was created rather than their welfare level. No question should just be 'do future people have rights?', people should be able to answer from their gut on the cases. Also please flag which pairs of positions the quiz can't really separate — my hunch is harmless wrongdoings and the historical facts view collapse together on most of these, and I'd rather know than pretend its clean.
