Is there a name for this thing? I'm a backend developer, no philosophy or psych background, so forgive me if this is textbook stuff. I keep noticing a pattern when people solve a new problem by analogy to an old one, and the pattern isn't the analogy itself — it's what happens to the concepts on either side of it. Example from work: we ported a rate limiter from one service to another, and someone on the team started calling part of the new one "the scheduler" even though it doesn't really schedule anything. But the name stuck, and because of it a bunch of intuitions from thread schedulers transfered over — people now reason about "starvation" and "fairness" for a component that has no threads and no queue in the usual sense. The concept bent to fit the mapping, and after a few months it's hard to remember it was ever a stretch. I get the same feeling doing letter puzzles with my daughter. "ABC is to ABD as IJK is to what?" is easy — IJL, take the successor of the last letter. But "ABC is to ABD as XYZ is to what?" caused a genuine family argument, because to answer it at all you have to quietly decide what "successor of Z" means: wrap around to A (giving XYA)? say the rule just dies at the end of the alphabet and do something else entirely? Different choices about what the concept means produce different completions, and the interesting part was watching everyone redefine "next letter" mid-argument without noticing they were doing it.

So the phenomenon I want a name for: when a mapping between two situations is doing real work, the concepts being mapped don't stay fixed with the mapping discovered among them — the concepts themselves get stretched or redefined to make the mapping go through, and once it does, the stretch gets absorbed until it looks like it was there all along. It's not plain analogical reasoning as far as I can tell, since there the concepts seem to stay put and only the correspondences are new, and it's not metaphor, because metaphors don't have to be *correct* about anything and nobody builds systems off them. My sister did a cog-sci degree years ago and just said something hand-wavy about how "everything is analogy," which was no help at all. Is there an actual established concept or literature for this — concepts bending to serve a mapping, rather than the mapping being assembled out of concepts that were already fixed? If there is, how exactly is it different from ordinary analogy-making? And if there's no settled term, could you coin one and give a definition sharp enough that I can tell whether our "scheduler" case counts as an instance, or whether that was just sloppy naming that happened to work out?
