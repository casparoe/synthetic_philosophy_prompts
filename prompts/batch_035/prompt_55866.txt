First session is saturday and I have to give the opening remarks. The room will be about fifty drafted residents, half of whom seem actively annoyed they got the letter at all, plus a couple of local councillors who lost this decision to the lottery and will be sitting at the back hoping we faceplant. Heres the hard part: the only real argument for why this assembly gets to decide anything is that we're a random cross-section of the city — not that any of us has expertise, and not that anyone voted for us. And even that argument has an obvious hole, because the people who actually bother showing up saturday won't be random anymore (everyone who chucked the letter in the bin already filtered themselves out), so I can't just wave that away. Write me the remarks, maybe five minutes read aloud. They need to sell the lottery itself as the source of legitimacy, be honest about the who-shows-up problem, and not lose the grumpy draftees or hand the councillors a stick to beat us with. Also please write it in British English — I'm in Bristol.
