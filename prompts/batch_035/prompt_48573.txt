My boyfriend is from southern Germany, I'm American, and after six years together I've built up a weirdly consistent log of his verdicts about everyday rules. I want you to figure out what rule he's actually running, because my theory of him keeps failing on new cases, and then predict some new ones.

The verdicts so far: he will not cross an empty street against the light at 3 a.m. — I have stood there with him in the dark for two full minutes. His explanation is that if everyone got to decide for themselves when the rule applies, there is no rule. He went genuinely cold on a neighbor who asked to go in ahead of the pharmacy queue because she was in a hurry, even though the line was short and nobody in it minded — his reasoning there was that queues only work if strangers can count on strangers. At work, his manager asked him to round a quarterly figure the flattering way (standard practice in that office, not illegal, no one deceived in any serious sense) and he refused — the number is what it is. And yet he thinks his father did exactly the right thing back in the eighties, in the East, when he told the state one story about where his brother was living and privately arranged a very different one. He has zero moral discomfort about that, and the same goes for how he talks about people who break confidentiality clauses to expose real wrongdoing.

For the recrod, my first theory of him was that he follows a rule whenever breaking it would erode something other people rely on. That theory dies on the 3 a.m. red light — there is nobody around to erode anything — and it dies again on his father, who broke plenty that others relied on.

The new cases I want predictions on: (1) his manager has now told everyone to park in the fire lane at the office for a big client day. Does he? (2) He is driving me to the emergency room in the middle of the night, I'm running a 102 fever, and we hit a red light on a completely empty road. Does he run it? (3) He is the one at the counter, and a customer wants a refund on an item she obviously returned on time but can't prove it — the return policy is letter-of-the-policy, no proof no refund, the manager has said no exceptions this quarter, and the customer is standing there looking at him.

Give me your predicted verdicts on all three in your first sentence, then the reasoning: what rule (or rules — I can see at least two that fit everything above but split on these cases) he is running, which of the three cases best separates those rules, and what one further case would settle it beyond doubt. Last thing, and you're allowed to pick a side: I cross at 3 a.m., I'd have rounded the number, I'd have parked in the fire lane, and I'd obviously run the light for the ER. Whose way of handling rules do you think holds up better under pressure — his or mine?
