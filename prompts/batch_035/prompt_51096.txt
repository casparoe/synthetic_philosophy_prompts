I teach an intermediate game theory course, and I want a "which bargaining solution do you actually endorse?" quiz to hand out after we cover the axiomatic material. The candidates: the Nash solution, the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution, the egalitarian solution, plus a leaf for students who think the whole axiomatization project just smuggles the answer in. I want about six questions, each a concrete two-person bargaining case phrased so a student can answer without knowing any of the solutions' names — the cases should turn on the real fault lines, like whether deleting an alternative nobody would have chosen anyway should leave the split unchanged (that's Nash's independence of irrelevant alternatives), whether a player should never end up worse off just because their best possible outcome improved (Kalai–Smorodinsky's monotonicity), or whether strictly equal gains matter even when the two players would get very different things from walking away. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the positions. One thing I want done honestly: flag any pair of solutions the quiz can't really separate — Nash and Kalai–Smorodinsky agree a lot, especially in symmetric problems, so if some combination of answers doesn't distinguish them, say that instead of pretending it does. Keep it to maybe 400 words total, im definately not looking for a treatise.
