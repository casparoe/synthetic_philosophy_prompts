Quick one for you. I'm teaching a 300-level metaphysics seminar this fall and the personal identity unit ends with mind uploading, which the students always think they have opinions about until you press them. I want a decision-tree handout that sorts them by what they actually believe about survival-through-substrate-change, not by which label they like the sound of. If a student answers "patternism" on a quiz I've learned nothing; if the quiz tells me they're a patternist because of how they answer five concrete cases, that's a teaching instrument.

Build the tree from the cases first, then locate positions in the space, in that order — I don't want the standard encyclopedia arrangement. The nodes should be questions a student can answer without knowing which named position it feeds into, and each node should be a concrete scenario rather than an abstract thesis. The cases I want in there somewhere, because they do the real splitting work:

- Destructive scan-and-instantiate (original brain destroyed in the process, emulated person wakes up with the question "did anyone wake up?")
- The same scan, but the original wakes up too, and lives for another twenty years before dying
- Gradual replacement: neurons swapped for functionally identical silicon one at a time over months, in situ, no interruption of consciousness
- Gradual replacement where the swapped-out neurons aren't destroyed but uploaded to run in parallel as the process goes on — so by the end you have two continuous histories where there was one
- Pause and resume: the whole emulation is frozen for a decade, then restarted bit-for-bit; also the version where it's frozen mid-thought and restored from an hour-old backup after a crash (nobody thinks the crash victim "died" when their laptop rebooted — why is this different, or is it?)
- Partial upload: only enough of you to run, missing some memories, or with huge gaps in the life-narrative

The leaves should include at least: the strict pattern-identity view (right arrangement, right memories, doesn't matter what it's made of); the biological or animalist view (you go where your organism goes, so every one of these is a death plus something else); the continuity-of-process view that says uploading is you only if done gradually and in place; the branching-sensitive views; and the Parfitian deflationist who says identity is the wrong concept to bring to any of this and what matters is psychological connectedness whether or not it's identity. If you think there's a leaf I'm missing that the logical space demands — like the dualist-survivalist position or the view that what matters is the continuity of consciousness specifically rather than psychology — put it in and tell me why the cases force it.

Two things I care about for the pedagogy. First: mark any two leaves the tree genuinely can't separate — my suspicion is the pattern-identity and Parfitian-deflationist positions agree on almost every case and only come apart somewhere in the branching scenarios, and I want that flagged rather than papered over. Second: note the unoccupied combinations of answers, if there are any — answer patterns no live position endorses — and say whether nobody's there because the view is incoherent, or just unattractive, or honestly just unexplored. That last category is what I want to bait my best student with for their term paper.

The audience is undergrads who've read some Parfit but no fancy philosophy of mind, so the node questions should be phrased so a smart sophomore answers them from gut intuition about cases, not jargon.

And one thing — if you think the tree format is the wrong instrument for this topic, say so plainly instead of building me a bad tree. I half suspect the branching cases make clean binary trees lie, since a student's intuitions there might be principled but non-binary, and if that's right I'd rather hear it now than find out when a student writes "the flowchart broke" on their course eval. Also push back if you think any of my cases smuggle in an assumption — I wrote the parallel-upload one myself and I'm not sure it's even coherent.

Length is whatever the tree needs; the handout is two pages front and back, so don't pad. No bibliography, I'll graft citations in later.
