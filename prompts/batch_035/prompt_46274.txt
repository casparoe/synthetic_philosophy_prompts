Before I commit to defending a broadly relationist view of space in my dissertation, I need to know exactly what such a view owes me. Write the desiderata: what any adequate relationist theory of motion must deliver, with the case or consideration motivating each requirement. At minimum, the list should say something about how the theory handles Newton's bucket and the two-globes thought experiment, whether it can distinguish uniform velocity from rotation/acceleration without positing anything absolute, what it should say about a bucket spinning in an otherwise empty universe (does the water climb the sides, and what fact could make it so?), how it should relate to the observation that Newtonian mechanics has Galilean rather than full Leibnizian symmetry, and whether it must extend to a relational treatment of relativistic spacetime or may treat that as a separate fight. Distinguish non-negotiable requirements from ones that are really preferences of one relationist faction (e.g., Machians demanding that all inertial structure supervene on matter distribution, versus more permissive relationists allowing relational laws without relational ontology). Then flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied — that possibility is the part I most want to see — and say which single requirement does the most to eliminate candidate theories. A one-paragraph scorecard of Leibniz-style primitive relationism and Machian relational dynamics against the list is welcome but optional; keep the weight on the criteria themselves.
