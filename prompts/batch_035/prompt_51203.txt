My grad seminar has two people who've been sniping at each other for years and have finally agreed to an adversarial collaboration — I've been tasked with drafting the joint statement and I'd like you to actually write the thing. One of them still works with the criteria of authenticity (multiple attestation, embarrassment, dissimilarity, coherence, Aramaic traces, historical plausibility) and thinks the quest for the historical Jesus is a perfectly live research program that just needs the criteria applied carefully. The other thinks the whole criteria enterprise collapsed over the last couple decades — it's circular (the criteria smuggle in a picture of who Jesus was, then "find" it in the sources), dissimilarity in particular can only recover a Jesus who couldn't have founded the church or fit into Judaism, and anyway "authenticity" is the wrong target: we should be studying how Jesus was remembered and what the memory reception tells us, not grading individual sayings as authentic or inauthentic.

Write the document they should be able to sign: a numbered agreed-claims section first (real claims, not filler like "sources must be handled critically" — if that's all they share, say so plainly, but I suspect they share more: both think there was a historical Jesus, both trust some broad strokes, both reject the old no-confidence position), then the disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording of its own view — including whether "authentic saying" even names a coherent target, which is one of the things they shout about. Then the cruxes: for each one, pick a concrete case that both sides would agree bears on it — an actual saying or episode you choose, where each camp's criteria or memory-framework gives a different prediction about what careful analysis of it should show — and write down each side's prediction in advance. If you think part of their dispute is really about the word "authentic" rather than about facts, say which part and why, but commit — don't give me "it may be partly verbal," decide. And don't soften the ending: if one camp's position ultimately forces them to admit the whole quest was answering a question nobody needed answered, or the other camp's forces them to concede the criteria can sort material but never settle who Jesus was, write that down as something they'd have to put their name to. Format: numbered agreed claims, then a disagreements table with a crux column. Keep the typos out of the joint statement, obviously, that parts got to be signable.
