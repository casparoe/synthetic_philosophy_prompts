I'm taking a philosophy of free will seminar and there's a girl in my study group, Priya, whose judgments on the cases are wierdly consistent, so we've started keeping a list of her verdicts to predict her on the new ones. Her track record so far: in the classic Frankfurt case from the paper we read (Black hiding in the background, ready to block Jones if Jones showed any sign of deciding not to shoot, but Jones decides on his own and Black never lifts a finger) she says Jones is fully responsible. In the variant where Black actually does step in and forces the decision, she says not responsible. In the manipulation case we discussed, where neuroscientists secretly rewrite a woman's values and motivational structure and she then acts from the rewritten values, she says not responsible — even in the variant where the new values are nicer and the woman wholeheartedly endorses them afterwards, which surprised me but apparently not her. In an ordinary case set in a deterministic universe, no neuroscientists, no funny backstory, someone just steals a bike out of laziness: responsible. And when someone decides on her own to stay in a room that happens to be locked (she couldn't have left anyway, but she never tried and never would have): responsible for staying.

Next week we get two new cases and we have bets going on her. Case one: the exact same value-rewiring as the manipulation case, except it turns out it was caused by a spontaneous brain lesion — no person, no plan, no one behind it, nature just did it on its own. Case two: a couple decides before their child is born to make her a doctor, and they structure her entire upbringing toward it using only ordinary means (schools, praise, the books lying around the house), and as an adult she chooses medicine for reasons she'd endorse on reflection. What will Priya say about each? I'm honestly less interested in the answers themselves than in the rule she seems to be running: what are the candidate principles that fit her whole track record equally well, where exactly do those rules come apart on these two cases, which one should we bet on and why, and what further case would you throw at her to actually tell the rules apart?
