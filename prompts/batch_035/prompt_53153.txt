My friend Jake is a hard aggregator, and his way of arguing for it is that an avalanche is just a lot of snowflakes: no single snowflake is dangerous, but pile enough of them up and they bury a village. In his telling, tiny harms work the same way — each speck is negligible, but aggregate enough of them and, like the avalanche, they outweigh the single big catastrophe. Each speck is better than the torture would be, but the specks collectively are worst than the torture.

My other friend Priya thinks this is exactly where the analogy cheats. Her counter is that a thousand people each carrying one brick is nothing like one person carrying a thousand bricks: the load distributes, and nobody strains. On her view suffering is like load — what matters is what happens within a single life (or a single moment), and there's no cosmic scale that adds everyone's specks together the way snow accumulates on one mountainside. The dust-speck case aggregates across *persons*, not just moments, and persons are like separate carriers: the sand never piles on any one head.
