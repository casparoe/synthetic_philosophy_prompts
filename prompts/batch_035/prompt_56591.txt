I'm trying to reconstruct, from memory, the explanation my housemate — a metaphysics PhD student — gave me at dinner for why the "conditional analysis" of dispositions is supposed to have collapsed, so that I can figure out how much of it to trust. She spoke fast and I may have garbled a word here or there, but roughly this is what she said:

"Dispositions like fragility or solubility were traditionally analyzed as conditionals: something is fragile just in case it would break if struck. C. B. Martin killed this with his live wire example. Imagine a wire hooked up to an 'electro-fink' — a safety device that makes the wire go dead the instant anyone touches it. The wire is live, so it has the disposition to shock, but the conditional fails: touch it and nothing happens. That's called a fink. Then a guy called Andy Johnston gave us masks: a vase swaddled in bubble wrap is still fragile, but it won't break if you drop it, because the packing masks the disposition rather than removing it. Between them, these cases show that no disposition is equivalent to any conditional about what would happen if its stimulus obtained — the conditional analysis is dead beyond repair, since patching it just invites more counterexamples. And there's a further consequence: the bubble-wrap case shows that whether something has a disposition depends on what surrounds it, so dispositions aren't intrinsic properties of their bearers at all. That's why people working on powers these days have given up on understanding dispositions in terms of stimulus-and-manifestation pairs."

Something about the last two claims sounded like conclusions being smuggled in rather than demonstrated, but I don't know the literature well enough to say. Please audit her summary claim by claim.
