so we're doing philosophy of science and I have to hand in a short "where I stand" doc on Popper and Darwinism, and before I do I want someone to poke holes in it. here's roughly what I believe:

1. falsifiability is still the best handle we have on what makes an inquiry scientific. every rival account (confirmation, paradigms, research programmes, whatever) either collapses into it or is too vague to demarcate anything.
2. Popper was basically right in his original complaint: the theory of natural selection as usually stated — survival of the fittest, with fitness defined as whatever survives — is dangerously close to a tautology. nothing you could observe would directly refute that core on its own.
3. when Popper took it all back in the late 70s (iirc he literally called it a recantation) that was mostly diplomatic. the logic of the original point still stands; all he really did was shift the testability from the theory as a whole onto particular Darwinian hypotheses — mimicry, galapagos finches, that kind of thing — and the bare theory stayed what it was.
4. evolutionary biology is nonetheless a paradigm of good science, honestly one of the most succesful things we've ever done, and the creationists who try to use Popper's point to argue evolution "isn't science" have made a mistake somewhere. I'm just not sure where.
5. Marxism and psychoanalysis on the other hand really are pseudo-science in exactly Popper's sense: their practitioners had an inexhaustible supply of face-saving reinterpretations, so the theories could never lose. Darwinians, whatever else you say about them, don't play like that.
6. and the classic falsifier still works: precambrian rabbit fossils (the Haldane line, tho even that attribution is shaky i think) would kill evolutionary theory dead. so it's not like the whole thing is immunized against refutation.
