Compare and contrast "echo chambers" and "epistemic bubbles." I've heard these used interchangeably all the time, but apparently theres a real distinction in the epistemology literature: a bubble works by *omission* (you just never hear the missing voices), whereas an echo chamber works by *discrediting* (you hear the outside voices fine, but you've been pre-loaded with reasons to distrust them). Lay out how each concept works, how they form, and how hard each one is to escape from.

Then apply it to a harder case: a Kuhnian research community doing normal science inside a paradigm, trained to see anomalies as puzzles rather than counter-evidence and to be suspicious of outsiders who reject the paradigm. Is that closer to a bubble, an echo chamber, both, or neither? And does your answer change depending on whether the paradigm is currently thriving or in crisis? Defend a clear verdict, don't just survey the options.
