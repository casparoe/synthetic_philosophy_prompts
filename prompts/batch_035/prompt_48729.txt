Is there an established name for the following? I keep running into people who don't so much lie to themselves as arrange to never receive the truth in the first place. My mother refused to open her biopsy results until after my sister's wedding. A guy I know asked all of us to tell him if his girlfriend was obviously seeing someone else, then blew up at the friend who actually did it. And I've been putting the scale in the closet from thanksgiving to new years. What gets me is the planning involved: to dodge a piece of information you have to have a pretty good idea of what it is. Some part of you already knows the answer, you're just refusing to let it get promoted to something you officially believe. So is this even self-deception in the sense philosophers worry about? You're not believing something you know to be false, you're managing to never form the belief at all — which maybe sidesteps the whole paradox of being both the con and the mark. I know lawyers talk about turning a blind eye, but that's about liability, not the mental structure. I want to know whether this thing has a real name, how it's supposed to relate to plain old self-deception, and if there is no name, coin one and tell me exactly where its edges are — for instance, does it still count when the information is sitting right there and you just don't look, versus when you actively arrange your life so it never reaches you?
