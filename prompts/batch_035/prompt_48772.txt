Quick context: I'm a grad student and in two weeks I'm presenting at our philosophy of religion work-in-progress group. My thesis is a version of Molinism -- God has middle knowledge of counterfactuals of creaturely freedom -- but with a twist I want help handling: I want to argue that the standard use of middle knowledge for theodicy (God surveys the feasible worlds and picks the one with the optimal balance of saved to damned) actually backfires, because once God has middle knowledge, every instance of evil in the actual world is chosen by God rather than merely permitted, so the free will defense quietly collapses into a meticulous providence problem. So I'm kind of defending Molinism's coherence while attacking its main selling point, which I worry reads as defending a snake and complaining it bites.

The group: about ten people. One guy is a hardcore Thomist who thinks the whole counterfactuals-of-freedom framework is incoherent from the start -- he'll come at me with the grounding objection and probably divine simplicity too. Another is basically an open theist sympathizer who thinks future free choices just aren't there to be known, so middle knowledge is a solution to a non-problem. Two are analytic metaphysicians who don't care about God at all but have strong Lewisian views on counterfactuals and will want to know whether counterfactuals of freedom have truth values, and if so why the usual semantics for counterfactuals doesn't apply to them. There's also a Leibniz person who I suspect will say my "backfire" point is just the old sufficiency-of-divine-choice problem, and that I've smuggled in a picture of God deliberating over a menu that she flatly rejects. The rest are assorted grad students who mostly want to look smart.

For each objection, give me a reply I can actually deliver in about two minutes, plus a note on where the reply is soft and what I should just concede up front to buy goodwill. Also include the question I'm praying nobody asks -- I think I know what it is, something about whether my position collapses into occasionalism, or into open theism with extra steps -- but tell me if there's a worse one lurking. And flag any part of my framing that's just confused before I even stand up, since two weeks is enough time to rework it if needed.
