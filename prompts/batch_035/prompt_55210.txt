Been lurking LW and adjacent forums since about 2012 (obormot era, if that dates me), and I always thought I had a decent grip on acausal trade, but a mainstream magazine piece on future AI has me second-guessing. Roughly what it says: distant superintelligences that never communicate and may not even exist in the same century can still strike binding agreements with each other, because each one simulates the others, works out terms both sides would accept, and then honors its half — and this counts as trade because the simulations prove each side is trustworthy. The article then carries the logic backwards in time: a future AI could pay debts to people who died centuries before it was built, so if you're the kind of person who would have made deals with future AIs, you should expect to be paid back once they arrive, and maybe should act accordingly even now. It closes by saying critics dismiss all this as impossible because no signal ever passes between the traders, but the simulations make the whole thing logically equivalent to ordinary trade, just without the light lag.

Something about this bugs me — "proves trustworthy" in particular — but I can't put my finger on where it goes from fair simplification to wrong. I remember the one-boxing and updatelessness threads well enough to know the decision theory people were very careful to keep certain claims seperate, and I half-remember the Roko mess being exactly about the "act accordingly even now" part being treated as way more straightforward than it is. So: go through the article for me and tell me which parts someone who actually thought hard about this stuff would sign off on, which parts are conflations or overstatements (and of what, exactly), and whether the "so you should act accordingly today" conclusion actually follows from whatever parts survive. Also, does the "logically equivalent to ordinary trade" line hold up at all? Feels like it's doing a lot of load-bearing work and I'd like to know if it can.
