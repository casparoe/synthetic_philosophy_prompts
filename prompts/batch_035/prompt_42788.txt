My country has a national election coming up and I'm genuinely stuck on what to do with my vote, and honestly on whether to cast one at all. I live in a fairly marginal constituency, so it's not totally hopeless that my vote could matter, though obviously the odds are still tiny. The situation: there are two major candidates with a realistic chance. One of them is clearly worse on basically everything I care about. The other one is bad in one specific way that really gets to me — I volunteered for years with an organization this candidate's party voted to strip of funding, and some of my friends were directly harmed by that policy. Then there's a small third party I actually like, which polls around 3% and has no chance of winning my seat. My options as I see them: hold my nose and vote for the lesser evil, vote third party as a kind of signal, formally spoil my ballot as a protest (which is counted and reported here), or stay home.

I've been arguing about this with two friends and I feel like they're each holding one half of something. One of them says my vote is basically pointless causally — one vote among tens of thousands — so I should feel free to do whatever expresses my values, and for him that means voting third party or spoiling. The other one counters that if everyone reasoned like that democracy would collapse, so I have a duty to vote, and having accepted that duty, the only responsible move is to vote against the worse candidate — anything else, he says, is treating my ballot as a personal diary entry instead of a piece of collective machinery. What bugs me is that they seem to be using totally different pictures of what a vote even *is*: one treats it as a tiny act of expression that's mine, the other as a contribution to a joint action where the point isn't my individual impact but my participation in the procedure, like showing up for jury duty. And there's a nagging third thought I can't shake: if I vote for the lesser evil, am I partly responsible for the bad things that candidate does, in a way I wouldn't be if I abstained? My "clean hands" friend says yes, that abstaining or spoiling keeps me out of it. That feels both right and like a cop-out.

I don't want a survey of positions or a "here are the considerations, you decide" — I've basically done that myself and it's why I'm stuck. Take a stance: tell me what you'd actually do in my situation, which of my three friends' pictures (if any) you think is closest to correct, whether the complicity/clean-hands thought is doing real work or is just confused, and defend your call against what you take to be the strongest objection to it. It's fine to say where you're least confident, but commit to a recommendation.
