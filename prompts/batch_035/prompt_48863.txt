I host a philosophy of mind podcast that's pretty deep-cut, and next month's guest is a philosopher who has spent her whole career on teleosemantics -- the idea that what fixes mental content is biological function, so a belief or perceptual state means what natural selection designed it to indicate. She's more in the Millikan-style camp where the consumer side matters (what the representation is *for* downstream), not so much the older Dretske learning-based story. I dont want her to just recap the view for the millionth time -- she's good at that and it eats the whole episode if I let it. Draft me eight to ten questions with follow-ups keyed to the answer she'll most likely give.

A few things I definitely want covered, at least one question each:

- Swampman. The molecule-for-molecule duplicate of a person that appears by lightning, no evolutionary history. If her line is that the duplicate's states have no content or only indeterminate content, the follow-up should press on whether that's a bullet she's biting or something she thinks is actually an insight -- and whether intuitions about a case with no history should count as evidence at all.
- The indeterminacy about which function a mechanism has. Selection rarely favors a mechanism for one clean thing; a signal-carrying state might have been favored partly because it also calms the organism down or is cheap to produce. How does she pick out the content-relevant function without just smuggling in the content she was trying to explain?
- Novel representations. The first time a creature ever tokened some state, or represents a brand-new predator, there's no selection history with that state yet. Where does the content come from at the moment of first use -- does she go etiological, or does she quietly allow forward-looking propensities?

Also, at least one question should push her on artificial systems: if you train a network by gradient descent for millions of episodes, is error-minimization in training a kind of selection in the relevant sense? Can her view say whether the trained net's activations have content, or does it turn out that only things with literal lineages can represent anything? I'm curious whether she thinks that's an embarassing edge case or actually the view working as intended.

End with one question about what would genuinely count as evidence against teleosemantics -- not just another patchable problem, but something that, if it came out a certain way, should make an honest defender give it up. Keep the phrasing conversational enough that I can read it out loud without it sounding like a take-home exam.
