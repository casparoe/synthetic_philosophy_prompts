i'm about a year into a postdoc on rationality and decision-making, and i have maybe two years of real working time left before i need to turn it into something. my topic, loosely, is: is honoring sunk costs always irrational? the received view is that yes, obviously, bygones are bygones, but there's a scattered pushback literature and i honestly can't tell which parts of it are worth building on versus which are just clever contrarianism. so instead of a survey, i want a prioritized take: which open questions in this debate most deserve work right now, and why those and not the others.

some threads i've bumped into, so you know what i mean by "in the weeds" — don't treat this list as exhaustive, or even correct:

- the thought that having paid a sunk cost is evidence about your own past valuations, so refusing to "honor" it can amount to throwing away self-knowledge (what you were willing to pay reveals something about what continuing is worth to you now, or does it? that's sort of the question)
- persistence as a commitment device: maybe honoring sunk costs is only rational where others, or future-you, can observe the honoring, and the debate has never cleanly separated the intrapersonal case from the interpersonal one
- a suspicion that a chunk of the "fallacy" findings in the psych experiments are really cases where continuation has unmodeled costs or option value, so the fallacy is partly an artifact of simplified payoff matrices
- whether the whole framing survives once you notice that in real cases what's sunk isn't a cost but a bundle — money, yes, but also years and reputation and relationships — and the clean argument only touches the money part

for whatever you rank highly, i want the actual reasoning: what makes it upstream of the rest, whether it's tractable with formal decision-theory tools versus needing empirical work i can't run, and what would have to happen for it to move down the list. equally important, tell me what here is overrated — where settling the question wouldn't change anything downstream. and please don't give me a reading list, i have those; what i don't have is a judgement about where the live problems are.
