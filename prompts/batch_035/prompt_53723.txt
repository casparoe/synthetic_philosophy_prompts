I teach a small honors seminar on self-locating belief, and for our unit on the Sleeping Beauty problem I'd like a one-page decision-tree handout that takes a student from Beauty's epistemic situation — she knows she is awake inside the experiment but not which awakening this is — to a position on the credence she should assign to the coin's landing heads. Build the tree from the logical space rather than from a list of names: each branch node should be a question a student can answer without knowing which position it leads to, e.g., whether the awakening itself gives Beauty any evidence about the coin, whether the number of potential awakenings should affect how her evidence is weighted, and whether the question of her credence is even well posed as asked. The leaves must include the halfer, the thirder, the double-halfer (who agrees with the halfer on waking but differs in her conditional credence given that it is Monday), and at least one view on which the original question is ill-posed or in need of reformulation; add further leaves as the space requires. Please test the tree against two variants the seminar will discuss: an extreme version in which tails results in a thousand awakenings (to catch students whose answer flips with the numbers) and a version in which Beauty is offered a small-stakes bet at every awakening (to separate views about credence from views about what she should do). Flag any pair of leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate, and note any combination of branch answers that lands on no defended position, with one sentence on whether that empty cell reflects incoherence, unattractiveness, or merely that no one has explored it. Assume the students know the standard setup but none of the literature.
