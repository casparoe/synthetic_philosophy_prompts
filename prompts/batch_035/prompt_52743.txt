I'm a science journalist. Five years ago I wrote a long piece about de-extinction where I described the project as "bringing back" lost species — with the quotes doing a lot of ironic work, since my whole point was that you'd get a engineered lookalike, not the same species. The phrase took off and lost the quotes. It's now cited in policy debates as a straight endorsement of resurrection biology, and rereading the piece honestly, I can see how they got there: the skepticism only shows up two paragraphs in, and the headline (which an editor wrote, not me) reads completely straight. A journal has asked me for a follow-up "correcting the record." My editor says obviously I get to say what I meant — it's my piece. But do I? I genuinely can't tell anymore how much of the irony was actually on the page at the time versus how much lived in my head, and how much I'm retrofitting now that I dislike who's using the piece and for what. Is there even a fact of the matter about what my article meant, and if there is, is mine the last word on it — or can an author misread her own text? Also worth saying: what should the follow-up actually do differently, given that the headline is what everyone saw and it was never mine to begin with?
