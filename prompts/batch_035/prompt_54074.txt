I've been reading the consciousness threads since the old LessWrong days — long enough to have watched the "dissolve the question" move get applied to free will, to personal identity, and then to run aground, visibly, on qualia. I think I've assembled a settled position out of the wreckage, and before I write it up as a top-level post I want a consistency check. I've seen too many people build a view out of locally persuasive pieces without noticing that the pieces have incompatible load-bearing walls. Here are my commitments:

1. Functionalism about mind. Everything mental is fixed by functional organization. An exact functional duplicate of me would have the same mental life I do, including everything worth calling its conscious life, and a good-enough upload would be conscious in the full sense.

2. Illusionism about phenomenal consciousness. There is no irreducible "what it is like" property. The felt hardness of the hard problem comes from introspection misrepresenting our own internal states — representing functional, relational states as intrinsic, private, ineffable ones.

3. The meta-problem is the real problem. What actually needs explaining is why brains like ours generate hard-problem judgments at all: why we report ineffable qualia, why the explanatory gap strikes us as unbridgeable from the inside. I take this to be an ordinary problem of cognitive science, about a quirk of the human self-model, no more mysterious in kind than why we don't notice our own blind spots.

4. When the meta-problem is solved, the solution will be illusionist. The mechanistic story about why we make qualia reports will not need to posit any phenomenal property doing any work.

5. And yet the conviction that there is something it is like to be me does not budge when I contemplate all of the above. At the object level — the 3 a.m. level, not the argument level — illusionism strikes me as false, and I think the persistence of this conviction is epistemically significant. It is not merely one more qualia report for the meta-problem to explain away; it is a datum that pushes back against commitment 4.

6. I hold that the zombie intuition is some kind of cognitive illusion, but also that an illusion requires a real relatum — you cannot misrepresent nothing. Whatever introspection is misdescribing is really there; it just is not what introspection says it is.

Go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it, rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally bad, and say for each what would have to give and what it costs. Finish by telling me which single revision would clean up the most. I suspect that at least one of these is far worse trouble than it looks and that at least one that looks like trouble isn't, but I genuinely don't know which is which, so don't let my ordering decide anything — audit all of it.
