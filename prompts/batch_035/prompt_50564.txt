i'm a C programmer who wandered into a philosophy of mind reading group, and we've been doing the zombie thing. someone explained the conceivability argument the usual way: you can coherently conceive of a being physically identical to me but with no inner experience, so the physical facts don't fix the facts about experience, so physicalism is false. and i immediately thought: this is just a build with NDEBUG defined. same source code, same machine, but you compile with assertions disabled — every assert() becomes a no-op, the binary behaves identically from the outside, passes the whole test suite, and there's simply nothing happening inside at those points. qualia = the assertions. a philosophical zombie is a release build of a human.

i floated this and the group split three ways. one guy said it actually helps chalmers: the compile is demonstrably conceivable, you can do it on your laptop, so experience comes out as something strippable that the behavior survives without. another said it begs the question, because a compiled-out assert isn't something extra to the program — it's the same code, just with checks silenced — and whether experience is a check versus the whole running program is exactly what's at issue, so the analogy assumes its conclusion. the third said it's not even an argument, just the intuition pump wearing a compiler's clothes, and i should stop showing off.

so audit the analogy for me. give me the explicit mapping first — what plays the role of the source code, the compile-time flag, the binary, the external test suite, and especially the log lines: a debug build writes "assertion passed" messages, and a zombie still sincerely reports having experiences, so does anything in my picture correspond to the reports, or does that already expose a gap? then the break points — i can see one myself: NDEBUG removes code that we know existed, we have the other build on disk, whereas the zombie thought experiment is supposed to show the "experience layer" might not be physical at all, which is not like removing anything you can point to. are there other disanalogies that matter more? then separate the ones that are fatal for the use it's being put to from the ones that aren't.

and then actually take a side: does the conceivability argument come out stronger, weaker, or basically unchanged once you force it through the build metaphor? don't hand me "there are considerations on both sides" — commit. if the analogy dies, tell me whether there's a better programming analogy that does the work (perfect emulation under a profiler? a program with a slow path that never gets executed? a fork that diverges only in unobservable state?) and what the original argument was obscuring that the better analogy makes visible. my own hunch is the release-build picture quietly assumes there's a source code level where the qualia visibly live, which is more than chalmers is entitled to, but i want you to tell me if that hunch is right or if im being too impressed with my own metaphor.
