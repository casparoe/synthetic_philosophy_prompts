I'm taking philosophy of mind this semester and we just spent two weeks on Dennett and the color phi phenomenon -- the one where you flash a red spot on one side of a screen, then a green spot on the other side, and people report seeing a single spot moving across that changes color somewhere in the middle, even though no spot in between was ever displayed. Dennett says there are two candidate stories about what's going on in the subject's head: an "Orwellian" one, where you first have the experience of red-then-nothing-then-green (or of the dot moving red), and then your memory record gets rewritten afterwards so you sincerely misreport what you experienced; and a "Stalinesque" one, where the brain's "editing room" holds the raw feed and splices in the color change before anything gets broadcast to consciousness, so there never was an unedited experience to misremember. His punchline is that no experiment could ever distinguish these two, that there's no fact of the matter about which is right, and that this shows the whole idea of a Cartesian theater -- a place in the brain where it all comes together and the show goes on for an audience -- is confused.
