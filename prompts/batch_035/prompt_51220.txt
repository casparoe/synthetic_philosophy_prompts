I'm on the student union council at my university, and we have a decision coming up this thursday that I need to write a memo for. Quick context: I'm also taking a course on democratic theory this semester, and we just finished a unit on referendums (switzerland's system, brexit, california ballot propositions), so I keep feeling like this stuff is directly relevant, but I'm not sure how much of what we learned actually applies to our little case. That's partly why I want your help.

The situation: a petition with enough signatures came in demanding a campus-wide referendum on whether the union should cancel its contract with the food service company that runs the main cafeterias (over its labor practices) and redirect the roughly $40k in student fees toward starting a student-run co-op alternative. Some councillors want to just hold it and treat the result as binding. Others say it's really a budgeting/feasibility decision that councillors were elected to make, that turnout will be maybe 10-15% anyway, and that most students voting won't have any idea what the transition would actually cost or how the co-op would work. One more wrinkle: if it passes, a small group gets hit hard -- about 30 students currently work at those cafeteria outlets and would likely lose those jobs, and they're a much smaller group than the voters who'd decide.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate before the meeting. It should recommend one concrete course of action to the council -- hold it as binding, refuse it, or hold it with conditions (quorum threshold, supermajority, required public deliberation events beforehand, whatever you think actually makes sense) -- with reasoning the other councillors could engage with, and it needs to take seriously the strongest point on whichever side loses, not just steamroll it.

One thing I really need: in the parts of the memo that lean on the referendum theory stuff, please be careful to distinguish what's basically settled among people who study direct democracy from what's genuinely contested. Like, I know there are real live debates about whether referendum outcomes should be treated as a unified "will of the people" at all, whether majority votes should decide things that concentratedly harm a minority, and how bad low-information voting actually is for legitimacy -- I don't want the memo to present one side of any of those as the settled expert view when it isn't. If something you'd want to claim is genuinely contested, either flag it as contested in the memo itself or build the recommendation so it doesn't depend on settling the debate. My course taught me just enough to know I could get this embarassingly wrong in front of everyone.
