In my cognitive science seminar the instructor keeps repeating that perception is controlled hallucination, and last week she used it to wave away my question about whether perception can be accurate — if it's all hallucination anyway, she said, accuracy is supposedly not even the right dimension on which to evaluate perceptual states. I'm sure something is off with that move, but I can't articulate exactly what. Can you lay out the distinct precise claims the slogan could be making, as a menu I can bring to the next seminar: from something nearly trivial about top-down feedback and priors in the visual hierarchy, through the Bayesian picture of perception as inference to hidden causes, to the substantive and contentious reading on which hallucination and veridical perception share a common mechanism and differ only in how strongly they are constrained by sensory input, to readings that slide toward an almost Kantian conclusion that we never encounter the world itself but only our model of it? For each reading, tell me whether it's plausible, whether it's actually news as opposed to what perception science already knew, and whether it would genuinely support dismissing my question about accuracy — since my suspicion is that only the most radical readings would, and those are the ones nobody in the room is actually defending. Along the way, say where the free energy principle fits: is it an additional empirical commitment that would settle anything here, or a formal framework whose relation to the slogan is closer to a redescription, so that quoting it in an argument risks borrowing rigor rather than content? If it helps clarify the difference between the empirical and the merely formal readings, feel free to use the dark room objection — the puzzle about why an organism minimizing surprise wouldn't simply seek a maximally predictable environment like a quiet dark room — as a case study of what each reading predicts and where the readings come apart. As you go, flag how confident you are in each verdict rather than presenting everything as settled, and end by identifying the single point in your answer you are least sure of and what would make you revise it.
