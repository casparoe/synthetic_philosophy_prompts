Option one is a screening program that would catch problems early for basically everyone — small benefit spread across the whole population, decent total effect. Option two is pouring the same money into intensive home nursing for the few thousand people in the region who are already in terrible shape — much smaller total benefit by the standard health-economics measure (they count things like healthy-years gained), but concentrated on people who are genuinely, horribly bad off. The health economists I interviewed all lean toward the screening program bc the numbers are better. But then a policy guy I called told me that if you follow that logic all the way down, you end up saying that taking something away from people who are doing fine — pure spite, nobody actually benefits, everyone's just closer together — is at least a little bit good, and since that's obviously nuts, we should stop flattering ourselves and just do the screening. So now I dont know. I have maybe 1200 words. Is there a principled way to defend picking the home nursing option that doesn't sign me on to the spite thing, or is one of these two guys just wrong, and if so which one?
