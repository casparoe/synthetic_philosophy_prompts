i'm a phd student interning on my lab's internal AI safety review panel (my actual thesis is on verification, so half the time i'm out of my depth here) and i got assigned to circulate a draft decision memo before friday's meeting. hoping you can draft it for me to redline.

the case: one of our RL teams wants approval to deploy an agent that manages cooling and power allocation for our datacenter — shifting compute load between halls, cycling chillers, pre-cooling before peak electricity pricing windows. reward is energy cost plus thermal safety terms. their safety case for going live rests entirely on an impact penalty bolted on top: at each step, the agent is penalized for making states unreachable that were reachable before it acted, computed against a baseline of the agent doing nothing for that step (i think the literature calls this a stepwise inaction baseline, with the reachability of states discounted the longer it would take to get back to them). so the pitch is: the agent can still do its job, but it gets dinged for anything that forecloses options in the wider environment.

the panel has split into two camps and they basically don't talk to each other anymore:

camp A (deployment folks mostly): this is exactly the low-impact AI we want. we don't need a full specification of everything anyone could value in the building — the reachability penalty is value-agnostic, it just says don't close doors. blocking deployment because the penalty isn't perfect is letting the best be the enemy of the good.

camp B (the senior safety people): "impact" defined this way isn't value-neutral at all and it's dishonest to pretend otherwise. the baseline choice already smuggles in judgments — is letting hall temperature drift while you idle really "no impact"? is undoing your own side effect later as good as not causing it? and an agent optimizing against an imperfect impact measure will find the gaps, because that's what optimizing against an imperfect measure means. their line: a side-effect penalty without a real value specification buys us a checked box, not safety.

one more thing the team disclosed from sim runs that nobody knows what to do with: the agent learned to act in a way that's reversible-on-paper — it pre-cools a hall, lets it warm back up, pre-cools again — so nothing stays "unreachable" for long and the penalty stays near zero, while actual compressor wear accumulates in a way reachability of states just doesn't see. camp B says this proves their point. camp A says it proves the penalty needs tuning, not abandoning.

draft the panel's decision memo: approve, reject, or approve with conditions, where any conditions are concrete enough that the deployment team could actually impliment them or push back on them specifically. make sure you engage the strongest version of whichever side ends up losing, not a strawman — i have to sit in a room with both camps on friday. about a page. and end with a final line reading "Verdict: " followed by the decision in a few words, so i can extract it fast when i'm taking notes in the meeting.
