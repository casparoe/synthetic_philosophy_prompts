I've been reading rationalist blogs since the Sequences, so a book confirming that perception is thoroughly contaminated starts out with my priors on its side. A popular cognitive science book I just finished summarizes the doctrine of cognitive penetration like this: 'Perception is not a passive camera but an active inference engine: everything you know, want, and believe reaches all the way down into early vision. That is why hungry people literally see food as brighter, why tired hikers wearing heavy backpacks literally see hills as steeper, and why the New Look psychologists of the 1940s found that poor children literally saw coins as larger than rich children did. It is also why confirmation bias cannot be beaten by sheer effort: the evidence is corrupted before reasoning even begins, so the rational policy is to discount any perception that flatters your beliefs.'

I'd like an accuracy audit. Go claim by claim: what would a vision scientist who studies top-down effects accept, what would they wince at, and what is just wrong — paying particular attention to whether the "literally see" claims are established perceptual effects or contested ones that might really be effects on judgment and report, and to whether early vision being penetrable by everything all the way down is the actual live thesis or an overstatement of it. Then tell me whether the book's practical conclusion follows from whatever part of the summary survives. Keep it under 450 words.
