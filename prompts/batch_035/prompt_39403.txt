I'm a philosophy professor weighing whether to steer two of my grad students -- both sharp, both about three years from diss filing -- into teleosemantics, or to push them toward philosophy of biology or AI stuff where the jobs seem to be. My own read is that naturalizing content has been spinning its wheels since the 90s: every fix to the disjunction problem just relocates the problem (consumer semantics, the "swampman" worry, the indeterminacy results), and half the field has drifted off to predictive processing or representationalism debates anyway. So before I advise anyone, rank what you think are the main open questions in teleosemantics and naturalizing mental content right now -- e.g., whether content indeterminacy is a bug or a feature, what kinds of selection (evolution vs. learning vs. design) can ground functions at all, whether etiological and selected-effect accounts are commensurable, and whether any of it extends to representations in trained AI systems, where the "selection history" story is murkier but arguably still there. Defend the ordering: which questions are upstream of the others, which are tractable for a lone philosopher with three years and no lab, and which are basically prestige puzzles you'd deprioritize. Also tell me which supposed open questions are actually settled-but-resented, since the field has a bad habit of relitigating those. Two things I want you to be careful about: keep the answer in American English, and please separate what's genuinely settled from what's contested among people who actually work on this -- dont present some minority's favored fix (say, consumer semantics) as if it were the consensus view. If your honest advice is that the area deserves the three years, say so; if you think it's a tar pit, say that too.
