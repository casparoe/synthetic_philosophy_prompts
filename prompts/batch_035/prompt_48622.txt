I'm teaching a philosophy of history seminar this term, and the next unit is on collective memory versus the historical record -- when the two conflict, which one should give way? For the seminar I want a real case study, and I've settled on the 1938 War of the Worlds broadcast, because it has an unusual structure. The collective memory is clear: Orson Welles's radio drama caused a nationwide panic, families fleeing their homes, highways jammed, people treated for shock. But when scholars finally went back to the archives decades later, the story largely collapsed -- the audience was much smaller than remembered, reports of panic were scattered and mostly secondhand, and the "mass hysteria" narrative was pushed hard by newspapers who had an obvious interest in portraying radio, their rival, as dangerous. So the record contradicts the memory here, but the twist is that this "memory" wasn't some slow-grown organic tradition that drifted from the facts; it was manufactured almost immediately by interested parties, and then hardened into something that functioned as genuine collective memory for decades, shaping what everyone "knows" about mass media and panic.

Here's what I want you to do with it. First, analyze the episode through the standard collective-memory-versus-history framework: does the memory, on Halbwachs-style thinking, do its own legitimate work regardless of accuracy, such that correcting it with the record misses the point? Second, and this is where I want real engagement: does the fact that this memory was partly manufactured change its standing? Is a fabricated-but-durable memory less entitled to deference than an organic one that happens to conflict with the record, or, once it has done the work of organizing experience for eighty years, is its origin irrelevant? I don't want a neutral survey of the options here -- I want you to take a definite position on which should give way when memory and record conflict, and defend it, including saying honestly where this case fits or doesnt fit your position. If the case shows the standard dichotomy is off, say so and tell me what you'd put in its place, but commit to something.
