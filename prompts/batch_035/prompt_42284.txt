Write me the minutes of an ethics review board (think university IRB meets HR grievance panel, deadpan bureaucratic voice, around 600 words) that has been asked to rule on Abraham's request for an exemption from the universal prohibition on killing your child. The commitee is competent, well-meaning, and has a category for everything: religious accommodation, concientious objection, greater-good waivers — after all, they once approved Agamemnon, who sacrificed his daughter for the fleet and gave a speech to the assembly that everyone present could understand. The running joke is that every category fits Agamemnon and none fit Abraham: his case can't be stated as a rule anyone else could follow, it's just him and whoever he takes God to be, and he can't explain himself without either lying or turning his private reason into a public one, which would undo it. Keep Kierkegaard's actual distinctions straight — the universal vs the absolute duty, the tragic hero vs the knight of faith — without name-dropping him, or at most one deadpan aside that nobody on the board has read him. One committee member should ask "but what if everyone did this" and be genuinely satisfied with the answer they get. End with the board issuing a reasonable compromise (approved substitute offering, mandatory counseling, an incident-report form for Isaac to complete in triplicate) that shows they understood the universal perfectly and the case not at all. No winking, and the board isn't stupid — it just only speaks one language. It should be readable to someone who's never heard of Fear and Trembling and funny to someone who has.
