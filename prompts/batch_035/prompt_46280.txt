Not sure if this has a name or if im just describing two known things badly, so here goes. At my old job we came within a day of launching a pricing change that, as it turned out, roughly half the team privately thought was a bad idea. Nobody said a word in any of the meetings. Each person assumed that if no one else was objecting, everyone else must have information or conviction they lacked. It only fell apart because someone finally asked a naive question in a corridor and the dam broke. It wasnt that anyone was scared to speak up, really — it was that everyones own small doubt seemed outweighed by everyone elses apparent confidence, and it turned out all that "confidence" was just other people doing the same calculation. The group as a whole acted on less information than it collectively had.

I've since noticed the same shape elsewhere. A friend of mine chose her degree partly because it seemed like the thing everyone at her school was clearly doing for good reasons — she later found out most of them were just following the same signal off each other, like a queue outside a restaurant that exists because a queue exists. And in a seminar I sat in on, no one asked about a step in the argument that, when someone finally did ask about it, it turned out most of the room had been quietly puzzled by for the whole hour.

Someone once told me there's a concept called pluralistic something-or-other, about groups where everyone privately disbelieves a thing but thinks everyone else believes it, but that doesnt quite cover this — in our pricing meeting nobody had a *firm* private view that got suppressed, we each had a weak private signal that got swamped. I've also heard of information cascades, but my understanding is those are about people rationally copying earlier choosers when they cant see what those choosers knew, and in our case there was no copying of any actual choice — the "evidence" everyone was deferring to was pure absence of objection, which isnt information at all, its just the default.

So, questions. Is there an established term for exactly this configuration — each member has weak private evidence pointing one way, the groups public silence points the other way, and the silence wins with everyone individually? Is it the same mechanism as those other two things, or only superficially similar, and if so what actually separates them (like, what would you look for in a real group to tell which one youre seeing)? And if nothing in the literature carves this exact thing out, say so and give me a name and a proper definition, including the boundary cases — e.g. does it still count when one loud confident person triggers the silence rather than it emerging from nobody. I dont just want a label slapped on it, I want to know what the phenomenon is and isnt.
