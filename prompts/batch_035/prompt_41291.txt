so over the last couple years (a meditation retreat, then a lot of reading) I've converged on an axiology I want to defend at my ethics reading group, but first I want someone to check whether it even hangs together. call it tranquilism: nothing has intrinsic value except the absence of suffering and of craving. joy, beauty, achievement, whatever — they count only insofar as they crowd out suffering or craving. a perfectly calm mind with nothing going on isn't missing anything. that's (1).

(2) death is still a harm to the person who dies. someone drops dead instantly in their sleep: no pain, no forewarning, no dependents, nobody left to grieve. I think something bad happened to them, and I'd think this even if the rest of the universe winked out at the same second.

(3) I think a world where humanity is kept sedated — no suffering, no craving, everyone mildly comfortable in a fog, no art, no science, nobody falls in love or strives for anything — is worse than the actual messy world, even though the actual world contains vastly more suffering and craving. and I don't mean worse for me because I like drama, I mean really worse. if you handed me the soma button I wouldn't press it, and not just because of side effects I can't name.

(4) I think alot of temporary suffering is clearly worth it: rehab that hurts for months, breakups you owe somebody, telling a friend the thing he doesn't want to hear. I can't tell whether this is fine given (1) or quietly inconsistent with it — that one honestly confuses me.

each of these feels right to me when I consider it on its own. but last week a guy in the group said the best thing that can happen to a person on my view is that they stop craving, and dead people stop craving real fast, and I didn't have an answer. so: go through the four, tell me which are actually in tension and which only look like it, rank the real tensions by severity, give the cheapest fix for each, and then say which single revision would clean up the most. don't soften it, but also don't declare the whole thing a wreck unless it really is.
