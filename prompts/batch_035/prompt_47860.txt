My flatmate has been on a finitism kick ever since he got into the kalam cosmological argument, and he has this Hilbert's Hotel thing he deploys on me over dinner that I cant quite knock down. As he tells it:

(1) Suppose an actually infinite collection could exist — say, a hotel with rooms numbered 1, 2, 3, ... , all occupied.
(2) Then the hotel is full.
(3) Yet a new guest can always be checked in: everyone shifts from room n to room n+1, and room 1 is now free.
(4) Even infinitely many new guests can be: everyone moves to room 2n, and all the odd-numbered rooms open up.
(5) Now suppose the guests in rooms 3, 6, 9, ... all check out — that's infinitely many guests gone.
(6) Then the guests in rooms 1, 4, 7, ... and 2, 5, 8, ... check out too — again, infinitely many guests.
(7) After step (5), infinitely many guests remain. After step (6), none do.
(8) The same number of people left the hotel in (5) and in (6).
(9) A hotel where "infinitely many leave" leaves infinitely many occupants in one case and zero in another is absurd, so an actually infinite hotel cannot exist.
(10) And what goes for the hotel goes for any actual infinite — including an infinite past.

He is very happy with the conclusion, especially the infinite-past bit. What bugs me is that (10) also seems to cover the natural numbers (there just ARE infinitely many of them, they're not some process), infinitely many points between my two hands, and infinitely many future Tuesdays if the universe doesnt end — and even he doesn't want to deny those, he just says the math ones are "only potential," which I honestly dont understand at all. My instinct is that the trick is hidden somewhere around (8), or maybe in what "full" is doing in (2)-(4), but I can't make either precise. So: which step should I reject, what exactly is wrong with it, and what does rejecting it cost? If I'm forced to choose, I'd way rather keep the infinitely many naturals than keep any premise of this argument, but I want to actually understand what I'm paying, not just wave my hands.
