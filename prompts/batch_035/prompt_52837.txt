Just found out I'm pregnant (10 weeks, keeping it quiet for now) and the last month has basically been one long exercise in deciding who gets told what and when. Somewhere in there I noticed a pattern I can't find a word for, so I'm asking here.

The same fact about me feels either like nothing or like a genuine violation depending on how it travels and who it lands on, and the charge doesn't track the fact itself at all. My doctor knows everything — obviously fine. My sister guessed from a photo, also fine, mostly funny. But the same information reaching my manager's manager through the office grapevine would feel like a real breach, even though he's a decent guy who would handle it kindly. Meanwhile, a few years ago I told a total stranger on a train basically everything during a miscarriage scare, and that felt like nothing — a relief, if anything. And the reverse happens too: a close friend passed something I'd told her to another close friend, same circle, people I like, and that stung like a betrayal even though no "new kind of person" learned anything.

So it's not about how many people know, or how close they are to me, or whether they'd be nice about it. It seems to be something about the route the information took. Is there an actual concept for this in the privacy literature? If there is, I want to understand it properly, not just get a label — specifically whether it covers the grapevine case, where the harm seems to be the channel rather than the recipient, and the gossip case, where the recipient was fine but the passing-it-on itself was the wrong. And if nothing established fits exactly, I'd rather you coin a term with a definition and the edge cases worked out than tell me "it depends on context." I already know it depends on context, thats kind of the whole problem.
