I attend a small discussion group that meets to argue about mind uploading and future technology, and at the last session a friend there shut down my hesitation about destructive scanning — the kind of teleportation or uploading procedure in which the original is destroyed while an exact psychological replica goes on — with the one-liner that personal identity is not what matters. I take the line to descend from Parfit's work on personal identity, but in context it was doing real work: it was meant to show that my hesitation at the scanner's threshold is confused in principle, that stepping in is not a gamble with anything death-like at all. I don't think the one-liner by itself can carry that conclusion, and I want to see exactly where the slack is, because I suspect that several very different theses travel under the same sentence, only some of which would give my friend what he wants.

Please lay out, as an ordered menu, the distinct precise claims that the slogan could be expressing — starting with the weakest reading that even Parfit's staunchest opponents could accept without discomfort, moving through substantive theses about what prudentially matters in survival, and ending with the most radical readings, on which questions about whether some future person is me are in some sense empty or indeterminate rather than merely unimportant. For each reading, state plainly: whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or simply false; and, most importantly, whether that reading would actually support the use my friend made of it — the conclusion that there is nothing rationally to fear in destructive scanning, so that my hesitation rests on a mistake rather than on a defensible weigh of what is lost. In working through the menu, please address at least the following points: the difference between identity mattering intrinsically and identity mattering only derivatively, as the contingent one-to-one shape that our concept imposes on something else that matters, such as psychological connectedness or continuity; what happens to the various readings in fission cases, where whatever is claimed to matter can apparently be preserved in full while identity is not preserved at all; and whether the matters in the slogan is prudential, moral, or both, since my friend's complacency at the scanner's threshold seems to trade on a particular answer to that question without ever stating it. Conclude by identifying which reading my friend was most plausibly invoking, and what exactly he would still need to defend — over and above reciting the slogan — for his dismissal of my hesitation to go through.
