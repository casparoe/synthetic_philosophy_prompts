Writing up my views on weakness of will for a seminar and before I embarass myself I'd like a consistency check. Here's the package, as honestly as I can state it:

1. Akrasia is real. You can intentionally do something -- no compulsion, no ignorance, no change of mind mid-action -- while at that very moment judging that, all things considered, you ought not do it. Socrates was just wrong that this can't happen.
2. I'm a Humean about motivation: judgment by itself never moves you, desire does. (I think this is compatible with 1 but flag it if you disagree.)
3. Almost everything that gets called weakness of will day to day -- the cake, the doomscrolling, skipping the gym -- is really just hyperbolic discounting. At the moment of action you simply prefer the cake, full stop, and no judgment is being violated at that instant. So there's nothing irrational happening right then; the person just has steep time preferences.
4. But time-inconsistent preferences ARE irrational. An agent whose preferences flip depending on when you ask them is not a coherent agent, and precommitment -- alarm across the room, deleting the apps, Ulysses and the mast -- is rational repair, not cheating.
5. When I compare the person who genuinely acts akratically (in the sense of 1) with the person who just hyperbolically discounts, the akratic one seems more blameworthy to me. I want to say the difference is that only one of them betrayed their own judgment, but I cant quite make this precise without it sounding like I'm just restating the difference.
6. And maybe the self that precommits is the real one -- the preferences you have when calm and far from temptation are somehow more yours than the ones you have with the cake in front of you. I keep going back and forth on this.

Which of these pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them as all equally bad, and for each one say what would have to give. Lead with your bottom line -- first sentence should be your overall verdict, no preamble or restating my views back at me. And since I suspect I'll get talked into whatever you say: tell me honestly what consideration would flip you to the opposite verdict on the biggest tension, and where your own reasoning about this is most likely to go wrong.
