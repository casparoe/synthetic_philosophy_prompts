My department's theory reading group has spent three weeks on the utility monster without converging, and I've realized we're not actually disagreeing about the case — we're disagreeing about what the case even needs to be true in order to work. Before the next session, I want a mapping of where the credences really come apart, so please reason in probabilities rather than positions.

Here is the proposition to evaluate, stated as precisely as I can: classical hedonic total utilitarianism holds that the right act is the one that maximizes the sum of pleasure minus suffering over all sentient beings, presupposing that utilities can be cardinally compared across persons. Nozick's utility monster is a being whose capacity for utility — either in magnitude or in its returns to resources — is so much greater than ours that funneling every resource to it yields a larger aggregate than any distribution to everyone else. The question: what is your credence that this case is a successful reductio of classical hedonic total utilitarianism — that is, that the theory is unacceptable as stated specifically because of what it implies here, not because of hedonism's other problems or comparison problems that arise elsewhere?

Please decompose that credence into at least the following terms, assigning each a rough number with one or two lines of justification, and add any further terms you think belong in the tree:

(i) The probability that a being with superlinear returns to resources is metaphysically possible at all — is there anything in the very concept of welfare that forbids utility gains that outstrip the resources producing them, or does the possibility just follow from how a pleasure scale could in principle work?

(ii) The probability that the interpersonal cardinal comparability the setup presupposes is coherent rather than a fiction the formalism smuggles in — can the monster's utility really be placed on a single scale with mine, or does the case trade on an assumption that no defensible version of the theory actually needs?

(iii) Conditional on (i) and (ii), the probability that the total view genuinely implies devoting everything to the monster, rather than some auxiliary assumption — about aggregation, about how sums behave at the extremes — doing the real work.

(iv) The probability that a theory's implying such a verdict counts against the theory at all, as opposed to being a datum about our parochial intuitions — in other words, that bullet-biting is not an adequate response here.

Then combine the terms into your overall number, and tell me which term your final answer is most sensitive to, and what argument or piece of reasoning would move that term the most.

Two further things. First, my own variant, which I'd like folded into your answer where it matters: suppose there is no single monster, but a modest population — a few hundred beings — whose utility scales superlinearly together, such that the aggregate is maximized by serving them and no one else. Does the reductio survive losing its single-subject punch, or does it become strictly weaker or strictly stronger? I want to know whether your decomposition treats the one-monster and many-monster cases as turning on the same terms.

Second, for context on where we actually stand: my credence that the monster is a successful reductio is around 0.5; a senior colleague's is around 0.9; a classmate's is below 0.2. My colleague insists the case is exactly parallel to Parfit's repugnant conclusion — a bullet any consistent total utilitarian must bite, and a cost of biting it that reveals something wrong with the axioms; I accept the repugnant conclusion as a bullet I'm willing to bite and don't count it against the theory, which is presumably why I sit in the middle; my classmate thinks neither case counts against the theory at all because thought-experiment verdicts about beings that cannot exist are not evidence about axioms. Given your decomposition, which single term is each of the three of us most plausibly disagreeing about, and which sub-question, if we somehow resolved it definitively, would most compress the spread among our three numbers?
