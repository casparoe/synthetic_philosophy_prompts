I hold a luck egalitarian view of distributive justice: inequalities are just when they trace back to voluntary choices for which the parties can be held responsible, and unjust when they trace back to circumstances beyond the parties' control. But my version is qualified in two ways that I suspect do most of the work, so please direct your answer at the qualified version, not the textbook one.

First, I accept an asymmetry: the view tells us whom to compensate for bad luck, but it does not tell us to confiscate the gains of good option luck. Someone who freely takes a risk and loses has a claim on us; someone who freely takes the same risk and wins keeps the winnings. I defend this asymmetry on the ground that responsibility-sensitive equality is a corrective principle, concerned only with lifting people out of shortfalls that are not their fault, not with total redistribution.

Second, I attach a sufficiency floor: no compensation rule may leave a responsible person below a decent minimum — the imprudent gambler who has spent all her compensation payment still gets some further assistance — but I insist this floor is a humanitarian add-on, outside egalitarian justice proper, so that luck egalitarianism itself can remain harsh about the consequences of choice above the floor.

What would count against this position? I want the defeaters ranked, from fatal to merely awkward, and I want you to distinguish three kinds: empirical or formal results the view predicts and could get wrong; cases it cannot describe without embarrassment (I'm thinking of the expensive-taste problem, the person who voluntarily cultivates a preference for champagne and soufflés, and whether my asymmetry can refuse to compensate her without refusing to compensate the victim of a plain brute-luck shortfall); and pressure from neighbouring views, especially the charge that responsibility-sensitivity means abandoning the imprudent and that my sufficiency floor is an ad hoc concession sufficientarianism is entitled to make but luck egalitarianism is not. For each defeater, say whether a committed defender of my qualified view would concede it counts against them, or dismiss it, and what the dismissal costs. Finally, assess honestly whether my two refinements have insulated the view: is it now a hybrid that collapses into whichever rival is convenient, or a coherent division of labour between egalitarian and sufficientarian commitments? If it is the former, is that fatal, or is a mixed view of this kind entitled to stand on its own?
