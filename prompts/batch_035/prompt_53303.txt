I've been reading about the three-way distinction between negative, positive, and republican liberty, and I want to know whether my gut verdicts on particular cases add up to any coherent view. An indulgent slave master who never issues a single command and lets his slave come and go as she pleases: the slave is still unfree. A smoker who genuinely wants to quit but keeps failing: unfree. A resident of a company town where a single firm owns the only employer, grocery, and pharmacy: unfree. Someone too poor to ever afford a holiday abroad: not unfree, merely unlucky. A citizen living under a law she detests that was nonetheless passed by a fair democratic majority: free. A worker whose contract allows the employer to sack her without cause at any moment, though in twenty years it never has: unfree. A person who can't drive because she never learned: not unfree. My tentative principle is that unfreedom requires an identifiable other person or agent who is somehow responsible for it — but I can see this pulls toward pure negative liberty, and negative liberty can't accommodate the indulgent master or the sackable worker. Is there one principle that captures all seven verdicts, or close to it? Please state it precisely, say what it predicts for a case I haven't listed — someone whose phobia stops her from leaving the house — and be honest about which of my verdicts no workable principle can save. I'm especially interested in whether my verdict on the smoker can be kept without also keeping the one about the holidaymaker.
