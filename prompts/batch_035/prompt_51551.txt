Exam question:

"Judy Benjamin parachutes at night into territory partly held by the Blue Army and partly held by the Red Army. Each army's territory contains both swamp and dry ground, so her epistemic space divides into four quadrants — Blue-and-swamp, Blue-and-dry, Red-and-swamp, Red-and-dry — and her prior distribution over them is uniform (1/4 each). By radio she receives the message: 'If you are in Blue territory, the probability that you are in swamp is 0.9.' She learns nothing else. (a) Explain why ordinary conditionalization on this message is unavailable. (b) Does the message give her any reason to change her probability that she is in Blue territory? Justify any updating rule you rely on. (This is a variant of van Fraassen's Judy Benjamin problem.)"

Student's answer:

"(a) Conditionalization works by ruling out worlds: the probability of the worlds incompatible with the evidence is set to zero and the remainder renormalized. The radio's message rules out no quadrant of Judy's epistemic space. She is told neither that she is in Blue territory nor that she is in swamp, and the message is consistent with her being in Red-and-dry. So the message is not a proposition she can conditionalize on, and Bayes's rule has nothing to operate on. It would be a mistake to restore conditionalization by reading the message as the material conditional (not-Blue or swamp) and conditioning on that: doing so would lower her probability for Blue territory by counting every Red world as confirming the message, when the message was in no way evidence about Red territory.

(b) What she learns is a conditional-probability assignment: her new P(swap | Blue) is 0.9. Adams's Thesis tells us that the probability of an indicative conditional just is the corresponding conditional probability, and Lewis showed in 1976 that this identification is compatible with the probability calculus, so the message can after all be represented as a single proposition, 'If Blue then swamp,' with probability 0.9. But no update on Blue follows from it. The constraint P(swap | Blue) = 0.9 fixes only the ratio of swamp to dry within Blue territory; it is compatible with any posterior value of P(Blue), since for any value there is a distribution satisfying it. So every posterior probability for Blue is rational, and therefore the only rational choice is to stick with the prior, P(Blue) = 1/2. The message gives her no grounds for change, and that is the moral of the Judy Benjamin problem: a conditional is evidence about the interior of its antecedent, not about the antecedent itself."
