My housemate is way into Dennett's four kinds of creatures — Darwinian (only blind evolution ever did any "trying" for them), Skinnerian (they can learn, but only through their own trial and error out in the world, sculpted by reward and punishment), Popperian (they run the trials offline, in an inner model, before acting), and Gregorian (named after Richard Gregory iirc — they get handed mind-tools from their culture, words, maps, notations, stuff that restructures the whole task). She sorts basically every animal and gadget she sees into one of these and it has honestly become a bit of a party trick at this point.

Her track record so far:
- a moth bashing into the lamp over and over: Darwinian, obviously
- a pigeon that needed a few hundred trials to learn to peck a key for grain: Skinnerian
- a rat at a junction in a maze that stops and turns its head down each arm before committing: Popperian (the trials happen in its head, she says)
- a chimp fishing termites with a stick after stripping it: still only Popperian — she was firm that a stick you hold is not a mind-tool, it just extends your arm
- me navigating with a paper map, marking my route in pencil: Gregorian

Here's my problem: I can see at least two different rules that fit every one of these calls. One is about where the trying happens — in the world, in the head, or outsourced into stuff around you. Another is about what gets improved — just the behavior, or the whole task setup, i.e. whether the tool changes the problem itself rather than the solution. And there might be others I haven't spotted.

She's coming over next week and I want to be ready with good cases. Give me her likely calls on each of these: a New Caledonian crow that bends a hook out of a strip and uses it to fish, where juveniles mostly pick up the design from watching adults; an octopus that learns to unscrew a jar purely by watching another octopus do it, never getting rewarded itself; and a big language model, which never does anything in the physical world at all but is made entirely out of words it got from everyone else. For each case, tell me what each of the candidate rules predicts and where they diverge. Then give me your best guess at her actual call, with reasons, and finally one further case I could throw at her that would genuinely split the candidate rules — one where they can't all give the same answer, so her call would tell me which rule she's really running.

Keep in mind: she's a Dennett loyalist, but she also likes being surprising.
