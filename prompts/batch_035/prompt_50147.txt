I help run a little film discussion group, and an argument from last week has been stuck in my head since. We were talking about review aggregators, because someone noticed the same movie can sit at like 85% on Rotten Tomatoes and a 45 metascore on Metacritic, and studios happily put whichever number looks better on the poster. The way these sites work is actually pretty different: Rotten Tomatoes just counts professional reviews as thumbs-up or thumbs-down and reports the percentage that came in positive. Metacritic converts each review into a number, weights some publications more heavily than others, and averages everything into a single 0-100 score. And then there's the IMDb user score, where the "judges" are basically whoever made an account. Three numbers, three methods, one movie, and the industry treats all of them as if they measure the same thing.

What I want to figure out is what each of these practices presupposes about taste. Does the Tomatometer presuppose that aesthetic judgment is fundamentally binary and that the right move is just to poll — like, the standard is whatever the majority of critics converge on? Does Metacritic presuppose something much stronger, an actual common quantitative scale of quality, the way temperature is a scale, such that one movie can be strictly "twice as good" territory as another? And does the user score presuppose nothing about quality at all and just measure audience satisfaction? I'm also bothered by the weighting thing: Metacritic quietly deciding that certain outlets count more seems like it smuggles in a claim about who the real judges are, without ever saying what makes them real.

The framework I keep reaching for is Hume's "Of the Standard of Taste," where the standard comes from the joint verdict of qualified judges — the ones with practice, lots of comparison, freedom from prejudice, good sense. Naively an aggregator looks like exactly that machine: poll the critics, let the verdict emerge. But the Tomatometer's binary threshold throws away exactly what a Humean judge would care about (degree of admiration), and the user score lets anyone vote regardless of Hume's qualifications. So: reconstruct the implicit theory of taste behind each of the three practices, tell me whether the industry is committed to mutually inconsistent theories in different places or whether they're all somehow compatible, and say what an aggregator designed from scratch around Hume's standard would have to do differently — who counts as a judge, how you'd ever identify them, and what you'd even report at the end. And if you think Hume is just the wrong lens entirely — say the sites really presuppose something like prediction markets for consumer satisfaction rather than any standard of taste — I'd honestly like to hear that argued too.
