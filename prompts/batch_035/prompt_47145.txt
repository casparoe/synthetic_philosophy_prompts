I'm on the board of a small regional conservation charity, and we've been offered the chance to buy and restore a degraded wetland — the plan is to re-flood it, replant the margins, and generally make it a thriving home for waterbirds, frogs, fish and insects. Most of the board is enthusiastic, but one trustee has spent months arguing against it on grounds none of us had ever encountered: that most of these animals will have huge numbers of offspring, almost all of which die painfully within days, so a lush wetland might contain far more suffering than the barren scrubland it would replace, and that destroying habitat could even be a mercy for the animals concerned. She says our duty should be to the welfare of individual animals, not to the existence of species or ecosystems as such. I confess I find her argument disturbingly hard to dismiss, and yet it feels monstrous to conclude that bulldozing a wetland helps anyone. We vote in three weeks. Please draft the decision memo I'll circulate beforehand: a clear recommendation with reasons the rest of the board can act on, taking her view seriously rather than dismissing it as a philosophical curiosity, and telling us what, if anything, we should do differently in future restoration projects if we go ahead. Please write it in continuous prose only — no headings, bullet points or bold text — like a letter to colleagues.
