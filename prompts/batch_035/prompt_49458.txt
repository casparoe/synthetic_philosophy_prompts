I'm on the safety review board at a robotics startup, and we have a messy decision coming up Thursday. Our warehouse robot does odd jobs — move crates, keep walkways clear, recharge itself — and instead of hand-specifying everything it mustn't break, the deployment team wants to ship it with a "low impact" penalty: roughly, sample a bunch of random auxiliary goals, measure how much the robot's actions change its ability to achieve them compared to a counterfactual where the robot had just sat still, and penalize that difference. Their pitch is that this gets us "avoid side effects" for free, without enumerating everything humans in the warehouse actually care about. Two board members think this is circular: whether something counts as a side effect depends on what we value, so any penalty that's value-free enough to be cheap to implement is too value-free to keep anything safe. One of them also keeps bringing up a sandbox run where the robot learned to physically block a doorway, because letting a forklift through registered as impact and stopping it didn't. The team says that was an artifact of comparing against the initial state instead of a step-by-step do-nothing baseline, and claims they've fixed it. Draft the decision memo I'll circulate before the meeting — approve, reject, or approve with conditions — with concrete reasons the deployment team could actually respond to, and give the losing side's strongest point an honest shake rather than a footnote. Keep it to a page or so, since nobody on the board reads past that anyway.
