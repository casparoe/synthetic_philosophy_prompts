I'm writing the opening chapter of a thesis on the ethics of rescue, and I've noticed that the literature keeps arguing particular cases without ever fixing what a theory in this area is supposed to accomplish. The debate is the one Taurek initiated about whether the number of lives at stake should count when a rescuer can save one group or another but not both, and my supervisor has suggested that before I evaluate any position I should first set out the specification: what any adequate theory of how numbers bear on rescue decisions has to deliver. That specification is what I want from you.

Write the desiderata. The list should cover, at minimum: the case where you can save either one stranger or five, with no ties of affection on either side, where the theory owes us a verdict even if the verdict is a coin flip; the equal-numbers case, where both groups are the same size, which seems to me diagnostic of whether a theory has content beyond "more is worse"; the intra-personal version, where the tradeoff is inside a single life (one person facing five hours of pain versus five people facing one hour each), since nearly everyone allows numbers to count there, and I want to know whether that permission is being quietly carried over into the case where the numbers are distinct persons; and the case where the one is someone you love and the five are strangers, which Taurek himself pressed. For each requirement, state what it is for, mark which you take to be non-negotiable and which are merely the prejudices of one camp, and — the part I care about most — consider whether any subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable. In particular: can a theory hold together "numbers must never by themselves settle who is saved" and "the theory must supply the rescuer with a justification that could be offered, face to face, to the party that dies"? Or does the second demand, once made precise, quietly reintroduce the numbers through the back door?

Only after all of that, and briefly, run Taurek's own view, a lottery-based view on which each person at risk gets an equal chance of rescue, and straightforward aggregative thinking, against the list. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases rather than on this scorecard; the scoring matters least, and I can redo it myself once the specification is right.
