I teach a graduate seminar in the philosophy of mathematics, and next week's session concerns actual infinities. I intend to stage a structured debate between two positions and then circulate a written comparison to the students as a seminar memo, so I am asking you to write that comparison for me. Please do not include citations, references, or suggestions for further reading; the students will have done the primary reading, and I want only the reasoning.

The two positions are these. Position A is the reductio strand, descending from the finitist reaction to Hilbert's Hotel: the paradoxes of the infinite are not merely surprising but are evidence that the actual infinite is incoherent, and a theory of infinity that systematically overrules every intuition those paradoxes elicit has explained the intuitions away rather than explained them. Position B is the Cantorian strand: the Hotel and its cousins show only that our size judgments, trained exclusively on finite sets, fail to transfer to the infinite case, where equinumerosity under a bijection is what cardinal size means; on this view, saying a fully occupied hotel can always accommodate another guest is not a contradiction but the same fact described in a vocabulary tuned to finite hotels.

To keep the discussion from collapsing into recitation, I will put to the class a variant I have constructed, which I call the demoted hotel. The hotel begins with every even-numbered room occupied and every odd-numbered room empty. A renovation is then announced under which every single guest will be moved to a different room, and no new guests will arrive. The rule adopted is this: for each k, the guest in room 4k+2 moves to room 4k+4, and the guest in room 4k+4 moves to room 4k+2. After the renovation, every guest has changed rooms, no guest has left, and the occupancy pattern is numerically identical to what it was before: the even rooms are occupied, the odd rooms are empty, and nothing whatsoever about the hotel's occupancy has changed.

Please compare the two positions, devoting particular attention to the following three questions. First, the dialectical status of intuition: on Position A, the queasiness the paradoxes induce is data; on Position B, it is an artifact of misapplied finite-set instincts. Is there a principled way to adjudicate between treating an intuition as evidence and treating it as noise, or does each side inevitably beg the question against the other? Second, the word "full": in describing the original hotel I have equivocated between "full" meaning that no room is free under the current assignment and "full" meaning that no additional guest could be accommodated, and the students should be made to see that the paradox trades on sliding between these. Does the Cantorian's insistence on separating the two senses fully dissolve the paradox, or does it merely relocate the puzzle into the question of why the separated senses coincide in the finite case and come apart in the infinite one? Third, the demoted hotel specifically: the renovation is a global change composed entirely of changes that collectively have zero effect. Position A should want to argue that something like this is a defect in the notion of an actual infinite; Position B should want to argue that it is the finite-tuned notion of change failing to transfer, exactly parallel to the failure of the finite-tuned notion of fullness. I want you to assess which position handles the case better, and to say whether the case is merely a repackaging of the original Hotel or raises something genuinely new — in particular, whether a change with no effect differs relevantly from a bijection with a curious property.

Somewhere in your treatment, I would also like the students confronted with the finitist's awkwardness in stating their own view: the thesis that there are no actual infinities is naturally glossed as a claim about all numbers, all sets, or all possible concrete collections, and it is not obvious that this can be said without quantification that the finitist should themselves find suspect. Indicate how seriously this costs Position A, without pretending it is decisive.

Begin your response with your bottom-line judgment of the comparison in the first sentence, then give the reasoning. No preamble, no restatement of my request, and no closing summary section.
