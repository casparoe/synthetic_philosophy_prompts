so my writing group does this thing where we each bring a poem on something from a class we took this year, and i took a phil of mind seminar that spent two weeks on panpsychism, which mostly meant two weeks on the combination problem. i want to bring a sestina, and i have a specific idea about how the form should carry the argument, tell me if its off.

the thought: in a sestina you get six end-words that return in every stanza, permuted but never added to. that maps (i think?) onto the subject-summing version of the combination problem — the micro-experiences come with their small palette of qualities, you rearrange and recombine them at every level of organization, and the question is whether a new subject ever shows up as something over and above the rearrangement, or whether you just get the same old fragments shuffled. so: the six end-words should all come from the micro-level vocabulary (warmth, pulse, tone, whatever you choose), and the poem is never allowed to name the macro-subject directly. the unified experiencer is the thing that never arrives at the end of a line — the reader should feel that absence as the poem's whole burden. the envoi can break toward it but shouldnt resolve it cleanly: either lean toward "it never arrives" (the panpsychist's embarassment) or toward "it was there in the combining all along" (the structuralist escape hatch), but dont have it both ways.

accuracy matters. none of the "so you think rocks are conscious" stuff — the micro-subjects should be recognizable as what panpsychists actually claim, spatially tiny, non-compositional. if you want, the envoi can nod to the old James-style worry that no number of lower feelings ever sums to a higher one, but paraphrase, dont quote him.

and honestly, if you think the whole mapping is confused — that end-words are words and not experiences, that the sestina machinery is a clever pun rather than an argument — say so plainly instead of forcing it. same if you think subject-summing is the wrong half of the problem to build on and the palette should be doing different work.

deliverable: the sestina itself, then one section titled "notes on liberties" (roughly 150-250 words) covering where the poem leans on contested readings, which version of the combination problem it stages, and any place you deliberately bent the metaphysics for the verse.
