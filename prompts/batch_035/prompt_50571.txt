A history podcast I listen to gave this rundown of the Browning vs Goldhagen debate about the Holocaust perpetrators, and I want to know how much of it I can beleive. The host said, roughly:

"There's this famous study by a historian named Browning of a battalion of middle-aged German reserve policemen in Poland who ended up shooting tens of thousands of Jews and deporting even more to the camps. What he found was that these were totally ordinary working class guys, not Nazi fanatics, not specially selected, basically just drafted into police duty. His explanation was basically the Milgram experiment at scale: they pulled the trigger because they were ordered to and people just obey authority. He also thought that basically anyone put in their position would have done the same, so there was nothing special about the Germans at all. Then Goldhagen came along with a book arguing the opposite: ordinary Germans were so soaked in antisemitism by that point that they actually wanted to kill Jews — they weren't just following orders, they were eager participants. So basically you have to pick: either everyone is a potential perpetrator given the right situation, or the Germans of that era were uniquely poisoned by hatred. It can't be both."
