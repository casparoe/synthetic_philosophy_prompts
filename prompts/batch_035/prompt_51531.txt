I write novels, and the one I'm working on now has a fifteen-year-old narrator who becomes convinced she has dissociative identity disorder after falling down an online rabbit hole -- the plot is partly about how getting the label changes who she thinks she is. A friend who does philosophy of science mentioned Ian Hacking's looping effects, how classifying people changes the people classified and eventually reshapes the category itself, and honestly that's kind've exactly what my book is doing. So now I'm a bit nervous. I want a procedure I can run on my own drafts -- I tend to write two or three characters carrying some psychiatric label per book, my audience is YA-adjacent, and there's a real chance my depictions land on readers who are actively hunting for words for what they feel. Give me the questions to ask in order about a given portrayal, what a sufficient answer to each looks like, where the procedure stops at "rewrite or cut this," and where it has to hand off to a judgment call between me and my editor because the question is about values not facts. I don't have a clinician on call and can only afford a sensativity reader for one character per book, so the procedure has to be runnable by me alone with a draft and a weekend. Then run it on three things: the self-diagnosing teenager (where the looping is the whole point of the novel and I really don't want to cut it), a minor character with fairly textbook OCD that I've written for warmth rather than drama, and a character whose "condition" I basically invented by blending symptoms from a few real ones because it served the metaphor. Be honest about where the procedure will tell me to do something I won't like, and about what it can't settle at all.
