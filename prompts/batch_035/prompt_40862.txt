In a paper on political liberty I'm drafting, I list my own commitments as follows and want a consistency check before I submit it. (1) Negative liberty — the absence of interference by others — is the right concept to use when assessing how free the members of a society are. (2) Republican freedom as non-domination is a genuinely distinct concept, not a refinement of (1), and I agree with Pettit that the slave of a kind, non-interfering master is nonetheless unfree. (3) I endorse Berlin's warning about positive liberty: no coercion may be justified by appeal to a person's "true" interests or authentic self, and history shows where that reasoning leads. (4) I defend paternalistic laws such as seatbelt and helmet mandates, and I hold that they can be justified without invoking any positive conception of liberty. (5) A state's mere possession of an unexercised power — one that could be used to interfere but has not been, and is checked by strong conventions — does not by itself make anyone less free. Which pairs of these commitments are in genuine tension, and how badly? Which apparent tensions dissolve on inspection? For each real tension, what is the cheapest repair and what does it cost elsewhere in the set? Present the tensions from most to least severe, and tell me whether a single revision would clean up the most — if so, which one.
