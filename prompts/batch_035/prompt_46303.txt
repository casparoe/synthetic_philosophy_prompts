I have been having a version of the same argument for over a decade — it started in the comment threads of a now-dormant rationality blog, resurfaced on the forums during the whole "religion as load-bearing social technology" discussion cycle, and has recently flared up again because my interlocutor and I both have children now and are making opposite choices about what, if anything, to raise them into. We have agreed to stop trading anecdotes and each collect an independent, decomposed credence before comparing notes, so I am outsourcing half of that to you.

The proposition, stated as precisely as I can manage: holding fixed a person's other circumstances — income, geography, family stability, temperament — being a sincere and moderately observant member of a mainstream religious tradition makes that person, on average, somewhat more likely to behave in ways that a morally serious and well-informed observer, religious or not, would judge to be morally better. We are explicitly bracketing the object-level theological question: whether or not any religion is true, this is about the instrumental effect. My friend puts his credence around 0.75; I put mine around 0.4, and I suspect my number is low mainly because I cannot get the confounders under control, which is not a principled reason to discount the effect.

Give me your own credence for the proposition as stated, and then decompose it. At minimum I want numbers, with a sentence or two of justification each, for the following terms — plus any terms you think I have wrongly omitted, which I will consider a legitimate correction to my framing:

1. The definitional term: the probability that "morally better" here is determinate enough, across the reasonable range of moral frameworks (consequentialist, contractualist, virtue-ethical), for the proposition to have a stable truth value at all, rather than silently tracking conformity to whatever the observer's local majority happens to approve.

2. The selection-versus-treatment term: of the observed correlation between religiosity and prosocial behavior at the individual level, roughly how much is genuine treatment effect versus people with preexisting prosocial inclinations selecting into religion — noting that the direction of the selection itself may depend on the surrounding society.

3. The community-versus-belief term: conditional on there being a real treatment effect, the probability that it is carried by embedded community, repeated ritual, and visibility-to-congregation rather than by doctrine or professed belief, such that a functionally equivalent secular institution would reproduce most of it.

4. The ecological term: the probability that the cross-national picture — several low-religiosity societies sustaining very high generalized trust, alongside high-religiosity societies with low trust — is actually informative about the individual-level question, rather than confounded by wealth, institutional quality, and state capacity in a way that makes the inference an ecological fallacy.

5. The substitution term: the probability that a fully secular society can sustain the trust and cooperative surplus that religious participation seems to provide at moderate scale, given that the existing secular substitutes (Sunday Assemblies, sports clubs, hobby communities, organized volunteering) appear to have much higher churn and weaker long-term commitment.

Combine these into your overall number by whatever method you think honest — they are not independent, so tell me how you handled the correlations. Then two further things. First, tell me which of these terms my friend at 0.75 and I at 0.40 most likely disagree about, given that we both accept the same descriptive literature and both know about the Nordic cases; I want to know whether our disagreement is about the world or about the wording of the proposition. Second, tell me which single term you are least confident in and what specific evidence — a study design, a natural experiment, a historical case — would move your overall number the most. I am less interested in whether you land near 0.4 or near 0.75 than in whether you can show me the joints at which the question actually comes apart.
