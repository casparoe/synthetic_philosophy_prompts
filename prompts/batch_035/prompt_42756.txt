I'm a PhD student in metaphysics, and this term our department is piloting a small adversarial-collaboration scheme, modeled on the ones experimental psychologists have run: two researchers who have been deadlocked on a question for years agree to facilitated sessions and a single co-authored document at the end. I've been assigned as rapporteur to the first pair, and my immediate task is to produce a draft of the joint statement that I can put in front of them at the opening session — one they could, after amendments, actually sign.

The two are a structuralist who defends the identity of indiscernibles against Max Black's two-sphere universe, chiefly by appeal to weak discernibility in the manner of Simon Saunders: the spheres in Black's world, though matched in every monadic property, stand to one another in an irreflexive relation — each lies at some fixed distance from the other — and this, she holds, is enough for them to be discernible and hence not identical, so the principle survives the counterexample in weakened form. His opponent holds that Black's world is a genuine refutation: the obtaining of any such relation between "the two" presupposes exactly the numerical distinctness it was supposed to ground, so weak discernibility relabels the conclusion rather than earning it; if there are indeed two spheres, they are individuated, if at all, by primitive thisnesses — haecceities — rather than by any qualitative or relational facts, and the principle at best survives as a regulative maxim rather than a truth. They have been circling this for the better part of a decade, mostly past each other.

Please draft the joint statement. It should contain a numbered agreed-claims section covering what both can sign — at minimum, what Black's setup does and does not show about the strong and weak forms of the principle, whether the case turns on any substantive assumptions about the symmetry of space rather than on Leibniz's law alone, and which parts of their disagreement are genuine as opposed to verbal. This should be followed by a disagreements table stating the residual disagreements in wording each side would accept for its own view: I expect one line to concern whether weak discernibility is a legitimate form of discernibility or a smuggling-in of the conclusion; another the coherence of a one-sphere world qualitatively indistinguishable from Black's two-sphere world, and what its coherence or incoherence would establish about haecceitism; another whether the principle is a necessary metaphysical truth, a contingent deliverary of physics, or a norm of conceptual hygiene. For each crux, add the case, thought experiment, or argument both sides agree would bear on it, together with each side's prediction about that case, recorded as it would be made in advance of examining it. Where you find that part of the dispute rests on an ambiguity in the terms "indiscernible" or "ground of distinctness," mark which part and explain why, in wording both parties would sign off on.
