I'm a science-and-ideas columnist working on a piece about what happens when the invention of tradition gets automated. Consider an online community of a few hundred thousand people that has adopted, as its founding ritual, an annual holiday whose supposed medieval origins were fabricated wholesale by a language model last year — nobody seriously disputes the fabrication, yet the observance is now genuinely meaningful to participants and is beginning to be handed down to their children. If Eric Hobsbawm and Benedict Anderson could be shown this case, what would each of them make of it? I want you to argue from their actual commitments rather than general vibes: does the language model play the role of the nineteenth-century nationalist intellectual who fabricates ceremonies, or of the print technology that Anderson saw as the material precondition of national imagining — or does it collapse the two in a way that unsettles their frameworks? And would either thinker treat the community as genuinely imagined in their sense once the fabricated origin story is known to be fake and known by all to be known? Please also say where you would expect the two to disagree with each other on this case, and which of the two frameworks you think fares worse under it and why.
