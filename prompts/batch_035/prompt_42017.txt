I'm taking a course on early modern philosophy and have become increasingly fascinated by Leibniz, partly because of how strange his ambitions look in hindsight. Beyond the better-known metaphysics of monads, he spent much of his life championing the characteristica universalis — a universal formal language in which all human knowledge could be expressed — together with a general calculus of reasoning. His hope was that philosophical and even theological disputes could someday be settled the way we settle arithmetic problems, with disputants calmly sitting down together to compute the answer. A famous exclamation of his, often rendered in Latin as a single imperative verb, captures this optimism: rather than shouting at each other, the parties to a dispute should simply calculate.

At the same time, I've been reading contemporary discussions of global coordination and existential risk, where something called the "singleton hypothesis" comes up: the idea that Earth's future might come to be governed by a single decision-making agency — whether established by treaty, conquest, or a decisive technological lead — that coordinates all strategically relevant action on the planet. Importantly, a singleton need not be a tyranny; it could be democratic, or a tightly coordinated network of institutions that functions as one decision-maker. The argument in favor is that a singleton may be the only stable way to prevent arms races in destructive technologies and to enable very long-term planning; the argument against is that it risks locking in bad values or halting the epistemic experimentation that competition between independent powers provides.

My question: how would Leibniz react to the singleton hypothesis? Please write an essay predicting his likely position, grounding your answer in his actual commitments — his faith in the power of formal reasoning to resolve conflict, his doctrine of pre-established harmony among autonomous monads, his theodicean claim that this is the best of all possible worlds, and his career as a courtier and advisor who was himself quite canny about the dangers of absolutist princes. Which of these commitments pull him toward welcoming a planetary singleton, and which pull against? In particular, consider whether he would care about the difference between a singleton grounded in genuinely shared rational insight versus one grounded merely in power, and whether his pluralism of monads is compatible with a single apex coordinator at the level of human institutions. Conclude with your own judgment on whether Leibniz, all things considered, should be counted a friend or a foe of the singleton hypothesis.
