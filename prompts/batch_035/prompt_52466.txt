I'm a PhD student in cognitive science preparing a follow-up to the experimental work on stakes and knowledge ascription, and I want to get the theoretical framing right before preregistering the study.

Here is my understanding of the background. Epistemic contextualism and pragmatic encroachment are, as far as I can tell, two names for the same thesis: how much evidence it takes to count as knowing depends on the practical stakes, so whether a subject "knows" that, say, the bank will be open on Saturday varies with how much turns on being right. The experimental philosophy literature on stakes effects appears to have borne this out empirically, which — if I'm reading it correctly — should settle the old dispute between contextualists and invariantists, leaving only a terminological choice to be made. That framing generates two questions for my design.

First, a purely practical one: which of the two labels is now standard in the field, so that my paper uses the preferred term? Second, and more pressing: the stakes manipulations I've seen vary what is at stake for the subject inside the vignette, but if the thesis concerns the truth-conditions of "knows" as used in a context, it seems the relevant context belongs to the ascriber, not the subject — so shouldn't the manipulation instead vary the reader's stakes, since the reader is the one making the ascription? How would you advise me to operationalize the construct, and should I expect the subject-stakes and ascriber-stakes manipulations to yield different results, or are they just two routes to the same effect?
