I've fallen down a rabbit hole about the Löbian obstacle and before I read anyone's proposed fix I want the spec straight in my head. So: an agent that reasons by formal proof about the world can't in general prove that its own future reasoning is sound -- that's just Löb's theorem biting -- which means it can't verify a successor/self-modification, and pairs of such agents can't easily prove they'll cooperate with each other. Write the desiderata that any adequate solution has to meet: what it has to actually deliver (verified self-modification? mutual cooperation in a prisoner's dilemma? robustness to agents that exploit whatever trust scheme you set up?), which distinctions it has to respect (e.g. between the agent trusting its logic and trusting that its logic is sound -- I suspect a lot of fixes quietly slide between those), and what it can't give up (computability, not being exploitable, not just defining a new agent from scratch and declaring victory). For each requirement say what the case is behind it, and mark which ones you think are non-negotiable versus just what a particular camp (decision-theory people, formal-verification people) cares about. Most importantly, tell me if you think some subset is jointly unsatisfiable -- it would be a shame if the requirement of provable non-exploitability and the requirement of achieving cooperation can't both hold, since that tension is arguably the whole point. Don't score existing approaches like logical uncertainty or proof-based decision theories against the list, the list itself is what I want.
