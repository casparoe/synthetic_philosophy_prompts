I'm TA-ing a virtue ethics seminar and the last session is on situationism, the whole "maybe character traits are basically an illusion and situations do the work" attack from social psych (milgram's prods, the seminary students who stepped over a slumped stranger in a doorway because they were running late to give a talk on the Good Samaritan parable, that kind of thing). I want to close with a poem instead of another round of discussion, and I've become kind of obsessed with the thought that a sestina is the perfect form for this, because a sestina literally takes the same six words and rearranges them differently in every stanza — same elements, different arrangement, which is almost exactly what the situationist claims about people.

So: write a sestina where the six end-words are split three and three. Three trait words — courage, kindness, honesty — and three situation words — hurry, room, prods (yes, "prods," as in the experimenter's lines that kept pushing subjects forward). The form should do the arguing. In the first three or four stanzas, let the situation words dominate the sense, so the trait words mostly survive by getting bent into situational readings: kindness that only shows up when nobody's watching the clock, courage that turns out to be not being in a hurry, honesty that depends on who else is in the room. Then let the final stanza or two push back, with the trait words regathering their force — but please don't resolve it into a tidy victory for either side, since honestly our seminar didn't resolve it either and I don't want the poem to fake a confidence we didn't earn.

For the envoi (three lines, all six words used, two per line), I want each line to pair exactly one trait word with one situation word, so the close forces the two vocabularies to share single lines together — that's the whole debate compressed into three lines. Someone in the class who's read their Doris or their Harman should nod along and be able to say which stanzas are the situationist's and which are the virtue ethicist's reply, and someone who hasn't should still get a real poem about being prodded. Aim for it to work on both levels, anway — that's the whole point of ending the semester this way.
