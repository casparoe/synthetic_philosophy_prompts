I serve on an incident-review board that has to assign responsibility for harms caused by autonomous systems, and I've come to suspect that most disagreements about responsibility gaps are really disagreements about what such a theory is for. So before I read further, I want the specification: what any adequate account of responsibility for harms produced by autonomous systems must deliver. I'll propose some candidate requirements, and your job is to say for each whether it is a genuine desideratum or the prejudice of one camp, whether the list is jointly satisfiable, and which entry does the most work in excluding candidates. The candidates: the account must handle the original gap case, where a learning system produces an outcome no designer foresaw or directly controlled; it must not collapse into the many-hands verdict in which everyone involved bears a vanishing share and no one is answerable; it must handle redundancy, where the harm was shaped by the training data, the deployment decision, the override policy, and the regulatory sign-off, with no counterfactually necessary link, so that standard causal tests exonerate everyone; it must distinguish what was foreseeable at design time from what became knowable only through deployment or incident data, and assign different weights accordingly; it must remain action-guiding for a body that must issue a determinate verdict within weeks, not a research program; and it must register the mismatch I actually encounter, namely that the parties most legitimately blameworthy are rarely the ones best placed to prevent recurrence, so an account that only ever blames the preventer, or only ever blames the foreseeer, is useless to me. I particularly want your view on whether the action-guiding requirement is in tension with proportionate distribution under redundancy, since I suspect it is. Then, briefly and without scoring in detail, say which of three approaches best survives the list: keeping designers responsible through a forward-looking duty to design for reasonably foreseeable misuse; requiring meaningful human control, so that an outcome is traceable to human moral judgment and the system's behavior tracks human reasons; and dissolving the gap by shifting to institutional or strict liability. Write in continuous prose only, with no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables, as if composing a letter.
