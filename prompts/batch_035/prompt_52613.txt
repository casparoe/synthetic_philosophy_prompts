Last night a friend and I spent several hours arguing about actual infinities, and I would like to test whether my side of the argument survives a more careful formulation than the one I managed at her kitchen table. She is a philosophy graduate student who holds that completed infinite totalities are coherent, and she leaned heavily on Hilbert's hotel: the fully occupied hotel with countably many rooms that can still admit one new guest, by moving the occupant of each room n to room n+1, and can even admit countably many new guests at once, by moving the occupant of room n to room 2n and filling the odd-numbered rooms. I write functional code for a living, and my instinct throughout was that I construct and manipulate objects of exactly this kind every day — lazy lists, infinite streams defined corecursively, on which such reindexings are one-liners. She replied that I was conflating the potential with the actual; I replied that her resort to cardinal arithmetic invoked distinctions I could not even state in my own terms. We ended nowhere.

Here is what I would like from you. Restate the standard mathematical case for actual infinities — the hotel argument, Cantor's diagonal proof that the reals are uncountable, and the countable/uncountable distinction generally — in the vocabulary I actually use: lazy evaluation, streams, cons and tail, guarded corecursion, bisimulation, productivity in the type-theoretic sense that every demanded element is delivered in finite time, and the fact that a consumer of a stream ever sees only a finite prefix. Do it as faithfully as you can, without smuggling in set-theoretic assumptions and presenting them as translation. Then give me a residue report with three parts. First, what maps cleanly: for instance, does the hotel correspond to nothing more than taking the tail of the stream of natural numbers and consing a new head onto it? Second, what can be stated only with distortion, and what cannot be stated at all — I suspect the hierarchy of infinities, in which some completed totalities strictly outnumber others, has no counterpart in my vocabulary, since from the standpoint of productivity all infinite streams look alike; tell me whether that suspicion is correct, and whether anything else fails to carry. Third, tell me where the potential/actual distinction itself lands: does it correspond to the difference between a productive corecursive definition, which is never more than the generator of finite prefixes, and the stream as a completed object available to bisimulation reasoning — or does the distinction dissolve entirely in the translation, as I rashly claimed over the table? Conclude by saying whether the untranslatable residue is better read as a blind spot in my vocabulary or as a feature of the original mathematics that does not earn its keep, and be candid if the honest verdict is that my "it's just lazy evaluation" was only half right.
