I've been in and around the LLMs-are-simulators-vs-agents argument for like two years now and it never goes anywhere. On one side you have people saying a model trained on next-token prediction is playing characters -- it simulates agents, it isn't one, and any agentic behavior you elicit is a mask worn by a persona while the underlying model is more like a shapeless pile of heuristics with no stable goals. On the other side you have people saying no, the model itself is the right locus: train on human text long enough and something with fairly stable preferences and instrumental reasoning falls out, and the character framing is just an artifact of how we happen to prompt it. And there's a third camp that thinks the dichotomy itself is confused because agency isn't a thing a system either is or isn't, it's a thing a whole deployed setup -- weights plus scaffold plus context -- can have more or less of.

The frustrating part is that everyone agrees on the architecture, everyone agrees on the training objective, and nobody has moved an inch. So what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is it verbal, about whether "agent" picks out a behavioral pattern or an internal structure? Are the sides implicitly answering different questions -- what the model is versus what we should treat it as in deployment? Or is there a hidden empirical question in there (do the weights contain something like a stable goal-directed core, or just context-dependent personas?) that interpretability could in principle settle -- and if so, would either side even accept that as settling it, or would they just relitigate what the result means?

My hunch is its mostly two questions run together under one word, but I can't quite make that stick because both sides do seem to make claims that are claims about the weights, not just about usage. Diagnose it for me using what each side actually says, and tell me what would count as progress here -- a sharper vocabulary, a specific kind of interpretability finding, a conceptual distinction people could actually agree on -- or whether this is one of those cases where the impasse is telling us the question is badly posed and we should just drop it.
