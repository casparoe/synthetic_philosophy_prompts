Is there a name for this? I got put in charge of my company's support chatbot a few months back (small company, I wear a few hats, long story), and one of my jobs is deciding at what point the bot has to confess it's a bot. Watching users interact with it, plus reading way too many flagged-ticket threads, I keep noticing the same pattern and I cant find the word for it.

People don't seem to judge "is this a mind" by counting successes. They judge it by failure modes, and the evidence is wildly asymmetric. Our bot can hold up its end of a conversation for thirty turns and users are still on the fence about it; then it repeats the exact same apology twice and the verdict snaps instantly to "it's a bot, obviously." Same thing in reverse: a real person who answers too fast and too politely gets suspected of being one, and then a single typo or one cranky non-sequitur clears them completely. One machinelike error outweighs any amount of humanlike success. It's basically how captchas work — one failed squiggle identifies you as a bot, but no number of passed squiggles proves you're human. Disconfirming evidence is cheap, confirming evidence never quite arrives.

I know about the ELIZA effect (people over-attributing understanding to a simple program), but that's the opposite direction — people getting fooled — not people running this error-first detection procedure. And I've heard the phrase "reverse Turing test" thrown at captchas, but I dont think that captures the asymmetry either; it sounds more like just running the test with a different subject. What I can't tell is whether the philosophy literature on the Turing test and behavioral criteria for mind already has a concept for this — detecting (or ruling out) a mind by its characteristic ways of failing rather than by its successes. Or is it just the ordinary logic of any diagnostic test, applied to minds, with nothing special about minds at all? That second possibility actually bugs me, because it feels like there's something specific here about how people treat a mind as the kind of thing that must be shown consistent across time, while a bot can be convicted off one slip.

If there's an established term I want the real one, plus how it relates to the nearest neighbors and where it parts ways with them. But if the nearest candidates don't actually fit, tell me that plainly and try to carve the concept out properly — what it is, what it isn't, where the edges are, and what would count as a borderline case. And please push back if I'm actually describing two seperate phenomena and mushing them together (the asymmetry and the failure-mode-as-fingerprint thing might come apart — a captcha scores errors without caring that they're machinelike in character). I might be.
