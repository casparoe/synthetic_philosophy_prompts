I run a small center that funds three decision theorists for two years, and I have to pick a single research theme on the coherence arguments for expected utility maximization — the family of money-pump and representation-theoretic results that purport to show that any rational agent's preferences must be those of an expected utility maximizer. The practical stake is real: our conclusions feed into debates about whether sufficiently capable AI systems will end up expected-utility-like as a requirement of rationality rather than a fact of their architecture, and that question matters for how such systems are modeled and forecast. So the prioritization should be sensitive to that downstream use.

Rank the open questions we could make the theme, and defend the ordering. Candidate questions include, at least: whether money-pump arguments survive agents that act resolutely on prior plans, or that adopt a blanket policy of refusing any trade away from an option they already hold, so that the intransitivity is never cashed out into an actual loss; whether the axioms can be derived one at a time or only as a package, such that loosening any single one collapses the whole argument; whether intransitivities that can only be exploited by a pumper who would itself take losses, or only stochastically, are genuine rational defects at all; whether agents with incomplete preferences are immune to pumps or merely vulnerable to a stochastic variant; and whether the arguments transfer to bounded agents for whom being pumped out of a few cents once may cost less than the cognitive vigilance needed to avoid it. But I'm not wedded to that list — argue for additions and cuts.

Use explicit criteria: how much downstream work depends on the answer, tractability for theorists working a priori over a two-year horizon, and neglectedness relative to the questions everyone already rehashes. Also tell me what would move an item up or down — what a partial result on one question would do to the priority of the others.
