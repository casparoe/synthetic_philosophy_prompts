So here's something I keep bumping into as a science and ideas journalist, and I'd like your read on it as a diagnosis rather than a verdict. The debate over illusionism about phenomenal consciousness seems to me to be completely stalled, and I want to work out what kind of stall it actually is.

Quick sketch so we're on the same page. The illusionists -- Dennett was the big one, Keith Frankish has carried it forward -- say roughly this: the idea that we have intrinsic, private, ineffable qualitative properties of experience (the "what-it's-like" stuff) is a sort of user-illusion generated by the brain's model of itself. There's no phenomenal layer over and above the functional one. The other lot -- Chalmers, Galen Strawson to an extent, various panpsychists -- say the appearance of phenomenality isn't the sort of thing that can be illusory, because the appearance just is the datum: if it seems to you that there's something it's like, then there is something it's like, and no amount of neuroscience touches that.

Here's my working hypothesis, offered as a hypothesis. It seems to me both sides use claims of the form "it seems thus-and-so" in different ways, and the fight is really about the epistemic status of those claims. For the anti-illusionist, a seeming report is incorrigible data that any theory must accommodate; for the illusionist, it's just more output of the system doing its introspecting, which can be wrong about its own workings like any other measurement. If that's right, the disagreement is partly verbal, but not entirely -- there's a live question underneath about whether the brain's model of itself has the kind of privileged access that would make its reports unrevisable.

So: is my diagnosis right, or am I missing a substantive empirical or conceptual question that's doing the real work? And given whatever diagnosis you land on, what would actually count as progress here -- a distinction, a formal result, the right experiment, or just a change of subject? I ask because I'm planning a long piece and I don't want to write another "philosophers disagree about consciousness" sampler; I want to say something true about why nobody moves.
