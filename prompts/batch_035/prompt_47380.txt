As a novelist I've run with Dennett's four kinds of creatures -- Darwinian (hardwired reflexes only), Skinnerian (learns by trial and error in real time), Popperian (can rehearse internally, let the hypothesis die instead of itself), Gregorian (gets handed mental tools, mostly culture and words, that reshape what it can even notice) -- as a shorthand for building characters. But I want to stop winging it and have an actual procedure I can run on every character in a draft, one by one, before I show it to anyone. Please write me that procedure: the questions to ask in order, what answer puts the character at each level, what to do about characters who sit at one level for some things and another for others, and where the procedure should admit it can't decide and just hand it back to me as a judgment call about the story I want to tell. Then run it on three characters: a servant girl who can't read but is brilliant at reading people, a scholar who is erudite but never once changes his plan when facts change, and a toddler. Also, be upfront about which parts of Dennett's scheme are settled background and which are contested or just his own framing -- I don't want to build characters on something that only looks like established science, and please flag it if the hierarchy itself (each level supposdly subsuming the last) is doing work that the actual biology and psychology doesn't support.
