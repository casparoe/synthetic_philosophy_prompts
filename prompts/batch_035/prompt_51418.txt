I'm a backend engineer (no formal philosphy background, everything I know about ethics I absorbed from arguments with my college roommate) and every December I go through the same dumb cycle with myself about how much of my salary to give away. The roommate is a hardcore utilitarian and keeps sending me stuff about how I should basically live on almost nothing and give the rest, and my sister thinks anything past a few percent is performative guilt. I don't want another lecture, I want a procedure -- like a runbook for an on-call incident, something I can sit down with once a year over a weekend with a spreadsheet and actually follow.

From arguing with the roommate I've gathered that there's this thing called the demandingness objection to consequentialism: if the theory says you have to keep giving until the next dollar does more good for you than for someone worse off, that's just too much to ask of a person, and that's supposed to be a serious problem for the view, not just people being lazy. And I gather there are a bunch of responses -- some say the theory is right and it's fine that it's this demanding, some say you're allowed to keep enough for your own projects and relationships (agent-centered prerogatives I think?), some make it about fairness and not being the sucker who does everything while other givers free-ride, and I lose track after that. I don't need you to settle who's right. I need the disagrement turned into something operational.

So: write me a yearly decision procedure for the "how much do I donate, and how much of my time/volunteering counts toward it" decision. The questions in order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each one, where the procedure is allowed to stop with "you're done, go live your life," and where it has to say "no, you actually have to give more than is comfortable." Be honest about which steps are just a values judgment dressed up as arithmetic -- like, don't pretend the number I pick for "how much do I need to keep to not resent this" is objective. And where the procedure would genuinely give different answers depending on which philosophical response to demandingness you load into it, say so instead of hiding it.

Then run it on three people so I can see it work: (1) me -- mid thirties, no kids, decent salary, currently giving about 4% and feeling vaguely bad about it; (2) a coworker in the same boat except she has two kids and is helping support aging parents on another continent; (3) the roommate himself, who would happily give 50% if the procedure told him to, and who I suspect the procedure shouldn't let do that. I want at least one worked case where the procedure gives an answer the person running it won't like -- if it always comes back "you're fine," it's not a procedure, it's a hug.

Constraints: it has to be runnable by one non-philosopher in an afternoon, every input should be something I can actually check or estimate myself (no "compute the marginal utility of a dollar to a stranger halfway across the world"), and it should say what it does NOT settle -- I know there's a whole separate fight about whether it's better to help strangers far away vs people in your own community, and I'd rather the procedure just tell me where it punts on that and why than pretend it doesn't exist.
