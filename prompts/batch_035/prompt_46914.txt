For the third year running, my Kant reading group has had the same fight about the murderer at the door — a would-be killer asks where your friend is hiding, and the question is what Kant, or Kantianism, requires of you — and nobody has moved an inch. The texts are the same and the arguments are old: one faction holds the standard reading of the 1797 reply to Benjamin Constant, on which Kant bites the bullet — lying to the murderer is wrong, and if you lie and, through some turn of events, your friend dies, you are responsible for it, whereas if you tell the truth you are not. A second faction offers a reconstruction: the bullet doesn't need biting, because deceiving the murderer makes you complicit in whatever his plans become, while refusing to lie leaves the deed his own — so Kant's verdict is defensible on Kantian grounds. A third faction goes textual: the murderer case comes from Constant, not from Kant's major works, and the essay is a polemical artifact, so the whole debate targets a version of Kant no serious interpreter holds. Each faction wins a different point every session and none of them concedes the others, which makes me suspect the disagreement is not really about Kant at all. My hypothesis is that there are two questions run together under one heading — a historical question about what the man actually held, and a moral question about whether lying in such cases is ever wrong — and that the sides are quietly answering different ones. Is that the right diagnosis, or is this a verbal dispute about what counts as "defending Kant," or a genuine clash of bedrock intuitions about complicity, truth-telling, and responsibility that argument cannot move? Please give a diagnosis supported by what each faction actually says, and then tell me honestly what would count as progress: a distinction between the interpretive and the moral questions, a new way of framing the complicity argument, more careful attention to the texts, or nothing — and if nothing, why not. Please write your answer in American English.
