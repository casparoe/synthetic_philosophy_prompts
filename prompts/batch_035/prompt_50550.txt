I write backend code for a living, so my philosophy background is basically arguing in comment threads, but I've gotten obsessed with this question about possible worlds and what our words pick out inside them. Here are my gut verdicts, and I want to know if there's one principle that captures them all or whether I'm just being inconsistent.

1. A world where the clear wet stuff in the rivers is XYZ instead of H2O: I can picture it fine, no contradiction anywhere. So "water could have been XYZ" seems possible to me.
2. But also, now that we know water IS H2O, the sentence "water isn't H2O" feels like "a square circle" — not false, just incoherent. So "water could have been XYZ" also seems impossible to me. Great.
3. The folks on Twin Earth (whole planet where the watery stuff is XYZ) say "water is XYZ" back in 1600, before anyone has chemistry: I think they said something true. But if an Earthling in 1600 somehow voyaged there, pointed at the XYZ, and said "this is water," I think that's true too. These feel right together, but I can't explain why without cheating.
4. A world where the guy we call Gödel actually stole the incompleteness proof from a man named Schmidt, so the person who did the work was Schmidt: I think in that world "Gödel proved incompleteness" is false, because the name follows the guy, not the "whoever actually proved it" job description. And this somehow feels like a different kind of question from the water ones even though it looks identical (a word, a world, what the word picks out there).
5. "I am here now" — I can say that in any situation whatsoever and never be wrong, I seem to know it in advance without checking anything, yet each particular time it could easily have gone otherwise. So it's necessary-for-me but contingent-about-the-world, and I hate it.

My best guess at a principle: there are two evaluation passes — one where the words get bound to whatever they pick out in the world being described, and one where they keep the bindings they have at home — like pass-by-value vs pass-by-reference, or parsing a config at deploy time vs at runtime. Maybe "possible" sometimes means one pass and sometimes the other, and I can't tell which the fuss is about, or wether it's even coherent to run both.

So: is there a single principle that gives me all five verdicts, or close? If two of them genuinely can't coexist, which one do I drop and why? State the principle in one sentence up front, then run it through the cases, and finish with its verdict on a new one I'm stuck on: in 1750, before chemistry settled anything, a scientist says "for all we know, water might turn out to be something else entirely." Was he describing a genuine possibility, or only failing to know a necessity? Keep the whole answer under 400 words.
