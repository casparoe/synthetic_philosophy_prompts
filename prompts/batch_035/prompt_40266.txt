I never studied philosophy — I run a plumbing business and mostly think about these things on the drive home — but I've spent a couple of years working out my own position on free will, and I'd like someone to check whether it actually hangs together. Here is where I've landed. (1) Everything that happens in my head, including every decision I make, is fully caused by prior events; nothing non-physical ever steps in. (2) This morning I chose tea over coffee, and I believe that if the universe could be rewound to that exact moment — every particle where it was, every one of my reasons and desires exactly as they were — I might still have picked the coffee; that, to me, is what it means to say I could have done otherwise. (3) At the same time, I think that if some enormous computer had predicted my tea choice a week in advance from the state of my brain, the prediction wouldn't make the choice any less mine or any less free. (4) I hold an alcoholic who destroys his family fully responsible for his drinking, but I hold a man who developed a brain tumor that produced identical behavior responsible for none of it — and I'm comfortable with both verdicts even though I say both behaviors were caused in exactly the same way. (5) I take "he couldn't have done otherwise" as a genuine moral excuse: when it's true of someone, blame is out of place. (6) I don't think free worth the name requires that predicting me be impossible in principle. My own suspicion is that (3) and (6) together quietly destroy (2), though I can't make that argument precise, and something also feels wrong about (4) and (5) sitting side by side, but I can't put my finger on what. Which of these commitments are in real tension, which only appear to be, and what is the cheapest repair? Please reply as a numbered list of exactly five points, each one or two sentences, with no introduction or conclusion: list the genuine tensions first, worst first; then any tension that dissolves on a closer look; and end with one point naming the single cheapest fix and what it costs.
