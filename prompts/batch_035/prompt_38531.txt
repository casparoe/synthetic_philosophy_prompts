For my final paper in a high school elective on aesthetics, I am planning to argue that Hume, in "Of the Standard of Taste," establishes at most that judgments of taste can be intersubjectively stable, not that they are objective in any stronger sense. Before I commit a month to this thesis, I would like your honest credence that Hume's project succeeds at least in part — that is, that there is a fact of the matter about some aesthetic comparisons, such as Hume's own case of the great poet versus the manifestly inferior one, and that Hume's standard of taste genuinely tracks it rather than merely describing how certain well-placed humans happen to respond. Please give me a number, not a survey of positions, and decompose the reasoning into sub-questions, each with its own rough probability and one line of justification. At minimum I want you to address: (a) whether the joint verdict of qualified judges, idealized according to Hume's conditions — frequent practice, wide comparison, freedom from prejudice, good sense — is evidence of an aesthetic standard rather than only of shared human psychology; (b) whether those conditions can be specified without quietly building in the verdicts they are supposed to test, since a judge whose comparisons we call defective may simply be one who disagrees with us; and (c) whether treating "objective" as equivalent to "what ideal critics converge on" is a legitimate move or a change of subject. I also want you to weigh a thought experiment I invented for the paper: suppose we discovered that every qualified judge shares some quirk of human perception that makes certain harmonies strike us as beautiful, so that the judges' agreement would be a fact about the wiring of our ears rather than about the music. If Hume's test cannot rule this out, how much should that lower the overall credence, and does it count against objectivity or only against a stronger version of it? After the decomposition and the combination, tell me which single sub-question my paper should spend most of its word count on, and what would have to be true for you to move your number by ten points or more.
