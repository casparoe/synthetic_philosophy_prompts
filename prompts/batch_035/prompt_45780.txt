I'm taking a philosophy of language course this semester and we just did a unit on slurs, and it made me realize I have all these gut reactions to cases where slurs get said but no idea what rule is actually behind them. So I wrote down my verdicts on a bunch of scenarios and I want to know if there's a principle that captures them, or if my gut is just a mess. Here's the list. A Black guy greeting his friend with the reclaimed version of the n-word: obviously fine. My linguistics professor writing a slur on the board and using it in example sentences all semester: fine, and honestly the fact that it's fine feels like important data. A white student in that same class reading one of those same example sentences aloud when called on: makes me wince, though I can't point to anything he's doing differently with the word than the professor is. My Brazilian friend singing along to a rap song that uses the word: not fine, but also not the same as the guy at a party who quoted a stand-up bit as cover for just saying it — that guy was clearly way worse. A documentary subtitle that faithfully renders the slurs shouted in archival footage from a civil rights protest: fine. My aunt calling me a slur for Italians — I'm half Italian and she's fully: fine, kind of sweet even. The same aunt using that slur about a stranger: not fine, and wierdly much less fine than when she used it on me. And one I keep flip-flopping on: a non-Black guy who grew up in a mostly Black neighborhood and has used the reclaimed word in exactly the ways his friends use it since he was eight — my gut says still not fine, but my gut also agrees the intent, the relationship, and the meaning-in-context are all identical to the fine cases, so I don't know what's left for the verdict to be tracking.

So is there a principle that gets all or most of these? Maybe something about who the word is "for," or what speech act you're actually performing, or which community licenses your use, or something about the word's content vs what it signals about the speaker. Whatever you propose, please test it against each of my cases, tell me which verdicts it can't save, and be honest about whether those verdicts should go or whether the principle should get more complicated to keep them. And then tell me what your principle says about two cases I couldn't decide at all: a text-to-speech system reading a court transcript that contains slurs out loud, and a stand-up comedian doing a bit that's genuinely *about* how slurs work — not just using them for shock value — who isn't a member of any of the targeted groups.
