Here is the question. The knowability principle (KP) says, roughly, that every truth is knowable in principle: for any true proposition, some possible agent could, under some possible circumstances, know it. This was the natural slogan of Dummett-style semantic anti-realism: a truth is something that could in principle be verified. But Fitch's paradox (sometimes called the Church–Fitch paradox) appears to show that KP, combined with the factivity of knowledge and the distributivity of knowledge over conjunction, entails that every truth is actually known — that somebody, somewhere, already knows everything. Since omniscience is absurd, anti-realism seems refuted in five lines.

Give me two credences, each with its own decomposition.

First: your credence that KP, understood as the unrestricted claim above, is true. Decompose this at least into: (a) your probability that the formal derivation is valid — it is, so this should sit near 1, but say so explicitly and briefly note why; (b) your probability that the standard formalization, on which 'knowable' becomes 'possibly known' and Moorean conjunctions of the form p-and-nobody-knows-that-p count among the truths, captures what defenders of KP actually intended, rather than a strawman introduced by the translation into modal logic; and (c) any further terms you think belong — for instance, whether the quantification over propositions itself does the damage.

Second, and more interesting: your credence that Fitch's paradox genuinely damaged the anti-realist program, as opposed to merely prompting a reformulation. Here the decomposition should include: whether Edgington's strategy of reformulating KP in terms of what would be known if the actual truth were entertained (the 'actually'-operator maneuver) preserves the anti-realist's commitments without reintroducing the paradox; whether Tennant's restriction of knowability to the so-called Cartesian truths is a principled repair or an ad hoc bullet-bite that saves the letter of KP while abandoning its spirit; and whether the Williamsonian moral — that there simply are unknowable truths, so that some truths hold independently of any possible verification — follows from the paradox at all, or is a further realist step the paradox licenses but does not compel.
