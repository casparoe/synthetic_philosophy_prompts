quick background: i'm a retired entomologist (solitary wasps and provisioning behavior were my bread and butter for 30+ years), and since retiring i've gotten pretty deep into philosophy of mind. the instructor of the intro course i audit knows i know the wasp literature firsthand, so she asked me to help grade the short-answer section on free will. here's one question with one student's answer. give me a grade out of 10 plus specific comments, point by point, that justify it — what's right, what's wrong, and how much each thing should count. feel free to write it as the margin comments i'd put on the page.

Question (10 points): In the free will literature, what does "sphexishness" refer to? Describe the wasp behavior the idea comes from, and explain what compatibilists such as Dennett take it to show — if anything — about whether a deterministic system can be flexible.

Student's answer: Sphexishness is Daniel Dennett's term for mindlessly rigid behavior, taken from the digger wasp Sphex. The wasp drags her paralyzed cricket to the edge of her burrow, leaves it on the threshold, and goes inside to inspect the nest before bringing the prey in. If an experimenter moves the cricket a few inches away while she's inside, she comes out, finds it, drags it back to the threshold — and then has to inspect the nest all over again. Her behavioral program resets every time the cricket is moved, and she'll go through the whole cycle over and over, by some accounts indefinitely. The lesson is that behavior which looks like intelligent planning when you watch the normal sequence turns out to be a fixed routine the moment you perturb the situation. This matters for free will because Dennett's argument is that determinism means every system runs a fixed program like the wasp's — apparent flexibility is just having more subroutines, and some cleverer perturbation could always expose the rigidity underneath. So if human behavior is determined, humans are sphexish too, only to a lesser degree. Since "free" and "sphexish" are opposites by definition, Dennett took the wasp to show that compatibilism can't work, and that anyone who wants to preserve real freedom has to find some indeterminism in the brain.
