Also the recklessness has to fall out of the cluelessness reasoning itself, not from the decision-maker having weird values or a badly behaved prior. After the scenario, walk through how the main responses to cluelessness handle it — the symmetry argument, computing expected values with priors over ripple effects, and falling back on commonsense morality — and tell me which feature of the case is doing the work. Then flip it: if you remove that feature, does the case collapse back into the ordinary kind where cluelessness motivates caution, or does something else keep the recklessness going?
