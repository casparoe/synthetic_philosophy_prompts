Context: I'm a PhD student in formal epistemology, and our department has a "poetry and probability" night (yes it's a thing, mostly an excuse to drink, and this semester I got roped in). I promised to bring a sonnet about Jeffrey conditioning. I know, I know.

Assign yourself the right role here: you're a formal epistemologist who can actually write verse, not a general assistant decorated with poetic adjectives. And please think before rhyming -- start with a short brainstorm in prose (a few sentences is fine) about what the core idea is and what a sonnet could *do* with it, then write the poem.

What I want the form to track: the octave should set up the old picture, the one where evidence is all-or-nothing. You learn E, full stop, E goes to 1, and conditional probabilities get promoted wholesale -- clean, symmetric, and quietly committed to the idea that experience always hands you a certainty. Then the volta should be the actual problem that motivated Jeffrey: the candlelight case (his old example -- examining a piece of cloth by faint candlelight). You look at the cloth and you don't learn "it's green," and you don't learn "it isn't" -- you just come to believe it more, say to 0.7. There's nothing in your learning stream to condition on; the old machinery has nowhere to grip. The sestet should carry the rescue, and it's genuinely sonnet-shaped: rigidity (the conditional credences in anything else, given the cloth's color, stay frozen) plus the mixture -- your new credence in H as the two old conditional credences weighted by your new partial belief. Then the closing couplet should land the demotion: plain conditioning isn't the paradigm and Jeffrey's rule the kludge, it's the other way around -- ordinary conditioning is just the special case with the dial jammed at 1 or 0.

Constraints: Shakespearean sonnet (abab cdcd efef gg), iambic pentameter as close as you can get it without wrecking the sense. It should work as a poem, not a formula in rhyme, but accurate enough that someone in my area wouldn't wince -- so please don't portray it as vague updating, and don't conflate it with just having uncertain evidence *about* E, which is a different thing you can go on to condition on; the whole point is there's no proposition in the stream at all.

After the poem, add a short note seperating what's settled from what's genuinely contested among people who work on this. My sense: the rigidity/mixture math itself is settled, but whether rigidity is always rationally required, and whether every learning experience can be represented as Jeffrey conditioning on some partition, are live -- and I'm honestly not sure where the literature currently stands on that last one, so flag it as contested rather than pronouncing on it either way. Also mention any liberties the poem takes (e.g. if you flattened something to make a line scan, say so).
