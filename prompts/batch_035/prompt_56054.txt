For an assignment in my high school philosophy elective, please write a short story of roughly 1,500–2,000 words that dramatizes a Frankfurt case: a situation in which the protagonist could not have done otherwise, because some person or mechanism stands ready to prevent any alternative course of action, but in which this standby arrangement never actually intervenes, so that what the protagonist does flows entirely from her own will and reasons. The philosophical stakes are the principle of alternative possibilities — the idea that someone is only responsible for an action if they could have done otherwise — and the story should leave the reader genuinely unsettled about whether the protagonist deserves blame or credit for what she did, given that the safeguard never fired. Please avoid the stock setups, such as a neuroscientist with implanted electrodes or a demon issuing threats; find a subtler mechanism, and structure the story so that the reader learns of the standby arrangement only near the end and must then reassess the judgments they had already formed. Do not resolve the philosophical question by narrator's fiat or a tidy closing moral; let the events and the protagonist's interior life carry the ambiguity. Write the story in natural American English.
