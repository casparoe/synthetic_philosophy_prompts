Take L. A. Paul's argument in *Transformative Experience* that experiences like becoming a parent are both epistemically and personally transformative, and that standard decision theory therefore gives no rational verdict on whether to undergo them. Restate that argument as faithfully as you can in my vocabulary: what exactly corresponds to the claim that you cannot know the utility of an experience you have not had, once the value function is learned over features that themselves change after the transition, and what corresponds to the claim that the choice changes your utility function, once the reward is non-stationary in a way the agent's own action causes? Then give me the residue report: which parts of the argument translate cleanly, which only with distortion, and which have no expression in my vocabulary at all — in particular, whether anything in RL plays the role of the first-personal, experiential character that the utility assignment is supposed to be about, and whether my formalism has any counterpart to Paul's question of which self's utilities, pre- or post-transformation, should be assigned to the outcome. Finally, tell me whether the residue points to something my framework genuinely cannot say, or to something in Paul's formulation that was never as precise as the decision-theoretic setup it attacks.
