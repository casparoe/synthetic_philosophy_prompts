I've roped two colleagues who've been sniping at each other for a decade into an actual adversarial collaboration, and somehow I'm the one drafting the joint statement before our first meeting. One is a Samuelsonian revealed-preference guy to the bone: utility is nothing but a bookkeeping device licensed by the representation theorems, preferences just are consistent choice patterns, and any talk of "real welfare" behind choices is metaphysics dressed up as economics. The other does welfare econ and subjective well-being research: utility is a genuine psychological magnitude, preferences can be mistaken about your own good, and a pure choice-based view blesses adaptive preferences — the sweatshop worker who "chooses" her job, the battered spouse who says she's happy — as welfare by fiat.

Write the draft they should both be able to sign. I want a numbered section of claims they genuinely agree on — they do agree on more than either admits, e.g. that Samuelson's original programme doesn't need utility talk at all, that Afriat's theorem tells you something real about when choices are rationalizable, and that the preference-reversal literature is embarrassing to both of them — but phrase each claim so that each would actually put his name under it, not a watered-down nothing. Then a disagreements table: each disagreement stated twice isn't needed, just once, but in wording each side would accept for its own view (so the welfare guy gets "preferences fail to track well-being in systematically predictable ways," not "revealed preference is heartless"). Then the cruxes — I suspect one is whether choice data alone can ever falsify the claim that someone prefers what they choose, and another is whether interpersonal comparability of utility is a scientific question or a normative one, but you tell me. For each crux, name the case, experiment, or argument both would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. If some of the fight strikes you as merely verbal — say, over the word "utility" itself — say which part and why.

Keep the whole thing under 700 words. That's tight for this but it needs to be signable, not a page of platitudes. And please don't let either of them win in the drafting — last attempt the revealed preference guy rewrote the disagreements table down to three rows, all of which he conceded nothing in.
