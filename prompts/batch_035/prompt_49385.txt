I've been trying to figure out where my mind actually stops, and I figured the way to do it is to go by my gut reactions on specific cases and see what comes out the other end. So here goes. My phone with my calendar, notes, and to-do lists: honestly feels like part of my mind at this point — when it dies I feel like I've forgotten things, not lost a tool. An old paper notebook where I've kept a journal for a decade: also feels like part of me, in a way a printed encyclopedia on the shelf does not, even though both are just paper with words on them. My wife, who remembers all our social obligations because I never do: I rely on her memory the same way I rely on my phone's, and losing it would feel like losing my memory too, but calling her brain part of my mind feels wrong — that's *her* mind. Glasses: obviously not part of my mind, no one thinks that, and yet I depend on them for basically everything I see, way more than I depend on my notes app. Facts I learned with flashcards until they're stuck in my head: clearly internal, that's just memory. But an AI assistant that's read everything I've ever written and finishes my sentences the way a good collaborator does: feels more mind-ish than the encyclopedia, but less than my notes app, and I can't say why.

My tentative rule is something like: something is part of my mind if I use it in cognitive tasks without double-checking it, and if losing it would feel like forgetting rather than like breaking a tool. But this rule seems to make my wife's brain part of my mind, which I don't accept, and it's fuzzy on the glasses.

Is there a principle that gets all my verdicts, or close? If two of my verdicts have to go, tell me which ones and why those rather than the others. And then tell me what the principle says about two cases I haven't listed: a blind friend's cane, and a navigator in the days before GPS who'd memorized the coastline but kept the charts in a locker below deck. Also, definately tell me if you think one of my verdicts is just sentimentality about my phone.
