I'm TAing an intermediate ethics course, first set of essays I've had to grade on my own, and I want a second opinion on one of them. The question on the assignment was:

"Strawson (1962) argues that even if determinism were true, this would not by itself undermine our practices of holding people morally responsible. Explain how his distinction between the participant and objective attitudes, and the reactive attitudes, are supposed to establish this, and show how his framework accommodates those we refrain from holding responsible."

The student's answer, copied below:

---

In "Freedom and Resentment" (1962), P.F. Strawson argues against both the pessimist, who thinks determinism threatens moral responsibility, and the optimist, who tries to justify our responsibility practices by pointing to their social utility. Strawson's central move is that both sides presuppose that reactive attitudes like resentment, gratitude, indignation, and forgiveness stand in need of justification from outside. But for Strawson these attitudes are justified precisely because they are natural: they are an inescapable part of ordinary human relationships, and so the pessimist's demand that we justify holding people responsible if determinism turns out to be true is simply misplaced.

Strawson distinguishes the participant attitude, which we take toward most people most of the time, from the objective attitude. The objective attitude is the detached, clinical stance of viewing someone as an object to be managed or treated rather than as a participant in interpersonal relationships, and it involves no ordinary reactive feelings toward them at all. When someone acts under duress or by accident, we take the objective attitude toward them: we don't resent them, since they didn't mean the harm, and we regard them instead as a case for treatment or policy. This is how excuses operate within Strawson's framework.

Finally, Strawson argues that adopting the objective attitude toward people wholesale and permanently is not really a psychological possibility for us. Our commitment to the reactive attitudes is too deep; we could no more give them up across the board than we could decide to stop feeling pain. Hence determinism, even if true, could not by itself make us abandon holding people responsible.
