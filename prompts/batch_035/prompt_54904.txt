i'm a retired geochemist — 40 years, mostly isotope work on sediment cores — so my whole career was built on taking other people's word for things. i never re-ran anyone's mass spec myself, i took the published numbers on faith and built on them, and science worked fine that way. now i'm 72 and having a family problem i can't settle the same way, and i want to know whether the difference i'm feeling is real or just sentimentality.

short version: my brother and i haven't spoken in six years, over money and some things that got said at our mother's funeral. i'd been assuming i was done with him for good. over the holidays my daughter sat me down and told me flat out that cutting him off for the rest of our lives is wrong, that i'd regret it. she knows both of us, she's the most fair-minded person i've ever met, and honestly i trust her judgment about people more than my own — especially here, since i'm obviously not a neutral party. in the lab i would just accept the expert's verdict and act on it, and part of me wants to do exactly that: take her word, pick up the phone, be done deliberating. but another part of me feels that would be cheating somehow — like the conclusion wouldn't really be mine, even if it happened to be the right one. my late wife used to say some decisions you can't borrow, tho she never explained it in a way that satisfied me.

so my question: is my daughter's verdict enough for me to go on, or is there actually something wrong with outsourcing a decision like this instead of reaching it yourself? and here's the part that bugs the scientist in me — if she'd told me a plain fact (say, that he'd been sick, or that he'd tried to reach out and i'd missed it), i'd take that on her word without a second thought and nobody would call that a failure on my part. why does the moral verdict feel different, and should it? if it turns out there are real reasons deference works fine for facts but not for judgments like this one, i'd like to know what they are before i dial.
