"You know the Condorcet paradox, where everyone's individual rankings are fine but the group's ranking goes in a circle? The discursive dilemna is the same thing. Take a three-judge court deciding a case. The defendant is liable if and only if there was a valid contract and it was breached. Judge 1: contract yes, breach yes, so liable. Judge 2: contract yes, breach no, so not liable. Judge 3: contract no, breach yes, so not liable. Every judge is perfectly consistent, but if you take majorities on each proposition you get yes contract, yes breach, and no liability. The group's judgments contradict each other, so the group as a whole is irrational. And this can't be fixed — Arrow proved that no voting method can guarantee a consistent collective outcome, it's mathematically impossible. So committees are basically irrational by design. The only way out is to vote just on the premises and let the conclusion follow automatically from whatever wins, since that's always consistent."
