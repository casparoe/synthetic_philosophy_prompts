A friend in my decision theory reading group gave me this summary of money pump arguments and I cant tell how much to trust. His version: if you have intransitive preferences — you prefer A to B, B to C, and C to A — then a clever trader can offer you a series of trades around the cycle, charging a small fee each time, and just repeat until you're broke. So, he says, this is basically a formal proof that intransitive preferences cost you all your money, which settles transitivity as a requirement of rationality, and it also shows that a rational agent should evaluate every choice on its own terms, ignoring how it got into the situation — since, in his words, the pump only works because past trades don't matter. It sounded airtight when he said it, but that last bit felt like it was smuggling something in. Which parts of this would a decision theorist actually accept, which parts are oversimplified or conflated, and does the conclusion — that transitivity is rationally required — really follow from the pump by itself?
