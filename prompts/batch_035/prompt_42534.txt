In my decision theory seminar we discussed Ellsberg's two-urn problem: one urn is known to contain 50 red and 50 black balls; the other contains 100 red and black balls in entirely unknown proportions. Asked whether she would take an even-money bet on red from the known urn, a classmate of mine (Dana) said yes; asked the same about the unknown urn — she could pick either color — she refused both sides. Two other students then argued about whether her refusal was rational.

(1) Miguel said it was flatly irrational. However vague her evidence, Dana has some precise credence in red-from-the-unknown-urn; call it p. At even odds, p above one half demands betting on red, p below one half demands betting on black, and refusing both sides requires p to be exactly one half. But Dana accepts the corresponding bets on the known urn, where her credence is also one half. No single p fits her pattern, so the pattern is inconsistent.

(2) Yuki answered that this inconsistency is evidence against the demand for a single p, not against Dana. The refusal is systematic: for any bet on the unknown urn at odds where she would accept its known-urn counterpart, she declines. That is just the behavior imprecise probabilities were introduced to model — her credence in red-from-the-unknown-urn is an interval or set of values rather than a number.

(3) Miguel replied that this multiplies entities beyond need. A rational agent's preferences must satisfy the sure-thing principle; if Dana's preferences violate it, the rational response is to revise the preferences, not to invent a new kind of credence that certifies them. Vindication-by-redescription is not vindication.

(4) He added that, in any case, precise probabilists have constructed models that reproduce her choices without any intervals: a prior over the urn's composition, combined with a discount on the utility of bets whose probabilities are grounded in that prior rather than in known frequencies. So nothing about Dana's behavior forces imprecise probabilities on a Bayesian.

(5) Yuku countered that this rescue concedes the substance while preserving the letter: the utility discount is doing exactly the work the interval was supposed to do, so it is the same caution kept under different bookkeeping, and the dispute between them is merely notational.
