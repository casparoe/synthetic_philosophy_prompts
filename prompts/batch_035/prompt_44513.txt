I'm taking a phil of language course and we just started on vagueness, and I've been trying to write down my own gut reactions to see whether they add up to anything consistent. Here's where I land on a bunch of cases. Ten thousand grains of sand arranged in a pile: definitely a heap. Zero grains: definitely not. "There is some specific number n such that n grains makes a heap and n-1 doesn't" — that sounds obviously false to me, like someone's playing a trick. But "a ten-thousand-grain pile is either a heap or it isn't" sounds true, I can't even make sense of denying it. And yet if you point at a man who is exactly 175cm and ask "is he tall?", my honest reaction is that it's neither true nor false, or that either answer is somehow acceptable — which seems to be in tension with the heap disjunction thing, since "he's tall or he's not tall" still feels true even when "he's tall" on its own feels like it has no truth value. Also, each single step feels airtight: remove one grain from a heap and it's still a heap, I'd endorse that for any particular removal you show me — but I obviously can't endorse all the removals at once, given I endorse that zero grains isn't a heap. And one more that keeps bugging me: my friend claims "rich" works differently from "tall," because with money there's a background of legal and social conventions (tax brackets, "high net worth" thresholds) that could in principle sharpen it up, whereas "tall" has nothing like that. I half-buy this but can't tell if it's a real difference or just vibes.

So my question: is there one principle about how vague predicates work that captures all these verdicts, or close to all? If not, which of my verdicts would you overturn, and why that one rather than another? Please stress-test whatever principle you end up with — what does it predict for a case I haven't listed, like a woman who's the 4th-tallest in a group where everyone else is between 160 and 162cm, so she's clearly tall-for-that-room but globally borderline? And tell me honestly which of my verdicts your principle just cannot accommodate. I want actual verdicts and the reasoning behind them, not a tour of the standard position labels.
