My sister-in-law is the sort of person who has a firm yes or no ready whenever a conversation drifts into whether something counts as *real*. Over the years I've collected her track record: money — real, including the numbers in bank accounts, not just the paper. The English word "the" — real, as a thing, not just marks or sounds. The center of gravity of her car — real. The equator — real. The mate-in-three pattern in a chess position — real. The spam filter's belief that a particular message is spam — real, she says, fine to plan around. And the face she sees in the water stain on her bathroom ceiling — not real, just her; noone else sees it and it does no work.

We're getting into it again over the holidays and I want to be ready. The cases I'm expecting to come up: Sherlock Holmes (not a real person, sure, but a real *something*? she'll have to say something); the particular way the light falls across her kitchen counter around 4pm, which she uses as a clock and has never mentioned to a soul; sun signs — the whole zodiac, half the planet projects it, there are even rules for applying it, and it predicts basically nothing; and a pattern in some weather data that only a machine-learning system ever found, which forecasts better than anything humans had come up with, but that no human can state or picture.

So, what's she going to say about each of the four? What rule is she actually running? And more to the point: what other rules fit her record so far just as well but come apart on these new cases? Which of the four is the best one for telling those rival rules apart? And if her verdicts on all four still leave it open, give me one further case that would settle which rule she's really got.

(She keeps going on about real patterns — I think she got this from some philosopher or other — but I care more about her record than about the label.)
