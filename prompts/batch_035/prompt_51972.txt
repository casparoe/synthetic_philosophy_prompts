I teach an intro ethics course and every year the same thing happens: half the class writes "I'm a moral relativist" on the first assignment, and when I press them it turns out they mean at least four different things. Some think moral claims are true but relative to a culture, some think they're all false, some think they're only *justified* relative to a framework, and one guy every year who just meant "people disagree about morals a lot." So I want a sorting quiz for the first week: "what kind of moral relativist are you actually?" Eight questions, each a concrete case rather than "do you think morality is relative?", with two or three answer options each, and a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto leaves: descriptive relativism only; relativism about justification (the Wong-ish idea that claims are reasonable or unreasonable relative to a framework, but that a few things — gratuitous cruelty for entertainment — are unreasonable anywhere); truth relativism indexed to the agent's group (Harman-ish); truth relativism indexed to the speaker's own standards, the quasi-indexical view where "their practice is wrong" really means wrong-by-my-lights; Williams-style relativism of distance, where there's no fact of the matter across a big enough historical gap even though it's still true for us; and flat-out error theory. If some combination of answers has no natural home on the tree, say so, and tell me whether that's because nobody actually holds it or because my tree can't separate two leaves.

Cases I want worked in somewhere: a society whose practice — elaborate ancestor feasts, say — strikes you as pointless but which they wholeheartedly endorse; the 18th-century slaveholder judged from today (did we get it right and them wrong, or is there no fact of the matter across that gap?); a dissident *inside* a society who accepts its standards but thinks they're being misapplied — whose standards make her claim true, the society's actual ones or some tidied-up version?; a practice that fails by the society's OWN standards, which I want as a control question that shouldn't sort anyone; the pork case where the same sentence is uttered by a member of the observing culture vs. a member of the practicing one — same claim or different claims?; and a "moral flat earth" case like torture-for-fun that no actual society endorses, to catch students who only relativize about the easy stuff.

Two constraints on your answer. First, I want you to actually make the calls: where the literature is a mess or one of my cases could plausibly sort two ways, pick one placement and give your reason in a sentence — no "it could go either way," no offering me both options to choose between. Second, the whole deliverable — questions, options, scoring key, and the note on any empty cells — has to come in under 750 words, hard limit. It's going on a single-page handout, so cut fat, not substance. Also dont bother with citations or further reading, the students wont read it anyway.
