So last night I got into a really long argument with my housemate Maya, who's finishing a math PhD, about whether mathematical objects actually exist. I've always leaned platonist — maybe 0.7 credence that numbers and sets exist mind-independently in some abstract sense. She put herself at like 0.25 and described her position as "realist about truth values, not about things": she thinks statements like the Riemann hypothesis were already true or false in 1859 before anyone could prove anything, but she denies there are number-objects out there — there's just the natural number structure, and talk of "the number 7" is a convenient way of pointing at a place in it (I think she said she got this framing from some philosopher, Maddy or someone). Her best moment was the monster group: she said nobody decided what the monster would be, finite group theorists stumbled on it, and then its properties kept turning up in places nobody put them — she mentioned some thing about a j-function coefficient being literally one more than its smallest representation dimension, which she says nobody chose and nobody could have guessed. "That's what discovery looks like," she said, and then immediately ruined my side by asking how exactly my causal machinery is supposed to make contact with the number 7, Benacerraf-style.

Here's what I want from you. Give me your own credence for the proposition that mind-independent abstract mathematical objects exist (objects, not just structures and not just useful fictions), and decompose it: rough probability that our best scientific theories genuinely require quantifying over such objects (indispensability), rough probability that the epistemic access problem is fatal for platonism even if indispensability holds, rough probability that Maya's truth-without-ontology stance is coherent — because if it's not, her 0.25 has to move somewhere, and rough probability that mathematical practice is better explained as discovery than invention, one line of justification each. Then combine them however you think is right, tell me which term the final number is most sensitive to, and tell me where you think the actual disgreement between me and her most likely lives — which of the sub-questions is doing the work to separate a 0.7 from a 0.25. Also: what would move your number the most? Like, if someone actually carried out a Field-style nominalization of physics, or if a natural mathematical question got proven independent of ZFC in a way that made "true or false" start to feel like the wrong question — which of those (or something else) would shift you hardest, and in which direction? I want a number and the argument behind it, not a survey of positions.
