My wife and I are mid-way through IVF, and we've just been told we have three viable embryos and we need to pick one. Embryo 1 carries a variant that means roughly a coin-flip chance of a serious heart condition that tends to show up in your 30s, though it's treatable-ish and getting more so. Embryo 2 is clear on that front, but of the three it came out worst on the polygenic risk stuff for depression later in life. Embryo 3 is unremarkable on everything — no bad scores, no great ones — but it was the lowest quality embryo and the clinic gives it maybe 20% lower odds of making it to a live birth at all.

We're not asking you to run the numbers for us, but we keep going round in circels on the arguments around the choice. My wife's line is that whoever we pick will just be our kid, fully and unconditionally, so there's no one standing outside the decision who gets wronged — the people the other embryos would have become were never going to exist anyway, and you can't owe anything to someone who never exists. Something about that seems right and also like a trick.

Then last weekend my sister, who's been deaf since birth, asked what we'd do if the screen had flagged her condition instead of a heart thing, and said that the whole business of screening problems out quietly tells people like her that their lives were preventable mistakes. She wasn't angry, she was just asking. And now I can't work out whether her point applies to our actual situation — the three embryos sitting in front of us — or only to the broader practice of screening at all, and whether those two things can really be pulled apart.

So I guess my questions are: are we choosing on behalf of a child, or among possible children, or neither? Does the "no one is wronged" argument actually let us off the hook for just picking whichever one we feel like, or does it in some odd way make the choice more demanding rather than less? And is my sister's worry something that should change what we do at the clinic on Tuesday, or only what we say about it afterwards? Please answer in British English.
