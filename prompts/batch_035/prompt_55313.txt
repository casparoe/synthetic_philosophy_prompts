My friend and I spent a long evening arguing about whether our city should adopt a pretrial risk-assessment algorithm, and by the end it was clear we were arguing past each other: she was demanding that the tool's errors fall equally on all demographic groups, I kept insisting only that its scores mean the same thing for everyone, and neither of us ever laid out what an adequate fairness requirement for this setting would even have to accomplish. Before we argue about any particular algorithm again, I want the specification. Assume the setting is concrete: the tool scores arrestees for pretrial release, the outcome it is trained on is re-arrest rather than re-offense, and re-arrest rates are known to differ across neighborhoods partly for reasons uncorrelated with actual offending. Write the desiderata as exactly five numbered requirements, each a sentence or two, with the motivating case or consideration behind it, a mark of whether you take it to be non-negotiable or a preference of one camp, and an indication of whether it can be jointly satisfied with your other requirements when true re-offense base rates differ across groups. At least one requirement must concern the gap between the label we can measure and the quantity we actually care about. If you believe your five are jointly satisfiable, say so within the items and defend it; no introduction or conclusion beyond the five.
