Write a dialogue between a spacetime substantivalist and a relationist about Newton's bucket, but with a twist on the usual setup. Imagine a universe containing just the bucket, half-filled with water, and a single distant star. At first the star is close enough that the relationist can comfortably say the water climbs the sides because the bucket rotates relative to the star. But then imagine the star being gradually dimmed and pushed away, until the universe contains the bucket alone. The two characters should disagree over what happens to the water during this: the substantivalist presumably insisting nothing changes, since the rotation is relative to space itself, while the relationist owes a story about whether the concavity fades out gradually with the star's removal or drops off at some threshold -- or whether it was never really about the star in the first place. Have them press each other on whether "rotating relative to nothing" is even a coherent notion, and on whether a relationist who helps herself to inertial structure (local inertial frames etc.) has smuggled in most of the substantivalist's position already. Both should be equally sharp, and it should be real back-and-forth -- each one responding to the other's actual point, not alternating monologues.
