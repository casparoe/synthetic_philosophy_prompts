I've been reading about Hegel's master-slave thing, and the part that sticks with me is the idea that the master kind of loses anyway: the only person around to recognize him is someone he's dominated, and recognition from a person you've crushed doesn't count for much. So I've been trying to figure out, going by my gut on cases, when recognition from another being actually counts as recognition, and when it's kind of fake or empty. Here are my verdicts:

- Praise from a rival in my field who I respect and who genuinely competes with me: counts a lot, maybe the realest kind there is.
- A glowing performance review from my own manager: counts, mostly.
- A glowing review from my direct report, someone whose career depends on staying on my good side: feels nice but doesn't really count. It's like the master-slave thing, the power relation contaminates it.
- My dog losing her mind with joy when I come home: counts?? I can't make myself say it doesn't, and it's not just mood, I actually feel seen in some thin way.
- Five stars from anonymous strangers on a delivery app: counts for basically nothing.
- A stranger online writing a long comment about how my essay changed their life, when it's clear they misread the entire point: doesn't count, and somehow feels worse than silence, like they're congratulating someone who doesn't exist.
- A dead novelist whose books describe exactly how I've felt my whole life and never told anyone: I feel recognized by her, even though she died before I was born and never met me. Sort of counts, in a way I can't quite defend.
- A huge fan who admires me but whose judgment is terrible in every other area of their life: counts a little, but way less than it should given how enthusiastic they are.

My own stab at a principle: recognition counts when the recognizer (a) sees the actual me and (b) is free to see me otherwise, i.e. nothing structurally forces the seeing. The rival passes both tests, the report fails the second, the misreading stranger fails the first. But this principle makes the dog a disaster (she's about as unfree and uncritical as a recognizer gets) and I have no idea what to do with the dead novelist, since she's not even a contemporary interaction.

So: is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? Is my two-condition thing repairable, or does it need to be scrapped? And then there's the case I keep going back and forth on, which my principle says should count but my gut resists: a chatbot that has read everything I've written and responds to it in a way that no human ever has. Does that count as recognition, or is it more like the five stars, or something else entirely? I want you to actually take a side, tell me which of my verdicts has to go if the best principle can't save it, and tell me what the principle predicts for a case I haven't listed -- like whether recognition counts when it comes from a whole subculture that has decided I'm a prophet (thats worship maybe, not recognition?) or from one person after I'm dead.
