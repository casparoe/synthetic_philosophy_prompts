In the epistemology of testimony seminar I help run, the anti-reductionist side keeps winning arguments with one analogy: accepting a speaker's word is like accepting perceptual experience. You do not first establish that your eyes are reliable before trusting them, the story goes; perceptual appearances carry default entitlement, and rejecting or defeating that entitlement requires positive grounds. If testimony is a parallel channel of belief-formation — a further faculty, alongside perception and memory, for getting information from the world — then the Humean demand that each hearer reconstruct inductive credentials for trusting speakers is misplaced: it is the demand that one do with testimony what nobody does with vision. The reductionist side fires back with its own picture: testimony is not a fresh channel but a chain of secondhand reports, each a copy of a copy, which must eventually borrow its warrant from unassisted observation by someone upstream, and which therefore stands to perception more as a photograph of a photograph does than as an eye does.

I am not convinced either picture is doing the work its users assign to it. The perception analogy seems to me to import a lot: that the source of the information is not an agent with interests, that the channel can be bad but not deceptive, that there is no analogue of the possibility of asking the source again, that the subject could not have gotten the content any other way. Some of these look load-bearing for the default-entitlement conclusion and some look incidental, but I cannot sort which is which, and neither side in our discussions has tried.

Please audit both analogies and give me the result as a JSON object with the following fields:

- "mapping": an array of objects, one per substantive point of the perception analogy, each with fields "perception_side", "testimony_side", and "holds", where "holds" takes the value "yes", "partly", or "no", plus a one-sentence "comment". Cover at least: the information source and whether it has intentions; entitlement arising from the channel's constitution versus from evidence about its reliability; the role of defeaters; the possibility of re-querying or clarifying the source; and memory as the hard intermediate case (a channel with no agent at the far end that still somehow carries content from the past).
- "disanalogies": an array of objects with fields "feature", "load_bearing" (true or false), and "why". "Load_bearing" should mean: were this disanalogy taken seriously, it would undermine the specific conclusion the analogy is used to license — that hearers have default, non-reductive entitlement to accept testimony. Distinguish these from disanalogies that are real but idle for that purpose.
- "reductionist_picture_audit": a short string assessing whether the chain-of-copies image is better than its rival — in particular whether it can accommodate cases where the information could never have been observed by anyone (e.g., testimony transmitting skills or standards rather than observed facts), and whether it smuggles in an atomism about reports that actual chains of corroboration and institutional transmission do not have.
- "verdict": a string stating whether the default-entitlement conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, and whether it survives in full, in weakened form, or only for restricted kinds of testimony.
- "better_analogy": an object with fields "source" and "testimony_as" naming, if there is one, a better comparison than either of the two in play, and a field "breaks_at" saying where even that one gives out.

Please confine yourself to what you can argue from first principles and the standard case material of this debate (Reid's and Hume's original positions, the contemporary reductionism/anti-reductionism dispute of the sort Coady and Burge frame); do not pad the answer with surveys or reading lists.
