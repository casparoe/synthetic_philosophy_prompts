I run a philosophy reading group for non-philosophers -- 10 people, mostly engineers, one 90-minute evening session. Design a game that makes them feel hinge epistemology before I ever name it (they shouldn't hear the terms until the debrief). What I have in mind is something like a staged trial or inquisition: one team gets to raise doubts about anything the other team claims to know, but the rules make a doubt cost something, or require the doubter to supply grounds. The players should end up unable to raise certain boring doubts -- that the session is still happening, that the chairs didn't vanish when they left the room, that the facilitator wasn't replaced by a duplicate over the break -- not because they have evidence for those claims, but because trying to doubt them makes the whole practice of doubting misfire. Please give me rules, materials, a timeline, and a payoff scheme that doesn't need real money. Then a debrief that maps what actually happened onto the distinction between a hinge and an ordinary known proposition: a standing commitment you don't evidence-away but rather argue from. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (someone being a smartass and just refusing to play, someone escalating to demon doubts too early, etc.) and be honest about which feature of the idea no game can reproduce and I'll have to supply myself at the end.
