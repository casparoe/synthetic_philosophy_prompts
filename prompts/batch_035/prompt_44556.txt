I teach a philosophy seminar on the ethics of violence, and for years I've been in a reading group with a historian friend -- let's call her Dana -- where Pinker's big decline-of-violence book keeps coming back around like a bad penny. Last week I finally started cataloguing Dana's verdicts across all the cases we've ever argued about, and now I want to figure out whether there's a stable rule underneath them or whether she's just winging it case by case. Her track record:

- Medieval European homicide rates vs. modern ones: decline, and it counts. She's adamant about per capita -- her standard line is that Genghis Khan was worse than Hitler once you adjust for population size.
- Public executions as spectacle, dueling, judicial torture: all count, all declined, all part of the good news.
- Corporal punishment of children, school caning and spanking at home: counts, and its decline is real progress as far as she's concerned.
- Factory farming: does not count. Billions of animals killed a year, but she says that's an industry, not violence -- nobody's attacking anybody.
- The 90s Iraq sanctions and the child mortality bump: doesn't count either. Side effects of policy aren't killing, even forseeable ones, she says.
- Deliberately spreading smallpox to a population: counts. That's violence, full stop.
- When I pushed her on whether "is the world becoming less violent" is even a question with an answer, she said homicide rates have answers but the big question is partly just a choice about what goes in the numerator.

Three new cases are coming up at our next meeting, and I'd like your predictions before I place my bets:

1. Deaths of despair in the US -- suicides and overdoses way up, but no particular perpetrator you can point to.
2. Long-term solitary confinement -- the state intends serious harm to a specific person, but the harm is psychological rather than a blow.
3. Drone strikes that kill the intended target plus bystanders the operator could reasonably forsee were there.

Predict her call on each. Then give me the candidate rules that fit her existing record about equally well, tell me where those rules diverge on the three new cases, say which rule you'd bet on her running and why, and propose the further case -- one she hasn't ruled on yet -- that would best split the remaining candidates. For what it's worth, my own hunch is that her factory farming ruling has nothing to do with animals at all, but I want to hear your read.
