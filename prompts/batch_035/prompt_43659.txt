I am preparing a seminar unit on the inefficacy objection: the thought that because a single contribution to a collective harm makes no perceptible difference — one person's emissions, one steak, one vote — individual restraint is pointless, and the burden of argument falls on those who claim otherwise. For the unit's closing session I want a structured comparison of the standard replies, not a survey essay, and I want it as data I can put on a slide. Please judge the replies on their merits; do not cite literature or lean on names, and do not pad rationales with authorities — the arguments are common property and should stand or fall on their mechanics.

The replies to compare (R1–R6):

R1 — Expected difference: even if my act almost never changes the outcome, it has some probability of being the one that crosses a threshold (a pivotal vote, the purchase that tips a supplier's decision), and a small probability times a large effect yields a nonzero expected difference.

R2 — Contributing without difference-making: my act can be a contributing cause of the harm even when it is not individually sufficient for it — each participant in a collective wrong contributes to the outcome, as each striker of a victim contributes to the death, even though no single blow is the difference-maker; difference-making is simply the wrong causal standard for shared agency.

R3 — Stochastic production response: producers adjust output to demand in small increments and with some noise, so each purchase slightly raises the expected quantity produced; the expected harm falls on someone, even if no particular victim can be traced to my purchase (and even if, in any given case, the marginal unit would have been supplied by someone else's demand instead).

R4 — Integrity and clean hands: treating one's own act as if it mattered is part of not being the sort of person who free-rides on others' restraint, and of keeping one's hands off a predictable collective wrong; the point is not to change the world but to be answerable for one's share of it.

R5 — Fairness / no exceptions: if everyone reasoned that a single act is pointless, the harm would occur and everyone would be worse off; gaining the small benefits of contributing while relying on others' restraint is unfair in the way free-riding is unfair, whether or not one's contribution is pivotal.

R6 — Group-level wrong, individual share: the wrong is predicated of the practice, not the person — the collective act is wrong, and the individual's act is wrong in virtue of being a knowingly willing part of that practice; individual blame is not inherited from the group but constituted by the individual's intentional participation in it.

For context, the skeptic I want the replies measured against argues as follows. P1: no single individual's contribution changes any outcome. P2: an act cannot be wrong in virtue of features of a group of which it is a part — wrong-making is not inherited from collectives. C: therefore individual restraint lacks even pro tanto reason behind it.
