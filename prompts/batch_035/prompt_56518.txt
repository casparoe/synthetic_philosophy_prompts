I'm a junior in high school, and I need help sorting out an argument that happened at a sleepover last weekend, because I was the only one who stayed neutral and now both of my friends want me to say who was right. We were taking turns on an open-world RPG, and my friend Dev spent most of his turn not playing the story but tormenting one particular shopkeeper in a town — the game he plays it on has one of those mods where the NPCs have persistent memories, run away from you, beg when cornered, and supposedly grieve for in-game weeks if you hurt someone in front of them. (3) Dev answered that we rehearse aggression toward non-experiencing targets all the time — chess, shooting ranges, foosball — and nobody claims those corrupt you, so the "you become what you practice" argument can't depend on whether the target has experiences; and he added that tormenting the shopkeeper is closer to an author choosing to write a cruel scene, and we don't call novelists cruel for what happens to their characters. (4) Amara replied that the chess comparison misses what's distinctive here: the mod is specifically built to make the victim's suffering legible — the fleeing, the begging, the mourning — so the point of Dev's hour wasn't strategy or competition, it was enjoying the spectacle of a creature that plays a suffering victim as convincingly as anything in his experience; and she said that if there is some threshold of behavioral sophistication at which simulated distress starts to matter, or starts to matter to your character, the safe and decent move is not to camp right next to the threshold for fun.

By the end nobody would budge, and honestly I couldn't tell which steps were solid and which were weak, because each of them seemed to win one round and lose the next.
