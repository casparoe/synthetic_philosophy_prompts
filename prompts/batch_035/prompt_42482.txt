I'm a retired physical chemist (spectroscopy, mostly, thirty-some years of fitting peaks to Lorentzians) who got pulled into philosophy after my daughter lent me Kripke's book on Wittgenstein and rule-following, and now I can't stop chewing on it. Our retirement book club wants each of us to write down a philosophical profile so the others can poke holes in it, so here's mine, as best I can state it:

1. Every scientific claim I ever tested rested on finitely many measurements, yet the claims themselves were about infinitely many cases, and I never thought that made them meaningless — a calibration curve fit to twenty points still says something about the twenty-first sample.
2. Meaning, including the meaning of theoretical terms like "mass" or "gene," is fixed by use — by how the community of practitioners actually goes on, not by some mental object sitting in anyone's head.
3. There were right and wrong answers about what "gene" meant even before anyone could sequence anything; when molecular biologists later found junk DNA and split genes, they were finding out something about what the term already meant, not changing the subject.
4. Computers genuinely follow rules — I spent a career writing and debugging code, and the machine isn't secretly interpreting the program in some gerrymanderd way; the program just is the machine's dispositions.
5. I'm pretty sure the large languge models don't really mean anything by their words — they've only seen a finite corpus of text, so nothing fixes what they're talking about when they say "electron."
6. When the entire lab, me included, made the same arithmetic slip in a paper's supporting information, we were wrong — there was a fact of the matter about that sum even though every one of us was disposed to write the wrong number.

Does this package hang together? It feels fine to me but my daughter makes a face whenever I describe it, which I've learned is data. Go through the commitments and tell me exactly which pairs clash and how badly, ordered from worst to mildest, and what the cheapest fix for each clash would be. If you think one of the clashes is only apparent, say which one and why. Give me exactly five numbered points, a sentence or two each, no introduction and no conclusion — I want to paste it straight into the club's shared doc.
