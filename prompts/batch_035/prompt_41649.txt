I'm a graduate student in law and philosophy, and I've just come back from an interdisciplinary workshop on technology regulation where the extended mind thesis did a lot of work without ever being stated precisely. The presenter, building on Clark and Chalmers's famous case of Otto (who relies on a notebook the way Inga relies on biological memory), argued roughly as follows: since cognition can extend into artifacts, a smartphone that someone uses to organize their life is a constituent of their mind; therefore, seizing and searching that phone without a warrant is closer to compelling a person to testify against herself than to searching her apartment; and, further, damaging someone's assistive device is a wrong to the person herself rather than mere property damage. In the Q&A it became clear that at least four different claims were being run together, and I want to be prepared for the follow-up session.

Please lay out, as a menu rather than an argument for one reading, the distinct precise theses that the slogan that cognition extends into the world could be expressing — from the near-trivial (minds causally depend on things outside the body) through intermediate readings (the parity claim that if an external process plays the same functional role as an internal one it counts as part of the cognitive process) to the strongest constitutive readings on which external items are literally parts of the mind. For each reading, say whether it is plausible, whether it is philosophically substantive or a relabeling, and — most importantly — whether it would actually support the two legal conclusions the presenter drew, if either. I'd also like a verdict on whether the slide from Otto's notebook, which Otto consulted constantly and which was his on any reasonable description, to ordinary phones, which are only intermittently relied on, is doing hidden work. Please conclude with a note on which reading you think the presenter actually needs for the argument to go through, and whether it's a reading a defender of the thesis should want to accept.
