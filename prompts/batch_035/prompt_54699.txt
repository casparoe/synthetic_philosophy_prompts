Quick background so you understand where I'm coming from: I'm american, grew up fully on the free-speech-is-the-answer diet, and a few months ago I moved to Germany for work. Last month a guy I know here got fined over some genuinely nasty posts about refugees — no call to violence, but way past rude, the kind of thing that in the US would just get you ratio'd. My German friends' reaction was basically "well, yes, that's what the law is for," and mine was basically horror, and since then we've been going in circles every time it comes up. We're not even disagreeing about the same thing anymore, we're just talking past eachother, so I want to force us through something structured instead of another bad-tempered dinner.

Write the joint statement we should have produced by now: the document you'd get from a good-faith adversarial collaboration between a committed First Amendment camp (viewpoint neutrality, counterspeech as the remedy, deep distrust of any state deciding which opinions are allowed) and a committed European camp (dignity and the stability of democracy come first, the "militant democracy" idea that a state may guard itself, and the thought that some speech drives whole groups out of public life regardless of what it "argues" for). I want:

- a numbered section of claims both sides could actually sign — about what hateful speech does, what laws can and can't realistically achieve, and where both agree the genuinely hard cases sit
- a disagreements table: each disagreement stated twice, once in wording the First Amendment side would accept for itself and once in wording the European side would accept for itself, so neither one feels strawmanned
- the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling one would actually move at least one side. I suspect one is whether hate speech works mainly through persuasion (so more speech fixes it) or through intimidation (so it silences people no matter how good the counter-arguments are), and another is how much you trust any state, once empowered to police opinions, to stay inside the lines it was given — but keep, drop, or replace these as you see fit
- for each crux, a concrete case — real or hypothetical — that both sides would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction about that case written down in advance, so we can actually check later who called it
- and if you think part of our fight is purely verbal, like maybe about what "censorship" even means when it's a fine rather than imprisonment, say which part and why, because that would honestly save us a few evenings.
