For my IB Theory of Knowledge essay I am trying to determine what the word "game" actually means, and I have been collecting my gut verdicts on cases in the hope that they will reveal a definition. Someone in my class told me that Wittgenstein thought a term like "game" has no common essence at all, only a family resemblance among its uses, but that strikes me as giving up before the search has been properly run, so I want the search run. Here are my verdicts. Pickup soccer after school, my little cousin's ring-around-the-rosie, and solitaire on my phone are all clearly games, and so is playing fetch with my dog, at least for the dog. Professional wrestling, where the outcome is scripted in advance, is not a game for the performers, and yet a fantasy league in which fans draft wrestlers and score points plainly is a game, which already strikes me as strange. A slot machine is not a game, even though it has rules, a win condition, and voluntary participation, and this is where I run into trouble, because solitaire is solitary and mostly luck and still counts as a game, so the slot machine verdict and the solitaire verdict pull in opposite directions. My friend who speedruns a single level thousands of times, openly hating it, purely for a world record, feels less like a game than like labor, but I cannot hold onto that feeling without also disqualifying professional athletes, who play for money. The one case I keep flip-flopping on is a chess engine playing itself overnight, thousands of matches in a row: nobody is playing, and yet the humans running the tournament still call each match a game.

My own attempt at a principle was that a game is a rule-governed activity you take up voluntarily, in which winning is possible, and which you do at least partly for its own sake rather than for what you get out of it, partly cribbed from a definition by a philosopher named Bernard Suits, who characterized playing a game as a voluntary attempt to overcome unnecessary obstacles. The problem is that the for-its-own-sake clause seems to disqualify grandmasters and the miserable speedrunner, and dropping that clause appears to let the slot machine in. So I would like you to test my principle and repair it if you can, or tell me if it cannot be repaired.

Please answer as a numbered list of exactly five items and nothing else, with no introduction or conclusion. Each item should be one or two sentences: a candidate principle, ordered from most to least promising, stating precisely what it says a game is, which of my verdicts it flips, and what it predicts for the chess engine playing itself. Exactly one of the five items must be the Wittgensteinian option, that no principle is there to find and my verdicts hang together only by overlapping similarities with no single thread, and for that item you must say whether it counts as giving up or as the honest result of the search.
