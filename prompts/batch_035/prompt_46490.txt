I'm writing a term paper on axiomatic bargaining theory, and I've ended up with an argument whose conclusion I find too convenient to trust: that the long-running contest between the Nash solution and the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution was never really open. Here it is.

1. Monotonicity is a requirement of rationality, not a normative taste: if one bargainer's options improve while the other's are held fixed, it would be perverse for the improved player to come away from the settlement with less.
2. In the two-person case, the two solutions agree on Pareto optimality, symmetry, and invariance to affine rescaling of utilities; they differ only in the fourth axiom. Nash requires independence of irrelevant alternatives, while Kalai and Smorodinsky proved that replacing it with individual monotonicity yields a unique solution — theirs.
3. The Nash solution violates monotonicity: there are pairs of bargaining problems in which one player's attainable utilities rise across the board while the other's ideal point is unchanged, yet the Nash bargain leaves the improved player strictly worse off.
4. So the only thing the Nash solution has that Kalai–Smorodinsky lacks is IIA — and IIA is the axiom social choice theory discredited decades ago. Arrow's impossibility theorem shows it cannot be satisfied alongside premises that are plainly true, and a condition that fails for aggregating preferences should not survive as a pillar of bargaining theory.
5. Therefore the axiomatic ledger is strictly one-sided: every admissible consideration favors Kalai–Smorodinsky, and the Nash solution's continued prominence in textbooks and applications is inertia rather than judgment.

This proves too much — a controversy sustained this long by serious people can't be that easy to close. I half-suspect the cheat is in step 4, but a friend who read my draft insists the real problem is in step 1, and I don't see that at all. Tell me which step to reject, why it looked fine, and what rejecting it costs; in particular, say what I pay for keeping step 1, since I would much rather give up any version of IIA than monotonicity. Also tell me what would change your mind: what argument or consideration would lead you to reject a different step instead, and where is your own diagnosis most likely to go wrong?
