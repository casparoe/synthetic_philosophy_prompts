My seminar this term is on Newton's nonscientific manuscripts (the prophecy stuff, the anti-Trinitarian treatises, the letters to Locke about the corrupted scripture passages) and a guy in my study group swears he's found a way to make Newton's own theology eat itself, and I cant actually find the exit. The argument, numbered like he gave it to us:

(1) For Newton, the sin of idolatry is a sin about the object of worship: you're an idolater if what your worship actually goes to is a creature (or God-under-a-creaturely-disguise) rather than the one true God. That's the whole crime, in his notes over and over.

(2) But Newton also held that God's essence is not knowable by us at all -- we know him only by his dominion over us and his effects in the world -- so every concept anyone ever forms of God is stitched together out of creaturely materials: fathers, kings, causes, whatever the phenomena happen to show.

(3) When a person worships, the worship goes to the object as they conceive it; the mental image is what the prayer is actually addressed to, not some conception-independent referent sitting behind it.

(4) So by (1) through (3), every act of worship ever performed by anyone, Newton's own included, is directed at a creaturely construct, and is therefore idolatry.

(5) But Newton plainly took himself to be worshipping the true God and reserved the charge of idolatry for others -- that was his whole case against the Trinitarians, that they gave divine honors to a creature. So either he owes us a principled distinction between his worship and theirs, or his accusation backfires on him.

He says this shows the anti-idolatry zeal at the center of Newton's theology was incoherent and not merely heretical. For what its worth, premise (2) seems rock solid to me, and I really want to keep the thought in (5) that the zeal was the engine driving everything else -- the biblical criticism, the quarrel with Athanasius, all of it. Walk me through exactly five numbered items, one per step of the argument, each just a sentence or two saying whether giving up that step is a real exit and what it costs. No intro, no conclusion. And dont go easy on me -- I keep rereading the thing and the steps all look fine one at a time, which is exactly what's worrying me.
