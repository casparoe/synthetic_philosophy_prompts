quick setup in case you need it: Scorpio is a sniper who kidnaps a 14-year-old girl and buries her alive with limited air, Harry tracks him to a stadium at night, shoots him, then steps on the wound until he gives up the location — and by the time they dig her up the girl is already dead. then Scorpio walks anyway because the search was illegal, and eventually Harry kills him and throws his badge into the water.

my paper idea: the stadium scene is the paradox of deontology on film. the consequentialist says torture him, obviously, a life is at stake; the deontologist refuses to torture, and if the girl dies because of that refusal, the restriction looks irrational — what could it possibly be for? that's the Scheffler-style setup. and the badge throw at the end could be read as the film acknowledging moral residue or dirty hands, which is exactly what the "deontology is irrational" objection says can't be explained.
