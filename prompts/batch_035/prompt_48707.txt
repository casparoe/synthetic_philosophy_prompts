I'm the only privacy person at a ~150-person company that ships a bunch of small consumer apps, and product managers come to me maybe three times a week with "is this a privacy thing?" right before launch. So far I've been going on gut plus half-remembered compliance training, and I'd like to replace that with an actual decision procedure. Can you turn whatever the philosophy of privacy actually says -- control over your information, restricted access to your person and spaces, information flowing according to the norms of the context it came from, that whole cluster -- into something I can run in fifteen minutes in a meeting? I need ordered questions, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, where it ends with "fine, ship it," where it ends with "no," and where it explicitly hands the question back to me because it's a value judgment that a checklist can't absorb. It should also say what counts as a privacy problem when no user complains, since half my PMs think privacy is just status anxiety and I need something to say back to them. Then run it on the three things on my desk right now: a change to our messaging app that flips read reciepts from opt-in to on-by-default; a neighborhood app that displays doorbell footage of package thefts with house numbers "mostly" blurred; and a new feature where the app infers which of your contacts you're closest to, entirely on-device, with nothing ever leaving the phone. Constraints: our CTO signs off on whatever I bring him, and his test is basically "will a journalist write about this," so the procedure has to survive that guy. Be honest about where it'll misfire, and flag it if one of the three comes out in a direction I won't like, because I suspect one does.
