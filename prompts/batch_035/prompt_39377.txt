I'm a novelist working on a collection of near-future stories about reproductive technology, and the next one is giving me trouble. I want a story built around the procreation asymmetry — the idea that bringing into existence a person whose life would be happy doesn't seem to do anything good *for them* (they can't be glad they were born, since there was no them beforehand to benefit), but bringing into existence a person whose life would be miserable does seem to do something bad to them. My problem is that every time I try to dramatize this it comes out as a philosophy lecture with characters stapled on, and my editor already flagged my last draft for having a genetic counselor monologuing at a couple like she's reading an abstract.

So here's the setting I'd like you to work with, but feel free to push back on it: a small private clinic in a provincial city, maybe ten years from now, that offers what they call "trajectory reports" — cheap probabilistic forecasts of a prospective child's life quality, not dystopian-precise, just actuarial. Two clients come in the same week. One couple learns their child would most likely have a life that's good but not great — solidly worth living, no more. Another learns their child would likely have a life just barely on the wrong side of the line. The clinic's policy people are privately divided over whether the second couple is being *harmed* in the making while the first couple is *owed* nothing either way, and I want the story to make the reader feel why that asymmetry is intuitive and also why it starts to wobble when you push on it (e.g. the couple who could make a very happy child and choose not to — nobody says they wronged anyone, but my gut isn't fully comfortable with that either, and the story shouldn't be either).

Please keep the non-identity stuff mostly at the edges — the forecasted children don't exist yet as the same people who'd exist under a different decision, and the story can gesture at that vertigo without a character explaining it aloud. I want no scene where anyone says the word "asymmetry". What I'm hoping you can pull off is something where the philosophical pressure comes through the clinic staff disagreeing over what to tell the two couples, or through one client who keeps asking "compared to what?" until the question stops having an obvious answer. Maybe 1500-2500 words, plain style, no sci-fi flashiness beyond the premise itself.
