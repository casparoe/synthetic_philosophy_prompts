My buddy Marco and I do these long Thursday hikes and we've had this running argument for like two years about when something counts as "emergent" versus when it's just the small pieces doing their thing and people being impressed. He's handed down verdicts on a bunch of cases over the months and I actually keep a note on my phone. So far: the temperature of a gas is NOT emergent, it's just an average of how fast molecules are zipping around, nothing new. A traffic jam that forms on a circular test track with no accident, purely from drivers braking — that one IS emergent for him, he loves saying the jam is a thing that crawls backwards up the road and makes YOU slow down, and no single car contains it. A hurricane: not emergent, it's just a bunch of warm wet air doing exactly what warm wet air does. Starling murmurations: emergent. One protein folding into its shape: not emergent, "that's just chemistry doing its thing." An ant colony finding the shortest path to food: emergent. And consciousness is his favorite example, obviously emergent.

Next Thursday I want to bring some fresh cases and bet beers on his answers. I'm thinking of: (a) whether life itself is emergent — like a single cell that maintains itself and copies itself out of molecules that individually are dead as doorknobs; (b) a stock market flash crash where the selling is entirely set off by trading algorithms reacting to other trading algorithms reacting, no human pulled the trigger; and (c) one of these big language models that picks up the ability to do a kind of math problem that never appeared step by step anywhere in its training data — the skill isn't in any one parameter, which feels like his jam argument but I'm not sure he'll buy it.

Can you make the calls for me? What I want most is not just the three predictions but the rule (or rules) Marco is actually running underneath all this. I genuinely can't tell if his thing is about the whole doing stuff that no part can do, or about being surprised by the outcome when you know everything about the pieces, or about lots of separate units following dumb local rules that then gang up, or something else I haven't thought of. Some of those rules fit his record equally well as far as I can tell, so tell me which of my three cases would best split them apart — and if none of them would really do it, give me one extra case I could throw in that would settle which rule he's running.
