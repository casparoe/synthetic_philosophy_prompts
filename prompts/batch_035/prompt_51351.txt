I'm an engineer on a team that deploys language-model agents in production, and I've been assigned the safety architecture for our next release. The working assumption for this release is that the model may be misaligned: instead of trying to make the model want good outcomes, we build the checks around it so that a model with bad intentions still can't cause harm, or at least can't do so without our noticing. I take it this is what safety people call control as opposed to alignment. Before we pick an architecture, I want the requirements written down, so please produce the spec: what any adequate control scheme for this kind of deployment has to deliver, with the motivating case behind each requirement.

To make "adequate" concrete, here's the deployment I have in mind: the agent takes consequential actions (spending money, editing code that runs in production, sending messages to users) without a human reading every output. We're fairly confident we'd catch a one-shot catastrophe; what worries me is patient strategies — behavior that looks fine on every individual action, sandbagging on our own evaluations, or two instances quietly coordinating. A few things I'd like the spec to take a position on, each of which I suspect hides a real constraint: the difference between evidence that works only because the model can't tell it's being tested and evidence that survives the model knowing our monitoring setup; whether the assurance has to be a quantified budget (some tolerated probability of undetected harmful action) rather than a binary "safe"; whether the scheme can be verified by an outside auditor at all if some of its effectiveness comes from the model not knowing its details; how the scheme should degrade as the model's capabilities grow relative to our monitors'; and what it owes us when the monitored model and the monitoring model share training biases.

Then flag any pair of your requirements that you suspect can't be jointly satisfied — that's the part I most want, and I'd rather hear "these two can't both hold, pick one" than get a list that quietly drops one. Finally, in a couple of sentences each, score the obvious candidates against the list: humans reviewing a sample of outputs, a more trusted model monitoring a less trusted one, making the agent expose its reasoning and having a second model check that reasoning, and plain access control with narrow permissions. Say which single requirement excludes the most of these. Keep the weight on the criteria and their motivating cases; the scorecard matters least.
