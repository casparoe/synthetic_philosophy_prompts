my brother drowned last week (bad dive off a dock, he was under for a while before anyone found him). he's on a ventilator. the doctors ran their tests two separate times, including the one where they disconnect the machine to see if he'll breath on his own, and they're now saying he's brain dead, that in our state that legally means he died that day, and they want to talk about organ donation. here's where I'm stuck.

his heart is beating on its own. he's warm. when the nurse repositioned his arm it moved up toward his face and mom swears dead men dont move. the doctors say the movement is spinal reflexes and that the breathing test proves the brain stem is gone, but watching it I didn't experience it as reflex, and I can't make myself unsee it.

for what it's worth, I've always been the "you are your brain" guy in the family. whenever these things came up at dinner I'd say the rest is plumbing — heart, lungs, whatever — the person is the brain, and when the brain's gone the person's gone, full stop. standing next to that bed I'm discovering I don't believe it nearly as much as I thought. what I feel is that he's gravely injured and still here.

and there's one more wrinkle. a social worker mentioned that if we're not comfortable deciding now, there's another route: we withdraw the ventilator, wait for his heart to stop, and they can still recover some of the organs after — but she said they only wait something like two minutes after the heart stops before they start. two minutes. my whole life I'd have said the stopped heart is the real death, the one our ancestors could actually see, and even that now feels like it's being rushed. I also keep thinking about the fact that I've read of pregnant women in his condition being kept on machines for months so the baby could be born — which seems to cut both ways in my head, like his body is the kind of thing that can keep going without him, or like maybe the person isn't as gone as the paperwork says.

mom is looking to me to tell her what to think and I have to give the hospital an answer. so, please: is he dead? was he dead when the second round of tests came back, or does he only die when the ventilator comes off, or is even the two-minutes-after version not really death yet? take an actual side — don't just tell me it's a personal or religious decision, I've got a chaplain for that. but I also want you to say how confident you are in each step of your answer and flag the part you're least sure of, because I'm going to have to repeat this to my mother and I'd rather know where the ground is soft.
