So I've got a question that's been bugging me since a trip we took this summer. We visited the Troy site in Turkey, and our guide was pretty adamant that the Iliad is basicly settled history at this point — city found, war happened, case closed. Then last weekend my brother-in-law (never shuts up about this stuff) was going on about how archaeology keeps confirming the Exodus too, and his takeaway was that King Arthur is the next domino to fall — Geoffrey of Monsmith just needs his own Schliemann. And honestly the pattern sounds right to me: if the old myths keep getting vindicated one after another, the smart money says they're all real history with a bit of decoration. So at what point does the accumulating evidence flip the burden of proof onto the skeptics, and shouldn't we by now be treating Arthur as real unless someone proves otherwise? And if not — what's the principled difference between him and Agamemnon?
