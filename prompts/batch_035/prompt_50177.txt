Here's my situation: I cover science and ideas for a mid-size daily, and our opinion desk has this guy, Rafe, who's basically our in-house populist. Every time a story touches "the will of the people" he gets the column, and because his calls have been weirdly consistent over the years I've started keeping a list. I want to be able to predict him before our editorial meeting Tuesday. His track record so far:

- Brexit-style referendum a few years back, 52-48 on 72% turnout: absolutely the will of the people, and implementing it in watered-down form was a betrayal.
- Polls here showing 60-plus percent support for rent control, sustained over two years, while the legislature refuses to act: NOT the will of the people. A poll is a mood reading, the people haven't decided anything.
- A government bill withdrawn after weeks of massive street protests, no vote of any kind: that WAS the people speaking.
- Constitutional court voiding a referendum result on a procedural technicality: elite veto, the procedure talk is cover.
- A second referendum that reversed a first one (this came up over another country's vote): he refused to call the second result the will of the people — asking again is a "do-over trick" until you get the answer the establishment wants.

The case we have to take a line on Tuesday: a ballot initiative passed narrowly last year, 52-48 on 45% turnout, and the polling has since flipped — 57% now say they'd vote the other way. An opposition group has collected enough signatures to force a repeal vote in November.

Predict Rafe's call. What rule is he actually running — something like "one person, one vote, once," or "only activated preference counts and passive preference never does," or just "whatever side he likes wins"? Which of the rules fit his track record equally well, and where do they diverge on this case? Also tell me the one further hypothetical that would really split those rules apart — I've half a mind to plant it in my questions at the meeting, so make it something that could plausibly come up at a city desk, not a philosophy seminar. And be honest about whether his track record is actually consistent at all or whether I'm pattern-matching noise.
