I run a philosophy of biology reading group, nine grad students, one 2-hour session, and I want a game where the group collectively turns into a system that behaves as if someone designed it, while each person only ever sees their own tiny local rule. My rough idea (steal it or fix it): everyone gets a card with a simple behavioral rule, they play iterated rounds of some simple resource game against a changing environment, and losers get swapped out for copys of winners' rules with occasional small mutations. After enough rounds the surviving rule-set should fit together into something that makes sense as a well-adapted whole — even though nobody in the room ever held the design in their head, and nobody could even see beyond their own card. The debrief should land the point that the rationale the system embodies was free-floating the whole time — reasons without any reasoner — and then push on whether something nobody ever reasoned through really deserves to be called a reason. I want rules, materials, timings, a payoff scheme that works with points instead of money, an honest note on what predictably goes wrong the first time through, and which part of the idea (say, that genuine rationales are supposed to be normatively evaluable, not just selected-for) no game can actually reproduce no matter how I set it up. Keep the whole thing under 800 words — I want rules I could print on one page and run from cold.
