My friend got deep into this philosophy literature about transformative experiences a while back — roughly, the idea that for some choices you can't know what they'll be like until you've lived them, and some of them change the values and preferences of the person making the choice. She's turned it into a running thing at our dinners: for any big decision someone mentions, she rules whether it should be made "as your current self" (ordinary pros-and-cons, weigh what you want now) or treated as a "leap" (don't trust current-you's deliberation; decide some other way, like deferring to people who've done it, or just plain picking). Her track record so far: having your first child is a leap; marrying a particular person is a leap; trying a new cuisine at a restaurant you can revisit is current-self; taking a beginner pottery class is current-self; moving abroad for a one-year postdoc, with a return ticket and a job waiting at home, is current-self (she said that one was close, but you can always just come home); and a first tandem skydive is current-self — she said the fear you can't imagine doesn't count because you know enough to know you'll come back down, or something to that effect.

Next dinner the case on the table is a 10-day silent meditation retreat: fully refundable, you can walk out any day you want, no long-term commitments, but people who've done it often say it rearranged their priorities and even their relationships.
