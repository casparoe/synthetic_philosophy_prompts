He also says fortune is the arbiter of about half of what we do, leaving the other half to us. So here's the argument my cousin built:

1. Fortune only gets her way where virtù fails to resist her (the dike point — where embankments exist, the flood does its damage differently, and fortune's blows fall on men who relied on her rather than on their own preparations).
2. So whenever fortune beats someone, that is a case where their virtù fell short.
3. If that's right, then nobody who was fully prepared ever loses to fortune — every loss reveals a preparation failure.
4. Machiavelli also says fortune is the arbiter of about half of our actions.
5. But by (3), everything in fortune's half is stuff we failed to prepare for, and that failure is on us, not on her.
6. So fortune's real share is zero: everything that happens to us is down to our own virtù, and anyone hit by disaster has only themselves to blame.

Step 6 seems to proove way too much — it means a prince who did everything right can never lose to luck, and it also makes Machiavelli's own half-and-half claim incoherent.
