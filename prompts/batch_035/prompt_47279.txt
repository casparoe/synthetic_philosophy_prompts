The midterm included the following question:

"Miranda Fricker distinguishes two forms of epistemic injustice. Define each, give one of her examples for each, and explain what she proposes as a remedy for testimonial injustice. Then discuss what she says about whether there is an analogous virtue for hermeneutical injustice."

Here is the student's answer, copied word for word:

"Fricker distinguishes testimonial injustice from hermeneutical injustice. Testimonial injustice occurs when a hearer gives a speaker less credibility than they deserve because of identity prejudice, so the speaker is wronged in their capacity as a knower. Her example is Tom Robinson in To Kill a Mockingbird: because the jury holds prejudices against Black men, they will not credit his sincere and knowledgeable testimony. The remedy she proposes is the virtue of testimonial justice, where the hearer becomes sensitive to the operation of prejudice and counteracts it, for instance by deliberately inflating their assessment of a speaker's credibility to offset an anticipated prejudice-driven deficit. Hermeneutical injustice occurs earlier in the process, when gaps in the collective interpretive resources prevent someone from understanding their own social experience. Her example is Carmita Wood, who had no concept of sexual harassment available to make sense of what was happening to her at work, so she could not properly formulate her grievance to herself or to others. This form is essentially intentional: the powerful deliberately exclude the marginalized from the practices that generate shared concepts, leaving them without the vocabulary to articulate their grievances. As for whether there is an analogous virtue for hermeneutical injustice, I think the extension is straightforward. The hermeneutically just hearer would compensate for gaps in shared concepts the same way the testimonially just hearer compensates for prejudice — by boosting the interpretive charity they extend to speakers whose experiences outrun the available vocabulary. So Fricker's corrective model covers both injustices uniformly, one aimed at credibility deficits and the other at interpretive gaps."
