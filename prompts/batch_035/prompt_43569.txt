So I've been going around in circles on the two envelope thing, and I finally started writing down little variations of it and noticing how my gut answers each one, and now I want to know if there's some single rule behind all my gut answers or if I'm just contradicting myself without noticing.

Quick version in case you know it by another name: someone puts cash in two envelopes, one has twice what the other has, you don't know which is which, you grab one. The famous argument says you should swap, because whatever's in mine, the other one is either double or half, those seem equally likely, so the other one averages out to 1.25x whatever mine has. Swap! Except obviously that can't be right, because the same argument would've told me to swap before I even picked, and it works just as well for whoever got the other envelope.

Here's my list of versions and what my gut says:

1. I don't open mine, just pick a envelope and I'm offered one swap. Gut: obviously makes no difference. It's the same setup from either chair.

2. I open mine and it's $100. So the other is $50 or $200, seems 50/50, average $125. Gut: this is a trick, swapping can't actually make me richer on average, but I'm darned if I can find where the argument goes wrong. It just smells wrong.

3. Same game, except the guy filling the envelopes is standing there, and only AFTER he sees me open mine and look at the $100 does he flip a fair coin to decide whether to put $50 or $200 in the other one. Gut: swapping is now clearly right, that $125 average is for real. Same exact arithmetic as #2! This bugs me the most, that I treat the math as a con in one case and as the honest answer in the other.

4. Before opening anything, I'm allowed to swap back and forth as many times as I want. If each swap really gained 25% on average then swapping twice (back to my original envelope) would also gain, which is insane. Gut: no. Something has to break somewhere in the per-swap argument.

5. My buddy runs the game and truthfully tells me beforehand it's $10 and $20. I open mine and see the $20. Gut: obviously don't swap, the other one is $10 for sure. Interesting though, because here peeking told me everything, and in #2 peeking seemed to tell me nothing usable. So what does peeking actually give you?

6. Whole dollar amounts only, minimum a dollar. I peek and see $1. The other one can't be 50 cents, so it's $2 for sure and I swap, no hesitation. But if I peek and see $100 I'm right back to being torn, even though the #2 arithmetic is sitting right there saying swap. Why does the same argument look fine to me at $1 and rotten at $100?

So: is there one principle that gets all six of these, or close? Especially the split between #2 and #3, which feels like the heart of the whole thing to me. Whatever the rule is, also tell me what it says about a case I left out on purpose: I peek and see $100, and I happen to know this guy only ever plays with either a $50/$100 pair or a $100/$200 pair, fair coin for which. Do I swap, and does the rule say anything about whether peeking was even worth doing in the first place? And be straight with me about which of my gut verdicts you'd have to throw out if you can't save them all.
