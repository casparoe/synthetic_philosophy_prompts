Bit of an unusual request but here goes. Six months ago I got hit by a drunk driver while biking -- I'm mostly recovered but my knee is wrecked for good. Ever since, my feelings toward this guy have been doing something I cant figure out. On the one hand I'm furious at him, on the other I've been reading about Galen Strawson's Basic Argument and honeslty it's messing with me. The way I understand it, the argument goes: to be truly morally responsible for what you do, you have to be responsible for how you are in a way that led to what you did. But to be responsible for how you are, you'd have to have made yourself that way, and you can't do that, because you'd already have to have been a certain way before you made yourself, and you'd need to be responsible for THAT, and so on forever. So nobody is ever responsible for anything in the deep sense that would justify real blame.

I can't find a hole in it, and I've tried. But I also can't shake the feeling that if I just accept it, something in my life goes wrong -- not just my anger at the driver, but my gratitude to the surgeon who rebuilt my leg, my sense that the friend who covered my rent for two months was genuinely being good to me, not just causally helpful in the way a vending machine is helpful.

So here's what I want: make the strongest argument you can that the Basic Argument does NOT show that these attitudes of mine -- anger, gratitude, holding people responsible -- are confused or unjustified. I'm not asking for a survey or a both-sides steelman, and I don't want a reading list. Pick what you think is the strongest line against the argument and actually argue it. If your honest view is that the argument only kills some inflated sense of "ultimate" responsibility that no normal person ever had in mind, say so, but then explain why ordinary responsibility isn't just that inflated sense wearing a disguise -- Strawson's whole point, as far as I can tell, is that the everyday notion is supposed to collapse into the inflated one when you push on it. And if the only move left is "okay, the deep backward-looking sense is gone, but blame can be rebuilt on forward-looking or character-evaluative grounds," I want to know why that rebuilt version is still really blame and not just... me managing my feelings with extra steps. I guess what I'm really asking is which parts of my moral life survive even if the argument is sound, and I want an argument, not comfort.
