In the US states that allow it (like Oregon's Death with Dignity Act), the patient has to self-administer the medication -- the doctor can write the prescription but can't put the cup in your hand, and if your disease has progressed to where you physically can't lift it yourself, you're just out of luck. Meanwhile the Netherlands, Belgium, and Canada let the physician directly administer after a review process, and those countries also allow withdrawal of life support, where a doctor does a positive act (pulling the ventilator) that foreseeably causes death. So my question for my paper: what does the self-administration requirement actually presuppose? Or is it more about keeping the physician's causal hands technically clean, or just a political compromise that got dressed up as a principle? And here's the part I really want your help with: does the rule actually cohere with the fact that these same jurisdictions let doctors withdraw treatment, which is also a doctor's act that foreseeably kills? Please distinguish what the requirement strictly presupposes from what it merely sits comfortably with, and tell me what the practice would have to look like if we took each of the candidate theories seriously (e.g., would a pure autonomy view collapse the self-administration rule entirely, and would a strict sanctity-of-life view ban withdrawal too?).
