I'm in a political philosophy seminar and we just spent two weeks on the gender-equality paradox — the finding that in countries that score highest on gender equality (Norway, Sweden, etc.), sex differences in occupational choice are as big or bigger than in less egalitarian countries, e.g. women making up a smaller share of STEM students there than in places with more traditional norms. For the final we have to submit a "position statement" and the rest of the seminar gets to pick at it, so I wrote down what I actually believe, but before I hand it in I want a sanity check on whether it even hangs together. Here's my list:

(1) I take the empirical finding at face value: where constraints on choice are fewer, sex differences in occupational choice get bigger, not smaller. (2) I think the best explanation is at least partly biological — if you loosen constraints and the differences grow, that's decent evidence you're revealing underlying preference differences rather than creating them. (3) On the justice side I'm a liberal about careers: equal formal opportunity, no discrimination, and beyond that, whatever free individual choices produce is just — so I'm against quotas or representation targets. (4) But I also believe there's no such thing as a preference-neutral upbringing: even in Norway, kids get socialized, gendered norms are everywhere, so nobody's occupational "choice" ever comes from a blank slate. (5) And I think whether a difference traces to biology or socialization is basically irrelevant to whether the resulting pattern is just — source is a descriptive question, justice is about the process. (6) Oh, and methodologically I trust revealed preferences (what people actually pick when stakes are real) over what they tell surveyors.

Some of these feel like they might pull against eachother but honestly I can't tell which pairs are real problems and which just sound bad next to each other. Go through them and tell me which pairs are in genuine tension and which only look that way (and why the apparent ones dissolve). Rank the real tensions by severity instead of treating them all as equally fatal, and for each one say what would have to give and what it costs me to give it up. Lead with the bottom line — one sentence up front on whether my package is consistent or not — then the reasoning, no preamble or restating of my views.
