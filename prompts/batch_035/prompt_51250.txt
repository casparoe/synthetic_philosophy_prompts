so last night my friend and i argued for like three hours about inheritance tax — it started because her family's cottage is about to pass to her and there's a tax bill coming, and by the end we'd dragged in everything from "is it even her money before it's hers" to whether parents reading to their kids is a form of cheating. i went to bed annoyed because i'm not sure i actually have a consistant position. here's what i wrote down as my actual views, can you check whether they hang together:

(1) the core of distributive justice, for me, is equal opportunity: it's bad when your life chances depend on which family you happened to be born into, in a way that doesn't apply to inequalities that track your own choices and effort.

(2) parents conferring advantages on their kids is not just permitted but a good — bedtime reading, paying tuition, leaving them something. i dont think a parent who scrimps for decades to leave the kids a house did anything unjust. family partiality is part of what families are, not a bug.

(3) inheritance tax is still basically the fairest tax there is, because the heir did nothing at all to earn it — it's a windfall, and windfalls are exactly the thing you should tax.

(4) the "it's double taxation, the dead person already paid tax on that money" objection is a bad one and i'm tired of it. the tax falls on the recipient, not the dead person, who by definition isn't harmed.

(5) but i also think there's something real in the idea that the person who earned the wealth gets to say what happens to it, including after death — that's why im uneasy about calling a bequest a pure "windfall" when the will is carrying out the earner's wishes, not the heir's.

(6) and i'd exempt or defer tax on family farms and small businesses so heirs don't have to sell, because dismantling a going concern that took generations to build just to pay a bill seems grotesque. the cottage feels the same way to me, honestly, even though i can hear the tension with (3).

(7) the real enemy is dynastic wealth — concentration sitting across multiple generations — so lately i'm attracted to taxing each recipient's lifetime total of gifts and inheritances (everything they ever receive cumulated) instead of the estate, with big receipts taxed hard and modest ones barely at all.

go through the set and tell me which of these actually clash, which only look like they clash, and rank the clashes by how bad they are. for each real one say what would have to give and what that fix costs me. and then which single revision would clean up the most — i keep flip-flopping between "drop the desert talk from (3)" and "shrink (1) down to formal legal equality only" and i genuinely can't tell if either is necessary or if i'm misdiagnosing where the pressure is coming from.
