I'm presenting next month at an interdisciplinary workshop on the gender-equality paradox — the finding that sex differences in occupational and study choices tend to be larger, not smaller, in countries scoring higher on gender-equality indices — and I need a prep sheet for the question period. My thesis is that the paradox licenses no inference at all about nature, culture, or justice, because the top of the equality rankings is dominated by affluent welfare states whose distinctive labor-market structures — large public sectors, compressed wage scales, extended education systems — re-sort men and women across occupations for reasons having nothing to do with what unconstrained choice would reveal, so the correlation is a developmental confound rather than a window onto preferences under freedom. The audience is hostile from both directions: two behavioral geneticists who take the paradox as evidence that sex-typed dispositions express themselves once constraining norms loosen, a quantitative sociologist of occupational sex segregation who takes it as evidence of durable stereotyping that the equality indices don't capture, and a philosopher of science specializing in cross-cultural measurement who doubts any single number meaningfully aggregates gender equality across nations in the first place. Draft the objections in roughly the order they'll come, phrased the way each of these people would actually phrase them, with a two-minute reply to each, a note on where the reply is soft and what I should concede up front rather than dig in on, and the question I'm hoping nobody asks — for what it's worth, I'm most dreading something about the original secondary-school findings, since the adolescents involved hadn't yet entered any labor market, but judge for yourself what the worst one is. The question period is only fifteen minutes, so assume I need to survive at least one exchange with each camp. Write the whole thing as continuous prose only, the way you'd write me a letter: no headings, bullet points, bold text, or tables.
