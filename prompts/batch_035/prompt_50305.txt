For an extended essay I'm writing on animal communication, I don't want another survey of theories — I want the specification first: the standard any theory of signal honesty has to meet before it deserves evaluation. Please write the desiderata. What must an adequate explanation of why signals stay honest between animals with conflicts of interest actually deliver? Which phenomena must it cover (traditional costly displays like peacock tails, signals that appear cheap yet remain honest, alarm calls that benefit listeners but seem to expose the caller, cooperation between long-term partners like cleaner fish and their clients, communication among relatives)? Which distinctions must it respect — for instance, between a cost paid by every sender regardless of quality and a cost that bites harder for low-quality senders, or between honesty maintained by the signal being physically impossible to fake and honesty maintained by the consequences of being caught faking? For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and mark which are non-negotiable for a theory of honesty and which are commitments of particular camps. Most importantly, tell me whether some subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — in particular, whether a single theory can accommodate genuinely cheap honest signals while preserving the thought that cost is what enforces honesty, or whether that tension is a result in itself. Close with a brief scorecard of how the handicap principle, explanations based on signals being unfakeable indicators of quality, and models based on repeated interactions and punishment do against your list, and say which single requirement excludes the most candidates.
