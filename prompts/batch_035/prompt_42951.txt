My wife and I have been married for twelve years, and we've always kept a shared budget for charitable giving, which we've mostly split down the middle. She cares deeply about animal welfare and has been vegetarian since her teens; I believe that relieving extreme human poverty is the most urgent cause, and I've never seen eating meat as a moral problem in itself. We've always accepted this difference politely and each given in our own direction.

A few weeks ago she made me a proposal I haven't been able to stop thinking about. She said she would volunteer one Saturday a month at the shelter I support and redirect a quarter of her giving to my preferred organization, if in exchange I gave up meat entirely and permanently. She's not asking me to pretend I agree with her; she's asking me to live as if I did, because my doing so costs me little (I could take or leave meat) while mattering enormously to her, just as her Saturdays cost her some time while mattering enormously to me. On paper it looks like a deal where we both end up with more of what we care about, the way two people with different appetites might trade at a dinner table.

But the more I turn it over, the more tangled it seems. First: if I genuinely don't believe eating meat is wrong, am I doing anything morally admirable by abstaining, or am I just buying her satisfaction like any other gift? Does it matter that she will be volunteering for a cause she thinks is merely less important than her own, while I will be refraining from something she thinks is actually wrong? The concessions seem asymmetric in a way I can't pin down.

Second: is there any principled way to compare the sizes of our moral concessions, or is it inevitably horse-trading? Her offer has parts I can measure (hours, money) and parts neither of us can (a lifetime of never eating meat again, and what my abstinence is "worth" to the animals, on her view). If I counter-offer, am I putting a price on my conscience in a way that corrupts it? A friend of mine dismissed the whole thing as just "doing favors for your spouse," but that doesn't seem right either: we're each being moved by what the other thinks is morally at stake, not by each other's personal preferences, and that seems like a different kind of transaction.

Finally, she feels her convictions more intensely than I feel mine, and she knows it. I worry that makes me a worse negotiator: I'd be tempted to accept a lopsided deal just to end the conversation, the way a person who cares less about a house's price still gets steamrolled by one who cares more. Is there a fair way to structure an agreement like ours so that intensity of conviction doesn't simply translate into bargaining power?

I'd be grateful for your thoughts on whether accepting her proposal would be admirable cooperation between two people with different moral views, or a muddying of them — and if it's the former, how we might think clearly about what a fair exchange of moral concessions would look like.
