My mum has a running habit of sorting everyone at the family sailing club into "knows how" or "doesn't know how", and her past calls have been pretty firm. The old joiner who can't explain a single one of his joints but never botches one: knows how. My cousin, who has read every book on swimming ever written and gives brilliant stroke corrections but can't stay afloat for ten seconds herself: doesn't know how. The nephew who stood up on a surfboard exactly once, by sheer fluke, and never again: doesn't know how. And my nan, a lifelong pianist, who mum happily says knows how to play even while she's fast asleep in front of the telly.

Three new people have turned up and I want to predict her calls. First, a former downhill champion whose knees are shot — she can't ski a metre anymore but she coaches with uncanny diagnostics. Second, a chatbot that outputs flawless, individually tailored swimming instruction (I know, it can't swim, but bear with me). Third, a mate who had never once driven a car, then got behind the wheel after mainlining instruction videos for a weekend and drove faultlessly round the block first go — and hasn't tried since.

Could you answer in four named sections, in this order: "Her calls" — her likely verdict on each of the three, a couple of sentences apiece; "The candidate rules" — the different rules that fit her track record equally well, one short paragraph each, and I suspect there are at least two in the running though there may be more; "The decisive case" — which of the three, or of some further case you'd invent, would best pull those rules apart, and what each rule actually says about it; and "My bet" — one paragraph on which rule you'd back her to be running and why. Keep the whole thing under 700 words, it's going in the club newsletter.
