Me and my friend Maya argued for like three hours last night about why pain is bad, and I want to settle it with a quiz. Her line was basically that pain is just a signal, like a check-engine light — it's bad because of what it tells you about your body. Mine was more that the awfulness is right there IN the sensation, so even if it told you nothing you'd still want it gone. We ended up going through a bunch of weird cases (the pain asymbolia stuff, people on morphine who say they still feel the pain but it doesn't bother them, people born unable to feel pain at all) and I realized I dont actually know how many distinct positions there even are, or which one I hold.

Can you write me a which-theory-of-pain's-badness-do-you-actually-hold quiz for my blog? Roughly:

- Eight to ten questions, each a concrete case with two or three answer options. The cases must include at least: pain asymbolia (feeling pain, recognizing it as pain, just not caring about it); the drugged patient who reports the pain but says it doesn't bother them; congenital insensitivity to pain; and one case of pain misrepresenting things, like phantom limb or referred pain, where the felt location doesn't match anything going wrong in the body. You can add others (a runner's burn? childbirth? torture?) but every question has to be answerable by someone who's never read a paper on this — no jargon inside the questions themselves.
- Build the taxonomy from the logical space, not from a list of isms. So first lay out the distinctions (roughly the ones we were circling: is the badness in the feel itself, in what the feel represents about the body, in some kind of command the body issues, or in a downstream reaction of dislike/distress that can come apart from the feeling?), then locate the actual positions in that space. Give each leaf the literature's name if there is one (I gather some people call one of these imperativism? if so use the real term) and mark the rest unnamed — don't invent names.
- A scoring key mapping answer patterns onto leaves, and for each leaf one sentence on its most awkward commitment.
- Flag any two positions the cases can't actually separate. I suspect the represents-damage camp and the body-issues-a-command camp collapse together under every case we could think of last night — if your quiz has the same problem, say so plainly instead of pretending two leaves are distinct.
- Also flag combinations of answers nobody has defended, and for each say whether you think that's because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored.

And the point is to sort readers by what they say about cases, not by which label they like — so no question that just asks whether pain is bad in itself. Thats literally the thing we're trying to diagnose. If it's easy, include a question or two that also tells us something about Maya and me: which of us, if either, comes out holding a coherent position.
