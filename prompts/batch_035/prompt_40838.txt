the knowability paradox literature feels like its been stuck in a loop for decades now. the proof itself is like three lines: if every truth is knowable, and there's at least one truth nobody actually knows, you can derive that every truth is already known. so the Dummett-style anti-realist is left with a menu of escape hatches -- restrict knowability to 'cartesian' propositions (ones that don't entail their own unknowability), restrict it to what's still knowable at a time when the relevant info hasn't been lost forever, go intuitionist so the Moorean conjunction p-and-nobody-knows-p stops counting as a truth, or bite some other bullet -- and the realists reply that every one of these restrictions is ad hoc, reverse-engineered from the theorem instead of coming from any independent motivation. nobody moves. my working theory is that the formal stuff is a proxy war and the real fight is the old one about whether truth can outrun verifiability, in which case no amount of cleverness about modal logic will budge anyone. but maybe I'm wrong and it's a genuinely technical dispute about the logic itself (does knowledge distribute over conjunction, can 'knows' be idealized away from actual finite knowers, etc.). diagnose what kind of disagreement this actually is, whether any of the proposed restrictions on knowability is principled enough to convince a neutral, and what -- if anything -- would count as real progress here.
