I'd like a thought experiment built to order, in the area of lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism and the commensurability of pleasure and pain. Its purpose is to test whether the lexical threshold in that theory does any real work: whether the theory's disagreements with its neighbours are generated by the threshold itself, or merely stipulated by how the case is drawn.

The case must produce a four-way split between two options, A and B:

1. Total hedonic utilitarianism recommends A.
2. Simple negative utilitarianism — on which no amount of pleasure can compensate for any pain, however mild — recommends B.
3. Lexical-threshold negative utilitarianism also lands on A, but this must not be automatic. The pains in the case, though individually below the lexical threshold, must be large enough in aggregate that a reading of the threshold as applying to summed pain would flip the theory to B. So the case must force the lexical theorist to commit to a per-episode (or per-person) reading of the threshold rather than an aggregate one, and the walkthrough must state that commitment explicitly.
4. A two-scale view, on which pleasure and pain are measured on independent scales such that neither can offset the other and cross-scale comparison is undefined, must refuse to rank A and B at all — declaring the comparison ill-formed rather than merely close.

Constraints on the scenario: realistic and mundane — a municipal decision about whether to hold a mass-participation event (a city marathon is the obvious candidate, but the choice is yours), with no science fiction, no perfect information, and no one whose welfare is uncertain. All pains in the case must fall clearly below the lexical threshold, which you should locate at roughly the level of attention-dominating pain (a severe migraine, a kidney stone), so that blistering, cramping, and day-long fatigue sit comfortably below it. The pleasure side must be diffuse across many people rather than concentrated in a few, to block utility-monster replies; and the aggregate quantity of below-threshold pain must be substantial — thousands of aching runners, not a handful — so that the case cannot be dismissed as painless, and so that the aggregate-threshold question is genuinely live. The scenario itself should be one paragraph.
