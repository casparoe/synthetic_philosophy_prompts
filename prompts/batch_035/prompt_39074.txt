I read a popular psychology book last month (not going to name it, doesn't matter, it's the kind of thing you see everywhere) and it summarized the "judgment" side for laypeople roughly like this: 'The judgment theory, associated with philosophers and also with cognitive therapy, holds that an emotion simply is a judgment — a belief about the world. Fear of flying just is the belief that flying is dangerous. It follows that if you correct the belief, the emotion disappears; this is why cognitive behavioral therapy works. And it follows that the theory is refuted by phobias: the arachnophobe who believes perfectly well that the spider in the glass is harmless, and is terrified anyway. Meanwhile neuroscience shows emotions are feelings generated by the amygdala, so the whole debate is dated anyway. The upshot: think your way out of bad feelings, because feelings are thoughts.'

I have my suspicions (the "feelings are thoughts" bit at the end sounds like it's smuggling something past me), but honestly I can't tell which parts are standard simplifications, which are distortions, and which the theory's defenders would flatly deny. And then: does the book's practical upshot — that CBT's success shows emotions are thoughts, so think your way out — follow from anything that survives your corrections?
