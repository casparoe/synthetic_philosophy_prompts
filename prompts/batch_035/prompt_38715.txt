I'm a PhD student in historical sociology, and I'm helping draft the field exam for our comparative-historical methods course. Please write one essay question on Braudel's distinction between the longue durée and the time of events, together with a model answer of roughly 600 words and a marking scheme totaling 20 points. The question must not be answerable by reciting Braudel's three levels of temporal analysis; it should force students to defend or attack the framework's boundaries. Here is the crux I want built into the question: a colleague of mine claims that some events are "structurally dense" — that a single short episode, such as a catastrophic hurricane destroying a port system or an overnight currency reform wiping out a landed class, can compress a slow structure into a few days and thereby function simultaneously as event and structure. The exam question should ask students to assess whether Braudel's framework can accommodate structurally dense events, or whether admitting them collapses the very distinction between geographical/social time and individual time that the framework depends on. The model answer should take a definite position and defend it against the strongest counterargument, and the marking scheme should name the specific moves that separate an A answer from a B answer — for example, whether the student notices that treating a hurricane as an event versus as a manifestation of climate depends on the historian's explanatory target rather than on the hurricane itself.
