My sister and her friend Marcus got into it after our reading group, of all things, and I couldn't referee it, so I'm bringing it here. The topic was the color phi phenomenon -- you know, where you flash a red spot and then a green spot a little ways away, and instead of seeing two blinks, people see one spot gliding across that turns green partway through. Even though there's never actually a middle position shown.

Dev's take: the brain is holding the raw signal back, and you don't become conscious of anything until it can hand you the whole edited event -- spot moving, changing color on the way. So there was never a real moment of experiencing just-red-then-just-green with the motion tacked on later. Consciousness itself was delayed by however many milliseconds so the tape could be fixed before broadcast. She called this the Stalinesque option.

Marcus said no, what happens is you experience the flashes more or less on time, veridically, and then a later process reaches back and rewrites the record of what you experienced -- the way old newspapers get retyped. The lie gets inserted downstream, in memory, not at the level of the live experience itself. Orwellian, in her terms. She said this is just how the report ends up the same either way: your mouth says "it moved and turned green," but what got falsified differs.

A few rounds worth of actual exchange:

Dev said rewriting a conscious memory minutes later is one thing, but here the rewrite has to complete within a tenth of a second or so, and she doesn't believe the memory system works on that timescale. Better to say there was nothing to rewrite -- nothing was ever conscious in the unedited version.

Marcus answered that Dev's alternative has the brain sitting on visual information while the world moves on, with consciousness perpetually running behind. If that were the default, catching a baseball would be impossible. So on-time experience has to be the norm, with fast correction as the exception for weird lab stimuli.

Dev came back with what she called her trump card: the reported motion takes on the color of the *second* spot. That means the brain couldn't have computed the percept until the second flash arrived, so the content of the experience depends on later information -- and if Marcus says the original experience happened AND then got corrected, Dev says she's double counting. You get two experiences, an original one and the doctored one, and the subject could never report the first one even in principle, which Dev thinks shows the "first one" was never a real item.

Marcus wasn't done. She said unreportable isn't the same as nonexistent, and pointed at backward masking in the lab, where a briefly shown stimulus gets erased by whatever comes right after it within tens of milliseconds -- which to her looks like exactly the fast memory-editing Dev said was impossible, and it's routine. Also she thought Dev's trump card conflates the content of the report with the timing of the experience: that the percept was computed late doesn't mean it was experienced late.

By the end Dev was saying Marcus needs a little theater where experiences line up to be watched, and Marcus said Dev needs exactly the same thing, since her delayed broadcast has to premiere somewhere too. They turned to me and I honestly could not say. Can you go through their exchange step by step and tell me who's right at each point -- not just who wins overall, but which specific moves hold up and which don't? And if there's something both of them are missing that would dissolve part of the fight, I'd want to know that too.
