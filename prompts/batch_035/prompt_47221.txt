My boyfriend and I have been together five years and we're stuck on a decision that's turning into a whole thing, and I want someone outside our friend group to tell me whether his method for settling it is actually fair or just sounds fair.

The situation: he's been offered a job in a city ~1000km from where we live now. It's basically his dream job in a field he's trained years for, and if he turns it down there won't be another shot like this. But my whole life is here — my parents are getting old and I'm the one who helps them out, my job is one I love and can't easily transfer, and honestly I'd be pretty miserable uprooting. His claim is that his misery if he stays (spending his life wondering what if) would be *much bigger* than my misery if we go, so going is what we should do. To make it less hand-wavy he proposed a system: we each rate, honestly, on a 0-10 scale, how bad each option would feel for us personally, then we pick the option with the lowest total unhappiness across the two of us. He says this is objective because the 0-10 scale is publicly calibrated — everybody knows what a 7 out of 10 bad feels like, same as everybody knows what 7 out of 10 spicy means.

When I told my friends about this over dinner, two of them pushed back in different directions and now I don't know what to think. One said the whole comparison is fake because there's no common unit — when he says 8 and I say 8 we're just uttering the same word, not measuring the same thing, so adding his number to mine is like adding his kilograms to my pounds. The other one said if the numbers really are incommensurable then we're at a genuine tie, and the fair way to break a tie is a coin flip — that way neither of us is claiming our bad feelings count for more, we just let chance decide, which he said is actually more respectful of us as equals. My boyfriend thinks they're both overcomplicating it and that people compare each other's suffering all the time (we say "I had it worse than you" and everyone understands), so the numbers are just making explicit what we already do.

So, is his method fair? Is the "calibrated scale" argument any good, or is my first friend right that the addition is meaningless? And if it *is* meaningless, does that actually mean we're at a tie — like, does "can't be compared" imply "can't be distinguished", or could it still be true that one of us would genuinely lose more even if no procedure can show it? The coin flip idea appeals to me but I can't shake the feeling that it treats a real asymmetry between us as if it didn't exist. Would love some actual analysis and not just "communicate more" — we've communicated plenty, that's how we got to spreadsheets.
