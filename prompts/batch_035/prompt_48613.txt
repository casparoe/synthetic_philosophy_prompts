I've ended up holding a view I half-jokingly call "dormant Malthusianism": Malthus wasn't wrong, he was early. The positive and preventive checks were real, the Industrial Revolution was a one-time escape funded by a stored-energy subsidy (fossil fuels) plus a one-time behavioral shift (the transition to small families), and once those run their course the old mechanisms just pick back up where they left of. The theory isn't refuted, it's asleep.

Tell me what would count against this. Not the obvious "he was wrong about Victorian England," everyone gets that. I mean: which observations, over say the next century, would have to obtain for the theory to stay asleep in a way that counts as refutation rather than dormancy? And which developments would count as it waking up — and would a committed dormancy-theorist have to treat those as confirmation, or could the counting be gerrymandered after the fact?

Here's the part I most want: an honest reckoning with whether my view has been insulated into unfalsifiability. Every time the doom doesn't arrive on schedule I say "dormant"; the worry is that "early" can stretch indefinitely and the position never risks anything at all. Is there a version of the view that actually sticks its neck out — some observable, some time frame, some development the view predicts that its rivals don't — or does it collapse into "bad things happen when resources get tight," which is safe and useless? And if it does collapse, is that fatal for a view of this kind, or just the honest price of doing long-run theory?

Also weigh in on the retrospective side, seperate from the future stuff. My story leans on Malthus looking good for pre-industrial regimes — real wages pinned near subsistence, late marriage as the preventive check, Ireland as the positive check doing its worst. How much of that fit is genuine and how much is me writing history backward to rescue the theory? I don't want a bibliography or citations, just your assessment.

Keep the whole thing under 650 words. Rank the candidate defeaters from fatal through awkward to merely inconvenient, say which ones a committed holder of the view should concede as counting against it versus dismiss, and end with a plain answer to whether the view as I hold it is falsifiable at all — and if not, whether that's a bug or just what long-run prediction looks like.
