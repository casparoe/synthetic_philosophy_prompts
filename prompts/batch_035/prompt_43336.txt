My philosophy of probability study group has been stuck on the Sleeping Beauty problem for what feels like forever, and I've started to wonder whether the fight is even about what people think it's about. Quick setup in case: Beauty is put to sleep on Sunday, a fair coin is flipped, and if it lands heads she's woken once on Monday; if tails she's woken Monday and Tuesday, with a memory wipe in between so the wakings are subjectively identical. She's told all of this in advance and knows she can't tell which waking she's in. When she wakes up, what should her credence be that the coin landed heads?

The obvious answers both have serious people behind them, and in my group neither side has moved an inch in months. The halfers say it should stay 1/2: she knew on Sunday that she would be woken at least once and would learn nothing new on waking, so nothing happened that should change her credence, and Bayesian updating on information you already had is a no-op. The thirders say 1/3: condition on tails there are two indistinguishable wakings to one under heads, so her current situation is twice as likely to be a tails-waking, the same way drawing a ball from a doubled urn shifts your odds. Each side has what looks like a knockdown reply to the other. Thirders point out that if Beauty is offered a bet on heads every time she wakes, she loses money in the long run if she bets at even odds, so 1/2 seems decision-theoretically busted. Halfers reply that this conflates credence with betting behavior when the number of bets you place depends on the outcome you're betting on, which is exactly the weird feature of the setup, and that per-experiment the fair odds are still even. There's even a third faction in my group, the double-halvers, who insist that Beauty's credence in heads AND in it being Monday should both stay at 1/2 when she wakes — which everyone agrees is inconsistent with ordinary conditionalization, and they just bite that bullet. Meanwhile someone keeps bringing up the anthropic parallels, like the doomsday argument and Fine-tuning-style reasoning, as if solving Beauty would settle those too, which makes me suspect half the group isn't even arguing about the same question as the other half.

So what I actually want isn't a verdict on the right answer — we've heard all the verdicts. I want a diagnosis of the standoff itself. Is this a substantive disagreement about probability, or is it partly verbal, with the two camps meaning different things by "credence" (something like a long-run betting frequency versus a rate of being right in possible centered worlds, or whatever)? Is the real hidden question decision-theoretic — about what you do with a credence when you're paid per awakening — rather than epistemic? Do the two sides disagree about which of Beauty's possible situations count as "data" she can update on? Or is it bedrock: two starting intuitions (don't update on old information vs. weigh centered possibilities proportionally) that are each locally rational and neither side can argue the other out of? My own hypothesis, for whatever it's worth, is that a lot of the heat comes from people implicitly choosing between a per-awakening and a per-experiment evaluation without noticing they've chosen, so the same number means different things to different people — but I'm not sure that explains why people who agree on the betting setup still fight about the credence itself.
