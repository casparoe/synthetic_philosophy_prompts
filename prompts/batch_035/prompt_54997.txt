Question: "Given a two-person bargaining problem specified by a feasible set S of utility pairs and a disagreement point d, define the Kalai–Smorodinsky solution geometrically, and contrast its axioms with Nash's: state one axiom satisfied by the Nash bargaining solution but not by Kalai–Smorodinsky, and one satisfied by Kalai–Smorodinsky but not by Nash, saying precisely what each axiom asserts."

Student's answer: "Both solutions take as given the feasible set S of utility pairs and the disagreement point d. Nash's solution is the point of S maximizing the product (u1 − d1)(u2 − d2); Kalai–Smorodinsky's is the point where the Pareto frontier of S intersects the ray drawn from d through the utopia point, whose coordinates are each player's maximum feasible utility. Because the intersection lies on this ray, each player receives the same fraction of their maximum possible gain, so KS divides the gains in proportion to what is possible for each player, whereas Nash maximizes the product of the gains. Axiomatically, both solutions are Pareto optimal, symmetric, and invariant under positive affine transformations of the utilities, and KS is in fact the unique solution satisfying these three axioms together with monotonicity. The difference lies in how each handles changes to the bargaining set. Nash satisfies independence of irrelevant alternatives: removing alternatives, so long as the chosen point remains feasible, never changes the agreement. KS satisfies this same IIA axiom, since its ray and the Pareto frontier are both determined by the original problem and are unaffected by which alternatives are available. What KS adds, and Nash lacks, is monotonicity: when the feasible set expands so that some player's maximum rises, that player does at least as well as before."
