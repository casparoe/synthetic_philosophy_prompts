Draft the joint closing statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed endurantist — who holds that a persisting object is wholly present at every moment of its existence — and a committed perdurantist — who holds that persisting objects are four-dimensional wholes with temporal parts. The collaboration has run for a year and covered the usual ground, plus a case they devised themselves: a wooden ship whose planks are removed one at a time over a century and simply destroyed, never replaced, until on some final day the last plank is burned. By the end, each camp says something the other finds barely intelligible: the perdurantist is tempted to say the ship's later temporal parts are "smaller" in a way that tracks the dwindling planks, and is unsure what to say about the instant of ceasing to exist; the endurantist says the ship was whole and entire at every moment it existed at any size, and ceased to exist exactly once, at some precise threshold they admit is hard to locate. Write the document both parties could sign: the claims they genuinely agree on (including about what the disagreement is not about, e.g. that they are not disagreeing about any observation, and about which formulations of the problem of temporary intrinsics they each concede the other can answer); the residual disagreements, each phrased in wording its own side would accept; and the cruxes — I suspect one is whether "the ship is smaller than it was" is literally true or a loose way of speaking, and another is whether the relativistic argument from the frame-relativity of simultaneity carries any weight. For each crux, name an argument, case, or choice-point both sides agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. If part of the dispute strikes you as merely verbal, say which part and why.

Format the deliverable as a numbered section of agreed claims followed by a disagreements table with these columns: Disagreement; Endurantist's accepted wording; Perdurantist's accepted wording; Crux it tracks; Shared test or argument; Endurantist prediction; Perdurant prediction. Close with a one-paragraph joint statement (under 120 words) on whether the collaboration found any genuine point of disagreement that could in principle be resolved.
