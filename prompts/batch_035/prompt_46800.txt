And here's the writeup I need to grade (from a group member, mid-20s, has read a bit of the literature but is definitely not an expert):

"The simple conditional analysis says that to call a glass fragile is just to say that if the glass were struck, it would break. Fragility is analyzed as a conditional about what would happen under certain circumstances, not as some extra property the glass has.

A fink is a mechanism that would remove the disposition if the situation that would trigger it starts to occur. Lewis's famous electro-fink illustrates this: a fragile glass is wired up to a device that, at the very moment the glass is struck, makes the glass lose its fragility (say, by instantly melting and annealing its surface) before it can shatter. So the glass is fragile, but the conditional 'if struck, it would break' is false, which refutes the simple analysis.

A mask works the same basic way as a fink, just mediated by something else: it removes the disposition when the trigger occurs. For example, poison has a disposition to kill when ingested, but if the victim also takes an antidote, the antidote removes the poison's disposition to kill, so the poison is still deadly but the conditional fails.

So yes, finks and masks together show that dispositions are not real intrinsic properties. Since the conditional analysis fails, there is nothing left for 'fragility' to be besides a handy summary of our predictions about how the object will behave in interactions, and those predictions are always about relations to other objects, not about the glass itself."
