I'm a population geneticist by training — I think natively in selection coefficients, effective population sizes, drift, linkage disequilibrium, heritability, mutation–selection balance, and the Price equation — and I know the memetics literature only at the popular level, Dawkins's replicator proposal and its descendants. Restate memetic theory as a body of claims in my vocabulary, as faithfully as you can, and then give me a residue report.

To give you a sense of the mapping I'd want attempted, not as a checklist you must complete: Is a meme best treated as an allele, as a haplotype, or as the lineage of a segregating variant? What plays the role of the population, the generation, and the effective population size? Is meme fitness a per-capita replication rate to which something like Fisher's fundamental theorem could apply, or is frequency-dependence so dominant that the quantitative apparatus collapses? Does anything like mutation–selection balance or drift in small populations have a cultural counterpart, and could it in principle be measured the way I would want to measure it? Please treat the dual-inheritance tradition as shared ground — I know gene–culture coevolution and the models of vertical and horizontal transmission that come with it — so concentrate on memetics as its own proposal, including where its proponents took it after Dawkins.

Then tell me what didn't survive the translation. In particular, I want your honest assessment of three criticisms I've encountered: that cultural inheritance is blending rather than particulate, which was fatal to Darwin's own theory before Mendel; that cultural variation is directed rather than blind, so nothing plays the role mutation plays for me; and that selection on memes is inextricably mediated by selection on the human minds that carry them, leaving no clean way to assign fitness at the level I would need. For each, say whether it is an artifact of forcing my vocabulary onto memetics, a genuine gap memetics has never filled, or something my vocabulary cannot express at all. Where it is the last, tell me whether the inexpressible residue points to something real that neither framework captures — something about meaning, persuasion, or the interests of the host — or whether it points the other way.

Finally, give me your considered view: is memetics a research program that population-genetic tools could in principle discipline into something quantitative, or is the translation so lossy that the differences are the whole story? If the former, say what the first serious model would have to contain; if the latter, say precisely where the analogy stops paying rent.
