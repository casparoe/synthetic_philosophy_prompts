Before I read any more proposed solutions to the combination problem, I want to be clear about what would count as solving it. Please write the desiderata: what any adequate account of how micro-experiences compose into the unified experience of a macro-level subject has to deliver — which cases it must get right, which distinctions it must respect, and which assumptions it is not entitled to make without argument. The purpose is fixed: the solution is meant to show that panpsychism does not quietly reintroduce the strong emergence it was advertised as avoiding, so the list should be written so that a "solution" meeting all of it would genuinely be competitive with dualist emergentism and with illusionism, not merely internally coherent.

I don't want the usual recitation of the problem's sub-problems, so here are the angles the desiderata must cover. First, additivity: many treatments assume phenomenality composes like mass, but plenty of legitimate quantities compose non-additively (vector quantities, temperature, entropy of coupled systems), so the list should include a requirement that any composition rule be stated explicitly and defended, not smuggled in. Second, the fate of the micro-subjects: I've noticed defenders tend to say how micro-experiences could jointly ground a macro experience while staying silent on whether the micro-subjects survive combination, are absorbed into the macro subject, or cease to exist when combination begins — I want a desideratum that forces an answer, since each option carries a cost that should be visible in the specification. Third, reversibility and degree: the account must work run backward, so it must say something about split-brain patients, hemispherectomy, dreamless sleep, and anesthesia — and combination cannot be all-or-nothing, so a partial-composition case needs to be handled. Fourth, non-arbitrary boundaries: the account must distinguish the brain's one subject from any merely causally coupled aggregate, and it should be tested against at least one borderline case of your own construction — for instance, a swarm of eyeless flatworms coordinating chemically into something that behaves as a single organism, or the passengers on a train causally coupled through their phones — and it may not stay silent on whether such systems are subjects. Fifth, the palette and structure: micro-qualities must be able to compose the full range of macro qualities, and phenomenal structure must connect to physical and functional structure without collapsing into a straightforward identity claim. Beyond these, add whatever else you think genuinely belongs, and mark each desideratum as either non-negotiable across the debate or a preference of a particular camp, saying which camp and why.

Two things I most want to see: whether any subset of the requirements is jointly unsatisfiable — if the specification itself contains a hidden impossibility result, say so, since that would be the interesting outcome — and which single requirement does the most work in excluding candidate solutions. Finally, note at least one region of the logical space of possible solutions that no desideratum on your list excludes but that nobody, to your knowledge, actually occupies, and diagnose whether that's because the region is incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored. Please keep the emphasis on the criteria and the motivating cases rather than on scoring particular existing theories against them; I'll do that part myself.
