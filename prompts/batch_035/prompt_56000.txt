Here's an old comparison I keep chewing on and I can't tell if it actually holds up. Some Victorian scientist (Huxley, I think it was) said the mind is like the whistle on a steam engine: the engine's machinery produces the whistle sound as a side effect, but the sound doesn't push the train anywhere. The fuel, the pistons, the wheels — that's what moves the train; the whistle is just something the engine makes while it works. Swap in brain and consciousness and you get: your experiences are real, they're genuinely there every time the brain does its thing, but they don't push anything around. I first ran into this in a forum thread about free will and honestly found it kind of a relief — it lets you say consciousness exists without having to explain how some ghostly feeling could shove neurons around. The trouble is the more I think about the actual train, the more the comparison seems to fall apart on me.

Because here's what bugs me: the engineer can hear the whistle. So can the neighbors — if it's too loud at 5am they file a complaint, and that complaint was caused by the whistle. If I say my toothache is killing me, and consciousness is supposed to be the whistle, then the pain should be doing the exact same work the whistle's sound did — making the report happen. But the whole point of the comparison was that the whistle does nothing to the machinery. So my sentence about the pain can't be caused by the pain, and then I have no idea what makes it a sentence about the pain rather than about anything else. And there's another wierd thing: there's nobody standing outside my brain the way the neighbors stand outside the track — the only "hearing" of consciousness that ever happens seems to be done by the very machinery it's not supposed to touch. My brother in law says I'm overthinking it and the analogy is only meant to show a byproduct can be real without being needed, not to explain how we manage to talk about it, but that feels like dodging the interesting part to me.

So can you audit this thing for me? Like: what actually maps onto what (the engine, the steam, the whistle, the engineer, the neighbors, the station schedule the whistle announces, and so on), which parts of the train picture are quietly doing work that the mind version can't do, and whether the conclusion it was being used to support — that consciousness can just be a harmless byproduct and once you say that there's nothing left to explain — survives once you take seriously that we spend half our waking lives reporting on our experiences. If the analogy is broken beyond repair, is there a patched version, or a better analogy altogether that keeps the comforting part without the hole? I have no philosophy background, I just argue about this stuff at thanksgiving, so plain language please.
