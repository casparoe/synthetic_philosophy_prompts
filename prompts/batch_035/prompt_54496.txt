My friend Deb is the most consistent moral judge I know, which is honestly annoying, so I've started keeping a list of her verdicts and trying to work out the rule she's running underneath them. Her rulings so far: two lads leave the pub equally drunk and both drive home, one clips a cyclist and puts him in a wheelchair and the other gets home fine — she thinks the first is a genuinely worse person who deserves prison, while the second basically made a mistake lots of people make and got lucky. A careful driver doing everything right whose car kills a kid who ran out from behind an ice cream van — zero blame, she gets emotional just thinking about what that driver is going through now. A bloke from a truly awful childhood who thumps someone in a pub — she says you cant judge him like you'd judge someone with a lovely mum who does exactly the same thing. And her nephew, who's generous because he was just born that way, gets less credit from her than a bloke she knows who's stingy by nature but fights it every single day and gives to charity anyway.

She's coming to stay next month and I want to have some test cases ready for her. What will she say about these two? First: two surgeons each attempt the same risky operation that was their patient's only chance, one patient dies and the other pulls through — is the losing surgeon more to blame, the way the unlucky drink-driver was, or is that completely different? Second: a driver with a brain condition no test available at the time could ever have caught, who blacks out at the wheel and kills a pedestrian — is that like the ice-cream-van driver or not, and why? I'm honestly less fussed about her answers than about the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that would fit every verdict she's given so far just as well, and which case — mine or one I havent thought of — would do the best job of telling those rules apart.
