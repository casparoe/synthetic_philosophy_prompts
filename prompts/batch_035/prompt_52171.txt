Background so you know where I'm coming from: I'm a retired physical chemist. Spent thirty-odd years measuring reaction kinetics and transport properties, so entropy and the second law were my daily bread, but I only started reading philosophy seriously after I retired and moved to the countryside. Now I've got time on my hands and a stack of books about time itself, and I've ended up holding a view I'd like to stress-test.

The view: the thermodynamic arrow of time -- the fact that entropy increases toward the future but not the past -- is explained by a special, very low-entropy boundary condition at (or near) the beginning of the universe, what I gather the philosophers call the Past Hypothesis, together with the time-reversal symmetry (or near-symmetry) of the fundamental dynamics. Given that starting condition, the standard statistical-mechanical machinery makes entropy increase in both temporal directions away from that boundary more or less inevitable, and everything else that gives time its direction -- why we remember the past but not the future, why causation seems to run one way, why stirrings don't unstir -- is downstream of that. So my question: what would actually count against this package? I'm not after the lazy "it's a description, not an explanation" complaint unless you can make it really bite. Tell me which observations, arguments, or possible discoveries would genuinely damage the view, and rank them from fatal to merely awkward. For example: does it matter if the fundamental dynamics turn out to violate time symmetry outright (I've read there are small violations in particle physics)? What if cosmology settles on no beginning at all, or on a high-entropy initial state? What about the objection that the Past Hypothesis itself cries out for explanation, or the worry that it makes observers like us wildly atypical in some measure? Which of these would a committed defender concede counts against the view, and which would they wave away -- and are they right to? Finally, an honest assessment of whether the hypothesis has been qualified into unfalsifiability (I keep seeing escape hatches: maybe the condition is "simplicity" rather than low entropy, maybe we live in a fluctuation, maybe it's a fact about a whole ensemble of universes), and if so, whether that's a problem for a posit of this kind or just a brute fact we've dressed up in explanation's clothing -- rather like the values of the constants, which as a chemist I never felt needed justifying.

A couple of housekeeping requests: please write your answer in British English, and bear with me if I've made typos -- typing this in a hurry. Also, I can follow the physics fine but the philosophy is new territory for me, so where a term of art comes up (typicality, say, or a "measure" over initial states), a plain sentence saying what it means would go a long way.
