Write an essay on Death in Damascus — the Gibbard and Harper case where you run into Death in Damascus, he mutters that he was expecting you tomorrow in Aleppo, and since he's a perfect predictor, whichever city you pick turns out to be the one he's already in. The usual story is that causal decision theory comes out unstable here: deciding to go to Aleppo is evidence he's in Aleppo, so you should stay; but settling on staying is evidence he's in Damascus, so you should go — you're forever revising with no fixed point. Here's the twist I want the essay to actually be about: suppose Death's prediction is only right 80% of the time. Work through whether that breaks the instability, and in particular whether flipping a coin between the cities helps — notice that the answer may depend on what "80% accurate" even means (is he right about your final act with probability .8 no matter what you do, or does he just predict your strategy and then both of you independently sample from it?). Be careful with that distinction, since it changes whether randomizing buys you anything. Weigh the main lines of argument (CDT's instability, what evidential and updateless styles of reasoning say here, whether mixed strategies are even in the option set for a decision theory), but don't end non-committal — defend a concrete recommendation, even if it's conditional like "flee to Aleppo only below some accuracy threshold, coin-flip above it." End the essay with a final line beginning "Verdict:" giving your recommendation as a short phrase.
