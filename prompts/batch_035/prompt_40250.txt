quick bit of background: i've gotten deep into the pragmatic encroachment literature lately and honestly i'm tired of reading papers that argue for the view before telling me what the view is even supposed to accomplish. so before I take sides, I want the spec. write me the desiderata: what any adequate theory of whether practical stakes affect knowledge has to deliver, with the case or consideration behind each requirement, and flag which ones you suspect can't be jointly satisfied -- that tension is the part I actually care about.

a few things I'd want on the list, but I'm open to being told these are wrong or camp prejudices:

- it has to handle the standard bank-type stuff in both directions, and also cases where stakes LOWER the bar. my pet case (be honest if you think it's disanalogous): two inspectors with identical readings from the same testing protocol on whether a bridge is load-bearing; one is signing off on a footbridge, one on a highway bridge. same evidence, different practical stakes, and my intuition is the footbridge guy can sign off and the highway guy can't -- but maybe that's just competence-talk smuggling in?
- it has to explain why, when I learn about the stakes only AFTER hearing the knowledge attribution ("wait, it mattered?!"), the attribution doesn't feel like it shifted. if stakes are supposed to be doing semantic or epistemic work, this temporal asymmetry needs a story.
- it has to keep the subject's stakes and the attributor's stakes straight. contextualism and subject-sensitive invariantism both get to raise and lower thresholds, but they disagree about whose situation does it, and there's got to be some datum (disagreement cases? the "yesterday I knew, today the bank is the same but my mortgage is due" case?) that separates them.
- it can't collapse into skepticism via lottery/preface cases: if "practically relevant alternative" just means "any alternative it would be bad to ignore," everything is relevant everywhere and nobody knows anything.
- it has to say whether encroachment generalizes. does it apply to justified belief? to rational credence? if it only applies to knowledge, why is knowledge special -- and if it applies everywhere, is it even a thesis about knowledge anymore?
- it can't contradict the plausible thought that what evidence you have is one thing and what you should do with it is another. i.e., don't let "it's more practical to act on p" quietly turn into "the evidence for p got better." this seems like the one that quietly rules out a bunch of views, but I might be overvaluing it.

also please mark which requirements you think are non-negotiable vs. which are just what one camp happens to care about, and tell me which single requirement does the most exclusion work. no need to actually score contextualism vs. SSI vs. classical invariantism against the list, I can do that part myself, I just keep going in circles on the criteria.
