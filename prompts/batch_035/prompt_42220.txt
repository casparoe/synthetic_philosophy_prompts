every few months i end up arguing about anthropics and i finally want to write down where i actually stand, except i suspect the positions dont hang together. here's the list:

(1) the doomsday argument basically works — my being around the hundred-billionth human is real evidence there won't be trillions more of us. (2) i'm a thirder on sleeping beauty, that one feels obvious to me. (3) the presumptuous philosopher thing is just wrong — a theory that predicts a way bigger universe doesn't get to be thousands of times more credible just because i exist in it. (4) related, i reject the self-indication assumption: finding out that i exist at all is not evidence for worlds with more observers. (5) despite (1), i'm still a committed longtermist — x-risk work dominates the cause portfolio because of how big the future could be. i actually think (1) and (5) are fine together but people keep telling me they're inconsistent, so maybe deal with that too.

one wrinkle i can't shake: suppose we're on the verge of creating trillions of digital minds, and i count those as observers in my reference class. doesn't my own doomsday reasoning then go haywire — doom (or no digital-mind explosion) within decades at nearly certainty? that feels definately too strong to just accept.

go through the list and tell me which of these actually pull against each other and how badly, which tension is only apparent, and what the cheapest fix is. rank the tensions by severity rather than listing them all as equally bad.
