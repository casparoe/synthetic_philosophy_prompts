I'm taking a graduate seminar on modality, and for our final session on de re modality I've been asked to close with a short creative piece. Write a villanelle in the voice of Lewis's Humphrey — the possible election loser whose example is meant to sting counterpart theory — the night after conceding. One refrain should carry the transworld-identity thought (it is literally he who wins in the other world), the other the counterpart-theoretic reading (what wins there is only someone resembling him, so the comfort is not his to take). The form should do real work: the refrains return verbatim as the stanzas around them shift, so that the identity theorist hears one man persisting across worlds while the counterpart theorist hears numerically distinct occurrences of a mere counterpart relation — and the closing couplet, where both refrains finally stand together, should make visible why Humphrey reportedly felt the loss either way. Someone in the seminar who has read the debate should be able to tell which refrain is which and why the poem sides or refuses to side. Use British English throughout. Follow the villanelle with a note of no more than 120 words saying where the poem takes liberties with the theory (e.g., simplifying the counterpart relation to resemblance, ignoring ersatzist alternatives) and which reading a Lewisian would dispute.
