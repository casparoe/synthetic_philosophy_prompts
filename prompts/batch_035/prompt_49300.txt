I'm on the board of a mid-sized peace research institute — the kind of place that publishes reports, runs a small fellowship programme, and gets asked to co-sign declarations that other NGOs circulate. We've been invited to add our name to a public statement for an upcoming anniversary of some international treaty, and the centrepiece sentence of the statement is roughly: "measured per person, the world today is less violent than it has ever been, and the long-term trend bends away from war." Signing would be good publicity for us, and most of the board wants to. But we're not agreed on whether the sentence is even true, or honest to sign.

The fight, as it stands: our demographer says per-capita is the only fair measure, since a world with eight billion people can suffer twice the absolute number of war deaths as a world of four billion without being any more violent per life lived — comparing raw totals across eras with wildly different populations is just a category error, and she thinks the "per person" qualifier in the statement already handles this. Our historian disagrees, and not on the numbers: he says per-capita framing lets a growing population launder the totals — a world with more deaths but more people isn't obviously "less violent" in any sense that matters to the dead, and he doesn't want the institute blessing a statistic that smuggles in a moral judgement as a measurement choice. Meanwhile our resident statistician has been circulating a note arguing that if conflict deaths follow something like a fat-tailed distribution, then the absence of a great-power war for seventy-odd years is very weak evidence about any trend at all — the "decline" we've observed might just be the calm you'd expect even if nothing had changed about the underlying risk. And one of our fellows, who works on deprivation and sanctions, says the whole exercise is confused until you fix what counts as violence at all: if famines under blockade and deaths from political neglect count, the ledger looks quite different than if you only count battlefield deaths and homicides.

I'd like you to draft the decision memo I can circulate before our vote next month. It should recommend one of three options — sign as drafted, sign with amended language, or decline — with actual reasoning the board can act on, not a survey of everyone's position. It needs to engage seriously with the strongest point on whichever side ends up losing, not just wave it off, and it should take a position (with reasons) on whether there is a genuine fact of the matter about whether the world is becoming less violent, or whether it's all relative to a choice of measure — and if it's relative, whether there's some phrasing we could honestly sign that doesn't commit us to a contested measurment convention. Please keep it under 800 words and written so a non-specialist trustee could follow it.
