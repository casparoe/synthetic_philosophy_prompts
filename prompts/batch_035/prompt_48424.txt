My friend has this habit of pronouncing on whether people are free or not, and her track record so far: a slave whose owner never orders her around and lets her do whatever she likes, but could sell her on any given afternoon if the mood struck — not free. A shopkeeper living under a dictator who has never once touched the shops but can shut any of them down whenever he feels like it — not free. A citizen whose only laws are ones she voted for herself, banning just assault and theft, stuff she'd never dream of doing anyway — free. And a guy who's scared of flying and so never leaves the country — free, she says, since nothing is stopping him but himself. Anyway, she's coming to dinner and I get to bring the cases. One is my uncle, who after a stroke can't travel, even though noone and no law would stop him and he wants to badly. Another is a woman whose father could legally forbid her anything he pleased, but adores her and never has. The last is a guy shipwrecked on an island he can't leave, and he very much wants to. Do you think she'll call each of them free? Honestly I care less about the answers than about the rule she seems to be running, the other rules that would fit her record just as well, and which of my three cases would do the best job of splitting those rules apart.
