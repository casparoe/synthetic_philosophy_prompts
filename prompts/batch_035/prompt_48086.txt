I'm putting together teaching material for an intro philosophy of science course, and I want a thought experiment about the underdetermination of theory by evidence that doesn't lean on any of the stock examples. Build me a case with two rival theories that agree on every possible observation — actual, future, and merely possible — where both are internally coherent and, crucially, neither is just a notational rebranding of the other. Constraints: no evil demons, no brains in vats, no interpretations of quantum mechanics, and none of those cheap tricks where theory T' is just T plus a rider saying nothing observable ever differs — the empirical equivalence has to fall out of the theories' own content, not from a bolted-on clause. Ground it in an everyday domain like cooking, gardening, bicycle repair, or a sport, where the underdetermination arises naturally rather than being pasted on.

Then do the walkthrough: (a) give a short argument — a mapping or derivation sketch, not a bare assertion — that the two theories really can't be pulled apart observationally, even in the limit of ideal evidence; (b) make the usual tie-breakers split instead of aligning, i.e. one theory should win on simplicity while the other wins on conservatism, or one unifies better while the other possts fewer kinds of stuff — your pick, but they have to genuinely conflict; and (c) say which feature of the case is doing the work if you come down on one side as rationally preferable, what happens if you delete that feature, and how a constructive empiricist would react to the whole exercise (do they even have to choose between the two theories?).

The point of the case is to show first-years that underdetermination doesn't need exotic physics to show up in real inquiry, so a case that stipulates its own conclusion rather than earning it from the theories' contents counts as cheating. If you can't meet every spec exactly, tell me which one you had to bend and why. Answer in British English please, and keep the scenario itself to a paragraph or so before the walkthrough.
