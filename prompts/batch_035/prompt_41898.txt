Trying to figure out what I actually think about which bits of culture spread for our benefit vs for their own, going by my gut on cases. Chain letters that threaten bad luck if you don't forward them: obviously spreading for their own sake. Literacy: spreads because it's insanely useful to the people who pick it up. The "we only use 10% of our brains" thing: spreads because it's juicy, not because it's true. Knocking on wood: persists because it's cheap and sticky, not because it works. Earworm pop songs: mostly self-propagation, not merit. Tipping in restaurants: nothing legally forces it, yet it persists everywhere, and I say that one's kept around because it genuinely smooths social interaction. Religious commandments to have lots of kids and raise them in the faith: I keep flipflopping — feels like the religion is doing some of its own epidemiology there, but the comfort believers get is real too. Here's my problem though: the "honey never spoils" fact is true AND useful AND super sticky, and I want to put it on the literacy side, but honestly most people who repeat it do so because it's fun to repeat, not because it's done anything for them. Is there a clean principle here (something like selection on host benefit vs selection on transmissibilty) that captures my verdicts, which ones would it make me give up, and what does it say about a new case I'm unsure of — a mnemonic so catchy that students remember it for life even after they've forgotten what it was for?
