I am a novelist, and I have just begun a book whose spine is a question I would rather not fumble: what marriage is actually for. The plot follows a couple married forty years in a small town, their son and his husband in the middle of adopting, and a stable triad among the son's friends, so the novel will end up dramatizing commitments I have so far held without ever auditing them together. Before I commit them to fiction, I want to know whether they hold as a set.

Here they are. (1) Marriage is not merely a contract between two people but a distinctive public institution, and its institutional point — the reason a society maintains it at all — is the stabilization of a household into which children may arrive. (2) Legal recognition of same-sex marriage was not merely permissible but overdue, and I would defend it without hedging. (3) I do not think the state should recognize marriages among more than two people; I always found the old rhetoric linking same-sex marriage to plural marriage dishonest, and I have never felt the least temptation to revise (3). (4) The state's marriage law should ask no one, gay or straight, whether they intend to have children or are able to; no fertility requirement, no child-rearing intentions test, for anyone. (5) Adultery is a distinctive wrong inside marriage — not merely a broken promise but a violation of the goods the institution exists to protect — and yet I think the state should be largely indifferent to marital conduct in divorce law. (6) Marriage's meaning is partly constituted by its history, so that what marriage is and who may enter it is never fully up to a single generation to decide afresh; but I deny that this fact is, by itself, any reason against changing it.

Audit these for me. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and in each case what would have to give and what the revision would cost? Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally grave, and tell me which single change would clean up the most. Where you must lean on a substantive position rather than a settled one — for instance, on what kind of wrong the exclusion of same-sex couples was, or on whether that kind of argument transfers beyond two — please mark it as contested and say where people who actually work on these questions disagree, as distinct from where the public debate misdescribes their disagreement. I would rather know where the ground is soft than be given a confident floor to build my novel on.
