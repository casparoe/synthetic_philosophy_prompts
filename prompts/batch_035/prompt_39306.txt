I am compiling a diagnostic quiz on population axiology for a philosophy workshop whose participants range from PhD students to well-read laypeople. The quiz's purpose is to sort respondents by which population-ethical position they actually hold on the question of what makes one population better than another, rather than by which position they find rhetorically attractive, so no question may use the position labels themselves. Please write it with the following specifications.

First, eight to ten diagnostic questions. Each question should present a concrete choice between two described populations -- for example, a small population whose members live extremely good lives versus a vastly larger population whose members live lives that are only barely worth living, or a population to which extra people with good lives have been added without any change to the existing members' welfare -- and ask which is better (or whether they are equally good, or incomparable). The cases must be answerable purely by comparing the described worlds; they must not presuppose any theoretical vocabulary. The set of cases should between them force every respondent onto one side of each of the following fault lines: whether adding welfare-positive lives to an unchanged population is an improvement; whether total or average welfare is what tracks betterness; whether a life of very low but positive quality can, in sufficient numbers, outweigh a smaller population of extremely high quality; whether quality below some threshold cannot be compensated by quantity; and whether claims about 'making people better off' restrict comparisons to populations containing the same people.

Second, a scoring key mapping patterns of answers onto named positions, which must include at least: the total utilitarian view (which accepts the Repugnant Conclusion), average utilitarianism, critical-level views, person-affecting views that restrict what counts as benefiting or harming, lexical or perfectionist threshold views, and variable-value views that let the importance of additional lives diminish with population size. The key should explain, for each position, which answer pattern reveals it and which single question is the cleanest giveaway.

Third, the key should be built from the logical space rather than from the list of names: set out the questions and the combinations of answers they permit first, then locate the actual positions within that space, and note which combinations are unoccupied. For each unoccupied combination, say briefly whether you think nobody holds it because it is incoherent, because it has unattractive implications people refuse to spell out, or simply because no one has explored it. If two of the required positions cannot genuinely be separated by your questions -- for instance if the cases do not distinguish them -- say so explicitly rather than pretending the instrument is sharper than it is.

Fourth, please write the entire quiz and key in British English.
