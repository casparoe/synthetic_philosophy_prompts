I teach a graduate seminar unit on future contingents and want a decision tree that takes a student from an initial commitment to a named position. Entry point: the sentence "There will be a sea battle tomorrow." Build the tree from the logical space rather than a list of names: first the diagnostic questions and the combinations of answers they permit, then locate the positions in that space — Łukasiewicz's three-valued semantics, supervaluationism, the open-future branching view, Ockhamism with its privileged actual future, the Peircean "as-of-now" view, and straight bivalent fatalism. Every node must be a question a student could answer without knowing which leaf it leads to; in particular, include a node on the tautology test ("Either there will be a sea battle tomorrow or there won't" — true today, false today, or neither?) and a node on whether one possible future is already privileged as the way things will go. Note any unoccupied combinations and say briefly why nobody is there (incoherent, unattractive, or merely unexplored), and flag leaves the tree cannot cleanly separate — above all, say whether it can pull apart the open-future and supervaluationist leaves, and where Aristotle's own view in De Interpretatione 9 would sit, given that interpreters genuinely disagree about which leaf, if any, is his.
