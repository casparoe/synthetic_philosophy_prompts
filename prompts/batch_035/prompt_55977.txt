So I've held this view for a while: reductionism about testimonial justification — the idea that when someone tells you something and you end up justified in believing it, that justification isn't anything new. It's built out of materials you already had: your inductive track record with this speaker or speakers in general, your perceptual data, background knowledge. Testimony is like a pipe, not a spring. The main reason I like it is that it keeps the epistemolgy tidy — one fewer basic source of justification that needs its own story.

Lately tho I've been worried the view has started circling the drain, and I'd rather have a real map of its exposure than a vibe. So: what would count against it?

Things I specifically want you to think through:

- The track record itself. The obvious reduction runs an induction on past testimony that panned out — but nearly everything I know about my own past testimonial successes came in through testimony. My memory of being told X, my belief that this newspaper is generally reliable, all of it went through the pipe. Does that make the reduction circular? And if so, is it the fatal kind of circularity or the allegedly benign kind (the way perception is sometimes said to validate itself)? Where exactly is the line between those two, and does the reductionist actually get to stand on the benign side?

- The toddler problem. Little kids seem to pick up alot from testimony — word meanings especially — before they could possibly have anything like an inductive base about speaker reliability. If reductionism says they therefore have no testimonial justification, is that a bullet worth biting, or one of those bullets that ends up going through the shoe into the foot?

- Cases where the testimony outruns everything else. A mathematician tells me a proof is sound and I can't check a line of it; a radiologist reads my scan. The anti-reductionist says these beliefs are justified and there's nothing upstream for the justification to reduce to. The reductionist says it reduces to the expert's track record — but my evidence of that track record is itself testimonial. Is there any way to state the reduction so it doesn't just get pushed back one level every time you ask? If not, does the view ever actually bottom out anywhere?

- Concessions from the home team. I don't want only the standard anti-reductionist hits. Which objections do committed reductionists themselves admit count against the view (even if not fatally), and which do they wave off as missing the point? Draw that line for me.

Please rank everything: fatal if it works, serious, merely awkward. And be honest about whether the view has been qualified into unfalsifiability — for instance "local" reductions that only ever get applied where there's already independent evidence, or fallback appeals to default entitlements that start looking like anti-reductionism with the serial numbers filed off. If that's happened, is it a problem for a view of this kind, or is it just how epistemology goes? For what it's worth, the thing that would move me most is a clean described case of testimonial justification with literally no non-testimonial upstream. If you think no such case can even be coherently described, say so and tell me why — that would be its own kind of answer.
