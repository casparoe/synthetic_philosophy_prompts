I run a small blog mostly read by policy people and lawyers who like philosophy as a hobby, and I've been sitting on this idea for a post series on official state apologies — think parliamentary motions apologizing for slavery, church-burnings, internment of ethnic minorities during a war, forced sterilization programs, that kind of thing. Instead of writing another essay about whether Japan should apologize for X or Britain for Y, I want to do one of those "which position do you actually hold?" quizzes, because my comment section constantly talks past each other: someone says an apology is meaningless unless it comes with money, someone else says the money is the only meaningless part because the gesture is what carries the moral weight, and a third person thinks the whole thing is a category error since the state apologizing is like a hammer apologizing.

So: please write me the quiz. Eight to ten questions, each one a concrete case with two or three answer options, and then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto positions. The cases should be hypothetical or thinly-fictionalized (a president apologizing for a massacre 80 years ago where all victims and perpetrators are dead; a government apologizing for something a previous regime did after a revolution that repudiated everything it stood for; an apology offered while the wrongful policy is arguably still ongoing, like colonial-era land seizures never reversed; an apology to a minority where some members of the minority say they don't want it; an apology that is explicitly worded as regret-about-what-happened rather than an admission of fault, and whether that dodge actually changes the thing's moral character). I don't want any question that just asks "do you think the state is a moral agent?" — that's asking people to pick a label, and the whole point is that my readers will discover they hold a weird combination of views once they're forced to answer about cases.

A few specifics on the scoring key. I'd like the underlying tree to be built from the load-bearing questions rather than from the named positions: something like (a) does the apologizing entity need to be the *same* entity that did the wrong, and what does "same" even mean across regime change, (b) does an apology require someone wronged to receive it, (c) is the apology a speech act with specific felicity conditions or just an expression of sentiment, (d) can an apology be owed to people who weren't victims themselves but inherited the disadvantage. Then locate the actual positions in that space — the "states are persistent corporate agents that can genuinely owe things" view, the benefit-tracing view where the issue is just who profited and what flows from that, the narrative-identity view where the apology is about what kind of nation it is, and the deflationary view that these are political speech acts whose value is forward-looking (shaping norms, deterring repetition) and nothing deeper. Also, since my audience loves this stuff: flag any combinations of answers that no real position occupies, and say whether you think nobody's there because the combo is incoherent or just because no one's bothered to defend it. And please flag explicitly which pairs of positions the quiz genuinely cannot separate, so I can be honest with readers rather than pretending the instrument is sharper than it is. No need for citations or names of specific real-world apologies — hypothetical cases work better anyway, keeps the comments from derailing into one country's grievance politics. Maybe keep each answer option to a sentence or two so the quiz is actually completable on a phone.
