I sit on the steering committee of our national commission reviewing the law on sex work. Our current statute follows the Nordic model: selling sex is lawful, purchasing it is a criminal offence, and third-party facilitation such as brothel-keeping is criminal. Two factions have now emerged within the commission. The first proposes a hybrid: retain the offence of purchasing sex on the street, but license indoor establishments where purchase would be lawful, on something like the German or Dutch regulatory model — the argument being that this draws the trade indoors and away from residential areas while preserving deterrence at street level. The second wants full decriminalization on the New Zealand model, with the state withdrawing from the transaction altogether.

I must draft the recommendation paper the commissioners will vote on, and one thing troubles me that none of the position papers confronts. The Nordic model rests on a conceptual claim: that the transaction is inherently an abuse of the person selling, which is why the law criminalizes only the buyer, and the state refuses to treat the exchange as a market like any other. The hybrid, however, seems to abandon that claim indoors — a licensed brothel just is the state treating the exchange as a market like any other — while keeping it outdoors, which looks less like a principled position than a zoning preference. I can also see the rejoinder: the state already tolerates such asymmetries elsewhere (alcohol is governed differently by venue, without anyone supposing that drinking is wrong in a bar but wrong-in-principle in a park), and the hybrid can accordingly be defended as harm management rather than moral judgment.

Draft the recommendation memo. It must open with a concrete bottom line — endorse the hybrid, reject it in favour of full decriminalization, or retain the current model — followed by the reasoning, and it must include an honest treatment of the strongest argument for whichever option it rejects. Keep it under 650 words, and do not pad it with empirical figures; the vote will turn on the coherence of the positions, not on numbers none of us can independently verify.
