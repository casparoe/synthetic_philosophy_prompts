My wife and I are midway through IVF and facing a decision our clinic's counselor has not been much help with. Our seven-year-old daughter has a bone marrow failure condition that her doctors say can only be durably treated with a transplant from a closely matched donor, and neither we, nor any relative, nor the international registry has turned up a match. So we asked our fertility clinic to run tissue typing on our remaining viable embryos alongside the usual screening. Two are usable. Embryo A is a full match for our daughter; its screening panel, however, shows a substantially elevated genetic risk of type 1 diabetes — a serious, lifelong, though manageable condition. Embryo B carries no elevated risk on any of the screened conditions, but would not be a match. The doctors tell us that cord blood collected at A's birth would probably suffice for the transplant, with a marrow donation later in childhood as a backup possibility.

We disagree about what to do, and the disagreement has gotten sharper the longer we've held it. My wife's position is that we should transfer B. We owe the child we bring into the world the best life we can give them, and knowingly transferring an embryo carrying an elevated disease risk, when a healthier one is sitting right there, strikes her as a dereliction of that duty. She is also troubled by the motivation itself: having a child partly in order to harvest tissue for her sibling feels to her like treating the second child as a supply line, and she worries it will shadow how we regard her, and how she regards herself, for life.

My position is that I cannot make my wife's objection work. Consider the child A would become: call her Alice. If we transfer B, Alice never exists — some other child, call him Ben, does. So the diabetes risk cannot be a harm we inflict on Alice, since the alternative for her was never a healthier life but no life at all. If anything, Alice has reason to be grateful for the choice that brought her into existence, even burdened with an elevated risk. My wife says this reasoning proves far too much — that it would license discarding any embryo whenever another is marginally healthier, and that it treats the comparison between two possible children as though it were a comparison within one child's life. She also insists that even if no one is harmed, something can still be wrong with it, in something like the way there is something wrong with conceiving a child solely to have a live-in caregiver, even if the child is well loved and would not otherwise have existed.

We need help thinking this through rather than a verdict we can live with. Which of us has the better of the argument, and where exactly does the other's reasoning go wrong? In particular: does the fact that the alternative to a given child's existence is that child's nonexistence, rather than some other life, really dissolve the concern about knowingly choosing a riskier embryo — and if not, what does the concern amount to, and whom does it wrong? And does the donation purpose taint the child, or her relationship to us and to her sister, in a way that persists even if we love her fully and she endorses the arrangement once she is old enough to understand it? Please give us the strongest version of each side rather than a compromise, and tell us plainly what you would counsel us to do if we take each consideration seriously.
