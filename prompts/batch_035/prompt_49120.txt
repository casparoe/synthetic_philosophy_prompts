I am a novelist three hundred pages into a counterfactual work: a Europe in which movable type was never invented, where everything my characters want, fear, and pray for is recognizably ours, but the machinery by which ideas travel has been subtracted. Plotting it has drawn me into a dispute I now cannot get out of, between two parties I will call, for my own convenience, the compounding side and the converging side. The compounding side — the economists of increasing returns and lock-in, who point to the famous surviving keyboard layout and to railway gauges and to how often the winning technology was not the best one — treats history as a heap of small accidents, each settling the conditions for the next, so that removing any of them unravels everything downstream. The converging side — most historians, and some philosophers of technology — holds that if not this inventor then another, if not this route then another: that the deep constraints (geography, demography, the economics of copying books by hand) would have produced a similar world on a similar schedule, and that the counterfactual imagination is a novelist's vice dressed up as an argument. Both sides have made their moves many times, everyone knows them by heart, and nobody moves. My working hypothesis, offered strictly as a hypothesis: the two sides are describing history at different grains, so that the phrase "the same outcome" names two different questions — the same tokens (this alphabet, this empire, this exact machine) versus the same types (cheap text, mass literacy, urbanization, something one would or would not recognize as the Reformation) — and each side wins the game it is playing while believing it has won the game the other is playing. Diagnose the disagreement for me: verbal or substantive; a hidden empirical question on which no attainable evidence could be decisive; a difference about what counts as an explanation rather than a coincidence; or two temperaments that were never actually in contact. Support the diagnosis with what each side actually claims, and then tell me honestly what would count as progress — a distinction, a method, a kind of evidence — because I need to know, chapter by chapter, how much of the world I have built I am licensed to keep.
