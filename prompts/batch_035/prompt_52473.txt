Been keeping a list of little cases about when you cause something by NOT doing something, becuase I want to figure out if there's a principle behind my gut verdicts, and honestly right now I don't think there is one.

The list:

1. You ask me to water your plants while you're away, I say yes, I forget, they die. I caused them to die. Obviously.
2. Your other neighbor, who you never asked, didn't water them either, and easily could have. He didn't cause anything. Kind of a jerk move to just watch them die, but not a cause.
3. Now the awkward one: you asked BOTH of us, and either of us alone would've been enough. We both forget. Both causes? Half-causes each? Neither?? I want to say both, but I can't explain how that doesn't add up to two full causes.
4. A lifeguard on duty doesn't notice a swimmer drowning: caused. A random beachgoer who could've swum out but didn't: not caused. And note the counterfactuals are identical — had either acted, he'd have lived.
5. Same setup as 4, but the beachgoer is literally the only other person on the beach, it's shallow water he could've just waded into, and he didn't feel like it. I flip-flop on this constantly. "Let it happen" feels right, but "he caused it" keeps sneaking back in.
6. My grandfather "could have" watered the plants except he's been dead for ten years. Obviously not a cause — but again, counterfactual-wise nothing changes, had he watered them they'd live. So whatever rule kills this case has to be about more than the counterfactual.
7. I tell a third neighbor "don't bother, I've got it covered," she stays home because of that, and then I forget too. Feels different from case 1 — like I caused the death twice over, once by talking her out of it and once by forgetting. Is that two causes, one by commission one by omission, or just one messy cause?
8. Every person on earth "could have" watered your plants if they'd known and walked by. None of them are causes. So the cutoff is somewhere between "the one person you asked" and "everyone," and I can't locate it.

My tentative principle was something like: an omission only causes when the omitter had a duty (promised, on the job, etc.), and only duty-bearers are even candidates for causehood. But that forces case 5 to come out "not caused" no matter how lazy the beachgoer was, which I'm not sure I can live with, and it's silent on case 3.

So: is there a principle that gets all of these, or close? What does it say about case 5, the one I keep going back and forth on? What would it predict for cases I haven't listed — say, a doctor who's the only one who could treat a patient but is off duty at a different hospital across town, or a security guard whose whole job is watching one door, who steps out for a smoke right when someone walks through it? And be honest: which of my verdicts does the best principle just have to overturn?
