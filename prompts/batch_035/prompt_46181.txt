So my phil of science reading group has spent basically the whole year on verisimilitude and we've hit a wall I don't fully understand. Quick setup so you know where we're stuck: after Miller and Tichý showed in the mid-70s that Popper's old definition of truthlikeness orders false theories backwards on some comparisons (a strong false theory ending up farther from the truth than a weaker false one it entails), the field split. One camp — content people, roughly following Miller himself — says get closer to the truth means having more true content and less false content, full stop, and similarity between claims or between states of the world is just a mirage that depends on how you translate between languages. The other camp — likeness people, Oddie and Niiniluoto and that crowd — says no, closeness to the truth is a similarity relation between the actual state of affairs and the states a theory allows, so you measure distance in a space of possible worlds or states, and Popper's failure was just ignoring that dimension.

We can state both sides' formal machinery fine. The problem is that when we push on it, nothing seems to move, and I've started to suspect the whole fight is weirder than a normal substantive dispute. Here's the thing that got me to write this: I gather that at some point people noticed the two approaches can be translated into each other — you can reconstruct the content ordering as a likeness ordering over a suitably chosen space and vice versa — and the upshot some people drew was that there's no real disagreement at all, just two formalisms with different primitive vocabularies. If that's right, then what have Oddie and Miller et al. been disagreeing about for decades? But when I brought this up in the group, the likeness person said no, the translation trick just shows each framework can mimic the other's ordering, and the real question is which ordering is the right one, which the translation doesn't touch. And then we went around the same circle again.

So what I want from you is a diagnosis, not a survey. Is this a verbal dispute about the phrase 'closer to the truth'? Is it a substantive disagreement about the extension of the concept, where both sides agree on the formal facts and split on which ordering fixes what we meant all along (in which case it's arguably conceptual-analysis-all-the-way-down and I want to know whether that makes it unresolvable by argument)? Or are the two camps secretly answering different questions — one asking what makes a theory better for the purposes of Popperian acceptance and risk-taking, the other asking what makes theoretical progress a kind of approximation — and the single label 'verisimilitude' is gluing two projects together that should never have been in one seminar? Or something else entirely, like a fight about what a philosophical analysis owes its users: the content people seem to want a definition that's language-invariant and usable blind, while the likeness people are happy to make truthlikeness depend on which features of the world you care about, and maybe that tolerance is the actual fault line.

For whatever it's worth, my working hypothesis is that it's the last one — a disagreement about whether truthlikeness is allowed to be interest-relative — but I hold it loosely, so tell me if the evidence points somewhere else. And given your diagnosis, say what would count as progress here: a new formal result, a distinction nobody's drawn yet, or an admission that the field settled into a stable division of labor years ago and our reading group is just late to noticing. It would also help if you could say whether the stalled-ness is a sign the question is ill-posed or just that it's hard, because honestly I can't tell those apart anymore and its affecting how I plan the rest of the group's year.
