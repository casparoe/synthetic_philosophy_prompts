Trying to work out what I actually believe about virtual objects and virtual harm by just going with my gut on cases, and honestly my gut is not being consistant. My friend's MMO account got hacked and the guy sold his rare mount and kept the gold -- that feels like actual theft to me, like something you could report to the police, even though the mount still exists in the game's database and my friend can still look at pictures of it. But when my nephew's "friend" deleted his Minecraft world as a joke, I told him it was mean but not a crime, not really, even though he'd poured more hours into that world than my friend ever did into that mount. Spending real money on a cosmetic skin in some game seems fine, slightly silly but fine. A veteran scamming a rare item off a new player with a rigged trade feels scummy and ban-worthy, but somehow in a different bucket than the hacker, though I can't say why. When griefers crashed an in-game funeral that a guild held for a player who actually died, that reads to me as nearly desecrating a memorial -- monstrous, not just rude -- even though on paper they only killed some pixels that would respawn. And yet I don't think the dead guy's character is real in any sense, and if the guild "inherited" his stuff I'm not sure that's a real inheritance. Also, and this is embarrassing, I got genuinely upset when my old Pokemon save file corrupted, while still maintaining that "losing" Pokemon isn't losing anything.

The principle I keep reaching for is something like: the objects aren't real, but the time and experiences are, so harm tracks irreplaceable wasted time, not lost things. Does that survive contact with my cases? The funeral one doesn't seem to be about time at all, and the mount one seems to be at least partly about the thing. So -- is there a principle that captures most or all of my verdicts? If you have to overturn some to get a clean one, tell me which and why those are the right ones to drop. And then tell me what your principle says about the case I keep flip-flopping on: a server rollback wipes a week of everyone's progress. Nobody took anything from anybody, no one chose anything, and yet I feel the players were harmed and I can't say of what.
