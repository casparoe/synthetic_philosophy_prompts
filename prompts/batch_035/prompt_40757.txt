Two people in my consciousness reading group have been going at it for a year now: one's a committed higher-order theorist (a state is phenomenally conscious only if there's some higher-order representation of it), the other's a committed first-order theorist (the feel of an experience is fixed entirely by what the first-order state represents and does — no monitoring needed on top). Write the joint statement they should have produced instead of another shouting match. I want: the claims both could sign about what any theory of phenomenal consciousness owes us, about how reliable introspective report is, and about cases like blindsight and subliminal priming; the exact disagreements, each phrased so its own side would accept the wording of its own view; the cruxes — I suspect one is whether there can be a phenomenal difference that makes no difference to anything reportable or cognitively accessible, and another is what confabulation-style introspective mistakes actually show about the machinery; and for each crux, a piece of evidence or an argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. To make the crux section concrete, have them consider a hypothetical "monitor-blocker": a drug that shuts down all higher-order monitoring while first-order processing runs completely normally — would there still be something it's like, and how could anyone ever check? Format it as a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. And if you conclude that part of their dispute is purely verbal (say, over what "phenomenal character" is even supposed to mean), say which part and why.
