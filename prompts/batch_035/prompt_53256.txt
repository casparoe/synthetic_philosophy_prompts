i'm a phd student in philosophy of mind (dissertation on the metaphysics of consciousness measures, fun times) and i keep running into the same analogy in talks and pop-sci stuff about integrated information theory: Φ is the new temperature. the story goes something like — temperature used to be a spooky folk feeling of hot and cold, then thermodynamics plus statistical mechanics showed it's just mean kinetic energy, a real measurable quantity with a proper science behind it. consciousness is our spooky folk concept of "there being something it's like," and integrated information is going to do for it what kinetic energy did for temperature: turn a mystery into a quantity you can in principle compute for any physical system. my office mate is fully sold and says once you accept the analogy the weird results of IIT stop being weird — the photodiode having a tiny bit of experience, the densely connected grid having more than a feedforward net with identical input-output behavior, the simulated brain on your laptop having none — because nobody thinks the *number* "temperature" is spooky once you have the microphysics.

my problem is the analogy feels like it's doing more work than it's licensed to. temperature got reduced from the bottom up: the macro-variable was derived out of the microtheory, with bridge laws, and the whole thing works off things like the thermodynamic limit. IIT arguably runs the other direction — it starts from phenomenological axioms (intrinsic existence, composition, information, exclusion) and tells you what the physical substrate must be like wherever there is consciousness. also temperature is multiply realizable and substrate-independent in a way Φ famously is not — the exclusion postulate plus the claim that only the physical substrate and not the simulation carries experience seems like exactly the kind of feature the temperature story never had (a simulated gas doesn't make your computer hot either, but nobody takes that as deep). and nobody had a "hard problem of temperature" — there was no first-person datum of what-it's-like-to-be-warm left dangling after the reduction, whereas that's supposedly the whole explanandum here.

so audit the analogy for me. lay out what's supposed to map onto what — folk feeling of warmth, the thermometer, mean kinetic energy, the reduction itself, whatever else is in the neighborhood — then where the mapping breaks, and crucially which of the breaks actually matter for the conclusion my office mate wants to ride to (consciousness is a measurable natural quantity, the mystery dissolves, the panpsychist-seeming results are just the price of admission like a tiny bit of temperature in everything... wait, that last bit is exactly the kind of thing i mean, does the analogy even support it?). and tell me if there's a better one nearby — entropy, mass, solidity — that would carry the load the temperature analogy can't, or whether the whole "consciousness is a quantity like other quantities" framing smuggles in something temperature never had to face. don't just tell me IIT has critics, i know; i want to know what the analogy itself can and can't carry.
