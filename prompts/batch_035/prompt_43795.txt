I'd split each place's chances evenly between its two spots, so my full picture is five spots: 50 / 12.5 / 12.5 / 12.5 / 12.5.

Today I got two messages. The gym guy said, if you left them here, it's the lockers and not the stretching floor — about 4 to 1. The barista said basically the same thing: if they're at the café, counter rather than table, also about 4 to 1. Neither of them has actually looked, they're just going off how these things usually go. The gym guy knows my habits pretty well, the barista barely knows me, if that matters.

Marcus says that's naive — his move is to treat each message as if I'd been told the actual likely outcome: cross out the stretching floor and the table, pretend I'd learned "lockers and counter," and renormalise. That takes the flat from 50% up to two-thirds, which he says is just how updating works — the spots all compete, so boosting some of them has to squeeze the rest. Priya says evidence about the café reaching into my kitchen is spooky action at a distance, and that if a conditional about the gym can change how likely the keys are to be in my flat, something has gone wrong with the maths. Marcus says probabilities aren't little boxes that get to opt out of new evidence.
