Take Moore's proof of an external world — the lecture in which he stands up, holds out his hands, and takes the existence of his hands to entail that external things exist — together with the standard responses: the complaint that he hasn't ruled out dreaming, his own reply that he knows his premises with more certainty than any premise in the sceptic's argument, and the more recent objection that whatever warrant he has for the hand-premise fails to transmit to the conclusion. Restate this whole dispute in the vocabulary I actually used at the lab — calibration chains, systematic versus statistical uncertainty, control samples, blind analyses, discovery thresholds, the distinction between a detector reading and a claim about what produced it — as faithfully as you can, and flag every place where you had to fudge. Then give me the residue report: which parts of the philosophers' argument have no counterpart in how we handled trust in instruments, and whether that residue shows my vocabulary is missing something or that the dispute was less substantive than it sounded. I have a suspicion that the dreaming objection functions like an untestable systematic uncertainty that we learned to live with rather than answer, and that the transmission-failure objection is the analogue of what a colleague meant when he said a discovery claim made from a calibrated instrument still isn't the same thing as knowing the instrument was pointed at what we thought it was pointed at — but tell me honestly where those analogies break. Lead with the residue, keep the whole thing under 800 words, and do not soften the translation to flatter either the physicists or the philosophers.
