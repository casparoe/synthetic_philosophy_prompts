ok so I'm a longtime lurker on LessWrong and adjacent forums, and for the last few weeks I've been stuck in a comment thread about Kim's causal exclusion argument. Quick setup so we're on the same page, no need to lecture me on it: physicalism gives you causal closure (every physical event that has a cause has a sufficient physical cause), we all want to say mental events cause physical stuff (my deciding to wave causes the waving), and nobody accepts rampant systematic overdetermination. Kim's conclusion: the physical cause does the whole job, so the mental cause either gets reduced into it or excluded outright. Fine, I know the moves. The problem is that everyone in the thread, on both sides, keeps just asserting their conclusion — "the brain does it, period" vs "but the decision is what mattered!" — and no one has produced a case that actually isolates the variable. I've given the map-is-not-the-territory speech myself on this very forum, more than once, which is exactly why I'm suspicious of it here: it can't be doing the work, it has to be shown doing the work.

So here's what I want you to build me, to specifications:

A thought experiment where, holding the complete physical state fixed at the moment of action, the mental-level description still comes out as the better cause of the behavior — "better" in the sense a Yablo-style proportionality constraint gives you: a cause shouldn't be too coarse or too fine, it should include exactly the features that mattered for the effect and no more. The trick I want built in: the physical realizer of the mental state must contain a bunch of detail that is causally idle with respect to the effect — incidental neural background, activity that would've been going on regardless, stuff like that — so that the neural event, as physically described, is a strictly worse cause than the mental event it realizes. And crucially the verdict has to be generated, not stipulated: someone who applies Kim's argument to the case straight should first feel the pull of exclusion, and then the proportionality comparison should make the exclusion verdict look wrong. A case that just declares the mental the winner is worthless to me and will get eaten alive in the thread.

Constraints: realistic setting, something like an office or a kitchen or a commute, definately no zombies, no psychokinesis, no "then a miracle occurs," and no lab equipment doing implausible things. The scenario itself should be one paragraph. After that:

1. Show the work: name the mental cause, name its physical realizer, say which parts of the realizer are idle with respect to the effect, and run the proportionality comparison in both directions so it's visible that the mental description wins and the neural description loses.
2. Tell me which single feature of the case is doing the work, and what happens if I delete it — does the case collapse back into a bog-standard exclusion case where Kim just wins?
3. Give me Kim's best comeback — my guess is it's something like proportionality being a pragmatic/epistemic nicety about description, while causation itself is an all-or-nothing matter at the fundamental level — and then tell me honestly whether that reply actually saves exclusion or just changes the subject. I'd rather hear "the comeback works and your case is dead" than get a rigged ruling.

I'm not asking you to settle the mind-body problem in a forum comment, obviously. I want a case I can drop into the thread that forces people to argue about the right thing for once.
