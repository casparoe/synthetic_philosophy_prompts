Every year me and two friends from college, Maya and Dev, split a charity donation, and this year the planning dinner turned into a three hour philosphy fight. I got nothing resolved and honestly couldn't follow all of it, so I'm hoping you can referee. (Please answer in American English.)

The setup: Maya had done a lot of reading after her grandmother's last months in hospice, and she'd come out of it a negative utilitarian of a particular kind. Her view, as she put it, is that ordinary pains and pleasures trade off against each other normally — a stubbed toe can be outweighed by a good dinner, fine. But there's a threshold. Certain sufferings, the truly awful ones, are such that no amount of happiness, however large, no matter how many trillions of blissful lives, can ever compesate for even one instance of them. She said watching her grandmother made this obvious to her: nothing anyone could have poured into the other side of the scale meant anything at that point.

Dev opened with the classic move. He said if suffering dominates like that, then her view endorses a button that painlessly ends all life instantly — no more suffering ever, no one feels a thing, problem solved. He said any theory that even nudges you toward pressing that button is broken, so hers is broken.

Maya's response was that the threshold is exactly what blocks the button. On her view the below-threshold sufferings dominate, sure, but above the threshold ordinary welfare still counts — so pressing the button means throwing away all future lives of ordinary happiness for the sake of guaranteeing zero agony, and that's a real cost her view recognizes. She accused him of running the objection against pure negative utilitarianism and pretending it hit her version.

Dev's second move was the cliff problem. He said her threshold is a metaphysical cliff, not just a bookkeeping line: a migraine one tiny increment worse than the threshold is lexically worse than a billion years of bliss, while a migraine one tiny increment milder can be outweighed by an extra scoop of ice cream. So one barely-detectable increment of headache severity flips the entire value of the universe. He said no sane account of value has discontinuities that violent, and if she softens the threshold into a slope she's just back to normal utilitarianism wearing a costume.

Maya answered that lots of real things have fuzzy thresholds without being fake: heaps, adulthood, brain death. We manage. She said demanding a sharp line before her view counts is like refusing to believe in baldness until someone tells you the exact hair count.

Dev's final word was that those are all practical and legal thresholds, drawn for convenience where the underlying facts are continuous — nobody claims the hair count line is a joint in nature. But hers is supposed to be a fact about how value actually stacks up, and a fact like that can't lean on the same "we manage" shrug.

Then they both looked at me, and I paid for dessert. Can you go through the exchange move by move and say who's right about what? Not just a winner overall — where each of them is solid, where they're sliding something past the other, and what (if anything) both of them are missing about the whole framing. I did notice neither of them ever said a word about whether the original donation should just be split, but maybe that's my problem.
