I'm on the board of a small nonprofit and we're redoing how we pay people, and the last meeting turned into a weird philosophy fight. One board member, Dana, has been reading about those capuchin monkey experiments where the monkeys freak out when they get a cucumber and their neighbor gets a grape. Her take: our feeling that unequal pay is wrong is just the same evolved inequity-aversion circuitry firing off, it's a bias like any other, so we're free to set it aside and just pay people whatever the market says their labor is worth — if interns will work for free, nothing's wrong with that. Another member, Priya, countered with what she clearly thought was a knockdown arguement: if even monkeys have this reaction, then fairness must be tracking something real in the world, it's not just a human convention, so we're actually obligated to equalize everyone's pay. Everyone left treating it like science vs science, and now I have to write the decision memo.

I feel like both of them are doing something illegitimate with the exact same fact, but I cant put my finger on what. Is there actually any way to get from "our moral intuitions evolved and monkeys have them too" to a conclusion about what we ought to do, in either direction? And if there isn't, what does that do to Dana's move specifically — she'd presumably say that if the fairness intuition is just bias, ignoring it is the rational thing to do, and honestly I can't immediately see what's wrong with saying that. Help me figure out what to put in the memo and how to explain to both of them where their arguments are actually landing.
