I'm in a philosophy of mind reading group and next month it's my turn to bring the discussion case, and I'm tired of everyone reaching for the China brain or Block's giant lookup table over and over. So here's the assignment I've set for myself, and I want you to actually execute it: build a thought experiment that attacks functionalism *through* multiple realizability itself. The target verdict is that a serious functionalist is committed to attributing mental states to the system, but the way the system happens to be realized is what makes that attribution feel absurd — and the absurdity can't lean on any of the usual crutches.

Constraints: no lookup tables, no nation of humans passing messages around, no ant colonies, no demons, and definitely no "it just seems wierd that silicon could think" substrate chauvinism, since functionalism was invented specifically to beat that. Present-day-ish technology only, and the scenario itself should be one paragraph. Also the case has to *earn* its verdict from the theory — no stipulating "and of course there's no mind in there"; the description has to be complete enough that the functionalist analysis generates the attribution on its own.

My friends already burned two attempts and I want you to avoid their moves. One proposed a mind realized by millions of internet-connected smart thermostats, each playing the role of a single neuron — I think that's just the China brain in a new outfit, so it's out. The other proposed stretching the realization over geological time, each state transition in the causal network happening a million years after the last one, so the "organism" is only a mind relative to a God's-eye fast-forward. I actually suspect that one fails too, but for a reason I find interesting: the functionalist can reply that functional organization is always individuated at some temporal grain, that nothing in the theory fixes which grain is mandatory, and so the slow realization is a verbal dispute rather than a counterexample. If you agree both attempts fail, your case should be one where that "wrong grain / it's merely slow" reply is *not* available — that's the part my friends haven't managed.

After the scenario, do four things: (1) show the functionalist derivation — which functional states the system has and why the theory is on the hook for the mental ones; (2) name precisely which feature of the realization is doing the anti-functionalist work, not just gesture at the whole setup; (3) say what happens when you delete that feature — does the case collapse back into a boring, harmless MR case (different substrate, same mind, all fine)? and (4) give the best functionalist reply and tell me whether it's a principled extension of the theory or an ad hoc patch, since that's what I actually want to fight about in the group. Keep the whole answer under 600 words — I get five minutes to present and I don't want to overrun. And if you think my constraints can't be jointly satisfied and one of them has to give, say so and say which, because honestly that would also be a fun thing to bring to the group.
