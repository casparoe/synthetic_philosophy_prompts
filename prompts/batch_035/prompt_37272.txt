Been reading consciousness content for years -- rationalist blogs, Dennett, the whole memeplex -- and somewhere along the way I became a true believer in the multiple drafts thing, specifically the timing claim: in the color phi and cutaneous rabbit setups, asking whether the subject really had the experience at t1 or whether it got revised later is a question with no answer, like the Orwell vs. Stalinesque bit where, since the subject's later reports are identical either way, you can't distinguish the regime that rewrites the records from the one that edited the show. Timing is just more content in the stream; there's no extra fact about when content "enters consciousness," because there's no theater for it to enter.

So what would count against this? Not the hard problem, I've heard that one to death. I want the actual defeaters, ranked from fatal to merely awkward: what empirical result would have to come out (from Libet-style timing work or whats succeeded it) for me to give this up; which cases the view struggles to even describe; and whether it's been qualified into unfalsifiability, because the standard move nowadays is "any apparent timing fact is just more drafts," and honestly I can't tell if that's a deep insight or a dodge. Which of these would a committed multiple-drafts person concede as counting against them, which would they wave off, and if the thing really is insulated from every observation, is that fatal or actually fine for a claim of this type?
