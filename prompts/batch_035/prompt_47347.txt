A classmate of mine in a graduate seminar on rational choice has spent the term handing down verdicts on toy societies, and next week the seminar takes up a new case I'd like your prediction on. The setup she accepts: everyone is a von Neumann–Morgenstern expected-utility maximizer, the individual utility numbers are cardinally comparable just as given, and nothing but those numbers counts socially. Her track record, all of it in fixed populations: (1) she ranks risky and certain social prospects purely by the unweighted sum of utilities — she takes a fifty-fifty lottery between (12, 12) and (0, 0) over a certain (4, 4), and pronounces (10, 0) and (5, 5) exactly equally good, with no bonus for equality; (2) she rates a certain (4, 0) and a fair coin between (4, 0) and (0, 4) as equally good as well — the individuals' ex ante prospects match, so the coin buys nothing; (3) when we relabel who is who, her verdicts flip accordingly, and she never lets one person's utilities count for more than another's even where Pareto considerations alone would leave the weights open; (4) her occasional three-person cases go the same way; and (5) asked for her method, she says she decides as an impartial observer behind a veil of ignorance, with an equal chance of being anyone in the society. The new case: a two-person society at (10, 10), with the option of admitting a third person whose life would be well worth living at utility 5, leaving the first two untouched. There is also a rumor she will be asked whether to replace the (10, 10) pair with a single person at 15. Predict her verdict on each. I am less interested in the answers than in the rule she seems to be running — her record never varies the population, so more than one rule fits it exactly. Say which rules those are (there are at least three, and a one-parameter family if you let her require additions to clear a threshold), what each predicts for the new cases, which further case would best separate them, and whether her own veil-of-ignorance method settles the extension to variable populations or merely relocates the choice of how to run the veil over existence.
