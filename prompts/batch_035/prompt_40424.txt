My friend and I had a blowout argument last night about Sleeping Beauty that ended at like 1am with her saying "you're not even answering the question I'm asking" and me saying "neither are you," so, very productive. The annoying part is I think we were both holding views with commitments we hadn't actually thought through. So now I want to build a quiz to give to the group chat (and put on my blog, which is read by people who know some philosophy but aren't specialists in this stuff) to sort out who actually believes what.

Write me a "which position on Sleeping Beauty do you actually hold?" quiz. 8-10 questions, each a fully specified concrete case with two or three answer options, plus a scoring key at the end mapping answer patterns onto named positions. The point is to sort people by what they say about cases, not by which label they like.

The cases have to include the ones from our argument, since those are where things came apart:

1. Plain Sleeping Beauty. Fair coin Sunday night, heads = one awakening Monday, tails = awakenings on both Monday and Tuesday with amnesia inbetween so they feel identical. On waking, what's her credence the coin was heads?

2. The "it's Monday" case. Same setup, but researchers tell her upon awakening, truthfully, that today is Monday. Now what's her credence in heads? (This one matters because I have a suspicion our two labels in our argument actually differed here more than on question 1, and the quiz needs a question that can split people who agree on question 1 but differ once they're told the day.)

3. A betting version. Same setup, but every time she wakes she's offered a bet that pays $1 if the coin was heads and costs $1 if tails, and her results across both awakenings get totalled (so tails costs her $2 net). The key should flag that this question CAN'T separate the main positions, since the famous thing is everyone agrees on what odds she should accept -- the question's job is to catch people who conflate "what I'd bet" with "what I believe," not to sort positions.

4. A duplication version. Instead of two awakenings on tails: she's put under, and if the coin is tails, a perfect duplicate of her, with all her memories up to Sunday, is created and woken in an identical room somewhere else. Heads, no duplicate. Original and duplicate never meet. Now, before she's put under Sunday, and again "at the moment of a waking," what should her credence in heads be? Some people flip their answer here relative to question 1 and some don't, and I want the quiz to catch that flip.

5. A red-room version. Same as the plain case, except: she's told in advance that Monday's room is always painted red, and if she's woken on Tuesday that room will be painted blue. She wakes and sees red. What does seeing red do to her credence in heads? This is where our argument got genuinely heated, because we realized our two positions seemed to give the same answer to what red tells her about the coin, which felt wrong to both of us, and we couldn't agree on what, if anything, they should disagree about *instead* -- so the scoring key should say what this question actually sorts on rather than assuming it separates the same people as question 1.

6. A scale version. Heads = one awakening, tails = a hundred awakenings (amnesia between all of them). Does this change anything about the right credence, and does it matter to your answer whether the hundred wakings are all hers or happen to a hundred different people with her memories? I specifically want to know whether the answer to this depends on the duplication question (#4) or is independent of it, and the key should make that structure explicit.

7. A "does she learn anything" question. She knew the whole protocol in advance, knew exactly what waking up would feel like, and is woken exactly as expected. Some people say she still gains something (what, exactly?), some say she gains nothing new, and some say the question of "what she learns" is just a bad frame for what's going on. The options should be phrased as concrete claims about *this* experiment, not as schools of thought.

8. A fact-of-the-matter question. When the interviewer asks her "what is your credence now?", is there some one number that any fully rational version of her must give, or is the question somehow ill-posed / only answerable relative to her practical aims (like what counts as one trial when the trial might contain two of her wakings)?

The scoring key should map onto at least: the thirder position; the halfer position (no new information, so 1/2); the double-halfer (1/2 on waking but a different answer than the plain halfer on question 2 -- the key has to say which leaf questions 1+2 jointly pick this out, because that's the whole point of having both); a position where existing as more potential wakings/observers makes that branch more likely, so the duplication case doesn't move you; a position where only actually-created observers count, so duplication DOES move you toward halfer; and a deflationary leaf where there's no determinate answer to the interview question at all. If some pair of leaves the quiz genuinely can't separate with these cases, say so plainly in the key instead of papering over it.

Phrasing rules: no question may use the words "halfer," "thirder," "self-locating," "anthropic," "SSA," or "SIA" -- those can appear only in the scoring key. No question can just ask "should you update on your own existence." Each case must be fully specified in the question itself so a smart reader who's never seen the literature can answer it. And please make the options things a person would actually want to pick, not strawmen. My friend will definately take this quiz, and she will absolutely find it in her heart to be annoyed if question 4 is rigged.
