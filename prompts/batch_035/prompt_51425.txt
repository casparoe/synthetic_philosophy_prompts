So my brother and I have been going back and forth about the omnipotence paradox for weeks and I want to write down where I actually stand before I keep arguing, because I suspect my position has a crack in it somewhere. Here's the package:

(1) Omnipotence just means the ability to do anything that's logically possible. God can't draw a square circle, but that's not a real limitation, because "square circle" doesn't describe a task at all — it's just a string of words. Failing to do the impossible isn't failing.

(2) On that view the stone paradox is a non-problem. A stone too heavy for an omnipotent being to lift is a contradiction in terms, so being unable to make one is no deficiency. God can make stones of any size whatsoever, and that's the end of it.

(3) God essentially cannot lie and cannot do evil. His goodness is part of his nature the same way his power is — he couldn't be God without it, so the inability is not like my inability to fly, it's more like the way bachelors can't be married.

(4) But here's the thing: there's nothing contradictory in God uttering a false sentence. "God tells Moses the moon is made of cheese" describes a perfectly coherent scenario — I can picture it, nothing about it contradicts itself — it just would never happen. Unlike the square circle, it's a genuine possible state of affairs that God can't bring about.

(5) God created beings with free will whose choices he doesn't determine, and I think that's fine for omnipotence, since making someone freely choose something is itself a contradictory description. No task is being left undone there.

(6) God also can't create a second omnipotent being, and I'm inclined to just accept that as another instance of (3)-style necessity, though I'm honestly less sure.

Do these hang together? My brother keeps saying that if (3) and (4) are both true then the slick move in (1) and (2) is compromised — you can't say contradictions aren't tasks and then quietly subtract lying from the task list on totally different grounds. I think he might be onto something but I also think he might be equivocating. And I can't tell whether (5) and (6) are the same kind of case as (3) or the same kind of case as (1). Rank whatever tensions you find by how bad they are, say which ones are only apparent, and tell me what the cheapest repair would be and what it costs. I'm not attached to any particular one of these, I just want to know where the structure actually gives.
