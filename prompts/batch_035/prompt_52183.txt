Hey, so I'm a science and ideas reporter and I'm working on a piece about the aid-in-dying bills working their way through a few state legislatures. Last month I interviewed a bioethicist who has spent decades advising hospital ethics committees, and I noticed she sorts every case into "killing" or "letting die" before she says anything about whether it's right or wrong. I've got pages of her rulings in my notes and I want to figure out her actual rule before I call her back for follow-ups, so I don't ask her stuff she's obviously already answered. Here's the track record, as best as I've transcribed it:

- Doctor unplugs a ventilator at the competent patient's request: letting die, and fine.
- Doctor never starts a ventilator that would have saved the patient: letting die, fine.
- Doctor injects a lethal dose at the patient's request: killing, and she says wrong, full stop.
- Doctor gives escalating morphine for pain that everyone knows will shorten the patient's life by days: not killing, fine — she was pretty dismissive of anyone who can't see the difference between that and aiming at death.
- The old bathtub pair, where one guy drowns his cousin for the inheritance and the other stands by letting it happen while the cousin slips underwater on his own: first one kills, second lets die, but she insisted they're morally identical.
- Patient voluntarily stops eating and drinking, staff provides only comfort care: letting die, fine.

The new cases I need to write about: (a) the bills would let doctors prescribe a lethal dose that the patient swallows themselves, weeks later, at home — the doctor never touches the patient and isn't even present; (b) a nastier one, where a ventilator gets unplugged on a surrogate's demand, the doctor's own stated aim is that the patient die (say the doctor believes the patient is suffering with no chance of recovery), and the patient never left any request of their own.

Given her record, what will she say about these two — for each, is it killing or letting die, and is it OK? It seems like at least two rules fit everything she told me, something like "who actually caused the death" versus "what the doctor was aiming at," and I suspect they split on my cases, which would make for an intresting angle. Tell me which rule you'd bet on and why, and what other case I should ask her about that would definitly separate the rules. Since you're working off my shorthand notes rather than her exact words, please say how confident you are in your guesses and single out the one you're least sure of.
