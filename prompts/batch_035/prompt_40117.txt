I write a column for a science-and-ideas outlet — mostly risk, uncertainty, decision-making stuff — and somewhere along the way I became friendly with a guy I'll call Marek, who I met while reporting a piece on Ellsberg-style paradoxes. He's become my go-to soundbite whenever I need a quote about how people handle unknown odds. We also have a standing low-stakes poker game, and a while back I started keeping a notebook of his calls on little hypothetical bets I spring on him between hands. I want your read on what rule he's actually running, because I've got a couple of new ones planned for next game night and I definately can't predict him.

The setup I always use: an urn with 100 balls, each one red or blue. The guy who filled it swears there are between 20 and 80 red balls and between 20 and 80 blue ones, but wont say anything more than that.

His verdicts so far, from the notebook:

1. Offered an even-money $10 bet on red from that urn, or the identical even-money bet on heads of a fair coin, he takes the coin. Offered either side of the urn bet at even money — bet on red OR bet on blue — he declines both.
2. But at 5-to-1 in his favor ($50 if red, $10 against), he'll take the urn bet. Same at 5:1 on blue — he'll take either color at those odds.
3. Last month I offered him a package: two legs, $12 each. Leg one pays $40 if red (nothing if blue), leg two pays $40 if blue (nothing if red). Each leg alone he turned down flat when I later tried to sell it to him by itself — but the package, both legs for $24 total, so a guaranteed $40 back no matter the draw, he snapped up instantly.

Here's the plan for next time. Early in the evening I'll get him to take the red bet at 5:1 (he always does), so he'll be sitting on +$50 if red / −$10 if blue. Then, in one scenario or the other:

New case 1 (the double-down): I offer him a bet that pays $90 if red and costs him $40 if blue.
New case 2 (the hedge): instead, I offer him one that pays $14 if blue and costs $8 if red.

What's he going to say in each? Honestly I care less about the yes/no than about the underlying rule, and here's what's bugging me: I can see at least two or three different stories that fit everything in the notebook so far. Something like — he prices each offer on its own by its worst case across some range of probabilities; or he prices the urn at a flat 50/50 but demands a big edge before he'll touch anything ambiguous; or he judges each offer by what it does to his whole book rather than one bet at a time. Lay those candidates out, tell me which of his past calls knock out which stories (I have a feeling the even-money refusals and the leg-vs-package thing already kill some obvious candidates — maybe even some textbook ones), then give me your best guess on the two new cases with your reasons. And finally: what single further offer, at some future poker night, would settle whichever disagreement you think is still live? Bonus points if you can also tell me whether his prices would move if I let him draw a few balls with replacement before he bets.
