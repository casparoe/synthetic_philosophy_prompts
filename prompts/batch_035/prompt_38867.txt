My labmate showed me color phi a while back -- two dots, red one on the left, green one on the right, flashing on and off in alternation, and you don't see two flashes, you see one dot gliding across and changing color partway along. I got nerd-sniped into arguing about WHEN the experience of the color change happens, and now I have an argument I can't find the exit to:

(1) In the phi experience, the dot is seen changing from red to green at a point midway along its path, which given how fast it seems to move is a moment before the second dot actually appears.
(2) The green can only enter my experience once the second dot has shown up and been processed.
(3) So the experience of the change was built after the second flash, even though it presents the change as happening before it.
(4) An experience happens when it seems to happen. If it seems to you that you experienced the change at moment t, then you did experience it at t -- that's the one thing introspection is authoritative about, if it's authoritative about anything.
(5) So I experienced the color change before the green dot appeared on screen: the experience ran ahead of its own causes.
(6) If consciousness can do that even once, then introspective timing data is worthless as evidence about when anything is ever experienced.

My labmate says the only escape is either the brain rewrote my memory after the fact (so there was briefly a real experience of all-red motion that got erased and replaced) or the brain holds experience back a beat and edits it before letting it air, like a censor on a delay. But those two stories feel genuinely different -- in one there was an experience that later got falsified, in the other there was no experience at all until the edited version -- and I can't see how any experiment could ever tell them apart, which maybe means neither is right?? Step (4) is the one I most want to keep, giving it up feels like admitting my own mind can fool me about the order of my own experiences. Which step do I give up, and what does it cost?
