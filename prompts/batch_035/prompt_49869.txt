For a seminar on Mill I've been asked to write down where I stand on Mill and empire so the rest of the group can attack it. Here's my position, more or less: (1) the anti-paternalist core of On Liberty is the part of Mill still worth using today; (2) the concession he makes in that book about despotism over "barbarians" being legitimate when it genuinely aims at their improvement is a throwaway hypothethical, not load-bearing, and it shouldn't reframe how we read the rest; (3) his 35 years at the East India Company, including his defense of Company rule against the transfer to the Crown, are compatable with his liberalism — he thought the Company buffered India from the worst pressures of British public opinion, and he thought Indians were on the same developmental track and would eventually be fit for self-rule; (4) his side in the fight with Carlyle over the "Negro Question", and his insistence in the Logic that national character comes from circumstances rather than race, show that his hierarchy of societies was developmental and correctable rather than biological, which makes him much better than most of his contemporaries; (5) and I think that developmental hierarchy was doing real justificatory work and rightly so — pretending every society in 1850 was equally ready for representative institutions would just have been false. So: is this a consistent package? Which pairs actually pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what's the cheapest fix and what would it cost me? Rank the tensions by severity instead of flagging everything equally. Also tell me how confident you are in each diagnosis and single out the one you're least sure about — I don't want the seminar to anchor on your shakiest call without knowing it's shaky.
