I'm prepping a lecture segment on Dennett's tower of generate-and-test -- the Darwinian / Skinnerian / Popperian / Gregorian creatures thing from Darwin's Dangerous Idea and Kinds of Minds -- and I want one really well-made example species to anchor it, instead of the usual grab bag of separate examples for each rung. Here's what I need you to build:

Construct a fictional-but-biologically-plausible species (corvid or cetacean-grade intelligence, no uplift, no magic neural hardware) together with the observations an ethologist would record on it, such that the creature comes out Popperian on one reasonable reading of Dennett's criteria and merely Skinnerian on another, while the Gregorian question stays genuinely open. Specifically:

1. Offline trial-and-error: there has to be evidence the creature mentally tries out candidate actions before acting -- that's the whole point of the Popper rung, hypotheses dying in the creature's stead instead of the creature -- but the evidence should be the kind a skeptic could redescribe as very elaborate Skinnerian shaping by past reinforcement. Design the observations so both readings stay live.

2. One culturally transmitted behavior (a cache-map, a call notation, a grooming sequence that encodes third-party relationships -- pick something that fits the species) that is a plausible candidate for a mind-tool: it doesn't feed the creature or scare it, it just makes it better at a task by handing it a ready-made structure to think with. Build the case so that if you count that behavior as a mind-tool, the creature is Gregorian; if you insist mind-tools are essentially linguistic, it isn't. Don't stipulate which answer is right -- I want that to fall out of the criteria, not be written into the scenario.

3. A control condition where the creature fails in a revealing way. Rung placement shows up in errors as much as successes, so include a condition where a hypothesis is visibly dying without the creature paying for it -- or where it stubbornly pays anyway, if that's what your design needs.

After the scenario, walk me through it: what the Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian diagnoses each say about each behavior; where the two Popperian readings diverge and which feature of the case is doing that work; what happens to the whole case if you just delete the mind-tool behavior; and how a defender of the cumulative reading of the tower -- each rung presupposing the ones below it -- would attack your construction. Last thing: be honest about whether a single real species could actually carry all these features at once, or whether the case quietly cheats by packing into one brain things that would never co-occur together in nature.
