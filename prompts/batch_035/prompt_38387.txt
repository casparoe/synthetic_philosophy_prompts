I teach a mid-level ethics seminar, about 15 students, one 75-minute session. Design an activity where they run into incommensurability of values before I ever name it. My idea: they're a scholarship committee that has to pick one winner from candidates who shine in totally different ways, with no common currency on offer — but design the whole thing properly. Give me the candidate sheets (what the dimensions should be, so that no obvious weighting scheme pops out), a first round of easy comparisons to lull them, then a sweetener round where a small concrete improvement gets added to one candidate and they re-rank — I want to use that to separate the people who were nearly indifferent from the people for whom the improvement doesnt tip anything, if you see what im after — and then a forced-choice round where the deadline hits in 60 seconds and they must either flip a coin or stand behind somebody. The debrief should map what happened onto the standard better/worse/equal breakdown plus the leftover fourth case, say what the coin flips and the non-flips each mean, and press on whether more information would ever have helped — I want them to see the difference between a choice that's hard because you're ignorant and one that's hard because the values just dont line up, and I want to know how to get that distinction across without lecturing. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time (I assume some will immediately invent a points system, and I want to know whether to let them run with it), and be honest about which part of the idea this activity can't reproduce — my guess is the part where the choice actually changes the chooser, maybe something about whether, after they commit, they start finding new reasons for their candidate, and whether that's discovery or confabulation. One more thing: they shouldn't hear the terms "incommensurable" or "on a par" from me until the debrief is basically over.
