My PhD advisor is retiring after 40 years in a philosophy department, and I'm giving the toast at her retirement dinner. Her whole career was a defense of structural realism -- the view that what's real, or at least all we can ever know, is structure: the relations between things, not the intrinsic nature of the things themselves. And she was hardcore about it, the ontic version: no hidden inner nature to recover, no 'the thing itself' underneath, just the network. She used to joke that she couldn't prove her husband had any intrinsic properties, only that something reliably occupied the husband-position in her relational web, and that this was enough for a good marriage.

The room will be half family and half philosophers of physics who will absolutely catch it if I say something she'd have corrected me on mid-sentence. So here's the constraint: no sentimental stuff about her 'true self' or 'who she really is deep down' -- she hated that kind of talk, she'd argue with a greeting card. Write me a toast that describes her only the way she'd let herself be described: through her relations -- to her students, her co-authors, her rivals at conferences, her terrible old cats. It should land as warm and funny for the whole room, not as a lecture with a punchline bolted on. Five minutes max.
