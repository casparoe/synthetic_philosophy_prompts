I have to sign off on the dashboard within the month. Please describe the most important implications of Goodhart's law and specification gaming for my situation: not the slogan, but the working consequences for how a small civic institution should choose, cap, rotate, and publicly contextualize the measures it will be rewarded and punished by. I've run into attempts to subdivide Goodhart failures into types — cases where a measure merely tracks the goal imperfectly, cases where optimizing the measure changes the relation between measure and goal, and cases where an adversary actively exploits the gap — and I can't tell which of these subdivisions actually matter when the people being measured are my own librarians rather than competitors. I'm also unsure whether a metric that carries no budget consequences can still corrupt the institution's self-understanding, and how to think about measures as one-off readings versus standing incentives. Please indicate where you are extrapolating beyond settled ground rather than reporting consensus, and end by saying which argument or consideration would most change your overall verdict — I would rather know where the reasoning is most likely to fail than receive a confident brief for any particular dashboard.
