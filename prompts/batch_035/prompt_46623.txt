I am a novelist working on a speculative novel about a biological survey of an Earth-like planet, and I have hit a structural problem in my ethics subplots that I suspect only a properly designed thought experiment can solve. My protagonist, a field ethicist, travels with a colleague who believes that moral status tracks brain size — though he is more sophisticated than a simple mass-champion: pressed, he moves fluidly between absolute brain mass, encephalization quotient relative to body size, and total neuron count, taking whichever measure currently favors the beings he prefers to exploit. I refuse to have him lose by authorial fiat; I want the world itself to corner him. So I need you to design the corner.

Construct for me two non-human species, one paragraph each, engineered so that the three brain-size metrics come apart and point in opposite directions. The first species should have the largest absolute brain mass of anything the survey has found, and a high encephalization quotient, but a neuron distribution and behavioral profile that make any capacity-based claim to rich suffering or self-awareness doubtful. The second should have a brain that is humiliatingly small on all three conventional measures, yet display the kind of evidence — grief-like behavior, planning across seasons, recognition of individual dead, consolation of the distressed — that we normally take, in elephants and corvids, to indicate morally considerable minds. The species must be biologically plausible: no cosmic intelligences, no telepathy, no brains doing things neurons cannot do, and the design should stay within the range of real precedents, such as the way birds pack primate-like forebrain neuron counts into walnut-sized skulls, or the way a large-brained animal can concentrate most of its neurons in structures devoted to sensory and motor housekeeping rather than anything we associate with experience.

The moral stakes must be generated, not stipulated. Do not write "and they are not sentient" or "and they feel pain deeply"; describe only anatomy and behavior, and let each position derive its own verdict. After the two paragraphs, walk me through the reasoning of four characters in turn: one who holds absolute brain mass decisive, one committed to encephalization quotient, one to total neuron count, and a capacity-based sentiocentrist. Then tell me honestly which descriptive feature of each species is doing the work, and what happens to the thought experiment if that feature is subtracted — I want to know how load-bearing my own construction is, since a clever reader will poke at exactly that joint. Finally, give me the strongest counter-move still available to my brain-size colleague once the evidence is in — ideally one that forces him to retreat into unfalsifiable territory, since that is precisely the retreat I want to dramatize — and one or two suggestions for staging the confrontation so that it plays as a scene rather than a seminar.
