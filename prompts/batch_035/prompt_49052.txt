Every year I teach the causation unit in my medieval philosophy course, and every year half the class walks away convinced that al-Ghazālī is just Hume with a turban — no necessary connection, fire and cotton, regularity, done, done. I want a decision tree that makes the real distinctions impossible to blur, and I want your help building it.

The setup: we've just finished the seventeenth discussion of the Incoherence, where al-Ghazālī says "the connection between what are believed to be the cause and the effect is not necessary" and works his way to the case of "the burning of a piece of cotton at the time of its contact with fire." The tree should drop the student right there: fire touches cotton, cotton burns, and the question is what exactly did what. (Averroes is fair game as an extra leaf if you want him, but not required.)

Branch nodes should each be one question a student can answer from the armchair without knowing which position it feeds — things like whether the fire contributes anything of its own to the burning, whether the fire-cotton connection is necessary or merely habitual, whether divine causation competes with or includes created causes, whether created things can be genuine agents. Phrase every node so its answerable before the student knows the labels, and keep it to six or so nodes so the whole thing fits on one handout page (front and back is fine). The point is that students sort themselves by what they say about the case, not by which Muslim/Christian/Enlightenment brand they like the sound of, so no node can be a disguised "are you an occasionalist?" question.

Leaves must include at least: al-Ghazālī's own Ash'arite occasionalism, Avicenna's essentialist causation, Aquinas-style concurrentism, mere conservationism (God creates and sustains, but doesn't directly cause the burning), Malebranche's occasionalism, and plain Humean regularity. Build the logical space first from the node answers, then locate the positions inside it — don't just list the names and hang questions on them after the fact.

Two things I specifically want beyond the tree itself. First, note any combinations of node answers that nobody occupies, and diagnose the emptiness: incoherent, obviously unattractive, or just unexplored? Second, flag which leaves the tree genuinely cannot separate. My hunch is al-Ghazālī and Malebranche end up glued together unless you find a node that splits them — maybe on whether the denial of created causal power extends to human agency, or on the role habit plays in the epistemology — but be honest with me if the split can't be made to work at this level of abstraction. And if al-Ghazālī and Hume can't be kept apart either (both deny necessity, after all), then the tree probably needs a node about what grounds the regularity, so say so rather than papering over it.

Bibliography not needed, just the tree and the commentary on it. Thanks!
