I'm a critical care trainee doing a bioethics module, and I've been asked to write up my position on when someone has died for a journal club (mixed audience of clinicians and a couple of philosophers). Before I present I want a consistency check, because a few of them keep needling me and I can't tell if they've found something real. Here's the set of things I actually believe:

(1) Death is a biological fact about an organism, not a convention we adopt -- whatever we say about criteria, a body either has died or it hasn't; there's no third option where it depends on us.
(2) Whole-brain death is death, and not in a "we decided this in 1968" way -- people were brain-dead before we had tests for it; the concept caught up to the world.
(3) What makes death the loss of the integrated functioning of the organism as a whole. That's why a patient in a permanent vegetative state is alive -- the brainstem still integrates the body -- and why I have no time for talk of "death of the person" as something separate from death of the body.
(4) The dead donor rule is correct: you never take vital organs from someone who is alive.
(5) Donation after circulatory death as actually practised is fine: withdraw the ventilator as planned, the heart stops, you wait two minutes (some centres say five), declare death, and procure.
(6) Taking that same patient's organs two minutes before the heart stops would be killing them.

The needling I keep getting is that (5) and (6) together mean the moment of death depends on whether anyone intends to resuscitate, which they say wrecks (1). I cant fully answer that, but I also suspect they're so pleased with it that they've missed other problems in my list. So: which pairs of my commitments actually clash, which only look like they clash, and what's the cheapest fix for each? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all the same, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. For what it's worth I'd rather keep (4) than anything else, and I don't much want to lose (3) either. Write in British English please -- half the club will be pedants.
