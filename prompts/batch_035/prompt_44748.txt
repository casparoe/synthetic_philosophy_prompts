I'm on the curriculum committee of a smallish department, and something keeps happening that I can't find a name for. Last month we were deciding whether to make a stats course mandatory for the major. Everyone accepted (it's basically written into our bylaws) that the course should be mandatory if and only if it both (a) covers material students can't get anywhere else in the program and (b) students reliably struggle without it. We voted on (a): 4-3 yes. We voted on (b): 4-3 yes. Then someone said, great, so it's mandatory. And the room errupted, because when we finally took the direct vote on "should it be mandatory," it came out 4-3 no. Here's the thing: every single member of the committee was perfectly consistent in their own views. Three people were yes/yes, three were no/no, and there were two people who split (one yes on (a) no on (b), one the reverse). It's the committee as a body that ended up believing an inconsistent package: majority yes on the premises, majority no on the conclusion that follows from the premises by rules the group itself had signed off on. The same thing happened again last week with a hire -- the ad listed two required qualifications, a majority of us thought the candidate met each one, and yet the straight up-or-down hire vote went against her. So now I'm wondering whether we just have weird bylaws or whether this is a thing.

I know about Condorcet's paradox, the rock-paper-scissors cycles thing, but that's not this. Nobody has cyclical preferences, every individual is fine, it's only the group-level set of accepted propositions that's incoherent. And I gather Arrow's theorem is somewhere in the neighborhood, but that's about people ranking candidates and independence of irrelevant alternatives and so on, whereas what I've got is people giving yes/no answers to propositions tied together by an "if and only if" that everyone accepts. So my questions: does this exact configuration have an established name and literature behind it, and if so how is it usually stated and how is it distinguished from Condorcet and from Arrow, since people keep telling me those are the relevant results and neither seems to be? And if nothing in the literature captures it exactly, then I'd want you to say so honestly and then coin a term for it with actual edges -- does it only show up with two premises joined by an "iff", or does any logical connective produce versions of it? Is majority rule special, or would any way of combining the votes hit some version of the problem? Does it matter that we voted on premises first rather than the bottom line directly (they gave opposite answers, which is what unsettled people)? I'm not after just a label, I want to know what the phenomenon is and isn't, and where its boundaries are.
