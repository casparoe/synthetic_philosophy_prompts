could you write a sonnet about epiphenomenalism, specifically the paradox of phenomenal judgment? the octave should set up the classic picture — qualia as the steam off the engine, the shadow of the train, causally idle while the neural machinery does all the actual work. then the volta is the speaker realizing the awkward part: this very poem, the words saying the red is vivid or the ache is real, was caused by neurons and not by the redness or the ache itself. thats the paradox — if epiphenomenalism is true, my reports about my qualia cant be caused by the qualia, yet those reports somehow seem like the one thing i couldnt be wrong about. the closing couplet shouldnt try to resolve this, just let it sit, and ideally the couplet itself should do the trick of being a sincere first-person report that, on the theory its describing, has no business being about anything. accurate enough that someone who knows the zombie twin stuff wouldnt wince, but it should still work as just a poem if you dont know any of the philosophy. loose iambics are fine, shakespearian-ish rhyme scheme is fine, dont contort the sense to fit the meter
