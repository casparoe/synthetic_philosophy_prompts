The student wrote: "Aristotle argues in Nicomachean Ethics V that a just exchange is one of equality between the parties involved. Since a builder and a shoemaker are both people of equal standing, the products of their labor must be equal in value, and exchange is just when the two sides exchange equal value. This points to something like a labor theory of value: the value of a product is determined by the labor that went into it, so equal labor exchanged for equal labor is the basis of just exchange. Money comes in as the neutral measure of this labor value — it's a convention that lets us compare the labor embodied in a house and in a shoe. But Aristotle was pretty hostile to all of this. He thought commerce aimed at profit was unnatural, and that coined money itself was an unnatural invention, since wealth in exchange has no natural limit. So while he could explain just exchange between producers, he basically condemned the market activity that exchange makes possible, which is why later scholastics had to fix his account."
