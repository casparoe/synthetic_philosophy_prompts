My cousin got me into thinking about this Sleeping Beauty thing and now I think I've argued myself into a corner. The setup I'm working with: Beauty agrees to the whole thing on Sunday. A fair coin gets flipped Monday night, after she's already asleep. Heads: they wake her just once (Monday) and erase her memory after. Tails: they wake her Monday AND Tuesday, erasing her memory in between, so each time she wakes up she can't tell which day it is or wether she's been woken before.

Here's my reasoning:

(1) On Sunday night she has to think heads is 50/50. It's a fair coin and it hasn't even been touched.
(2) When she wakes up during the experiment, she learns nothing new about the coin, because she was told the full setup in advance and knew she'd find herself awake and groggy no matter what the coin does. So on any waking she should still be 50/50 on heads.
(3) Now suppose that during one waking, the experimenters tell her: "today is Monday."
(4) If it's heads, today is definately Monday. If it's tails, today is only Monday half the time (the other half of the time it's Tuesday). So "today is Monday" fits heads twice as well as tails, and she should update to 2/3 on heads.

But I can't accept that conclusion. In my version the coin gets flipped Monday night — it hasn't been flipped yet at the moment she's told it's Monday. How can being told the day of the week make a not-yet-flipped fair coin more likely to come up heads? That's like the calendar reaching into the future. It feels like the argument proves too much.

So something has to give and I don't know what. Which step should I give up, and what do I lose by giving it up? For what it's worth, (2) is the step that feels most solid to me, but I want to know what I'd be paying to keep it.
