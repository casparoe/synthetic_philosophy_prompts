Right, so — I spent 35 years as a fisheries biologist, mostly herring and mackerel stock assessments, and since retiring I've got properly hooked on philosophy, especially the bits about other minds. I've ended up in a running argument about farmed crustaceans with two people who each lean on a different analogy, and I'd like to know how far each one actually holds.

His: a prawn or a lobster is like a wind-up toy. Poke it, it twitches; there's a mechanism in there, but nobody's home. (He's a retired trawlerman, thinks all this animal welfare talk is soft.) He uses it to say the welfare fuss is sentimental nonsense — no point spending money on gentler handling or stunning before killing, because there's nothing in there to be kind to. Hers: a crab is like a cat in a suit of armour — same inner life, same feelings about things, just a different chassis. (My daughter runs a marine conservation charity.) She uses it to say farmed crustaceans deserve the full regime we give mammals, humane slaughter included.

Could you audit both? I'd like a correspondance table for each — what's supposed to play the role of the spring and the gears in his, and of the cat's feelings in hers — and then where each mapping breaks. I know a little from the old job, so don't dumb it down: they've got a chain of ganglia rather than one big centralised brain, the nociception-versus-pain distinction, those experiments where hermit crabs give up a good shell to avoid a shock, the way they groom at wound sites, local anaesthetics seeming to settle them down. And the argument is really about concrete practices: eyestalk ablation of breeding females in shrimp hatcheries, high-density grow-out ponds, killing in ice slurries. So for each analogy, tell me which disanalogies actually matter for the conclusion being drawn and which are just decoration. What is each one smuggling in — his that mechanism means no experience, hers that you can swap the body like a coat and the occupant stays the same? And do the conclusions survive: his that welfare spending on these animals is wasted, hers that the standards should be identical to what we demand for mammals? If neither analogy is doing the work its user thinks it is, say what would be a better one, or whether deciding this by analogy at all is backwards.
