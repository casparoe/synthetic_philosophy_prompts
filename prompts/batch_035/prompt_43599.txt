Two new cases come up next week, and I'd like a prediction. First, the same stamped 70% coin, except that a colleague who has watched thousands of its flips tells her it actually comes up heads about 90% of the time. My suspicion is that more than one rule fits her record so far — an always-match-the-stated-chance rule, a treat-stated-chances-as-evidence-about-the-true-chance rule, maybe others — so tell me which rules are genuinely still in the running, what each one predicts for the two new cases, which of the two cases (or some further case you devise) would best separate the surviving rules, and your best guess at her answers with your reasons. Along the way, please flag what is settled versus genuinely contested among people who work on deference to objective chance — for instance, whether such deference needs an admissibility restriction, and whether deterministic devices can carry nontrivial chances at all — and don't present a contested position as the consensus one.
