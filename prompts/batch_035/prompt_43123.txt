So i've been going down a rabbit hole on the Rietdijk-Putnam argument lately and I want an essay that actually takes a side instead of doing the usual "philosophers disagree, the end" thing.

Quick recap of the setup so we're on the same page: the argument is that special relativity forces the block universe. Since simultaneity is relative to an observer's frame, two people passing each other on the street (even at tiny relative velocity) will disagree about which distant events are "now." So an event that is in the unresolved future for one of them is already a settled fact for the other. There's a famous version with people walking past each other and a faraway galaxy, where one person's "now" includes a decision that for the other hasn't happened yet. If both observers' "nows" are equally real, the future is as fixed and ontologically on par as the past. Eternalism, block universe, no becoming, no open future.

The standard presentist comeback is to posit a preferred foliation of spacetime, and everyone groans because it seems ad hoc and in tension with the spirit of relativity. But here's the thing I keep chewing on and want the essay to really engage with: cosmology kind of hands you a foliation for free? The universe is (to a very good approximation) homogeneous and isotropic, and there's a natural rest frame defined by the cosmic microwave background, with a cosmic time that all comoving observers share. Plenty of working cosmologists effectively treat cosmic time as *the* time of the universe without feeling dirty about it. So a presentist could say: relativistic simultaneity is just a coordinate convention (Reichenbach-ish point about distant simultaneity being conventional anyway), while real becoming happens along cosmic time slices. Does this dissolve the Rietdijk-Putnam argument, or is it a cheat?

Some things the essay should probably wrestle with: (1) whether "natural for doing cosmology" translates to "metaphysically privileged" at all — a foliation being convenient isn't the same as it being where reality lives; (2) the worry that even with a foliation you don't automatically get becoming — slicing a block into layers doesn't make one layer "move", you seem to need something extra over and above the slicing to recover an open or growing future; (3) the fact that our actual universe isn't exactly the smooth idealized cosmological model, we're lumpy and moving relative to the CMB frame, so how pristine does the privileged slicing need to be?; and (4) whether Rietdijk-Putnam style reasoning was ever really about coordinates in the first place, or about the deeper point that the theory just has no room for an objective global present, in which case pointing at one convenient coordinate system doesn't touch it.

Please actually reach a verdict though — either the cosmic time move defeats the argument, or it doesn't, or it wins something but not everything (like maybe it saves a global present but not becoming). Justify whatever you land on, don't just list positions and shrug. No reading lists or citations needed, I care about the reasoning. It can be a longish essay if that's what it takes.
