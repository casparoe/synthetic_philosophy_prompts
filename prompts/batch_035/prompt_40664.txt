After a forty-year career in biophysics — much of it spent on the intrinsic stochasticity of ion channels in neuronal membranes — I have retired and taken up philosophy, reading on personal identity and mind uploading. I have discovered that I hold firm verdicts about cases of gradual neural replacement, and I want to know what principle, if any, captures them. Please act as an expert philosopher of personal identity, take the time to brainstorm two or three candidate principles before committing to one, and then tell me plainly which of my verdicts your best principle cannot save.

My verdicts are these. (1) If a single neuron of mine is replaced by a silicon device reproducing its input–output behavior exactly, I survive; the same for a thousand. (2) If all of my neurons are replaced one at a time over many years, each mimicked exactly, I survive the process. (3) If the same replacement is compressed into a minute, I still survive. (4) If I am destructively scanned and an exact functional duplicate instantiated all at once, I do not survive — that is a death plus a copy, however good the copy. (5) During a gradual replacement, suppose each removed biological neuron is kept alive in a dish, and halfway through the process the biological neurons are reconnected to one another and run in parallel with the silicon remainder in my skull: my instinct is that the biological system is me, but that the half-biological system in my skull is also me, and I cannot accept that both are. (6) Since a fair share of what I would call my spontaneity is, as I know professionally, membrane noise, I am inclined to think that replacements which are functionally identical except for eliminating that noise — leaving a smoother, more deterministic me — do not quite preserve me, though I cannot see why noise should be load-bearing. (7) If the whole system is replaced and then run at half speed, one subjective day per two days of wall-clock time, I survive.

I suspect the hard pair is (2)/(3) against (4), and that (5) is in tension with nearly everything else. Is there one principle that delivers all or most of these verdicts, and if not, which simpler principle should I accept, and which verdicts must I give up? Please state the principle precisely enough to generate verdicts on cases I have not listed, tell me what it says about the case I keep going back and forth on — a replacement that proceeds neuron by neuron over years, but where each silicon duplicate runs in parallel with its biological counterpart for a month before the biological neuron is removed, with the two synchronized every night — and be explicit about where the principle is doing real work versus where it is merely redescribing my intuitions back to me.
