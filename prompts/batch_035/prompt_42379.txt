I'm a novelist three chapters into a first-contact book, and the whole plot rests on the dark forest idea from Liu Cixin's trilogy — that the sky is quiet on purpose, that staying silent and hidden is the dominant strategy for any civilisation that lasts long enough, because you can never verify another species' intentions and capabilities can shift faster than trust could plausibly be built across light-years. My last two novels had no aliens whatsoever, so my editor is already nervous, and my readers are exactly the sort of people who send long angry emails about game theory. So before I commit several years to this thing, I want to know how defensible the bet actually is: what's your honest credence that the dark forest hypothesis is, roughly, true of our galaxy — that the best explanation of the Fermi observation is deliberate, strategic silence rather than rarity, extinction, or indifference?

I don't just want a number, I want the decomposition. Terms like: your credence that the Fermi observation even needs a strategic explanation rather than rare Earth or a Great Filter behind us; your credence that the game theory holds up once you pick at it — whether hiding really dominates broadcasting when everyone else is also reasoning about reasoning, whether a first strike is ever actually cheap and safe relative to the risk of waiting, whether the "chain of suspicion" is a stable equilibrium or just an elegant story; and any other terms you think belong (detectability of past leakage, whether dead civs are more likely than hiding ones, whatever). Give each a rough probability with one line of justification, show me how they combine into the overall figure, then tell me which term you're least confident in and what evidence or argument would move it the most. For reference I'm sitting around 0.3 and my partner, who has actually read all three books, is at about 0.6, so it'd be useful to know where our disagreement most likely lives too.

One novelist-specific ask at the end: two of my characters — the ship's xenologist and her captain, who are locked in a fight about whether to send a signal — will disagree about exactly one of your sub-questions in the book. Which sub-question would make for the sharpest and most honest argument between them? I want the scene to be about the load-bearing bit, not some decoration. Please answer in British English, and don't be shy of decimal points; my editor thinks readers hate numbers but my editor is wrong about a lot of things.
