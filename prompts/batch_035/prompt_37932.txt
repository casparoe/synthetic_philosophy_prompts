Hi, so I'm a journalist — science and ideas beat, mid-sized outlet, the kind of person who writes up replication crises and fMRI feuds for a living — and I'm trying to frame a piece I can't quite get a handle on, so I'm hoping you can help me find the right conceptual grip on it.

Over the last year or so I've interviewed a bunch of philosophers for unrelated stories, and something kept nagging at me: every once in a while one of them would settle a point by saying, roughly, that our intuition about some little hypothetical scenario is just clear — the scenarios involve things like a guy who believes it'll rain and turns out right only by luck, or whether a name someone inherited from a garbled story attaches to the original person it was about or to whoever the whole community now uses it for, or whether someone whose choices are fully the product of their upbringing and circumstances can be said to have chosen at all. These gut reactions get treated as data. Meanwhile my day job is constantly reminding me how little of what feels universal actually is — the facial expressions literature got complicated, and everyone's now aware that behavioral science leans absurdly hard on college sophomores in rich countries. And I'm told that when you actually take those philosophy scenarios to different populations — different countries, different languages, sometimes just different majors — the gut reactions split in systematic, patterned ways. Not confusion, not inattention: the intuitions themselves seem to track where people grew up.

So my question: is there a name for this? Two names, really — one for the finding itself (that these reactions vary by culture in a structured way), and one for the methodological embarassment it creates for anyone who leans on their own intuitions as evidence about knowledge, freedom, reference, whatever. Cultural relativism is obviously adjacent, but that's a claim about truth being relative to a culture, and what I'm pointing at seems to be about the reliability of a particular introspective data source, which feels like a different animal. Garden-variety "people disagree across cultures" also doesn't cut it, because philosophers are presumably doing something more specific than expressing preferences. If there's an established concept that covers exactly this configuration, tell me what it is and where its edges are — and if there isn't one crisp label, I'd genuinely rather you coin something and define it, because a bare name won't help me structure the piece. In particular I need the carve: is the variation in the intuitions themselves, or just in how people parse the wording of the scenario (which would make it a translation problem instead)? And is the person with the divergent intuition wrong about the concept, or are they in some sense running a different concept, and does that distinction even hold up? My editor keeps asking what the story is and all I have so far is vibes, so the sharper the boundaries the better.
