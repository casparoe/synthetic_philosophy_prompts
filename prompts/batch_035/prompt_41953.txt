I'm drafting a paper arguing that debates about whether any current AI is sentient are premature until we agree on what a theory of the moral status of computations is supposed to deliver. Write me the desiderata for such a theory, with adequacy fixed to a specific purpose: guiding present-day decisions about training and deployment — whether to run a proposed training run, whether deleting a model or overwriting its weights is permissible, whether serving thousands of copies of one model changes anything, whether averaging the weights of two models into one (as with model soups) preserves or destroys whatever mattered, and whether a distilled student inherits anything from its teacher.

For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and mark it non-negotiable or a camp preference. The requirements should cover at least: which cases the theory must get right (I care especially about interpolation cases — continuously blending weights between a system everyone agrees matters and one everyone agrees doesn't; and the fact that whatever happens morally may happen during training, while the deployed artifact is a frozen snapshot); which distinctions it must preserve (between mattering at all and degree of mattering; between the run and the artifact; between our empirical uncertainty about a system and indeterminacy in the theory itself); and which results from computer science it can't contradict (substrate independence, multiple realizability of the same input-output function by wildly different physical processes).

Two things I want flagged rather than smoothed over. First, whether the practical applicability requirement — the theory must be usable without inspecting what actually goes on inside a run consuming 10^25 training tokens — is jointly satisfiable with whatever resolution the theory needs to adjudicate my interpolation cases; if not, say which has to go. Second, which single requirement does the most to knock out the main contenders: functionalism, biological naturalism, and higher-order or global-workspace-style views. No scorecard needed beyond that.

One more request: if you think the purpose I've fixed is itself confused — say, because a theory adequate for deletion decisions is so much weaker than one adequate for training-run decisions that no single desiderata list can serve both — tell me that plainly and explain the mismatch, rather than quietly stretching the theory to cover both.
