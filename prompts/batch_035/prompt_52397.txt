My friend Marek and I got into a two-hour argument after our formal epistemology reading group last thursday, and I want someone to adjudicate the wreckage. The topic was logical omniscience, which Marek summarized for me like this (paraphrasing his rant, but faithfully, I was taking notes by the end):

"So the thing with epistemic logic is that it makes everyone a genius by construction. Knowledge is closed under logical consequence: if you know p, and p logically entails q, then you know q. In the possible-worlds setup this falls straight out of the semantics — knowing p just means p is true at every world you can't distinguish from the actual one, and since logical truths hold at every world whatsoever, every agent automatically knows all of logic and all of mathematics. So in the formalism every knower is omniscient, and real finite minds like ours just don't fit the model. And it's the same deal with introspection: on the very same semantics, if you know p then you know that you know p, so the omniscience problem and the introspection problem are really one problem wearing two hats. The fix people usually reach for is to just drop the closure axiom and move to one of those non-normal or neighborhood logics, and then you've got a logic of real bounded agents. But the deeper point is that since actual agents are so obviously not omniscient, the whole possible-worlds framework is a toy — formal logic cosplaying as psychology. It tells you nothing about what actual knowers are like."

I have a feeling he's right about the setup — the closure principle and the bit about valid formulas holding at every world sound like what we read in the seminar — but wrong about what follows from it. The "just drop the axiom and you're done" part sounds way too easy to me, and folding introspection in as "one problem in disguise" felt off at the time, though I couldn't articulate why mid-argument. Also I remember the professor suggesting the omniscience result is supposed to be a feature that reveals something about the concept of knowledge, not just a bug, which doesn't square with Marek's whole framing, but maybe I misremembered.
