My brother and I have been arguing about what counts as art all month, so I'm going to list my actual gut verdicts and I want someone to tell me if there's a principle behind them, because I sure can't find one. Duchamp signing a urinal and putting it in a show: art, obviously (annoyingly). The exact same urinal sitting in the plumbing aisle at the hardware store: not art. A painting that is a perfect forgery of a Vermeer, indistinguishable even to the experts: art, but I feel like it's art in a different way than the real Vermeer, and I can't explain why, since visually they're identical. A tree root that the wind shaped into something you'd swear was a sculpture, placed on a pedestal in a gallery with a title card: art, I think? A photograph of that same root, printed huge and nicely lit: definately art. The fingerpaint marks a chimp at the zoo makes: not art, but I can't put my finger on why not, since the root counts and nobody made that either. Cave paintings: art. My five year old's macaroni collage: art, though bad art. An image an AI generates from a lazy three-word prompt: art, I guess, but somehow less than the photo, and I can't tell if that's about the prompt or the machine. A conceptual piece where the "work" is just a certificate entitling the owner to spend ten minutes trying to imagine a square circle: art, though this one makes me want to give up on the whole question. My tentative principle is something like: art is anything made or presented with the intention that it be attended to in the way we attend to art — but I don't think that gets the chimp right, and it maybe doesn't get the root either.

Can you act as a professional philosopher of art and think carefully about this, maybe brainstorm out loud for a bit before commiting to an answer? Then tell me: is there one principle that saves all (or nearly all) of my verdicts, and if so, what is it exactly? Which of my verdicts would a good principle have to overturn? And then tell me what your principle predicts for the case I keep flip-flopping on: a scammer who sells "outsider art" that consists of plain river stones he found, each sold with a fake backstory about the mysterious artist — nobody made the stones, but he did write the backstories, and the buyers genuinely attend to the stones as art. Art or not, and why?
