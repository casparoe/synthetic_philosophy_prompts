Is there an established name for the following argument? Take two observers who pass each other at the same spot, moving at slightly different velocities. In special relativity their planes of simultaneity tilt relative to each other, so they disagree about which distant events are happening "now"; and because the disagreement grows with distance, even across a faraway galaxy one observer's "now" can include events that the other would place years in my future. The argument then runs: for that distant observer, the decision I will make next Tuesday already lies in their present, so it is a fixed fact about the world; and since the physics privileges no observer over another, my future must be fixed, full stop. I have run into this in discussions of the "block universe" and of eternalism, but as far as I can tell those name a picture of what spacetime is like, not this specific inference from simultaneity to the fixity of the future. Does the inference itself have a name and a literature? If so, I would like to know how its proponents answer the obvious objection that what is simultaneous with a distant moving observer is a coordinate artifact that should carry no ontological weight, and whether the conclusion is supposed to be determinism specifically or merely that future events exist. If there is no canonical name, tell me what the nearest concepts are and how the argument differs from each.
