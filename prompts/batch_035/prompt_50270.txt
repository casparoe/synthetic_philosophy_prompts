Is there a name for this? My dad died a couple years ago and I noticed something weird afterwards. It's not that I miss him — I do, but that's not it. It's that there's a version of him in my head that keeps working. When I was weighing whether to take a job across the country, I caught myself running the argument past him, and internal-him came back with an objection I hadn't thought of, and it was a good one, and the real him never actually made it because the real him never heard about the job. Sometimes model-dad is better at being him than he was, or at least more consistent. And it's not just him. I have a living friend whose taste I trust more than my own, and when I'm stuck between two options I literally ask "what would Maya say," and that settles it, even against my own first reaction. So the phenomenon, as best I can describe it: I've incorporated other peoples' judgment into my own deliberation as a standing voice with real authority, and the voice keeps operating after the person can't — the model stops updating but it doesn't shut off.

The closest thing I've read is Hofstadter in I Am a Strange Loop, where he suggests his wife's "I" was partially copied into his brain and keeps running there after she died. But he seems to mean something more like her pattern persisting in him, and he's honestly kind of fuzzy on whether the copy is really her or just his impression of her. That fuzziness is part of my question. When internal-dad objects to something, and real dad would have objected too but for dumber reasons, whose objection am I actually hearing? And when I "ask Maya," am I really consulting her, or just laundering some preference of my own that I'd feel silly owning directly?

To be clear what I'm NOT asking about: it's not grief exactly — the grief people talk about keeping a bond with the dead, rituals, talking at the grave, and that's not this, there's no relationship-maintenance in it for me, model-dad is more like a tool. And it's not the freudian super-ego thing, which I gather is about parental authority getting installed in you, because dad's voice doesn't mainly show up on moral stuff, it shows up on practical judgment, and the Maya case isn't authority at all — it's just that her model outperforms mine. So: is there an established concept, in philosophy or psychology, for outsourcing parts of your own judgment to simulated other people, alive or dead? If the field doesn't have a single term, what would you call it, and where would you draw the edges — like, does it still count when the model is of someone I never met (a dead writer whose taste I've internalized from their books), or when I know the model's advice is bad but I follow it anyway out of loyalty to the original? I'd rather have a real carving of the thing than just a label, so if you give me a term, tell me what it includes and, more importantly, what it leaves out.
