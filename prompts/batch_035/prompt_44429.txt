I run a small metaphysics blog aimed at advanced undergraduates, and my next post is about dispositions, finks, and masks. The germ of the idea came from a running argument with two friends: one insists that a vase swaddled in styrofoam is still fragile even though it would survive a drop, the other thinks calling it fragile while it is packed is a confusion the conditional analysis was right to expose, and I keep changing sides depending on which example we happen to be discussing. So instead of another expository post, I want a sorting quiz titled something like "which theory of dispositions do you actually hold?", which will force readers to commit on cases rather than vibes.

Please write eight to ten questions, each presenting a concrete scenario with two or three answer options. The scenarios must include, roughly in this order of difficulty: a straightforward case (a glass dropped on tile); a finked case where something would intervene to remove the disposition before it could manifest, in the style of C. B. Martin's sorcerer example; a masked case where an intrinsic barrier prevents manifestation without removing the disposition; an antidote case where an independently introduced interfering agent blocks manifestation; and a mimicry case where something with no relevant disposition at all would produce an effect indistinguishable from the manifestation. You should also include at least one question probing whether a disposition must have a distinct categorical base, e.g. by way of fundamental particles whose charge or spin seems to be nothing over and above a power, and one question probing whether dispositions are genuinely causally efficacious properties or mere shorthand for how their bearers behave. Every question must be answerable by someone who has never heard the terms "fink", "mask", or "antidote" — no question may just ask "do you accept the conditional analysis?" — and options should be phrased as claims about the case.

The scoring key should map answer patterns onto at least these positions: the simple conditional analysis, defended by biting the bullet on finks and masks; a refined or qualified conditional analysis that adds clauses about interfering factors, with the perennial worry that the repairs are ad hoc; a Humean view on which dispositions are real but supervene on distinct categorical properties; the dispositional monism of writers like Alexander Bird and Stephen Mumford, on which some dispositions have no categorical base at all; and an eliminativist line in the spirit of D. H. Mellor, on which dispositions are not genuine properties. After the key, I want you to take a stand: say where you would land if you took the quiz honestly, and defend a definite verdict on the fink case and on the mask case, rather than telling me the options. Finally, flag any two positions your questions cannot cleanly separate (be specific about which pair of answer patterns they share), and identify any combination of answers that no live position occupies — and say, for each such empty cell, whether you think it is empty because the view is incoherent, because it is unattractive, or simply because no one has explored it yet.
