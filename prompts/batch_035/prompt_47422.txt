I'm putting together a position paper for a jurisprudence reading group on the Hart–Devlin debate, and before I circulate a draft I'd like to know whether my overall stance actually coheres, because I have a nagging suspicion that at least a couple of my commitments are quietly at war with each other. Here is the set. (1) I'm with Hart against Devlin on the central question: the state has no business criminalizing conduct merely because the majority considers it immoral, and the proper business of the criminal law is preventing harm to others — at a stretch I would also allow preventing grave, unavoidable offense to unwilling witnesses. (2) That said, I think Devlin was onto something as a sociologist: I believe a shared moral code really is a load-bearing structure of a society, the way its constitutional arrangements are, and that a society whose common morality genuinely dissolves doesn't merely change but dies. (3) I support criminalizing cruelty to animals even in the hard case where a man torments only his own animal, nobody ever finds out, and the animal dies — and I refuse to justify this via the Kantian maneuver of grounding it in damage to the tormentor's own humanity or in the distress of potential human observers, both of which strike me as lawyers' dodges. (4) On sexual morality I come out exactly where you'd expect a Hartian to: I think consensual incest between adults in private should be legal, yet I'm comfortable keeping public sex acts criminal even when the only person who ever witnesses one is an accidental passerby who is merely shocked and moves on. (5) Finally, I support seatbelt and helmet mandates and a ban on selling heroin even to a fully informed adult, and I've convinced myself that this is fully consistent with the harm principle because self-inflicted harm is still harm.

So that's the package. What I'd like is an audit rather than a survey: go through the commitments, tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look that way, rank the tensions by severity, and — this is the part I most want — make a definite call about which single revision would clean up the most, and say what that revision costs me. Don't present me with a menu of equally weighted options and let me choose; I want you to commit to a diagnosis, including of the pairings you think dissolve on inspection, and to explain why the apparent conflict there was only apparent. For context, I should say that I genuinely don't know where the trouble is: I suspect the animal cruelty and paternalism items are fine and that if anything it's the sociology one that's doing damage, but I've flip-flopped on this enough that I don't trust my own ranking, which is why I'm outsourcing it. If it matters for your diagnosis, my intuitions about harm are pre-theoretical — I've never sat down and decided whether animals or future selves or "society" itself fall inside the harm principle's circle of protection, so feel free to tell me that my problem lies precisely in having never made those calls.
