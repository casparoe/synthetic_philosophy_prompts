I run a philosophy meetup and want a decision-tree handout that sorts members by their actual response to Bostrom's simulation argument, not by the label they'd pick for themselves. The tree should start from someone who grants the argument is valid — the three propositions really can't all be false — and then branch on questions a person can answer without knowing where the branch leads, things like 'would a functionally perfect computer copy of your brain have experiences?', 'is it coherent to assign *yourself* a probability of being simulated, or does that self-locating move fail?', or 'if our simulators exist, are they probably simulated too?'. Leaves I want included: the Bostromian who concludes we're likely simulated, the Chalmers-style view that even if we're simulated, tables and pains and careers are all still fully real, the consciousness objection (substrate independence just fails), and the 'civilizations survive but never bother running ancestor sims' rejection. Also: at least one node should use the case of a civilization that runs exactly one high-fidelity ancestor sim ever and then stops — does being in that scenario change your credence, and why? Empty branches should be noted with a sentence on whether nobody sits there because the view is incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored, and flag any pair of leaves the tree can't really separate. Ten-ish nodes max, phrased so everything is answerable by reasoning alone, no appeals to who said what or citing the literature. Keep it casual enough that people argue over it at the bar after.
