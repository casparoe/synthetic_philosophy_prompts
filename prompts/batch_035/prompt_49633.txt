I write novels for a living, and I've gotten myself into a philosophical muddle about what's actually happening when my characters start "misbehaving," so here's my current package of beliefs and I want an honest audit of whether it hangs together:

(1) I'm sold on the intentional stance being how belief-talk gets its grip: beliefs and desires are real patterns in a system's behavior, real the way a center of gravity is real — good for prediction and explanation, but nothing over and above the stuff the pattern is in. (2) Relatedly I'm a thorough physicalist: all the causing gets done at the substrate. Dennett's careenium — the billiard-ball universe with the simm-level patterns rolling around on top of it — is basically how I think minds work, and I take it to show that the simmball level does no pushing whatsoever. The balls move how they move, full stop. (3) I'm a determinist about my own brain, including while I'm drafting. (4) And yet when I'm drafting, my characters make choices I didn't plan, and I go back and rewrite the plot because "she would never do that," and I can't quite bring myself to treat that as a pretty metaphor. It feels like the character-level facts are doing genuine explanatory work for why my fingers typed what they typed — like the simmballs shoving the billiard balls around. (5) I've also come to think fictional characters have beliefs and desires in literally the same sense that you and I do — if belief is stance-pattern, characters are nothing but pattern, so if anything they're the purest case. (6) Separately, (3) and (4) have never kept me up at night — a deterministic brain can still be surprised by its own output, that's just what drafting is, so I think that pair is fine.

So: which of these are actually in tension, which only look like it, and what's the cheapest fix? Rank the tensions by severity, tell me what would have to give for each and what it costs me, and then say which single revision would clean up the most. I honestly don't know where the bodies are buried — e.g. I can't tell whether (5) rescues (4) or sinks it, since a character doesn't even have a careenium under her. Go through the pairs, don't just lecture me about downward causation.
