I'm a second-year philosophy student taking a metaphysics course centred on the problem of universals, and I also have a part-time job helping curate the schema of my university's biological specimen database. Researchers keep submitting terms they want modelled, and for each one somebody has to decide whether it becomes a repeatable class or property in the schema — something many specimens can share — or is recorded as a one-off label attached to particular records. The database manager wants a written procedure the curators, none of whom have studied metaphysics, can run on any submitted term in a few minutes; he also wants it grounded in the actual philosophy rather than ad hoc, and the course has convinced me this decision is a live version of the one-over-many problem: when many specimens are blue, is that one thing they share, or only a resemblance? Please build the procedure out of what the main positions on universals imply for modelling — realism about shared properties, resemblance nominalism, and the contrast between sparse properties that deserve ontological weight and abundant ones that merely re-describe what's already there. Give the ordered questions, say what counts as a sufficient answer at each step, mark where the procedure terminates with 'record as an individual' as against 'admit the class or property', and be explicit about the steps that are judgement calls the philosophy cannot settle and that must be handed to a person. Then run the procedure on three submissions: a researcher who wants flower colour treated as a shared property across specimens; one who wants 'collected by a famous naturalist' modelled as a class of specimens rather than as a relation to a person; and one who wants a hybrid zone — a population that is spatiotemporally continuous, interbreeding, and fading out at the edges — modelled as a class. I suspect the procedure will say that the last one is an individual rather than a class, which is the answer that researcher will hate, so please explain that case carefully. Please write the whole answer in British English.
