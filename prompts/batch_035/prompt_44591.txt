been rereading Strawson's Freedom and Resentment with my reading group and something keeps bugging me that he kind of hand-waves: the reactive attitudes get treated as one big family that stands or falls together against the objective attitude, but they obviously don't all wear down at the same rate. like, I can know everything about someone's rotten childhood and bad wiring, be fully in objective mode about them as a case history, and still feel a flash of gratitude when they hand back the wallet I dropped — whereas resentment toward them seems to actually erode the more real the objective picture feels to me. or is it the other way around, and gratitude is the fragile one? honestly not sure, which is why I want your take.

so here's the ask: build me a tier list (or just a ranked list, whatever) of these by how readily they give way to the objective attitude once you stop fully taking the participant stance toward the person:

1. resentment when someone wrongs you personally
2. gratitude for a benefit conferred
3. vicarious resentment / indigation on behalf of a specific other who's been wronged
4. generalized indignation at the violation of a moral demand as such (someone cuts in a line you aren't even in)
5. guilt
6. shame
7. hurt feelings at being slighted by someone you love

for each one, a couple sentences on why it lands where it does — especially flag which ones you think are exempt-able the way Strawson says we already partly suspend the participant stance toward children and the seriously mentally ill, versus which ones just ARE the participant stance and can't be worn without it.

then the payoff question: does your ordering give ammo to the optimist of Strawson's scenario — the one who thinks we could progressively thin out the whole economy of attitudes and run society on something like detached, benevolent management of people as objects of policy — or does it back his pessimism that this is a fantasy for anyone who actually has to live among humans? and if the answer is mixed, say which end of your tier list the optimist's project would have to eat first, and whether the whole economy could really survive losing that end.

don't worry about being "balanced," I want actual positions. also feel free to tell me my resentment-vs-gratitude hunch up top is wrong if you think it is, but then explain what the right contrast pair would be.
