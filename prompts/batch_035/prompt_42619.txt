been thinking about divine hiddenness lately (i'm a backend engineer, so no formal philosophy, bear with me) and I keep bouncing between two framings of the problem and I genuinely can't tell if their actually different or just the same argument with different thresholds. I want you to compare them properly.

Framing 1 — call it the "uptime" framing, since it's basically an SLA complaint: if a loving God exists and wants a personal relationship with every person, that's like a service promising high availability. Nonresistant nonbelief — people who honestly cannot believe, not because they're mad at God or suppressing something, but the evidence just isn't there for them — is like a failed request that isn't the user's fault. If the service never responds to some users no matter what they do, either the service doesn't exist or the SLA promise was never real. So the hiddenness problem is just: there exist failed requests through no fault of the client.

Framing 2 — call it the "sharding" framing: the problem isn't that God is unreachable, it's that reachability is weirdly unevenly distributed. Some people report vivid, persistent experiences of God, others get literally nothing their whole lives while sincerely trying. So the complaint shifts from absence to unfairness — the evidence isn't missing so much as it's badly load-balanced.

So: are these two distinct problems, or one problem? Could someone reasonably concede the sharding point (yes, the distribution is unequal) while denying the uptime point (each person who genuinely seeks gets enough)? Which framing is actually harder for the theist to answer — or does it depend? And here's what I really want to know: does the standard theist toolbox (free will style defenses, soul-making, skeptical theism about why we'd expect to judge God's reasons) work the same against both, or are some of those tools only good against one framing and useless against the other? Like it seems like skeptical theism maybe answers the uptime complaint but does nothing about the fairness of the distribution.

Also, does switching framings change what would even count as hiddenness being "refuted"? Say a village of lifelong nonresistant nonbelievers suddenly starts having religious experiences — does that move the needle on framing 1, framing 2, both, or neither? Justify your answer, and if you think one framing is secretly incoherent or collapses into the other, say so and explain why.
