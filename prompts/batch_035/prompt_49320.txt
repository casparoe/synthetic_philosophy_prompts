So here's my situation: I spent 38 years as an atmospheric scientist, mostly modelling how aerosols disperse, and since retiring I've fallen down the philosophy rabbit hole. Something I keep noticing is that my instincts as a scientist — you add things up, you compare totals, that's just what a quantity IS — keep colliding with the way moral philosophers talk. Nowhere has this gotten me in more trouble than a debate that's been raging for two years in the online group I'm in. You probably know it: the torture vs. dust specks thought experiment, where you choose between one person being tortured for 50 years and some absurdly large number of people (think 3^^^3) each getting a dust speck flicked into their eye. One of our members — I'll call him the Aggregator — is adamant that if you grant interpersonal comparability of wellbeing at all, then numbers have to dominate eventually, so the specks are worse and the torture is the right pick, full stop, and anyone who says otherwise is flinching from the math. Another member — call her the Thresholder — says tiny harms like dust specks fall below some line and simply don't count up, or don't count up the way the Aggregator assumes, and that treating moral badness like a conserved fluid you can pour between vessels is exactly the mistake.

They argue past each other constantly, and I've finally convinced them to try writing down an adversarial collaboration document instead of another 400-reply thread. This is where you come in. I want you to draft the joint statement they should be able to both sign. It should have a numbered section of claims both accept — about the setup itself, about what each side is and isn't committed to, maybe even about cases where they'd agree the specks win (e.g., if the "speck" were upgraded to a migraine, is that still a speck?). Then a disagreements table where each remaining point of contention is phrased in wording the *other* side would sign off on, which is the part these two have never managed. Then the cruxes — the subquestions such that settling one would actually move someone — and for each crux, a concrete test case or argument both agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. A few variants I'd like them forced to confront: the intrapersonal version (one unlucky person getting all 3^^^3 specks spread over an immortal lifetime — does the Thresholder accept *that* aggregation, and if so why does it change when the specks go to different people?); the bounded version (a speck for every human alive today — does anyone still pick the torture there, and if not, at what multiplier does it flip?); and the disaggregation worry (if you can't aggregate specks up, can you dis-aggregate a torture down — trade one 50-year torture for two 25-year ones, four 12.5-year ones, and so on until the difference between the camps vanishes or explodes?).

Two more things. First, if you think part of their fight is merely verbal — e.g., they mean different things by "worse" (total badness of the outcome vs. what a virtuous agent may choose to do, or something about how they individuate events) — say which part and why, since I suspect there's some of that but I cant quite pin it down. Second, dont have them wimp out into vague compromise language: I want the residual disagreement made exact enough that I could tell, five years from now, whether either of them actually moved. You can leave their names as I've given them. And feel free to tell me if one of my variants quietly begs the question against one side — that sort of thing is exactly why Im asking for outside help instead of drafting it myself, I'm too fond of the Aggregator's summation habits to be trusted.
