Is there a name for this? I keep noticing cases where a group of people manages to do something that looks genuinely goal-directed -- it responds to setbacks, adapts, converges on a good solution -- even though nobody aims at the goal and there was never a moment where anyone decided, alone or in a meeting, that this is what "we" should do. Students wear a diagonal path across a quad, the university fences it off, and within a couple weeks the crowd has found the next best diagonal, no planning involved. A research field drains out of a program over a decade: each scientist left for their own local reasons, nobody ever declared the thing dead, but the field as a whole behaves like it is trying to abandon it -- a failed replication speeds up the exit, a big success slows it down. Its clearly not joint action like two people carrying a table (no shared intention, half the participants arent even aware they're participating), and its not a corporation voting on a pivot either. But it's not a dumb aggregate like average height -- there's something functional and pressure-responsive about it. The closest things I know of are invisible-hand style spontaneous order and maybe swarm intelligence, but those feel like descriptions of a mechanism, not of the goal-directedness itself. So: does this have an established name, in collective intentionality work or anywhere else, and how does it relate to the standard stuff on shared intention and group agents? If nothing fits exactly, coin a term and give me a real definition. I want the carving -- what exactly the phenomenon is, what its not, and where the edges are. Like, is a market bubble an instance? Is a Wikipedia article? Is a standing ovation, once the first three people clap?
