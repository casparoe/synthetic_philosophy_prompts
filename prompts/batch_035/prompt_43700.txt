Marxist states professed the doctrine that the economic base determines the political, legal, religious, and cultural superstructure, which merely reflects it — yet in practice they treated the superstructure as both a lever and a standing threat. They ran mass literacy campaigns, sent agitprop trains through the countryside, mandated an official aesthetic, rewrote textbooks, and persecuted churches and disapproved art, as though doctrines left unmanaged could bring the whole system down. Engels saw the tension early: in a letter of September 1890 to Joseph Bloch, he insisted that "the ultimately determining element in history is the production and reproduction of real life," while conceding that the elements of the superstructure "also exercise their influence upon the course of the historical struggles and in many cases preponderate in determining their form." What view of the base–superstructure relation does the revolutionary state's conduct presuppose — that the base fixes only a long-run equilibrium, leaving the short run open to deliberate intervention, or that ideas have causal power outright? Reconstruct the implicit theory, and say whether Engels's qualification actually licenses the practice or instead licenses nearly anything, so that a doctrine which cannot forbid a propaganda ministry can no longer distinguish itself from the idealism it opposed. Then say where the practice is philosophically underdetermined: does it rest on any base–superstructure metaphysics at all, or only on the instrumental thought, available to any ruling class, that persuasion is cheaper than force? Finally, tell me what would have to change in the conduct of such a state if the presupposed view were replaced by strict determination, on which the superstructure tracks the base automatically and every ruble spent on shaping consciousness is wasted.
