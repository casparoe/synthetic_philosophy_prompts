Write the joint statement that two economists could sign after a year-long adversarial collaboration on price controls. One is broadly skeptical of price controls and inclined toward market-based solutions; the other is broadly supportive of the two policies at issue and sees them as instruments of distributive justice. They have argued past each other in public for years, and this document is what a good-faith collaboration between them should produce.

The occasion for the collaboration is a concrete proposal before a mid-sized city council: (1) a local minimum wage set at roughly 60 percent of the area median wage, phased in over three years, with a lower training wage for the first six months and a tip credit; and (2) a cap on annual rent increases for sitting tenants at the rate of inflation, with rents free to reset when a unit changes tenants, and new construction exempted from the cap for its first twenty years. The city has restrictive zoning and a rental vacancy rate below four percent. Note that these design details matter: the collaboration should treat the proposal as designed, not as a generic price ceiling or floor, and both signatories should agree on at least one claim about how the vacancy-reset and new-construction exemptions change the analysis relative to a hard cap.

The joint statement should contain the following:

1. A numbered section of agreed claims. Include claims about what the standard competitive model implies as a matter of theory (as distinct from what it predicts about this city), about incidence, about which questions are positive and which normative, and about the interaction between these policies and pre-existing regulations — both should be able to sign something on whether capping rents where zoning already restricts supply is a different question than capping rents in a market where entry is easy. Include at least one claim each economist concedes to the other that they would not have written before the collaboration.

2. A disagreements table. Each row should contain a single disagreement, stated twice: once in wording the skeptical economist would accept as fair to his view, and once in wording the supportive economist would accept as fair to hers. Flag which disagreements are substantive and which are at least partly verbal — for instance, whether a minimum wage should be modeled as a price floor at all if employers have monopsony power or if wages are set by bilateral bargaining, and whether a cap on sitting tenants' rents is better described as a price ceiling or as a transfer from landlords' quasi-rents.

3. A cruxes section. Identify the sub-questions such that settling one would move at least one signatory, including at least one empirical crux (such as the elasticity of demand for low-wage labor in a labor market of this size, or the effect of sitting-tenant caps on future construction through landlords' expectations of eventual vacancy resets) and at least one normative crux (such as whether transfers of a given size to low-wage workers or sitting tenants can justify efficiency losses of a given size, or whether some labor or housing transactions are objectionable as exploitation in a sense that goes beyond their terms). For each crux, describe a concrete piece of evidence, argument, or observed outcome both would agree bears on it, and record each side's prediction in advance.

4. A short closing section stating what both would count as this policy succeeding and what they would count as it failing, by criteria each accepts — specify thresholds where possible rather than vague qualitative outcomes.

Constraints: do not cite specific studies, name researchers, or include a bibliography; where evidence is relevant, describe the kind of evidence and what it would show. Throughout the document, distinguish claims that are settled among economists (stating, where relevant, that they are settled only conditionally on a model's assumptions) from claims that are genuinely contested, and do not present either signatory's contested view as the consensus of the field. Where the two sides' disagreement turns out to reduce to different uses of a term, say so rather than papering over it.
