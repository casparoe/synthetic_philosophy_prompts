I've gone down a rabbit hole (pun intended, you'll see) of perception stuff after a friend sent me a video about the color phi thing. The setup: a green dot flashes, then a moment later a red dot flashes a short distance off, and instead of two dots you see one dot gliding across and turning from green to red partway along. The wierd part is the timing — the dot seems to have already started changing color before the red one ever shows up, which is impossible. So either my brain held the whole little experience back until the red flash arrived, and only then screened it to me complete, or it screened it as it went and my memory rewrote the tape afterwards, like a ministry fixing old newspapers. Those are the only two stories I can come up with, and I can't tell which one happened, or even whether the question makes sense.

Here are some more cases with my gut verdicts, because I'm trying to figure out if there's a rule underneath all this. Same two dots but a full second apart: then I definately just experienced a green dot sitting there, and then a red dot popping up somewhere else — no motion, no reach-back, the experience got fixed as it went. So my gut says the reach-back trick only works on short timescales. But then there's the cutaneous rabbit thing (my friend did it to me after the same video): a couple of light taps on the wrist, then one near the elbow a moment later, and you feel distinct little hops marching up your forearm at spots nobody touched — and they feel like they happened right then, at their felt times, not like something pasted in afterwards. Same with the stopped-clock thing, where you glance up at a clock and the second hand seems hung up for too long before it ticks: that long first second felt long while it was happening, I'd swear, not like a memory that got stretched later. And yet with dreams I go the other way — I'll wake from a long dream whose ending obviously got built around whatever noise woke me, and I'm fine saying the dream never really had that ending, my memory just stitched it on. But if the end result looks identical either way, what makes the dream case a memory trick and the clock case a reach-back?

My tentative rule is something like: inside a few hundred milliseconds the brain can hold the tape and fill it in once later info arrives; past that, experience gets fixed as it goes and only memory can lie. But this rule bugs me. During the gap in the phi thing, was I having an experience or not? If the whole thing was only settled after the red flash, it's like the earlier stretch of experience happened at no particular time, or before the information it was about even existed, and that sounds incoherent to me. The rabbit verdict doesn't obviously fit either, since there I'm saying the hops were really there at their felt times — fixed early, inside a short window. So, what I want to know: is there a principle that captures all my verdicts? If not, which ones would you throw out, and why those? And what does the surviving principle say about a case I haven't given you — say, someone mumbles the middle of a sentence and the last word tells me what the mumbled word must have been, and afterwards it feels like I heard it clearly at the time — is that perception reaching back, or memory covering for it? I don't know any of the technical terms for this stuff, so plain English please.
