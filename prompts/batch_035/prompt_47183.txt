So I went down a metaphysics rabbit hole about dispositions (fragility, solubility, being irritable, that sort of thing) and it turns out my gut has opinions that don't obviously hang together. I wrote down my verdicts on a bunch of cases and I want to know if there's one principle that captures all of them, or whether I'm just being inconsistent. Here are my calls:

- A wine glass packed in foam and bubble wrap, inside a padded box, inside a safe: still fragile. However much stuff is between the glass and a hard floor, it doesn't stop being fragile.
- A vase sitting on a pedestal next to a very fast robot that's programmed to catch it the instant anything so much as grazes it: NOT brittle. And this bugs me, because structurally this feels like the foam case — something standing between the glass and shattering — but my gut gives opposite answers.
- That guy who's irascible — hair trigger, always about to blow up — but his friends secretly dose him with a drug that removes his short temper the exact moment he starts getting angry: I flip-flop. Part of me says he's still irascible (the temper is in him, the drug is outside interference), part of me says a man who can never actually blow up just isn't irascible anymore.
- A match in a room where a furnace constantly burns off all the oxygen: still flammable. The match hasn't changed.
- A sleeping pill that would make you drowsy, except you also took a stimulant that completely blocks it: I say the pill still has its sedative power, it's just being defeated.
- A person on a medication that would make them violently ill if they drank, so they don't drink: they still have the ability to drink, obviously. (This one feels easy but I'm including it because I suspect it's secretly the same shape as the others.)

My tentative idea is something like: things keep their dispositions when the blocker is external to them, and lose them when the block gets built in. The foam is outside the glass, so the glass stays fragile. But the robot is outside the vase too, and I said the vase isn't brittle, so that principle is already in trouble.

So: is there a single account of when something has a disposition that delivers my verdicts? I suspect the answer has something to do with the difference between something that merely stops the manifestation from happening versus something that would remove the disposition itself, but I can't draw that line without asking which side of it the angry guy's drug is on — it both blocks the outburst and undoes the temper. Please give me the principle (or principles, if you think I'm secretly running two), tell me what it predicts for cases I haven't listed — like an aspirin swallowed alongside a drug that neutralizes it, or salt in a solution that's already saturated — and be honest about which of my verdicts you'd throw out rather than rescue. Also the flip-flopper: if you can settle the irascible guy one way or the other, I'd love to know what settles him.
