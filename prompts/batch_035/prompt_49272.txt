My friend and me have been doing this thing for philosophy club where we make up cases and she gives verdicts on whether the person in the case *knows* the thing. Her track record so far: you read a thermometer and get the right temp, but you know the thermometer is broken (it's right by luck) — she says no knowledge; you clearly see the science building on fire but some random guy with zero credentials tells you its just a projection, and it really is a fire — she says you know; you take a drug you know makes 90% of people hallucinate a yellow bird, you see one, and there really is one — no knowledge; and there's actually misleading evidence sitting in a drawer somewhere that nobody ever finds — you still know. Next week she's ruling on this one: your optometrist tells you that today your color vision is only 70% reliable, you look at a card, and you correctly call it red. I want you to answer as exactly 5 numbered points, one or two sentences each, no intro or conclusion: (1) the candidate rules she could be running that fit all four of her earlier verdicts, (2) what each of those rules predicts for the optometrist case, (3) your best guess at her verdict and why, (4) which of her earlier cases is the closest analog, and (5) a new case (make one up) that would best tell the candidate rules apart.
