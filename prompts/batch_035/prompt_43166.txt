Two colleagues of mine have agreed, after years of talking past each other, to write an adversarial-collaboration statement on immigration, and I've been asked to draft it. One holds that immigration restrictions are a serious rights violation — she says the claimed right to exclude amounts to a right to keep people imprisoned in their countries of birth. The other holds that a political community's right to exclude outsiders is a straightforward extension of its right to self-determination, no different in kind from an association's right to refuse new members. Draft the joint statement they should be able to sign. It should contain: the claims both could accept, including some non-trivial ones — about the difference between refusing would-be members and deporting long-term residents, about what restrictions short of exclusion (visa caps, point systems, delay) might legitimately achieve, and about the conditions under which either side would grant exclusion becomes impermissible (famine at the border, etc.); the residual disagreements, each phrased so that its own side would accept the wording — for example, whether self-determination includes control over membership specifically or only over the community's political character, and whether exclusion wrongs those excluded or merely fails to benefit them; the cruxes, one of which I suspect is whether the right to exclude scales — whether what may be done to a single would-be entrant may also be done to a million, or whether numbers change the moral arithmetic in a way other rights don't; and, for each crux, a case, argument, or policy scenario both would agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. If any part of their dispute strikes you as verbal rather than substantive, the statement should say which part and why — both have said they would rather discover that than keep arguing. Use a numbered agreed-claims section followed by a disagreements table. Finally, since I have to send this back to two people who each know the literature far better than I do, flag the claims or formulations you are least confident they would actually endorse as written, and say where you remain unsure whether a disagreement is genuine or merely a difference of words.
