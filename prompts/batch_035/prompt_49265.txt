I lead a small team fine-tuning a language model for competition math and code, and I have two weeks to decide how to spend a fixed labeling budget. For outcome labels I'm set: final answers can be checked automatically and essentially for free, so I can generate as many of those as compute allows. For process labels I have two options, both unappealing — human annotators grading each reasoning step against a rubric (expensive, slow, and noisy about what even counts as a "good" step), or a stronger model acting as a judge of intermediate steps (cheap and dense, but I've watched it reward reasoning that merely mimics its own stylistic tics, and I can't verify whether a step-level label is tracking the actual cause of the final answer rather than surface plausibility).

One teammate argues that outcome-only supervision will let the model learn spurious shortcuts and produce right answers for wrong reasons — including fluent reasoning chains that are fabricated after the fact rather than genuinely driving the solution — so supervising the process is the only way to know we're getting correctness for the right reasons. Another argues that outcome supervision at scale discovers reasoning strategies better than anything a rubric or a judge could encode, that rubrics mostly launder the judge's preferences into our model, and that a procedure that reliably yields correct answers just is good reasoning for our purposes; anything more is asking the data to certify something no labeler can actually see.

Before I commit: what is the real disagreement here, and which parts of it can be settled by evidence we could gather versus which are genuinely philosophical? Is it about whether correct reasoning is constitutive of mathematical correctness or merely instrumentally valuable, about which failure mode (silent shortcut learning versus bias laundering from the process judge) is more damaging and harder to detect downstream, or about something else entirely? And is there a principled middle path, or does any hybrid scheme just inherit the weaknesses of both while giving us a hyperparameter to argue about?
