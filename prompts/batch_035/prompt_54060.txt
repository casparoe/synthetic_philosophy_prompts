quick context: I'm a PhD student in philosophy of mind, and for two years I've been TAing the semantics course in a computational linguistics master's program. their usual commencement speaker cancelled on them last week, and somehow the job fell to the grad student who happened to be around. so I need a ~5 minute commencement address, and I want it built around the thing Dennett keeps hammering on about Darwin and Turing — competence without comprehension. Darwin's version: evolution assembled eyes and wings and, eventually, us, with nobody understanding anything along the way; nothing had to grasp aerodynamics to build a bird. Turing's version: instead of trying to say what understanding is, you build something that does what understanders do and see how far that gets you. the catch is the room. the graduates mostly just wrote theses about getting LLMs to do things, and they will catch every sloppy statement of the idea. but the back rows are parents and grandparents, some of whom think chatgpt is basically a person already, and at least one of the faculty (formal semantics, old school) thinks deep learning is a fad thats almost over. I want the inversion to do real structural work in the speech, not just get name-dropped in the opening paragraph. specifically, I want the advice part of the address — the part where commencement speeches normally say garbage like follow your passion — to come out of the inversion itself: the honest lesson for these students is that they will spend their careers being competent before they comprehend, in their models, their jobs, and probably their lives, and that this isn't a bug, it's how everything from evolution to debugging actually works. so give them that straight, but make it land as encouraging rather than deflating, and make sure the family members who have never trained anything in their lives can follow at least the Darwin half without any technical setup. one more thing: no quotes and no citing people like it's a paper, it's a speech — paraphrase freely. they also asked for something at least a little funny, and I'd rather the humor come from the inversion itself than from generic grad school jokes, so keep those seperate.
