and here's the student's answer:

"Kant's notorious position on the murderer at the door comes from his 1797 essay 'On a supposed right to lie from philanthropy', a reply to Benjamin Constant. Constant had argued that truthfulness is a duty only towards those who have a right to the truth, and no one can have a right to a truth that harms them. Since the murderer at the door has no right to my answer, lying to him should be permissible. Kant's reply is that truthfulness in statements one cannot avoid making is a duty owed to humanity in general, so a lie wrongs humanity even when it happens to benefit the particular person addressed.

The underlying argument, though, is simply the Categorical Imperative. The maxim of lying to the would-be murderer in order to save a life cannot be willed as a universal law, because a world in which everyone lied whenever lying saved a life is a world in which the practice of assertion has collapsed: no one would believe anyone, and the lie could not achieve its purpose. As Korsgaard shows in her 1986 paper on the right to lie, the maxim therefore fails the universalisability test, and this is what settles the case: the CI directly forbids the lie to the murderer.

Kant adds a striking claim about responsibility. If you lie, and unbeknownst to you the friend has slipped out the back and meets the murderer on the road, you are the author of the harm; whereas if you tell the truth and the murderer then finds the friend anyway, you are not the author of what follows. This asymmetry is meant to show that the consequences of lying fall on you, while the consequences of truthfulness do not.

Constant's position, by contrast, collapses into consequentialism: since he permits lying whenever it saves a life, he is really judging duties by their outcomes.

So the prohibition on lying survives the murderer case: the universalisability test forbids the lie, and the responsibility argument shows why lying is more dangerous than it looks. The only cost is Kant's apparent willingness to let the friend die, but as he would say, duty is not suspended by evil."
