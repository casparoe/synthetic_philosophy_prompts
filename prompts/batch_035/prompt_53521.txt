Been covering science and misinformation for a few years now, and somewhere along the way I picked up gut verdicts about which communities are epistemically broken, and I'd like to know if theres actually a principle behind them. The anti-vax facebook group that explains away every contradicting study as pharma shilling: echo chamber, obviously. My suburb where everyone happens to vote the same and nobody ever meets a disbeliever: that feels like more of a bubble — missing voices, not explained-away voices. The niche research subfield I did a story on, where the scientists know the rival literature inside out but have a whole folklore about why the other lab's methods are hopelessly confounded: that one still feels chambered to me even though they're extremely well informed and engage constantly with the other side. Same with the debate forum where people read opposing arguments all day but only ever to dunk on them. Yet I also think the guy who blocked all his disagreeing friends last election is somehow less chambered than his brother who still follows opponents but believes every single one of them is lying for money — which makes me suspect exposure isn't doing the work I assumed. Is there one principle that captures all these verdicts, or do some have to go? And if you can state one, what does it predict for the case I genuinely can't decide: a recommender that steadily ramps up opposing content but always frames it so it loses the argument?
