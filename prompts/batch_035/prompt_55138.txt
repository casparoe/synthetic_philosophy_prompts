I sit on the standards council of a certification program for farmed seafood; our welfare standard currently covers only finfish. A pending proposal would extend it to farmed whiteleg shrimp, with two new requirements: effective stunning before slaughter (shrimp are at present typically killed in ice slurry or fresh water, and there is evidence they remain responsive for a substantial period), and a prohibition on eyestalk ablation of breeding females, a routine procedure performed to boost egg production. Proponents on the council point to the behavioral and physiological evidence that decapods are capable of something worth protecting — nociceptors, protective behavior, willingness to trade off injury avoidance against other goods — and to a recent government-commissioned review concluding that decapods should be treated as sentient. Opponents reply that the evidence does not establish pain proper rather than mere nociception, that effective stunning technology for shrimp is untested at commercial scale, and that a costly standard small producers cannot meet will simply push them out of the program, leaving those animals with no protection at all. Draft the memo I will circulate before the council votes: it must give a clear recommendation — adopt, reject, or adopt with conditions such as a phase-in or provisional standard — engage seriously with the strongest argument on the losing side, and respond directly to the worry that a standard no one meets does no good. Please present the memo as exactly five numbered points, each a sentence or two, with no introduction or conclusion.
