I've been reading LessWrong-adjacent discussions of personal identity for over a decade, and I've finally tabulated my gut verdicts on survival cases, most of them descended from Parfit's teletransporter. I'd like your help extracting a principle from them, because I can't make them cohere.

My verdicts, such as they are:

1. A scanner records every detail of my brain, disintegrates my body on Earth, and builds an exact replica on Mars. Gut says: I survive. This is just transport with extra steps.
2. The same scan, but the Earth body is left alive. Now the Mars person is not me; the person standing on Earth is. If both claim my name and my bank account, the Earth one wins.
3. My neurons are replaced one at a time by functional silicon duplicates over six months, with no interruption of consciousness. Gut says: I survive. If anything I barely care.
4. The same replacement done in fifteen minutes under general anaesthesia. This should be equivalent to case 3 on any view I can articulate, yet my confidence that I survive drops sharply, which annoys me.
5. My grandmother's memories and personality were erased by dementia over a decade, yet she clearly survived throughout; she was right there. But if psychological continuity is what constitutes survival, it's unclear what remained to survive.
6. A man dies. A year later, an organisation reconstructs a perfect copy of his pre-death brain-state from meticulous records and runs it. Gut says: he did not survive; he died, and something new began. But the pattern resumed just as it does after a night's sleep, and in case 1 the pattern also merely resumes after a gap. I cannot hold 1, 6, and "survival is pattern-resumption" together.

My tentative principle: survival is continuity of the pattern as an ongoing causal process, not mere preservation of the pattern information. This seems to handle 1 versus 6, and maybe 2, but I can't see how it accommodates 5 without stretching "causal process" beyond recognition, and it says nothing about why 4 frightens me more than 3.

So: is there a principle, stated precisely, that captures these verdicts, or as many as possible? Please say which clause is motivated by which case, which of my verdicts any credible principle would have to overturn, and what the principle predicts for two cases I haven't listed: (a) the non-destructive scan of case 2, where the Earth body is then destroyed in an accident one hour later, before either body has had any new experiences; and (b) a branch in which the machine announces, before scanning, that the Earth body will be painlessly terminated after a week of ordinary life.

Please respond in British English.
