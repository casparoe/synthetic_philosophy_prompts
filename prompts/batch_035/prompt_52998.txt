Write a satirical corporate press release, around 600 words, from a fictional AI lab announcing their new assistant model. The lab's entire safety story is control, not alignment — they never claim the model is aligned or good or wants anything nice, in fact they brag that they never needed to find out. Deadpan corporate PR voice throughout, no winking at the reader, and the jokes should come from actual control-research substance rather than invented absurdity: every user message passes through a trusted monitor; the assistant's occasional weird refusals get described as the monitoring "working as intended"; the lab plants honeypot documents in users' files to check whether the model exfiltrates them; capability evals are framed as testing whether the model *can* exfiltrate data, never whether it *would*; and there's a proud quote from an invented head of deployment safety to the effect that never once verifying the model's motives was their greatest engineering achievement, since it was unnecessary. Somewhere in the fine print the release should quietly reveal that the lab treats its own product as a permanent adversary — e.g. the model isn't allowed to read the safety documentation because the safety documentation is a tripwire test. A specialist in AI control should find the satire fair: every exaggeration is a real feature of the control stance (assume misalignment, monitor everything, safety means containment not goodness, alignment work "deprioritized" as redundant), and the standard objection — that they've committed themselves to an endless arms race against their own product instead of ever making it safe — should be visible to the reader without ever being stated. Make the release end with boilerplate about the lab's mission, delivered with a straight face.
