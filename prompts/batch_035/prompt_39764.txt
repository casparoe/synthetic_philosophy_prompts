Construct a thought experiment about origin essentialism — the Kripkean thesis that a given person could not have developed from a different sperm and egg — built around tetragametic chimerism, the documented phenomenon in which two fertilized eggs fuse early in development and grow into a single person carrying cells from both original zygotes. The case is meant to test whether origin essentialism is one thesis or several, so it must force apart the three most natural precisifications: origin as the gamete pair, origin as the zygote, and origin as whatever biological individual resulted, however it came to be. Constraints: keep the scenario realistic — a fertility clinic or an ordinary pregnancy, no cloning vats, teletransporters, or brain swaps; do not stipulate any modal verdicts inside the scenario (the verdicts must fall out of the theories, not be written into the setup); and keep the scenario itself to one paragraph. After the scenario, show that it meets the specifications: for each of the three precisifications, say which alternative origins it counts your chimera as possibly having and which it rules out; note the mirror-image pressure from monozygotic twinning (one zygote, two people) and say whether your case actually needs that addition or stands on the fusion alone; identify exactly which feature of chimerism is doing the philosophical work; and then explain how a committed gamete-origin essentialist would amend the view to survive the case, and what that amendment costs her elsewhere. Conclude with a verdict: does the case refute origin essentialism, or only reveal that its defenders have never been arguing for a single claim?
