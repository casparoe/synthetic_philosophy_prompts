I've been asked to write a short position paper on the singleton hypothesis for a seminar on existential risk, and before drafting it I'd like a consistency audit of what I actually believe:

(1) Racing dynamics are the core danger: labs and states competing toward transformative AI will cut corners on safety for structural reasons — not because anyone is stupid or evil — and the only genuine fix is coordination strong enough to remove the race altogether, which in the limit means some form of global singleton. I take the pro-singleton argument seriously.

(2) Concentrated and unaccountable power reliably goes bad over time. I regard this as close to a law of political psychology, and it makes me a decentralist in ordinary politics: enthusiastic about antitrust, sympathetic to devolution, and hostile to world-government proposals of every flavor, including the benevolent-sounding ones.

(3) In the medium term I favor international compute governance — licensing of frontier training runs, monitoring of large clusters, frontier capabilities held by a small number of vetted actors — and I'd defend this even though I can see it amounts to deliberately building a partial singleton by hand.

(4) The worst outcome of AI development is not extinction but premature value lock-in: a stable singleton that entrenches our parochial, half-worked-out values and permanently closes off moral progress.

(5) Moral progress is real, and future people, given room, will probably arrive at better values than ours; we therefore have a duty not to hard-code our current conclusions about the good into any durable system.

(6) Whatever regime eventually emerges should permanently entrench a few protections — perhaps a ban on existential-risk experimentation without something like global consent, perhaps a guaranteed right of exit — written so that they cannot be repealed, even by overwhelming majorities later on.

Please audit this set. Which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how severe is each? For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what that would cost me elsewhere in the list. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. I don't know which commitment I'd give up — that's why I'm asking — so don't assume I have a favorite.
