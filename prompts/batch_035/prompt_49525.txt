I work in live sports broadcast — the truck side, not the studio side — so my daily vocabulary is stuff like the switcher, the replay op, the EVS server, the 7-second profanity delay, clean feed vs dirty feed, program out, and the as-run log that tells the client exactly what went to air and when. A friend of mine who reads a lot of philosophy got me into a debate about Dennett's color phi thing, and I want to see how it maps onto what I actually do for a living.

As she explained it: you flash a red dot on a screen, then a blue dot a bit to the side, and people report seeing a single dot moving across, changing color partway through — even though at the moment the red dot was up, the brain couldn't have known a blue dot was coming. Dennett says there are two ways to describe what happened. The Orwellian story: the subject really did experience it one way, and the record got rewritten afterward, like history being retroactively edited. The Stalinesque story: the edit happens before the content ever reaches consciousness, the brain staging something that never "aired" in real time. And Dennett's own move is to say the whole question is ill-posed — there's no determinate fact of the matter, because there's no single place and time where the content crosses into consciousness in the first place.

That last part sounded crazy to me, because in my world there is ALWAYS an answer. The delay buffer is a physical thing. Either that frame sat in the buffer or it didn't. If a producer kills a replay in the delay line, that's Stalinesque, full stop, and the as-run log will show it. If something goes out live and the only thing that gets changed is what's in the server afterwards, that's Orwellian, and the log shows that too. Determinate facts everywhere. So either Dennett is missing something obvious, or the brain lacks something my truck has, and I want to know which.
