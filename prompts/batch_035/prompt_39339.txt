phd student in negotiation research here, and i do side work with our university's ombuds office mediating faculty disputes. I've been reading about safe Pareto improvements — the idea that when two parties are about to burn value fighting (litigation, departmental warfare over a hire, months of committee meetings), there's often a deal like flipping a weighted coin between the two outcomes that both would accept if they could commit, since the fight destroys value neither of them gets. The ombudspeople I work with basically do this by gut, and I want to give them something better.

Can you write a decision procedure they can actually run in a twenty minute mediation session? Constraints: the parties wont share their private valuations, the ombudsperson has no enforcement power, and it has to handle cases where the disputed thing isn't a divisible resource — a coin flip over who presents the joint work at a conference is a different problem from splitting lab space. The procedure should say which questions to ask in what order, what counts as a sufficient answer to each, when to stop with "no SPI here, just help them negotiate," and where the honest answer is that a value judgment can't be procedure'd away — for instance whether it's even okay to offer people a lottery over something they think shouldn't be left to chance. Then run it on two cases: two labs burning months fighting over time slots on a shared sequencer, and a department deadlocked over whether to hire in subfield A or B, where the coin-flip-looking move just hides the fight rather than dissolving it. Also tell me what the procedure can and can't do about the failure mode where a deal that's safe on paper gets unwound anyway because one side quietly pays the other to renege.

Lead with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give the reasoning, no preamble and dont restate my question back at me.
