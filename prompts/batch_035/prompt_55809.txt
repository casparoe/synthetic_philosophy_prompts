My best friend and I have argued for years about when people deserve blame, and over that time she has settled verdicts on a long series of made-up examples. She claims her rule is the simple one — no blame unless the person could have done otherwise — though I've noticed her rulings don't always obey it. Her track record so far:

1. A man votes for a crooked candidate entirely on his own; a neuroscientist, secretly monitoring him, would have overridden his choice at the first sign of hesitation, but never had to. She blames him.
2. A bank teller hands over the vault keys at gunpoint. No blame.
3. A woman, without knowing it, is given a drug that makes her want to ruin a colleague's career, and she does. No blame.
4. Someone lies on a job application, under no pressure whatsoever. Blame.
5. Someone falsifies a report because their boss threatened to dock fifty dollars from their pay. Blame.

A new example has now come up between us. Dana was raised from infancy inside a closed group that indoctrinated her into its cause before she was old enough to evaluate anything. She has since left, has seen the outside world, has examined what she was taught with every resource available to her, and endorses it wholeheartedly — she says it is more truly hers than anything else about her. No one threatens her, and she can walk away at any time. She betrays a stranger to protect the group.

Predict her verdict on Dana. What rule is she actually running, given that her stated rule already collides with her first ruling? Which other rules fit her track record just as well, and what does each of them say about Dana? Which of her earlier rulings is the closest analog here? And if I want to find out which rule she is really running, which further example should I put to her — one where the candidate rules come apart?
