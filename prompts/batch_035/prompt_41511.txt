I am defending my dissertation in six weeks, and I would like a question-period preparation sheet. The thesis I will defend is a localized dialetheism: the semantic paradoxes (the Liar, Curry-style conditionals, truth-predicate iterations) force us to accept that some sentences in a narrowly circumscribed semantic fragment of the language are both true and false, while the consequence relation governing that fragment is paraconsistent rather than explosive, so the contradictions do not spread. My argumentative route has three steps I care about defending as a package: first, that the Liar reasoning is not a defective argument to be diagnosed away but a sound argument to a conclusion we find intolerable only because we have assumed explosion; second, that inconsistency can be quarantined by subject matter rather than by weakening the logic globally — ordinary mathematics and science simply never encounter the predicates that generate the contradictions; and third, a further twist of my own, that the quarantine does not need a "classical recapture" story for the non-semantic part of the language, because the two truth-like notions involved (a transparent truth predicate that obeys the T-schema unrestrictedly, and an operator-style notion used for asserting and generalizing) come apart exactly at the points where classical recapture is usually invoked. On my view, once the split is made, there is nothing left to recapture.

The committee is the sort of department where classical first-order logic is taught as simply "logic": four of the five members work in mainstream metaphysics and philosophy of science and use reductio arguments daily, and the fifth is a proof theorist who thinks about structural proof rules for a living and has no patience for any logic that is not well-behaved with respect to a consequence relation she can actually work with. None of them has any sympathy for dialetheism as such; the metaphysicians in particular are apt to hear "some contradictions are true" as a category error rather than a claim. But they are fair, and one of the metaphysicians has a longstanding interest in the normativity of belief and will likely press on what it could even mean to accept a contradiction.

Please prepare my Q&A prep sheet accordingly: the eight or so objections I am actually going to get from this specific committee, not the objections I would get from a conference of paraconsistent logicians — ordered by how much damage each would do to the defense, each phrased in the idiom that member would actually use (the proof theorist's objections should sound like a proof theorist's, the metaphysicians' like metaphysicians'), each with a reply I can deliver in about two minutes, and a note on where that reply is soft. Include the question I am hoping nobody asks. Since my quarantine story turns on the two truth notions coming apart, please also flag any objection that targets the split itself rather than the dialetheism, as those are the ones my own committee may not think to raise but that I should be ready for anyway.

One further request. I do not want a pep sheet: where you judge an objection to be more damaging than my available reply, say so plainly. For each objection, tell me what would change your assessment of whether my reply works — which premise of my three-step package, if shown to fail, would collapse that reply, and where in your own reasoning about the audience you are least confident, so that I know which parts of the prep sheet to stress-test with my advisor before the defense.
