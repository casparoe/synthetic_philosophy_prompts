The passage reads as follows:

"Amartya Sen, the Nobel economist, transformed the study of famine with his 1981 book, which analysed four catastrophes: the Irish famine of the 1840s, the Bengal famine of 1943 under British rule, Mao's Great Leap Forward, and the Ethiopian famines of the 1970s. Taking Bengal as his test case, he demonstrated that there was no general shortage of food — supplies in 1943 were no lower than in ordinary years — so the true cause was what he called an entitlement failure: because wartime inflation had carried the price of rice beyond their means, people simply could not afford to buy the food that was there. From this he established the general principle that famine is never a natural disaster but always a political crime. Famines occur only where governments face no consequences for allowing them, and his figures prove that no famine has ever taken place in a functioning democracy. The lesson for today is that when famine appears on our screens, the correct response is not to ship food but to hold the responsible governments to account in court, since the food is always there and the guilt always lies with the regime."
