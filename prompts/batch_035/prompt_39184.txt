I'm a software engineer with no philosophy training, but I've been reading up on the question of whether dreams are experiences, and I'd like a dialogue between the two sides so I can watch the actual argument play out. One character, M, follows Norman Malcolm: dream reports are not reports of experiences had during sleep, and the very idea that dreams are episodes occurring in real time while we sleep is a conceptual confusion — asking what the dream was like before the sleeper woke is not a question our dream-concept answers. The other, D, follows Daniel Dennett's 1976 paper "Are Dreams Experiences?": dreams are experiences, but their composition time is indeterminate — the narrative might be composed during sleep or written at the moment of waking, and nothing available in the report itself could distinguish the two. Please make both characters competent and make them actually respond to each other, with the argument driven by three specific pressure points: (1) the classic sleep-lab result that subjects awakened five versus fifteen minutes into REM report dream durations proportional to the elapsed time, which looks like evidence of real-time unfolding; (2) lucid dreamers who signal prearranged eye-movement patterns during REM, which looks like concurrent awareness and reportability, and which reportedly led even Dennett to concede ground later in his career; and (3) a thought experiment of mine, in the spirit of a closed-loop system I might build: suppose a device detects a nightmare mid-REM and injects a spoken word into the dreamer's auditory stream, and the dreamer later reports the word appearing at a specific point in the plot, with the device's timestamp matching. D should treat my device as a read-write probe that settles the timing question; M should push back that a match between the timestamp and the report no more proves when the experience occurred than a saved game file proves when it was played — and both should have to say precisely what in their view that experiment would or wouldn't establish. Don't let the dialogue end in agreement; instead, let them locate the exact residual disagreement once the empirical smoke clears — I suspect it bottoms out in what counts as a criterion for saying an experience occurred at all, and I'd like to see whether that's right or whether the dispute has more substance than that.
