I'm in a philosophy of science course and we just spent two weeks on Lakatos, and last friday the discussion section completely derailed into a fight between two of the grad students about whether string theory counts as a degenerating research programme. It went like this. (1) Maya said it's almost a textbook case: forty-plus years old, no novel prediction ever confirmed, and every empirical setback gets absorbed by adjusting the protective belt — supersymmetry doesn't show up at the LHC? raise the mass scale; the cosmological constant isn't zero? posit a landscape of 10^500 vacua. That's degenerating by Lakatos's own lights, all belt and no corroborated excess content. (2) Dan said the mapping matters and she's got it wrong: the hard core is something like "all fundamental physics is unified in a string/quantum-gravitational framework," not any particular claim about colliders, and mature programmes always patched their belts while they matured — Newton's programme carried the Mercury perihelion anomaly around for decades and still won, and Lakatos explicitly warned against instant rationality and said degenerating programmes can come back. (3) Maya said that's exactly the move Lakatos flagged in his Marxism example — endlessly reinterpreting the theory so nothing can ever count against it — and anyway the difference is Newton's belt eventually produced a novel prediction like Neptune, whereas string theory has drifted to a place where it can accommodate any observation in principle, which is worse than being wrong. (4) Dan countered that she's reading "novel fact" as temporally-new-prediction only, but that's a crude reading of Lakatos — the black hole entropy counting, where microstates in string theory reproduce the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy, counts as theoretical progressivity, and Weinberg actually predicted a small nonzero cosmological constant on anthropic grounds in the late 80s, years before the accelerating expansion was discovered. (5) Maya's last word was that math being internally consistent doesn't corroborate anything about *our* world, and that if you stretch "novel" to cover anything the theory later explains nicely, then every programme is progressive and the demarcation criterion does no work.
