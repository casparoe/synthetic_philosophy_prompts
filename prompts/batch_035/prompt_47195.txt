Quick background so we're synced. In a counterfactual mugging, a predictor flipped a fair coin before ever contacting you, and you only get approached in the losing branch: pay a small sum now, and had the coin landed the other way, a much larger sum would have been yours iff the predictor foresaw that you'd pay. Updateless reasoning pays. In XOR blackmail, a predictor who knows whether you have some dirt (termites, an embarrassing file, whatever) sends you a demand iff (you have the dirt XOR it predicted you'd pay), and paying does nothing at all about the dirt — updateless reasoning refuses. What I want to probe is whether updatelessness treats "which game am I even in?" uncertainty the way it treats ordinary risk — do the two verdicts just blend into an expected value, or does something weirder happen when your own policy is part of the mechanism?

The case to build: one agent, one envelope demanding $1000, and the agent cannot tell which structure generated it. Some honest outfit — a bookie with a decades-long verified track record, no sci-fi, no brain scans; their one superpower, predicting whether you'd pay this exact letter, you may treat as perfect — first predicts whether you'd pay. Then they flip a fair coin you never see. Heads: they send you the letter, and had the coin come up tails, a payoff B would land in your account iff they'd predicted you'd pay (the mugging leg). Tails: they send the same letter iff (you have the dirt XOR they predicted you'd pay), and paying buys nothing about the dirt (the blackmail leg). The letters are word-for-word identical, all settlements happen after you answer, so when the envelope arrives you know the protocol but not the coin, not the dirt, not their prediction.
