As a journalist who covers science and ideas, I've spent the last year writing pieces that lean heavily on structural explanation: climate outcomes from incentive gradients, epidemic curves from housing stock, populist surges from wage stagnation. That habit of mind has pushed me toward a fairly strong structuralist view of the Nazi case, and I'd like it stress-tested. My working position is that Weimar's collapse into a genocidal, expansionist dictatorship was overdetermined by structural conditions — the Treaty of Versailles settlement, the depression, the weakness of democratic legitimation in a young republic, the antisemitism embedded across the German and European right, the organized street violence, and the pre-existing machinery of racial hygiene and eastward-expansionist fantasy that predated Hitler by decades. On this view, Hitler was a trigger, not a cause: remove him and you get some authoritarian, antisemitic, revisionist catastrophe — perhaps a military junta, perhaps a Strasserite or nationalist-conservative regime — likely without the specific industrialized genocide, but not a functioning Weimar. The strongest version of my view holds that even the Holocaust was substantially determined by the interaction of wartime radicalization and the bureaucratic machinery already available, and that a different leader would have produced a different atrocity profile but a comparable one in kind, not just in scale. I know the intentionalist side of the historians' debate pushes back on exactly this, but I confess I've never really worked out what evidence would move me rather than just give me a rival narrative to hold alongside mine.

So tell me what would count against my position, sorted honestly by how decisive it would be. I want defeaters, not just objections: which kinds of evidence — about Hitler's specific and irreplaceable role, about the contingency of the July 1932-to-January 1933 sequence, about comparative cases like Mussolini's Italy after his removal, about what the July 20 plotters themselves intended, or about how the machinery of euthanasia and mass killing actually got built — should genuinely lower my confidence, and which merely add detail. Distinguish what a reasonable structuralist should concede as counting against the view from what a dogmatic one would explain away, and tell me if you think my position has quietly been qualified into unfalsifiability — whether the move where every bad historical outcome confirms the structure and every good one gets explained away is one my view is actually making. Finally, tell me plainly where your own reasoning in this response is most likely to fail and what argument or piece of evidence would flip you to a different verdict than the one you've given. Please keep the whole thing under 700 words; I need it tight enough to publish as the kernel of a column, so no throat-clearing and no literature review.
