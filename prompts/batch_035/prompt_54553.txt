I'm a retired spectroscopist who fell into philosophy a few years back, and the question I keep gnawing on is whether "better than" is transitive. You've got one camp -- the non-transitivists, call them -- building those spectrum cases where a long bout of torture seems better than a slightly longer bout of slightly milder torture, and you chain these down until you've got an afternoon of mild discomfort that's apparently better than the original torture. Then on the other side you have people (decision theorists mostly, plus some ethicists like Rachels' critics... though honestly Rachels is on the non-transitivist side, never mind) who insist that transitivity is constitutive of rational preference, and that any spectrum case must be sneakily changing the comparison relation along the way. Suppose these two camps agreed to run a proper adversarial collaboration. Write the joint statement they'd produce: a numbered section of claims both camps could sign (about the spectrum cases themselves, about what decision theory actually presupposes, about whether money pumps and Dutch-book style arguments bite here), then a disagreements table with each residual disagreement phrased so that its own side accepts the wording, the cruxes, and for each crux an argument or test case both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction written down in advance. If any part of their dispute strikes you as merely verbal -- people using "better than" for different relations, say -- flag which part and why. Finish with a machine-readable line of the form \remaining{N} where N is the number of substantive (non-verbal) disagreements left in your table. I'd rather have a short honest document than a long diplomatic one, keep it tight.
