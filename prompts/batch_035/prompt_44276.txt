I'm a second-year PhD student preparing a dissertation prospectus, and I want to apply for a nine-month dissertation fellowship with it. My topic is deflationism about truth, and my half-formed idea is this: deflationists claim that truth is not a substantive property and does no real explanatory work — it's just a device for expressing infinite conjunctions, generalizations, and the like. But the explanationist defense of scientific realism seems to need truth to be explanatorily powerful: the claim is that the truth of our theories (not each disquoted instance of them) explains their predictive success. My suspicion is that this apparent conflict partly rests on sliding between two different things people call "truth": a logical device for generalization, and a target property that inquiry aims at, that convergence between methods would track, and that explains why instruments built from our theories don't collapse. If that distinction is genuine, maybe deflationism survives the no-miracles argument intact; if it isn't, maybe deflationism is quietly committed to treating truth as substantive after all, and the minimalist should concede more than they usually do.
