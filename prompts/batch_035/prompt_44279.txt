Is there a proper name for this? I'm in a couple of online communities — one is a parenting forum that's very into a particular sleep-training philosophy, the other is a neighbourhood group fighting a specific local development. In both I've noticed the same thing and I want to know whether the people who study this stuff have a word for it.

Here's the thing: neither group avoids the other side's arguments. Everyone can recite them. The forum has a sticky post titled "what they say and why they're wrong," and in the neighbourhood group people do live reenactments of arguments with the developers' consultants, doing the voices. So it's not like nobody has heard the objections. But the versions we've all learned are slightly off — the other side's case as we know it is a bit stupider and a bit meaner than the case they actually make.

That's the setup. The part I'm really asking about is what happens when a genuine outsider shows up — a parent who sleep-trained and it went fine, or an actual consultant. What they say never matches the script, so it gets rounded to the nearest line in the script, and answered with the rehearsed rebuttal for that line. If they protest that's not their argument at all, the protest is also in the script, as a move ("they always deny it first"). So real people arrive and get processed as performances of characters we already know. And wierdly the group isn't hostile to outside sources as such — members will cheerfully admit the other side has some points — it's just that the only other side any of us has ever met is our own impression of them. You can't fix it by exposure either, because exposure is what got us here.

My friends just call this an echo chamber, but at this point that word covers everything from "my feed agrees with me" to "society," so I want something more precise. Specifically this configuration: the other side's arguments ARE encountered, but only ever in our own reenactment of them, so the encounter feels like engagement while never once being one. If the literature has an established term, tell me what it is, and how it differs from the nearby things I can think of — an echo chamber proper, a straw man (the script isn't really a straw man, it's more like a slightly degraded copy nobody believes they misread), or just being well-informed about your opponents. If there's no established term, say that plainly and propose one, with a definition that marks the edges: for example, does the phenomenon require the group to believe outsiders are lying or shilling? Mine mostly doesn't, which is exactly why I'm unsure it counts as an echo chamber at all. I'm not after a label to drop at parties — I want to know what the thing is and isn't.

Whatever you conclude, end with the following line, in exactly this form and with nothing after it:

Verdict: [one short phrase — either the established term, or "no established name — proposed: X"]
