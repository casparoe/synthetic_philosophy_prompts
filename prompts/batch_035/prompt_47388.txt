Been reading about Gelfand's tight vs. loose cultures stuff (the idea that some societies have really strict norms and punish deviance hard, while others are chill about it) ever since a LessWrong-adjacent post referenced it, and something keeps bugging me about the tight side. Concretely: in Germany, pedestrians will stand at a red crossing light at 3am on a completely empty street, and — this is the interesting part — they'll sometimes scold strangers who cross, even though there's literally zero cars for a kilometer in either direction. The scolding can't be about harm, since nothing bad could happen. And it's not just Germany: HOA boards in the US fining people for grass half an inch too tall, workplaces treating a minor dress code violation as if it were insubordination, that whole flavor of thing. My friends and I argued about this for like two hours and got nowhere so I'm outsourcing.

My question is: what view of rules and rule-following does a practice like that presuppose? The obvious candidate is something rule-consequentialist — the norm only does its job if it's (near-)universally followed, so each 3am crossing erodes it, and the scolding is costly punishment sustaining a public good. But I can also see a Kantian universalizability reading, or a Schelling-style coordination story where it's the predictability of the rule, not its content, that carries the value. Do these rival theories actually diverge anywhere in what they'd license — e.g. would one of them say scold only in public but let unobserved violations slide, since those can't erode anything, while another says the act itself is the problem? And is the practice even internally consistent? The same people who scold jaywalkers often selectively exempt themselves from other norms (fudging taxes, minor piracy, whatever), which seems awkward for the norm-maintenance story — does the practice presuppose one view in the scolding and a different one in the self-exemptions? Finally: what would actually have to change in the practice if a society consciously adopted a looser, more act-by-act view — would the norm just collapse, or would it get re-scoped into something like "cross when there are cars around, wait otherwise," and is that re-scoped rule even stable, or does it depend on the kind of judgment calls that the tight rule exists to avoid making?
