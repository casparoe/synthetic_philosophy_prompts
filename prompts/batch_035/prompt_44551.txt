I need some advice about a grad school rec letter. I agreed to write one for a student who is a nice kid but honestly pretty mediocre, and I don't want to tank his chances, so I wrote a letter where every sentence is true but carefully chosen — I praise his attendance and his enthusiasm for the material, mention he completed all the assigned readings, and leave out anything about ability. My chair says this is totally fine, and actually not even sneaky: everyone on an admissions committee knows how to read this kind of letter, so no one is being deceived — I've asserted nothing false, and the reader will come away with exactly the right conclusion, which happens to be the true one. So where's the harm? My partner thinks it's actually worse than just writing "he is brilliant," because a flat lie might get caught, while this does the damage quietly and lets me keep my hands clean, telling myself I never said one word that wasn't true. Now to top it off, one program sent a form that asks me point blank, yes or no, whether I'd rank him among the strongest students I've taught in ten years. So — is the coded letter really fine, is my partner right that it's somehow worse than outright lying, and what do I even do about that form?
