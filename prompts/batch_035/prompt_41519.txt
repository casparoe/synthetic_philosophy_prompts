so i've been going down a rabbit hole on wild animal suffering — the whole thing where most animals in nature are r-selected, have huge numbers of offspring, and nearly all of them die young, often badly, so it's genuinely unclear whether a thriving ecosystem is a welfare plus or a welfare disaster. write me a villanelle about this. one refrain should be a line about the forest as a nursery where almost nothing makes it to adulthood, and the other about the plowed field or the parking lot as a strange kind of mercy (fewer animals = maybe less total suffering). make the form do real work: a villanelle keeps hauling the same lines back, which is exactly what r-selection does — generation after generation repeating, each one mostly dying — so the repetition itself should feel like the biology. as the poem goes on the refrains should land differently each time they return, the way the same claim about nature sounds crueler or kinder depending on which side you just heard from. dont let the closing couplet pick a winner: put the two refrains next to each other and let them refuse to merge, since that's honestly where the argument sits. someone who knows the net-welfare-of-ecosystems debate should recognize the actual crux (are short lives worth living? does total suffering just scale with headcount?) and not just get generic "nature is harsh" vibes.
