I'm preparing a short position paper on the ethics of promising, and before circulating it I'd like a consistency audit of my starting commitments. I'd like you to take on the role of a professional moral philosopher doing this audit — please think carefully, and consider starting with a quick brainstorm of all the possible pairwise tensions among my commitments before deciding which ones are real.

Here is my position:

(1) The binding force of a promise comes from the social practice of promising, not from any act of self-binding. When I promise, I don't somehow legislate for my future self; rather, I invoke an institution that precedes and surrounds both promiser and promisee, and the obligation is a creature of that institution.

(2) That said, once a promise is made it binds me regardless of my own interests: I must keep it even if keeping it turns out to be very costly to me, and even if I could break it in a way the promisee would never detect.

(3) Promises extracted under coercion or desperate need do not bind. If a mugger makes me promise to bring him money next week, I have no obligation to show up, and I think the same is true, or nearly true, of promises made under severe economic duress.

(4) Promissory obligation is owed to the promisee in particular. The promisee can release me from a promise; a third party cannot. If I break my promise, the person with the standing to complain is the person I promised, and no one else as such.

(5) A promise to do something immoral generates no promissory reason at all. If I promise to help a friend conceal a crime, the promise is void from the start; it does not create an obligation that is then merely outweighed by other considerations.

(6) A pre-existing society is not necessary for promising. Two castaways on an island, with no one else around, could create the practice of promising between themselves and thereby genuinely bind one another.

My questions: Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension with each other, and which pairs only look like they conflict but dissolve on closer inspection? For each real tension, explain exactly what motivates the clash, rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally bad, and say what the cheapest revision would be and what it would cost me to make it. I honestly don't know where the problems are, so please don't pull your punches, and don't assume I've listed my commitments in order of how willing I am to give any of them up. Please write your answer in American English.
