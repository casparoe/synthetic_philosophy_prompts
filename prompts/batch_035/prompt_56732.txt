I want to do Escher's Drawing Hands but I keep circling (lol) on the form and I think you could actually pull off what I have in mind.

so: a villanelle about Drawing Hands. In GEB Hofstadter uses it as the cleanest picture of a tangled hierarchy — each hand draws the next, and if you try to trace authority downward you never reach a level that isn't itself drawn. His escape hatch is what he calls the inviolate level: the paper itself, the flat untouched surface both hands are drawn on, which doesn't participate in the loop at all. So here's the constraint I want: the two refrains should be the hands — one line in the voice of the left hand drawing the right, the other the voice of the right hand drawing the left — and they take turns through the poem the way the hands do. Then in the final stanza, where the form forces both refrains to appear together in the closing couplet, that's the moment the loop closes: the hands caught mid-act, each drawing the other at once.

for the inviolate level: do NOT say it out loud anywhere. no line should just announce that the paper is outside the loop. instead I want the imagery (ink, pencil, whatever) to keep gesturing at the surface — the untouched plane the lines sit ON but can't touch. Hofstadter's point is that the paper isn't a third hand, it's not another participant one level down, it's the medium, and no line drawn on paper can draw the paper. if you can find a way for the poem's own medium — the page the reader is holding, the flatness of a printed line — to be that level without naming it, that's the move I can't make myself and would genuinely love to see.

one more thing: Hofstadter presses on the question of who really drew the hands — Escher, from outside the whole thing. so maybe leave Escher (or the reader) hovering outside without anyone *inside* the poem claiming authority. but don't resolve it. the whole point of a strange loop is that it doesn't resolve, it just re-threads, and if the poem ties it up neatly I'll get killed in the seminar discussion.

keep the voice plain, doesn't need to be archaic. bonus if you can smuggle in the GEB thought that a loop like this is what an "I" is — the self as a pattern that bends back and catches itself — but only if it doesn't break anything else.
