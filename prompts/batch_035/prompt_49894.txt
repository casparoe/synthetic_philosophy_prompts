I run trust and safety policy at a mid-sized discussion platform (not one of the giants, a few hundred moderators, mostly generalists with language skills, no legal team of our own). We keep fighting about the same thing: what counts as a good enough reason to take content down. I want to move us off vibes and onto something principled, and the principle I keep coming back to is the harm principle -- the state/society may only restrict conduct to prevent harm to others, not because the conduct is immoral, offensive, or bad for the person doing it. Obviously we're not a state and we're not jailing anyone, so the whole thing needs translation, which is exactly where I want the thinking done.

Write me a decision procedure my moderators can actually run, in the five-ish minutes they have per decision, that decides whether a piece of content stays, gets deamplified, or gets removed. Ground it in the harm principle but adapted: figure out what "harm" has to mean for this to be usable (physical harm? financial scams? reputational damage from false accusations? recruitment into violence?), and be explicit about where you're drawing the line between harm and mere offense, and between harm to others and self-regarding choices (e.g., pro-eating-disorder content -- is that self-harm or harm to others, and does it matter?). It should distinguish removal from mere distribution decisions -- part of the team thinks "we didn't remove it, we just stopped recommending it" is not a restriction at all, and I want the procedure to force that debate into the open rather than smuggle it.

Make the procedure honest about its own soft spots: which steps are really judgment calls in a costume, where a moderator's own politics will leak in, and where the harm principle just runs out of things to say (I suspect slow diffuse harms, like a community being steadily driven off the platform, are one of these).

Then run it on three cases so we can see it work: (1) a post accurately doxxing someone who was just publicly fired and including their home address "so people can protest", (2) a viral post claiming a common childhood vaccine ingredient is toxic, posted sincerely by a worried parent, and (3) a user whose handle contains an ethnic slur, arguing it's reclaimed usage and part of their identity. I'd like at least one of these worked cases to come out in a direction I won't like -- I'm pretty pro-removal on all three, honestly -- and tell me which one.

Also flag what the procedure can't settle and needs a human values decision instead, since those are the ones that should end up on my desk and not in the moderator queue.
