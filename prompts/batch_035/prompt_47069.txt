I lead regulatory and ethics review at a company that will shortly ship an autonomous lawn mower whose blades kill or injure large numbers of insects and small amphibians each season. A colleague on the design team has become convinced by the view that sentience comes in degrees and that moral status should be scaled accordingly; she thinks our product's welfare policy should treat insect harm as a real cost, weighted by some fractional factor, rather than as zero. Before we commit to a policy, I want to understand what actually follows from her gradualist position, and where it does and does not differ in practice from the simpler line that beings below some cognitive cutoff count for nothing.

Construct a thought experiment to these specifications. The scenario must be one my product team could genuinely face — no hypothetical minds, no perfect information, no trolley-style abstraction. Within it, two views must be forced to the same action for the same practical reasons, despite disagreeing sharply about the underlying metaphysics: (1) a gradualist view on which moral weight scales with the degree of sentience, and (2) a threshold view on which beings below a certain cognitive cutoff carry no moral weight at all. A third view must dissent from both and recommend a different action: (3) a view on which our duties regarding creatures like insects are indirect — grounded not in their experiences but in the effects of such practices on ecosystems, or on our own characters. No verdict may be stipulated; each must fall out of the theory's own reasoning applied to the facts of the case. After the scenario, walk through the reasoning each view gives, identify the single structural feature of the case that collapses the first two views into behavioral agreement, and tell me whether that feature is a genuine constraint of my situation or an artifact of a coarsened action space that we could simply refuse to accept. Then give the gradualist's best countermove — the smallest realistic modification of the case under which she and the threshold theorist come apart — and what the threshold theorist would say in response. Keep the scenario itself to one paragraph.
