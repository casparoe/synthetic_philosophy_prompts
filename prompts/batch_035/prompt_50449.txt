Something I keep going back and forth on: airlines and a bunch of companies now sell carbon offsets, and people who buy them seem to treat a fully offset flight as morally fine — the emission is cancelled out by funding a reduction somewhere else. But lots of the same people (myself included, honestly) also feel you shouldn't fly more than necessary even when you offset everything, and would recoil at applying the offset logic anywhere else. Nobody thinks you can offset cheating on your taxes by donating to the tax office, or offset eating meat by paying someone else to go vegetarian, or punch a guy and then write a cheque to his favourite charity. So what does the offsetting practice actually presuppose about harm? That emissions are fungible, impersonal quantities that can be traded across people and places — basically an aggregative consequentialist picture where what matters is the total and not who caused what? Or is there some non-consequentialist story that licenses offestting carbon specifically but not offsetting a lie? And where does the leftover feeling that you should still fly less even when offset fit — is it consistent with whatever theory makes the offset ok, or is it a holdover from thinking of emissions as somehow personally yours, which is odd given economists keep telling us one individual's flying makes no marginal difference to the climate anyway? I guess what I want is the implicit theory reconstructed: what strictly has to be true for carbon offsets to make sense, what the practice merely fits well with, and what would have to change if we followed the fungibility all the way down (meat offsets, lie offsets, maybe worse).
