"Earman and Norton's hole argument is one of the most discussed pieces of philosophy of physics from the last few decades. Explain how the argument works against manifold substantivalism. In your answer, make sure you cover: (a) how the diffeomorphism is set up and why it needs to be the identity outside the hole region; (b) why the manifold substantivalist is committed to treating the shifted and unshifted models as representing distinct possible worlds, and why this appears to threaten determinism; (c) what the argument is supposed to establish about substantivalism -- what kind of conclusion it is and what kind it is not; and (d) briefly sketch one substantivalist reply, such as so-called sophisticated substantivalism, and its costs."

Here's the student's answer, typed out more or less as they wrote it:

"The hole argument is basically Leibniz's old shift argument dusted off and applied to general relativity. In GR we have a bare manifold of events plus fields defined on it, most importantly the metric field. Take a region of the manifold, the 'hole,' and apply a diffeomorphism that drags the metric and matter fields around inside the hole but is the identity everywhere on the boundary and outside it. Outside the hole nothing changes; inside, the shifted fields now assign different values to each manifold point than they had before. Both models satisfy Einstein's field equations, and they agree on all point-coincidences, that is, on which field values coincide with which others. Einstein himself later used this point-coincidence idea to argue that only coincidences are physically meaningful.

The argument turns on general covariance. The principle of general covariance says the laws take the same form in every coordinate system, and this is what generates the hole construction: because the theory is generally covariant, the diffeomorphically related models are both solutions, so the theory itself underwrites the shift.

Now, the manifold substantivalist says the points of the manifold exist in their own right, independently of any fields. So she must say the two models are genuinely different: the metric has different values at the very same manifold points. Since the shifted and unshifted models agree outside the hole at some initial time, the initial state plus the laws doesn't fix what's inside the hole later, so determinism fails. And since the two models are empirically indistinguishable anyway, substantivalism has been straightforwardly refuted: no observation could ever decide between them.

The obvious escape is sophisticated substantivalism, which says we should treat diffeomorphically related models as representing the same world. But this is just relationism under a different name, so the substantivalist who takes this route has really given up the game."
