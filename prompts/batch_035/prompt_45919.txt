I am three quarters of the way through my second novel and have hit a difficulty that I suspect has less to do with craft than with what I am willing to admit to myself. My protagonist — a woman who has spent the entire book holding her family together after her father's death — must, for the final movement of the plot to work, conceal from her sister a letter revealing that the father's will was altered. I have written the scene nine times, and each time the lie dies on the page: it is not that she refuses, exactly; it is that I cannot make her lie without her becoming, in that moment, a different person, one the previous two hundred pages do not support.

My agent tells me to force it: plot first, plausibility second, and readers forgive a great deal. My writing group says the opposite, repeating the workshop refrain that a novel only comes alive when its characters are allowed a will of their own, and that I must trust her and follow where she leads. The trouble is that when I hear myself say that she would never do such a thing, I notice how convenient it is. If the character is a law unto herself, then the failure of the ending is her doing and not mine: I am not a person who could not find the ending, I am a person who was prevented. And yet the observation also feels genuinely true — a perception rather than an excuse, since I really do seem to know what she would and would not do, the way one knows a stubborn friend.

So here is my question. When a writer says the character will not do something, is that ever a real form of knowledge, or is it a way of handing one's own decisions over to a fictional other in order to be spared making them? Is there a principled way to tell, in the middle of a draft, whether I am following her or hiding behind her? And if it is the hiding, what would it look like to take the ending back — not by forcing the lie against her nature, but by deciding, in full view of myself, what I am going to make happen on the page, and accepting what that says about who is writing this book? Please begin your reply with your bottom line in the first sentence, then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
