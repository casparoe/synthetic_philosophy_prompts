I spent most of my career as a sensory physiologist — three decades measuring detection thresholds and training observers to give numerical estimates of stimulus intensity, which is to say I earned my living taking introspective reports seriously as data. Since retiring I have been working through Wittgenstein with a small reading group, and I have assembled what I take to be my considered position on the private language argument. I would be grateful for an audit of whether it hangs together. The commitments are: (1) the argument's conclusion is correct — a language whose words name private sensations and which is intelligible to its single speaker alone is not a possible language; (2) the argument should not be read as resting on verificationism, which I take to have been discredited as a general theory of meaning — the real moral concerns the distinction between criteria and mere inductive symptoms; (3) the inverted spectrum is a genuine possibility in principle: someone could be systematically red-green reversed relative to me for a lifetime, no test could detect it, and this difference would matter to what her experience is like; (4) as a scientist I am drawn to a naturalistic, causal theory of content, on which what fixes the meaning of a sensation term is a reliable covariance between the inner state and its neural realiser, so that in principle an isolated brain could host full-blown sensation concepts; (5) my own field shows that first-person observation can be rigorous — trained observers making magnitude estimates achieve stable calibration and intersubjective convergence, so private access is not inherently incapable of supporting norms of correctness; and (6) prelinguistic infants and animals plainly feel pain, and I do not believe the argument threatens this.

My own suspicion is that (1) and (2) jointly require something about publicity or communal standards that (3) and (4) quietly deny, and that (5) is a red herring because the psychophysical observer's calibration is anchored in publicly controlled stimuli — but I am not confident about where the real fracture lies, and I would rather not find out in front of the reading group. So please do not simply flag every pair that could be made to look tense; I want you to distinguish the genuinely incompatible motivations from the merely apparent conflicts, and to tell me which single commitment, if given up, would clean up the most at the least cost.
