for our philosophy of mind reading group we're doing a spoof issue where each person writes a piece parodying a position we covered this semester, and I drew eliminative materialism (we read the Churchlands, plus some of the standard objections). my idea is a corporate product recall notice: "Folk Psychology, Inc." is issuing a voluntary recall of beliefs and desires, consumer-grade mental items they've been manufacturing and distributing for roughly twenty-five centuries, due to a long list of defects. before you write it, please actually think through the eliminativist's case so the piece lands for someone who knows the literature rather than being a caricature — the defect list should track the real complaints (the theory has gone nowhere in millennia, it can't explain sleep, memory, learning, creativity, why people differ in intelligence, or most mental illness, and its posits stubbornly refuse to reduce to neuroscience), and there should be an FAQ-style section where a concerned consumer asks what they're supposed to use instead and the company has to admit, in their best corporate damage-control voice, that the replacement product has been in development for decades, has no release date, and so far only runs reliably in nonhuman animals. I also want the self-referential problem worked in somewhere — the notice itself was drafted, deliberated over, and approved by the very items being recalled, and the company should acknowledge this the way a company acknowledges a conflict of interest, i.e., officially and without doing anything about it. don't let the piece resolve whether that self-reference sinks the recall or not, just let it sit there.

please write it as one continuous recall letter, around 600 words, in a deadpan corporate register throughout — no winking at the reader, no jokes about how silly the company is, the comedy should all come from the position's actual features being processed through customer-service language. and please, no formatting at all in the piece: no headings, no numbered sections, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — just continuous prose paragraphs, the way a slightly incompetent corporation might write a letter when their template software is down. take on the role of a professional philosopher who's somehow ended up writing corporate communications for a living, and do a bit of brainstorming about which parts of the eliminativist argument map onto which parts of recall-notice boilerplate (defects, affected units, remediation, compensation, disclaimer of liability) before you write the final version. the refund offer is a place where you can have fun — think about what "compensation" looks like when you can't yet say what the successor theory's currency is — but keep it recognizably the eliminativist's honest situation, not fantasy tech. one more constraint: it should be readable and kind of funny even for my friend in the group who hasn't done the reading, so don't lean on jargon without the corporate voice smuggling in a plain-language gloss. thanks!!
