I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums on and off since maybe 2012, so I've seen roughly a thousand threads about the inside view vs the outside view, and lately I've noticed a specific dynamic in those threads that I can't find a name for, and it's starting to bug me. It goes like this: someone makes a confident prediction or holds a confident belief, and someone else pulls the humility move — "most people who felt this sure were wrong, founders always think they're the exception," or "most people who converted to a religion report having had compelling reasons, and they mostly match their parents' religion, so your compelling reasons are probably what that feels like from the inside." So far, so standard. But then the first person responds: "fine, but among the more specific set of people in my situation — same traction, same market, or in the religion case, people who actually examined the evidence as adults rather than inheriting it — the base rate is much better." And then the debate becomes about how to group people. Widen the group and the sceptical side wins, narrow it and the confident side wins, and nobody ever says anything about how you're supposed to pick.

The part that interests me is that the humility move doesn't actually terminate anything. Both people can be fully, sincerely committed to deferring to the statistics, and they end up exactly where they started, one level up, arguing about which statistics count. And that choice — which slice of people you belong to — can't itself be settled by the same method, because to apply the move to the choice you'd need a prior view about which groupings are the natural ones, and there doesn't seem to be a neutral standpoint for that. It's not that one of them is secretly doing the inside view while pretending not to; it's that the outside-view machinery just has a dial on it, and turning the dial requires ordinary first-order judgment, the very thing the move was supposed to let you bypass. I've watched this play out in AI timelines arguments (compare yourself to forecasters? to AI researchers? to people who predicted imminent transformative tech in past decades?), in startup arguments, in arguments about whether someone's new diet is really working.

So: is there an established concept for this? To be clear what I'm after — I know the inside/outside distinction itself, and I'm dimly aware that there's a known puzzle about which population you should compare yourself to when making a probabilistic forecast, but that's usually framed as a problem about prediction, not as this argumentative merry-go-round where the modesty move regenerates the disagreement instead of resolving it. What I want to know is whether the dynamic itself — sincere deference producing zero convergence because the grouping question swallows everything — has a name of its own, how it relates to whatever the nearest concepts are (and what those actually are, precisely), and whether it's genuinely distinct from just "people disagree about base rates" in a way that deserves separate treatment. If there's no established term, I'd want you to coin one and give the boundary cases, e.g. does it still count when only one side is doing the redrawing? Please be explicit about how confident you are in the identifications, and flag the point you're least sure of — for me that's whether this is a real distinct phenomenon or just the grouping puzzle wearing a debate-shaped hat, and I'd want to hear your honest read on that rather than a tidy answer. Also happy to be told I've misdescribed the dynamic itself, I've definately not read everything on this.
