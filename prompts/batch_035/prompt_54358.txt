Something I see all the time on the forums I read — and I've basically said a version of it myself — is that the internet made everyone meaner because it turned life from an iterated prisoner's dilemma into a one-shot one. Village life was repeated play with the same people: the shadow of the future did the policing, tit-for-tat could actually work. Now huge chunks of interaction are one-shot games with strangers you'll never see again, so the equilibrium flipped to defection — flame wars, drive-by insults, scamming, hit-and-run reviews. And people use it to conclude that trust only comes back if we reintroduce iteration somehow: real names, reputation scores, smaller communities.

How far does this actually hold? Give me the mapping first: who's a player, what's in the payoff matrix, what plays the shadow of the future, where's tit-for-tat, is there noise. Then where it breaks. Two things I keep snagging on: people cooperate in genuinely one-shot games all the time (tipping in a city you'll never revisit, helping lost tourists), and if the answer is "that's indirect reciprocity through reputation," fine, but doesn't that mean the one-shot vs iterated line the whole story runs on was never the real variable? Also, platforms do give people persistent handles — that's iteration of a sort — and comment sections are still way worse than the corner store. So tell me which disanalogies actually matter for the pessimistic conclusion, whether it survives them, and whether whats really going on online is something the PD frame never had a slot for — audience size, say, or the fact that your "partner" is a feed and not a person.
