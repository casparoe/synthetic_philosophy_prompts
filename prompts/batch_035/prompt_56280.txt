For a philosophy of mind reading group, I've been asked to write up my commitments about the self and consciousness so the others can poke holes in them. Before I circulate it, I'd like an audit. Here is the set: (1) I'm a physicalist, and I take the computational view seriously — whatever consciousness is, it's a functional organization that distinct physical systems can each instantiate, so a sufficiently detailed brain duplicate would host its own stream of experience. (2) I regard the unity of consciousness as a hard datum: my visual and auditory experiences are not merely simultaneous, they are experienced together, and no physicalist theory I know of has a satisfying account of that co-consciousness. (3) I've become genuinely drawn to Schrödinger's late position, which he took from Advaita Vedanta: that the multiplicity of minds is an appearance and there is in fact a single subject of experience, which would dissolve the otherwise inexplicable fact that "I" am anchored in this particular body rather than another. (4) I'm a Parfitian reductionist about personal identity: there is no further fact about my persistence over and above psychological continuity and connectedness. (5) I hold that there is no survival of death in any sense. Please tell me which pairs of these are in genuine tension, which only look to be, and how badly the genuine ones conflict. For each real tension, say what would have to give and what giving it up would cost, and rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all alike. Finish by telling me which single revision would clean up the most.
