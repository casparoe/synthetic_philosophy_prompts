So I've been going down a rabbit hole on the classic hedonism question — what do all pleasures actually have in common? — and the more examples I collect the worse it gets. A back scratch, the moment a proof finally clicks, schadenfreude when your annoying coworker gets passed over, the floaty feeling after a long run, being so absorbed in a puzzle that you forget time exists, relief when a panic attack subsides. Do any of these share one thing? Heres what I want you to do. Rate the main contenders on a scale of 1 to 10 for how well they handle my list of cases, where 10 means you basically believe it and 1 means its hopeless. The contenders as I understand them: (1) the phenomenal answer — there's a single felt quality common to all pleasures; (2) pleasure isn't a feeling at all but a pro-attitude toward a state, roughly desire-satisfaction; (3) the representationalist answer — pleasures represent their objects as good for you; (4) the adverbial move — experiences are just had "pleasantly" the way a run can be had gracefully, no shared stuff required; (5) a friend of mine swears by what he calls the "grip" view — a pleasure is any mental state that captures and holds your attention AND that you'd endorse continuing, nothing more than that. After the scores, tell me which single case from my list does the most damage to your winner, and if you had to patch the winner with exactly one modification, what would it be. No reading lists please, just scores and the actual reasoning. And be honest about whether my friend's grip view is secretly just the adverbial one wearing a hat.
