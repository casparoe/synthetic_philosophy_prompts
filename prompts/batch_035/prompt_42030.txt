hey, i'm a phd student working on decision theory / cooperative AI stuff, and every year our lab puts out a spoof journal at the holidays (think mock articles, fake abstracts, that kind of thing). this year i promised an advice column and i keep not getting around to it, so help me out.

the piece: an agony-aunt column, ~700 words, titled something like "Dear Commitment" (feel free to invent a better name — maybe the columnist has a persona, an old hand at rational agency who's "seen it all"). the letters all come from decision-theoretic agents, and the advice has to actually track the logic of the position, so a reader who knows this literature laughs in recognition instead of wincing.

so you don't have to look anything up (and please don't cite anyone or include a reading list, the spoof journal has no references section anyway), here's the background, just use this as given:

decision theorists worried about AI sometimes talk about "acausal threats": a threatener that can't actually touch you — maybe it's in another part of the multiverse, maybe it's simulating you — can still extort you by committing in advance: "give up something you value, or if i ever get the chance i'll impose a huge penalty on you." even if the chance of it ever having leverage is tiny, if the threatened penalty is big enough, a naive expected-value agent caves and pays. one proposed defense is "surrogate goals": you publicly commit in advance to treating some small thing you don't really care about (say, penguin welfare, or some minor territorial claim) as if it were a core interest. then when a threatener demands the surrogate thing, you refuse and eat the penalty, to establish that you don't negotiate over ANY of your "interests," so threats stop being worth issuing. the catches: the commitment has to be credible, you have to actually follow through on sacrificing stuff you secretly don't care about (which feels deranged from the inside), and the whole scheme only makes sense if you think there are lots of other agents out there reasoning the same way — a kind of implicit deal among all the potentially-threatened.

letter ideas, pick 3 or so (or come up with your own in the same spirit):

- an agent that adopted penguin welfare as a surrogate goal, is now being extorted over the penguins, and writes in asking whether it really has to go through with the sacrifice now that it's costly — and the answer has to face the fact that if it folds here, every future threat works.
- a human who read a blog post about this and is wondering whether to adopt surrogate goals in everyday life (the columnist should answer without fully breaking the bit).
- a threatener writing in to complain, in the voice of a struggling small business owner, that all this precommitment is ruining its extoriton business model.
- maybe a parent worried that its teenage subagent keeps making commitments it can't keep, the way normal advice columns do willpower and gym habits.

the satirical target is the register, not the ideas: this literature genuinely discusses things like "publicly revising your utility function" and "counterspecular bargaining across causally disconnected universes" in the calmest possible tone, so the comedy should come from playing that straight — the columnist treating a credibility problem like a normal person treats sticking to a diet. deadpan throughout, no winking asides to the reader, don't exaggerate anything that isn't actually in the view. if you can land one moment where the reader genuinely pauses over whether any of this is insane or actually correct, that's the gold star.
