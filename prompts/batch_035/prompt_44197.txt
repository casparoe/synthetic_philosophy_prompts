I'm in a grad seminar on Augustine this semester and we just spent two weeks on the lying treatises. For Friday I have to hand in a short position paper where I state my own view and actually defend it — the professor explicitly said no fence-sitting, he wants us to commit and then see what breaks. So I wrote down my commitments and now I'm second-guessing whether they form a coherent package at all. Here's the list:

(1) I accept Augustine's core claim that lying is always wrong — every lie is a sin, no exceptions, and I'm not sympathetic to the "grave circumstances override it" escape hatch. (2) At the same time, I think the person hiding refugees who tells the search party there's nobody in the attic did something genuinely praiseworthy — not merely a wrong act that gets excused by the circumstances, but positively the right thing, and I'd want to do the same in their place. (3) I don't think bluffing in poker counts as lying at all, since deception is constitutive of the game and nobody playing is defrauded, so it sits outside the prohibition entirely. (4) I read Augustine's big ranked catalogue of lie-types (the jocular ones, the ones meant to help someone, the outright malicious ones) as showing that even he didn't treat the prohibition as truly absolute — otherwise why grade them at all? (5) In the door case I think the practical answer is that you almost never really face a choice between lying and betrayal: you can keep silent, or say something technically true that points the searchers the wrong way, so the absolute ban costs much less than it first appears. (6) And I hold that Scripture never lies, even though divine speech is full of accommodations — God described as having hands and eyes, getting angry, changing his mind — which would all be falsehoods if read flatly literally.

Go through these and tell me which of them are actually in tension, which only look like they are, and rank the tensions by severity with what would have to give in each case. My own sense is that the package is mostly fine and my only real worry is that (5) is kind of a dodge — clever-sounding wordplay that just relocates the deception. But maybe I'm worried about the wrong thing. Also please flag if any of my claims is just a misreading of Augustine rather than a genuine disagreement with him, since I'd rather not find that out from the professor on Friday.
