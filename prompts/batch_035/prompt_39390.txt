so i've been reading the rationalist sphere since roughly the old lesswrong days and the block universe is one of those things i just cached as settled years ago: rietdijk-putnam, two people walking past each other on the street disagree about which distant events are simultaneous with "now", relativity of simultaneity is baked into the physics, so there's no objective present, so eternalism, done. every so often i'd see someone in the comments try to rescue the flowing now and i'd feel smug and move on.

lately though ive been less smug. someone pointed out to me that our actual universe has a preferred foliation anyway (cosmic time in the standard cosmology), that there's a whole literature on whether simultaneity is just conventional (reichenbach's epsilon stuff), and that the rietdijk-putnam argument quietly assumes things about what foliations are ontologically loaded with. i don't want to just re-cache eternalism because it was in the sequences, i want to actually know what the constraints are.

so: before i go read any more philosophy of time, write me the desiderata. what any adequate theory of time has to deliver, given that it has to interpret the physics we actually have and not leave me confused about my own experience. the stuff i'd want on the list, at least — you tell me what really belongs:

- handling the rietdijk-putnam style cases: if two observers genuinely disagree about which distant events are "now," what follows about the present, and what exactly is the argument smuggling in
- whether relativity really forbids a preferred frame metaphysically, or only empirically — and whether the fact that cosmology hands us a cosmic time anyway lets presentists or growing-blockers off the hook, or whether that's cheating
- temporal experience: why it so insistently seems like stuff flows, and whether a theory is allowed to just explain the seeming away or owes us more
- the manifest image stuff generally — persistence through time, planning, regret, the sense that the past is fixed and the future open — how much of that can a theory reinterpret before it's explained away rather than explained
- whether "time is just causal structure" (the timeless physics move, the block-with-no-time-at-all endgame) is actually a theory of time or a refusal to have one

for each requirement say what motivates it, whether it's non-negotiable or just the prejudice of one camp, and be honest about which ones a die-hard eternalist like me might be over-weighting. then the part i most want: tell me which subset of the desiderata is jointly unsatisfiable. like, can you keep presentism + take special relativity fully at face value + no preferred frame, or is something forced to give, and is the resolution "pick your poison" or "here's the only consistent package"? don't do a scorecard of presentism vs growing block vs moving spotlight vs eternalism — i'll do that myself. i just want the spec, written carefully enough that i can't smuggle my cached conclusion through it.
