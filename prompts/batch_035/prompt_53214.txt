An airport pop-science book I skimmed had a chapter on the history of morality, and it gave this rundown of Peter Singer's expanding circle idea. It said roughly: morality started out as an evolutionary trick -- genes for looking after kin and trading favors, which is why early human groups cared intensely about their own clan and nobody else. Then, as civilization developed, reason kicked in and people started extending concern to the tribe, then the nation, then all of humanity, and now Singer thinks the circle is expanding out to animals and maybe even future generations. The book said this is basically a one-way ratchet: each generation's circle is wider than the last, moral progress is built into history, and so we can be confident that eventually everything capable of suffering gets taken in. It also argued that because our moral feelings are just leftovers of genetic self-interest, Singer's view ends up implying that moral judgements are illusions -- the circle only feels like it's getting bigger because the propaganda of our genes got more sophisticated. The takeaway seemed to be that ethics is riding a wave that science set in motion, so we can relax about it.

I feel like something in there is off -- the second half especially doesn't sound like something Singer would sign off on, and the first half sounds suspiciously tidy.
