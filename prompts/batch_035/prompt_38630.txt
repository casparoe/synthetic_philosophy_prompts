I edit a handout for a reading group of archaeology graduate students and philosophers, and I would like a one-page decision tree that takes someone from the starting claim "this society collapsed" to a named position in the debate over what collapse is. The nodes must be questions answerable from concrete cases rather than from school labels, since the point is to sort people by what they say about the evidence, not by which books they cite. Suggested nodes, which you may revise: whether the population mostly died, dispersed, or stayed in place under different institutions; whether the trigger was internal (the society's own escalating complexity ceasing to be worth its costs) or external (drought, plague, volcanic winters, the vanishing of trade partners); whether what was lost was human welfare, organizational complexity, or merely the palaces and scribes that produced our written sources; and whether "collapse" ever names a causal process rather than a dramatic outcome. The leaves should include at least the classic catastrophist reading, the diminishing-returns-on-complexity account, the "transformation rather than fall" school, and the deflationist view that collapse is a narrative imposed on ordinary change. Use the Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, the Classic Maya, and Easter Island as recurring test cases at the nodes, since each has been claimed by more than one of these positions. Flag any pair of leaves your tree cannot cleanly separate, and identify at least one logically available combination of node answers that no one actually defends, diagnosing whether that spot is empty because the view is incoherent or merely because nobody finds it attractive to hold.
