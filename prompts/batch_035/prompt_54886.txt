I do risk consulting — mostly geotech and civil engineering stuff, some project cost work — and I've been asked to write up "my approach to probability" for an internal training doc. Before I put it in front of colleagues I want to know if my working views are actually a coherent set or a mess held together by habit. Here's what I genuinely believe when I do my job:

1. Single-case probabilities are grounded in frequencies over reference classes. When I tell a client there's a 5% chance that this particular embankment fails, what makes the number real (or as real as it gets) is the failure rate in the class of comparable embankments.

2. Narrow the class whenever you can. "Embankments" is worse than "soft-clay embankments of this height in this seismic zone," as long as the narrower class still has enough cases in it. That's basically my whole methodology.

3. Two competent people looking at the same data should end up at the same number. If they don't, one of them made a mistake somewhere. Disagreement is a failure to converge, not "different priors" — I've never bought the reasonable-people-disagree line when it comes to numbers.

4. I use reference-class forecasting for schedule and cost overrun and I trust it. When the method says a client's one-of-a-kind tunnel project will over run by 180%, I tell them that, over their objection that "this project is different."

5. But I think the doomsday argument is garbage. Inferring how many humans will ever exist from my birth rank feels like a trick, and when people try the same move on questions I care about I wave it off.

6. And I think good geopolitical forecasters have genuine skill on unique questions like "war in X within a year" — in a way that's more than just clever class-picking.

Does this hang together? I have a nagging feeling 2 and 3 can't both be true — who decides which class is the right one to narrow toward? — but I've never worked it out properly, and I can't tell whether 4 and 5 flatly contradict each other or whether I can hold them apart somehow. Rank the tensions by severity, say which ones only look like tensions, and tell me which single revision would clean up the most. Don't go easy on me, this is going in front of people who argue for a living.
