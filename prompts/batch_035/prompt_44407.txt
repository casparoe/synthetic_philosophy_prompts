I'm drafting a position statement for a working group that advises policymakers on invertebrate welfare — the kind of context where the UK's 2022 extension of legal sentience to decapod crustaceans and cephalopods set a precedent, and where the next live questions concern insects in agriculture and research. Before I submit my views to the group, I want a rigorous consistency check of the package I've found myself holding. Here are the commitments as I've written them down:

1. Sentience is a matter of degree, not a light switch. What determines degree of sentience is functional organization — the kinds of information-processing a system performs, their integration, their richness — so the difference between a bee and a dog, and between a dog and a typical human, is a difference in degree along a common scale, not a difference in kind.

2. Moral status is a continuous function of degree of sentience. There is no threshold below which a sentient being's interests drop out of the moral picture altogether: any being with any nonzero degree of sentience counts, weighted by how sentient it is.

3. Interpersonal aggregation is legitimate. Weighted interests can be summed across beings, and a large number of smaller weighted interests can, in principle, outweigh one large one. I reject the view that the separateness of beings blocks addition.

4. Nevertheless, I am confident that no number of bee headaches — even billions of them — could outweigh a single human's death. Something about the human's loss seems lexically protected in a way that my commitment to additive weighting of degree-scaled interests does not obviously allow for.

5. I hold a Kantian side-constraint against using any sentient being merely as a means, and I hold that this constraint is exceptionless and, crucially, not scalar: it applies with full force to every sentient being regardless of how low its degree of sentience. At the same time, I support insect farming as a replacement for wild-caught fish in feed supply chains, since I judge it a vast improvement in aggregate weighted welfare.

6. The badness of death scales with the richness of a being's forward-looking psychology: death is a very small harm for a bee and a near-zero harm for a nematode, because there is little self-directed future to lose.

7. Thermostats and current language models have exactly zero sentience, and I take this to be a principled clean zero rather than a vague boundary case — despite my gradualism about sentience in (1).

Please go through these and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension, which only appear to be, and how badly. For each genuine tension, say what would have to give and what the cheapest available revision is. Then tell me which single revision would clean up the most overall, and flag anything in my framing that presupposes an answer rather than earning one — for instance, whether (6) could do any real work toward rescuing (4), or whether the zero in (7) is as stable as I'd like it to be. I'd rather be told that a commitment is untenable than have it quietly papered over, but I also don't want to be sold a crisis where there's only an appearance of one. Rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them as equally serious.
