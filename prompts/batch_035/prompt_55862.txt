I serve on the clinical ethics committee of a hospital network that is progressively deploying autonomous systems — medication-dispensing robots, triage support software, an algorithm that reallocates nursing staff overnight without sign-off. The philosophers on the committee keep invoking responsibility gaps: cases where such a system causes serious harm and none of the humans in the chain — designer, hospital administrator, operator, regulator — seems to satisfy the conditions for moral responsibility. One committee member puts her credence at 0.65 that our hospital will, within a decade, face a case where this is not merely an epistemic or practical problem (we can't figure out who is responsible) but a genuine metaphysical one (no one is), because the harm arises from system behavior that no one could predict or control and that no one's earlier choices can be traced to in the right way. Another member puts his credence at 0.2, on the grounds that collective responsibility, responsibility for risk-imposition, or responsibility for delegating in the first place will always absorb the harm. Give me your own credence that a genuine responsibility gap of that metaphysical kind can occur — that is, that there are possible cases where an autonomous system causes serious harm and no individual or collective agent is morally responsible for it — and decompose the number into the sub-questions it rests on: whether blameworthiness really requires something like a control or foreseeability condition; whether tracing always reaches back to an earlier voluntary choice; whether collective responsibility can genuinely close the gap or only smear it; whether responsibility for creating the risk already suffices for responsibility for the outcome; and whether talk of gaps just reflects a mismatch between our inherited responsibility framework and new technology rather than a real absence. Assign each term a rough probability with one line of justification, combine them, tell me which term drives your number most, and say which of my two colleagues you take yourself to disagree with more and on exactly which sub-question. Close with \answer{...} containing your credence, a number between 0 and 1.
