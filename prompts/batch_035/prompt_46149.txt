The constraint: everyone in the class can already recite the standard five-day backwards induction, so the question has to do more than elicit that. Build it around a variant I want to use. The question should ask what happens to the backwards induction under this announcement, and in particular whether an exam on the fifth weekday (suppose the die came up 5) counts as a surprise, given that on Friday morning the students know it is either today or never. I want the question to force a genuine choice about what "surprise" means in the paradox: mere non-deducibility from the announcement, or something stronger, like having no credence on the day that dominates the alternatives.

The model answer should be about 600 words and must take a definite position on the variant — whether the announcement is consistent, whether the induction has a defensible base case here, and whether the Friday exam is a surprise — rather than laying out the possible lines and leaving the reader to pick. The marking scheme should total 20 marks across the must-hit points, and for each point it should say what full credit requires and what a merely competent answer typically misses, so that the scheme actually separates A answers from B answers. Finish with a short note on the two or three mistakes you expect from students who memorized the lecture but haven't thought through this variant.

Please return everything as a single JSON object with these fields: "question" (the full question text exactly as students would see it), "target_crux" (one or two sentences stating which conceptual point the question is designed to isolate, and why that point rather than the standard induction is what a top answer must engage), "model_answer" (the roughly 600-word model answer), "marking_scheme" (an array of objects, one per must-hit point, each with the fields "point", "marks", "full_credit_requires", and "typical_B_answer_misses"), and "common_mistakes" (an array of strings describing the mistakes the recitation-based student makes on this variant specifically).
