Here's the gist of the relevant paragraph:

Peirce founded pragmatism in the 1870s. His maxim was that the meaning of a concept is nothing over and above its practical consequences, and he used this to define truth as whatever the community of scientific inquirers will eventually agree on — so for Peirce, truth is essentially a prediction about where research is headed. James took the maxim and stretched it into a full-blown theory of truth: beliefs are tools, an idea is true if it works, if it pays, if believing it gets you through life. That is why James thought religious faith could be true simply by being consoling. Peirce was so appalled at what James had done to his brainchild that he renamed his own view pragmaticism, deliberately picking a word ugly enough that nobody would steal it. The moral is that once you give up truth as correspondence, you are stuck with truth as usefulness, and usefulness is relative to whoever is doing the believing — which is where post-truth comes from.
