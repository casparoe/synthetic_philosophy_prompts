my reading group has spent this semester on decision theory, and last session we hit Hofstadter's superrationality — the idea that in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, if you and your co-player are rational in the same way and each knows it, you can reason that whatever you conclude, they conclude too, so it's effectively one decision being made twice through two people, and you should both cooperate. we've been using the standard payoffs (3 each for mutual cooperation, 1 each if you both defect, and the sucker's 0 if you cooperate while they defect). anyway, half the group thought superrationality was the most beautiful thing we'd read all year, and the other half thought it was just a logical sleight of hand — my choice doesn't cause theirs, and no amount of "we reason identically" turns a symmetry into a causal link.

I want to close the semester with a poem for the last session. I'm thinking a pantoum, because the form is almost eerily made for this: each line comes back two stanzas later in a new context, the same words doing different work — which is pretty much the superrationalist's whole claim about what happens when two identical reasoners run the same argument. I'd like the poem to be in the voice of one player the night before the game, deciding whether to cooperate, with the returning lines enacting the "they know that I know that they know" recursion — so that by the time a line circles back, the reader has already heard it inside the other player's head, and the repetition starts to feel less like echo and more like evidence.

The last stanza is the important part. I want it to land on the critic's objection — that mirrored reasoning, however elegant, never makes my cooperation cause theirs, and that "conclusion by symmetry" is exactly the kind of inference we teach people not to make — but the poem shouldn't decide who's right. honestly that's where our own discussion ended up, unresolved, so let the two refrains at the end sit side by side as the two positions, passing each other one last time. it should be accurate enough that someone who knows the superrationality literature (Hofstadter's lottery, the standard complaints) nods along instead of wincing, but it also has to work as a plain good poem for the two group members who've never heard the term. no sermonizing at the end please
