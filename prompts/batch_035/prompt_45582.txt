our book club did Niall Ferguson's Empire a few months ago and now we're halfway thru Shashi Tharoor's Inglorious Empire, and the thing that keeps bugging me is that everyone in this whole fight — both authors, the reviewers, definitely our group — argues about the Raj like its an accounting problem. you list the debits (the drain of wealth, the famines, deindustrialisation, divide-and-rule), you list the credits (railways, english, the legal system, being plugged into world markets), and then the entire debate is just about which side the net figure lands on. ferguson's side says the ledger comes out positive, tharoor goes through the same ledger and says it obviously comes out negative, and nobody seems to ask whether the balance-sheet picture can carry that weight.

so audit it for me. what actually maps onto what: who is the account holder supposed to be — the nation? which nation, india or britain or both? what's a "credit" vs a "debit" when the people who paid and the people who recieved mostly aren't the same people? what's the unit that famine deaths and miles of track can both be entered in, or is that exactly where it breaks? what does the analogy quietly import — commensurability, treating two hundred years and millions of actors as one agent with a coherent set of intentions, some counterfactual baseline like "india without the british" that every entry gets measured against and that nobody can agree on? and here's the key question: once the disanalogies are on the table, does ferguson's conclusion, or tharoor's, actually survive — or were both of them only ever licensed by the ledger itself? if the balance-sheet picture is doing the argumentative work rather than the entries, tell me that, and suggest what a better frame would look like.

one more thing: please be honest about what's basically settled among historians versus what's genuinely contested — stuff like the size of the drain or how much deindustrialisation happened seems to be fought over, and I dont want a contested number or counterfactual presented like its consensus.
