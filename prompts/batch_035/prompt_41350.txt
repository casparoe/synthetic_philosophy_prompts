Quick context: I'm in a grad reading group on social epistemology and we've spent the whole semester on the reductionism vs. anti-reductionism thing about testimony. For the last session each of us has to circulate a one-page "position profile" that the others get to attack, so before I send mine out I want a sanity check on whether my views even form a package. Here's the set as it stands: (1) I'm a reductionist about testimonial justification — there's no default entitlement to believe what you're told, and my warrant for trusting any speaker ultimately traces back to my own inductive evidence that testimony is generally reliable. (2) Behind that sits what I actually care about: an "nothing comes for free" principle, all warrant has to originate in the subject's own perception, memory, or inference. (3) At the same time I'm a pretty uncomplicated reliabilist about perception and memory — no requirement that the subject have accessible reasons, a faculty being reliable is enough. (4) I also think a typical four-year-old *knows* things like her grandmother's name or that penguins live where it's cold, having been told, and that she's justified, even though she plainly has no track record with sources and no real concept of what a source is. (5) I hold that testimony transmits warrant but never generates it — it's a conduit, not a wellspring. (6) And I trust scientific consensus completely on things I don't remotely understand myself, climate modeling say, and think I'm fully rational in doing so.

What I want is an audit, not a lecture. Go through the commitments and tell me which pairs are genuinely in tension and which only look like it — for what it's worth, the ones I lose sleep over are whether (1) can even handle mundane stuff like believing a stranger who tells you the time, and whether (5) is flatly inconsistent with the existence of collaborative knowledge, since it looks like no individual ever has the warrant in those cases. But I might be wrong about where the problem is. Rank whatever tensions you find by severity rather than treating them all as equally fatal, say for each one what would have to give and what giving it up would cost me elsewhere, and then tell me which single revision, if I made it, would clean up the most. I genuinely don't know which commitment I'd drop, which is kind of why I'm asking.
