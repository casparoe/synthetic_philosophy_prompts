For years I've followed the Fermi paradox discussions on rationalist blogs and forums, and I've settled into a package of views about it. Before I write it up, I'd like an audit of whether it hangs together, and if not, what has to give.

Here is where I stand: (1) The Fermi paradox is a genuine puzzle that "intelligence is just rare" cannot solve — the galaxy is old enough that even one expansionist civilization arising billions of years ago would have settled everything, including our own solar system, so something has to explain why that never happened. (2) I accept the dark forest picture: the silence is best explained as a stable equilibrium in which civilizations keep quiet and strike preemptively at whatever reveals itself, since intentions cannot be verified across interstellar distances and striking first is cheap relative to the risk of being struck. (3) I favor aggressive listening, but I believe deliberate high-power transmissions to other stars should require international coordination rather than being sent unilaterally by whoever feels like it. (4) I think humanity ought to settle the solar system and eventually send probes to other stars — expansion is the rational hedge against extinction, and I would expect any competent civilization to reason the same way. (5) I think our routine radio and radar leakage is far too faint to be detected beyond a few light-years, so it poses no meaningful risk and there is no case for curbing it.

Which of these commitments are genuinely in tension, which only look to be, and what is the cheapest revision for each real conflict? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than presenting them flatly, and close by identifying the single change that would clean up the most. Please write your reply in American English.
