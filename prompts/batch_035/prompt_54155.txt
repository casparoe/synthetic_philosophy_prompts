Build me a thought experiment on the knowability principle from Fitch's paradox — the one where "all truths are knowable" plus modest epistemic logic gets you to "all truths are known," which is crazy. I want a toy world where this plays out concretly rather than symbolically. So: design a small finite world — a handful of truths, maybe two ordinary knowers with realistic limits (they get tired, they can only check one thing at a time, some facts are about each other) — such that every truth in the world really is knowable by somebody at some point, and yet the Fitch derivation, run on one particular truth, spits out a proposition that's genuinely unknowable, not merely unknown. Constraints: one paragraph for the scenario, no idealized verification-god agents, and the paradox has to be generated by what the knowers can and can't do — don't just stipulate the unknowability. After the scenario, walk through the derivation step by step inside your world and tell me which step does the work. Then say whether a Dummett-style move (indexing knowability to the time the truth holds) or a Tennant-style restriction (only "Cartesian" truths — ones not known to be false — need to be knowable) would rescue the principle in your world, and what each rescue costs you; if they pull in different directions given how you built the world, I want to hear about that too.
