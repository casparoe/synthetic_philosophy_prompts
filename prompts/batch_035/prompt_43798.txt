In an online group I help moderate, which argues about whether computers could ever suffer, one member closes every thread with the same comparison: a weather simulation doesn't make it rain on the machine, so a simulation of pain doesn't make anything hurt — and since nothing hurts, there's nothing there that could be owed anything. What gives me pause is that she also accepts that dogs can suffer, even though she can no more look inside a dog's head than inside a server, which makes me suspect the comparison is quietly deciding which cases get which treatment. Could you set out what is supposed to map onto what — the storm, the rain, the machine, and the dog — then identify where the mapping breaks, paying particular attention to whether the comparison smuggles in the idea that the badness of pain lives in what a state does to the world (the rain, the whimper) rather than in the state going on inside something? And finish by telling me whether her conclusion — that a machine has no moral claim on us no matter what it is computing — survives once the broken parts of the comparison are set aside, or whether it was never supported by anything but the comparison itself.
