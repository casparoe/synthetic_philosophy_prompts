ok so this one's a parody job posting, in that earnest corporate-hr voice that every company with a "trust and safety" team seems to use. heres the setup: a fictional mid-size company (call it something bland like Cloudbank Analytics or make up your own) is hiring a Senior Algorithmic Fairness Engineer after their risk-scoring model just had a very bad week in the press, and the posting promises, in complete sincerity, everything at once.

the heart of it is the numbered requirements section, and the jokes have to come from getting the impossibility results right. i want the posting to demand, as hard requirements, things that are provably incompatible whenever base rates differ across groups: risk scores that are well-calibrated within each group, AND equal false positive rates across groups, AND equal false negative rates across groups, AND parity in whatever accuracy metric the comms team likes that week. there should also be a clause that the model may not use group membership as a feature, right next to a clause that it must "correct for historical disadvantage experienced by protected groups." dont explain why this is impossible, dont wink, just list it all in flat HR bullet points with the same confidence as "proficiency in python." someone who actually knows the fairness literature should be able to tell the author understands why calibration and equalized odds cant coexist with different base rates — the satire lands only if it reads like a reductio of how these reqs actually get written, not just "fairness is hard lol."

sprinkle in the usual corporate furniture: a mission statement about "earning trust through transparency," a salary band, a perks list (maybe something like access to the fairness dashboard, which currently shows one green checkbox), the EEO boilerplate — which you can make quietly load-bearing if you want, e.g. the company being an equal-opportunity employer that requires its models to be equal-opportunity scorers in every sense simultaneously. and end with a line where applicants who demonstrate that the requirements are jointly unsatisfiable are thanked for their rigor and forwarded to a different opening — maybe in legal, maybe a philosophy postdoc, your call.

keep it deadpan throughout. around 550 words, formatted like an actual posting: job title, "about us," "what youll do," numbered requirements, perks, how to apply. no citations or paper references anywhere, just the technical content wearing a lanyard. and please dont have the posting itself realize anything — the whole piece only works if nobody at this company has ever once read past the abstract. also, one of the requirements should mention the model must perform "at least as well as the current system on day one," because of course it must.
