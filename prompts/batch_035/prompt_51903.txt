Is there an actual name for this pattern? At my job we do these deliberative exercises before big policy calls — small group gets briefed, discusses for a weekend, then they poll everyone. What keeps happening is that the raw poll results are a mess: answers flip depending on question order, on who spoke last, on whether people got lunch. And the facilitator keeps saying the results don't matter anyway, what matters is "what participants would have concluded if they'd had another month and better materials." Which is, notably, not something anyone ever concluded. It's a hypothetical preference that no one holds, being treated as more authentically theirs than the preferences they actually reported.

I feel like philosophy has to have fought about this exact move — taking someone's idealized preference (what they'd want after full information, perfect calm, however you define it) as the *real* one, over their actual stated one. I've picked up fragments: economists who want to "clean" preferences before aggregating them, welfare theorists talking about cognitive psychotherapy or whatever it was, and some AI-adjacent essay about extrapolating what humanity would want if we were smarter and knew more, which struck me as the same move at species scale. So what I'm after is not just a shelf of names — it's the carving. What is the general concept, what are the nearest established versions of it, and where are the actual edges between them (does the idealization differ in kind between the individual case and the group/species case, or just in size?).

And the part I most want a handle on: the idealization is never actually carried out. There's no way to run "you but fully informed" to completion, so in practice "the idealized preference" means whatever whoever's doing the idealizing imagines it means. My facilitator's version of what we'd conclude was suspiciously close to her own view. Is there a term for this — the idealization being underdetermined, so it defaults to the idealizer? If yes, name it; if not, coin something and define it in a way that keeps it distinct from just "ignoring what people say" and from garden-variety paternalism.

I know the label isn't the point, so give me the analysis first. But do end with a single line in the form \answer{[best label, or "no established term: <your coinage>"]} so I can grep it out of your reply later. thx.
