I'm on the safety team at a lab that expects, within a couple of years, to be training models whose alignment we won't be able to verify before deployment — so any case for deploying them will have to rest on control measures (monitoring, sandboxing, staged capability rollouts, containment, assured shutdown) rather than on knowing the model is benign. Before we design anything concrete, I want the specification such a control regime must meet to count as adequate. Write the desiderata: the assumptions about the threat model it must state explicitly rather than leave implicit (which capabilities, which kinds of misalignment, what the model does and doesn't know about the regime itself); the cases it must handle — sandbagging during evaluation, a model that is evals-aware and behaves well under monitoring, collusion between instances, monitors that are themselves ML systems the deployed model can probe; the distinction it must respect between reducing risk and relocating it; how it must treat quantitative claims (e.g., an upper bound on escape probability) given that the adversary adapts to whatever bound we advertise; and what it owes us when its own assumptions fail mid-deployment. For each requirement, give the case or consideration behind it, and say which requirements are non-negotiable and which reflect one camp's priorities. Flag any subset of the desiderata you suspect is jointly unsatisfiable — for instance, whether adaptivity of the adversary and a meaningful probabilistic bound are in tension, or whether assured shutdown is compatible with increasingly relying on the model itself to maintain the systems that enforce it. Then, briefly, assess how a typical defense-in-depth setup with human review of flagged actions does against the list, and name the single requirement that knocks out the most existing practices. Keep the weight on the criteria rather than the scorecard. Please state how confident you are in each part of the specification and single out the requirement you are least sure belongs on the list at all.
