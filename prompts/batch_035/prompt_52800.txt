Last weekend I was at a light installation consisting of two lamps, one red and one green, fixed about a metre apart. The lamps alternated: the red lamp on the left would light for a moment, then, after a brief dark interval, the green lamp on the right, over and over. What I saw was not two lamps blinking in place. I saw a single spot gliding smoothly back and forth between the two positions, changing from red to green partway through each pass. Stranger still, I could point to roughly where along its path the colour change occurred — a little left of the middle.

It was only afterwards that I realised this cannot have happened the way I experienced it. At the moment the spot seemed, midway through its journey, to begin turning green, the green lamp had not yet been switched on. There was no green anywhere in the scene or in the light reaching my eyes at that point; the relevant information simply did not exist yet. Nevertheless, my experience presented the change as occurring then and there, and it left me with a definite memory of where and roughly when it happened.

My companion, who is a neuroscience graduate student, says this is a known effect and that the obvious explanation is that my brain composed the experience after the fact: it waited until the information about the second colour arrived, wrote the change into the middle of the sequence, and delivered the whole pass to me with an invented timestamp. On that account, the experience I seem to remember having at the halfway point never actually occurred then; it was fabricated afterwards. This troubles me more than the display itself did. If that is how it works, it is unclear in what sense I perceived anything in real time at all. It begins to look as though my experience is a continually revised manuscript rather than a live broadcast, and that I am in no position to say, from the inside, which of my moments happened when they seemed to.

So my question is this. Was the colour change genuinely experienced at the time it appeared to occur, with only my memory being revised afterwards? Or was the whole experience composed on a delay, as my companion maintains? And, more importantly, does the difference between those two stories actually hold up — in a case like this, is there a determinate fact of the matter about when the experience of the change happened, or does that question presuppose something about the structure of the mind that this display gives us reason to doubt?
