I lead a small robotics team, and after our model-based reinforcement learning stack started planning before acting — simulating candidate actions and discarding the bad ones — a colleague joked in a design review that we had finally built a Popperian creature. The joke landed well enough that I've been asked to open our next internal seminar with a forty-minute explanation of what the term actually means: Dennett's hierarchy of Darwinian, Skinnerian, Popperian, and Gregorian creatures, and the idea of evolution as a series of progressively faster generate-and-test loops moving the testing off the organism's body.

The audience is fifteen engineers with no philosophy background but strong intuitions about search, simulation, and model-based versus model-free control. Note that I want a plan for the explanation, not the explanation itself. Specifically:

1. What must come first in the sequence, and why — what does the whole ladder depend on that the audience won't already have in place? In particular, decide whether the "trial and error in the environment vs. trial and error in an inner model" framing should be established before any of the four creature-types is named, or whether the creatures should come first and the abstraction be extracted from them, and commit to one ordering with your reasons.

2. Pick the single carrying example that will run through all four levels — it must be one concrete system that can be re-described at each rung without hand-waving. Commit to one; don't offer me a menu. Say what the example must have and what it must lack (for instance, whether starting from something robotic, something biological, or something everyday risks smuggling in the conclusion or making one rung feel like a cheat).

3. The misconceptions I should expect from this particular audience, in the order they'll actually form, and exactly where in the sequence to head each off. My guesses: that the ladder is an intelligence ranking with humans at the top rather than a description of where the testing happens; that a Skinnerian creature is just a dumb one; that our planner literally is a Popperian creature rather than a loose analogy that might break in ways worth knowing; and that "Gregorian" implies culture is a separate kind of thing rather than another tool-bearing environment. Tell me which of these is most dangerous for engineers specifically and where the defusal goes.

4. One question I can ask at the end that would reveal whether someone has the dependency structure rather than just the vocabulary — and what a merely-vocabulary answer would look like, so I can tell the difference.

5. What to openly refuse to cover at this level — I assume the full Popperian epistemology and the memetics — and how to say so without it sounding like a dodge.

Where you have a choice between two defensible options at any point, make the choice and defend it; I'd rather have a plan with commitments I can push back on than a survey of possibilities.
