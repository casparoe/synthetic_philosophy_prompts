I need your help with a situation that my rationalist meetup has managed to turn into a slow-motion epistemic crisis.

For the past eight months, my friend Devon and I have disagreed about a fairly load-bearing question for our group's planning: how likely it is that transformative AI arrives before mid-century, which feeds into everything from our career advice to how much of our communal budget goes to long-shot causes versus near-term ones. Neither of us is a specialist; we're just two people who've read a lot of blogs. When we realized we'd both internalized the idea behind Aumann's agreement theorem — that agents with the same prior and common knowledge of each other's posteriors can't agree to disagree — we decided to settle this properly. Over a series of long sessions we pooled our evidence, walked each other through our reasoning step by step, and updated out loud. Our estimates did move toward each other, but they're still about fifteen percentage points apart, and by the theorem's lights that residual gap shouldn't be possible between honest agents. So we've concluded that one of us must be irrational and have started auditing each other for motivated reasoning, cached thoughts, and status games; our Discord now has a tribunal channel; and last week Devon accused me of refusing to converge in order to win, which is not an accusation either of us can absorb gracefully.

Lately I've started to suspect the whole procedure rests on an assumption we never actually checked, and that "one of us is being irrational" might not be the only available moral. So: where has our reasoning gone wrong? Which of the theorem's premises is least likely to hold for two non-ideal humans like us, and does that failure point suggest anything constructive? And is there any principled way to decide when two people have exchanged enough arguments that further attempted convergence is bad epistemic practice — versus just splitting the difference, or agreeing to disagree and getting on with our lives? Practical guidance appreciated: several friendships in this group are at stake, and the tribunal channel is not helping.
