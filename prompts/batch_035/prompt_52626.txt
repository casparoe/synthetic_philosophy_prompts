I am a novelist at work on a speculative manuscript in which a fictional research institute, having achieved a crude form of personality reconstruction, revives John Stuart Mill in the 2040s to serve as an external reviewer for its flagship project: an AI system intended to act on humanity's coherent extrapolated volition. In the chapter I am currently drafting, the reconstructed Mill sits through a seminar in which the engineers explain the basic idea — that the system should do what humanity would want if it were wiser, better informed, and thinking more clearly, rather than what poll respondents say they want today — and then delivers his assessment. I want the scene to be philosophically credible rather than merely theatrical, and so I would value your judgment on what Mill's position would actually be.

My instinct is that the utilitarian Mill would find a great deal to like here: his test for higher versus lower pleasures in terms of the verdict of those who have experienced both looks structurally like an appeal to idealized rather than actual preference, and the engineers in my chapter are hoping he will say exactly that. But I suspect the matter is more complicated, and this is where I need your help. The Mill of On Liberty was fiercely protective of individual self-direction and suspicious of anyone claiming to know people's interests better than they know them themselves; he might regard a machine that overrides expressed preferences in the name of what we "would" want after extrapolation as a particularly insidious form of paternalism. There is also the question of whether CEV aggregates individuals at all in the way a utilitarian would demand, or whether it preserves some separateness of persons that Mill the liberal would insist on.

How do you think Mill would come down on this, if I am to write him faithfully? I would like you to weigh the evidence on each side of his thought, explain which strand you think would dominate and why, and identify any aspect of CEV (its coherence assumption, its extrapolation mechanism, its treatment of disagreement among extrapolated volitions) where you think Mill's reaction would be distinctly negative, so that I can dramatize at least one genuine point of friction rather than an unqualified endorsement.
