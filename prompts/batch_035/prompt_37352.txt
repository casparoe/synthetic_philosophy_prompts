I'm helping organize the closing session of a graduate workshop on the philosophy of statistical mechanics, and I've been asked to contribute a poem instead of the usual summary talk. Write a sestina on the thermodynamic arrow of time — the puzzle that the underlying dynamics are, near enough, time-symmetric, while the macroscopic world is flagrantly not: cream spreads through coffee and never gathers itself back together, eggs scramble and stay scrambled, we remember the past and can only guess at the future. I want the poem to sit inside that tension rather than describe it from outside.

Let the form do the physics. The six end-words are your microstates: the same six words recur in every stanza, exactly as the time-reverse of any realized trajectory is equally permitted by the laws — nothing new is ever added, and nothing is destroyed. What changes is their order and, with it, the macrostate: the sense each recurrence makes at the stanza level should degrade or drift, so that identical words keep doing different work. Exploit the arithmetic of the sestina itself: the permutation of end-words, applied enough times, would eventually return the words to their original order, but the convention of stopping at six stanzas keeps that return just off the page — recurrence guaranteed in principle, unreachable within the poem, much as the recurrence time for a coffee cup dwarfs the age of the universe. Then let the envoi, which gathers all six end-words into three compressed lines, do the work of coarse-graining: the fine-grained detail forced into a summary state, with the past hypothesis or the heat death implied at the close rather than announced.

Accuracy matters to this audience: entropy's increase should come out as an overwhelmingly probable trend rather than an absolute law, and if you gesture at the low-entropy initial condition as the source of the arrow, gesture accurately — the asymmetry lives in the boundary condition, not in the dynamics. Someone who knows Boltzmann and Loschmidt should be able to say which stanza-level move is doing which piece of the argument, and someone who doesn't should still come away with a good poem about the strange asymmetry of becoming.
