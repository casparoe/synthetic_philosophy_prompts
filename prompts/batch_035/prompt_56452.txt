Retired chemist here, spent 35 years doing synthesis work before philosophy got its hooks into me, so bear with me. In my line of work we always liked to say that structural formulas are the closest thing humanity has to a universal language — a chemist in Osaka and a chemist in São Paulo can read the exact same benzene ring without a word passing between them, the notation just carries the content directly. So when I started reading about Otto Neurath and his ISOTYPE pictures — those rows of little figures where one soldier stands for a million, plus his slogan about words dividing and pictures uniting — it seemed obvious to me: he was trying to do for social statistics what Kekulé did for us, build a notation so direct that the eye simply reads the fact off the icon, with no seperate natural language in between. And honestly people do use it that way, arguing that if we just designed the icons well enough, science communication wouldn't need translation or statistical literacy at all — the picture would just show you the truth.

But the more I read, the more this bothers me. Because this is the same guy who's famous for the point about science being a boat we have to rebuild plank by plank while out on the open sea, with no dry dock to haul it into, no neutral vantage point to check it against. A universal picture language that shows truth directly to the eye sounds an awful lot like a dry dock. So I'd love an audit of my chemistry analogy. What exactly is the benzene ring supposed to be in ISOTYPE — the individual figure, or the whole chart? What plays the role of valence rules, the syntax that tells you what combinations are even possible? Where does the mapping break down — my guess is a picture of a "worker" or a "ton of coal" doesn't decompose the way an atom in a formula does, but I don't trust my own instincts here. And most importantly: does the no-translation, no-statistical-literacy-needed conclusion survive once the disanalogies are on the table, or was the analogy doing all the work? And relatedly — was Neurath himself even after anything like that, or have I just projected my chemistry dreams onto him?
