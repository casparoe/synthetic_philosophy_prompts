My uncle had a stroke a couple of years ago, and one of the odd things that came out of it is that he now has what a magazine article once called pain asymbolia. He says he still feels pain — the sensation is fully there, he'll rate a bad tooth a seven out of ten — but it just doesn't bother him. At the dentist he'll tell you it hurt like hell and then immediately start chatting about lunch. Before the stroke he was completely normal about pain, hated injections.

This blew up at Sunday dinner last weekend into an argument between my cousin (she's a physio) and my mum, and I honestly couldn't tell who was winning, so I'm bringing it here. It went like this:

(1) My cousin said Uncle basically proves pain isn't bad in itself. What's bad is the suffering — the minding of it — and he's lost the minding while keeping the feeling. So all those years the family treated "pain" as the enemy, we were actually fighting the distress, and she said doctors should be aiming at the distress, not the raw sensation.

(2) Mum said no, he's not a counterexample, he's an injury case: the part of him that registers how bad pain is got switched off, so of course he can't report the badness — that's like reading a broken fuel gauge to prove the tank was never empty in the first place.

(3) My cousin came back with the fact that he's not confused or miserable — he laughs about it, and he says if you offered him a pill to make pain hurt the way it used to, he wouldn't take it. If the badness were really there independent of him, he'd be losing something, and by his own lights he isn't. And whose lights could count for more than his?

(4) Mum answered that his lights are exactly the thing that's damaged — you can't use the patient's contentment as the measure of what he's lost, any more than you could ask a man with no sense of smell whether he misses his wife's cooking and take a shrug as your answer. The shrug is the symptom, not the verdict.

Then they both turned to me and I had nothing. I have no philosophy background, I've just been turning this over on my own since, and I'd genuinely like to know how it gets sorted out. Please go through the exchange move by move and tell me who's right at each step — and also whether there's something both of them are missing that neither of them brought up at all. Please write the answer in British English, spellings and everything, since I'll be reading it back to them. Sorry if this is a bit long, its been on my mind.
