In debates about the status of ontology, an analogy I keep running into goes like this: the dispute over whether mereological composition ever occurs is like the dispute over whether Pluto counts as a planet. When astronomers reclassified Pluto, nothing in the heavens changed — no observation was at issue; the quarrel was over how to draw the boundary of a term for our classificatory purposes, and it was settled by convention, in that case by an official vote. Deflationists about ontology (easy ontologists, and some friends of quantifier variance) lean on this: the debate over whether there exists such an object as the sum of my nose and the Eiffel Tower, they say, is similarly verbal — the world puts no constraint on the answer, and the only substantive question is which meaning of the quantifier, or of "compose," it is most useful to adopt. Friends of substantivity push back: "planet" was coined for piecemeal classificatory convenience, whereas the existential quantifier is a bit of all-purpose logical vocabulary, and whether it has multiple eligible meanings, or whether reality itself has a structure that one quantifier carves better than another, is exactly what is in dispute — so the Pluto case can't be assumed to model this one without begging the question.

Please audit the analogy. Give the correspondence table explicitly (planet ↔ composite object; the astronomical purposes that made the IAU definition apt ↔ the practical and theoretical purposes that might make one meaning of "exists" or "compose" apt; the stipulative vote ↔ the deflationist's recommended act of choosing a quantifier). Then identify where the mapping breaks, and separate the disanalogies that matter for the deflationist's use from those that don't. For instance: does it matter that "planet" was introduced by explicit convention with a known function, while the quantifier's function is essentially to state everything else? Does the fact that the Pluto dispute had obvious practical stakes — textbooks, mnemonics, national pride — while the composition dispute seems engineered to have none, strengthen the analogy or weaken it? Does the deflationist need the contested term to be a noun with a boundary-drawing use, and does the quantifier fail to be one? Finally, say whether the deflationist conclusion survives once the disanalogies are on the table, whether a restricted version of it survives (perhaps for some ontological disputes but not this one), and what a better analogy would be, if there is one.
