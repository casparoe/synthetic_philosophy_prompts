The analogy my theology reading group keeps circling back to: God's middle knowledge is like an author's knowledge of her characters. A novelist knows exactly what her detective would do if offered a bribe, what he'd say in every confrontation — a whole tree of woulds for every circumstance — and none of that knowledge makes the character unfree when he actually makes his choice in the story. The person pushing this is using it to argue that Molinism's counterfactuals of creaturely freedom are coherent and don't quietly smuggle in determinism. Audit the analogy for me: lay out the mapping (who plays God, who plays the free creature, what plays the circumstances, what plays the "world" God actualizes), then the places it breaks — the big one being that an author invents the woulds rather than discovering them, so is this middle knowledge at all or just determination with the serial numbers filed off? Also worth checking: characters only "exist" at one level of description, and the author is inside a story of her own in a way God presumably isn't. Then tell me whether her conclusion — that middle knowledge is coherent and freedom-friendly — survives once the disanalogies are on the table, or whether the analogy was carrying it the whole time, and whether some fixed-up version (authors reporting characters "taking on a life of their own") helps or is just more author all the way down. Say how confident you are in the verdict and point out the step you're least sure of.
