I'm a graduate student working on persistence through time, and I've come to suspect the endurantism–perdurantism debate is permanently stalled. The dialectic is entirely predictable: the perdurantist argues that objects having incompatible properties at different times — the bent and straight poker across the day — makes sense only if objects have temporal parts; the endurantist offers an adverbialist or relativizing account of so-called temporary intrinsics and objects that temporal parts merely relocate the puzzle, since a temporal part that is bent all over is itself something having a property at a time. Special relativity gets brought in as a supposed tiebreaker, and then defused, and everyone remains where they started. My working hypothesis is that much of the dispute is verbal: each side can mimic the other's explanatory successes with a paraphrase, so the real question is only which paraphrase into canonical quantificational idiom to privilege — a bookkeeping decision about what our variables should range over rather than a substantive disagreement about what exists. But I'm unsure, because each side does accuse the other of genuine costs — coincidence cases, ideological extravagance, ontological inflation — which suggests a real disagreement about how parsimony itself is to be measured: by the number of entities in one's ontology or by the number of primitive notions in one's ideology. Please diagnose the structure of the disagreement using what each side actually claims, tell me whether it is substantive, verbal, or a matter of the two camps answering different questions under one heading, and given that diagnosis, say what, if anything, would count as progress — a distinction, a formal result, an agreed class of cases — or argue that none should be expected, and why.
