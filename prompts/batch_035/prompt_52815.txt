I'm a PhD student in political philosophy, working mostly on theories of punishment — retributivism, expressivism, that literature — and I've been asked to contribute a response piece on the Dennett–Caruso debate about free will and moral responsibility. That means I know the punishment end of their disagreement reasonably well, but I'm less secure on the metaphysics, and I recently listened to a podcast episode recapping their co-written debate book that left me uneasy. The host summarized their positions roughly as follows:

"Dennett and Caruso actually agree that the traditional retributive picture is broken — that much gets missed. The real split is about what survives. Dennett is a compatibilist: he thinks that even if determinism is true, the kind of free will worth wanting is still ours, because evolution equipped us to anticipate consequences, respond to reasons, and regulate our own behavior — and that capacity is enough to make people genuinely deserving of praise and blame. Caruso is a hard determinist: he thinks determinism rules out free will, so nobody ever deserves blame for anything, and every act traces back to causes outside the person. That's why Caruso's quarantine model holds that punishment is never justified — dangerous offenders should simply be confined and treated like carriers of a disease, nothing more, until they no longer pose a threat. Dennett finds this far too soft: on his view, retributive punishment is precisely what wrongdoers deserve, so long as they had the cognitive capacity to know better. In the end, the choice is between saying criminals deserve to suffer and saying they're patients to be managed."

I'd like you to respond as a philosopher specializing in free will, moral responsibility, and the philosophy of law. Please think through the positions carefully before rendering verdicts, and go claim by claim through this summary, telling me three things for each part: what a fair paraphrase would be, what is subtly off, and what the relevant author would actually insist on.
