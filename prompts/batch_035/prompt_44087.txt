I'm a retired biochemist who fell into philosophy a couple years ago, mostly via a reading group at the public library. We've spent the last few months on population ethics and we're completely stuck on the procreation asymmetry. Everyone agrees that creating a person whose life would be full of misery is wrong, and everyone also agrees there's no duty to create happy people. Fine. But the second we ask WHY those two cases come apart, the room splits and hasn't moved since october. One side says it's obvious: the miserable life is bad FOR the person you created, the happy life is good for them, and only one of those generates an obligation. The other side says that's a dodge, and the whole puzzle only exists because we've all quietly agreed that every wrong needs a victim. We even tried the case where you can create one person with a happy life or a different person with a slightly happier one — nobody is harmed by being brought into existence (they wouldnt otherwise have existed at all), and yet it still feels like something worse happened. Both sides in our group claim this case for their own, which is never a good sign.

In the lab, when an argument dragged on for months, it was usually because one word was hiding two different measurements. So heres my working hypothesis: maybe "good for a person" splits into a no-loss reading (only someone who exists, or would exist anyway, can be benefited) and a gratitude reading (would the person be glad they exist?), and each side of the asymmetry debate has grabbed a different one. But I want a real diagnosis, not just mine. Is this a verbal dispute, or bedrock intuitions nobody can argue for, or two different questions being asked under one heading? And is there anything — a case, a distinction, anything — that would count as actual progress, or has this thing been open since Parfit for a reason?
