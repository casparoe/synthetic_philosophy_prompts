Doing the paradox of fiction in my philosophy of mind seminar and I've been writing down my gut verdicts on which emotional reactions to fiction are "appropriate" or rational or whatever. Crying at the end of a novel: appropriate, no regrets. Jumping at a jump scare in a horror movie: fine, that's just reflex, I don't even think it counts as an emotion. Feeling dread on behalf of a character who doesn't know she's walking into a trap: appropriate. Being scared OF the monster myself, like it could actually get me: not appropriate, kind of embarrassing. Being so angry at a fictional villain that I'm in a bad mood all evening: inappropriate -- but being quietly sad for two days after finishing a sad book feels completely fine?? I can't square those two. Feeling creeped out by an actor for years because of a role he played: not appropriate. Crying at a story a friend told me that turned out to be made up: appropriate, and it's basically the same tears as the novel, except I blame the friend and not the novelist. Is there one principle that captures all of this, or do I have to give up a verdict (I'd rather keep the sad-book one)? And what does the principle say about a video game where I'm the one choosing what the character does?
