My wife woke me at three in the morning, sobbing. In her dream, I had confessed to an affair with her sister, and it felt — she insists — completely real: the kitchen, my voice, the shame on my face. By breakfast she had calmed down, but she still wanted to talk it through, because in her view she had "gone through" something that night, and she felt I owed her seriousness rather than a pat on the head and a joke about dream-logic. Things got more complicated when my brother-in-law, who reads philosophy, told her that some thinkers doubt we have dream experiences at all. He suggested that the entire scene — the kitchen, the confession, the horror — might have been composed only at the moment of waking, like a story written at the very instant it's told, so that there wasn't just a false memory of suffering but no night of suffering to remember in the first place. My wife found this more disturbing than the dream itself: if her mind can retroactively fabricate a betrayal and a panic, what else does it fabricate? I'll add one more wrinkle: her sleep tracker recorded an elevated heart rate starting around ten minutes before she woke, which she takes as proof that something happened to her during the night, though of course the tracker can't tell us whether the kitchen and the confession were part of it. Can you help us work through this? Setting aside the affair that never happened: did my wife undergo an experience, or did she merely acquire one upon waking? Does her physical arousal settle the question, or is it consistent with either story? And does any of this matter for the practical question underneath — whether I owe her more than sympathy, and whether her demand that I take it "seriously" makes sense if the fear never actually occurred while she slept?
