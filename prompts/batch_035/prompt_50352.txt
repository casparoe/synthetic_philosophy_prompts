Our welfare econ reading group has been stuck for months on interpersonal utility comparisions and I'm starting to think the problem isn't technical at all, so I want a diagnosis of the impasse, not another survey of positions.

The situation as we've been taught it: Robbins in the 1930s declares that comparing one person's utility gain to another's is unverifiable and therefore outside economics proper, the ordinalist revolution mostly carries the day, then Arrow shows that without interpersonal comparisons you can't get a satisfactory social welfare function at all, Harsanyi shows that if you buy expected utility plus an impartial observer you get sum-ranking, and Sen keeps making the point that "we can't measure it" quietly slides into "there's nothing there to measure." And then everyone just... restates their position. Nobody has moved in decades.

Here's my working hypothesis, tell me if its wrong: the disagreement isn't empirical or mathematical, it's about the metaphysics of measurement. Roughly, each side has a tacit analogy and the debate never surfaces them. Either utility is like temperature — a real magnitude we simply lacked instruments for, in which case Robbins-style agnosticism was just an early stage of the science and the Harsanyi-type impartial-observer constructions are a kind of proto-thermometry — or it's like phlogiston, where dropping cardinal utility wasn't a simplification for convenience but an elimination, a discovery that there was no such quantity, in which case all the machinery people have built since (extended preferences, decision-theoretic representations of empathy) is rebuilding a ghost.

So the questions I actually want answered:

1. What kind of disagreement is this, really? Verbal (the two sides mean different things by "utility" — felt intensity on one side, preference-satisfaction on the other — and are talking past each other), substantive (there's a hidden factual question about whether cross-person magnitudes exist at all, and the whole debate is proxy warfare over that), or methodological (a fight about what economics is allowed to say that got projected onto the quantity itself)?
2. If it's the hidden-factual-question kind, what would settle it? Some eventual neuroscience of affect? A formal result showing comparisons can or can't be recovered from choice data? Or is it bedrock in the way some philosophers claim moral intuitions are — real disagreement, but not one that evidence of any kind could move?
3. Is there a reading on which the debate is ill-posed, e.g. "comparable" isn't one thing — maybe coarse ordinal comparisons (everyone prefers relief of agony over extra dessert for anyone) are fine while fine-grained ratio comparisons are not, and the stalled debate runs the two together?

Also, if you think my temperature/phlogiston framing is itself a mistake — say, because measurement practices come first and the metaphysics is retrofitted, which cuts against both analogies — I'd genuinely like to hear that, as long as you say what should replace it. What I don't want is a hedge on every point. Tell me which diagnosis you'd actually bet on and what, if anything, you think would count as progress here.
