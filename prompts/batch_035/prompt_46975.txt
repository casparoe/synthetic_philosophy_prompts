I teach an upper-level seminar on philosophy of probability (16 students, 80 minutes, a room with tables that can be rearranged into stations). We're about to take up the principle of indifference, and I want an activity that makes them feel Bertrand's chord paradox before they read it. The idea I have in mind: three stations, each with a large circle drawn on poster paper and a different, but individually plausible, physical procedure for generating a chord — for example, spinning two pointers on the rim to get endpoints, dropping a small dart at the circle to serve as a midpoint subject to some constraint, dropping a straight straw over the circle and taking the chord it crosses — with students recording whether each generated chord is longer than the side of the inscribed equilateral triangle. The catch I want them to discover: each group should be told only that they are generating a "random chord" on the circle, so that when the groups pool their relative frequencies and get visibly different numbers, they confront the fact that the phrase underdetermines the answer. Design the full activity: station-by-station materials and procedures (with enough concreteness that a TA could run it), roles for students, how to record and pool the data, a payoff or scoring scheme that gives each station a stake in its numbers without real money, and a timeline that fits the session. Then a debrief that maps each station onto the corresponding answer to the chord problem, shows why the principle of indifference sanctions each group's invariance claim when applied over a different variable, and connects this to the general worry that indifference is partition-relative. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong on a first run — e.g., procedures degenerating into biased sampling (students aiming the straw, or midpoint groups quietly resampling darts that fall outside some region) — and how to handle it without tipping the result. Finally, be frank about what the activity cannot reproduce: a real experiment settles the frequency question for a specified procedure but cannot arbitrate which indifference assignment over an unparameterized "random chord" is correct, since that question has no sampling answer — so say where in the debrief the instructor must supply that point rather than letting the data carry it.
