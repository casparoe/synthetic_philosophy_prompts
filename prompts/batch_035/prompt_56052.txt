I've just come out of a long argument with a friend who claims the American South's memory culture proves the German model can be exported more or less wholesale: defeat, a period of occupation and tribunals, a generation or two of mythmaking, and then a sincere reckoning arrives, as it eventually did in the Federal Republic. I'm skeptical but can't quite say why. Treat the contestation over Confederate monuments — the Lost Cause myth that produced most of them, the Charleston church shooting in 2015, Charlottesville in 2017, and the wave of removals after the George Floyd protests — as a case study for the framework of Vergangenheitsbewältigung. Map it carefully: what, if anything, plays the role of the Nuremberg trials, denazification, memorials at authentic sites, and the delayed turn toward sincere remembrance; whether Reconstruction counts as a failed occupation that produced a failed working-through rather than no attempt at one; and whether the fact that descendants of the defeated and descendants of the wronged live intermixed, rather than separated by a border, is a structural feature the German model simply has no analogue for. Then run the analysis in the other direction: does the Southern case show that 'working through the past' is not a checklist of stages at all, and that the German success owed as much to West Germany's political interest in rejoining the Western bloc as to any internal moral mechanism the theory names? I want you to be explicit about where the framework illuminates the episode, where it misdescribes it, and where it should honestly go silent rather than force an answer.
