A philosophy podcast I listen to summarized hinge epistemology for a general audience roughly as follows: "Wittgenstein's last notebooks, published as On Certainty, argue that all justification rests on hinges — basic certainties like that there is an external world and that other people have minds, which are held absolutely fixed. Hinges themselves cannot be justified, since any justification would have to presuppose them; and they are not knowledge, since knowledge requires justification, so at the foundation of everything we know lie things we believe with total conviction but do not know. On this reading, Wittgenstein ended up agreeing that Moore was right after all: once we see that 'here is one hand' states a hinge, Moore's proof of an external world goes through, just with an unjustified premise. Contemporary hinge epistemologists build on this: a hinge is warrant-conferring for the community whose hinge it is, so if a community's hinges include a young earth, young-earth creationism is warranted for them; and since hinges never change, disagreement between such communities cannot be resolved by evidence. The upshot is that hinge epistemology abandons the project of refuting the skeptic and settles for a modest relativism."

I suspect this is unfair in places — I recall that Duncan Pritchard, who more or less founded contemporary hinge epistemology, explicitly resists the relativism charge, and I thought Wittgenstein compared hinge commitments to a riverbed that can itself shift over time. But I don't know the terrain well enough to say exactly where the podcast goes wrong.
