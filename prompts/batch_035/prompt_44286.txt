Got into a heated argument with a friend after work about a wierd visual illusion, and now I want to see how the debate would actually go if we were both better at this. In the colour phi phenomenon, you show someone two dots in quick succession — first a red dot on the left, then a green dot on the right — and they report seeing one dot moving left to right that changes colour about halfway across, even though the colour it turns into can't be predicted until the second flash shows up. My friend says the brain must be rewriting the earlier part of the experience after the second flash arrives: consciousness gets retroactively edited, like rewriting git history after the fact. I say that's nonsense, it's more like buffering — nothing gets edited, the whole experience just isn't assembled and broadcast until both flashes are in, so your conscious 'now' always lags a little behind the world. Write a dialogue between the two of us where we genuinely go back and forth: whether the rewriting would ever be detectable from the inside, whether my buffering view means experience has no real 'now' at all, and whether either of us ends up committed to something strange like never being able to catch the brain in the act of deciding what we experience. Make us both land real punches — neither of us should be a pushover. And please write it in British English (colour, realise, and so on).
