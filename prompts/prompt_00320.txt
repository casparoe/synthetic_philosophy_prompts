I'm putting together a seminar on anthropic reasoning and cosmology, and I keep tripping over the Boltzmann brain thing myself, which is a bit embarassing given I'm supposed to be explaining it to my students next week. The setup: in an eternally inflating universe (or any sufficiently long-lived de Sitter-ish universe), random thermal fluctuations will eventually cook up way more freak "Boltzmann brains" with false memories than there are ordinary observers like us who evolved the normal way. So if I apply garden-variety self-locating/anthropic Bayesian reasoning - I'm just a randomly selected observer from the reference class of all such observers - it seems to spit out the conclusion that I'm almost certainly a Boltzmann brain having a hallucination right now, meaning basically none of my memories or reasoning is reliable. But if that's true, I shouldn't trust the very reasoning that got me there either, so the whole argument seems to eat itself.

My question is: doesn't this just show that Bayesian anthropic reasoning is fundamentally broken and we should ditch it as a tool for cosmology? A colleague in the physics dept said not exactly, that physicists actually run the argument in the opposite direction from how I'm running it, but I couldn't quite follow what he meant and I'd like to understand it properly before I stand in fron of a room of grad students and say something dumb.
