Last night a friend and I got stuck in a long argument about laws of nature that neither of us could resolve. She kept pressing the Armstrong/Maudlin-style objection that Humeanism gets the explanatory order backwards -- that laws are supposed to explain why the mosaic of particular facts is regular, not just be a compact redescription of that regularity after the fact -- while I tried to defend something like Lewis's best-system account and its appeal to counterfactual robustness. We never quite figured out whether the Humean has a real answer to the "explanatory priority" charge or is just biting the bullet. Could you write a dialogue between a Humean best-systems theorist and an anti-Humean necessitarian in which they work through this specific objection in detail, with each side directly engaging the other's strongest move rather than talking past each other?
