Then I read Kripke's "Outline of a Theory of Truth" and now I'm second-guessing whether I understood Tarski at all, since Kripke seems to be solving a similar problem but rejecting the whole hierarchy idea in favor of letting sentences like the Liar just come out "ungrounded" or neither true nor false within a single language.

Could you compare these two approaches for me? I'm especially curious what Kripke's fixed-point approach actually buys you that Tarski's hierarchy doesn't, and whether it really escapes the so-called "revenge" paradox (the thing where you introduce a sentence like "this sentence is not true or is ungrounded" and the problem just resurfaces one level up). My old colleague from the lab, who's now dabbling in formal semantics himself, insists Kripke's move is a genuine conceptual advance rather than just a technical repackaging of the same stratification idea, but I'm not so convinced yet -- feels like we're just hiding the regress rather than curing it. Which one of us is closer to right here?
