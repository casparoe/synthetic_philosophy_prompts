I run the summer reading program at my local library, and something about it has been bugging me. Kids get invited to a pizza party if they log 20 books over the summer, verified by a quick three-question multiple choice quiz online (mostly stuff like "what color was the cover" or "name one character"). This year our 'books read' numbers doubled from last summer, so on paper the program looks like a smash hit. But when I actually chat with the kids at the party, half of them just grabbed the thinnest picture books off the shelf, flipped through in five minutes, and breezed the quiz without absorbing anything. Meanwhile a kid who spent three weeks slogging through a real chunky fantasy novel gets credit for "one book," same as the kid who did twenty of those flip-throughs. My co-organizer says the numbers speak for themselves and we should run it exactly the same way next year since "reading is clearly up." I'm less convinced — bragging about the doubled book count feels kind of hollow to me, but i can't quite put my finger on why it bothers me. Am I overthinking this, and if not, what should we actually be doing differently?
