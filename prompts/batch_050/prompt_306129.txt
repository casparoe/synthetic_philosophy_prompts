I'm putting together a proposal for a one-semester graduate paper (around 7,000 words) in the philosophy of history, and I'd like you to write the proposal itself. My half-formed conjecture: when historians and archaeologists argue over whether a society "collapsed" — the Late Bronze Age palatial system, the Classic Maya, Rapa Nui — they are often running together at least four claims that can come apart: rapid loss of sociopolitical complexity, discontinuity of identity-constituting institutions and practices, demographic decline, and catastrophe in the welfare of the people living through it. The Maya case is suggestive here: elite institutions disappeared while population, language, and much of daily practice persisted, which makes me suspect that definitions of collapse as loss of complexity (in the tradition associated with Tainter) quietly build a contestable value judgment — that complexity is what matters — into a purportedly descriptive category. Write the proposal: state the question precisely (is "collapse" a unitary descriptive concept or a family of partially independent ones, and what follows for the debate over whether such societies failed or merely transformed?); explain why existing treatments leave this open; lay out the method, which should combine conceptual analysis with the three cases used as tests of the taxonomy rather than as illustrations; describe what a successful deliverable looks like within a semester's realistic scope; and say what would count as the project failing — in particular, which finding about the cases would show that the four-way distinction collapses back into a single concept after all, and where you judge the argument most vulnerable to exactly that. No bibliography or citation-style literature review; refer to positions and arguments by name and I'll add references myself.
