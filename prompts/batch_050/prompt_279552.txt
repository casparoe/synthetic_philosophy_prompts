So I do data work for a living and read alot of social science on the side, and I keep tripping over the same move, and I want to know if it has a name.

The move: someone explains an outcome by pointing at the values of the people involved, but when you ask how we know they had those values, the evidence turns out to have been assembled after the fact, from a stock of material so big it could have supported basically any conclusion. Three cases I've hit in the last year:

- A historian says a port city beat its rivals because its merchants had a culture of trust. The evidence? The surviving ledgers show tons of repeat business — which is to say, the winning.
- Commentators in the 60s said a certain country was poor because its religion taught fatalism. Same commentators, forty years later, same country booming, same religion: now it teaches diligence. Proverbs get quoted both times, just different ones.
- Business writers say a firm succeeded because of its strong culture, and when you ask how they measured the culture, its interviews done after the success, with the people who benefited from it.

Its not plain circular reasoning, because there usually is some independent-looking material — sermons, sayings, survey answers. It's just that the same kind of material seems findable in the failures too, if anyone bothered to look, and nobody ever states in advance what the culture would have to look like for the explanation to be wrong.

So: does this pattern have an established name? I know Weber and the whole protestant ethic thing is the big reference point for values-as-causes, but he actually seems to have been careful about this exact problem (checking places that had the values without the takeoff, and so on), so I'm asking about the sloppy version. Is there a term for it, how exactly does it differ from ordinary circular reasoning and from just saying "correlation isn't causation," and if there's no name, coin one with a proper definition — plus tell me what a legitimate culture-as-cause explanation would have to do to clear the bar, because I assume some of them do and I don't want to throw those out with the bathwater.
