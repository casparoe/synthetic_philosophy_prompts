I am designing a discussion unit on whether societies “collapse” or merely change, using three standard cases: the Late Bronze Age eastern Mediterranean, the Classic-period Maya lowlands, and Rapa Nui (Easter Island). A recurring problem in our conversations is that collapse sometimes means political fragmentation, sometimes demographic loss, sometimes abandonment of monumental practices, and sometimes simply the end of the arrangement historians happen to have labeled with one civilization’s name. I want to replace that loose usage with a disciplined concept without turning “collapse” into a purely technical term disconnected from ordinary concerns about catastrophe and failure.

Before proposing any definition, write the desiderata that an adequate account of societal collapse must satisfy. For each requirement, explain the case or intuition motivating it. The list should address, among other things: how to distinguish collapse from gradual transformation, elite turnover, conquest, migration, decentralization, and a mere loss of historical records; what the relevant unit of continuity is—population, territory, institutions, culture, political system, or collective identity; over what timescale decline must occur; whether the consequences must be bad for ordinary people rather than merely for rulers; and how an account should handle later recovery, successor societies, and descendants who preserve parts of the older way of life. Please distinguish non-negotiable requirements from useful but dispensable ones, and explicitly identify any requirements that appear impossible to satisfy jointly.

Then test the resulting list against the three cases at a broad level, without relying on obscure archaeological claims or specialist literature. Commit to a definite working definition of societal collapse, explain which two desiderata do the most to exclude rival uses, and say which of the three cases fits the definition most clearly and which fits least clearly. Where the evidence or ordinary language points in different directions, do not retreat into a menu of options: make the conceptual choice you think is best and acknowledge its most serious cost.
