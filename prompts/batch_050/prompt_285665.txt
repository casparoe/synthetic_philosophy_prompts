I'm writing up a handout on Harsanyi's aggregation theorem (the social aggregation one, not the impartial observer one) and I need a objections section that isn't just the greatest hits. Here's what I've already got, so please don't serve me any of these back:

1. The representation point (usually credited to Broome and/or Weymark): the theorem only shows social preferences can be *represented* as a weighted sum of individual vNM utility functions. Calling that a proof of utilitarianism requires a separate, unargued premise that vNM utility measures how good outcomes are for people. As it stands it's a formal result wearing ethical clothes.

2. The Diamond-style fairness objection: ex ante Pareto plus expected utility makes society indifferent between giving some benefit to person A for sure and flipping a fair coin over who gets it, whenever everyone is ex ante indifferent between the two. So the axioms forbid caring about fair procedures as such, which shows the Pareto condition is doing real normative work, not bookkeeping.

3. The normalization problem: vNM functions are only pinned down up to positive affine transformations, and the weights in the social sum shift around with how you normalize each person's utility. Without a substantive theory of interpersonal comparison, the theorem leaves the one thing that matters -- the weights -- floating.

4. Risk-attitude contamination: each person's vNM function encodes their taste for risk, so the social sum ends up partly tracking how risk-averse people happen to be, which seems irrelevant to how well their lives actually go.

5. Inheritance of EUT's problems: since both individual and social preferences have to be vNM-representable, every standard worry about expected utility theory (independence, Allais-type patterns) transfers straight over to the theorem.

Not a rewording, not a special case (so no second example with the Diamond structure, no additional EUT axiom someone might reject), and not two of them stapled together. Technical or philosophical, either is fine. If after trying you think everything else on offer really is a variant of one of these, you can tell me that instead -- but only after a real attempt.
