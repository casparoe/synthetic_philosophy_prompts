So I've been going back and forth with a friend from my old LessWrong meetup about Quinn's self-torturer puzzle, and we've hit the point where we keep talking past each other and I want to figure out where the disagreement actually lives. Quick recap of the setup in case it's been a while: there's a device permanently attached to you with 1000 settings. Setting 0 is nothing, setting 1000 is excruciating agony, and the jump between any two adjacent settings is completely imperceptible -- you cannot feel the difference between n and n+1 no matter how hard you attend. Each time you advance the dial one notch you get a serious payout (say $10,000). Intuitively each individual step looks like a no-brainer -- imperceptible cost, real money -- and yet the endpoint of doing that 1000 times is torture plus a pile of cash you'd give up anything to escape. My friend says the agent who keeps turning the dial is being irrational, full stop, and the puzzle shows rational preferences must be transitive and that "each step is fine" reasoning is a fallacy. I lean the other way: I think each step genuinely is rational and the puzzle shows something weirder, maybe that rationality is a property of policies rather than choices, or that vague preferences break the naive picture.

My credence is around 0.65, my friend's is around 0.05, so second: tell me which sub-question our disagreement most likely lives in, given those numbers.
