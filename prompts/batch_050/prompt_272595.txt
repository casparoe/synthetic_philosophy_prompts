(1) i'm a fairly standard gricean about the basics: there's a real distinction between what a speaker says and what they merely implicate, and lying is asserting something you believe to be false -- so deceiving someone with a literally true implicature is misleading, not lying.
(2) all else equal, lying is morally worse than misleading, even when both succeed in planting exactly the same false belief in the hearer.

(4) cases like irony and metaphor show that the line between what's said and what's communicated is blurry anyway.
(5) culpability tracks intention: two speakers who intend the same false belief in the hearer, for the same reasons, are equally blameworthy.
