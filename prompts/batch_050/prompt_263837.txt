I'm in year 11 and our school philosophy club is doing a session on political philosophy. Each of us has to write down our current position on a question and have it checked for internal consistency before we discuss it. My question is: is taxation theft? Here is where I've landed after reading around a bit:

(1) A person fully owns the products of their own labour, so taking part of someone's wages without their consent is theft — even when the state does it, and even when the money is spent on genuinely good things. Good ends don't launder a bad taking.

(2) A tax on the unimproved value of land is legitimate, because nobody produced the land; its value comes from nature and from the surrounding community, so reclaiming some of that value isn't taking anyone's product.

(3) Consent doesn't require signing a contract. If you keep living in a country and use its roads, courts and police, you have tacitly consented to being taxed for them, even if leaving would be expensive and disruptive.

(4) Sales taxes are not theft, because purchasing something is voluntary in a way that earning an income is not: you can simply decline to buy a taxed good, whereas most people can't realistically decline to work.

Could you audit this set for me? Tell me which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, which only appear to be, and how severe each real tension is. For each real tension, state what would have to give on each side and what the cheapest repair would be, then tell me which single revision would restore the most consistency overall. I haven't decided which commitment I'd abandon if forced, so please don't assume one is fixed.

Two requests about how you answer. First, lead with your bottom line in the very first sentence — the overall verdict on whether my position hangs together — then give the reasoning, with no preamble and without restating my question. Second, separate clearly what is settled ground in this debate from what is genuinely contested among philosophers and economists; please don't present a contested position (including any of mine, or any repair you recommend) as though it were the consensus view.
