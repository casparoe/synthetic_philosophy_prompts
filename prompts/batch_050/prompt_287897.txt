Bit of a weird hobby: I watch fringe history content the way some people watch true crime. Ancient astronauts, Atlantis hunters, the Fomenko people who think a big chunk of recorded history is duplicated and the timeline needs shortening. And across alot of it I keep noticing the same move, and I can't find a word for it.

The move is this. The guy doesnt actually present evidence FOR his theory. Instead he keeps a running list of things the mainstream account allegedly can't explain -- the perfectly fitted stones at some Peruvian site, two dynasties in different chronicles that look suspiciously alike, whatever -- and every item on the list is treated as one more point for the alternative theory. The official story is bleeding, therefore aliens. Or the shortened timeline. Or Atlantis.

But there are two features that make it feel like something more specific than generic bad reasoning. First, the list is a ratchet. When an item gets resolved -- some stonemason demonstrates exactly how to cut and fit stones like that with period-appropriate tools, say -- the item just quietly leaves the rotation. The total never gets revised down. Nobody says, okay, we had forty unexplained mysteries and now we have thirty-nine. Next week's video still talks about the mountain of unexplained evidence, same size as before. Second, the pile only ever counts against one side. Nobody runs the same list against the alternative: if unexplained stone-cutting is a strike against orthodox archaeology, shouldn't a theory about alien contractors have to explain the stones too, plus a hundred other things? Apparently the alternative wins by the official story losing, like there's only two options and one ledger.

I know the nearby concepts and I don't think it's any of them. It's not cherry-picking, because they're not selecting favorable instances of their own theory, they're collecting failures of someone else's. It's not a Gish gallop, because the point isn't overwhelming you in a debate, it works slowly over years. And it's not just arguing from ignorance, because often the item isn't even unexplained anymore, it just stays on the list.

So: does this exact configuration have a name? If it does, don't just give me the name, give me the definition and where its edges are, and if it doesn't, coin one, define it properly, and give me boundary cases. And here's the boundary case that actually matters to me: the people who argued humans were in the Americas before Clovis were also collecting failures of the mainstream account for decades, and they turned out to be right. So what distinguishes the crank version from that one?
