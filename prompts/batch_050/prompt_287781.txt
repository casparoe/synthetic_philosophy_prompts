I am the secretary of the fellows' appointments committee at a small Cambridge college, and I need your help before our next meeting. Our statutes say only that a candidate shall be elected if and only if the committee finds them satisfactory on each of three criteria: research, teaching, and contribution to college life. Each member records a judgement on each criterion and then a bottom-line vote for or against election; until now we have never had occasion to ask how the individual verdicts are supposed to combine into a corporate one. A colleague recently cornered me after dinner and claimed that our committee is a walking example of what he called the discursive dilemma, and having looked into it I am worried he is right. Our past decisions, with the votes, are as follows (the chair is always Dr A; ✓ means satisfactory, ✗ means not, and each member's bottom-line vote has always followed their own criterion judgements in the natural way):

- Candidate 2 (2021): Dr A ✗✗✓, against; Dr B ✓✗✗, against; Dr C ✗✓✗, against. Not elected.
- Candidate 3 (2022): Dr A ✓✓✓, for; Dr B ✓✗✓, against; Dr C ✓✓✓, for. Elected.
- Candidate 4 (2023): Dr A ✗✓✓, against; Dr B ✓✗✗, against; Dr C ✗✗✓, against. Not elected.

The new case, to be decided on Friday, is this: Dr A (the chair) finds the candidate unsatisfactory on research but satisfactory on teaching and college contribution, and votes against; Dr B finds the candidate satisfactory on research and college contribution but unsatisfactory on teaching, and votes against; Dr C finds the candidate satisfactory on all three and votes for. So on a head-count, each criterion attracts two satisfactories out of three, yet only one member of three supports election. Please think carefully and begin by enumerating, before committing yourself, every aggregation rule that is consistent with all four of our previous decisions taken together; I suspect there are at least three or four, including rules that aggregate the criterion judgements first, rules that aggregate only the bottom-line votes, rules keyed to how many criteria pass, and rules giving special weight to the chair. Finally, describe the one additional hypothetical case whose outcome would do most to discriminate among the surviving rules, so that I can put it to the committee as a test of what our practice has actually been. Please reply in British English throughout.
