We rolled out a new KPI for the warehouse teams -- packages scanned per labor hour -- and within two months the numbers looked great but actual throughput got worse, because people were doing stuff like batching small easy packages and letting the bulky ones pile up. When I brought this up, one of the senior managers just shrugged and said, well, when a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure, so this was inevitable and we should stop using metrics for targets altogether. I've been reading LessWrong and SSC for like a decade so I've seen the Goodhart thing a million times (including that taxonomy post breaking it into regressional/extremal/causal/adversarial flavors), and my gut says the manager is using the slogan as a thought-terminating cliche to win the argument, but I couldn't articulate on the spot what the slogan actually commits him to.

What I want is a proper disambiguation. Lay out the distinct precise claims that slogan could be making -- from weak stuff like "any proxy imperfectly correlates with the goal" up through "optimizing against a measure actively destroys its correlation with what you cared about" to the very strong "measures can never be used as targets at all" -- and for each one: what mechanism would make it true, whether it's a platitude, a substantive empirical claim, or just false, and crucially whether that reading would actually support my manager's conclusion (abandon KPI targets) rather than some weaker conclusion like "use multiple metrics" or "audit the proxy periodically". I suspect only the most radical reading gets him his conclusion and that reading is empirically false as a universal claim, but I want this laid out rigorously, not just my hunch confirmed. Also flag if some readings apply to our warehouse case and others don't, e.g. is what happened to us actually adversarial Goodhart or something dumber like a badly chosen proxy.

Format: give me your answer as a JSON object.
