I’m applying for a ten-week summer undergraduate research grant and need a proposal of about 900–1,200 words. My topic is fakes, forgeries, and why originals seem to matter. The half-formed idea I want to develop is that the usual debate treats “the value of authenticity” as one thing, when it might actually be a bundle of separable values: evidential value (the original gives us a causal chain back to the past), sentimental or identity-constituting value (this particular object matters because of our relationship to it), social-coordination value (museums and markets need some convention for privileging one object), and maybe a more mysterious “aura” value that people invoke when the first three explanations run out. I’m a longtime reader of rationalist blogs and forums, so I’m instinctively suspicious of that last one—it sounds like a label for an unexplained remainder rather than an explanation. But I also don’t want the proposal to smugly conclude that anyone who prefers the original is probabaly just confused or status-seeking.

The core question I want the proposal to ask is this: if a perfect duplicate gives observers all the same experiences and preserves all the same information as an original, can preferring the original be a coherent terminal preference, or must it always be justified instrumentally by evidence, memory, social coordination, or some other further good? Use a running thought experiment involving a gradually repaired historical artifact and an atom-for-atom copy made from the original’s blueprint: at what point, if any, does the repaired artifact stop being “the real one,” and does the copy ever acquire the original’s value merely by taking over its social role? The proposal should carefully separate that metaphysical question from the psychological question of why people react differently to originals and copies, and from the ethical question of whether museums ought to cater to the preference.

Please turn this into a research proposal rather than an essay defending my hunch. It needs a precise central question, an explanation of why obvious existing answers leave it open, a week-by-week plan for ten weeks, and an account of what a successful outcome would look like. Also include at least two plausible conclusions the project might reach and what considerations would decide between them, plus what would count as the project failing or turning out to be too confused to answer. Please don’t include citations, a bibliography, or a literature review by author name; I can add those later. Write it in American English.
