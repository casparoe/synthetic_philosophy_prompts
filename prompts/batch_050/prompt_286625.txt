So I cover science and ideas for a magazine, and for the last year and a half my beat has been war crimes trials, plus a reconciliation project where I've gotten to know a few elderly men who, fifty-odd years ago, were low-level guards, drivers and clerks for a genocidal regime. None of them seem like monsters in person, which is kind of the whole problem, and I've noticed a weird recurring thing whenever I talk about my reporting. I want to know if there's an established name for it, because I keep needing the word and not having it.

Here's the pattern. When I describe these men to prosecutors, or to victims' families, the explanation I always get back is about character: they were fanatics, they were cruel, something in them was rotten. If I so much as mention what social psychologists say about peer pressure, obedience, gradual escalation, people look at me like I'm making excuses for them. Then I go to an academic workshop and it's exactly inverted: the historians and psychologists explain everything by situation and role and incentives, and if some journalist in the room asks whether at least a few of these guys were just unusually brutal people, the room goes tense, like the question is naive or faintly disreputable. But here's what really gets me, and I've been thinking about this alot: both sides seem to hear every causal claim as a moral verdict. Situational explanation equals letting them off. Character explanation equals locking them up forever. It's as if there were only one dial, and turning it toward "understand why" automatically turns it toward "blame less". A friend who teaches philosophy tells me I'm circling the Browning–Goldhagen fight and the old explanation-versus-excuse problem, and I know about the fundamental attribution error, but that one's a bias about which cause you reach for, not this conflation where picking a cause gets felt as picking a punishment.

So: does this specific thing have a name? And I don't want just a bare label, I want to know what the nearest concepts are, how the thing I'm describing differs from each of them, and where its edges are. For instance, does it apply to explaining good deeds too, or only bad ones? Does it show up outside morality entirely, like when explaining somebody's obesity or bankruptcy gets heard as "so it's not their fault"? Is it one phenomenon or two different ones (one on each side) that only look symmetrical? If there's genuinely no established term, coin one and define it properly, with a couple of boundary cases. And please just write continuous prose, like an email back to me — no headings, no bullet points, no bold, no tables — since I'll be pasting chunks of your answer straight into my notes.
