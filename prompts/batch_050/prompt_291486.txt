I'm a second-year undergraduate taking a course in the philosophy of mathematics, and our departmental student society is putting together an end-of-term magazine with a satirical piece on each topic from the syllabus. I've been assigned Hilbert's hotel, and I'd like your help writing it.

Please take on the role of an experienced satirical writer with a serious background in set theory and philosophy of mathematics — the kind of contributor who gets the mathematics right before getting it funny. Think carefully about the underlying math before you write a word of prose.

The piece: a guest-relations page from the website of Hilbert's Grand Hotel, presented in the cheerful, frictionless register of real corporate hospitality copy. Structure it as three or four connected elements — for example, a page of frequently asked questions, a short run of guest reviews with the manager's replies beneath them, and a "planning your stay" notice. Around 700-800 words in total. The tone must stay deadpan throughout: nobody at the hotel finds anything unusual, and the comedy has to come entirely from staff and signage treating genuinely paradoxical features of actual infinity as routine operational matters — housekeeping logistics, loyalty-program terms, complaints handling.

The piece should draw its material from the actual mathematics, selecting from (you needn't use all of these):
- the manager's procedure for accommodating new arrivals in an already full hotel by asking guests to shift rooms, including the version needed for a countably infinite busload of new guests;
- the fact that some guests are asked to move rooms again and again, and what a patient front-desk reply to their complaints would have to concede;
- the old observation that the even-numbered rooms are exactly as numerous as all the rooms taken together, deployed as though it were a selling point;
- Cantor's result that the real numbers are uncountable — the hotel's one genuine capacity limit, so that when a tour group arrives with one guest per real number, the staff must turn it away, and this refusal should be the only moment in the piece where the hotel admits it cannot do something;
- a recurring guest who is a finitist and whose review disputes that the hotel exists at all; let his complaint land one real point before the manager's blandly courteous rebuttal.

The target is the popular treatment of actual infinity — the style in which completed infinities are presented as just very large finite things with amusing properties — and the satire should work by taking that presentation completely literally. A reader who knows the diagonal argument should be able to reconstruct exactly why the uncountable tour group cannot be fitted in from the manager's apology alone, without the piece ever stating the proof.

Before writing the piece itself, do a short brainstorm of about 100-150 words: list your candidate jokes, tag each one with the mathematical fact it depends on, and cut any whose humor would require the mathematics to be wrong. Then write the piece.
