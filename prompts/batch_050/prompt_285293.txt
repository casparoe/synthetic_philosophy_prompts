By strong longtermism I mean the thesis that, in most decision situations we actually face, the dominant consideration in how good an action is is its effect on the very far future — thousands of years out and beyond. 1. Conditional on humanity or its successors surviving for a long time, what's the probability that the total amount of morally valuable experience in the future is astronomically large — enough to swamp everything in the near term?
2. Given that, what's the probability that there exist actions available to us now whose far-future effects are both large and knowably-signed — i.e. the cluelessness objection fails for at least one class of interventions? 3. What's the probability that we're near something like a hinge of history — that the value of the entire future is unusually sensitive to what happens in the next century or two — versus the boring alternative where every era is roughly equally hingey and ours isn't special?
