Half the room thinks any aggregation of claims betrays the whole point of the view, the other half thinks refusing to aggregate makes contractualism absurd and you should just bend the framework to allow it (some version of balancing claims against each other rather than weighing outcomes).

Write the joint statement these two camps would produce if they ran an adversarial collaboration instead of arguing past each other. I want: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign -- e.g., about what justifiability-to-each-person requires, about what's wrong with crude utilitarian summing, about cases like the dying violinist vs. the million headaches; (2) the exact points of disagreement, each phrased neutrally enough that the side it describes would accept the wording; (3) the cruxes -- I'm guessing one is whether the individualist restriction is a commitment of the view or just Scanlon's implementation choice, and another is whether "each person counts for one" already smuggles in aggregation -- and for each crux, a concrete case or argument both camps would accept as bearing on it, with each side's verdict on that case written down in advance. If part of their fight turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.

Also flag where your reconstruction of either position is most likely to be unfair or wrong, and what argument or case would make you conclude instead that the collaboration fails -- i.e., that there's a real crux where one side simply has no defensible answer.
