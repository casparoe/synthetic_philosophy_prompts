Write a deadpan visitor's guide, around 700 words, to the fictional Museum of Suppressed History, an institution committed to treating three fringe theories as simultaneously and literally true: first, that Atlantis was a real, technologically advanced island civilization destroyed by catastrophe, as described in Plato's dialogues; second, ancient-astronaut theory, holding that monuments such as the pyramids and the Nazca lines could only have been built with extraterrestrial guidance; and third, a radical chronological revisionism holding that the standard timeline of history is padded with several invented centuries and that many supposedly ancient events are distorted duplicates of medieval ones. Format it as an actual guide — welcome message, permanent exhibitions, frequently asked questions, gift shop — and never break character. The comedy must come from the staff's earnest project of keeping all three stories consistent at once: if the standard chronology is inflated, the pyramids are not ancient after all, so the aliens must have dropped by rather later than advertised; Atlantis will have to be re-dated to the Middle Ages; and so on. Have the guide resolve at least three such contradictions with total confidence. Along the way it should exhibit, without ever naming, the characteristic moves that make this kind of reasoning unanswerable — missing evidence explained as suppressed evidence, scholarly consensus treated as itself proof of the cover-up, and every hard question deflected by merely asking further questions — so that a reader finishes with a felt sense of the real problem of demarcating pseudohistory from honest but heterodox scholarship. Do not name any real people or actual fringe authors.
