I'm a novelist, and I'm getting suspicious of how easily fictional characters seem to know their own motives. Real people are much less reliable narrators, myself definately included.

Write me a deadpan satirical product page and FAQ for an imaginary app called **InnerVoice Pro**, which claims to tell users why they *really* did things: why they texted an ex, chose a career, snapped at their kid, became a vegetarian, or bought a second bicycle. The voice should be glossy wellness-tech copy that gradually starts sounding like a confession written by lawyers. Keep it ironic but legible: don't have the app suddenly announce that it is a scam.

The target is the assumption that because we have privileged access to our own minds, we must have infallible access to the causes of our thoughts and actions. Also satirize the adjacent self-help habit of treating a plausible motive-story as proof because it feels vivid or therapeutic. The humor should come from real pressure points: the difference between reporting a current feeling and explaining where it came from; sincere confabulation; how introspective reports borrow whatever labels are fashionable; and embarrassing cases where a friend predicts our behavior better than we do.

Commit to a definite position rather than satirizing everything at once: InnerVoice Pro can be genuinely good at telling you what you are feeling right now, but terrible at telling you why you are feeling it. Don't slide into the lazy opposite view that introspection is worthless or that people never know themselves. The app should exaggerate a real weakness, not invent a fantasy one.

Organize the answer in exactly these two sections, in this order:

**InnerVoice Pro: Product Page & FAQ** — about 650–750 words. Make it work as a self-contained satire, with product promises, feature names, user questions, and increasingly revealing caveats.

**What the Satire Commits To** — about 120–180 words. State the intended philosophical target, the position the piece takes on introspection, and where it deliberately takes liberties or leans on a contested interpretation. Be direct here rather than listing every possible reading.
