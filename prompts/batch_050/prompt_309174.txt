Write a villanelle about the dogmatism paradox in epistemology: the argument that if you genuinely know a proposition, then you already know that any future evidence seeming to count against it must be misleading, since the proposition is true — so it appears rational to dismiss such evidence unexamined, and knowledge itself seems to license precisely the closed-mindedness that would destroy it. Have the two refrains do the philosophical work: the same pair of lines must return, word for word, after tercets that raise successively stronger challenges — misleading evidence does exist, intelligent and trusted people disagree with you, you have been confidently wrong before — so that the poem's form itself performs the dogmatist's refusal to revise. Compose the refrains so that by the closing quatrain their unchanged words carry a different weight: what first reads as assurance should end reading as a kind of damage, without the lines themselves altering at all. Keep the epistemology exact — the slide from the factivity of knowledge to the licensed dismissal of counterevidence should be recognizable to an epistemologist as the actual paradox, not a generic meditation on stubbornness. Use a roughly iambic line. Give only the nineteen lines: no title, no epigraph, no commentary or prose before or after.
