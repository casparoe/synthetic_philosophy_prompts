Build me a thought experiment that isolates the grounding objection to Molinism -- the worry that counterfactuals of creaturely freedom have no truthmakers at the moment God consults his middle knowledge -- in a way that can't be waved off with the usual "they're just true, prior to the decree" line. Specs: (1) an agent makes a free choice in circumstances that a creator deliberately selected partly because of how the agent would respond; (2) the scenario has to make vivid that, at the moment of consulting, neither the agent nor anything else exists that could make the relevant counterfactual true rather than false; (3) include a parallel secular version -- say a human orchestrator with near-perfect predictive powers over someone who already exists -- where the grounding worry plainly dissappears, so the disanalogy shows up cleanly. After the scenarios, walk through: what the Molinist says is doing the guiding, what the objector says is missing, and which single feature of the God-version carries all the weight (would the objection survive if the creator merely foreknows rather than decrees?). End with the strongest Molinist counter-move -- presumably something about whether truthmakers have to temporally precede what they make true -- and say honestly whether it answers the case or just restates the theory it's defending.
