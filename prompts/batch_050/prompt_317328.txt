I’m helping design a two-year research programme on natural kinds, with enough money for one philosopher and a few scientists from different fields to meet regularly. The question is whether scientific classification really carves nature at its joints, but that’s obviously too broad to fund as stated.

Which two or three open questions should the programme prioritise, and in what order? I’m especially interested in questions that sit between cases rather than questions about just one science: for example, whether kinds have to support induction to count as natural; whether a category can be natural at one scale but artificial at another; whether biological, psychiatric and social kinds need a different account from chemical or physical ones; and whether practical usefulness can make a kind natural without making every useful category natural.

One possible organising idea is what I’ll call a portability test: a classification is more natural the more it lets researchers carry successful explanations and predictions from one context, method or scale into another, rather than merely being convenient inside one specialised practice. Please assess whether that idea is worth building the programme around, and where it might break down.

Don’t give me a survey or reading list. I want a defended agenda: state your ranking criteria, say which questions are upstream of the others, which are tractable in two years, which are likely to produce only verbal disagreement, and what would make you change the ranking. Please also say how confident you are in the overall recommendation and explicitly identify the claim you are least sure about. Answer in British English, and don’t worry about being comprehesive.
