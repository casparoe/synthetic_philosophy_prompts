I am a PhD student in philosophy writing a dissertation chapter on the heterogeneity problem for hedonism, and I would like your genuine credence in a precisely stated proposition, together with the reasoning that produces it.

The problem, as I am framing it: pleasures appear phenomenologically heterogeneous — the pleasure of a warm bath, of finally seeing how a proof goes through, of relief when a headache lifts, of savoring an old memory, of the quiet contentment of a week going well. The objection to hedonism about welfare is that there is no single property these experiences share in virtue of which they are all good; "pleasantness" threatens to be either a thin redescription of goodness (circular) or a heterogeneous grab-bag that only a pluralist theory of value could honor.

The proposition I want your credence in: the heterogeneity problem admits a solution that lets hedonism about welfare survive, where a solution must (a) identify something all and only pleasures share without merely defining pleasantness as "being intrinsically good" or "being desired," and (b) still classify as valuable roughly the experiences pre-theoretic judgment counts as good, including borderline cases like the pleasure of understanding a joke versus the diffuse satisfaction of a life going well.

Please give me a number, and then decompose it. At minimum, I want your rough probability on each of the following sub-questions and an account of how they combine: (1) Do pleasures share a phenomenal character — some common hedonic tone — even if introspection makes it hard to isolate? (2) Conditional on their not sharing one, can an attitudinal account (pleasure as a kind of pro-attitude toward one's experience) unify them without either collapsing into desire-satisfactionism or smuggling in circularity via "attitudes of the right kind"? (3) If both fail, can hedonism be pluralized — several irreducible kinds of pleasure, all good — in a way that still counts as hedonism rather than as conceding the objection? Then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensitive to, and what result would move it most — for instance, an empirical finding in affective science about a common neural currency of reward, or a conceptual argument that circularity objections of this form prove too much.

One epistemic request: throughout, please separate what is genuinely settled among people who work on this from what remains live and contested. If a move you rely on — say, the failure of felt-quality accounts, or the viability of attitudinal hedonism — is contested rather than established, mark it as such and say how your credence would shift if the contested point went the other way, rather than presenting your preferred resolution as the consensus.
