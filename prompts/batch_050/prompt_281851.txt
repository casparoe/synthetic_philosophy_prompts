I recently watched a colleague lose their position, several speaking invitations, and a number of friendships over about two weeks following an online campaign against them, and my professional association has since asked me to help draft its policy on responding to such episodes. Before I read any of the existing literature on cancel culture and deplatforming, I want the specification first. Write the desiderata that any adequate theory of social sanction as punishment must meet: what it has to say about the boundary between punishment on the one hand and mere criticism, avoidance, or withdrawal of association on the other; whether an intention to make the target suffer is necessary, and how the theory handles cases in which no individual participant intends or foresees the aggregate harm; what it must say about proportionality when the sanction is crowd-sourced and no one controls its total weight; whether it must identify who has standing to impose the sanction, given that there is no sovereign here; and what it must say about the absence of anything like a hearing or an appeal. Give the case or consideration motivating each requirement, flag any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, and identify which single requirement does the most to eliminate candidate views. Then, briefly, score the obvious contenders — a forward-looking deterrent account, a retributive account, and a deflationary view holding that these episodes are not punishment at all but merely the exercise of others' freedom of association — against the list, keeping the emphasis on the criteria rather than the scoring.
