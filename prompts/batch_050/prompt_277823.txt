Take the gilets jaunes blowup in France in late 2018 — Macron's goverment schedules another hike in the carbon tax on diesel and petrol, rural and outer-suburban France erupts, there are protests and roundabouts occupied every weekend, and within weeks the increase gets frozen and then scrapped entirely. I want you to analyze the episode through the lens of textbook carbon pricing theory: Pigouvian taxation, revenue recycling, the double dividend, salience effects, the works. First do the mapping in that direction — what does the standard model say the government should have done with the revenue, the timing, and the packaging, and how much of the fiasco can you actually pin on bad design versus stuff the model doesn't even have variables for (fairness perceptions, distrust of Paris elites, the fact that the hike landed right after a tax cut for the wealthy, people having no realistic alternative to driving)? Then flip it around. The degrowth reading is roughly that people rejected a green surcharge bolted onto an unjust growth economy and that no amount of dividend-design tinkering fixes that — does that fit the facts better than the wonkish "just recycle the revenue properly" story, or does it overclaim in its own way? Finish by saying what, if anything, the episode should actually change about how economists design and sell carbon taxes — is it a confirmation of the theory with better implementation needed, an anomaly, or a case the theory never really anticipated? Don't sit on the fence at the end; pick a position.
