Write a parody FAQ page for a fictional retail bank, "Stakes First National," whose customer service policies are built on pragmatic encroachment — the idea that whether you count as *knowing* something depends partly on how much is at stake for you practically, not just on your evidence. The bank should explain to customers, in cheery corporate FAQ-speak, that whether a customer "knows" the branch is open on Saturday depends on how badly they need to deposit their check before Monday: if a $20 fee hangs on it, their memory of last week's hours is fine, but if a foreclosue hangs on it, then sorry, they didn't know, and should have called ahead to check. Keep it totally deadpan, around 550-700 words total, with the jokes coming from the actual philosophy (stakes-sensitivity, the knowledge norm of action, maybe a jab at invariantists who'd run a bank where everyone knows the same things regardless of what's riding on it), so an epistemologist would find it funny because it's *fair*, not because it misstates the view.

Structure it like this:
- "Frequently Asked Questions" — the bulk of it, maybe 5-6 questions with answers, each landing a different joke (e.g., a question about why the customer's knowledge got revoked when they took out a bigger loan, a question about the bank's premium "Higher Standards" checking account, a question from a confused contextualist asking whether the teller's *conversation* matters, to which the bank replies it doesn't care what conversation you're having, only what you'd lose)
- "A Note from Our Invariantist Competitors" — a short section (~100 words) where the bank trash-talks the rival bank down the street whose tellers insist customers know things even when it'd be ruinous to act on it
- "Fine Print" — 2-3 lines of terms-and-conditions-style boilerplate that's secretly one more philosophy joke

Don't explain the jokes anywhere in the FAQ itself; it should read like the bank genuinely doesn't see the problem.
