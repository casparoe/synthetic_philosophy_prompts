Something I keep noticing and I'm wondering if philosophers have a name for it. Say I overhear two strangers on a train arguing about which bridge floods first in heavy rain, and I just find myself believing them -- way more than I would if one of them turned around and told me the same thing directly. Or I find someone's old notebook full of soil pH measurements and I trust it more than if they had handed it to me to win an argument. The odd thing is that my confidence seems to go *up* precisely when the person wasn't trying to inform me at all: no intended audience, sometimes no intention that anyone would ever hear or read it. I know there's a big debate in epistemology about whether believing what people tell you requires independent positive reasons or whether being told something is itself a defeasible source of justification. But that whole debate seems to assume a speaker addressing a hearer. Is there an established term for this kind of case -- information you get from people who weren't testifying *to you*, or arguably to anyone at all -- and does it even count as testimony, or is it just ordinary evidence like a footprint or a tree ring? And if its not testimony, does that suggest the reductionism vs. anti-reductionism fight covers a narrower range of cases than either side tends to admit? Please don't just hand me a label, I want to know where the boundaries of the concept actually fall.
