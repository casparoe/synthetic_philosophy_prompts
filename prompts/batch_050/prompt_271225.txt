Okay so context: last month my brother-in-law backed my car into a pole and his whole defense, to me and to my wife, is that the way the evening unfolded he basically couldn't have done otherwise, and somehow this turned into a huge recurring argument at family dinners about whether "couldn't have done otherwise" actually gets you off the hook. A philosophy friend of mine said this is literally what Frankfurt cases are about and dared me to come up with one that doesn't cheat.

So: construct a thought experiment where someone does something blameworthy, is genuinely responsible for it, and genuinely could not have done otherwise. Constraints:

1. No mad neuroscientists, no brain chips, no demons, no counterfactual mind-readers. Everything in the scenario has to be stuff that could plausibly happen at an actual family gathering or workplace. If your mechanism for removing alternatives is a person standing by ready to intervene, they can be at most as good at predicting behavior as a spouse who knows you very well.
2. This is the part my friend says everyone fails at: there can't be a "prior sign." In a lot of these cases the agent was about to show some tell (an urge, a hesitation, reaching for something) and the intervener would have pounced on the tell, which lets the defender of the principle of alternative possibilities say the agent could at least have avoided the tell, so some alternative possibility survives. I want a case where whatever guarantees the outcome works independently of anything the agent was about to do or feel first.
3. Keep it to one or two paragraphs and make the agent's choice actually theirs when left alone -- they do the thing for their own reasons, the backup mechanism never fires, and it has to be clear the outcome would have been the same even if they'd started to waver.

After the scenario, walk me through it: why does the agent lack alternatives, why are they still responsible, which feature of the case is doing the real work in killing the flicker-of-freedom reply, and what's the strongest move a defender of "ought implies could have done otherwise" still has against it. Also tell me honestly whether my brother-in-law gets anything out of this, because I suspect he doesn't -- nobody was standing by to stop him from driving -- but my wife thinks determinism alone does the same job as your mechanism and I want to be able to say why that's different (or admit it isn't).
