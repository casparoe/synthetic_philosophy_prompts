I'm trying to work out, from my own case judgments, when a bare generic of the form "Ks F" or "Ks are F" is acceptable, and I'd like the principle behind my verdicts, if there is one. Here are the verdicts:

- "Ducks lay eggs" strikes me as plainly true, even though only females do it, and only seasonally; "ducks are female" strikes me as false, even though the very same birds are involved.
- "Mosquitoes carry malaria" is true, even though only a small minority of mosquitoes carry it; but "vending machines kill people" sounds misleading or outright false to me, even though vending machines kill more people in a typical year than sharks do, and I'd accept "sharks attack swimmers."
- "Books are paperbacks" is false even though most books are paperbacks. So majority prevalence seems to be neither necessary nor sufficient for a true generic.
- "Sea turtles return to the beach where they hatched" sounds fine; "sea turtles reach adulthood" sounds false, even though both concern a characteristic life-stage event of the species and almost all hatchlings die.
- "Ticks carry Lyme disease" and "mosquitoes carry malaria" both sound true, while "mosquitoes carry the common cold" sounds false. Something about danger or significance seems to be doing work here — but then I can't explain why "books are paperbacks" fails when it is statistically truer than the malaria claim.
- "A good referee doesn't favor the home team" and "boys don't cry" are both perfectly acceptable sentences, but they seem to operate by a different, normative mechanism, and I'm unsure the same principle should cover them at all.

Is there one coherent account of generic acceptance that delivers these verdicts, or nearly all of them? If so, state it precisely enough that it yields predictions for cases I haven't listed — in particular "cats purr," "lightning kills golfers," "Germans are punctual," and "this café's croissants sell out by nine." If no single principle captures everything, tell me which verdict you would sacrifice and why, and whether the normative cases require a genuinely separate story or can be folded in.
