I write a long-form science-and-ideas column, and I'm preparing a piece on a small but surprisingly durable dispute in metaphysics: how to analyze dispositions -- fragility, solubility, conductivity -- given the well-known troublemakers called finks and masks. For a sidebar to the article, I want to run an exercise in adversarial collaboration, and I'd like you to draft the joint statement that the two camps at this dispute should be able to produce together.

The two camps are these. First, the conditional analysts: philosophers who hold that saying a glass is fragile is, at bottom, saying something conditional -- roughly, that it would break if struck -- and who believe the analysis can be repaired rather than abandoned, even granting the standard counterexamples: the fink case (a live wire attached to a device that would instantly render it dead the moment anything touched it, so the conditional fails though the wire is live), and the reverse, intrinsic finks, where an object would lose its disposition in the very process of the stimulus. Second, the primitivists: philosophers who hold that masks settle the matter against any conditional analysis -- a fragile vase swaddled in packing foam that would absorb any blow is still fragile, yet the conditional "would break if struck" is false of it -- and who conclude that dispositions are irreducible modal features of things, directed at their manifestations in a way no conditional captures, with finks and masks merely exposing the same underlying mismatch. The analysts reply that masks show only that disposition ascriptions come with ceteris paribus structure, not that they're unanalyzable.

Please draft the document these two camps would sign. I want: (a) a numbered list of claims both sides accept -- for instance, what exactly the canonical fink and mask cases show, and what they don't show -- phrased so neither camp is conceding its position; (b) a statement of each remaining disagreement in neutral wording that each side would endorse as a fair rendering of its own view, taking care to separate genuine disagreements from ones that are merely terminological (I suspect some of the dispute over whether finks and masks are "the same phenomenon" is verbal, but I may be wrong); (c) the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one camp, where my guess is that the status of intrinsic finks and the correct treatment of the ceteris paribus clause are among them; and (d) for each crux, at least one case or argument both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance. To keep the exercise honest, please include this novel test case of mine in section (d): a demolition charge fitted with a "dead-man" circuit wired so that pressing the trigger simultaneously severs the connection between the trigger and the explosive -- both camps must say, and sign, whether the charge "has the disposition to explode when triggered" and what their respective analyses predict about it.
