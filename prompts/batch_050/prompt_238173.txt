I am a graduate student who has been circling the extended mind debate for a seminar, and I want to stop talking about Otto's notebook, which everyone in the room has an entrenched position on, and instead work through a case none of us has a settled intuition about. Consider my colleague Mara. For the past three years she has worn an earpiece assistant, trained on a decade of her own notes, email, and calendar, which answers factual questions in her ear in under a second. Three features of the setup seem to me to matter. First, the device does not store her records verbatim; it keeps compressed summaries and regenerates answers on demand, and it confabulates on roughly two percent of queries -- about as often, her neurologist jokes, as Mara's own biological memory does. Second, it is unavailable while charging overnight and in a few dead zones on her commute, so she has fluent access to it perhaps fifteen hours a day. Third, and this is the part I keep fixating on: Mara has largely stopped encoding certain facts biologically at all. When her grant officer emailed that the annual report is due March 3rd, she never read the message; the device logged the deadline, and she simply trusts that the date is available to her, the way you trust that your own memory holds a phone number you never rehearsed.

So here is the proposition I want your credence on, stated as precisely as I can manage: right now, before she has consulted the device, Mara believes that her grant report is due March 3rd. Please give me a number, but what I actually care about is the derivation. Decompose it into, at minimum: the probability that the correct account of belief individuation is functionalist enough that information stored outside the skull can constitute a standing belief at all, given everything you take to be wrong with the coupling-versus-constitution objection; the probability that, conditional on such an account being correct, this particular coupling meets the relevant criteria, where I want you to say explicitly which criterion is doing the most work against her -- the overnight unavailability, the two percent confabulation rate, or the fact that she endorses the device's outputs automatically but could not, in a moment of genuine doubt, tell device-delivered information apart from what she remembers natively; and whatever discount you apply to the first term for the possibility that the intuition that beliefs live in the head is tracking something real about what belief is, rather than being biochauvinist residue. A number and one or two lines of justification per term, then how the terms combine into your headline figure.

For calibration: my own credence is about 0.65, my advisor's is about 0.1, and we cannot locate the disagreement -- we both claim to accept the same functionalist framework, so I suspect one of us is being inconsistent somewhere in the middle terms. Tell me which sub-question our gap most likely lives in, and name the single piece of evidence, empirical or philosophical, that would move your own final number the most in either direction.
