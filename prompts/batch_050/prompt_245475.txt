I am trying to work out when one member of a group may take an irreversible step on the group's behalf without the others' agreement, and I would like to know whether my verdicts on the following cases sit under any single principle.

First, my verdicts. Five households share a private lane. One of them, tired of the potholes, hires a contractor to resurface it and sends the others an invoice: out of order, even though the lane genuinely needed doing. The same neighbour spends a Saturday picking litter off the lane without asking anyone: perfectly fine. One member of a research group posts a joint preprint after the others ask for another month of checking: wrong, even though a preprint can later be withdrawn. One engineer leaks documents showing that her firm concealed a safety defect, against the wishes of colleagues who helped gather the material: right, perhaps even obligatory. The company secretary of a small charity, unable to get replies from the other trustees, signs and files the annual return on deadline day: fine, arguably required. An on-call paramedic administers a promising off-label treatment which local protocols reserve for joint sign-off: wrong, even if the patient does well.

My tentative principle was: act unilaterally only when the step is reversible or when delay would forfeit the option altogether. But the preprint is withdrawable and still wrong, while the leak is irreversible and still right, so the principle is simultaneously too permissive and too restrictive. What repaired principle, or small set of principles, captures as many of these verdicts as possible? Please state it precisely, indicate which clause each case motivates, say honestly which of my verdicts you think must be abandoned, and give its verdicts on two cases I have not settled: a co-author who unilaterally retracts a paper after discovering an error the others consider minor, and a forum moderator who bans a popular member before a scheduled vote, fearing the vote will go the other way. Please reply in British English.
