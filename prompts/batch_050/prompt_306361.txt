I'm a journalist covering science and ideas, and I'm working on a column about who deserves what in an economy where more and more of the "hands" doing the work are contractors, gig workers, or outright machines. I keep coming back to Locke, and specifically to one line in chapter V of the Second Treatise that I think contains a time bomb nobody put in the headline version of him. Here it is (Locke, Second Treatise of Government, ch. V, §28):

"Thus the Grass my Horse has bit; the Turfs my Servant has cut; and the Ore I have digg'd in any place where I have a right to them in common with others, become my Property, without the assignation or consent of any body."

Notice what just happened. Two paragraphs earlier the whole theory rests on my labour being unmistakably mine, and on mixing that labour with the common to make something mine. But the servant cut the turfs. His hands, his sweat, his labour mixed with the common -- and yet Locke says, without flagging it as a problem, that the turfs become MY property. If labour-mixing is what generates title, the servant seems to have the stronger claim by Locke's own lights, and the fact that Locke slides past this makes me suspect the theory quietly assumes what it was supposed to prove.

Here's what I want from you, and please commit rather than survey the options: your credence (a number) that this is right -- that if the labour-mixing account of original acquisition is basically sound, then in cases like the servant's the property should default to the person whose labour was actually mixed, unless some genuinely independent mechanism (like consent via the wage) transfers it, and that Locke's unmarked slide to the master is a real crack in the theory rather than loose prose. But decompose it for me. Something like: the probability that labour-mixing grounds acquisition at all (given the standard tomato-juice-style objections); given that it does, the probability that the theory is indifferent between the labourer and the person paying him, versus actually privileging the hands; the probability that a wage contract counts as the kind of independent transfer that could do the work, given that the servant in a state-of-nature-style example arguably never consented to anything; and whatever other terms you think the credence actually depends on. Give each term a rough number with a line of justification, combine them, and then tell me which sub-question your final number is most sensative to and what argument or evidence would move it most. If you think I've misread the passage, that's fine -- say so, put it in the decomposition, and price it in.
