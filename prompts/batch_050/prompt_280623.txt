In their 2018 paper "AI Safety via Debate," Geoffrey Irving, Paul Christiano, and Dario Amodei propose training AI systems through a zero-sum game: two agents take turns making short statements on opposite sides of a question, and a human judge rewards whichever gave the most true, useful information. They motivate the proposal by noting the limits of direct judgment: "We could imagine a system trained to both give answers and point out flaws in answers; this gives a third level of difficulty. Flaws themselves may be too hard to judge: flaws could have their own flaws that must be pointed out to a human. And flaws of flaws can have flaws, etc." Their bet is that "optimal play in this game produces honest, aligned information far beyond the capabilities of the human judge."

Treat debate not as an engineering proposal but as a practice with an implicit epistemology, and reconstruct that epistemology. What view of argument does the protocol presuppose -- in particular, does it assume that every false or misleading claim has a defect that can be exposed locally, in a single move the judge can follow, and that recursive flaw-pointing converges on truth rather than on rhetorical advantage? What must it assume about the honest debater's prospects against an adversary who constructs an obfuscated argument, each of whose steps looks valid in isolation and whose flaw is visible only by holding the whole structure in view at once? Identify which rival views of justification the practice quietly rules out (for instance, holistic views on which warrant depends on global features of a body of beliefs, or views on which some truths admit no short, checkable justification), and say whether the practice strictly needs its strong presuppositions or could be restricted to domains where they safely hold. Take a definite position on whether debate, judged by the commitments it presupposes, can be trusted in domains where the judge cannot verify answers directly, and give your reasons rather than a menu of options. State your final verdict as a short phrase in the form \answer{...}.
