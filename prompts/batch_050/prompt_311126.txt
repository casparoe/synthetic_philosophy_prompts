Here's the practice I'm puzzled by. Copyright offices (the US one is the clearest case) have landed on roughly this policy for AI-generated content: an image or text produced entirely by an AI system from a prompt can't be registered, because there's no human author. A work where a human used AI as a tool can be registered, but only the human-contributed parts get protection, and applicants are supposed to disclaim the AI-generated parts. When someone tried to register an artwork naming his AI system itself as the author, it was flatly refused. And famously, when a macaque snapped selfies with a photographer's camera, the idea that anyone — monkey or photographer — held copyright in them was rejected.

Now here's the part that gets me. Photographs are obviously copyrightable. Even photos where the human contribution is paper-thin: an auto-timed camera, a trail cam left in the woods for a month, a long exposure where the photographer just pressed a button and walked away. Nobody blinks at the photographer owning those. So on one side of the line we say "a human pressed a button on a machine that did literally all of the image-making — that's authorship," and on the other side we say "a human wrote a detailed prompt, iterated it sixty times, curated hundreds of outputs — not authorship." I honestly can't see the principle doing the work here.

So: what theory of authorship and creativity does this patchwork presuppose? Some candidate ingredients to consider while reconstructing it: maybe it's that authorship requires intentional control over the specific expressive elements (the photographer supposedly "controls" the camera in a way the prompter doesn't control the model)? Maybe it's a causal story — there has to be a person at the origin of the causal chain, and prompt-writers are too causally "upstream" relative to what the model does? Maybe it's really just personhood — only persons can author, the monkey and AI exclusions are doing that work, and the photography side is doing something else entirely? Or is it a labor/desert theory wearing a disguise?
