Nietzsche in the second Untimely Meditation splits history into monumental, antiquarian and critical, and his whole point is that history is only worth anything insofar as it serves life — each mode helps in the right dose and damages us when it gets out of hand. Second: either way, what would its "disadvantage for life" be in Nietzsche's terms, and is there any dose at which it serves life — e.g. could instant access to a flattened average of the past actually free people from oppressive monumental narratives, which was supposed to be the critical mode's job?
