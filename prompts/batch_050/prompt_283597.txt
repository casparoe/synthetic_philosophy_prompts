I help run the discussion nights at a rationalist meetup — think readers of the Sequences, software engineers, a few biologists, people comfortable with the map-territory distinction but with no formal philosophy. Next month I'm presenting the extended mind thesis (Clark and Chalmers's Otto-and-his-notebook argument) in about thirty minutes, and I want a teaching plan, not a script: how to get this audience from zero to being able to defend the thesis against the two or three objections they will inevitably raise themselves.

Design the plan around these questions. First, the order of ideas: what has to be established before the notebook case can land, and what only makes sense after it? In particular, I'm unsure whether to introduce ordinary content externalism (Twin Earth, meanings not in the head) before or after Otto, since the extended mind thesis is a claim about vehicles of cognition, not content, and getting that wrong early seems fatal. Second, the carrying example: the notebook case is famous, but is it the right one for this audience, and what properties must whichever example I use have (reliability, fluency of access, automatic endorsement) and conspicuously lack (consciousness, being inside the skull) to do its job without inviting the obvious slippery slope where my laptop's entire hard drive counts as my memory? Third, the misconception map: name the three or four ways this specific audience will predictably misunderstand the thesis — I suspect conflating it with content externalism, treating it as the trivial claim that tools causally influence thinking, and inferring that the internet is one giant mind — and say at which point in the sequence each should be defused rather than answered later. Fourth, one check for understanding I can run live that would actually discriminate between someone who has the gears-level version and someone who just assents to the slogan. Finally, tell me what to openly refuse to cover at this level and flag as deferred, so the talk doesn't die in a caveats swamp.

Keep the whole plan under 800 words.
