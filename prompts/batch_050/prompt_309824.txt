I've been reading LessWrong and the EA Forum for years so I feel like I should be able to settle this myself, but I keep flip-flopping. Two people in my local rationality circle, J and M, had a row last week. J had drafted a pretty scathing critique of a big org's grantmaking and then binned it, citing the unilateralist's curse: in a community of a few hundred people who can each publish unilaterally, what gets done is whatever the keenest person wants done, so the fact that he was the keenest to publish is itself evidence his estimate was overcooked, and he should discount his own judgement.

M called this self-serving rubbish. Her points: (1) the curse assumes everyone's estimates are unbiased and independant, but people here have huge incentives not to slag off major orgs — funding, jobs, friendships — so the amount of published criticism sits well below the true average judgement, meaning J's estimate is if anything too low; (2) a norm that universalises to nobody ever criticising anyone can't be right; (3) J treated everyone else's silence as data about their private judgements, when silence under selective incentives tells you nothing.

J's replies: the curse is exactly about updating on others' actions when you can't see their information, and not-publishing is an action; universalising the reasoning properly wouldn't produce silence, just a higher bar that well-founded critiques still clear; and M's incentive story is unfalsifiable, since any silence at all can be waved away as fear rather than judgement.

M's last move: the curse models estimation errors as random noise, whereas here the errors are systematic social pressure, so it's simply the wrong model, and J was dressing up conflict-aversion in decision-theory language.

Can you adjudicate the individual moves rather than just declaring an overall winner? In particular: is everyone else's silence actually evidence J should update on, or is M right that it's noise? Does the universalising argument work? And is there something both of them are missing? British English please.
