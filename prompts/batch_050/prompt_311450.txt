So the strategy-stealing assumption, as used in alignment discussions: roughly, the claim that for almost any strategy an unaligned AI could use to take over, there exists a corresponding strategy an aligned AI could execute just as effectively, with the aligned AI's values swapped in as the goal -- so if we can build an aligned AI that's competitive on raw capability, alignment alone doesn't lose us the future. The objections I've already seen, and don't need repeated: (1) the objection from coordination costs -- a human/aligned coalition wastes resources on internal alignment and enforcement that a single unaligned agent doesn't, so "same strategy, swapped goal" isn't capability-neutral; (2) the objection that some strategies are only available to an agent willing to do things aligned systems refuse to do (scorched-earth threats, breaking commitments, exploiting humans it claims to serve), so the strategy space isn't symmetric; (3) the objection that strategy-stealing requires knowing the opponent's strategy to steal it, and superhuman strategies may be unrecognizable or unexecutable by us in time; (4) the no-free-lunch objection that the very features making a strategy good may depend on the goal (e.g., deception works better when nobody suspects your true aims, and an aligned AI can't fake aligned aims). Is there a fifth objection I'm missing -- genuinely different, not a rewording or special case of these four? Give me your best candidate and then actually commit: say whether you think it seriously dents the assumption or not, and why, rather than hedging both ways. American English please.
