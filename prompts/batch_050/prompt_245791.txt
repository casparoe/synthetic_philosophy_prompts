I sit on my university's apparel licensing committee — we decide which factories get to make the logo hoodies sold in the campus store. Our code of conduct has a clause nobody has ever had to actually interpret: wages must reflect "a fair share of the value the worker helps produce," not merely beat whatever else is on offer locally. The case before us: a factory abroad pays about 40% above the regional going wage, its jobs are the most sought-after in the area, and workers told our auditor they'd be furious if we pulled the contract. But on any reasonable reckoning of the surplus per shirt, workers capture very little — the factory's margin and our own licensing fee both dwarf the wage bill. Student groups say renewing makes us complicit in exploitation; the supplier's position is basically that the workers' own choices settle the question. Draft the written opinion I'll circulate before the vote: recommend renew, renew with conditions (specify actual conditions and mechanisms, not vague aspirations), or exit. The opinion needs to take seriously the argument that a transaction both parties freely choose, and that leaves both better off than their next-best alternative, cannot be wrongfully exploitative — and then say what "fair share" could even mean if that argument is right (maybe nothing beyond the market wage), and what it should mean if the argument is wrong. Whatever we recommend, give the strongest version of the losing side and say honestly what it would take to answer it.
