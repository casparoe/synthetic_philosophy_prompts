A buddy of mine wants me to go in on this trading scheme with him. The way it works: you put in your whole stake, and every month it either goes up 50% or down 40%, and he's confident its basically a coin flip which one happens. He's run the numbers and the expected return per month is positive -- on average you make about 5% a month, which beats any index fund by miles. So he says anyone who passes on it is just being irrationally risk-averse, since the math obviously favors playing.

But when I picture actually doing it -- reinvesting everything, month after month, for years -- I have this persistent feeling I'd end up poorer than I started, maybe nearly wiped out, even though I can't find the error in his calculation. Each month the expected value is genuinely positive. So where's the catch?

Who's right here? If he's wrong, tell me exactly where the mistake lives, because "on average you gain 5%" doesn't seem like it contains an error. And if he's right, explain why my doom-feeling is so compelling anyway. Either way, I'd like the general lesson: when is "the average outcome" the right thing to base a decision on, and when does it actively mislead you?
