I teach the Phenomenology every other year, and a pattern in academic life keeps reminding me of the lordship and bondage chapter, though I have become convinced it is not quite the thing Hegel diagnosed. Three instances. First, a senior colleague of mine, much decorated, circulates drafts and begs for criticism; he receives only praise, and he has told me he no longer believes a word of it -- not because he thinks his readers are lying, but because he cannot tell. Second, a doctoral student described her admiration for her supervisor as entirely genuine, and then immediately wondered aloud whether she would feel it if her career did not depend on him; she now discounts her own judgment. Third, I have noticed the same structure in myself: praise from my students moves me less than identical praise from strangers, even when I have every reason to think the students' praise is accurate.

What strikes me is that nothing here need be insincere. Hegel's point, on the standard reading, is that the lord is cheated because recognition from an unfree consciousness cannot satisfy him -- the source is defective. In my cases the source may be in perfect working order; what fails is that neither party can rule out the structural explanation, and the mere availability of that explanation, known to both sides, does the damage even if it is false. It also seems distinct from preference falsification, where people conceal what they actually think, and from the literature on adaptive or deformed desires, where the deformation is real; here the recognition might be fully warranted and still fail to transmit. And while an economist might say the praise is simply costless talk, that misses the student's case, where the doubt infects the praising itself rather than its reception.

Does this configuration have a name of its own, in or out of the recognition literature? If so, I want more than the label: what exactly is the phenomenon, how does it differ from Hegel's diagnosis and from each of the adjacent notions above, and where are its edges -- does it arise under dependence without hierarchy, as in a child's love for a parent, and does it require that the recognition actually be discounted, or only that it be discountable? If there is no established name, please coin one, define it, and mark the boundary cases.
