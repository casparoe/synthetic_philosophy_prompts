I'm putting together a revision quiz for my philosophy of mind students on the simulation argument, and I want it to sort them by what they actually believe rather than by which name they've heard of. Write me a decision tree that starts at the node "I take seriously the claim that we might be living in a computer simulation" and ends at named positions. The first branch should be about whether a computer could be conscious at all (substrate independence), the next about whether simulating a world like ours down to whatever detail matters is physically feasible for any plausible future civilization, the next about whether anyone would actually bother or be allowed to run such simulations even if they could, and only after that the counting argument about simulated vs non-simulated observer-moments. Phrase every branch as a question someone could answer without already knowing which position it sorts them into. Each leaf should get a name (Bostrom's own agnostic three-way disjunction, the biological naturalist's no, the feasibility skeptic, the indifference/curtailment horn, full-blown we-are-almost-certainly-simulated, Chalmers-style "it's real anyway" virtual realism, and at least one position nobody seems to defend) plus one sentence on its most awkward commitment. Also, somewhere in the tree add a branch about whether being simulated would change how you ought to live, beacuse my students always end up there. Keep it to about 8-10 leaves, flag any pair of leaves your tree can't genuinely separate, and for any combination of answers nobody occupies, say whether that's because the view is incoherent or merely unpopular.
