I want the principle behind my verdicts about pleasure, if there is one — a solution to the heterogeneity problem, or a demonstration that my verdicts cannot be jointly saved. Here are the cases, with my judgments as they actually strike me:

1. A hot bath after a day spent in cold rain. Pleasure, paradigm case, with a felt quality I could point to if asked where the pleasure resides.
2. Finally seeing the answer to a crossword clue that nagged me all afternoon. Pleasure, certainly — but as far as I can tell there is no feeling here at all, let alone one shared with the bath.
3. Scratching a mosquito bite. Intense, compulsive, locally very good — and yet I deny this is a pleasure. Relief is not pleasure.
4. The end of a dull headache I had stopped noticing until it stopped. I call this a pleasure, even though it is structurally identical to case 3: the cessation of a bad, with no positive sensation added.
5. Reading that my preferred candidate won the election. I am pleased; I sit with the news and nothing happens in me that I could call a feeling. If being pleased is a pleasure, then pleasure need not be felt.
6. The masochist who enjoys his pain. I want to say the pain is not a pleasure but that he takes pleasure in it — so "pleasure in X" and "pleasure that is X" come apart.
7. Bitter coffee, aversive to me for years and now a pleasure. Whatever pleasure is, it appears to be acquirable by habituation without any change in the underlying sensation.

My tentative principle: a state is a pleasure if and only if the subject, at that moment, wants it to continue for its own sake. But I can already see it stumble. The crossword insight is over by the time I want anything; the masochist wants the pain to continue, yet by my own verdict the pain is not the pleasure; and there are things I want to continue that do not seem like pleasures at all — a deadline-driven flow state, for instance.

So: state the principle, or two or three live candidates, precisely. For each candidate, say which of my verdicts motivates which clause, what it predicts for cases I have not listed — the pleasure of a warm shower one is too distracted to notice, pleasure reported from a dream one cannot recall, a pleasure so mild one cannot tell whether it is present — and which of my verdicts it must overturn. If the cleanest theory overturns cases 3 and 4 by explaining the itch/headache asymmetry away as an artefact of how we attend to relief, I would like that case made honestly rather than stipulated. And if the right conclusion is that there is no unitary phenomenon here at all — that "pleasure" is a family-resemblance term and the heterogeneity problem dissolves rather than gets solved — say what my verdicts look like under that diagnosis, and which of them were tracking something real.
