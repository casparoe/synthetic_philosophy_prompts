So next month I'm giving a talk at the hospital where I did my clinical ethics fellowship -- it's their quarterly grand-rounds-style thing for the ethics committee plus whoever shows up. My thesis is basically Rachels' line pushed harder: the distinction between killing and letting die has no intrinsic moral significance, and the cases where it seems to matter (the bathtub contrast and so on) only show that other stuff is doing the work -- intentions, motives, the agent's relationship to the victim. But I'm adding my own wrinkle, which is that the distinction isn't even a *reliable proxy* for those things in clinical settings, so hospital policy that treats withdrawing ventilation as morally fine but a lethal injection as categorically different is resting on a confusion. I even have a bit arguing that withdrawing treatment is often more "doing-like" than a one-off injection, causally speaking.

The audience is the tricky part. It's maybe fifteen people: three or four palliative care physicians whose entire practice is built on the comfort of the withholding/withdrawing vs. euthanasia line (one of them, Dr. Haddad, is quite senior and quite sharp), a Catholic chaplain who'll come at this from doctrine on ordinary vs. extraordinary means, a couple of nurses, a hospital lawyer who mostly cares about liability, and one philosophy PhD who did her dissertation on Kamm and will be the only person in the room who's actually read the literature. Everyone else knows the distinction as *policy*, not as a philosophical problem.

Prepare me for the Q&A. I want the objections in roughly the order they'll actually come up, phrased the way *these* people would phrase them -- so the palliative care objections sound like a clinician talking about patients and families, not like a seminar participant. For each one: how serious is it really, what should I concede (I'm happy to give ground on policy pragmatics, less so on the core claim), and what do I say in the two minutes I'll get. Also flag the weakest joint in my own argument -- the place where, if the Kamm person pushes, I'm genuinely not sure I have an answer -- and tell me the question I'm hoping nobody asks, so I can at least have something ready if they do. One more thing: I suspect the chaplain and Dr. Haddad will tag-team in a way that's friendlier than it looks, so if you can game out how that goes, even better.
