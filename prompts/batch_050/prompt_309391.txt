Before I read any more proposed answers (eliminativism about the vector potential, holonomy interpretations where the loops are the fundamental ontology, "sophisticated" views that keep the gauge-variant stuff but quotient it somehow), I want the spec first. What would an account of gauge structure have to do to even count as an answer to the question of what's real?

So: write me the desiderata list. Every requirement should come with the case or result that motivates it, not just a label. Some things I suspect have to be on there: it has to handle the Aharonov-Bohm effect, where there's apparently a measurable phase difference and no local field to blame it on, which is the case everyone uses to beat up on everyone else; it has to explain why gauge transformations are supposed to be different in kind from ordinary symmetries like rotations -- if we don't quotient out rotated universes, why do we quotient out gauge-transformed potentials, and does the answer rely on something that cuts ice or just on the formalism happening to have redundancy in it; it has to say whether the hole argument is the same problem wearing a trench coat or a genuinely different one; it probably has to take seriously what constrained Hamiltonian mechanics and Dirac observables say, since that's where the formal claim that gauge generates no real change actually gets argued; and it has to deal with the awkward fact that gauge symmetry seems to come alive at boundaries, which looks bad for any view that says it was never real to begin with.

Mark which requirements are genuinely non-negotiable for any account and which are just one camp's taste dressed up as necessity.
