So I'm a retired chemist and about two years ago I fell down a philosophy rabbit hole, and now I'm in this little reading group that meets at the library. Last week we were doing a session on medieval Islamic theology, the Mu'tazila vs. the Ash'arites, and afterwards two members, Fatima and Greg, got into an argument that went on so long the librarian kicked us out. Fatima was defending the Mu'tazila line, roughly that good and bad are objective features of acts that human reason can know on its own, and God commands what is good because it is good. Her main move was: if good just means "whatever God commands," then saying God is just or praiseworthy becomes empty, since anything He did would count as just by definition, and the Quran's constant praise of God's justice would be trivial. Greg pushed the Ash'arite side: if moral truths exist independently and reason can access them, then there's a standard standing over God that He has to conform to, which compromises His omnipotence and sovereignty, and besides, God as creator owns His creation, so talk of Him "wronging" creatures is a category mistake. Fatima shot back that the Ash'arite can only avoid God commanding horrible things by saying God wouldn't do that, but on his view there's no fact of the matter about why He wouldn't, except His own say-so, so it's luck dressed up as piety. Greg then made a move I hadn't seen coming: he said the Mu'tazila position has an epistemic problem of its own, because rational people disagree wildly about what's good, so if morality were really luminous to reason we wouldn't need revelation to settle ethics at all, whereas on his view revelation genuinely does work.

I got home and realised I couldn't tell who'd won, and it's been nagging at me all week. Could you adjudicate their exchange move by move, like who's right about each specific point rather than just picking a winner? A couple of things I specifically want resolved: (1) does Fatima's "praising God becomes trivial" objection actually land, or can the divine command person answer it, (2) is Greg right that an independent moral standard really does limit God, or is that a confused picture of what "limiting" means, and (3) is Greg's disagreement-among-rational-people point a good reply to Fatima, or does it prove too much? And if there's something both of them are missing or assuming without noticing, I'd like to know that too, since I suspect there is.
