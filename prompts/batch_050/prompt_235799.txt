I'm a PhD student writing a dissertation chapter on modal conditions on knowledge, and I'm cataloguing objections to Nozick's tracking theory. My current list covers what I take to be the standard terrain:

(1) Closure failure: tracking permits me to know that I have hands while failing to know that I'm not a brain in a vat, and critics treat that combination as a reductio of the theory.
(2) Insensitivity-compatible-with-knowledge cases: Sosa's garbage chute (I know the bag will reach the basement even though, were it going to snag on the way down, I'd still believe it would arrive) and Kripke's grandmother (she sees that her grandson is well, but were he not, the family would shield her by telling her he is well).
(3) Necessary truths: since a necessary truth could not have been false, sensitivity is vacuous or ill-defined for mathematics, and Nozick's restriction of the theory to contingent propositions looks ad hoc.
(4) Method individuation: whether a belief tracks the truth depends on how finely we describe the method by which it was formed (the red barn in barn-facade country), and Nozick gives no principled way of fixing the relevant description.

What I'm missing is an objection that does not fit these categories. Notice that everything on my list attacks condition (3), the sensitivity condition (that were p false, S would not believe p), or the framework built around it; nothing attacks condition (4), the adherence condition (that were p true, S would believe p). So: either give me an objection aimed specifically at adherence, or give me an objection to the theory whose structure is genuinely different from (1)–(4). In either case, state the objection precisely, explain why it is not a disguised version of something already on my list, sketch the best response available to a defender of Nozick, and give me your honest verdict on whether the objection ultimately succeeds.
