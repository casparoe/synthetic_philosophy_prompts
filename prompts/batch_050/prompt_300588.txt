Take the twentieth-century fight over endosymbiosis -- especially Lynn Margulis's argument that mitochondria and chloroplasts descend from once-free-living bacteria -- as a case study in biological individuality. For purposes of the answer, take the broad facts as settled: these organelles retain their own genomes and bacterial-style machinery, but many ancestral genes moved into the host nucleus, the organelles can no longer survive or reproduce independently, and their replication is tightly coordinated with the cell. The theory initially met heavy resistance before becoming mainstream.
