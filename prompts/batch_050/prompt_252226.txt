I am a retired chemist who has recently begun reading philosophy in earnest, and I have just started G. E. Moore's Principia Ethica (first published 1903). In the Preface, Moore distinguishes two questions ethics must answer — what kinds of things ought to exist for their own sakes, and what kinds of actions we ought to perform — and then writes of the first question:

"It becomes plain that, for answers to the first question, no relevant evidence whatever can be adduced: from no other truth, except themselves alone, can it be inferred that they are either true or false. We can guard against error only by taking care, that, when we try to answer a question of this kind, we have before our minds that question only, and not some other or others; but that there is great danger of such errors of confusion I have tried to shew, and also what are the chief precautions by the use of which we may guard against them."

I would like help understanding how this methodological declaration bears on the conclusions Moore reaches later in the book, where he holds that the greatest goods we can know or imagine are personal affection and the appreciation of beauty. In my former discipline, a claim for which "no relevant evidence whatever can be adduced" would count as unfalsifiable and hence suspect; yet Moore clearly does not regard these judgments as arbitrary or beyond criticism, since he speaks of guarding against error and of "precautions" one may take. What, then, does the relevant scrutiny consist in, if not evidence? Is the appeal to intuition supposed to function analogously to observation in the sciences, and if so, what plays the role of replication when two careful judges inspect the same question and disagree — as people plainly do over the comparative worth of friendship, beauty, knowledge, and pleasure? Finally, is there a tension between this passage and the confidence of his later rankings: does his method entitle him to say anything more than that these are the things he himself, on careful reflection, finds good?
