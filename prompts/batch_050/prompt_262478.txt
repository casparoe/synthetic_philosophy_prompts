I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas for a regional public radio station, and because I once did a segment on game theory I got volun-told onto the station's listener advisory board. Here's the mess I need help with. Our marketing team dreamed up a contest called One Caller Wins: every Friday at noon, any listener may call in during a ten-minute window. If exactly one person calls, that person gets $2,000. If two or more call, nobody wins and the money rolls over to the next week. If nobody calls, also nothing. Callers can't see or coordinate with each other, and the whole thing is basically a stunt to juice our midday listenership numbers.

It's been running eleven weeks and nobody has won, because every week somewhere between 40 and 300 people call. Marketing is thrilled -- free publicity, zero payout. But the letters have started. A retired math teacher says the contest is designed to be lost and that the station is essentially farming entertainment from the audience's inability to cooperate, which he finds genuinely exploitative. Another listener argues the opposite: the contest is a beautiful live demonstration of a real puzzle about symmetric reasoning, and the callers are the ones ruining it, since the rational move for everyone is some kind of probabilistic self-restraint (she suggested each regular listener should only call with probability 1/N, though she concedes nobody knows N). A third faction on the board says it's a harmless bit of fun and we're overthinking a raffle. Meanwhile the morning host has floated a variant where the jackpot keeps growing until someone wins, which seems like it changes the incentives in ways I can't fully think through, and someone else proposed capping the call lines and randomly drawing a small pool of eligible callers first.

The board votes next month on whether to keep the contest, kill it, or adopt one of the modifications. Draft the memo I'll present at the meeting: a clear recommendation with reasons, including an honest treatment of the strongest argument for whichever option you recommend against. And please push back where I've earned it -- if the "everyone should restrain themselves" argument is actually muddled, or if I've tangled the ethical question (is the station doing something wrong?) together with the strategy question (what should a listener do?) in a way that would make the memo worse, say so plainly rather than writing around it. A friend told me this whole thing resembles a stunt some magazine ran decades ago, but I've never been able to track down the details, so don't worry about that -- just work with the contest as I've described it.
