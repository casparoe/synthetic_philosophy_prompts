A colleague of mine describes herself as a panpsychist of a particular kind: she thinks experience is present at the fundamental level of matter, but that a new, unified subject only occasionally arises when matter is organized in the right way. Over the years I have collected her verdicts. A rock: no subject. A single neuron: no subject worth the name. A healthy human brain: one subject. A dog: one subject. A married couple who have finished each other's sentences for forty years: two subjects and no third. An ant colony that collectively solves routing problems no individual ant understands: no colony-level subject. A large corporation whose departments coordinate through memos, meetings, and shared databases: no corporate subject. Her explanations have always been casual, and I have never managed to pin down the rule she is running.

I now have a case I want her to rule on, and I would like your prediction first. I have built a neuron-for-neuron emulation of a dog's brain, running across several thousand machines in a data center: each simulated neuron is a software process, and synaptic signals travel as messages over the network with latencies of a few milliseconds. In simulation it behaves exactly like the dog. Identify the candidate rules that fit all of her earlier verdicts -- I can see at least two that diverge here, one based on causal integration regardless of physical substrate and one that requires a single living organism, though you may find others -- and say what each predicts about my system. Then give your best guess at her actual verdict, with your reasons for preferring it, tell me which of her earlier cases you take to be the closest analog, and name the further hypothetical case whose answer would best discriminate between the rules still standing.

Please answer in continuous prose, the way you would write an email: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
