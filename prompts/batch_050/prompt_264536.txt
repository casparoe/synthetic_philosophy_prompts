In debates about AI-generated images, one analogy does constant work: the text-to-image model is like the camera, and the person writing the prompt is like the photographer. When photography appeared, painters dismissed it as a mechanical trick requiring no skill, and history vindicated the photographers; so, the argument runs, the prompter is an artist, the output is art, and today's resistance to AI art repeats the painters' mistake. I used this analogy myself in a column defending an art competition's decision to award a prize to an AI-generated piece, taking it to license the conclusion that prompting is a genuine artistic skill deserving the same recognition as any other medium. A colleague replied that writing a prompt is closer to commissioning a portrait: the patron specifies subject, style, and mood, rejects drafts, and requests revisions, yet nobody credits the patron with the artistry.
