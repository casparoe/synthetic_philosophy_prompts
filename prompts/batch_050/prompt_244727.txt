I suggested we just pick a voting method and move on, and two of my coworkers, Maya and Derek, immediately got into an argument that ate the whole meeting and honestly I still can't tell who won. Here's the exchange as best I remember it. Derek said it doesn't matter what we pick, because Arrow's impossibility theorem proves no voting system can satisfy a short list of obviously-fair conditions at once, so every method is equally broken and we should just grab one off the shelf. Maya said that's a misreading: Arrow's theorem is specifically about systems where voters submit rank orderings, and it says nothing at all about systems like score voting where each person rates every ticket 0-10, so his "everything is broken" conclusion doesn't follow. Derek came back that score voting only escapes the theorem on a technicality, and in practice everyone will just rate their favorite tickets 10 and everything else 0, so the extra expressiveness collapses into bullet voting and the "escape" is fake. Maya said fine, but every non-dictatorial voting method is manipulable somehow, so "it can be gamed" can't be the tiebreaker between systems -- what matters is how often the failures actually bite, and ranked methods have their own nasty failure modes, like a candidate that a majority prefers over everyone else still losing because first-choice votes get split. Then Derek made his final move, which is the one that stuck with me: he said for our specific case, some of Arrow's conditions aren't bugs we want to escape, they're features -- e.g. whether ticket A outranks ticket B genuinely shouldn't flip just because someone submits a joke ticket C at the last minute, and score voting lets that happen, so Maya's escape route is actually a downgrade for us.
