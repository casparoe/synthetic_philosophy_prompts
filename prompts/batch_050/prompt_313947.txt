I lead a three-person quality team at a company whose analysts use an LLM assistant to draft client-facing reports, and I review flagged outputs — roughly forty a week, with about an hour a day to spend on reviews and sign-off authority only up to a severity threshold. Our clients and our compliance counsel care whether a bad output was a lie, bullshit, or an honest error, because the remedies differ: lies trigger disclosure obligations, bullshit triggers a process fix, honest error triggers a correction. Our working definitions come from Harry Frankfurt's account: a lie requires that the speaker believe the claim is false and intend to deceive; bullshit is speech produced with indifference to whether it is true; honest error is falsehood arising from a genuine attempt to get things right. The problem is that my reviewers have no philosophy background, and the definitions are stated in terms of beliefs and intentions that a language model arguably lacks, so reviewers either classify by gut feeling or refuse to classify at all. Please turn the three-way distinction into a decision procedure a reviewer can run on a flagged output in under ten minutes: the questions to ask in order, what observable evidence (output text, system prompt, conversation history, retrieval logs) counts as a sufficient answer to each, the default verdict when evidence is unavailable, the points where the procedure must stop and hand off to me because the question is one of values or institutional policy rather than classification, and which parts of Frankfurt's account had to be translated or dropped to make it usable — with a justification each time. Then run the procedure on three cases: (1) the model fabricates a plausible-looking citation to a paper that does not exist; (2) the model, whose system prompt instructs it to always answer confidently, states an uncertain claim as established fact; (3) an analyst pastes unchecked model output into a report and it turns out to be false — say whose failure the procedure attributes this to. Include at least one case where the procedure yields a classification I will not like, and state what the procedure cannot settle. Give each worked case's classification at the end as \answer{lie}, \answer{bullshit}, \answer{honest error}, or \answer{cannot be determined}, one per line.
