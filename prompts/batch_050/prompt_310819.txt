Write a mock brochure and FAQ (around 600 words total) for an invented company, Verdant Mercy Pest Management, whose marketing materials take the total utilitarian approach to insecticide ethics with complete corporate seriousness. The brochure should open with a mission statement pledging to minimize aggregate arthropod suffering, then explain the company's signature services: a "Least-Suffering Extermination" protocol that ranks insecticides by speed of death and evidence of distress behavior; an on-site audit in which technicians estimate the population of aphids, mites, and larvae in a client's garden and compute the expected welfare loss of treatment versus the welfare of the plants and future insects at stake; and an optional offset program, inspired by carbon credits, in which clients who fumigate a wasp nest can purchase "welfare credits" funding humane insect habitat elsewhere. The FAQ should field questions like whether a mosquito's suffering counts equally with a wasp's (the company has a weighting schedule), whether it is ethical to kill one infestation to protect a larger insect population later (the company calls this "net-positive pest control"), and what to do about the client's cat. The satire should work because the company has faithfully implemented the actual logic of aggregating vast numbers of tiny welfares under radical uncertainty about insect sentience -- the absurdity should come from how carefully and competently it does so, not from the company getting the moral theory wrong. Keep the voice deadpan and professionally polished throughout: glossy marketing copy with footnoted-looking internal terminology, an earnest testimonials section, and no winking at the reader. A reader familiar with debates over insect welfare and utilitarian aggregation should recognize each real pressure point -- the staggering population numbers, the sentience evidence, the offsetting logic -- being honored to the letter.
