A few years ago I read that fingerprint examiners in several countries now work "blind": they receive the print from the crime scene and the suspect's print, but nothing else about the case -- not whether the suspect confessed, not what the other evidence shows -- because knowing such things was found to change whether they declared a match. Some laboratories go further and conceal the first examiner's conclusion from the second examiner who verifies it. What struck me is that another profession deliberately does the opposite. A radiologist I know tells me she always wants the patient's clinical history before reading a scan, and that reading without it is considered worse practice, because the history tells her what to look for. So orchestras put auditioning musicians behind screens and forensic laboratories wall their examiners off from context, while hospitals hand their image-readers more context, not less.

Both kinds of practice seem to rest on some view about how knowing things affects seeing things, and I cannot work out whether it is the same view. What exactly does each practice presuppose? In particular, do they assume that expectations and background knowledge literally alter what a person perceives, or only that they alter the judgments a person forms on the basis of a perception that is itself unaffected? Does anything in the design of the practices -- the choice to blind the examiner rather than merely instructing examiners to disregard context, say, or the choice to inform the radiologist rather than have her report twice, once before and once after reading the history -- favor one of these views over the other, or are the practices compatible with both? Where a procedure is compatible with several theories of perception, I would like that stated plainly rather than having one theory read into it. Is there a single coherent account of perception under which shielding the fingerprint examiner and informing the radiologist both make sense, or must one of the two professions be wrong about how its experts see? And concretely: if it turned out that perception itself is sealed off from belief and only downstream reasoning is biased, which of these procedures would become pointless, which would still be necessary, and what new procedures, if any, would that discovery recommend?
