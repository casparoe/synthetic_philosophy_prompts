I sit on the five-member council of a coastal town of about 40,000 people (I run a feed and tack store, no philosophy degree or anything like that) and we're drafting our first ever price-gouging ordinance. We got hit by a hurricane last month, power was out for nine days, and now everyone is mad and wants us to "do something" before next season.

Here's the factual mess I need the memo to grapple with, because I think it matters:

- Buck, who owns the hardware store two doors from me, trucked in about 200 generators from a wholesaler 400 miles away and sold them at roughly triple the pre-storm price. BUT he paid emergency freight, and the wholesaler had already jacked HIS prices up, so Buck's margin was maybe 30% better than normal, not 200%. Half the town wants his head anyway.
- The gas station on Route 9 kept prices normal out of principle. They sold out in six hours, the line was a nightmare, and guys were later selling filled jerry cans out of pickup beds at $20 a gallon with zero overhead. Nobody's mad at those guys because nobody knows their names.
- Out-of-state tree crews were charging $8-10k per tree on a roof. If we'd capped that, the crews tell me flat out they'd have just driven to the next disaster.
- One councilwoman wants a hard cap: no price more than 15% above the 30-day pre-storm average during a declared emergency, misdemeanor fines, first $500 of any fine to whoever reports it. Another member wants no ordinance at all and gave a little speech about prices being "information." A third floated this odd idea where instead of fining gougers the town just buys their stock at the high price and resells it at cost, which sounds insane to me but I can't fully say why.

I'm genuinely torn. My gut says what Buck did was wrong in a way the pickup-truck guys weren't, but when I interrogate the gut it mostly comes back with "well, he's visible." And the gas station's virtue didn't put gas in anyone's tank. At the same time, "the market handled it" feels obscene to say out loud when it's someone's grandma paying $9 for ice. I don't want the memo to just pick a team; I want it to tell me what the actual wrong is supposed to be (profiting off desperation? jumping the queue? being rich enough to pay?), because I suspect the right ordinance depends entirely on the answer.

Please draft the memo I'll circulate to the council before Thursday's vote, in these sections, in this order:

1. Recommendation -- one short paragraph, bottom line first: pass the cap, pass an amended version, or drop it. No throat-clearing.
2. The ordinance itself -- about a page. What the trigger is, what "excessive" means, whether documented cost increases are a defense, whether it covers hotels and out-of-town contractors and random resellers, and what enforcement realistically looks like for a town our size.
3. The reasoning -- about a page. Work through the generator, gas, and tree-crew cases above and show what the recommended rule does with each, including the awkward outcomes.
4. The other side's best point -- half a page. State the strongest argument against the recommendation as forcefully as its champion would, then say why it loses (or partially wins).
5. Questions for the city attorney -- a short bulleted list of legal/practical loose ends I should raise before we vote.

Around 1200 words total is fine. Write it like a council memo, not a term paper -- but don't sand down the hard parts.
