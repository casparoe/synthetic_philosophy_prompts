I run a reading group for grad students and a few sharp undergrads, around 14 people, and we have about 90 minutes. We're about to start on Brandom's inferentialism and every year the same thing happens: everyone nods along to "meaning is inferential role" as a slogan, and then three sessions later it turns out they're still secretly picturing meaning as little pictures or definitions in the head, and the inferential stuff is just decoration on top. I want an activity for before we read a word of Brandom that makes them feel what it would be for a concept to be nothing but its place in a network of moves.

Here's what I'm picturing, but fix or replace any of it: some kind of game where players get made-up words that are defined only by rules of use -- what you're allowed to say after someone plays them, what you have to accept, what counts as a legitimate challenge, what you're on the hook for if you say X -- and the group has to actually do something with them (sort things, win points, catch each other out) without anyone ever getting a definition. Ideally there's a moment where someone realizes they've learned a concept without ever being told what it "means," and maybe a moment where the facilitator quietly changes one rule mid-game and everyone watches the word's meaning shift even though "the word" stayed the same. If you can build in a mechanic that mimics the commitment/entitlement/challenge structure -- like one person keeping score of what everyone is now committed to -- even better.

Give me: full rules, materials (index cards and poker chips level of complexity, nothing fancier), a payoff or win condition that works without real money, a timeline for the 90 minutes, and a debrief script that maps specific things that will happen in the game onto inferentialist ideas (material inference, scorekeeping, meaning changing when the inferential connections change) without me having to lecture first. Also: what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this -- I suspect people will treat the rule cards as secret definitions to memorize rather than as moves in a game, so tell me how to handle that and the other two or three failure modes you anticipate. And be honest about which part of the view the game can't reproduce: my guess is it can show content-constitution by inferential role but not the normative pragmatics stuff (why anyone is entitled to anything in the first place, the social scorekeeping across a whole community), and if you think the game smuggles in a representationalist reading somewhere I want to know that too.
