I'm drafting a novel with a character who's basically a moral saint, and I keep testing my cast on my friend Dana, whose gut verdicts on which characters are genuinely admirable I trust more than my own. Her track record so far:

- Mireille: sunny, loves the work, gave up her music and every hobby, pours everything into strangers, and pointedly treats her own daughter exactly the same as any kid in the village. Dana: can't stand her, wouldn't want to be her OR have dinner with her.
- Beatriz: quietly joyful, no hint of grim duty, but she kept her one obsession (restoring motorcycles, badly) and quietly makes sure her own family and neighbors come first in her triage. Dana: this one I'd actually want in my life.

New character, Sol: warm and unforced like Beatriz, even more so, and she also keeps her one private passion (growing orchids). But she's rigorously impartial -- she set up her clinic so her own sister gets zero priority, and she moved abroad partly to stop herself from playing favorites.

What do you reckon Dana says about Sol? Her verdicts seem consistent with at least two different rules -- something like "sainthood is fine iff it leaves room for a life of one's own" versus "sainthood is fine iff the saint stays properly partial to their own people" (and note the first case seems to kill a third rule, the one where it's all about whether the goodness is warm rather than grudging). Tell me what each surviving rule predicts for Sol, which one you think is really driving Dana and why, which earlier character is Sol's closest analog, and if Sol genuinly underdetermines it, give me the next character sketch that would settle which rule she's running.
