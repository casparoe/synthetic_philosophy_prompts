Hey, so I'm a second-year PhD student working on metaethics (mostly moral language stuff) and I have to present my "position statement" in our work-in-progress group next week. Before I embarrass myself I want a consistency audit. Here's roughly where I've landed:

(1) I'm an expressivist about moral talk: when someone sincerely says "cruelty is wrong," they express a conative state (disapproval, acceptance of a plan, whatever your favorite is) — nothing gets described, no proposition gets asserted.

(2) My main motivation is the standard motivational argument: moral judgments motivate all by themselves, beliefs alone never motivate (I'm a Humean about motivation), so moral judgments can't be beliefs.

(3) I'm a plain cognitivist about epistemic normativity though. "You ought to believe what your evidence supports" states a straightforward fact, and going expressivist about that would be self-undermining anyway.

(4) I follow the minimalists on truth: "that's true" just endorses the sentence, so moral sentences are truth-apt and I can happily say "it's true that cruelty is wrong" without committing to any moral properties.

(5) On negation: "cruelty isn't wrong" expresses disapproval of refraining from cruelty — i.e., of not being cruel.

(6) I honestly think the whole Frege-Geach thing is overblown. Take "if cruelty is wrong, then it's wrong to teach your kids to be cruel" — that inference is just valid by logic, so expressivism inherits validity for free, no special semantic machinery needed.

(7) And when two people go at it over whether cruelty is wrong, that's genuine disagreement — disagreement in attitude rather than belief, but that's all real moral disagreement ever was.

So: does this hang together? I want an audit, not a lecture on expressivism generally. Format it like this:

**Real tensions, worst first** — go through the genuine problems in order of severity, maybe 120-150 words each; for each one say which commitments collide, how bad it is, and what would have to give.

**Only apparent tensions** — flag any pairs that look problematic but dissolve on inspection, ~80 words each, and say why they dissolve.
