Hofstadter's account of the location of meaning distinguishes three levels in any message: the frame message, which signals that one is looking at a message at all and indicates how to begin approaching it; the outer message, the decoding machinery or rules of interpretation by which symbols are turned into content; and the inner message, the content the sender meant to convey. His jukebox argument then says the inner message is not carried by the symbols alone the way a casual picture suggests, since the decoding machinery contributes essentially: with a sufficiently universal jukebox, the record itself becomes almost superfluous.
