Here's a chunk of the White Horse Dialogue from the Gongsun Longzi -- the advocate is mid-argument for his thesis that a white horse is not a horse:

"If one seeks a horse, yellow and black horses may all be delivered. If one seeks a white horse, yellow and black horses may not be delivered. Suppose a white horse were [just] a horse. Then what is sought [in the two cases] would be the same. If what is sought were the same, then 'white' would not differ from 'horse.' If what is sought does not differ, how is it that yellow and black horses are acceptable [in one case] and unacceptable [in the other]? That acceptable and unacceptable are mutually opposed is clear. Therefore yellow and black horses are the same in that they can answer to 'there is a horse,' but cannot answer to 'there is a white horse.' This is a white horse's not being a horse: it is certain."

Most reconstructions I've seen just say the advocate equivocates on "is not" (non-identity vs. exclusion from a category) and leave it there. But I think the more interesting soft spot is this delivery argument specifically. Please argue that it fails even if we grant the advocate that "white horse" and "horse" differ in meaning: the test he proposes quietly slides between two different things under the label "what is sought" -- the description you hand the stable hand, and the animal the stable hand hands back. To make the diagnosis stick, show that the very same argument form would prove that a lame horse is not a horse (seek a horse and a lame one may be delivered; seek a lame horse and only lame ones may be), and explain why that parallel should embarass the advocate rather than just multiply his conclusions. One caveat: if you think there's a charitable reading on which the delivery argument actually works, state that reading before knocking it down -- I'd rather not watch you attack a strawman.
