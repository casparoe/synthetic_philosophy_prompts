I’m setting an upper-level philosophy of language/mind exam and I need one essay question on Wittgenstein’s private language argument that cant be answered by trotting out the beetle-in-the-box and saying “inner processes need outward criteria.” The hook should be this made-up case: Ana gets brief migraine auras and, during one, privately “points” to the felt quality and names it Z. Later she wears a cuff that prints a number whenever the aura occurs; after a year she notices the numbers drift, but sometimes she overrides them because “that one isn’t really Z.” The question should force students to say whether Z ever becomes a concept, whether the cuff gives criteria or just relocates the problem, and why “seeming right to Ana” is or isn’t enough. Make them connect the diary/sensation case to rule-following: what would make a later use a correction rather than a new decision, and why the community can’t enter as a panel that votes on her headaches. Include a fair bit on the strongest pro-private-language move: first-person authority and the claim that not all sameness needs public checkability.

Deliver: (1) the exam question, phrased for a 75-minute answer and explicitly forbidding mere summary; (2) a model answer of about 700 words that argues a definite verdict on Ana while conceding the best opposing point; (3) a marking scheme out of 30 with five must-hit moves, marks for each, the exact move that separates an A from a B, and a one-line “how people usually muff this” note for each. The must-hits should include: no criterion vs no fact of the matter; grammar of “same” and “correct”; private ostension vs calibration; rule-following and new decisions; why publicity is about possible correction not actual eavesdroppers. Keep it free of citations and don’t quote Wittgenstein. Start directly with the exam question itself, no preface or commentary.
