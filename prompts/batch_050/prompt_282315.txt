A while ago I had to choose between two job offers -- a research position at a small institute in a quiet town and a policy role at a large organization in a city I love. After weeks of deliberation I concluded, honestly and not as a cop-out, that neither was better than the other. Then a friend asked me a question I haven't been able to stop thinking about. Suppose the institute had offered slightly more money, say a few thousand extra per year. Would that have settled it? Clearly not; a small bump like that doesn't seem capable of tipping a balance this deep. But now stack the thought experiment: a few thousand more, and a few thousand more again, and eventually, at some very large raise, the institute job plainly is the better option. So the original offers are tied, and the heavily sweetened one beats them, yet no individual sweetening step seems to break the tie. Something has to give, and I can't see what. I've since noticed the same shape elsewhere: comparing two fellowship applications where neither file is stronger, and where one extra decent letter doesn't decide it but a vastly better third letter does; comparing two ways to spend a charitable budget that differ on several dimensions at once.

What strikes me is what this isn't. With physical comparisons I understand the pattern: if I weigh objects on a crude kitchen scale, A and B can balance, B and C can balance, yet A and C don't, and that's just the scale's limited resolution hiding real differences. But in the job case there was no hidden difference my deliberation was failing to detect -- I had all the relevant facts, and the failure of the comparisons to chain up seemed to be a fact about the values themselves, not about my instruments. I'm also not sure this is the same as what philosophers call incommensurability, which I took to be about a wholesale absence of comparison, whereas here comparisons exist; the tie is real, it just behaves strangely under small improvements.

Does this configuration have an established name? I'd like more than a label: I want to understand how it relates to, and differs from, at least three nearby things -- mere limits of discrimination or measurement, ordinary vagueness (the way heap has no sharp boundary), and the possibility that one option really was better all along and I simply couldn't tell. In particular, does the phenomenon force a revision to what "equally good" or "neither better" means, since it apparently can't be treated as an equivalence that chains? And if no existing term covers exactly this case of ties that resist small improvements while collapsing under accumulated ones, could you propose one, define it, and mark its edges -- for instance, does it matter whether the successive improvements come in a dimension already present in the original comparison, as opposed to introducing an entirely new one?
