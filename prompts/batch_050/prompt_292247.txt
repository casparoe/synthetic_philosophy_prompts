ok so I've been chewing on this for months and I'm tired of going in circles, so I want to try a different approach. Everyone has an "explanation" for why time only goes one way -- entropy, the big bang, something about cause and effect, someone always brings up coffee getting cold. But every explanation I read feels like it just moves the mystery somewhere else, and I can't tell if that's because they're all bad or because I'm asking for the wrong things.

So before I read one more pop-science book on this, write me the requirements list: what would an explanation of why time has a direction actually have to do for me to accept it and stop feeling cheated? I'm not a physicist, my background is I fix bikes and I read a lot, so keep it in plain American English.

Things I want the list to cover, though you should add what I'm missing and cut what's dumb:

- I can remember yesterday but not tomorrow. Does a good explanation have to explain THAT, or is it allowed to just say memory is one more physical process and inherits whatever direction everything else has? Feels like cheating to me but maybe it's fine.
- The basic laws of motion supposedly work just as well run backwards (like a video of two billiard balls). So is it a requirement that the explanation not sneak in a direction the laws themselves don't have? Like if your explanation secretly assumes past and future are different in order to explain why past and future are different, that's circular, right?
- Why do all the one-way processes point the SAME way? Cups break, heat spreads, ripples move outward after you throw a rock, we remember the past. Is "they all have to line up" a genuine requirement or could a theory say it's a coincidence and still count?
- The whole thing seems to hinge on the early universe being in some special super-tidy state. Is a theory allowed to just take that as a brute given, or does it have to explain why the universe started that way? If it's allowed to take it as given, does that even count as explaining anything?
- Eggs break and never unbreak. But somewhere in there the explanation should probably say something about why unbreaking isn't IMPOSSIBLE, just never happens -- like is it a requirement to clarify whether the direction of time is a law or just a very very strong tendency? Because nobody ever says this part clearly and it drives me nuts.
- Does it have to say anything about the feeling that time "flows" or that now is special, or is it allowed to declare that a psychology problem and walk away?
- If the universe eventually dies heat death or crunches back or whatever, does the explanation have to still work, or is it fine for it to only cover our actual situation?

For each requirement give me the case or gut-puzzle behind it, and tell me which ones are genuinely non-negotiable versus which are just things one camp of people insists on. And the part I care most about: tell me if any of these requirements can't all be satisfied at once, because I have a strong suspicion the list itself is contradictory and THAT's why every book leaves me unsatisfied.
