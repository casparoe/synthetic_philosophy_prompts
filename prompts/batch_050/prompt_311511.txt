I'm prepping for a phil mind seminar (upper-level undergrads) and I want to make a one-page flowchart handout titled something like "so you think maybe everything is conscious -- now what?" It should take a student who accepts the starting point (experience exists, and nothing in the physical description of the world seems to settle where it shows up) and walk them down to a named position in the panpsychism neighborhood by asking them questions they can answer without knowing any of the labels first.

Some requirements so it doesn't turn into a glossary:

- Build the tree out of questions, not out of names. Each branch node should be a single question with two (occasionally three) answer options, phrased as a concrete commitment or a thought experiment, not as "do you accept subject-summing?" E.g. a node might ask whether it's coherent for there to be facts about what it's like to be a system even when no part of that system has those experiences, or whether "there is something it's like to be the pair of us" has to either be true in virtue of your experience plus mine or just not be a fact at all.
- The tree must include, as separate decision points, the three ways the combination problem is usually pulled apart: getting many subjects to add up to one subject, getting micro-qualities to yield the macro-palette (new hues nobody's parts had), and the grain/mismatch worry (why my experience is structured like a visual field when my brain's micro-parts aren't). Let the student's verdict on which of these is fatal vs solvable steer the path, not just be decoration at the end.
- Leaves I definitely want on there: constitutive panpsychism with phenomenal bonding, panqualityism, cosmopsychism (with the decombination problem noted at the leaf), emergentist panpsychism, and Russellian monism that stops short of panpsychism. If your tree also lands someone on a leaf nobody defends, keep it on the page, mark it "uninhabited," and add one line on whether that combo is incoherent, just unloved, or unexplored.
- Flag any pair of leaves your questions genuinely cannot tell apart (I'm suspicious emergentist panpsychism vs Russellian non-panpsychist monism might be one of those, but check me on that) rather than pretending the tree separates them.
- Cap it at about 12 leaves and make sure no path is longer than ~6 questions. At each leaf, one sentence on the view plus one sentence on its most embarassing commitment.

Don't give me a prose essay about the positions, just the tree itself (indented text format is fine), the uninhabited-branch notes, and the flag list.
