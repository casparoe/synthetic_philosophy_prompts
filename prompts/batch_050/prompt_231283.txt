Something that's been bugging me in epistemology. There's this old cluster of arguments: if my color vision lets me know the table is red, then it also seems to let me know, by one quick inference, that I'm not looking at a white table illuminated by red lights — even though I never did anything whatsoever to check the lighting. And the related case where you establish that your gas gauge is reliable by reading it and then "confirming" each reading against the gauge itself, over and over. Some philosophers say these inferences are obviously fine and any theory that forbids them is broken; others say they're obviously illegitimate and any theory that permits them is broken. This has been going for twenty-plus years and as far as I can tell nobodys moved an inch; each side just re-states its intuition with fancier machinery.

My working hypothesis: the dispute isn't really about these inferences at all. It's about whether knowledge is supposed to answer a first-person question (what may I use to settle things for myself, given where I stand?) or a third-person question (when is a belief in fact reliably produced?), and the two camps are scoring the same piece of reasoning by different standards without ever saying so.
