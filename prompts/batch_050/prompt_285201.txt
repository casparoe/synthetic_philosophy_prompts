I'm a philosophy PhD student and, through some mysterious administrative accident, I now sit on the small-grants ethics panel for an interdisciplinary research centre. We get about 25 proposals a year, usually for £5k–£50k, and the panel has maybe 45 minutes per proposal. Most panellists are sensible academics but not ethicists, so I can't just tell them to apply moral trade theory and hope for the best.

The awkward proposals are structured like this: two groups morally disagree, or at least rank moral ends very differently, but offer a package in which each side helps secure something the other side cares about in return for help with its own priority. I understand the basic idea of moral trade to be that a package can be morally better than the status quo by each party's own lights, even though neither party endorses every part of it. But in practice it gets confused with ordinary bargaining, bribery, moral compromise, laundering responsibility, and cases where the parties are happy because the costs are dumped on outsiders.

Could you turn the idea into a decision procedure the panel could genuinely use? I want:

1. A sequence of questions or tests to run, in order.
2. For each test, what evidence would count as passing it, given that we cannot demand a treatise from applicants.
3. Clear stop points where the answer is an automatic no.
4. Points where the panel should instead say this needs a political or values judgement, and send it to the centre's board.
5. A way of distinguishing a genuine moral trade from:
   - a merely prudential bargain,
   - each side reluctantly accepting a morally worse overall package to avoid deadlock,
   - one person acting from moral uncertainty rather than two viewpoints trading,
   - paying someone else to do the ethically dirty work,
   - reciprocal support for two projects that would have happened anyway,
   - and an arrangement that is mutually beneficial only because it imposes uncompensated costs on third parties.
6. The parts of the procedure that will inevitably be crude or liable to misfire.

Please pay particular attention to baselines and additionality: whether the supposedly traded benefit is genuinely caused by the deal, whether one side is being rewarded for stopping something it had no right to do in the first place, and whether irreversible harms or rights violations should be non-tradable even where both negotiating parties judge the package to be morally better.

Then run the procedure on these three made-up, but realistic, proposals:

- An animal-welfare group will publicly support a medically valuable mouse study if the laboratory puts an equal amount into validating a non-animal replacement and commits to switching if the replacement reaches an agreed performance threshold. The welfare group thinks the deal will reduce animal suffering overall, but its support may also make the present mouse study more likely to receive institutional approval.
- A global-health donor and a community-arts donor propose pooling their grants, with 70% going to malaria prevention and 30% to a local theatre programme. Neither donor independently thinks the other project is morally important, but both prefer this package to a deadlock in which the centre funds neither.
- An AI-safety organisation and an open-science organisation agree that a research lab will delay releasing a model for six months while the safety group funds independent evaluations; after that, the open-science group will help distribute the model unless the evaluations cross agreed danger thresholds. Both groups regard the arrangement as better than either immediate release or indefinite secrecy, but the people potentially harmed by release are not represented.

At least one application should produce a result our committee members probably won't enjoy, rather than every worked example coming out convenient. Please be explicit where the procedure has to override the moral judgements of the negotiating parties because of third parties.

Please answer in British English, and don't give me a bibliography or lean on citations; I'm after a usable instrument, not a literature review. Also, sorry for the rambling — this has occassionally turned into the hardest part of my admin job.
