There's been a running argument in a Discord server I'm in (long story) between two people about Aumann's agreement theorem, and after like eight months of it I told them they should just do an adversarial collaboration and write a joint statement already. Somehow they've now nominated me to draft the thing, even though I mostly just lurk, so: write the document for me.

The two camps: person A is basically a Hanson-style hardliner. His view is that if two people are honest truth-seekers, share a prior, and genuinely know each other's estimates, then they cannot agree to disagree — so when he finds himself in persistent disagreement with someone whose judgment he respects, he takes that as evidence that someone (possibly him) is being irrational, and that he should be updating hard toward them. Person B thinks the theorem is a lovely piece of math with close to zero practical content: common knowledge of each other's posteriors almost never actually obtains, the theorem is a static result about states of knowledge and says nothing about any conversational process or how long convergence would take, and anyway no real humans are Bayesians with a shared prior, so the whole thing functions as a rationalist shibboleth people wave around to win arguements.

What I need from the joint statement: (i) a numbered list of claims both would sign — what the theorem actually assumes, what it does and does not entail (I'm fairly sure both agree it doesn't tell you which side should move toward the other, or by how much, but verify that's signable), and what work the common prior assumption is even doing; (ii) the disagreements, each stated in wording its own side would accept — my suspicion is the real one is whether the theorem supplies a genuine norm bearing on actual human disagreement or merely an idealized benchmark, but don't just take my word for it; (iii) the cruxes — the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one of them. Candidate cruxes I overheard: whether approximate common knowledge of posteriors gets you approximate agreement, or whether small failures of common knowledge can sustain big gaps; and whether the follow-up result that two Bayesians who keep announcing their posteriors to each other eventually converge shows the theorem has practical teeth after all, or just moves the bump under the rug since that protocol is itself unrealizable in practice. For each crux, name a case, result, or observation both would accept as bearing on it, and record each side's prediction in advance. If part of their fight turns out to be verbal — I have a hunch the phrase "honest truth-seeker" is doing a lot of hidden work for A — say which part and why. Format: agreed claims first, then a dissagreements table, then the cruxes.
