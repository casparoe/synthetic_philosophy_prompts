I've been going back and forth between first-order and higher-order theories of consciousness for a while and I keep realising I don't actually have a fixed standard for judging them -- every paper just picks the two or three phenomena that make its own view look good. So before I read anything else I want the specification written out. What would a theory of consciousness of this whole family (higher-order thought, higher-order perception, self-representationalism, whatever) actually have to get right to count as adequate?

Give me the desiderata list. For each requirement: state it, give the case or consideration behind it, and say whether it's genuinely non-negotiable or just a prejudice of one camp. The stuff I'm thinking it needs to cover, at minimum:

- the standard puzzle cases (blindsight, unattended stimuli in inattentional blindness setups, peripheral vision) -- which of these does a theory have to get *right* versus merely have a story about
- the misrepresentation / targetless higher-order state problem: if a higher-order representation can occur while its target first-order state doesn't exist or differs from how it's represented, which state is conscious and whose consciousness is it? any theory that just says "this doesn't happen" seems to be dodging
- animal and infant consciousness: is ruling out creatures that plausibly lack metarepresentation a dealbreaker, or is that a bullet a theory is allowed to bite, and how would we even adjudicate that
- why consciousness should be tied to higher-order representation *at all* rather than this being an epistemic feature (how we know about our states) confused for a constitutive one
- the regress and cognitive-cost worries: how much mental machinery per conscious state is too much
- what the theory is even for -- should the standard be different if the goal is a scientific research programme versus a metaphysical account of what consciousness consists in? feel free to split the list by purpose if that matters

Then: flag any pair of desiderata you think can't be jointly satisfied, and tell me which single requirement does the most work in knocking out popular contenders. Only after all that, briefly score the main higher-order-ish families against the list (HOT, HOP, self-representationalism, and one first-order rival like global workspace or local recurrence as a control case) -- but keep that part short, the criteria are the deliverable, not the scoreboard.

Write it in British English please, and a table is fine if it helps.
