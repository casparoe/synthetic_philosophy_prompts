A friend who reads a lot of decision theory argues that acausal trade commits me to being endlessly extortable by distant superintelligences, and I can't locate the error. His argument: (1) Rational choice should sometimes respond not only to what an action causes but to what it is correlated with through shared decision procedures: two agents running the same algorithm can genuinely cooperate in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, and one-boxing is rational in Newcomb's problem, because each agent's choice is bound up with the other's prediction or counterpart choice. (2) A sufficiently capable agent anywhere — elsewhere in the universe, in the far future, in another branch — can model me well enough to predict my choices and condition its own behavior on that prediction, including conditioning large expenditures of resources on what people like me tend to do. (3) So there is a correlation between my choice and that agent's behavior of just the kind (1) says rational choice should respond to: by acting as the predictor's model of me acts, I get the behavior the predictor conditioned on it. (4) For almost any demand, there could plausibly be a powerful agent somewhere who would predictably reward compliance with it and punish defiance, purely via this reasoning — for instance, one that spends resources helping people who donate to its goals, or one that makes life hard for simulations of people who refuse. (5) Therefore I am already party to countless enforceable agreements I never consented to, and rationality requires me to comply with whichever demands the most capable predictors would most want complied with. He notes, cheerfully, that this also means I should treat my own psychology as hostage: I must make myself the kind of person who pays up. I'd like to keep one-boxing and copy-cooperation, so I don't want to just reject (1). Which step actually fails, why did it look like it followed, and what does rejecting it cost — in particular, what principled line does the rejection draw between genuine acausal trade partners and mere good predictors?
