I coach a university debate society and we're preparing a round on the ethics of human enhancement, and I want a model case analysis our debaters can argue against. Take the case of Oscar Pistorius: a double amputee who ran on carbon-fiber prosthetic blades, became competitive with elite non-amputee sprinters over 400 meters, and after a dispute over whether the blades gave him an advantage was eventually cleared to compete against able-bodied athletes, racing at the 2012 Olympics. Analyze this episode through the lens of the therapy/enhancement distinction. The salient facts, as I want them taken: the blades replace limbs lost to congenital amputation; the dispute was not about whether he may run at all but about which category of competition he belonged in; and the adjudicating bodies treated the question as empirical -- do the blades confer a net advantage relative to biological legs? -- rather than conceptual.

Here is what I want the analysis to do. First, map the case onto the two main ways of drawing the distinction: the normal-species-functioning account (interventions that restore or preserve normal functioning count as therapy; interventions that take someone beyond it count as enhancement) and the welfarist account (the distinction should track effects on the person's well-being, wherever normal functioning happens to fall). On each account, is fitting Pistorius with blades therapy, enhancement, or does the case show the account cannot classify it cleanly? Second, press on the awkward feature: suppose, for argument's sake, the blades at their best roughly replicate rather than exceed biological sprinting. Does the therapy/enhancement line then have any remaining work to do in deciding whether he should compete against able-bodied runners, or has the question quietly become a different one -- about fairness within a category defined by a technology baseline, about what the 'able-bodied' division is even for? I want the analysis to say explicitly where the distinction goes silent instead of forcing it to deliver a verdict. Third, run the case in the other direction: does the episode confirm the therapy/enhancement distinction by showing it handled a hard case, or does it reveal that the distinction is doing no independent moral work -- that everything decision-relevant came from the empirical advantage question plus a prior theory of what sport categories are, with 'therapy versus enhancement' as decoration? If the latter, say what a case would have to look like for the distinction to matter on its own.

Begin with your bottom line in the first sentence -- whether the Pistorius case vindicates, embarrasses, or simply bypasses the therapy/enhancement distinction -- and then give the reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of the question.
