I'm writing a column on whether "left" and "right" still carve reality at the joints, and I want to draft what an adversarial collaboration between the two camps would actually produce. Camp 1 (call them the essentialists) holds that the axis tracks something deep and stable — roughly, attitudes toward hierarchy and toward change — which is why the same polarity keeps reappearing across countries and centuries even as the specific policy bundle gets reshuffled. Camp 2 (the coalitionists) holds that the axis is a historical accident dating to the seating chart of the French Revolution, that the positions bundled under each label have no intrinsic connection (what does being pro-market have to do with being anti-immigration?), and that left/right survives only because parties, media, and voters keep coordinating on it as a shortcut. Draft their joint statement with this structure: first, a numbered list of claims both camps can sign (e.g., about how much the actual policy content of "left" has flipped over the last century, about what any theory of ideology has to explain); second, a disagreements section where each position is stated in wording that side would accept as its own; third, a cruxes section — the sub-questions where settling the answer would force at least one camp to update — with each camp's prediction recorded in advance (e.g., what the essentialists vs. coalitionists each expect to find when you check whether voters' positions on unrelated issues correlate more than party loyalty alone would predict). If part of their dispute turns out to be merely verbal, say which part and why.
