I'm a philosophy professor and, to my mild horror, I've been put on my university's research partnerships committee. We review proposed funding and collaborations -- an arms manufacturer funding drone autonomy work in the engineering school, a fossil fuel company endowing an energy institute, a pharma company that funds a lab but gets to sit on publication for 90 days, a donor whose money clearly comes from something ugly, that sort of thing. Meetings happen monthly, maybe three proposals a meeting, and the current process is just vibes plus whoever talks loudest.

I want to turn the philosophy of complicity into a decision procedure the committee can actually run. Here's the conceptual problem as I see it: in nearly every case we face, our participation makes no causal difference to the wrongdoing. The arms company makes drones whether or not our lab takes their money; the oil company drills either way; if we say no, some less scrupulous university says yes. So "we'd be causing the harm" is off the table as the analysis. The literature I know gives me a few candidate notions to work with -- participation in a collective project, making oneself available to be used by wrongdoers, contributing to the background conditions or legitimacy of a practice, wrongful benefitting, moral taint, expressing endorsement -- but nobody hands you an algorithm, which is fair enough, but I still have to get through three proposals before lunch.

So: give me an ordered checklist. The questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to move to the next question, where the checklist bottoms out in a clear no, and where it hands things back to committee judgment because the question is really about values and no procedure settles it (mark those spots explicitly -- don't pretend they're answerable). A few things it has to handle: the "someone else would do it anyway" argument, which comes up in every single meeting and which I suspect is both true and mostly irrelevant; the difference between taking money and doing the work, which I think matters but our CFO thinks doesn't; and reputational/legitimacy concerns, which the comms office treats as everything and I treat as close to nothing philosophically, though I'm willing to be told I'm wrong.

Then run the procedure on the three cases above -- the drone autonomy funding, the 90-day publication hold, and the tainted donor -- and make sure at least one of them comes out with a verdict I'll find uncomfortable in either direction. Also tell me where the procedure will systematically misfire, because I'd rather know than find out in minute 40 of a meeting.

Casual register is fine. Hard limit of 900 words, and lead with the checklist itself, not with throat-clearing about how complicity is a rich and contested notion -- I know, I put that in the prompt.
