Things that are bugging me, which you can reframe, merge, or reject:

- The laundering problem: if someone starts from terrible judgments and reflects carefully into a stable, coherent equillibrium, the method seems to just certify their prejudices with extra steps. Does an adequate account have to condemn the calm, well-read, internally consistent fanatic as unjustified, or is it allowed to bite that bullet? I genuinely can't tell which horn is worse.
- Order effects. My own considered judgments about trolley-style cases shift depending on which case I read first and how it's framed. If the output of RE depends on the order you feed the cases in, does the account need a fix (averaging over orders?? some kind of robustness check?) or is path-dependence just a fact about justification it should own up to?
- The filter problem: "considered judgments" are supposed to be the ones made calmly, competently, without self-interest, etc. But any filter strong enough to screen out prejudice starts to look circular — like you're just keeping the judgments that agree with the theory you wanted anyway. Desideratum: state the filter without cheating.
- The stopping rule. Equilibrium is a state; reflection is a process that's never finished. For my paper I literally need to know when I'm allowed to stop adjusting things and call it done. Does an adequate account owe me that, or is "reflect until further reflection stops changing anything" the best anyone gets?
- Why coherence carries any epistemic weight at all. Why should making my reactions more systematic get me closer to moral truth rather than just better organized about my biases? This feels like the biggest one and nothing I've read answers it head-on.
- Convergence. If two careful, informed reflectors reach different stable equilibria, does the method have to say at least one of them failed somewhere? Or is persistent pluralism a legitimate outcome of the method working perfectly?
- Novel cases: we have no pre-reflective judgments about, like, digital minds or deep future people. Can RE extend beyond its training data, so to speak, or does it only work where we already have stable reactions?
