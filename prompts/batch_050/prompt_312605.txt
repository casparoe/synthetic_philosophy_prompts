ok so I'm a software engineer, no philosophy background, and I recently fell down a rabbit hole on Hofstadter's old superrationality experiments. Quick recap in case the details are fuzzy: in the Platonia dilemma, a trillionaire gathers 20 people and says if exactly one of them sends a telegram by noon the next day, that person gets a billion dollars — but if zero or two-plus telegrams arrive, nobody gets anything, and collusion is forbidden. Hofstadter's superrational answer was that each person should send with probability 1/20, since everyone reasons identically and knows it. The Luring Lottery was the real-world version he ran in Scientific American in 1983: enter by postcard, as many entries as you want, prize of a million bucks divided by the total number of entries — readers mailed in googolplexes and the prize ended up being a fraction of a cent.
