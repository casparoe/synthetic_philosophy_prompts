I'm in Year 12 and help run my school's philosophy society (roughly fifteen students across Years 11–13, most of whom have never read any philosophy of medicine). For a session on the concept of disease, I want to build a "which theory of disease do you actually hold?" quiz that we can take at the start of the meeting, before anyone has heard the terminology. Please write it for me.

Requirements:

- Eight to ten questions, each built around a concrete contested case rather than an abstract principle. The following must appear somewhere: deafness (given that many Deaf people reject the idea that it is a disorder at all), the historical classification of homosexuality as a mental illness, grief after a bereavement, short-sightedness, and chronic fatigue syndrome, where clinicians disagree about whether there is any underlying biological dysfunction. You may add other cases, but no question should mention the name of any theory or ask anything like "do you think disease is a value judgement?"
- Each question should offer two or three answer options phrased as ordinary judgements about the case (e.g. what you'd say to a friend, whether a doctor who treated it would be making a mistake, whether the label could survive a change in social attitudes).
- A scoring key that maps answer patterns onto at least these four positions: the naturalist view that disease is a failure of normal biological functioning, specifiable without value judgements; the normativist view that disease is whatever we judge to be a bad enough departure from how a person ought to be able to live; the hybrid view that disease requires both a biological dysfunction and harm judged against social standards; and a sceptical or social-constructionist view that the disease/non-disease line tracks power and convention rather than anything in nature. If my labels misdescribe any of these positions, correct them.
- The quiz must sort people by what they say about the cases, not by which conclusion sounds nice, so the scoring key needs to show which answer combinations land on which view — and flag any pair of positions the quiz genuinely can't separate with these cases, rather than pretending to split them.
- A short note at the end on any systematic bias in the design: for instance, if my required cases skew towards psychiatry and classification disputes, say whether that means the quiz really measures views about mental disorder rather than disease in general, and what you'd swap in to fix it.

One request about how you handle this: if any of my required cases turns out not to separate the four views (or separates them in a way that can't be scored), tell me plainly instead of forcing a mapping onto it, and if the whole premise is flawed — say, because two of these positions only come apart in print and no set of everyday judgements could distinguish them — I'd rather be told that up front than receive a quiz that quietly doesn't work.
