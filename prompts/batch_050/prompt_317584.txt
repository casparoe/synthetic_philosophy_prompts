I’m in a high school philosophy club and we got into this argument about “equality of what?” after our teacher mentioned the welfare/resources/capabilities debate. I made up a policy to test it, and now I’m confusing myself. Imagine a school district gives every 16-year-old the same “opportunity account” worth, say, $8,000. Students can spend it on tutoring, therapy, transport to a better school, art classes, noise-canceling housing during exams, whatever. Here’s the twist: whatever they don’t spend gets paid out as cash when they graduate. Some students have disabilities or anxiety and basically need the whole account just to reach a normal shot at college; others are already doing fine and rationally keep half the money; a few spend it all on stuff that makes them happy short-term but doesn’t improve their future options; and some have expensive goals, like becoming an elite violinist, where $8,000 barely scratches the surface.

Please describe the most important implications of this setup for deciding what justice should equalize: welfare, resources, capabilities, or some hybrid. Don’t just define the three views like a textbook. I want the messy consequences, like whether the cash conversion clause is fair or secretly rewards people who were already lucky, whether society should compensate “expensive tastes” if the person didn’t choose them, whether someone who knowingly trades future opportunity for present happiness has a complaint later, and whether capability equality quietly smuggles in judgments about which lives are valuable. Also, does this example show that equality should be about a floor instead of strict equality, or does that dodge the actual question? I defintely want the strongest point on each side, not a “it depends” mush.
