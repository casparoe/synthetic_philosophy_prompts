ok so i need a thought experiment built to order on the whole "is taxation theft" thing, for a reading-group session where i want people to see that the slogan hides all the load-bearing premises. here's the spec:

Construct a scenario involving a single levy -- one levy, same levy -- that gets the verdict THEFT from a Georgist (land value tax on unimproved site value = fine, since nobody made the land; taxes on labor and what labor makes = theft) and the verdict NOT THEFT from a taxation-is-theft libertarian of the self-ownership + just-acquisition stripe (Nozick/Rothbard territory). So each side gives the opposite of its stereotyped answer, on the same instrument.

Rules:
- realistic setting, present-day, no sci-fi. something like municipal finance, a port authority, a water district is fine. one paragraph for the scenario itself.
- no cheating by relabeling: you can't make the Georgist condemn it by making it secretly a tax on buildings or wages in disguise. it must genuinely be levied on site value. the verdict has to fall out of the theory's own land/labor distinction, not from an administrative screw-up.
- same goes the other way: the libertarian's "not theft" has to come out of *their* premises (acquisition, transfer, rectification), not from them suddenly deciding states are cool.
- the scenario can't just stipulate the verdicts. if i can delete one sentence of background and the verdicts flip back to the stereotyped ones, that sentence was doing the work, so tell me which sentence it is.

After the scenario: state each side's premises, walk both verdicts step by step, then give me (a) which feature of the case is load-bearing, (b) how the Georgist fights back (they have a real move about when human-made ground stops counting as a labor product -- make them actually make it), and (c) how the libertarian resists counting a state-collected levy as rectification rather than theft. end with whether you think the case survives both counterattacks or collapses into "it's not really a tax."
