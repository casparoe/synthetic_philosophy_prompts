Is there a name for this? I've noticed a pattern in how people (me included, if I'm honest) talk about moral disagreement. When someone shares my moral view, I describe it as something they concluded or judged — "she opposes factory farming because she's thought about it." But when someone holds the opposite view, I catch myself explaining it instead of crediting it: "he only eats meat because he was raised to," "she's against it because she's squeamish," "they just haven't been exposed to the right experiences." And it runs the other way too — my meat-eating friends say vegetarians are sentimental, my vegan friends say omnivores are conditioned not to think about what they're doing. Each side treats their own position as an answer to reasons and the other side's position as a causal product, something to be accounted for by upbringing or gut reaction rather than engaged with.

The weird thing is the asymmetry. The very same process — growing up in a certain culture, having certain disgust reactions, imbibing your parents' values — gets called a "bias" when it produces the view I reject and a "considered preference" when it produces mine, and I can't figure out what the non-question-begging test for that difference would even be. There's a second puzzle underneath: sometimes learning the causal history of a moral view seems like it should deflate it, and sometimes it seems beside the point. Finding out that my discomfort with something traces back to my grandmother's rules doesn't obviously tell me whether the thing is actually wrong. So the same move — giving a causal explanation of a moral judgment — sometimes feels like debunking and sometimes feels like changing the subject.

I know this is somewhere near the "bias blind spot," but as I understand it that's about people rating themselves as less biased than others in general, not about this specific split between treating a moral view as a verdict versus treating it as a symptom. Is there an established term for this exact asymmetry in moral argument? If not, could you define one and mark out its edges — how it differs from plain motivated reasoning, and whether there's any principled way to tell when a causal story about someone's moral view counts as debunking it versus just committing the genetic fallacy. Don't just give me a label, I want to know where the phenomenon stops.
