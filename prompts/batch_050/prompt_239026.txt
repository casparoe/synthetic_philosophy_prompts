Ok so this is going to be a bit rambly, bear with me. I work at a memory care facility (not any kind of academic, just an orderly who reads a lot at night) and I've gotten obsessed with the philosophy of delusions. There's this resident who is totally convinced her son has been replaced by an imposter -- looks identical, sounds identical, but she insists it isn't him. She's completely normal about everything else. She does crossword puzzles, remembers my schedule, jokes around. She just will not budge on this one thing, and she also doesn't do anything about it, like call the police or whatever. She just sort of lives with it.

From my reading, there's a theory (two-factor I think it's called) where factor one explains where the weird idea comes from, like the familiar feeling is missing, and factor two explains why she doesn't just drop it, some kind of belief-evaluation problem. Fine. But then there's this whole other fight about whether delusions even ARE beliefs at all. One camp says of course they are, they're stated as beliefs, they're believed. The other camp says no, because real beliefs are supposed to guide your actions and respond to evidence, and delusions often do neither. And as far as I can tell this fight has been going for decades and nobody's winning. Same examples, same patients, same arguments, forever.

My guess -- and this is just a guess -- is that the two camps are secretly using the word "belief" for two different jobs, like one side means a box in a theory of the mind and the other side means an actual thing in the brain, and that's why they talk past each other. But maybe it's deeper than that.

So: please act as an expert philosopher of psychiatry and mind. Take your time, think carefully, and start by brainstorming every different KIND of disagreement this could be -- verbal, factual, about which cases count as data, whatever. Then give me your actual diagnosis of why this debate is stuck, and what would even count as progress -- a distinction, an experiment, a better question -- or argue that it's stuck for good and why.
