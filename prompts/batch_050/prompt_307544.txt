I teach an upper-level undergraduate seminar in philosophy of cognitive science, and I am setting the take-home final. I want one essay question on the two-factor theory of monothematic delusion that students cannot answer by reciting the standard Capgras gloss (aberrant autonomic response plus a reasoning/evaluation deficit). Please build the question around the following invented case, which I want reproduced verbatim in the exam paper:

After a head injury, Ms R. insists that her husband has been replaced by an impostor. Unlike typical Capgras patients, she shows normal skin-conductance responses to photographs of her husband's face. She freely volunteers that her own belief is bizarre and probably false, yet says she cannot stop feeling that the man is not her husband. She makes no effort to find her "real" husband, continues to cook his favorite meals, and reacts with ordinary grief when told (falsely, as a test) that the impostor has died.

The question should require students to use the case to do three things: (1) say which of the two factors each feature of the case puts pressure on, including whether a normal skin-conductance response threatens the first factor or merely one proxy for it; (2) assess whether the second factor should be understood as a deficit or a bias, given that Ms R. endorses and disavows the impostor claim in the same breath; and (3) take a position on whether Ms R.'s state is a belief, given her failure to act on it, engaging seriously with the double bookkeeping point rather than treating inaction as decisive either way.

Please write the question itself (worded as it would appear on the exam, answerable in about 90 minutes), a model answer of roughly 600 words, and a marking scheme out of 20 built on five must-hit points. For each point, give the marks available and one line on how students typically get it wrong — for example, treating the autonomic measure as constitutive of the first factor rather than as evidence for an anomalous experience, or describing the case feature-by-feature without saying what it shows about the theory's explanatory division of labor. The scheme should make it impossible to score above half marks by summarizing the two-factor theory without applying it to the case.
