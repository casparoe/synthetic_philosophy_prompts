After a long argument with a friend about whether our actions matter in an infinite universe, she closed with an analogy: an infinite cosmos is like Hilbert's hotel, already full with infinitely many guests, so that however many new guests arrive the register still reads full; likewise, she said, no finite act of good or harm changes the total value of an infinite world, and from this she concluded that comparing outcomes collapses under infinitude and ethics should retreat to the local and finite. Audit the analogy as an argument. Begin with the correspondence table: what plays the role of the guests, the rooms, the register, and the manager's device of moving every guest from room n to room 2n to free up space -- does that last operation correspond to anything in moral reality, and if so, does it leave value unchanged or not? Then separate the disanalogies that matter for her conclusion from the ones that don't: is the total value of a world better modeled by the hotel's cardinality, by its occupancy rate, or by neither, and does the hotel picture quietly assume that only the grand total counts, letting that assumption do the argumentative work? Finally, say whether some defensible version of her paralysis survives once the mapping is corrected, and if the hotel is the wrong picture of an infinite world, propose a better one.
