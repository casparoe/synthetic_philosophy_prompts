My opening objection is this. Consider Lebo, who has grown up in almost complete isolation under a coercive caregiver. The caregiver treats Lebo merely as an extension of their own will, so Lebo has never enjoyed a reciprocal relationship of recognition or care. Nevertheless, Lebo reflects conscientiously on how others might be affected by their actions and, upon first encountering strangers, immediately treats them with fairness and restraint. On your view, is Lebo already a full moral person? If so, relationships seem to be merely a causal aid or evidence of personhood, not constitutive of it. If not, you appear committed to saying that an isolated victim of abuse is less of a person and perhaps deserves diminished moral concern. Which horn of the dilemma do you accept, and can you avoid it without quietly relying on an individual mental capacity that does all the normative work?
