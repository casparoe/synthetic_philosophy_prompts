A few months ago, in the middle of a long argument with my partner, I said -- and I meant it -- something like: "I realize that I'm the one being unfair here, but I still don't believe I'm being unfair." Neither of us could make sense of the sentence afterward. It felt true, it even felt honest, and yet it sounded like a contradiction wearing a trench coat. That sent me down a rabbit hole, and I now know this is Moore's paradox in roughly its omissive form, and that there's a large literature on why such utterances are absurd despite the fact that the conjuncts could both be true of me at once. I facilitate a small philosophy reading group (six of us, mixed backgrounds -- two philosophers, one linguist, a psychotherapist, an engineer, and a lawyer), and I've proposed we spend our next cycle, roughly eight monthly meetings, on Moore's paradox and the cluster of questions around it: the norms of assertion, the transparency of belief, self-knowledge, epistemic akrasia, and the difference between what one can believe and what one can coherently avow.

Before I lock in the syllabus, I want a defended agenda rather than a survey. Which open questions in and around Moore's paradox are actually most worth our limited meetings, and in what order? I'd like the prioritization to be dependency-aware: for instance, does settling whether the paradox is fundamentally about assertion or about belief change how much the self-knowledge questions matter, or vice versa? Are the debates about whether Moore-paradoxical beliefs can be rational or even constitute knowledge upstream or downstream of the transparency question about belief formation? For each item you include, say why it's worth a meeting given our mixed group -- weighing philosophical payoff, tractability for non-specialists with the primary texts plus a small set of papers, and how many other questions hang on it -- and be explicit about what you'd cut and why it wouldn't change much downstream. If there's a live question I haven't gestured at that belongs at the top of the list, please add it and defend its ranking.

One more request: please be careful to separate what is genuinely settled from what remains contested among people who work on this. From my outside reading I get the impression that some points are common ground (e.g., that Moore sentences can be true while being unassertable) while the diagnosis of *why* they're absurd is up for grabs -- but I'm not confident I've drawn that line correctly. Where the field is genuinely split, present it as split rather than letting one diagnosis pose as the consensus, and note what evidence or argument would plausibly move a question up or down my ordering.
