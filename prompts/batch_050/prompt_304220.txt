ok so my roommate (math major, very annoying) hit me with this argument last week and I've been turning it over since and I genuinely can't find the hole, but the conclusion has to be wrong. It goes like this:

(1) Take any fact with more than one thing in it, like Maya being taller than Theo. That fact isn't just the three items Maya, Theo, and the taller-than relation sitting in a pile. A pile of things is just a pile of things -- if it were enough, then {Maya, taller-than, Theo} would be the same fact as {Theo, taller-than, Maya}, and obviously those say different things. So the relation has to actually *relate* them, not just be present.

(2) But taller-than is one more item in the pile. If it's just sitting there next to Maya and Theo, nothing about it being *there* connects it to them. So there has to be a further relation, call it R1, that hooks taller-than onto Maya and Theo.

(3) R1 is also just an item, so by the same reasoning there has to be R2 hooking R1 onto everything, and so on forever.

(4) An infinite chain where each link only works if the next one does never gets started, so nothing ever actually relates anything to anything. No unified facts, no propositions, just piles.

(5) And notice this doesn't just hit "taller than" -- it hits plain old predication too, "the cup is red" needs something tying redness to the cup. So really it shows there are no facts at all, including, yeah, the facts stated in premises (1)-(4).

He says the only options are accept the regress or accept that facts are "just brute" which he counts as the same as giving up. I really want to hang onto premise (1), it seems obviously right that a list of constituents isn't a fact. But something between (2) and (4) smells off to me and I can't articulate it. Which step do you reject, why does it look so convincing, and what does rejecting it actually cost you -- like, what theory of how propositions hang together do you end up committed to?
