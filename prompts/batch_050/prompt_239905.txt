I'm a journalist who covers science and ideas, and after one too many briefings where a climate person says "the models agree" and a policymaker hears "the future is basically a PDF," I want a satirical piece. Write an Onion-style news article, headline plus 600-750 words, reporting that the fictional Office for Atmospheric Reassurance has launched PRISM-∞, a climate "super-ensemble" that folds three very different animals into one official Confidence Flavor: initial-condition sprinkles (same physics, different weather noise), perturbed-parameter fudge (same equations, knobs twiddled), and structural uncertainty (actually different equations, including one holdout model where clouds are still "under review"). The joke should be that PRISM's Robustness Score keeps rising whenever disagreement becomes less visible, not less real: models that share code get counted as independent witnesses, an outlier with ugly tail risk is "down-weighted for negativity," and the press release converts deep uncertainty into a single tasteful gauge labeled PROBABLY FINE.

Keep it deadpan and make the humor come from getting the epistemology right: ensemble spread is not automatically a probability distribution; agreement can mean shared assumptions rather than truth; performance weighting can double-count model "cousins" and excommunicate the one weird run that mattered; robust decision-making means choosing plans that are survivable across many futures, not optimal under the flattering one. Include one pompous quote from an invented Senior Robustness Fellow, one exhausted mayor whose seawall passed the metric but still needs a gift shop, and a boxed glossary for ministers that definately makes things worse. No citations, no real institutes, no actual scientists -- target the ritual of turning climate uncertainty into a decision-ready smoothie, not a person. The satire should still be fair enough that a climate-model person winces, nods, and then quietly checks whether their own ensemble has a PRISM score.
