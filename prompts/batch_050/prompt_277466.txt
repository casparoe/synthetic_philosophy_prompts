I'm designing a 'which stance on collective memory do you actually hold?' quiz for a study group that's been reading around the ethics of remembrance, and I want the quiz to sort people by their verdicts on cases rather than by which thinker they name-drop. Please write eight to ten questions, each a concrete scenario with two or three answer options: for instance, a post-Franco-style pact where a country agrees not to prosecute or officially dwell on the dictatorship; a town that erects a memorial to victims of a massacre most residents would rather not discuss; a truth commission that offers amnesty in exchange for testimony; a demand to rename streets honouring a celebrated scientist who also did awful things; a government that seals offenders' juvenile records so they can start over; survivors who say the best tribute to the dead is to stop re-litigating the past.

Then a scoring key mapping answer patterns onto at least these leaves: the view that remembering is owed to the dead and constitutive of who 'we' are (Margalit-ish), the view that collective memory is mostly a political poison and peace sometimes requires deliberate forgetting (Rieff-ish), the Nietzschean view that forgetting is an active capacity needed for life and creation, and the 'never again' view that remembrance is justified purely as a warning/prevention instrument. If two leaves can't be separated by any set of case verdicts, say so explicitly rather than forcing it. And if some coherent combination of answers lands on a position nobody defends, flag it and guess why (incoherent, unattractive, or just unexplored). British English in your answer, please.
