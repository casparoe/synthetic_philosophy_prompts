I'm on a grad student panel next week where each of us has to state our view on abortion and then defend it against the audience, and I'd rather find the holes in my position now than up at the podium. Here's what I currently hold — tell me whether it all hangs together:

(a) Thomson's violinist case basically settles things for pregnancies resulting from rape: nobody has a right to use your body to stay alive without your consent, and unplugging yourself isn't murder, so abortion is permissible there even granting the fetus full personhood.
(b) At the same time, I genuinely find Marquis's future-like-ours argument compelling — what makes killing an ordinary adult wrong is that it robs them of a valuable future, a standard fetus has exactly such a future, so standard abortion is wrong for the same reason killing you or me is.
(c) Abortion after contraceptive failure in a consensual relationship is still permissible — she took reasonable precautions, and you can't get conscripted into nine months of pregnacy over a broken condom.
(d) Contraception itself is obviously morally fine.
(e) Killing a healthy newborn is seriously, straightforwardly wrong, full stop.
(f) The killing vs. letting die distinction does real moral work: refusing aid you never agreed to give is a different thing from actively killing someone.

Which of these genuinely conflict, which only look like they do on the surface, and what's the cheapest revision that makes the whole set consistent? Please rank the tensions by severity rather than listing every pair that raises an eyebrow, and for each one tell me what would actually have to give and what that costs. I honestly don't know which commitment I'd drop first, so don't pull punches.
