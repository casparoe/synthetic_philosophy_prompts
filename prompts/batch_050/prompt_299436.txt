I’ve got a homemade case that’s causing a fight about whether interventionism and counterfactual theories really disagree or just talk past each other. I want a number, not a general survey.

A sealed cooling box has a switch S and an internal clamp K. The box starts at noon iff S is UP and K is open. The setup is mechanically weird: any outside agent who directly moves S to UP also closes K. But an internal electrical fault could move S to UP without touching K.

In the actual world, S stays DOWN, K stays open, and the box doesn’t start. In the closest relevant world where an internal fault moves S to UP while K remains open, the box does start. But in every world where someone externally intervenes on S, the intervention also closes K, so the box still doesn’t start.

Let P be the claim: S’s being DOWN is an actual token-level cause of the box’s not starting at noon. By cause I don’t just mean a useful control point or the best thing to mention in an explanation; I mean one of the things responsible for this particular non-start.

My credence in P is around .7; my freind’s is around .1. Give me yours, between 0 and 1, and decompose it. Give rough probabilities for at least these sub-questions:

- whether causation requires the existence of a possible ideal or surgical intervention on the cause variable;
- whether the closest-world counterfactual is legitimate when its antecedent could only be realized by an internal fault rather than by an intervention;
- whether S-DOWN and K-OPEN are genuinely separate variables or one packaged physical variable;
- whether the disagreement concerns token causation itself or merely different concepts like control, explanation, and metaphysical production;
- whether the effect should be individuated as not starting at noon or as not being startable by an outside operator.

Say what verdict the counterfactual and interventionist approaches each give, show how your sub-probabilities combine into the final credence (a rough weighted calculation is fine), and flag any dependencies between the sub-questions. Then tell me which sub-question your number is most sensitive to, where my friend’s .1 and my .7 most likely diverge, and what single feature of the case you’d change to make the two theories deliver the same verdict.
