So the doomsday argument has been kicked around since the early 80s — Carter's original version, Leslie's book, Gott's delta-t paper, and then roughly four decades of replies and counter-replies — and as far as I can tell the debate has completely stalled. The weird part is that nobody disputes the math. Everyone agrees that if you treat your birth rank as a random draw from the set of all humans who will ever live and you start from some prior over total population size, conditioning on being roughly the 100-billionth human shifts your posterior toward "doom soon." The fight is entirely about whether that inference is legitimate. One camp says it's just Copernican reasoning applied to your temporal position and refusing it is special pleading; the other camp says you're "updating" on evidence you possessed all along (you've always known your birth rank), or that the whole thing hinges on an arbitrary choice of reference class (all humans? all intelligent observers? all observer-moments?), and the argument can be made to predict anything by picking the class.

My hypothesis, though I'm not confident in it: the actual disagreement isn't about doomsday at all, it's about SSA- vs SIA-style anthropic principles, and those can't be adjudicated empirically because any prediction you derive from one of them is itself evaluated under one of them — so the "presumptuous philosopher" type thought experiments function as intuition pumps rather than data, and each side just finds the other side's pumps absurd. If that's right, this is less like a normal factual dispute and more like a standoff over which epistemic norms to adopt.

So: diagnose it. Is this a substantive disagreement, a verbal one, or two camps answering different questions (something like "what should I expect given my evidence" vs "what will actually happen") under one heading? And given your diagnosis, what would even count as progress — a formal impossibility/consistency result, a betting scheme someone would actually accept as decisive, dissolving the reference class problem, or something else? Or should we read the 40-year impasse itself as evidence that anthropic self-location reasoning is just ill-posed?
