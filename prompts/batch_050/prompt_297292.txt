I run a monthly philosophy meetup in my city, mostly sharp people with zero formal training -- a couple of software engineers, a retired nurse, one guy who did a philosophy minor twenty years ago. Next session is on dialetheism: the view that some contradictions are actually true. I've got 75 minutes and a whiteboard. I want a plan for how to run the explanation, not the explanation itself.

Here's what I need from you:

The order of ideas, with the dependency spelled out. My instinct is that explosion (contradiction implies everything) has to come before the liar sentence, because otherwise the liar just lands as a cute puzzle and nobody feels any pressure. But you could also argue motivation-first: liar first, then "and here's the disaster classical logic has in store." Pick one and defend the choice -- tell me what breaks if you get the order backwards. Also tell me where paraconsistent logic enters: as the rescue after the disaster, or earlier as foreshadowing?

The one carrying example. I assume it's some version of the liar, but which one exactly, for this crowd? Plain "this sentence is false," or the strengthened version with "not true"? Whatever you pick, say what the example needs to have (self-containment? no empirical facts to check? a version they can hold in their heads without notation?) and what it must NOT have at this level. If Curry's paradox would tempt them, tell me why to keep it out.

The misconception map. I can already predict some: that dialetheists believe literally everything (trivialism), that this kills proof by contradiction, that "true and false" just means "true and untrue" in disguise, and that the whole thing is a language trick you can dissolve by banning self-reference. List the ones you expect, in roughly the order they'll surface during the session, and mark the exact point in my sequence where I should head each one off -- before it forms, or let it form and then use it?

One closing question I can put to the group that would actually tell me whether they got it -- not whether they agree with dialetheism, but whether they understand what the position is. Something where the wrong answers are diagnostic, i.e. tell me which misconception they're still holding.

And finally: what should I openly refuse to touch at this level (formal systems, revenge problems, gluts about motion or borders?) and how should I say "that's real but not tonight" without making it sound like hand-waving?

Keep it concrete. This group will bail on anything that smells like notation for notation's sake.
