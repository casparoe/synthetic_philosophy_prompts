I've been reading a bunch of social science lately and I keep running into the same move, and I want to know if there's a name for it. The pattern goes like this. Someone wants to explain why some group of people does X. The explanation offered is: the group values X, or has a culture of X. Fine so far. But then when you ask how we know the group has that value or culture, the evidence turns out to be... that they do X. So the value is doing all the explanatory work, but the only proof the value exists is the thing it was invented to explain.

Concrete cases I have in mind. One: a commentator says Americans work unusually long hours because of a deeply rooted work ethic inherited from their religious past; asked for evidence of this ethic independent of the hours, he cites surveys where Americans say work is important -- surveys conducted, presumably, among the people working the long hours. Two: a development report explains low savings rates in some region by a "present-oriented culture," and its cultural indicators are things like how much people save and whether they prefer smaller-sooner rewards in questionnaires. Three: someone explains a company's risk-aversion by its "culture," and culture is measured by asking employees how risk-averse the company is.

I know this is in the neighborhood of Weber's Protestant ethic argument, since that's the most famous attempt to explain economic behavior by a value, and I gather Weber himself was cagey about the direction of causation -- he talked about some kind of affinity or fit between the ethic and the spirit of capitalism rather than straightforward causation, though I'm fuzzy on whether that actually solves the problem or just renames it. It's also in the neighborhood of the old joke about explaining opium's effects by its sleep-inducing power, but that joke is about empty explanations of physical stuff and this seems subtler, because values and cultures are presumably real things that really do influence behavior -- so the explanation isn't necessarily empty, it's just... unverifiable in the way it's usually deployed?

So: does this failure mode have an established name in sociology, anthropology, or philosophy of social science? If there are several nearby concepts, tell me which fits best and how the others differ. And if nothing quite fits, coin something with a definition sharp enough that I could classify borderline cases -- like, would a genetic explanation of the hours ("Americans work long because of selection for industriousness") with the same evidence structure count, or is the problem specific to invoking values?

Oh and put a last line at the very end starting with "Verdict:" giving the single best-fitting term, or your coined one if nothing fits.
