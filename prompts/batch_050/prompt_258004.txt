I run a reading group on the foundations of quantum mechanics, and I want a worked example showing that treating the quantum state as a state of knowledge is not merely classical probability theory in disguise -- that the psi-epistemic program has real content and runs into real trouble. Design a toy model to the following specifications, then put it through a specific stress test.

The model. Restrict attention to a single qubit. Give the system a small, explicitly listed set of ontic states (no more than eight), and represent each preparation as a probability distribution over them, with measurements specified by response functions (conditional probabilities of outcomes given the ontic state). Include preparations corresponding to the four states |0>, |1>, |+>, and |->, and at least the two measurements that distinguish |0> from |1> and |+> from |->. No continuous state spaces, no infinitely many ontic states, and nothing the reader cannot verify with arithmetic.

Phenomena to reproduce. The model must recover, purely from its probability distributions and response functions: (i) perfect distinguishability of orthogonal preparations; (ii) imperfect distinguishability of non-orthogonal preparations, arising specifically from overlap between their distributions rather than being stipulated; (iii) a no-cloning result -- show that any copying procedure definable within the model must fail on at least one preparation, and explain the failure in the model's own terms; and (iv) a measurement-update rule, stating explicitly whether it is ordinary Bayesian conditionalization on the ontic state plus a disturbance, and if a disturbance is required, saying so honestly.

The stress test. Now take two independently prepared copies of the system, each prepared either as |0> or as |+>, and consider a joint measurement with four outcomes, each outcome designed to be impossible for exactly one of the four joint preparations (|0>|0>, |0>|+>, |+>|0>, |+>|+>). Using your model's actual numbers: attempt to assign response functions for the joint measurement so that each outcome gets probability zero on its corresponding joint preparation, assuming the two systems' ontic states are drawn independently from their respective distributions. Either show the contradiction that overlap forces, with the arithmetic displayed, or -- if your model somehow escapes -- exhibit the escape and state its price.

The write-up. After the construction, identify which single structural feature of the model (or of the stress test's assumptions) does the decisive work, and state what happens to the contradiction if that feature is removed. Then give the strongest reply available to a committed psi-epistemic theorist who wants to keep overlapping distributions and reject the test -- which assumption they would deny, and what denying it costs them for composite systems generally. Close with one paragraph on what the exercise does and does not establish about the claim that quantum states represent knowledge rather than reality. Throughout: the phenomena and the contradiction must be generated by the model, not asserted of it.
