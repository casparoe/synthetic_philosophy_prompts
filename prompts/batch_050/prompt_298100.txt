A memorial foundation I advise has an ethics board split between two camps who have been talking past each other for months. Camp one, the comparabilists, think atrocities can in principle be ranked -- by death toll, cruelty, intent, lasting harm -- and that refusing to rank is itself a covert ranking, since it treats them all as equally grave. Camp two, the anti-comparabilists, think ranking atrocities is conceptually confused (there's no common scale of evil) or outright obscene (it builds a heirarchy of victims and instrumentalizes the dead).

Draft the joint statement both camps could actually sign after a serious adversarial collaboration. It should be usable for the board's real problem: deciding how to split commemoration funding between, say, the Rwandan genocide, the Holodomor, and the transatlantic slave trade.

Use these sections, in this order: "What we agree on" (numbered claims, ~200 words; I'd guess both sides can sign things like 'comparing atrocities for historical explanation is different from comparing them for victim recognition' and 'not every comparison is a ranking'), then "Where we still disagree" (~200 words; each disagreement phrased so its own side accepts the wording, and say which disagreements turned out to be merely verbal), then "Cruxes" (~250 words; the sub-questions where an answer would move at least one side -- e.g. whether ordinal ranking really requires a common metric of badness, whether declining to compare is neutral or itself a comparison -- and for each crux, a concrete case or consideration both sides agree bears on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance). Finish with "Settled vs. genuinely contested" (~100 words): be explicit about what is actually consensus among people who think about this and what is still disputed, and don't dress up a contested view as the settled one.
