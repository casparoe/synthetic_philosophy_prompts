Two weeks ago my car broke down on a remote road in the middle of nowhere, with no phone signal and night coming on. After several hours a stranger stopped. He said he was driving out of his way to pick up a friend and would happily take me the forty miles to the nearest town, but only on one condition: that I promise to send him £1,500 within the month. He was quite open about his method. He told me he had spent years studying people and was a near-perfect judge of whether someone would keep a promise of this kind; he said he could see that I was the sort who would pay, and that this, and only this, was why he was prepared to stop. Had he judged otherwise, he would have driven on. I promised, he delivered me safely to town, and the money is now due.

Last night I had a long and rather heated argument about this with a close friend. Her position is that paying would be straightforwardly irrational. I am already safe; he cannot enforce the promise; nobody would ever know; and £1,500 is a sum I can ill afford, being between jobs. Whatever I now do cannot change what has already happened, she says, and deliberation should be about the future, which paying does nothing to improve. My instinct is that she is missing something important. The reason I am sitting here safely at all, rather than still on that road, is that he correctly predicted what I would do at exactly this moment. It seems to me that if people generally reasoned the way my friend recommends, people like my rescuer would stop stopping, and I would not have been rescued in the first place. But when I try to put this into a form that answers her point about the future, it comes out sounding like a muddle between cause and effect, since my paying now obviously cannot reach back and cause him to have stopped.

So: should I pay, and if so, is there a coherent way of defending the payment as rational rather than merely honourable? I would also like to know whether your answer changes if, instead of being a near-perfect judge of character, he was right about people only, say, nine times out of ten, or if the sum demanded were so large that paying it would genuinely harm me.

Please lead with your bottom line in the first sentence and then give your reasoning, with no preamble and no restating of my question.
