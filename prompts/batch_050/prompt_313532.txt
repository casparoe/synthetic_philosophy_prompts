I keep noticing a pattern in how people talk about age cohorts, and I want to know whether it has an established name. If it doesn't, I'd like one coined carefully, because I don't think any single concept I half-know covers the whole thing.

The pattern has three moving parts that always seem to travel together. First, the reference slides. A label like Millennial was introduced to mean people reaching adulthood around the year 2000, but in actual use it quietly kept meaning "people currently in their twenties," so that by the late 2010s columnists were applying it to teenagers — until a fresh label arrived to occupy the slot, at which point the old one snapped back to its official birth-year definition and everyone retroactively agreed it had meant that all along. Second, the attributions recycle. The traits pinned on each incoming cohort — lazy, entitled, fragile, unable to delay gratification — are nearly word-for-word the traits pinned on the previous cohort at the same age, which were dropped as that cohort grew older, apparently without anyone registering that what was being tracked all along was life-stage rather than character. Third, and strangest to me, the arbitrariness is openly conceded and makes no difference. The same people who will tell you the cutoffs are marketing artifacts, that someone born in December of one year and someone born in January of the next are being sorted into different kinds on no basis at all, will in the next breath explain a colleague's work habits by which side of the cutoff he was born on. People born near the boundary genuinely agonize over which label they "really" belong to, and having settled it, sometimes begin to conform to the profile.

So: does this configuration have a name? It is not plain stereotyping, since the stereotypes are assigned to a moving target and expire on schedule. It is not quite what I understand by essentializing a social group either, since that normally involves mistakenly taking the group to be a natural kind, whereas here nobody takes it to be one — the essence-talk coexists with a fully explicit acknowledgment that the kind is fictional, and even seems to feed on that acknowledgment, as though the irony licensed the inference. And while there is a famous idea about classifications looping back and changing the classified, that idea is usually told as a story about expert categories imposed on people, not about arbitrary ones people adopt about themselves with their eyes open.

What I'd like is the carving, not just a label. If the parts have separate names in different literatures, tell me what each is, how the thing I've described fits or strains each one, and which fits best. Where are its edges — does the same structure appear with usage-based groupings like "the TikTok generation," or with decades like "the sixties," or is something essential lost when the grouping isn't a birth cohort at all? And if nothing established covers the whole configuration, propose a term, define it, and give me cases that fall just inside and just outside it.
