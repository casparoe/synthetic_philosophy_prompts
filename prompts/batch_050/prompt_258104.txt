Ok so I run a small nonprofit hardship fund (about 40 grants a year, mostly a few thousand dollars each, decided by a volunteer intake commitee of six people with no philosophy background and maybe 45 minutes per case). Our founding charter says we should prioritize applicants whose misfortune came from brute bad luck rather than from risks they knowingly took on, which I gather is more or less the brute luck vs option luck distinction from luck egalitarianism. Problem is the charter just states the principle and leaves us to fight about everytime a hard case shows up.

Turn this into a decision procedure my committee can actually run on an application: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each one, where the procedure just stops with a no, and where it has to hand things off to a human because the remaining question is about values rather than facts. It needs to handle the cases that keep splitting us: people who declined insurance they could have afforded, informed gambles that went bad versus gambles where the applicant couldn't realistically have known the odds, and the messier one — choices that only count as "choices" against a background the person didn't pick (like turning down the one job offer in a dying town). Tell me which distinctions from the theory have to survive the translation and which I can safely drop for a 45-minute process.

Then run the procedure on three applications: (1) a fisherman who skipped boat insurance and lost his boat in a storm, (2) a professional violinist whose hand injury ended her career after she picked a famously risky profession over a safer offer, and (3) a factory worker whose town's single employer shut down two years after he turned down a transfer to another city. Include at least one where the procedure spits out an answer I won't like, and be honest about what the procedure can't do — e.g. where the luck test ends up punishing imprudence in a way that feels cruel, or forces us to snoop into applicants' lives in ways that are themselves objectionable. Please answer in American English.
