Construct a thought experiment in which a psychiatric classification produces a beneficial looping effect in Hacking's sense -- the people classified change in response to being classified -- even though the classification is invalid: there is no natural kind, discrete condition, or unified underlying process that it picks out. Constraints: the setting must be an ordinary outpatient clinic, not a satirical or dystopian one; the diagnosis must be plausible enough that competent, well-meaning clinicians would use it; and, crucially, the benefit must flow from the classification itself -- from how being labeled changes the patients' self-understanding, behavior, or social treatment -- and not merely from the label unlocking access to a treatment that would have helped anyone. After the scenario, walk through the mechanics of the loop step by step; explain why the benefit does not count as evidence that the category is real, and what it would take to distinguish the two empirically; identify which single feature of your case is doing the work in severing therapeutic success from validity; and finish with the strongest reply available to a clinician who insists that a diagnosis this useful must be tracking something true.
