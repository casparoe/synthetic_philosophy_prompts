I run a small ML team and we've been stuck in the same argument in code review for months. Half the team writes comments like "the model thinks this user is an admin" or "the agent wants to retry the failed request," and the other half says this is sloppy thinking and the model doesn't beleive anything, it just predicts tokens. Someone brought up Dennett and the "intentional stance" and now everyone has a different reading of what it actually settles. The closest I've gotten to philosophy is a Hofstadter book, so I'm out of my depth but I'm the one writing the wiki page.

What I want is a one-page decision tree for the wiki that takes a teammate from some neutral starting point (something like "this system reliably does the right thing in its domain") to a named position on when it's legitimate to say a system believes X or wants Y. The branches should be the real fault lines, not the labels: whether predictive success from treating it as a believer is enough on its own, whether it matters what's physically inside (transformer weights vs neurons vs a bimetallic strip), whether a belief has to be hooked into a whole web of other beliefs and desires to count, whether there needs to be a fact of the matter beyond the stance being useful, and anything else you think belongs. Each leaf should get a position name (or "unnamed but coherent" if nothing standard fits), and one sentence on what that position forces you to say about our LLM-based support agent specifically.

A few constraints. Please test each branch question against at least these cases so they actually discriminate: our support agent, a chess engine, a thermostat, and a dog. Phrase every branch question so someone can answer it without knowing which leaf it routes them to -- the point is to sort my team by what they actually think, not by who's read Dennett. Note any combination of answers that's unoccupied and say briefly whether that's because it's incoherent or just unpopular, and flag any pair of leaves your tree can't really separate. Max 6 or 7 branch points or nobody will read it.
