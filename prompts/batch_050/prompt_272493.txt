I've been collecting proposed solutions to Curry's paradox (the one where the sentence says, roughly: if this very sentence is true, then everything is true, or pick your favorite absurdity, and a few seemingly innocent steps land you there). Here's what I've got so far:

(1) Dialetheism -- accept the conclusion that the Curry sentence is both true and false, and block explosion with a paraconsistent logic, so the disaster is contained rather than avoided.
(2) Reject contraction for the conditional -- from 'if C then (if C then absurdity)' you can't collapse the two copies of C into one, so the proof breaks in the middle. The substructural route.
(3) Deny the sentence expresses a proposition at all -- it fails to have a truth value, or fails to say anything, so you're not licensed to start reasoning conditionally with it in the first place.
(4) Non-transitive logics -- keep every individual inference rule but reject Cut, so the argument's steps are each fine but you're not allowed to chain them together into the conclusion.
(5) Blame the fixed point -- the self-referential construction that produces the sentence was never guaranteed to define a coherent content, so there's no well-formed paradox to solve, just a naming trick.

Is there a sixth solution that's actually different from these? Not a mix-and-match of two of them, not one of them with extra steps, and not just 'use a truth hierarchy' unless you can say why that isn't (3) in a trenchcoat. If it's a real proposal someone has made, say whose; if it's your own invention that's fine too, but then tell me honestly whether it survives the obvious counterattack (e.g. whether it reinstates the paradox one level up).
