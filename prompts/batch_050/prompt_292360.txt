write me a satirical employee handbook, around 700-900 words, for Chamberlain Commons, a gated community that has reorganized itself around Nozick’s entitlement theory after reading the Wilt Chamberlain argument. It should be written in the chipper, bureaucratic voice of a real HR manual, with numbered sections and a FAQ.

Set it up so the satire comes from the actual theory, not a cartoon version: everyone starts with the same legitimate holding, residents voluntarily pay a little extra to watch Wilt mow the communal lawn in an unusually beautiful pattern, Wilt ends up much richer, and the Pattern Preservation Committee keeps trying to restore the original distribution every Monday. The handbook should explain why each voluntary transfer was supposedly fine, why the resulting inequality counts as just if the original holdings and transfers were just, and why constant redistribution looks like continuous interference rather than harmless tidying-up.

But also let the cracks show naturally: Who decides whether the original holdings were acquired justly? What happens if one resident’s grandfather stole the gazebo? When does pressure from neighbors stop being persuasion and become coercion? Does consent to one transfer settle the justice of the whole system? And what does the rectification office actually do with centuries of messy history? Include a Lockean proviso clause about leaving enough and as good for others, but make it hilariously unclear how the community measures that.

The tone should stay deadpan, with jokes like refund procedures for regretted applause, consent forms for admiring Wilt’s lawn stripes, and a rule forbidding residents from calling the outcome unfair merely because they dislike the pattern. Don’t have the manual say that whatever people voluntarily choose is automatically good; Nozick’s claim is about justice in holdings, not general moral approval. By the end, the reader should both laugh and be left wondering whether the Wilt Chamberlain argument proves too much or exactly what it says it proves.
