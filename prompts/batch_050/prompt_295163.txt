I'm part of a reading group on the philosophy of probability, and next week I'm responsible for presenting my own position on a puzzle we've been circling for a while, so I want a consistency check on my commitments before I defend them. The puzzle: a volunteer agrees to the following experiment. On Sunday she is put to sleep, and a fair coin is flipped. If it lands heads, she is awakened on Monday, interviewed, and the experiment ends. If it lands tails, she is awakened on Monday, interviewed, given a drug that erases her memory of that awakening, and awakened again on Tuesday for an identical interview. She knows the entire protocol in advance, and on any awakening she cannot tell which day it is or whether she has been awake before.

Here is roughly where I've landed. (1) On Sunday, her credence in heads should be 1/2, since the coin is fair. (2) On any awakening, before being told anything about the day, her credence in heads should remain 1/2: all she learns is that she is awake at some point in the experiment, which she knew in advance was guaranteed, so no relevant evidence has come in. (3) If, during an awakening, the experimenters tell her it is Monday, her credence in heads should stay at 1/2, because a Monday awakening occurs under both outcomes of the coin, so learning that it is Monday cannot favor heads over tails. (4) Ordinary Bayesian conditionalization is the correct way to update on new information. (5) If, at each awakening, she is offered a bet that costs her $40 and pays out $100 if the coin landed heads (with winnings and losses settled after the experiment), she should accept, evaluating the bet from the perspective of the awakening at which it is offered; by the credence in (2), the bet has positive expected value, and the fact that it gets placed twice in the tails branch is just what happens in the world where she loses. (6) Rational credence should line up with the limiting frequencies she would observe across many repetitions of a setup; and I grant that if this experiment were repeated hundreds of times, roughly two-thirds of all awakenings would occur in tails branches. I don't take (6) to force her waking credence down to 1/3, though I admit I can't fully articulate why not.

My question: does this package hang together? Please go through it pair by pair where it matters, tell me which commitments genuinely pull against each other and which only appear to, and rank the real tensions by severity. For each genuine tension, tell me the cheapest revision that resolves it and what that revision costs. Finally, if you had to pick a single change to the whole set that would restore coherence at the lowest overall price, which would it be? I'd ask that you engage with the commitments as stated rather than simply telling me which standard position in the literature I should adopt.
