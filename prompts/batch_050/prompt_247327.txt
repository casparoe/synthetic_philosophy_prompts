Write an essay on a controversy I have lately come to have a personal stake in. I was recently appointed to my hospital's diagnostic review committee, where physicians independently assess the same case files and then compare judgments. I assumed that when a colleague I regard as my equal, having read the same file, reaches a different diagnosis, I should substantially lower my confidence in my own. A senior colleague objected: the file's contents are the evidence, she said, and the fact that someone disagrees adds nothing about the patient -- to treat her disagreement as a reason is to let my judgment be dragged around by other minds rather than the case. I then noticed a further puzzle about my own position: if I remain confident in my diagnosis while also believing that a rational person with my track record and my peer's disagreement should not be confident, I seem to be in the position of believing something while believing I ought not believe it, and I am unsure whether that position is coherent or merely uncomfortable.

The essay should weigh the arguments on both sides of the disagreement controversy, drawing on considerations such as the independence of the other judgment, whether my colleague's verdict is itself evidence about the case, and what follows if refusing to defer becomes a policy everyone adopts. It should also examine whether evidence that my reasoning is impaired (say, fatigue or a poor record on similar cases) and evidence that a peer disagrees call for the same response, or whether these differ in a way my senior colleague's objection trades on. Finally, it should address the akrasia question: can it be rational to hold a belief one judges irrational to hold, or must the two judgments collapse into one? Reach a definite conclusion about what I should do on the committee and justify it, rather than surveying the options and stopping there.
