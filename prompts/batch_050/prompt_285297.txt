Build me a thought experiment where a one-shot destructive mind upload preserves the person, but gradual neuron-by-neuron replacement — the method everyone usually treats as the safe option — ends up killing them. Constraints: near-future tech only, no souls and no quantum woo; in the upload arm the scanning process destroys the biological brain, so their's no leftover original muddying things; and nobody in the story, including the subject, ever notices anything amiss — the lethal difference has to be invisible from both the inside and the outside. The divergence between the two methods must fall out of one identifiable feature of the setup, not just be stipulated. The point of the case is to test whether survival depends on the fidelity of the end product or on the kind of causal process that produced it. After the scenario, walk through how a couple of theories of personal identity (psychological continuity, some continuity-first or embodied view) rule on each arm, name the feature doing the work, and say whether removing it collapses the case back into the usual "gradual replacement is fine" verdict.
