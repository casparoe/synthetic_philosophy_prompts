I write for a magazine that covers science and ideas, and I'm finishing a long essay on Sidgwick and the dualism of practical reason, which he treated as the deepest unresolved problem in ethics: the apparent fact that both the egoistic axiom and the utilitarian axiom are self-evident, yet they can issue conflicting directives about the same life. The essay closes with a statement of my own considered view, and before it goes to my editors I want to know whether the position I've staked out is actually coherent or whether I've quietly committed myself to incompatible things. Here is where I've landed. First, I hold that rational egoism is genuinely rational: each of us has weighty, non-derivative reason to promote our own good, and this reason is not merely a disguised form of impartial concern. Second, I hold that the impartial point of view is equally rationally compulsory: the good of all, considered from no one's perspective in particular, gives me reasons just as objective as my own good does. Third, following Sidgwick, I hold that the dualism is genuine and cannot be dissolved by philosophical argument: neither source of reasons reduces to the other, and when they conflict there is no higher rational principle that settles the matter. Fourth, I hold that practical reason must be coherent in the sense of yielding determinate all-things-considered verdicts: it cannot rationally direct me both to do something and to refrain from doing it, full stop. Fifth, I accept a broadly Parfitian reductionism about personal identity: what matters in survival is psychological continuity and connectedness, and there is no deep further fact of the kind that would make the boundaries between persons metaphysically fundamental. Sixth, I'm a realist about normative reasons: there are objective, mind-independent facts about what I have reason to do.

Interviewing philosophers for this piece, I've had people tell me the set is obviously inconsistent and others tell me it's basically just Sidgwick's own position plus modern metaphysics of persons, and I can't tell who's right. Please audit it rather than lecture me on the history. Which pairs of commitments are in genuine tension, and which pairs only appear to be? I'm particularly unsure whether the first two commitments conflict on their own, or whether the trouble only enters through something else I've signed up for. For each genuine tension, say how severe it is and identify the cheapest revision that removes it, being explicit about what that revision costs. If two of my commitments are each standard on their own but rest on incompatible motivations, I want that spelled out precisely, including what the motivations are. And if one single revision would clean up the most, tell me which it is, but don't assume in advance that I know which commitment I'd be willing to drop.
