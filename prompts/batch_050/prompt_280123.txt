Write me a mock FAQ page, around 500 words, from the "Office of Eternity" — the celestial department that fields complaints from mortals about divine foreknowledge. The running joke is that every complaint arrives in tensed language (God knew yesterday what I would do today, so I couldn't have done otherwise) and the office's replies keep correcting it: from the eternal standpoint, where all of time is seen at once in a single unchanging present, nothing is literally fore-known, so strictly speaking there is no foreknowledge department and the office is only answering out of courtesy. The humour should come from getting Boethius right rather than from mocking religion: the tota simul idea, why seeing an event isn't the same as causing it (the office trots this out the way a helpline says have you tried turning it off and on again), and the distinction between it being necessary that what God sees happens, and the event itself being necessary. Include at least one question from a prisoner awaiting execution who is somehow not comforted, and one where the office's pedantry about tense is itself the joke — say, refusing to process a form because it was sumbitted in the future tense. Deadpan all the way through, British English spelling, and end with a single final line starting "Verdict:" saying in plain terms whether the office has actually solved the problem or just renamed it.
