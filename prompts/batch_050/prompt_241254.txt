Had a stupidly long argument with my friend Sam last night about when its okay to just flat-out assert something. His line was basically: if you honestly believe it and you're not trying to deceive anyone, say it, end of story. I kept pushing that no, you need to actually know — a well-informed guess that happens to land true isn't good enough. We got nowhere and now I'm annoyed.

Anyway, I run a first-year philosophy discussion section (14 students, 75 minutes) and I want them to feel this fight in their gut before I ever name it. Design me an in-class game or activity where students have to make assertions under some point system, and where the structure manufactures at least one moment where a student asserts something true on excellent evidence that was still lucky (Gettier-style), so the room genuinely splits over whether they played well or just got fortunate. No lecture up front and nobody should hear the phrase "knowledge norm" until after. I want: the rules, a scoring scheme that doesn't need real money, a rough timeline, and a debrief mapping whatever fight breaks out onto the belief norm, the truth norm, and the knowledge norm. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it, and which part of the real debate the game can't reproduce no matter how I set it up.
