For most of my career I have designed and maintained large relational database systems, and I am fluent in the formal side of that craft: schemas, keys, attributes, tuples, set versus bag semantics, query behavior, and the practical distinction between two rows being equal in every recorded value and two rows being the same row. My knowledge of physics, by contrast, is that of an attentive amateur. From my reading I gather that electrons of the same kind cannot be told apart by any measurement, that the mathematics of quantum theory treats swapping two of them as yielding no new state of affairs, that this particular way of counting states has real observable consequences, and that philosophers nonetheless argue about whether the particles are distinct individuals. Some apparently hold that each particle possesses a primitive thisness beyond all of its properties, others that only the pattern of properties exists and the particles are nothing over and above it, and still others that the question itself is badly posed.

I would like that debate reconstructed in the terms of my own discipline, as rigorously as you can manage. I have some guesses about how the mapping might go. The physicists' different schemes for counting states seem to correspond to choices I make routinely: assigning every row a surrogate key that records nothing about the world, or relying only on natural attributes and letting exact duplicates collapse, or imposing a uniqueness constraint so that no two rows may coincide on a given set of attributes. The philosophers' primitive thisness seems to correspond to a column that no query is ever permitted to read. But I would rather you work through the translation systematically than simply confirm or correct my guesses.

Concretely, then: render each of the main positions in the debate as claims about table design, keys, and query behavior; state which claims translate cleanly, which translate only with distortion (and identify precisely where the distortion enters), and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all. Then give me your assessment of what remains untranslated: is the residue a sign that my vocabulary is missing something real, or a sign that part of the philosophical disagreement is less substantive than it appears? I am more interested in the residue than in the parts that carry over intact.

Finally, please indicate how confident you are in each step of the translation, and single out the one point you are least sure of — whether the doubt concerns the aptness of a particular mapping, or the claim that something genuinely cannot be expressed in database terms.
