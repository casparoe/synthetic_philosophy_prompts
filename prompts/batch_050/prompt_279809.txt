Is there an existing name for this? I keep noticing it in myself and people around me. Say I form a view about something -- like which contractor to hire, or whether some study everyone's sharing is actually solid -- and then someone I respect disagrees with me, and walks me through why. In the moment I genuinely update. I'm not just pretending to be persuaded; I actually feel my confidence drop, I might even act on their view (hire their guy, whatever). But then, weeks later, I notice my confidence has crept back up toward where it started. And here's the thing: nothing new came in. I didn't re-read the study, I didn't get new data on the contractor. It's more like the memory of being disagreed with fades, and my old way of seeing it reasserts itself, and by the time I notice I'm basically back to my original view and acting on it again.

I've seen the same thing with a friend who's a doctor -- she'll defer to a colleague's reading of a scan, then months later realize she's gone back to reading similar scans her old way without ever deciding the colleague was wrong.

I know there's a whole literature on what you SHOULD do when an epistemic peer disagrees with you, but that's about the moment of disagreement. What I'm describing is about what happens after you've already done the right thing -- the slow reversion afterward. Is there an established concept for that specific failure? If there are a few nearby ones, please tell me what each covers and where mine falls outside it. And if nothing quite fits, coin a term and define it, including where the edges are (e.g., is it still the same phenomenon if new evidence does arrive but is too weak to justify the reversion? what if the reversion is actually correct?).

After the prose part, please give me a table with columns: concept (name, whether established or coined by you), what it covers, how it differs from my phenomenon, and fit (best / partial / doesn't fit). Include your coined term as the last row if you coin one.
