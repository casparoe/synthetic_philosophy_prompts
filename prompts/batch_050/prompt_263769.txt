I'm a journalist (science and ideas desk) working on a magazine profile of a New Testament scholar -- one of those historical-Jesus researchers who thinks we can seperate what the actual man probably said and did from what later Christian communities projected back onto him. I've been sitting in on her seminar and interviewing her between classes, and before I describe her method in print I want to sanity-check that I've actually got it. Her calls so far: the temple protest near the end of his life, authentic -- it turns up independently in Mark and John and fits the fact that he ended up executed as a political threat. The baptism by John, authentic -- multiple strands of tradition report it, and it's not something the church would invent, since being baptized by John makes John look like the senior figure. The predictions that the end was coming within his hearers' lifetimes, authentic -- nobody fabricates a prophecy that visibly fails. The long first-person discourses in John's Gospel where Jesus identifies himself with bread, light, vines, and so on, not authentic -- that material appears only there and reads nothing like the short, punchy sayings in the other gospels. And the detailed prediction of the temple's destruction, not authentic -- she thinks it was written back into his mouth after 70 CE by communities who had watched it happen.

The passage I actually need her verdict on (she keeps ducking my calls, deadline friday): the exchange where a man approaches Jesus calling him good, and Jesus bristles at the word -- why call me good, only God is good, that sort of thing. It survives in Mark alone; the later gospels that reuse Mark visibly soften the wording, which her own lecture notes flag as a tell that the early church found it awkward. So: what does she say about this one? I'm fairly sure more than one rule fits her five verdicts, so lay out the serious candidates, say which rule she's probabaly running and therefore which way she rules here, and give me your reasons. And if the rules genuinely split on this passage, describe a hypothetical passage that would settle which rule is hers if I ever get her on the phone.
