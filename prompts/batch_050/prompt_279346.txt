I'm in Year 13 and writing my philosophy extended essay on Simone de Beauvoir's The Second Sex, in particular her famous claim that one is not born but becomes a woman — her idea that femininity is something socially manufactured and then lived, rather than a natural destiny. I've decided to test her framework against a phenomenon she never lived to see: the "tradwife" trend on social media, in which young women adopt a highly traditional, mid-twentieth-century domestic role — cooking from scratch, homemaking, publicly deferring to their husbands — while presenting it as a freely chosen, even empowering, lifestyle. Some earn substantial incomes as influencers precisely by performing this submission.

Please predict, with arguments grounded in her actual philosophy rather than vague guesses, how Beauvoir would analyse this trend. Specifically:

1. Would she treat the tradwife's choice as genuine agency? These women appear to choose freely, often against the disapproval of their peers and the wider culture — or would she see it as her own concept of "becoming" at work, where the choosing self has already been formed by the very expectations it then embraces? Which reading is truer to her text?

2. Is there anything in her framework that distinguishes the tradwife from a woman who simply and authentically prefers domestic life? Or does her position collapse into the patronising claim that any woman who chooses tradition must be deluded or in bad faith? I want to steelman her here rather than assume she has a tidy answer.

3. What would she make of the economics of it — that these women often have careers, incomes, and public platforms through performing dependence? Does the fact that submission is here a route to financial independence complicate her analysis of women as economically reliant on men, or would she treat the income as incidental?

Finally, please indicate where you think her framework genuinely struggles with this case — the point at which the honest conclusion is that she would have had to revise something, rather than the framework absorbing the example smoothly. I'd rather know the weak points before my supervisor finds them.
