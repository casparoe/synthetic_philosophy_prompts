I am taking a philosophy of probability course this term, and my professor runs a recurring exercise in which she shows us a finite sequence of bits, tells us exactly how it was produced, and gives a quick verdict on whether the sequence itself counts as random. I have been recording her verdicts, and I want to see whether her next one can be predicted. So far: she said that a record of 400 actual fair-coin tosses, coming out roughly half heads with no obvious pattern, is random. She said that a perfectly alternating string, heads-tails-heads-tails for 400 positions, is not random, even though it has exactly as many heads as tails. She said that a string of 400 heads in a row is not random, and when a student objected that fair coins will eventually produce such a string somewhere, she answered that she believed that but still would not call the string itself random. She said that the first 400 binary digits of pi, written out in order, are random. And she said that a record of 400 tosses of a coin weighted to land heads about ninety percent of the time is not random, even though it was produced by a genuinely chancy physical process. Last week she announced the next case: a 400-bit string generated by a standard pseudorandom number generator on a laptop, seeded with the millisecond the program started, so the entire string is fully determined by a short seed, although it passes every statistical test for randomness she has ever shown us, including the ones the fair-coin record passed and the ones the digits of pi passed. She has not yet given her verdict. My questions are these. First, what do you predict she will say, and why? Second, what are the plausible general rules that fit all five of her earlier verdicts, and what does each of them predict about the pseudorandom string? I ask because I can see at least two rules she might be running that agree on everything so far but pull apart on this case, and I would like them drawn out explicitly rather than gestured at. Third, which of her earlier verdicts is the closest analog to the new case, and how much weight should that carry? Fourth, if her record genuinely underdetermines this verdict, what principle would she be committing herself to by ruling each way? And finally, what further case, if any, would best distinguish between the surviving candidate rules? Please answer in continuous prose, as if writing me a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, and no tables.
