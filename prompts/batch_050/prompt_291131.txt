Write a parody owner's manual for the human phenomenal self-model, in the cheery, unhelpfully confident tone of consumer electronics documentation. Around 600 words, with the usual sections: Getting Started, Normal Operation, Troubleshooting, Warranty & Liability. The jokes should come from taking Metzinger seriously, not from generic "are we in a simulation" stuff: the self is the content of a model rather than a part of the machine, so the manual has to keep congratulating you on being the pilot while insisting there is no pilot listed in the parts diagram; transparency means you can't perceive the modeling process itself, so any attempt to inspect the representational medium directly voids the warranty; the rubber hand illusion is a documented compatibility issue with third-party limbs; out-of-body experiences and depersonalization are known edge cases with suggested fixes like "wait for the unit to re-integrate." Stay deadpan, no winking at the reader. And somewhere in there, let the manual's own transparency claim create a real paradox about who, exactly, the manual is addressed to -- without ever flagging it as a joke.
