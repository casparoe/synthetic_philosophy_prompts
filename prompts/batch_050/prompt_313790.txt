i run product design review at a mid-size fintech (budgeting and savings app, ~2M users). once a month the design team brings me new "choice architecture" proposals — defaults, ordering, friction, prompts — and i have about 45 minutes per proposal to decide whether it ships, goes back for changes, or gets killed. right now i decide by vibes and it's starting to show: legal has asked me twice this quarter what our actual standard is and i couldn't give them a straight answer.

so: write me a decision procedure, grounded in what the nudge/paternalism literature actually has to say, that i can run on any proposal inside that 45 minutes. specifically i want:

- the questions to ask in order, cheap ones first, and what counts as a sufficient answer to each
- it has to handle the distinctions that seem to be doing the real work: whether the design changes the choice set itself or just the architecture around it; whether it's steering users toward their own good or toward our revenue; whether it works by counteracting a bias users have or by exploiting one; how easy the steering is to notice, avoid, or reverse; and whether users would endorse it if they knew it was happening
- mark clearly where the procedure stops with a hard no (kill it, no appeal) versus where it hands off to a human judgment call because the question is one of values rather than facts — and be honest about which steps are judgment dressed up as procedure
- where a test can't literally be run in 45 minutes (e.g. i can't survey users before every review), say what proxy to use and how badly the proxy might mislead

then run the finished procedure on three proposals actually sitting in my queue:

1. default new users into automatically sweeping spare change into savings, with a one-tap opt-out in settings
2. closing your account requires calling a phone line staffed weekdays 9-5, while signup takes ninety seconds in the app
3. a pre-checked box at signup adding a $4/month "credit monitoring" add-on — revenue loves this one, which is exactly why i want the procedure's honest output even if i won't like the answer

at the very end, give me one line formatted exactly like this: Verdict: case1 = ship/rework/kill, case2 = ..., case3 = ...

also flag what the procedure can't do — the class of proposal it will systematically get wrong. American English please.
