So I'm a retired condensed matter physicist, spent my career on crystal defects and phase transitions, and in retirement I've been reading metaphysics for fun. I just hit the statue-and-the-clay literature -- the lump that becomes a statue, the statue that survives losing a finger of clay but not being squashed, all that -- and I keep having the nagging feeling that from where I sit, none of the objects in this debate exist the way the philosophers talk about them. There's a region of space with some silicon, oxygen, aluminum atoms in a lattice, there's a wavefunction, there are coarse-grained descriptions at different resolutions. "The statue" and "the clay" both look to me like bookkeeping choices.

Here's what I'd like. Take the main positions in the material constitution debate -- the view that statue and lump are two things sharing all their parts, the four-dimensionalist view where they're different temporal slices or worms, the mereological essentialist view where the lump can't survive any part change, and the eliminativist view that there are just simples arranged statuewise -- and restate each one in the vocabulary I actually think in: many-body wavefunctions, phase space, coarse-graining, spacetime regions, decoherence, order parameters if that helps. Be as faithful as you can.

Then, and this is the part I really care about, give me the residue report. Which claims translate cleanly, which ones you had to fudge, and which ones have no expression at all in physics-speak? In particular: the whole debate seems to turn on these "persistence conditions" or "sortal" properties -- statue, lump, person -- and I cannot for the life of me see what physical quantity corresponds to those. Is that a defect of my vocabulary, or is it telling me the metaphysicians are arguing about something that isn't there? I have a suspicion it's a bit of both and I'd love to be argued out of it.

Also: I noticed the debate leans heavily on Leibniz's law, the indiscernibility of identicals. Translating that into a world where identity of particles is already weird (fermions!) might itself be interesting, if you think it bears on things.
