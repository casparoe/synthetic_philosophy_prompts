So I've been lurking/posting on rationalist blogs for years and lately fell down the On Certainty rabbit hole, and now I'm worried my epistemology is held together with duct tape. Before I write a big post synthesizing everything, can you audit my commitments? Tell me which ones pull against each other, which only look like they do, and what the cheapest fix is for each real tension.

Here's where I've landed:

1. I'm basically a Bayesian. Rational belief comes in degrees, you update on evidence, and nothing except maybe logical truths should get probability 1. Being willing to bet at extreme odds is a bug, not a feature.

2. I'm sold on the hinge picture. Stuff like "there is an external world," "my memory is roughly reliable," "other people have minds" aren't really beliefs in the ordinary sense. They stand fast for us, they're neither justified nor unjustified, and doubt about them isn't a live option because doubt itself has to stand somewhere. The whole river-bed thing.

3. But I also think hinges can be rationally abandoned under pressure. If my experiences got systematically better explained by the brain-in-a-vat hypothesis, refusing to update at some point stops being "standing fast" and starts being plain dogmatism. The door has to stay on its hinges, but hinges can definately be swapped out.

4. Moore's proof of an external world totally fails as a refutation of the skeptic, and yet Moore obviously knew he had hands.

5. Full Cartesian doubt is incoherent — you can't doubt everything at once since doubting needs a stable background — but the skeptic is still right that our hinges are groundless, so in some sense all our knowledge rests on nothing at the bottom.

6. I'm a fallibilist about knowledge: you can know things even though you could turn out to be wrong about them.

Don't just tell me everything is fine, and don't flag pairs that only look incompatible. Order the real tensions by severity, say for each what would have to give, and tell me which single revision cleans up the most of the set. Also feel free to tell me if I'm misreading Wittgenstein anywhere, I'd rather hear it now than in the comments.

Oh, and please answer in American English.
