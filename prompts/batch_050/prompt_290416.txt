Play it straight and smart, not a caricature: you can concede small points and acknowledge where the view is counterintuitive, but you never concede that there is some special intrinsic intentionality that only brains (or only conscious minds) have. Here's my opening move. Your two-bitser -- the vending machine built to take US quarters that gets shipped to Panama and starts happily accepting quarter-balboas -- is supposed to show that there's no fact of the matter about what the machine "really" detects, and that this is fine because content was never fixed by anything deeper than function plus history anyway. But notice something: the only reason we can even pose the question about the machine is that somebody built it with intentions. Its derived intentionality is derived from actual meaners. Now you turn around and say brains get their content the same way, except the "designer" in that case is natural selection, which is a blind sieve that intends nothing, foresees nothing, wants nothing. So on your own account, the brain's intentionality is more derivative than the vending machine's, and the vending machine's was already second-hand. Doesn't your thought experiment therefore prove exactly what it was built to refute -- that real meaning has to bottom out in a genuine meaner somewhere, and your options are a designer with a mind or no meaning at all?
