I'm running a 75-minute seminar on the unity of the proposition for about 14 philosophy undergrads. We've read a bit about Bradley's regress and the kids basically nod along but I don't think they *feel* why the regress is supposed to be a problem -- they just treat "the copula links subject and predicate" as obviously fine.

Design an in-class game or activity that makes them run into the regress themselves before anyone names it. My rough idea is something like: teams get handed "constituent cards" (things like SOCRATES, WISDOM) and have to physically assemble the proposition that Socrates is wise out of them, but with rules forcing them to keep adding connector pieces that themselves need connecting, plus a limited budget so it actually costs them something. You can take that sketch or scrap it -- but whatever you design, I need:

- concrete rules, materials, and how the thing is scored or "won"
- a second phase or rule change that lets them escape the regress somehow (e.g. connectors that relate their relata without needing further connectors) so they have to decide whether the escape is legit or just cheating-by-stipulation
- a debrief plan that maps what happened onto Bradley's argument and onto one or two standard replies (like the idea that some constituents are "unsaturated" and don't need linking), without you lecturing them on it
- what will predictably go wrong the first time I run this and how to handle it
- an honest note on which feature of the actual philosophical problem the activity can't capture and I'll just have to supply in discussion

Keep it runnable with stuff I can get from an index-card pack and a whiteboard.
