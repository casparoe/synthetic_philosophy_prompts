I spent last night in a long argument with a friend and could not locate the flaw in his reasoning, though I refuse to accept where it ends. His argument, as best I can reconstruct it, runs as follows.

(1) In any domain where many actors compete and the less efficient are eliminated, an actor who voluntarily accepts a cost that rivals do not accept will, over time, lose market share, influence, or position to those rivals.

(2) The vacated position is promptly occupied by the unrestrained, so unilateral restraint does not reduce the bad conduct; it merely changes who engages in it, while removing from the field the one actor disposed to behave better. The manufacturer who installs scrubbers while competitors do not goes under; the competitors remain, dirtier than ever.

(3) An action that predictably fails to improve outcomes, and predictably strips the well-disposed of influence, cannot be morally required of anyone.

(4) Therefore no participant in such a domain is obligated to exercise unilateral restraint: not the firm declining to pollute, not the state declining to arm, not the athlete declining to dope.

(5) If no participant is obligated to restrain, none is blameworthy for failing to; responsibility attaches, if anywhere, to the competitive system as a whole.

(6) But a system cannot be shamed, punished, or persuaded, so blame that attaches only to it accomplishes nothing — which is to say, in competitive domains, moral responsibility effectively dissolves.

I would rather surrender (5) or (6) than (1) or (2), since the empirical premises seem to me simply true, but I want to know what each rejection costs. Which step should I reject, and why did it look sound? In particular: is my friend ignoring a distinction between what an actor can be required to do alone and what actors can be required to do together — supporting regulation, enforcement, or coordination that changes the payoffs — and if so, does that distinction rescue blame for individuals, or merely relocate it to those who decline to coordinate?
