so i cover science and ideas for a general magazine and we're putting together a package on prediction -- why forecasters keep hitting walls even in domains where the underlying physics is completely nailed down. i pitched my editor this slightly daft idea of opening the package with a poem and to my shock she said yes, so now i actually have to commision one.

Write a villanelle about deterministic chaos. The two refrains should be the two claims everyone assumes can't both be true: that the system's laws fix every later state exactly, and that its long-run behaviour lies beyond any possible forecast. Let the form do the philosophical work. The refrains keep returning like the equations themselves, the same rule iterated over and over, but each recurrence should land somewhere new, the way two nearly identical starting points peel apart until nothing of one is visible in the other. Then in the final quatrain the two refrains finally stand side by side, and that's where the reader should see the point of the whole piece: the second claim doesn't deny the first, it follows from it, because sensitive dependence on initial conditions means no measurement is ever fine enough, not that anything goes. Keep it accurate enough that someone who knows what a Lyapunov exponent is wouldn't wince -- no implying that chaos is just randomness wearing a disguise, no quantum indeterminacy sneaking in through the back door, it's a classical story. And write it in British English please, the subs will only change it otherwise.
