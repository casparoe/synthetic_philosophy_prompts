Write me a deadpan satirical piece, roughly 450-600 words, in the form of a formal "Notice of Original Acquisition" that a man has laminated and nailed to a tree in a public park, announcing that the park is now his private property on labour-mixing grounds. He has, he explains, spent the afternoon raking a single path through it, and has also poured a can of his own tomato juice into the duck pond, thereby mixing something that was unambiguously his with something that was nobody's. The notice should pre-empt the obvious objections in numbered clauses: what distinguishes labour from a pleasant walk; whether "enough and as good" is left for others (his argument leans heavily on the existence of other parks); how charging admission satisfies the spoilage condition; and a tentative schedule of future acquisitions including the river, pending procurement of more juice. The humour has to come from Locke's actual theory of acquisition pushed straight-faced to conclusions the reader recoils from -- someone who knows the Second Treatise should feel the satire is fair, not a strawman -- and the guy must never once wink.
