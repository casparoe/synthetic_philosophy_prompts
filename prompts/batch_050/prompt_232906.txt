I've been reading rationalist blogs and forums for well over a decade, and one move keeps showing up in our local AI-safety arguments: appealing to Hofstadter's MU-puzzle as a parable about intelligence and constraints. I want one particular use of it audited.

The source material, as my friend tells it. The MIU-system has one axiom, the string MI, and four production rules: (1) if a string ends in I, you may append a U; (2) from any string Mx you may derive Mxx, doubling whatever follows the M; (3) wherever III occurs you may replace it with a single U; (4) wherever UU occurs you may delete it. The puzzle is whether MU is derivable. Grinding forward inside the system gets you nowhere visible; the resolution comes from stepping outside and noticing an invariant: the number of I's in any derivable string is never divisible by three (start with one; doubling preserves non-divisibility by three; the other rules change the count only by multiples of three), while MU has zero. The moral my friend draws, deploying the move people in my circles call jumping out of the system, or jootsing: intelligence just is this capacity to exit a formal system and reason about it from the meta-level, so no constraint its designers specify can hold a genuinely intelligent system. A boxed or rule-governed AI, he says, stands to its cage as the mathematician stands to the MIU-system: the cage is an object of study to it, not a boundary. No checklist of anticipated failure modes, compiled from inside the designers' system, will enumerate the route a smarter mind finds from outside it. He concludes that constraint-based safety is futile in principle, and that the only viable path is a system that wants the right things from the inside.

Audit the analogy, in American English, along these lines:

1. The correspondence table: what plays the role of the axiom, the production rules, the string space, the invariant, and the mathematician, when the target is a trained system subject to architectural or environmental constraints? Flag which features of the MU story have genuine analogs and which are borrowed scenery.
2. The load-bearing disanalogies. In particular: does the puzzle show that insight outruns mechanism, or something closer to the opposite -- that a structural constraint is absolute no matter how much inside-the-system work is done? Does the meta-level vantage grant new moves inside the system, or only knowledge about the system? Is the mathematician's outside view itself unmechanical, or could a theorem prover with arithmetic check I-counts too? And is a wall enforced outside a system's action space even represented anywhere in the MIU picture?
3. Which disanalogies don't matter for his conclusion, so I can stop conceding them in the thread.
4. Whether his conclusion -- constraints can't hold, only motivation can -- survives the repaired mapping; what conclusion about constrained intelligence the MU story actually supports, if any; and a better analogy if one exists.

End with a final line of the form \answer{X} where X is exactly one of: conclusion survives, conclusion survives in weakened form, conclusion fails -- judging his inference from the MU-puzzle to the futility of constraint-based safety, not the separate question of whether motivation-based alignment is wise.
