I'm in a moral psychology reading group and last week we got into the classic argument from moral diversity: cultures disagree wildly about what's wrong (trolley intuitions aren't universal, some societies moralize food and sex and purity stuff that WEIRD undergrads file under etiquette or personal choice, revenge killing gets treated as obligatory in honor cultures), so moral realism is supposedly in trouble. Someone pushed back with an analogy I've heard a bunch of times and want to actually stress-test: morality is like language. Every human grows up speaking some language, the surface variation is huge, but there's a deep universal grammar underneath, so French vs. Japanese doesn't disprove universal grammar and moral diversity doesn't disprove an objective moral core. It sounded slick in the moment but the more I think about it the more I suspect it's doing the debunker's work for them, since we don't think French speakers are *wrong* the way moral disputants think each other are wrong.

Audit the analogy for me. Give me the correspondence table first — what plays the role of the native speaker's intuition, the universal grammar, the dialect, the prescriptive schoolteacher who insists on rules nobody follows — and flag which correspondences are load-bearing vs. decorative. Then the disanalogies: where the normativity of morality (claims binding people who don't share your code, the possibility that a whole culture gets it wrong) has no linguistic counterpart, where the empirical premise on the language side is itself shakier than the analogy assumes, and whether moral dumbfounding (people insisting something's wrong while unable to say why) is really the same phenomenon as knowing a sentence is ungrammatical without being able to state the rule. Tell me what the analogy quietly smuggles in about nativism and about the shallowness of variation, separate the breaks that matter for the anti-debunking use from the ones that are just incidental, and if there's a better analogy for the same dialectic (mathematics? taste? digestion?) say which and why.
