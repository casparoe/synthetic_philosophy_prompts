A few months ago my father died, and at some point in the fog of that spring a well-meaning colleague who knows some physics tried to console me with a version of the Rietdijk–Putnam argument: since what counts as "happening now" depends on one's state of motion, and a distant observer moving appropriately would reckon my father still alive in her present, he must in some sense still exist — the future and past are all equally real, laid out in a four-dimensional block, and death is less like annihilation than like a place one no longer visits. I found the thought genuinely moving and also, on reflection, suspicious. The argument seems to move from "event E is determinate relative to some inertial frame's simultaneity hyperplane" to "E is real, full stop," and I cannot see why the physics licenses that step — relativity of simultaneity tells us that frame-relative assignments of coexistence disagree, not that every such assignment must be admitted into an observer-independent catalogue of what exists. But I am a lawyer by training, not a physicist, and I want the objection pressed as hard as it can be pressed rather than left as a hunch.

Please make the strongest rigorous case that the Rietdijk–Putnam argument equivocates on "real" or "determinate": that special relativity on its own delivers only a many-to-one relation between observers and their presents, that reaching the block universe requires an extra metaphysical premise — roughly, that whatever is real-relative-to-some-observer is real simpliciter — and that a presentist or growing-block theorist can deny that premise without contradicting anything empirically testable in relativity. Then do the honest second half: take seriously the obvious rejoinder that denying the premise makes reality itself frame-relative, which may be a stranger commitment than eternalism; assess whether the growing-block view in particular survives once simultaneity is frame-relative (whose edge is "the" edge of becoming?); and conclude by stating precisely what the argument does establish if it falls short of the full block universe — whether it is best read as ruling out a particular naive presentism while leaving a relativistically respectable presentism or perspectivalism untouched. I am not asking to be comforted; I am asking whether the consolation was built on an equivocation.
