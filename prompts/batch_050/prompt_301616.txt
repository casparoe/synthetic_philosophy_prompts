My half-formed idea: the discussions I've read tend to either declare the expectation undefined and stop, or adopt one fix (cap the number of flips, discount later rounds, or simply accept ln 2 as the value) without arguing for it against its rivals. I'd evaluate each remedy by three criteria: whether it is invariant under reordering how the outcomes happen to be listed, whether it agrees with simple dominance reasoning when one option is strictly better, and whether it behaves continuously as a cap on the game is relaxed. The question, stated precisely: does any remedy that assigns a definite value to the Pasadena game satisfy all three criteria, and if not, which criterion should give way, and what does the answer imply about whether expected value is the right currency for rational choice when payoffs are unbounded in both directions?
