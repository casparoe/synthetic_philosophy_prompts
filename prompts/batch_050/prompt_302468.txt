I'm a novelist working on a book about a circle of art critics who ruin a friendship arguing over whether a bad painting is bad. I want each of them to actually *hold* a distinct position on whether beauty is objective, rather than just quoting labels at each other. Here's a passage from Hume's essay Of the Standard of Taste that I keep circling around:

"There are certain terms in every language, which import blame, and others praise; and all men, who use the same tongue, must agree in their application of them... But when critics come to particulars, this seeming unanimity vanishes; and it is found, that they had affixed a very different meaning to their expressions."

Take that observation -- we agree on the words, we disagree on the cases -- and build me a decision tree for sorting positions on the objectivity of beauty. The branch nodes should be questions anyone could answer without knowing any philosophy (stuff like: if two people disagree about a painting and both are attentive and unhurried, must one of them be missing something? does "this is beautiful" state a fact or voice a response? could a whole culture just be *wrong* about what it admires?). The leaves should land on the main positions: eye-of-the-beholder subjectivism, something like Hume's ideal-critic standard, relativism about communities, full-on aesthetic realism, and the view that taste talk is more like cheering than describing. If two leaves can't actually be told apart by the questions, say so rather than fudging it, and if some combination of answers has no occupant, note it and guess why.

Format it like this: "What the passage claims" (~100 words, your reading of Hume there), "The tree" (the bulk, as long as it needs), "The leaves" (one or two lines each: the position, and the awkwardest thing it commits a charachter to), "Unsettled bits" (~100 words). In that last section, please be honest about what's basically agreed among people who work on this and what's genuinely contested -- e.g. don't present one particular reading of whether Hume himself ended up an objectivist as if it's the consensus, because I gather it isn't.
