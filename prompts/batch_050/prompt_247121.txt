So I sit on our city's public arts grant committee (I'm a novelist, the token "writer person" they put on it, and I mostly just drink the coffee and try to look thoughtful). We've got a decision due next month on a mural proposal that's gotten weirdly heated, and the chair asked each of us to circulate a short written opinion before the vote. Here's the case: a local painter, Dana, spent about two years in Oaxaca and learned a bunch of traditional Zapotec weaving motifs from a weaving cooperative there. The cooperative taught her willingly, gave her their blessing to use the designs, and her proposal includes sending them 5% of the grant money plus crediting them on a plaque. Her mural would cover the side of a grocery store with big geometric patterns adapted from those motifs. The objection came from a couple of folks at our public comment session, including one Zapotec woman now living here, who said that no individual weaver or co-op can "give away" something that belongs to the whole community, and that a white artist profiting off those designs in a city where actual indigenous artists struggle to get wall space is appropriation no matter how polite the licensing deal is.

Draft me the written opinion I'll send around, max 600 words. It needs an actual bottom line up front -- fund it, fund it with conditions, or don't fund it -- not a both-sides shrug, because we're voting. But I also don't want it to be a lazy one: the opinion has to take seriously the strongest version of the side it rules against. In particular I keep going back and forth on whether individual consent settles appropriation claims or whether there's some kind of group-level claim that no single member can waive, and if the latter, what that even means practically -- who speaks for the group, and does it follow that the co-op's own say-so counts for nothing? The committee's guidelines just say we shouldn't fund work that "exploits marginalized cultural traditions," which is doing no work at all here since both sides claim it.

Two small requests: at the end, add a line saying how confident you are in the recommendation overall, and single out the specific claim or move in the memo you're least sure about, so I know where the soft spot is if someone pushes back at the meeting.
