Ok so I'm helping a friend set up a mock constitution exercise for her civics students, and we need to pick a voting mechanism for citizen-initiated referendums in a fictional mid-sized country (think 8 million people, decent education levels, one dominant media company). Here's the shortlist we've come up with:

1. Classic single majority vote, like California propositions: simple 50%+1 wins, anyone can collect signatures to get something on the ballot.
2. Double majority: you need a majority of voters AND a majority of the country's 12 regions, to stop big cities steamrolling rural areas.
3. A mechanism I made up, which I'm calling "echo voting": the referendum gets voted on twice, 18 months apart, and only passes if it wins both times. Idea being that temporary outrage or a slick ad blitz can't push something through, since the second vote happens when the campaign money has dried up.
4. Supermajority requirement: needs 60% to pass.
5. Another one I made up, "quorum by knowledge": before voting, each voter answers 3 basic factual questions about the proposal (auto-generated from the official neutral summary), and only ballots from people who get at least 2 right are counted.

Rank these 5 mechanisms from best to worst specifically on ONE dimension: how well the final outcome reflects what the population would want after genuinely thinking it through, as opposed to what well-funded campaigns can manufacture in the 3 months before a vote. Give me the ranking plus maybe 2-3 sentences per mechanism explaining the placement, and then tell me which single weakness of your #1 pick you'd most worry about in practice. Also if you think the best answer is some hybrid (e.g. combining two of them), say so and slot it into the ranking as a 6th option.
