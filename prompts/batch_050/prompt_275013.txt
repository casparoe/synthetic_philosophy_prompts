I am a senior and the student representative on my school's five-member Honor Council. We are deciding a plagiarism case, and the faculty chair has asked each of us to circulate a short written opinion before Thursday's vote. Here are the facts. A student submitted an essay that was substantially copied from an online source. He admits he chose to copy it and acted entirely on his own. However, it also came out in the hearing that his older brother, who had access to his school account, had privately resolved that if the student showed any sign of writing the essay honestly, he would secretly replace the submission with the same plagiarized version. The brother never intervened, because he never had to. So the student could not have avoided submitting a plagiarized essay, even though his being unable to do otherwise played no role in what he actually did. Two members argue the standard suspension is obviously fair because the dishonesty was his own doing; two others argue our Honor Code conditions sanctions on the student's having been able to do otherwise, and here he could not have. One member suggested a middle position: the student briefly hesitated before copying, and that flicker of a chance to do otherwise is enough for responsibility. Please draft my opinion (about one page): a clear recommendation on the sanction and, if needed, on whether the Code's able-to-do-otherwise clause should be revised; the reasoning the Council could act on; and a fair statement of the strongest point on the side I reject. Don't include citations; our chair says the Code and the hearing record are all we may rely on.
