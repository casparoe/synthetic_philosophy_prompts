ok so my roommate is a philosophy phd student who's been obsessed all year with the internal vs external reasons fight — whether someone can have a reason to do something when nothing they currently want or care about connects to it. she's been giving me her verdicts on cases whenever i bug her, and i want to see if i can predict her next one before i ask. her calls so far:

- Marcus orders a gin and tonic at a bar and the glass actually has petrol in it, but it looks and smells exactly like gin and he has no way of knowing. she says he has no reason to drink what's in the glass, even though he wants gin and fully believes the glass has gin in it.
- Samira is playing chess, wants to win, and hasn't noticed that one particular move is the only move that doesn't lose — a few minutes of ordinary calculation from where she sits would show it. she says Samira already has a reason to play that move, even before she sees it.
- her cousin, furious after a fight, wants nothing in the world except to send a brutal email to his business partner tonight. everything else he cares about counts against it, he'd be horrified at himself in the morning, and even tonight he'd endorse cooling off if he actually thought it through. she says he has no reason to send it, even though the desire is real and intense.
- her uncle hates exercise, zero motivation toward it, but he cares alot about living to see his grandkids grow up, and if he sat down and thought honestly about his own goals he'd come around to wanting the morning walks his doctor keeps pushing. she says he has a reason to take the walks.
- the one that surprised me: a guy we both know who genuinely does not care about his future self — 22, no impulse to save, no health anxiety, you walk him through it and he shrugs. then someone got him to vividly imagine being 60, broke and sick, and he went quiet and opened a savings account. she says he had the reason to save all along, even before the imagination exercise worked on him.

next case i want to spring on her: a woman raised in a small closed religious community. she wants exactly the life she has — the domestic role, the routine, all of it — and her desires are mostly satisfied. she's not deluded about the facts, it's just that nothing she currently cares about gives her any deliberative path to wanting out. outsiders describe other lives to her and she finds them frightening and meaningless, and no amount of cool reflection on her own motives would get her to want to leave. people outside are pretty confident she'd flourish if she did.

would my roommate say this woman has a reason to leave? i'm less interested in your own answer than in: what rule best fits the five calls, what other rules fit them just as well, what each of those rules says about the new case, and then your actual best guess at her verdict and why. also give me the sixth case i'd have to run past her to tell your favored reading apart from the strongest rival.

and if you think the whole exercise is off — like five verdicts just can't pin down a rule here no matter what, or one of the cases as i've set it up smuggles in the answer — say that plainly instead of playing along.
