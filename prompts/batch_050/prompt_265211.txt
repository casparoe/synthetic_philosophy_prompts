I'm working on a novel where the main character keeps blowing off her deadlines to go out with freinds, and I'm trying to get the psychology right. I read up on hyperbolic discounting since it supposedly explains akrasia — people over-discount the future, so temptation wins. So in the story I want to establish that she just has a really steep discount rate, and that's why she fails even though she genuinely wants to finish her book.

But my beta reader pushed back and said that doesn't actually explain anything on its own — she said the interesting part only kicks in when the *timing* of the choice shifts, that the same person with the same discount rate will prefer the fun option when it's tonight but the book option when both are pushed a month out. I don't get that at all. If her discount rate is fixed, her preferences should be fixed too, right? A rate is a rate. Since a steep rate already explains why she caves, why would anyone need this extra timing business — is my beta reader just overcomplicating it?
