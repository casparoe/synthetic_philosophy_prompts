I'm writing a paper on original acquisition — how unowned stuff legitimately becomes owned in the first place, before any transfers or contracts enter the picture — and before I wade any deeper into Nozick and the left-libertarian litterature I want the spec, not more theories. What does a theory of original acquisition have to DO to be adequate? Write me the desiderata list.

Here's the passage that made me want this. Locke, Second Treatise of Government, ch. V, sec. 28:

"He that is nourished by the acorns he picked up under an oak, or the apples he gathered from the trees in the wood, has certainly appropriated them to himself. No body can deny but the nourishment is his. I ask then, when did they begin to be his? when he digested? or when he eat? or when he boiled? or when he brought them home? or when he picked them up? and it is plain, if the first gathering made them not his, nothing else could."

Everyone obsesses over the labour-mixing bit a paragraph up, but I think this is the load-bearing passage: Locke's own answer to his string of rhetorical questions is apparently "when he picked them up" — first gathering is what does it. So take the passage seriously as staking out a position on the moment and the act of acquisition, and build the desiderata around what any theory has to settle here. Things I want on the list, though correct my groupings if they're off:

- the cases it has to get right, or at least say something non-embarrassing about: acorns-and-apples cases, uncultivated land, and then the modern commons Locke never dreamt of — radio spectrum, geostationary orbital slots, deep-sea polymetallic nodules. Does "first gathering" even have an analogue for spectrum, which you can't pick up, only use? If a theory just goes quiet there, is that a defect or appropriate humility?
- the distinctions it can't collapse: possession vs ownership, use vs title, labour vs mere first arrival, and whatever the proviso (enough and as good left for others) is actually doing — is it a constraint on the act of appropriation or a standing defeasibility condition on the title once acquired?
- which desiderata are genuine requirements and which are prejudices of one camp (is determinacy of the acquired object — exactly how much you get by mixing labour — a real desideratum or just a Nozickian gotcha?)
- the pair of desiderata you most suspect can't be jointly satisfied. My guess is "first gathering suffices" and any proviso with teeth collide once scarcity kicks in, but maybe I'm wrong
- which single requirement disqualifies the most going theories

Then briefly — a paragraph each max, the criteria are the point, not the scorecard — how Locke himself, Nozick's version, and a common-ownership left-libertarian view do against the list. And tell me whether the "first gathering" answer is even consistent with the labour-mixing story Locke tells elsewhere in the chapter, or whether he's running two different theories at once. 800-1200 words excluding the quote, casual is fine.
