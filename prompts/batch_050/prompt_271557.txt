In my undergraduate historiography seminar we just finished a unit on Leopold von Ranke -- his insistence on working from archival primary sources, his techniques of source criticism, his ambition to reconstruct the past as it essentially was rather than to moralize about it, and his ideal of the historian's own personality receding behind the evidence. Afterwards a classmate and I got into an argument about what Ranke would make of Wikipedia, which is now where most people on earth actually encounter history. My classmate insisted Ranke would be horrified: anonymous amateurs, no archival training, articles citing newspaper summaries rather than manuscripts, endless revision. I suspect the picture is more complicated -- the requirement that every claim carry a citation, the public revision history that lets anyone inspect how an account changed, and the deliberate suppression of the author's personal voice all strike me as oddly congenial to his temperament, even if the actual sourcing practices fall short of his standards.

So: taking Ranke's historical method and his underlying convictions about what history is for as your basis, tell me what his considered verdict on Wikipedia would likely have been -- whether he would see it as a promising instrument for his kind of project, a corruption of it, or something in between, and how he would weigh its specific features (anonymous collective authorship, reliance on secondary sources, verifiability rules, perpetual revision). I'd also like to know where you think he would surprise us, i.e., where his reaction would cut against the obvious stereotype of a nineteenth-century archival purist.

Please don't hedge by laying out the case for and against and leaving me to choose: commit to a definite characterization of his position, give your reasons, and flag the one place where you think the evidence of his writings and practice most plausibly supports the opposite reading. Present your answer as exactly five numbered points, each one a sentence or two long, with no introductory or concluding paragraph.
