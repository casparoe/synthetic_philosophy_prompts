Trying to get the access vs phenomenal consciousness distinction straight and I keep running into the same wall with global workspace theory. As I understand it, Block says a state is access conscious when its content is poised for use in reasoning and rational control of action and speach, and GWT says a state becomes conscious when its content gets broadcast, made globally available to the rest of the system. But here's my problem: when my phone pushes a notification, that content suddenly becomes available to me for reasoning, reporting, deciding what to do — it gets broadcast to the system (me). So on the GWT definition, does the notification become conscious? And if the answer is no because the phone isn't the one using the info, then take a company instead: the CEO's announcement gets broadcast to every department, and each department integrates it into its own reasoning and planning. That looks exactly like a global workspace with a bunch of specialized consumer modules. So either GWT entails that companies are conscious of their internal announcements, or "global" secretly means "global within one brain" — in which case the theory just assumes brains are special instead of explaining why they are. What am I missing?

And a related thing: people keep citing GWT in arguments about whether LLMs are conscious. But if Block is right that access and phenomenal consciousness can come apart, doesn't GWT at most tell us about the access kind? Why does everyone treat it as settling the phenomenal question?
