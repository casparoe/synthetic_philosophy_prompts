I'm helping a small research center decide what to fund on virtue ethics after the situationist critique. They can support about six people for four years, split between philosophers and empirically minded researchers, but they do not want yet another broad review of whether character traits exist.

What are the 3 or 4 open questions that should organize the next stage of this debate, and in what order? I'm especially interested in questions like these, though you shouldn't feel bound by my list:

- what actually counts as “the same situation” when we test for trait stability, and whether the individuation problem is empirical, conceptual, or normative;
- whether virtue should be understood as a relatively unified disposition, a loose cluster of situation-sensitive capacities, or something more like a skill for recognizing what matters;
- how much moral responsibility survives if conduct is highly sensitive to mood, roles, peer behavior, framing, and other features agents barely notice;
- whether the practical upshot is mainly redesigning environments, training attention and affect, cultivating smaller “local virtues,” or some mix;
- whether organizations can possess or undermine virtues in a way that isn't just the sum of what their members do.

Please defend the prioritization rather than just ranking what sounds interesting. Use whatever criteria you think fit -- upstream conceptual importance, empirical tractability, stakes for education or institutional design, neglectedness, or how many other debates turn on it -- but make the tradeoffs explicit. For each priority, identify the crux: what result or argument over the next few years would make it more important, and what would make it drop off the list. Also flag one fashionable issue you think is overrated or badly posed.

If my framing smuggles in a confused contrast -- say, treating situationism and character as more opposed than they really are -- push back plainly instead of politely working within it. I'd rather have the agenda rebuilt than a tidy answer to a muddled question.
