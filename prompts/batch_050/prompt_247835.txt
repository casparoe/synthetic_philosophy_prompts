Exam question: Explain Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action. What position was he reacting against, and how does the doctrine connect to his broader account of moral knowledge? (10 points)

Student's answer: Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action says that genuine moral knowledge and moral action cannot be seperated. He was reacting against the Cheng-Zhu orthodoxy of his time, especially Zhu Xi's view that one must first investigate things and grasp principle before one is able to act correctly. Wang thought this encouraged people to postpone moral action indefinitely, always studying and never doing. His key claim is that someone who knows filial piety but does not act filially does not really know filial piety: real knowledge already contains the impulse to act, so knowledge is the beginning of action and action is the completion of knowledge. In this sense Wang's position is close to the pragmatist idea that knowledge is tested or confirmed through practice: only by acting do we find out whether we truly know something. The doctrine itself was not original to Wang, since the phrase unity of knowledge and action was first coined by Cheng Yi, but Wang gave it its most systematic defense. The doctrine also connects to his theory of innate knowledge (liangzhi): since the mind already possesses moral knowledge, there is no need to seek principle in external things, and to know the good is already to be moved toward it. This is why Wang reinterpreted the investigation of things as the rectification of the mind rather than the study of external objects.
