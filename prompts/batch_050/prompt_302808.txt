So I'm a retired zoologist — spent my career on comparative neuroanatomy, mostly mammalian brains, allometry, neuron counts, that sort of thing. Since retiring I've gotten into philosophy, and I keep running into the debate about whether brain size (or complexity, or neuron number, take your pick) could matter morally — i.e. whether it could ever be permisible to count the interests of a large-brained animal for more than the same interest in a small-brained one, other things equal.

What I'd like is this: take the main positions in the ethics-of-moral-status debate and restate them, one by one, in the vocabulary I actually think in. For me that means things like encephalization quotient, absolute versus relative brain size, neuron counts and the fact that neuron density scales diferently across clades (a crow's brain is not built like a rat's), cortical versus subcortical processing, allometric constraints, and the distinction between capacity and throughput. Assume I'm fully fluent in all that and only about a year into philosophy.

The positions I have in mind: the sentience-based view that anything that can suffer counts, and counts equally qua suffering; the view that the richness or weight of experience might scale with neural complexity, so that a whale's pain could literally be more morally weighty than a mouse's; the argument from marginal cases; and the contract-flavored views where moral status comes from being able to participate in the practice rather than from any fact about neurons. If I'm missing a standard position, add it.

Then the part I actually care about: the residue report. For each position, tell me which claims translate cleanly into the neuroanatomical vocabulary, which only translate if you fudge something, and which have no expression there at all. And give me a verdict on the residue — does it show something my comparative-anatomy frame simply can't say (fine, it's a tool with a job), or does it expose places where the ethicists are leaning on notions like complexity or sophistication that go mushy the moment you ask what they'd correspond to in an actual brain? My working suspicion is that much of the debate runs on an implicit scalar quantity that doesn't survive contact with comparative anatomy, since whichever measure you pick — EQ, total neurons, cortical neurons — scrambles the species ordering differently. Tell me whether that suspicion is the insight I think it is, or whether it misses what the moral claims were ever about.
