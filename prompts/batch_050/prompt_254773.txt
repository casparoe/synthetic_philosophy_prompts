A colleague of mine — I will call her Professor V. — has for years maintained a hard line against downward causation: in her view, the causal work in any physical system is done at the level of its smallest parts, and higher-level descriptions are at best economical summaries of microphysical goings-on. I have kept a record of her verdicts on cases where the question was whether some higher-level state of a system is genuinely doing causal work or merely redescribing what the substrate was going to do anyway. Four representative verdicts:

First, the thermostat that heats the room "because it thinks the room is chilly": not a genuine cause. The belief is ascribed from outside; nothing within the system reads or acts upon it; the very same behavior is compatible with indefinitely many rival belief-ascriptions, so the intentional gloss constrains nothing.

Second, the crowd that smashed the barricades "because it panicked": not a genuine cause. Crowd-level predicates summarize individual psychologies and license no portable counterfactuals; knowing that this crowd panicked tells one nothing reliable about what any crowd will do next.

Third, the glider in Conway's Game of Life that "destroyed the block": not a genuine cause. Pattern talk here is a viewing aid backed by a finite, precomputed catalog of interactions, not an autonomous dynamics; step outside the catalog and one is back to simulating individual cells.

Fourth, the trained neural network that classified the image "because it represents dogs": not a genuine cause. The alleged conceptual level cannot be specified precisely enough to generate predictions without consulting the weights; it is an interpretation laid over the computation rather than a level at which the computation can be redescribed and then left behind.

Now consider a final case, closely modeled on Douglas Hofstadter's careenium. A sealed tray holds vast numbers of tiny marbles, the simms, whose collisions are exhaustively fixed by mechanics, together with a handful of large balls, the simmballs, which are nudged about solely by the accumulated impacts of the simms. Nothing moves a simmball except simm impacts; the simm level is causally complete. Yet the system is so contrived that the simmballs' configurations spell symbols: an observer positioned far above, ignorant of the simms, watches the simmballs rearrange themselves into meaningful strings — upon a certain stimulus, they reliably come to spell HELP. Moreover, the symbol level is closed: given only the present symbol-configuration and the symbol-level dynamics, the observer predicts future configurations as reliably as anyone simulating every simm, and never needs to look down.

Please predict Professor V.'s verdict on this case: does the symbol level genuinely push the substrate around, or is this the old shorthand in a fancier vessel? In your answer, set out at least two general rules that are each consistent with all four earlier verdicts yet deliver opposite verdicts here; state what each predicts; give your best guess as to which rule she is actually running, with reasons for preferring it; and specify the single further case whose verdict would most decisively settle which rule it is.
