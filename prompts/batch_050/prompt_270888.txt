I write a magazine column on science and ideas, and I'm drafting a piece about how historians decide where one era ends and the next begins. At a conference last month I sat in on an exchange between two historians that I keep going back and forth on, and I'd like an adjudication.

Daniela argued that the Renaissance should be retired as a serious historical category. Nobody living through it experienced a rebirth, she said; the picture of a dark interval followed by a sudden recovery of light was crafted by the very people it flattered -- Italian humanists who needed the preceding centuries to look barren -- and then canonized in the nineteenth century by Burckhardt. A label whose genealogy is propaganda, she concluded, cannot also be a discovery.

Tomas replied that a period label doesn't require the endorsement of the people inside it. Nobody in 1964 walked around aware of living in the Sixties as we now use the term, yet it tracks a real clustering of changes. Something genuinely did shift in Italy between roughly 1300 and 1600 -- in patronage, in the recovery of classical texts, in visual representation -- and a name marking that cluster is a working tool, not a truth claim.

Daniela answered that the cluster is manufactured by selection: take Florence, a few wealthy patrons, and two dozen artists, and you can conjure an age out of any century. The supposed unity dissolves once you include peasants, women, or anywhere north of the Alps, and the same objection, she noted, has been raised against the Scientific Revolution -- another label imposed afterward that flattens two centuries of uneven, halting change into a single triumphant event.

Tomas's closing move was that even the critics cannot do without the word: they keep having to argue against the Renaissance, which shows it picks out something real enough to be worth denying. Daniela responded that this proves only institutional inertia -- we also keep having to argue against phlogiston.

Who is right about which specific points? Is there something both of them are missing -- for instance, about what a period label is even supposed to do? And who, on balance, has the better of the exchange?
