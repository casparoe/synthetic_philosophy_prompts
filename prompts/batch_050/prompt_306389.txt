My wife and I are finally doing IVF after three years of trying, and we just got back the results of the genetic screening on our embryos. We have six that made it to the freezer stage. Four of them came back clean on everything they test for. One carries a variant for a late-onset neurological condition (symptoms usually in your 50s, sometimes never at all). And one has a marker the clinic says is associated with a higher-than-average risk of severe depression, though the counselor was quick to say it's probabilistic, not destiny. We can only implant one at a time, obviously, and the rest stay frozen.

Here's what's messing with my head. My instinct was: just pick whichever of the four healthy ones graded highest and don't overthink it. But then I thought — if we'd gotten only the depression-risk embryo and nothing else, of course we'd implant it, and we'd love that kid completely, and it would be bizarre to say we'd done something wrong by having him or her. But if that's true, then why does it feel fine to implant that embryo when it's the only option, but somehow feel wrong to implant it when we have alternatives? It's the same embryo, same child, same life either way. The only thing that changes is what else was on the table. And I can't tell if the "feel wrong" part is tracking something real or is just squeamishness.

It gets worse the more I think about it. The clinic also offers, for a price, some of the newer polygenic screening stuff — height, and even some very shaky things that supposedly correlate with educational attainment. A friend of mine heard about that and said it was eugenics with better marketing. But the same clinic screens for Tay-Sachs and nobody calls that eugenics. So where's the line? If I have some obligation to pick the embryo most likely to have the best life, does that obligation stop at disease, or does it extend to anything the tests can see? And if there's no obligation at all and we're allowed to pick by gut feeling, then why does deliberately declining the Tay-Sachs screening seem like it would be an actual moral failing rather than just a preference? I'd really like help thinking through whether there's a principled answer here, or whether I'm just going to have to pick a place to stop and live with the arbitrariness.
