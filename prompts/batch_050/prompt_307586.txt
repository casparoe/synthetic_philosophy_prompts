I run a small philosophy of physics reading group (we're mostly grad students from different departments, plus one very stubborn postdoc in GR) and our discussion of presentism vs eternalism has been stuck for about two semesters now. Roughly: the presentists in the group lean hard on the phenomenology side -- the felt difference between past, present, future, the sense that time *passes*, that the future is open in a way the past isn't -- and they say any metaphysics of time that can't accommodate this is explaining away the datum rather than explaining it. The eternalists keep answering with physics: relativity of simultaneity, no privileged foliation of spacetime, the idea that "the present" can't do any work in our best theories. Whenever they meet, they basically talk past each other. The presentist says "you've described a 4D block but you haven't told me why anything *happens* in it," and the eternalist says "you've described a feeling but you haven't told me what in the world it's a feeling *of*," and then we go in circles.

Here's the wrinkle that made me want outside input: our GR postdoc claims the whole debate is secretly empirical and will be settled by whatever theory of quantum gravity we end up with -- if spacetime is emergent from something more fundamental, then whichever of presentism/eternalism fits the fundamental level wins, and the phenomenology is just folk data to be debunked. Meanwhile one of the presentists has started claiming the debate is actually *verbal* -- that both sides agree on all the physics and all the experiences, and only disagree about which collection of events gets the honorific "real," like two people arguing over whether a heap is "really" a heap. Interestingly, both of them offer these as reasons to *stop* caring about the debate, but the rest of us feel like something real is at stake and can't say what.

So: what kind of disagreement is this, actually? Is there a substantive question under the physics-vs-phenomenology shouting match, or are the sides answering different questions (one about what exists simpliciter, one about what explains temporal experience) under a single label? Is the postdoc right that it's hostage to future physics, or would a full theory of quantum gravity still leave the disagreement untouched? And is the verbalist right, or can you point to something the two sides would have to *predict differently*, in experience or in theory? Give me a diagnosis, not a survey -- and given your diagnosis, what would actually count as progress here, if anything?
