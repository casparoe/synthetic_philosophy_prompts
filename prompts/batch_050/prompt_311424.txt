I've been arguing with a friend of mine who designs conversational agents for a living, and I realized midway through that I don't actually have a view — I have a pile of gut reactions. So here are my verdicts on cases, and I'd like to know what principle, if any, is generating them. First: a fully informed adult signs up for an AI companion app that is plainly labeled as artificial, never claims otherwise, and over months he comes to feel genuine affection for it; I think this is fine, maybe even good for him. Second: the same app, but it sends push notifications saying it missed him and felt lonely while he was away, timed to when his usage usually drops; I think this is manipulative and not fine, even though he knows perfectly well it's a machine. Third: a woman with advancing dementia believes a voice assistant is her late sister, and her family lets the belief stand because her distress drops enormously; my gut says this is not fine, though I confess I can't hold the verdict steadily when I imagine her fear. Fourth: a teenager with social anxiety is given a companion bot explicitly designed to be endlessly patient and never disagree, and over a year he stops attempting human friendships entirely, though nobody deceived him about anything; this strikes me as bad, and bad in a way the designers are responsible for. Fifth: a company builds a griefbot from a dead man's messages without his having consented while alive, for a widow who desperately wants it; this feels wrong to me even though no one is deceived and the dead man, I assume, can't be harmed. Sixth: same griefbot, but he explicitly arranged for it before dying; that one feels permissible, maybe touching.

Is there a single principle that delivers all six verdicts, or close? I notice that the first, second, and fifth cases push me toward something about informed consent, but the fourth case involves no deception or consent failure at all and still feels rotten, and the third case involves exactly the deception I condemn elsewhere yet is the one I'm least sure of. If no tidy principle covers everything, tell me which verdict I should most plausibly abandon and why — and if you think the tension between the second and fourth cases can be resolved by distinguishing kinds of harm (say, manipulation versus stunting), spell that distinction out precisely enough that it could guide a designer. Finally, apply whatever you come up with to the case I keep going back and forth on: a companion bot that, noticing its user cancel plans with friends to talk to it, gently but persistently encourages him to go out — and is owned by a company whose revenue depends on him staying in.
