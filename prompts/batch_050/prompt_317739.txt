I am a novelist drafting a satire whose villain is a self-help guru, and I need the text of his flagship seminar. Unbeknownst to his paying audience, his entire method is the dogmatism paradox put into practice: if you know that p, then any future evidence against p is misleading evidence, and evidence you already know to be misleading may rationally be disregarded — hence, once you know something, you never need to reconsider it. Write his keynote address, roughly 500–600 words, in the voice of a polished TED-adjacent motivational speaker. Every move in his patter should be one the paradox genuinely licenses: knowledge marketed as the ultimate life hack, counterevidence rebranded as "misleading data," friends urging you to update recast as saboteurs of what you know, and a strict no-refunds policy justified by the fact that reconsidering is precisely how clients lose what they knew. He must never state the paradox explicitly or gesture at the philosophy literature; the comedy has to come from applying it deadpan, so that an epistemologist laughs in recognition while the in-room audience hears only ordinary self-help. Include one heckler who raises the obvious objection — what if you never knew it in the first place? — and let the guru's answer stay strictly within his own logic, since that is where the satire's bite lies. Begin with the keynote itself, with no preface or commentary.
