In a philosophy of mind seminar I attend, a discussion of whether large language models have beliefs was cut short by an exchange I have since seen repeated in several forms. One participant argued that if we count a model's behavior as sufficient grounds for ascribing beliefs to it, we might as well say that the thermostat believes the room is too cold. A second replied that the thermostat does believe the room is too cold, in the only sense of "believes" we were ever entitled to. Both leaned on the same sentence — the thermostat believes the room is too cold — one wielding it as a reductio, the other endorsing it as common sense, and neither paused to say what precisely they took it to claim.

Please disambiguate that sentence. Lay out, as a list, the distinct precise theses it might be expressing: for example, the thesis that any system whose behavior is usefully predicted by ascribing a belief thereby has that belief; the thesis that belief-talk is a useful fiction, literally false of thermostats but pragmatically licensed, and perhaps of us too; the thesis that belief requires no consciousness or representation, only the right functional organization, which thermostats instantiate in a minimal form; the thesis that belief comes in degrees and that thermostats possess a vanishingly small amount; and any others you think belong on the menu. For each reading, say whether it is a platitude, a substantive and defensible claim, or simply false, and give your reasons.

Then do the dialectical work: for each of the two uses in my seminar, identify which reading (or readings) the speaker would need the sentence to carry for the move to succeed. In particular, assess whether the reductio actually damages the claim about language models, given that a model's behavioral repertoire differs from a thermostat's along dimensions — generality, flexibility, sensitivity to content — that some readings treat as irrelevant and others as the whole story. Finally, say what, if anything, the slogan is incapable of settling no matter how it is read, and what a more careful question would look like in its place.
