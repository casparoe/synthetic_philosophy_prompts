I'm drafting a position piece for a workshop on the ethics of generative AI, and before I circulate it I want my commitments checked for coherence. Here is what I currently hold:

(a) Frankfurt's analysis is basically correct: lying requires that the speaker take something to be true and intend to mislead the audience about it, whereas bullshitting is defined by indifference to truth — the bullshitter may even say true things, but truth-value plays no guiding role in the production of the utterance.

(b) When LLM-based chatbots hallucinate — producing fluent, plausible, false statements — they are bullshitting in roughly this sense, and describing the phenomenon as bullshit rather than as lying or as error captures something real that the engineering vocabulary of "hallucination" obscures.

(c) These systems have no beliefs, intentions, or other propositional attitudes. There is nothing they take to be true, nothing they take to be false, and nothing they aim at.

(d) Whether an utterance is a lie, bullshit, or honest error is fixed entirely by the producer's attitudes toward truth, and not by the content of what is said, the audience's uptake, or the downstream consequences.

(e) Hallucinations are engineering defects — failures of a system to track truth — and the appropriate response is better training, grounding, and calibration, not moral condemnation of the model.

(f) It is nonetheless legitimate, and not loose talk, to fault the companies that build and deploy these systems for putting bullshitting machines into the world, and "bullshit" functions here as a morally charged term rather than a neutral classification.

(g) A human press secretary who reads out a prepared statement without knowing or caring whether it is true is a paradigm bullshitter, and the bullshit is attributable to them even if they did not write the words.

Which of these commitments are actually in tension with each other, and which only appear to be? For each genuine tension, state how severe it is and what would have to give, and for each merely apparent tension, show why it dissolves. Please rank the genuine tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally damaging, and then tell me which single revision — keeping my overall position recognizable — would restore the most consistency at the lowest cost.
