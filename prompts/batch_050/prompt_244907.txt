I am a PhD student in philosophy of biology writing a dissertation chapter on the concept of fitness, and I have noticed that my case intuitions do not obviously cohere. I will list my verdicts and ask you what principle, if any, unifies them.

1. Two genetically and phenotypically identical organisms in identical environments: one is killed by a lightning strike before reproducing, the other leaves ten offspring. My verdict: they were equally fit. Realized reproductive success is not fitness.
2. Compare two alleles: one reliably yields two offspring per bearer; the other yields zero half the time and four half the time. Same expected value, but my verdict is that the low-variance allele is fitter, and I want my account to explain why selection favors it.
3. A beak morphology that aids feeding in drought years and hinders it in wet years has no fitness simpliciter, only fitness relative to an environment. Verdict: fitness is essentially environment-relative.
4. In a very small population, the type with the higher expected offspring number is lost to drift while the other fixes. Verdict: the lost type was fitter nonetheless; extinction by drift does not retroactively reduce fitness.
5. A male morph with high mating success when rare and poor success when common: verdict, its fitness is genuinely frequency-dependent rather than a fixed propensity of the morph.
6. Defining fitness as actual reproductive output renders "survival of the fittest" trivial, and that triviality is a real defect an account must avoid. But I also hold that fitness is empirically measurable, so an account making fitness an unobservable metaphysical propensity with no epistemic handle is defective too.

What principle, or small set of candidate principles, best captures these verdicts? State each principle precisely, identify which case motivates each clause, and say honestly which of my verdicts it cannot accommodate if some must be abandoned (I suspect 1 and 2 jointly threaten any simple expected-value propensity view, and 5 threatens the idea that fitness is a propensity of the organism at all). Then tell me what the best principle predicts for a case I have not listed: the sterile worker ant, whose sterility was selected for. Finally, state what argument or case would lead you to a different conclusion, or where your reasoning is most likely to fail. Keep the whole response under 600 words.
