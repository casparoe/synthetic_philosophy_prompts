I work in the secretariat of a river-basin commission shared between three countries -- we set dam release schedules, fishing quotas, pollution limits, that kind of thing. Literally every decision we make kicks off the same fight about who gets a seat at the table: downstream villages in a fourth, non-member country, seasonal migrant fishers, a big hydropower utility, people arguing on behalf of future generations, and at least once someone claimed the river itself should be represented. I want a reusable decision procedure my team can run in about an hour, per decision, that outputs who has to be represented and roughly how heavily their voice should count. Ground it in whatever democratic theory actually has to say about this -- the all-affected-interests principle, the all-subjected-to-coercion principle, whatever else is load-bearing -- but it needs to be operational: the questions to ask in order, what counts as a good enough answer to each, where the procedure spits out "in" or "out" and where it has to hand off to a value judgment. Also have it take a position on two things I keep going back and forth on: whether the electorate should be reconstituted per decision or standing, and whether deeper stakes should mean more votes or just a louder voice. Then run it on two cases: a dam release schedule that floods farmland in the non-member country, and a fishing quota that mostly binds the migrant fishers who have no vote anywhere. Flag at least one case where the procedure gives an answer my boss will hate, and be honest about what it can't settle -- especially who gets to run the procedure itself, since that seems like the whole problem in miniature.
