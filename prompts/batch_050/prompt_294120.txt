You are a philosopher specializing in epistemology and the philosophy of language, preparing a graduate seminar on the norms governing assertion. I would like you to think carefully and, before settling on a final answer, briefly brainstorm the range of cases and puzzles that any candidate norm of assertion is standardly asked to accommodate.

Context: the debate I have in mind concerns what rule, if any, constitutes the practice of asserting. The main contenders are knowledge norms (assert only what you know), truth norms (assert only what is true), belief norms (assert only what you believe), and various justification or warrant norms (assert only what you have adequate evidence or justification for). I am not asking you to adjudicate between them directly. Instead, I want the specification first: the desiderata that any adequate account of the norm of assertion must satisfy to count as a serious candidate.

Structure your response in the following sections, in this order:

1. Brainstorming (roughly 150 words). A rapid, non-committal list of the phenomena, cases, and pressures you think might matter before you filter them. Include some items you will later discard, and say at the end of the section which ones you discarded and why.

2. The desiderata (roughly 500 words, the core of the answer). The requirements list itself. For each desideratum: state it precisely, give the case or consideration that motivates it (for example, whatever role you think lottery propositions, Moore-paradoxical utterances, Gettiered speakers, blameless false assertion, or challenges like requests to explain how one knows should play), and mark it as non-negotiable or as a commitment of a particular camp. Pay attention to distinctions the account must not collapse, such as the difference between an assertion being improper and being merely false, between criticizability of the speaker and defectiveness of the act, and between the norm that governs assertion and norms governing related speech acts.

3. Tensions in the list (roughly 200 words). Identify any pair or subset of your desiderata that you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied, or that can be satisfied only at significant theoretical cost. If the list turns out to be consistent, say so and explain why this debate is harder than one that contains an outright inconsistency.

4. Which desideratum does the most work (roughly 100 words). Name the single requirement that excludes the most candidate norms, and say which contenders it knocks out.

5. Brief scorecard (roughly 150 words, no more). Sketch how the knowledge, truth, belief, and justification norms fare against your list, without developing the full case for any of them.

Throughout, indicate how confident you are in what you claim, and in a final sentence single out the one point in your response you are least sure of.
