Years of reading rationalist blogs have left me genuinely unsure what to think about character. The heuristics-and-biases canon gets cited as showing that situations, not traits, drive behavior; meanwhile virtue ethics keeps being revived, and its defenders say the situationist critique attacks a strawman. Imagine the two camps -- a neo-Aristotelian virtue ethicist and a situationist skeptic about robust character traits -- agree to run an adversarial collaboration and co-author a joint statement. Draft that statement. It should contain: (1) a numbered list of claims both sides can sign, covering what the classic experimental record (obedience studies, bystander effects, mood and situational-pressure manipulations, the modest but real cross-situational correlations in personality data) does and does not establish, and explicitly including the concessions each side is actually willing to make -- the situationist grants that local, situation-specific dispositions exist and predict behavior, the virtue ethicist grants that full virtue is rare and that folk trait-attribution is badly overconfident; (2) a table of residual disagreements, each phrased so that both parties would accept the wording of their own position, separating the descriptive question (do robust, global, causally efficacious character traits exist?) from the normative one (does the virtue-ethical program of habituation and practical wisdom stand or fall with the descriptive one?) and from the practical one (should moral education and institutional design target character or choice architecture?); (3) the cruxes -- the sub-questions such that settling them would move at least one side. Two I suspect: first, whether trait-talk understood as statistical aggregation over many occasions is enough for the character the virtue ethicist needs, or whether virtue requires something causally thicker than a stable average; second, whether the situationist's own positive program of designing better situations tacitly presupposes exactly the cross-situational practical judgment it denies to ordinary agents. For each crux, specify a concrete study or case both sides agree bears on it and record each side's prediction in advance. Use this one as a worked example: an experience-sampling study finds that within-person variance in honest conduct across situations dwarfs between-person variance, yet individuals' rank-ordering is highly stable across years -- both parties must state beforehand which side, if either, this finding supports and what result would instead have made them update. Where a disagreement turns out to be verbal, say so in the statement rather than manufacturing disagreement for symmetry's sake.
