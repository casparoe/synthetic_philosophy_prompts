Ok so here's my situation. I teach a 200-level philosophy of mind seminar, 16 students, one 80-minute meeting, in a room with movable chairs and a big open floor, no tech beyond tape and index cards and maybe a bag of candy for stakes. Next week we're doing Hofstadter's careenium — the thought experiment from his Scientific American columns (collected in Metamagical Themas) where a vast pool table is covered with zillions of tiny hard marbles, the simms, bouncing around on their own little local rules, and at a coarser grain their clustering adds up to big slow coherent blobs, the simmballs, which is where the "symbols" live. His claim, roughly, is that the simmball level isn't just a lazy redescription we trot out because we can't track the marbles — it's where the real action is, and a mind is what you get when the simmballs get to boss the simms around without anything spooky happening down below.

Every year the same thing happens. They nod along, and then ten minutes later someone says but come on, the marbles do literally everything, the blobs don't push anything, they're just us watching from far away. Which is fair! That's the causal exclusion worry and its a good one. But then I ask them to actually predict or steer the system while staying at the marble level and they drown in it, which tells me the exclusion story and the "you'd be helpless without the symbol level" story are both getting their grip on them, just never at the same moment.

So: design me an activity that makes them feel both pulls in their bodies before anyone names anything. I don't want to lecture at all up front. Some sketch of what I'm imagining: the students themselves are the substrate in phase one, following stupid local rules so that macro-patterns emerge that nobody intended; then a phase where a couple of them are designated interveners who want to change what the system is doing, and they discover that every actual shove lands on an individual simm, yet the only tractable way to plan anything is to think and talk at the blob level — tracking sixteen people's local interactions in real time should be practically impossible, that's load-bearing, don't let it be easy. They should end up having lived the sentence "the simmballs never pushed anything" and the sentence "only the simmballs pushed anything worth predicting" and be unable to fully give up either.

What I want from you: the actual rules and payoff structure, simple enough to explain in five minutes; a timeline for the 80 minutes including when the pattern-observers get introduced; exactly when and how I reveal what they've been enacting (not before, and I don't want them to hear the word careenium until the debrief); and a debrief plan that maps each phase onto the three ideas — emergence of a level with its own dynamics, the exclusion problem, and explanatory indispensability vs. causal overdetermination. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong when I run it: e.g. some clever kid just tracks the micro-level anyway and spoils the magic, or students start openly coordinating so nothing is really "emergent" anymore, or someone declares the blob caused something in a way that overreaches and I need a good response that isn't just well actually. And be honest with me about what the activity can't reproduce — my guess is real semantic content and the self-reference, since in a brain there's no seperate observer standing outside the levels — so I know what I have to supply in discussion afterwards. Definately keep it runnable by one instructor with no TA.
