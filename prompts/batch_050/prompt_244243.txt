I'm a second-year grad student putting together a written statement of my views for a decision theory reading group that will work through it line by line, and I want to find the cracks before they do. We've spent the term on the foundations of expected utility theory — representation theorems, Dutch books, money pumps — and I find myself holding the following, which all seemed fine individually until I tried to write them on one page. First, I take the money-pump argument to settle that rational preferences must be transitive: an agent with cyclical preferences can be turned into a source of free money, and that's reason enough to condemn the cycle. Second, largely on the strength of arguments like that one, I take the coherence arguments collectively to establish expected utility maximization as the standard of rational choice — this is why I think deviations from it call for excuse or explanation. Third, I think the Allais pattern is perfectly rational: preferring a sure million to a near-sure gamble with a small chance of nothing, while also preferring the long-shot gamble when both outcomes are unlikely, seems to me like a sane way to value certainty, and I'd choose that way myself without feeling any pressure to revise. Fourth, I accept the Dutch book argument for probabilism about credences: degrees of belief that violate the probability axioms leave you exploitable, so rational credences conform to the axioms. Fifth, I think risk aversion about money is rationally permissible — declining a fair or slightly favorable bet because the downside stings more than the upside helps is no failure of rationality, just a fact about one's utility for money.

What I'd like is an audit, not a lecture: go through these five commitments and tell me which of them genuinely pull against each other, which pairs merely look like they might and dissolve on inspection, and — this is the part I care most about — whether the justifications I've attached to each commitment actually support that commitment or something narrower. I don't have a fixed view about which commitment I'd abandon, so please don't assume one is untouchable. Where two commitments conflict, say how severe the conflict is, what exactly would have to give on each side, and what the cheapest overall repair would be. Rank the tensions by severity rather than treating them all as equally urgent, and note explicitly where a conflict I might expect to find isn't really there.

One format request: please write your answer as continuous prose, like an email or a letter — no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no numbered lists, and no tables. I know an audit invites list structure, but I want to see the reasoning flow, with the ordering by severity carried by the writing itself.
