Two friends of mine read Dennett's Where Am I? -- the thought experiment where his brain is removed and kept alive in a vat, radio-linked to his body, which keeps walking around, and later a spare copy of the brain is made and the link gets switched between the two brains. They had a fight about it afterwards and I stayed out of it.

Marta said the story has a clear answer that Dennett is too coy to state: he goes where his brain goes, because everything that makes experience happen is in there, and the body is basically a remote-controlled vehicle. She said the switching case proves it -- when the link flips to the other brain, his point of view flips with it, so brain = seat of the self.

Dan said she's missed the whole point. The story is designed to break the "seat of the self" intuition: Dennett keeps framing it as him being stuck in the vat, but the story shows that framing is optional -- you can just as well describe him as out in the world where the body is, and neither description is forced by the facts. So there's no fact of the matter where he is, and asking is a category mistake. Marta replied that "where the experience is produced" and "where the person acts in the world" are two different questions and Dan is dissolving the first by talking about the second -- the vat answer is about experience, the body answer is about action, and only the first one is what "where am I?" means. Dan answered that this just shows the phrase is ambiguous, which is his point: once you disambiguate, the deep puzzle evaporates.

Who's right at each step? I'd like a verdict per point, not just overall, and tell me if both of them are missing something about what the thought experiment can and can't show. Also say how confident you are in your adjudication and flag the point you're least sure about.
