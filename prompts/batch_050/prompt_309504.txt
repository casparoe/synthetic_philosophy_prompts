I work as a distributed systems engineer, and I'd like some philosophy done in the vocabulary I actually use. My fluency profile: I'm comfortable with special relativity at the level of light cones, time dilation, and the relativity of simultaneity, but the concepts I reason with day to day are Lamport's happened-before relation, vector clocks, consistent cuts, and the fact that in a system with no global clock the events form only a partial order, and a global state is something you construct rather than something you read off.

I recently came across the Rietdijk–Putnam argument for the block universe. As I understand it, it goes like this: two observers in relative motion, passing each other on Earth, have different simultaneity surfaces; some event in a distant galaxy — say, a decision by aliens to launch a fleet — lies on one observer's present slice but in the other's future; if whatever lies on my present slice counts as real and settled for me, and each observer is equally entitled to their slice, then the launch is already real and settled tout court, even for the observer who would call it future; and generalizing this, every event, past and future, is on an equal ontological footing, and the universe is a four-dimensional block with no genuine becoming.

Here is what I want. First, a careful restatement of the argument in my working vocabulary. Translate simultaneity surfaces into consistent cuts, the light-cone structure into the happened-before partial order, "real and settled for observer X" into whatever the honest analogue is for membership in a cut, and so on, step by step, so that each premise and each inference of the argument gets a counterpart in distributed-systems terms. Do the translation honestly: if a premise can only be expressed by quietly building the conclusion into the definitions, flag that rather than smoothing it over.

Second, the residue report. Which parts of the argument survive translation cleanly, which survive only with distortion, and which have no counterpart in my vocabulary at all? I am suspicious of two points in particular and would like them checked explicitly: (a) in my world, an event's belonging to some consistent cut makes it part of a possible global state, not of the actual execution — does the argument need the stronger move, and can that move even be stated in cut-talk; and (b) my framework treats the future as constrained by the partial order but not necessarily determined by the present state — does the argument secretly require determinism, and if so, what is the counterpart of that requirement in a system whose processes and inputs aren't assumed deterministic?

Third, the upshot. Is the untranslatable residue a defect of my vocabulary — evidence that distributed systems simply have no room for claims about ontological fixedness — or is it a sign that the argument's metaphysical step trades on an equivocation, between "real for an observer" and "real simpliciter", that my vocabulary usefully refuses to reproduce? I want your defended view, not a menu of options.

Please end with a single line of the form "Verdict: ..." stating, in one short phrase, whether the argument's conclusion survives translation and, if not, at which step it is lost.
