My reading group is doing classical Chinese ethics this month and I got stuck on a passage from Mozi's "Universal Love III" (in the old W. P. Mei translation, the one hosted on the Chinese Text Project). Mozi is diagnosing why the world is such a mess, and he writes:

"If we should classify one by one all those who hate others and injure others, should we find them to be universal in love or partial? Of course we should say they are partial. Now, since partiality against one another is the cause of the major calamities in the empire, then partiality is wrong."

The thing is, I built an arguement out of this for our discussion and now it seems to prove way more than I signed up for. Here it is:

1. Mozi is right that the great evils -- wars of conquest, exploitation of the weak, cruelty to outsiders -- are all committed by people acting partially, caring about their own state or clan or family and not about others.
2. Confucian graded love just IS partiality, systematized: love your parents most, then your family, then your neighbors, then strangers, with the weight falling off as the relationship gets more distant.
3. If partiality is the cause of the world's calamities, then partiality is wrong. (This is just Mozi's own inference in the passage.)
4. So graded love is wrong.
5. And if graded love is wrong, then preferring your own child over a stranger's -- feeding yours first, pulling yours out of the burning building first -- is wrong for the same reason wars of conquest are, since both are partiality.
6. But nobody thinks a parent who saves their own child first has done anything wrong. So something in (1)-(3) has to give, and since (2) is a definition and (3) is Mozi's own move, it looks like (1) goes -- i.e. Mozi's diagnosis of the calamities is wrong.

Where does this go wrong, if it does? Every step looked innocent when I wrote it down. If I have to choose I'd rather keep graded love than Mozi's diagnosis, but honestly I'd like to keep both if that's available -- the passage does seem to identify something real about why people do horible things to outgroups. So tell me which premise or step to reject and what rejecting it costs, or whether the argument is actually sound and I should just bite the bullet and become a Mohist.
