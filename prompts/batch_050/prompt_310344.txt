I supervise a graduate epistemology seminar, and after our session on bootstrapping and easy knowledge two of my strongest students got into an argument that outlasted the seminar itself. Neither would concede, and they have asked me to weigh in; before I do, I want a careful outside adjudication of the exchange, both to check my own instincts and to structure our next meeting. The background is standard: Stewart Cohen defends the "basic knowledge" picture on which, absent defeaters, one can know that a wall is red simply by looking, and can then infer that one is not being deceived by red lighting; Jonathan Vogel's gas-gauge case presses the worry that an agent could seemingly bootstrap a source's general reliability out of nothing but the source's own outputs. Here is the exchange as I recorded it:

(1) Maya: The bootstrapping inference is epistemically worthless even if every step is logically impeccable. Suppose you consult a gauge, note its reading, independently verify the reading was accurate that one time, repeat the procedure, and finally conclude that the gauge is reliable. And in the pure version you skip the independent verification entirely and infer reliability from the sheer coherence of the gauge's outputs. Either way, no method can certify itself; if this reasoning conferred justification, every source whatsoever could pull itself up by its own bootstraps.

(2) Daniel: There is no special problem here. Grant basic knowledge: each individual step yields a justified belief. Once you have a stock of justified beliefs of the form "the gauge said P, and P," the inference to reliability is nothing but ordinary enumerative induction. Rejecting it means rejecting induction as such, which is skepticism, not a diagnosis.

(3) Maya: Track-record arguments are not ordinary induction. In genuine induction the premises -- the observations -- are independently warranted. Here the gauge's readings count as evidence for anything at all only conditional on the gauge being reliable, which is precisely the conclusion. The argument is circular in a way that "all observed emeralds are green, so emeralds are green" is not.

(4) Daniel: That conditionality charge proves too much. Perception is itself a gauge: my visual experiences constitute evidence only if my eyes are reliable. If Maya's objection worked, no perceptually grounded induction would be legitimate either, and we are back to the skepticism she claimed to avoid. If she wants to grant perception some antecedent entitlement, she owes an account of why an agent with a reasonable prior about the gauge cannot enjoy the same standing.

(5) Maya: That is exactly the distinction. Perceptual entitlement is non-inferential and antecedent; the bootstrapping agent, by hypothesis, has no prior reason to trust the gauge. If the agent did have independent evidence of reliability, the track record would add nothing of substance. So easy knowledge is either circular or redundant.

(6) Daniel: Then her target has shrunk to a fiction: an agent with literally no prior attitude whatsoever toward the gauge's trustworthiness. No rational agent is like that. Given any non-degenerate prior, the accumulating track record raises one's credence in reliability, which is all anyone should have wanted.

Please adjudicate this exchange move by move: for each of the six moves, say whether it succeeds, fails, or succeeds only against a restricted version of the opponent's claim, and justify the verdict. I also want your diagnosis of the underlying crux -- where exactly the disagreement bottoms out, and whether it concerns the individuation of evidence, the structure of inductive warrant, or something else. Finally, is there anything both parties are missing? In particular, I am curious whether the dispute would look different if framed in terms of whether warrant is transmitted across an inference rather than merely whether the inference is valid, and whether either side has adequately distinguished the kind of circularity that afflicts a particular argument from the kind that might afflict reliance on a rule or faculty as such. Please do not take my recording of the exchange as indicating which student I favor; I genuinely have not decided.
