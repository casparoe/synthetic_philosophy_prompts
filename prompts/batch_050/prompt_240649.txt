In debates about whether reinforcement learning agents could ever suffer, one side leans hard on the analogy that negative reward is like pain and positive reward is like pleasure -- training an RL agent gets compared to training a dog, where we obviously think the scolding does something to the dog. The other side replies that a reward signal is just a scalar in a log file, and calling it suffering is like saying a thermostat dislikes the cold. Audit the reward-as-valence analogy: what actually corresponds to what (is reward the analogue of pleasure itself, or of a dopamine signal, or of a fitness score?), and where does the mapping break -- reward vs. reward prediction error, the policy that got trained vs. the agent running at deployment, the fact that reward is typically computed outside the agent and may never be represented anywhere inside it? What does each side's version of the analogy quietly smuggle in about whether valence has to be consciously experienced to count? And once the disanalogies are on the table, does either conclusion -- RL agents can suffer, or obviously they can't -- actually survive, or is there a better source analogy to reach for (animal conditioning, economic utility, immune responses)?
