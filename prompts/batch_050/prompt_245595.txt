I'm prepping materials for my upper-division epistemology seminar, and this year I want to teach pragmatic encroachment / interest-relative invariantism partly by making the students laugh at it before we take it seriously. So: I'd like you to write the customer-service FAQ page for a fictional bank, "First Stakes-Sensitive Savings," whose entire business model is built on taking the bank cases at face value. The bank's official policy is that whether anyone there *knows* something -- whether the branch is open Saturday, whether your transfer cleared, whether your money is safe -- genuinely depends on how much rides on it for you. The FAQ should have deadpan entries like: a low-stakes customer asking if the bank is open Saturday and being told yes, we know it is; a customer who will lose their house if a check bounces being told that unfortunately nobody at the bank is currently in a position to know the same thing; an explanation of the "premium certainty tier" where for a monthly fee the bank will gather enough evidence to know things on your behalf again; maybe a note that closing times are listed as "known" or "merely believed" depending on the season. Around 500-600 words.

For your role: you're a comedy writer who also happens to have a PhD in epistemology, so think carefully about what actually makes the view tick before you start -- maybe brainstorm briefly (to yourself, not in the output) about which features of the position are funniest when literalized: the stakes-sensitivity of knowledge itself rather than just of assertion, the weird asymmetry between what you'd say about your own case versus someone else's, the way the view lets you lose knowledge just by learning that the stakes went up (an email titled "URGENT" that makes you stop knowing things would be a good bit), and the pressure it puts on the idea that knowledge is the norm of practical reasoning -- the bank could have a whole policy about when employees are permitted to *act* on what they believe. The satire has to land because it exaggerates something real, not because it invents a silly version of the view, so no strawmen -- a defender of pragmatic encroachment should be able to say "ok, fair, that IS awkward for me" rather than "nobody thinks that."

Keep it fully deadpan -- corporate FAQ voice throughout, no winking, no breaking character to explain the joke. And please don't name any actual philosophers in the FAQ itself; an invented founder or chief epistemology officer is fine and probably funnier. If you want a title/header and a footer with fine print, go for it, the fine print is a great place to hide the sharpest jokes.
