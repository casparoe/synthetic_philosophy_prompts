Hey, so I'm working on a novel and I'm stuck on something that's actually bugging me as a person, not just as a writer. My main character keeps ending up in situations where two moral demands collide, and my editor said one of my scenes felt "fake deep" because the dilemma dissolved the moment you thought about it for five minutes. Fair enough.

So I made a list of the kinds of dilemmas I keep reaching for:

1. Two duties of the same *type* clashing (keep promise to A vs. keep promise to B, roughly equal stakes)
2. Duties from totally different sources clashing (loyalty to a person vs. some impersonal duty like telling the truth)
3. "Dirty hands" situations, where whatever you do, you've done something wrong, and the only question is which wrong
4. Cases where the conflict is really just ignorance -- if the character knew more, one option would clearly win
5. Cases where the dilemma comes from the character having made a prior mistake that boxed them in

Can you rank these from most to least *genuinely* irresolvable -- like, which ones would still stand as real dilemmas after a very smart, very honest person thought about them for a year? Give each a quick sentence on why it lands where it does. Feel free to say two of them tie, or that one isn't a dilemma at all.

And then, the part I actually care about for the book: does your ranking change if you think about it from the *character's* perspective rather than a philosopher's? Because a dilemma that's "really" resolvable but doesn't feel resolvable to the person living it might still be the better material for fiction. Just a paragraph on that is fine.
