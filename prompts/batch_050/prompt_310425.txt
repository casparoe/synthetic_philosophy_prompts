I'm a PhD student working on the metaphysics of modality, and I'm supposed to be running a sort of diagnostic exercise for the second-year undergrads in my department before their essay on origins and identity. The problem is they all come in having read a bit of Kripke and they all say "obviously I couldn't have had different parents, but maybe different gametes, or maybe not, it's a hard question" and then the essay goes nowhere because they've never actually worked out which commitments they're willing to pay for. So I want to build a quiz that sorts people by what they say about cases, not by which label they've heard of.

Could you write me a "where do you stand on origin essentialism?" instrument, about eight questions. Each question should be a concrete case or a yes/no dilemma someone can answer without knowing any of the vocabulary -- things like: a twin case where the sperm cells get swapped so the other sperm wins the race (are you still you?); a case where your parents conceive via IVF and the technician picks a different sperm from the same batch with a genetically identical payload; a gradual-replacement case where the matter of the zygote gets swapped out molecule by molecule in the first hours; a case asking whether you could have been born to entirely different parents in a different country; and whatever else you think actually drives the positions apart. Two or three options per question.

Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the live positions in the space: strict origin essentialism where both the particular gametes and the genetic material are necessary; a weaker version where only the genetic profile matters and the gamete tokens don't; the counterpart-theoretic move where the whole question dissolves because "could have been" is just counterpart talk; the haecceitist view where your thisness could in principle have been attached to totally different origins; and the sortal-relativist line where whether origins are necessary depends on which sortal you're quantifying under. If two of those can't be seperated by any question on the quiz, flag that openly rather than pretending the quiz distinguishes them. And if there's a combination of answers that's perfectly consistent but corresponds to a position nobody holds, note the combination and give me one sentence on why the seat is empty -- incoherent, unmotivated, or just unexplored. The whole point is that they discover their label at the end, so nothing in the questions should tip off which answer is "the essentialist one."
