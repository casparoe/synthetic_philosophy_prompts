I am a journalist covering science and ideas, and I am writing an essay on why camera images seem to offer more direct access to events than testimony or drawings. Read Hitchcock’s Rear Window through the debate over photographic transparency: do photographs let us literally see the objects they depict, or are they merely unusually reliable traces of those objects? Distinguish transparency, evidential reliability, and the way looking through an apparatus can alter the scene being observed. Apply that distinction to Jeff’s binoculars and telephoto lens, Lisa’s entry into Thorwald’s apartment, and the climactic use of the camera flash, while also considering how Hitchcock positions the film’s audience as another observer. Does Jeff’s vindication show that his mediated looking amounted to knowledge, or only that a risky interpretation happened to be right? Please indicate how confident you are in each major interpretive claim and single out the point on which your reading is least secure.
