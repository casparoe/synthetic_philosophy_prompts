So in my history class we just finished this unit on the Holocaust and my teacher had us read about this big argument between two historians, Browning and Goldhagen, about why regular German guys in these police battalions ended up murdering tons of civilians. Browning basically says they were ordinary men and it was mostly the situation -- peer pressure, not wanting to look like a coward, following orders, getting gradually desensitized. Goldhagen says no, they were willing executioners, it was the culture, this deep antisemitism in Germany that made them actually want to do it. Fine, I get the debate.

But then for a project I was reading about the My Lai massacre in Vietnam in 1968, where American soldiers from Charlie Company killed hundreds of unarmed villagers, mostly women, kids, and old people, over a few hours. And I noticed people arguing about it sound EXACTLY like the Browning/Goldhagen thing, just transplanted. Some people say the soldiers were normal guys in an insane situation: they'd been losing friends to booby traps for weeks, couldn't tell civilians from enemies, were ordered by Lt. Calley, and everyone else was doing it. Others say it was more of a culture thing: the racism, the way soldiers talked about Vietnamese people as less than human, the body count system that basically rewarded killing. And then there's a third thing nobody fits in, which is character: some soldiers flat out refused to shoot, and this helicopter pilot Hugh Thompson literally landed between the soldiers and the villagers and threatened to open fire on his own side to stop it.

So here's what I want: analyze My Lai as a test case for the situation vs culture vs character debate. Do the mapping carefully -- what actually counts as evidence for each explanation here (like does the fact that some men refused break the situation story, or just show situations affect people differently)? Where does each theory overreach -- like, Goldhagen's thing was specifically about German antisemitism, so what does it even mean to apply a culture explanation to Americans in Vietnam, and does the hatred there work the same way as his version? And then run it backwards too: does My Lai show something is missing from BOTH Browning and Goldhagen, like the whole framing of "were they ordinary or willing" might be a false binary when one company can contain Calley, the guys who refused, and Thompson all at once? My teacher keeps saying good history essays use cases to stress-test theories and I want to actually do that instead of just summarizing.
