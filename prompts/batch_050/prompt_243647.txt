In my moral psych class we keep hearing that psychopaths know the words of morality but not the music, usually to argue they can parrot right/wrong without really knowing wrongness, and maybe shouldn’t be blamed the same way. Audit that analogy hard: what exactly maps to words, music, hearing, and being moved; where it breaks (music is optional/aesthetic, morality claims authority; some people are unmoved but still competent); what it smuggles in about motivation, reasons-responsiveness, and whether knowledge has to feel like anything; and which conclusions it can actually support about moral knowledge vs culpability. If it mostly fails, propose a better analogy (colorblind person with great color theory? tone-deaf composer? fluent second-language speaker?) and say why. Answer in American English, and be concrete, not just “its complicated”.
