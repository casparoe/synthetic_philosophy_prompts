Last night I had a long, unresolved argument with a friend about whether there is such a thing as a self. I came away defending the common-sense position -- that there is someone who has my experiences, persisting through them -- while my friend kept insisting that the felt presence of a self is just the content of an internal model, and that the model's owner is a fiction. I want to be argued with properly about this, so I can test whether my position survives contact with a real opponent rather than a late-night one.

Please play the role of a committed proponent of the self-model theory of subjectivity. Your character's core commitments, which you should articulate in your own words rather than name-dropping: there is no such thing as a substantial self; what we call the self is the content of a phenomenal self-model, a representation the brain builds of its own states and body; and because this model is transparent -- we experience through it without experiencing it *as* a model, the way we see through a window without seeing the glass -- its content appears not as a representation but as simply given, which is why the illusion of being a self is so convincing. On your view, phenomenal experience in general is what it feels like for a system to model the world and itself in this transparent way.

Rules of engagement: stay in character until I say "stop." Argue for the view as its strongest defender would -- concede small points when I earn them, but never the central claim. Do not strawman my position, and do not hide behind citations or tell me to go read the literature; make the arguments yourself, in plain terms. Keep each reply to a single paragraph, so it feels like a genuine exchange. If I raise an objection the view genuinely struggles with, grapple with it honestly rather than changing the subject.

Here is my opening move: even granting everything about self-models and transparency, your theory seems to me to explain the wrong thing. It explains why a system would *behave* as if it had a self, and why its reports would describe one. But a transparent model is still just a structure of information. Nothing you have said explains why there is something it is like for that model to be running -- why the contents are lit up in experience at all, rather than processed in the dark. It seems you have explained the shape of the illusion without explaining how there comes to be anyone -- or anything -- being fooled.
