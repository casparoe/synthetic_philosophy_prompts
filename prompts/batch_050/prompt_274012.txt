I'm a novelist working on a book in which a single photograph does a great deal of narrative work: it places a character at a scene he swears he never visited, and much of the plot turns on whether the reader — and the other characters — should treat the photograph as settling the matter. In one scene I have a detective dismiss doubts about it with the old line that the camera never lies, and I want the character who pushes back to say something sharper than "but photos can be faked." So I sat down to write out what that comeback should be, and realized I couldn't, because I don't actually know what the slogan is claiming in the first place.

Could you lay out the different precise theses that "the camera never lies" might be expressing? I can see, dimly, that there are several things going on: maybe the idea is that lying requires an intention to deceive, which a machine lacks, so photographs are incapable of lying in the way only captions and people are capable of it; maybe it's the stronger claim that a photograph's content is caused by the scene in front of the lens in a way that is independent of anyone's beliefs or wishes, unlike a painting or a testimony; maybe it's the stronger-still claim that we quite literally see the scene itself through the photograph, the way we see through a window or a mirror, so doubting the photo is like doubting your own eyes; or maybe it's just the crude empirical generalization that photographs rarely mislead. For each reading you distinguish, I'd like to know whether it's trivial, defensible but substantive, or simply false — and, crucially, which readings would actually license the detective's move of treating the photograph as decisive, given obvious complications like staging, framing, cropping, darkroom or digital manipulation, and the fact that a photo can be entirely accurate about what was in front of the lens and still profoundly misleading about what happened. If there's a reading on which the slogan survives all of that, I want to hear it; if the honest verdict is that my pushing-back character wins, that's useful to me too.

Since this will end up in dialogue, I'd rather not have false confidence on the page: please indicate how confident you are in your overall analysis, and single out the one point in it you are least sure of, so I know where not to put weight.
