Write a short story about the responsibility gap problem for autonomous systems -- the idea that when a self-directed machine causes harm, sometimes nobody left is fairly blameworthy (not the programmer who couldn't foresee it, not the owner who followed the manual, not the machine itself which isn't the kind of thing that can be guilty).

Some constraints so it doesn't turn into a generic killer-robot story:

- Set it somewhere low-key and non-futuristic -- a farming cooperative, a small harbor town, a regional hospital -- not a tech company or a battlefield.
- The harm should be real but not catastrophic. No death. Something like a ruined harvest, a wrongly denied insurance payout, a destroyed shipment. I want the gap to show up in a mundane context where someone still understandably wants *someone* to answer for it.
- Include at least one scene where affected people sit around and try to figure out who to be angry at, and the anger keeps sliding off every candidate.
- Nobody in the story should explain the philosophy out loud. No character gives a little speech about agency or moral luck. The reader should feel the gap, not be told about it.
- Somewhere in the story, include an object that gets passed between characters (a form, a key, a jar of something, whatever) whose journey quietly tracks how the blame keeps getting handed off too.
- Ending: leave it genuinely unresolved whether the community's response (whatever you invent -- shunning the owner, smashing the machine, a weird ritual of shared compensation, shrugging it off) was fair.

Roughly 700-1000 words. Thanks!
