Here's the paragraph, roughly as he pasted it:

"Rawls and Harsanyi both ask what principles rational people would choose behind a veil of ignorance, where nobody knows their position in society. Harsanyi showed, essentially as a theorem, that if you accept the standard axioms of rational choice, you must maximize expected utility, and assigning equal probability to being each person, you get utilitarianism. Rawls refused to accept the axioms of rationality, so he ended up with maximin: choose as if you will definitely turn out to be the worst-off person in society. Maximin is really just the same thing as minimax regret, and it's motivated purely by extreme risk aversion -- Rawls basically assumes decision-makers are terrified of bad outcomes. Since real people aren't that risk-averse, and since Rawls had to deny that probability applies to the veil at all, Harsanyi's version is the only one compatible with decision theory."

Some of this matches what I remember (the veil, the equal-probability bit in Harsanyi), but a few things smell off to me.
