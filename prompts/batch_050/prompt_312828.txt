I know the usual theorems (Blackwell-Dubins merging, Doob consistency) are on my side, and the lab's resident subjectivist keeps telling me I don't understand what the theorems actually say. So: what would count against my position? I don't want a generic "priors matter" speech, I want a map of my exposure. Which assumptions the convergence results actually lean on (absolute continuity, shared likelihoods, the i.i.d. setup), what the world or the practice of science would have to look like for each of those to fail, and which of those failures are things that plausibly obtain in real inquiry rather than just logically possible countermodels.

A few things I'm specifically unsure about: whether the "almost surely with respect to your own prior" qualification in Doob's result is a devastating circularity or a technicality my opponents overplay; whether a prior assigning zero probability to a region of parameter space is a fatal counterexample or just an injunction to not be dogmatic, which my position can absorb by adding a clause; and whether the realistic divergence in likelihoods across researchers (different background theory, different ideas about what would even count as evidence) dissolves my position into the trivial claim that people with the same evidence and same likelihoods and compatible priors agree. Rank the defeaters from fatal to merely awkward, say which ones a careful defender of washout would themselves concede as real limitations versus dismiss as confusion, and tell me honestly whether the position survives only by being qualified into something much weaker than what I've been arguing at lab meeting -- and whether that should bother me.
