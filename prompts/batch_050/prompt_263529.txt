I need help drafting a memo and I want the reasoning in it to actually hold up, not just sound confident.

Background: I'm a backend engineer at a mid-sized ecommerce company and somehow ended up on our "AI incident review panel" (five people: two eng, one legal, a support lead, a PM). Our job is to classify chatbot incidents under an internal taxonomy, and the classification has real teeth:

- Category D ("deceptive conduct"): triggers mandatory disclosure to every user who talked to the bot that month, a vendor audit, and compensation for anyone who relied on the output. It's reserved for cases where the system "misrepresents" things to users in a way analogous to a person misleading someone.
- Category E ("engineering defect"): treated as a bug, goes through the normal fix-and-verify pipeline, no disclosure.

The incident: a customer asked about the waranty on a backpack. The bot confidently described a "lifetime repair guarantee" that does not exist anywhere in our policies. The customer bought two backpacks partly on that basis and now wants the guarantee honored. Nobody disputes the facts.

Legal wants E: the bot has no beliefs or intentions, there's nothing it was trying to get away with, so calling it deception is confused — it's a defect, like a wrong price on the page. The support lead wants D, and she's been reading Frankfurt's On Bullshit. Her argument is that the bot is actually worse than a liar on Frankfurt's picture: a liar at least cares about the truth enough to track it and conceal it, while the bot is indifferent to whether what it says is true, it just produces whatever sounds right. So in her view "bullshit" is the correct label, and bullshit belongs in D because the whole point of D is indifference to the user ending up misled.

Draft the memo I'd circulate before Thursday's meeting (1–2 pages): recommend a category and a concrete course of action, including what we owe this specific customer, and give the strongest honest version of the losing side rather than a strawman I'd get torn apart for.

One thing I genuinely can't tell on my own: whether the lie/bullshit/error framing even applies to a system with no mental states at all, or whether we'd be importing a moral vocabulary that was built for people and just doesn't transfer. If you think the taxonomy itself is the confused part, or my summary of Frankfurt is off, say that plainly instead of politely working around it — a memo built on a bad premise is worse than no memo.
