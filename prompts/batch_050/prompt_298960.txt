Hey, so I'm writing up a chapter of my diss on self-representation and I'm stuck on something in Metzinger's self-model theory. As I understand it, his key claim is that the phenomenal self-model is "transparent," which means the system has full introspective access to its own representational machinery -- like the brain can literally watch the self-model being constructed from the inside. That seems like a really strong, empirically loaded commitment, and I'm trying to figure out how to test it.

If transparency really does mean that the construction of the self is introspectively accessible, then shouldn't trained meditators or at least very careful introspectors be able to directly observe the self-model being built in real time? I'm thinking I could use first-person contemplative reports as data either for or against the self-model theory. What would be the best experimental or phenomenological paradigm for getting subjects to catch the transparency of their self-model while it's actually running? Are there existing protocols where people report on the representational format of their experience rather than just its content? I feel like if nobody can actually introspect the construction process, that's a serious problem for Metzinger, and if some people can, that's a nice confirmation.
