I teach an upper-level seminar on conceptual engineering and am writing the final exam. Draft one essay question on ameliorative analysis that candidates could answer in 75 minutes and that cannot be answered by recounting Haslanger's distinction between manifest, operative, and target concepts. The question should force candidates to take a stand on the continuity problem: if an ameliorative project replaces a defective concept (say, our ordinary concept of woman or of race) with a purpose-built successor, in virtue of what is the successor still a revision of that concept rather than a new concept introduced under an old label? I want the question to require a criterion for successful revision -- something that says what must be preserved across the change (extensions? inferential role? the practical function the concept served?) -- and to make candidates apply their criterion to the worry that ameliorating a concept that partly constitutes people's social identities risks changing the subject on the very people the project was meant to help. Give a model answer and a marking scheme formatted as a numbered list of exactly five must-hit points, each stating the point, its marks out of 20, and the most common way candidates fumble it.
