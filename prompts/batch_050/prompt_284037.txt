I am a graduate student in philosophy drafting a short paper, and I believe I have found a clean internal refutation of David Lewis's modal realism as defended in On the Plurality of Worlds. The argument runs as follows. Lewis holds that absolutely every way a world could possibly be is instantiated by some concrete, spatiotemporally unified world, and that all of these worlds are equally real, none ontologically privileged over the others. But one way a world could be is: being the only world there is. So, by Lewis's own lights, there exists a world at which modal realism is false — a world with nothing whatsoever beyond it. His theory therefore guarantees the existence of a circumstance in which it is false; and since he insists on the ontological parity of all worlds, he cannot privilege the worlds where the theory happens to hold over the one where it does not. Either modal realism is true at every world or it stands refuted; it is not true at every world; so it stands refuted. I call this the isolation argument.
