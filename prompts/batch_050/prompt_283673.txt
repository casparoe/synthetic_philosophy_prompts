I help run a pub theology group (don't laugh) and next month I'm presenting my working position on God and free will, which is basically Molinism as I've pieced it together from podcasts and a couple of popular books. Before I get torn apart by the one bloke who did a theology degree, I want to check the package is actually coherent. Here's what I hold:

1. People have real free will, at least sometimes. When I picked the fish over the pie at lunch I genuinely could have gone the other way, and God didn't make me choose either way.
2. God has middle knowledge: before creating anything he knew, for every possible person in every possible situation, what they would freely do, and he used that when deciding which world to actualise.
3. There are no free-floating truths. If something is true, something in reality has to make it true.
4. God is perfectly loving and wants every individual person to be reconcilled to him, not just a good overall score.
5. Some people are in fact lost. I'm not a universalist.
6. God is not to blame for anyone's sin. He permits it, but the sinning is down to us.
7. Prayer really makes a difference: there are things God does because we asked that he wouldn't have done otherwise.

Which of these genuinely pull against each other? Give me the pairs or trios in real tension, ranked by how serious each one is, and say what the cheapest fix would be and what it costs. Also flag any that look tense but are actually fine once you think them through, because I'd rather not concede ground I don't need to. I honestly don't know where the weak joint is, so please don't just mark everything as a problem.
