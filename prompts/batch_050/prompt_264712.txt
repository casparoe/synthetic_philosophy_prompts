Okay so I write fiction, mostly quiet domestic stuff, but the novel I'm drafting right now has wandered into territory I'm not philosophically equipped for. One of my characters is a mid-level logistics manager at a poultry company, and the book keeps circling the question of whether anything he does -- signing off on orders, eating the product himself, skipping a vote -- actually matters morally, given that if he quit tomorrow the machine would run exactly the same. My friend who's a PhD student calls this the inefficacy objection and says there's a whole literature, but I don't want the literature yet. What I want is the spec first: before I let any theory tell me what my character's obligations are, I want to know what an adequate theory of individual responsibility for collective harms would even have to deliver.

So: write me the desiderata list. What cases must it get right -- the single chicken purchase, the vote in a national election, the long drive that adds imperceptible emissions, but also the weirder structural ones, like the firing-squad case where ten people shoot and any nine would have sufficed, or the "trigger" cases where one more order is exactly what tips the supplier into another production run? What distinctions can't it collapse -- actual difference made versus expected difference, being a cause versus being complicit, my act being wrong versus my act being harmless because someone else would've done it? And what results from just, like, arithmetic and probability does it have to stay consistent with? Give me the case or intuition behind each requirement, tell me which ones are non-negotiable and which are the hobbyhorse of one particular camp, and -- this is the part I care most about for plotting purposes -- flag any pair of desiderata you suspect can't be jointly satisfied, because if the spec is unsatisfiable that's basically a plot point for me.

If you want, at the end you can briefly run the two or three main families of views against the list (expected-difference consequentialism, some kind of complicity/participation view, whatever else), but keep that short -- I'd rather have the criteria done properly than a scorecard. Also tell me which single requirement eliminates the most contenders, since that's probably where my character's central self-deception is going to live. Casual is fine, definately don't need citations or anything.
