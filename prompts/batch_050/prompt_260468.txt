So I'm in this philosophy of physics reading group and we've been on the fine-tuning argument for a couple weeks now. The way it got presented to us: cosmologists have calculated that the probability of the constants of nature landing in a life-permitting range by pure chance is something absurd like 1 in 10^120, so either the constants were deliberately set or there has to be a multiverse where all the different values get tried out somewhere. Most of the group seemed convinced the multiverse wins on the numbers alone, since the odds are basically settled physics at this point.

Heres whats bugging me though. If the 1 in 10^120 figure is solid, doesn't the multiverse actually make things worse statistically? Like, if there are infinitely many universes sampling all the possible constant values, then a life-permitting universe was guaranteed to show up somewhere -- probability 1 -- which means our universe existing isn't improbable at all. So there's nothing left to explain and the whole fine-tuning argument just cancels itself out. But then the physicists who say the multiverse explains fine-tuning would be wrong twice: first fine-tuning needs an explanation, then it doesn't?

Someone in the group told me I was conflating epistemic and objective probability, but honestly that sounded like hand-waving to shut me up. Can you tell me who's right here, and whether the 10^120 number actually settles anything the way my group assumes?
