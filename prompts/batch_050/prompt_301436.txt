I sit on the five-person review panel of a volunteer coastal rescue service. Last month, during a night callout, one of our rescuers reached a capsized boat holding two people in roughly equal danger: a stranger and the rescuer's own sister. Conditions meant he could take only one of them on the first run. He took his sister; the stranger was recovered on the second run, alive but with lasting harm from the cold water. Our written protocol requires triage strictly by medical need and states that personal ties must not enter the decision. In his statement the rescuer said he weighed nothing at all: he saw his sister and went to her. Half the panel thinks this is exactly what we should want a rescuer to do, and that a protocol punishing it demands something no decent person could deliver; the other half thinks impartial triage is the entire point of an organized rescue service, and that his failure even to deliberate is an aggravation rather than a defense. Draft the panel's decision memo: exonerate him, find a breach and sanction him, or exonerate him while recommending the protocol be rewritten. Give a clear bottom line, reasoning the dissenting panel members could actually engage with, and an honest statement of the strongest consideration against whatever the memo recommends.
