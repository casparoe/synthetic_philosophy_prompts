Next month I’m presenting to a workshop of industrial-organisation economists and competition lawyers, most of whom know little about AI alignment, a paper arguing that AI labs’ highly visible safety commitments can intensify a commitment race: once rival firms treat restraint as a signalling strategy, each has incentives to make increasingly specific promises while quietly narrowing their scope, externalising the costs, or redefining when the commitments apply. My claim is not that commitments never work, but that voluntary safety commitments can select for firms that are best at making them strategically rather than best at complying with them.

Prepare me for the questions this audience is most likely to ask. Give roughly ten objections, ordered by likelihood and potential damage, phrased as an industrial-organisation economist or competition lawyer would phrase them. For each, give a two-minute reply, what I should concede, and where the reply is weakest. Distinguish objections about adverse selection, cheap talk, incomplete contracts, principal-agent problems, regulatory capture, and antitrust. Include the objection I should most hope nobody asks. Please write in British English.
