I recently started a job at a software company whose ad-targeting tools, I have learned, are also licensed to an agency that uses them for mass surveillance I consider wrongful. My own work is on the advertising side, and the licensing deals would proceed with or without me, so my colleagues and I keep circling the question of whether ordinary participation in an enterprise makes one complicit in wrongs one neither causes nor intends. Draft the joint statement that an adversarial collaboration between two camps could produce. The first holds a difference-making view: an agent is complicit in an outcome only if their contribution actually makes a difference to it, so purely overdetermined or counterfactual-dependent contributions are blameless in themselves. The second holds a participation view: one can be complicit simply by knowingly and intentionally contributing one's part to a collective venture one recognizes as wrongful, regardless of whether the outcome is overdetermined. The statement should contain: a numbered list of claims both camps can sign, including what each grants the other about overdetermination cases and about the limits of its own view; a disagreements table stating each remaining dispute in wording its own side would accept; the cruxes -- for instance, whether complicity tracks outcomes at all or instead the character of one's contribution, and whether diffuse, highly substitutable roles inside large organizations dilute responsibility; and, for each crux, at least one concrete case (such as a firing squad, a vote cast in a landslide, or a mid-level employee in a bureaucracy) that both sides agree bears on it, with each side's verdict on that case recorded in advance.
