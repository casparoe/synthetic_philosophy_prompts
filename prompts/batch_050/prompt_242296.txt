Is there an established name for the following pattern? Two analysts begin with sharply different views about some process and then both revise their beliefs in the light of the same accumulating evidence — say, a shared feed of daily measurements. Unsurprisingly, their predictions about next week's measurements steadily converge, in line with the familiar mathematical results to the effect that agents who see the same data eventually agree, whatever they started out believing. What strikes me is that the convergence is oddly selective. Ask them about anything the evidence cannot directly settle — what the process would do under an intervention that has never been tried, or some feature of the underlying mechanism that would leave the same signature in every possible observation — and they remain as far apart as ever, even after years of identical data. I have noticed the same shape in informal settings: two investors whose forecasts of next quarter's figures become nearly indistinguishable while their convictions about the company's fundamental character stay poles apart, or two doctors who agree completely about which treatments have worked so far but disagree permanently about why. So the washing-out of initial disagreement seems to proceed along exactly those dimensions the evidence can touch, and to stall completely along the rest. Is this selective, dimension-by-dimension convergence a recognized phenomenon with a name of its own, or is it merely an obvious corollary of the standard convergence theorems that nobody has bothered to christen? Please identify the nearest established concepts, explain precisely how the pattern I describe differs from each, and — if none of them fits — propose a name, a definition, and a few boundary cases. At most 250 words.
