I cover science and ideas for a general-interest magazine, and I'm fact-checking a scene from a piece I'm writing. At a workshop dinner I listened to two researchers argue about a puzzle one of them had posed: a wealthy patron offers a $1,000,000 prize, to be divided equally among however many people submit an entry by the deadline; entering costs nothing but a few minutes, and anyone may enter. Here's the exchange as I recorded it:

The first researcher said the rational move is not to enter. Everyone who thinks hard about this puzzle is reasoning the same way she is, so the only realistic outcomes are "nearly all of us enter" and "nearly all of us abstain" — and in the first outcome the prize is diluted to pocket change, while abstaining at least leaves the prize intact for someone. The second researcher replied that this confuses correlation with causation: her entering doesn't make anyone else enter, so she should reason about her own entry's marginal effect, which is to add one to the denominator — and if most others abstain, she walks away with a fortune.

The first shot back that the second was proving her point: if everyone reasons "the others will abstain, so I'll take the whole pot," everyone enters and the pot is worthless — the abstention verdict is the only one that doesn't self-destruct when universalized. The second answered that this cuts the other way: universal abstention is equally unstable, since it leaves $1,000,000 on the table that any single deviator could claim, so a rule that tells everyone to abstain is just as self-undermining as one that tells everyone to enter.

Neither conceded. For the piece, I need a careful referee's report: who is right about each specific claim — whether reasoning shared across agents makes "choose as your twins will choose" a legitimate constraint, whether the causation objection actually defeats it, and whether each side's preferred policy really is self-undermining when adopted universally — and is there anything (say, mixed strategies, or a distinction between what's rational for me and what's rational for us) that both of them are missing?
