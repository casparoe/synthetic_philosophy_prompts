I'm putting together a reading seminar on natural kinds for a mixed group of philosophers and working scientists, and I've noticed that the literature talks past itself in a frustrating way: essentialists, cluster-kind theorists, pluralists, and eliminativists about species all seem to be answering slightly different questions while claiming to answer the same one. Before we read any of them, I want to pin down what a theory of natural kinds is even supposed to do -- what it has to deliver in order to count as a serious answer to the question of whether the sciences carve nature at its joints, rather than a stipulation dressed up as a discovery. So: write the desiderata list. For each requirement, give the case or consideration that motivates it, and indicate whether you take it to be non-negotiable or merely the preference of one camp.

The cases I expect the list to be tested against include at least the following: chemical elements as the paradigmatic success story; jade, which turned out to be two distinct minerals (jadeite and nephrite) under one vernacular label; "tree," which is not a clade and yet seems to do real work in biology; biological species, given ring species and asexually reproducing organisms; and psychiatric categories, where everyone seems to want the kind to be real enough to guide research but no one thinks it carves like gold does. I'd also like the list forced to confront a case that gets less attention: kinds that exist only because scientific practice made them -- standardized units, model organisms, diagnostic categories that change the people so classified. Does being partly invented disqualify a category from being natural, or is that demand itself a prejudice the list should expose rather than encode? Among the distinctions I'd want any candidate theory forbidden from collapsing: real versus natural; naturalness as binary versus coming in degrees; kinds versus the properties in virtue of which things fall under them; and mind-independence versus interest-relativity.

Two further asks. First, identify any subset of the desiderata that you think cannot be jointly satisfied -- for instance, I have a suspicion that a strict mind-independence requirement and adequacy to the classificatory practice of the special sciences pull apart, and if the list itself turns out to be an impossibility result, I want that stated plainly rather than smoothed over. Second, briefly run three contenders -- microstructural essentialism, homeostatic property cluster views, and a thoroughgoing promiscuous pluralism -- against the list, but keep this scoring short; the criteria are the deliverable, not the verdicts. And please push back where warranted: if my framing presupposes something confused -- say, that there must be one theory covering fundamental physics, biology, and psychiatry alike, or that "carving at the joints" is a coherent metaphor to begin with -- say so directly instead of politely complying, since a seminar built on a bad spec is worse than no seminar at all.
