I help run a small nonprofit that does drowning prevention and rescue on a chain of lakes near where I live. We've got about $400k a year to spend, and the board is deadlocked over how to split it, and I honestly can't tell who's right.

Option A is our rescue boat program. Fast boats, trained crews, stationed at the busiest beaches on summer weekends. Over the past few years they've pulled maybe 15 people out of the water who would have drowned otherwise. These are people we know by name afterwards. We've met their families, they come to the fundraiser, the local paper runs the story. It's also, not coincidentally, why people donate to us at all. Nobody gives money for hypotheticals.

Option B is the boring stuff: free life jacket loaner stations at boat ramps, fencing off the two most dangerous quarry swimmimg spots, mandatory-swim-lesson subsidies for kids, signage in a bunch of languages. Our public health consultant ran the numbers and figures this package would save something like 6 or 7 lives a year versus the boat's 3. But here's the thing that keeps tripping the board up: if it works, nothing happens. No rescue, no story, no family thanking us. Some kid just... doesn't drown, and nobody ever knows it was going to be them. We can't even prove the counterfactual in any individual case.

Half the board says the math is the math, seven beats three, fund B. The other half says the boat crew saves *actual specific people we can point to*, while the prevention numbers are model estimates resting on a pile of assumptions, and that it's a weird kind of arrogance to trade away people you can see for people in a spreadsheet. One guy also points out that if we cut the boat, donations dry up within a couple years and then we can't fund *anything*, so the boat indirectly pays for prevention too.

I keep going back and forth. Is there actually a morally relevant difference between the drowning person you can see and the one who never shows up in anyone's records? And how much should the epistemic messiness -- the fact that B's numbers are estimates and A's are names and faces -- count against just doing what saves more people? Also is the fundraising argument a good one or is it a rationalization? Would love a careful take, not just "do the math."
