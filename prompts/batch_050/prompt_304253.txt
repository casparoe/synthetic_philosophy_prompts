I am taking a philosophy of mind course, and we have just covered functionalism and multiple realizability. Both my textbook and several classmates lean hard on the software/hardware analogy: the mind is to the brain as a program is to the computer that runs it. They use this to argue two conclusions: first, that the mental is in principle independent of any particular material, so a suitably organized machine (or octopus, or alien) could have a mind just like ours; second, that studying the brain's wetware is no more relevant to understanding the mind than studying silicon is to understanding a spreadsheet. Please audit the analogy. Give me the explicit correspondence table -- what plays the role of the program, the hardware, the code, the programmer, running the same program on different machines -- then identify where the mapping breaks down. I especially want you to address: whether the analogy smuggles in a sharp implementation/implemented distinction that may not exist in brains; whether programs are individuated independently of their physical realization in a way mental states plausibly are not; and what the analogy quietly assumes about the irrelevance of the substrate's intrinsic properties. Finally, take a definite position: state which of the two conclusions, if either, actually survives once the disanalogies are on the table, give your reasons rather than presenting the options neutrally, and if the analogy fails, propose a better one for capturing multiple realizability.
