Ok so I need a sanity check after a very long argument with my freind last night (she's doing a PhD in phil of bio and is extremely confident that selection history is basically the whole story when it comes to biological function). We got absolutely nowhere, and I realised the problem might be that my own views don't hang together. Here's roughly what I believe:

1. Malfunction is real. A heart that can't pump blood is defective, and that's not just a statistical claim about what's common — medicine gets to say an organ is failing, full stop.
2. Swampman has functions. A molecule-for-molecule duplicate of me assembled by a freak lightning strike has a heart whose function is to pump blood, and it's malfunctioning if it can't. My friend bit the bullet and said his heart has no functions whatsoever, which I find genuinely deranged.
3. First-generation traits can have functions. When a bacterium first acquires a mutation that produces an enzyme neutralising an antibiotic, that enzyme's function is to degrade the antibiotic — it doesn't have to sit around for a few rounds of selection before it 'counts' as having one.
4. Pumping is the heart's function, making noise isn't. Heart sounds are diagnostically handy, sure, but they're not among the heart's functions, and any account that can't deliver this verdict is dead on arrival.
5. Ecology talk is legit. When biologists say the function of a keystone predator is to keep prey populations in check, that's a genuine function attribution, not a loose metaphor.
6. Function claims genuinely explain. Saying the heart is there in order to pump blood explains why organisms have hearts — it's not just shorthand for a purely causal story.

So: which of these actually pull against each other, and which only look like they do? If there's an inconsistent subset, tell me exactly which commitments are doing the damage, what the cheapest repair is in each case and what it costs me. Rank the problems by severity rather than flagging everything as equally fatal. And don't just tell me to drop 2 because my friend said so — she also thinks all talk of things being 'for' anything is spooky vestigial teleology, so I've learned to discount her intuitions on this.
