So I'm a novelist (literary fiction mostly, but I dabble) and I keep running into this phrase at conventions and in writers' group chats: "the universe is a dark forest." I read the Liu Cixin book a few years back so I know where it comes from, but people now deploy it way outside the book, like it's an established answer to things. Last week a guy at a panel said it to shut down a question about why we haven't heard from anyone -- "dark forest, done, next question" -- and someone else used it to argue we should stop broadcasting anything into space ever, and a third person used it to mean something about human nature in general, like we're all hiding from each other in here too.

What bugs me is that it feels like it could be claiming like five different things and people slide between them mid-sentence. Is it a game theory claim (any two civilizations that meet can't trust each other, so shooting first is rational)? Is it an epistemic claim (we literally cannot know others' intentions at interstellar distances, and that's what forces the conclusion)? Is it a tech claim (destruction is cheap and defense is impossible, so power imbalances get settled violently)? Is it just an observation that the sky is quiet and here's one story that fits? Is it a claim about what *any* intelligent life must be like, or only about what happens *if* certain assumptions hold? Because some of those are interesting and some are basically "imagine everyone's paranoid, then everyone acts paranoid," which is circular, and some are empirical guesses dressed up as logic.

Can you lay out the distinct things the slogan could actually be asserting, roughly from weakest/trivial to strongest? For each one: is it defensible, what does it assume, and does it actually support the way people use it -- especially the "so that's why the sky is silent, case closed" move and the "so we should never transmit" move? I'd like to be able to tell, next time someone says it at a panel, which version they're running and whether it earns the shrug it came with. Also if the strongest interesting version turns out to need some premise that's doing all the work, name the premise.
