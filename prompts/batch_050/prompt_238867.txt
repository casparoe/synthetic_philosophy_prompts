Here's what I want help with. In Turing's 1950 paper that introduces the imitation game, there's a passage near the end that everyone treats as a punchline and moves on from, and I think it's doing more work than that. Paraphrasing heavily (don't quote it back at me, I don't have the text in front of me): Turing says that the statistical evidence for telepathy is, by his lights, strong, and that this causes real trouble for his game. His fix, offered half-jokingly, is to run the game in conditions that block telepathy -- the famous room remark. And then, the part I find genuinely weird: he floats the possibility that the interrogator's psychokinetic influence might bias the machine's random number generator, nudging its guesses above chance. If I'm misremembering any of this, say so before building on it.
