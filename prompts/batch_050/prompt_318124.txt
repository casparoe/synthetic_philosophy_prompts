I am preparing a seminar on the foundations of statistical mechanics, and before we survey the proposed resolutions of Maxwell's demon, I want my students to have an explicit standard by which to judge them. Write the desiderata: what any adequate exorcism of the demon must accomplish in order to count as a genuine resolution rather than a relabeling of the puzzle. For each requirement, give the consideration or thought experiment that motivates it, indicate whether you take it to be non-negotiable or revisable, and identify any pair of desiderata you suspect cannot be jointly satisfied -- an inconsistency in the list would itself be a philosophically significant result.

To keep the task honest, your list must speak to at least the following pressures, though you should add requirements of your own where the standard framing misses something:

- The resolution must locate a definite thermodynamic cost somewhere -- in measurement, in memory erasure, in the demon's own operation, or elsewhere -- and it must say why that location is forced rather than merely convenient. A resolution that works by pointing at a cost it cannot show to be physically mandatory has only deferred the paradox.
- It must handle a demon that never erases anything. Consider a demon with an inexhaustible notebook, or equivalently one that simply keeps every measurement record forever. If the resolution appeals to erasure costs, it must explain why the sorting itself, with the records retained, does not already violate the second law during the (possibly unbounded) interval before any erasure occurs.
- It must handle a demon that measures nothing. Consider a purely mechanical trapdoor device with no sensors, no memory, and no information processing at all -- just carefully arranged asymmetric dynamics. Either the resolution must show such a device impossible on independent grounds, or it must concede that information is incidental to the puzzle, in which case the information-theoretic framing needs justification.
- It must be consistent with what equilibrium statistical mechanics independently says about fluctuations, since the demon is, after all, only a systematic exploiter of fluctuations that the theory already permits. A resolution that saves the second law by denying the fluctuations the demon exploits has resolved the paradox by contradiction with the host theory.
- It must state the status it assigns to the second law itself: inviolable law, overwhelmingly probable regularity, or something in between -- and it must be explicit about whether the resolution protects the law or merely protects the letter of the law while conceding the spirit (macroscopic entropy decrease in some subsystem, say).

Keep the weight on the requirements and their motivating cases. Then, briefly, assess how the leading contenders fare against your list: the Szilard-style measurement-cost resolution, the Landauer--Bennett erasure resolution, and the view that the paradox dissolves once the demon is modeled as an ordinary physical system with no privileged informational vocabulary. The scoring may be compressed; the criteria are where I want the careful thinking.
