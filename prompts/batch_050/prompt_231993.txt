So my local discussion group (mostly people who originally met through SSC/ACX-adjacent meetups, we still argue about the same stuff) has drifted from books to films, and next up is Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind. I got voluntold to lead the session, and I want to run it through the ethics-of-memory debate I've been chewing on lately. Quick sketch of the two poles as I understand them, so we're working from the same picture: on one side, Avishai Margalit's idea that memory is a genuine duty, because remembering people and shared events is partly constitutive of "thick" relationships and communities — so forgetting isn't neutral, it's a slow betrayal or an erosion of who you even are. On the other side, David Rieff's case for forgetting: that memory, especially collective memory, keeps old wounds festering and feeds grievance spirals, and sometimes the healthiest and most humane thing a person or a society can do is deliberately let go.

The film seems like an almost suspiciously perfect test case, and I want a serious reading of it through this lens. Joel and Clementine both choose erasure, the procedure is framed as somewhere between medical consumer choice and self-mutilation, and the ending, with the tapes and the decision to try again anyway, seems to say something about whether the painful memories were load-bearing. Specific things I'm chewing on: does Lacuna's procedure count as a Margalitian betrayal of self, or does it fall outside his framework because it's voluntary and private rather than communal? Mary finding out she erased her own affair seems like the strongest evidence for Rieff's position blowing up in practice, is that right? And the finale: is choosing to proceed while knowing how it went last time a refutation of Rieff, or actually a concession to him dressed up as romance? Also, does the film cheat? The whole Montauk thing means the memories are never fully gone, so maybe its answer only works because it doesn't take real erasure seriously, and a truly Rieffian universe wouldn't give Joel that escape hatch. Push back on my framing wherever it's flattened the two thinkers — but work from the positions as sketched, not from citations I can't look up. Skip the plot recap, everyone in the group has seen it twice. And since I'll be presenting this to a room full of people who will pounce on any weak spot: tell me how confident you are in the reading overall, and single out the one claim you're least sure about so I know where the soft underbelly is.
