I'm a third-year PhD student writing my dissertation on the unity of the proposition, and I've reached the point in the chapter on Bradley's regress where I need to convince my committee that my own proposal isn't just one of the old moves in new clothing. So I've been cataloguing the standard responses to the regress as they appear in the literature, and I'd like you to help me stress-test the catalogue. Here is what I have so far: (1) the appeal to internal relations -- the regress is blocked because the relation of instantiation is internal to its relata, so no further relating relation is needed; (2) Frege's move that concepts are unsaturated and so connect to objects without any glue at all, with the regress itself arising only from our misleading nominalized way of speaking about unsaturated entities; (3) the trope-theoretic response, which relocates the unity problem into particularized properties so that no universal-instantiation link is required in the first place; (4) the states-of-affairs response, which takes facts as the fundamental unities and treats propositions as derivative on them rather than as complexes needing to be glued together; (5) Bradley's own idealist conclusion, accepting the regress as vicious and inferring that discursive, relational thought misrepresents an undivided reality; and (6) the deflationary response that the regress is harmless -- the infinite series of relating relations is generated automatically and benignly, in the way that the series of truths that p, that it is true that p, and so on is generated, and poses no threat to any single fact's unity.

What I want from you is a seventh response to Bradley's regress that is genuinely different from these six: not a rewording, not a special case of one of them, and not a mere combination of two. I realize this is asking for something that may not exist in the literature, and I'm fine with that -- a novel proposal is acceptable, arguably preferable, since if it were canonical I would already have found it. The proposal should engage the regress at the level of propositions or facts (not just shift to philosophy of mind or language without explanation of how the shift bears on the unity problem), and it should say something about what makes a collection of constituents into one unified whole rather than a mere heap.

Please write your answer as continuous prose, the way you would in an email or a letter: no headings, no bullet points, no bold text, no numbered lists, no tables. First present the seventh response itself, explaining how it blocks or dissolves the regress and why it doesn't collapse into any of the six on my list -- I want you to check each one explicitly, in prose, since that's the comparison my committee will demand. Then give me your honest assessment of whether the proposal is actually any good: what it costs, which of the six it most nearly resembles under pressure, and whether you think it survives the obvious first objection a sympathetic-but-skeptical examiner would raise.
