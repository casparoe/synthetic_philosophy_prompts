Draft the joint statement of an adversarial collaboration between a committed objective Bayesian and a committed subjective Bayesian on the question of whether there are rationally required priors. The objective Bayesian holds that, given only certain information, some probability assignments are rationally privileged over others -- for instance, that an agent told nothing about a die except that it has a long-run mean of 4.5 ought to adopt the maximum-entropy distribution consistent with that constraint, rather than any distribution that happens to cohere with her preferences. The subjective Bayesian holds that rationality constrains only coherence, conditionalization, and perhaps calibration to known chances, and that any prior satisfying these is as legitimate as any other; the demand for a uniquely correct prior is a holdover from logical theories of probability that collapsed under their own paradoxes.

Structure the statement as follows. First, a numbered section of agreed claims: I want substantive items both parties can sign, not platitudes -- candidates worth testing for joint signature include the adequacy of the probability axioms as coherence constraints, the lesson each side draws from Bertrand-style paradoxes (where uniform over one parametrization is non-uniform over another, as with the side versus the volume of a cube), the status of symmetry arguments when the symmetries are physical rather than epistemic, the role of long-run frequencies as evidence that forces posterior convergence regardless of prior, and whether both sides can agree that maximum entropy is at least a useful heuristic without agreeing it is a rational requirement. State each agreed claim precisely enough that it excludes something. Second, a disagreements table with four columns: the exact point of dispute, the objective Bayesian's formulation in wording she would accept, the subjective Bayesian's formulation in wording he would accept, and the crux or cruxes on which it turns. Third, for each crux, an argument, theorem, or thought experiment both sides accept as bearing on it, with each side's prediction recorded in advance -- for example, whether the wine-and-water problem (a liquid known only to be a mixture with the water-to-wine ratio between 1/2 and 2) shows that no transformation-invariant prior exists, or merely that the wrong invariance was imposed; and whether exchangeability results in the style of de Finetti dissolve the need for objective priors or presuppose a symmetry that is itself objective.

If some of their disagreement strikes you as verbal -- say, over what 'rational' or 'prior' is being used to mean -- identify which part and say why, rather than forcing it into the table as a substantive dispute. Close with a brief self-audit: flag the agreed claim you think is most likely to be rejected by an actual partisan of one camp, note where your own drafting most plausibly smuggled in a slanted formulation, and state what specific argument or result -- for instance, a compelling uniqueness theorem for maxent, or a demonstration that constraint-satisfaction underdetermines the distribution -- would require the whole statement to be redrafted.
