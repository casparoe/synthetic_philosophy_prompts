A colleague and I were discussing the foundations of welfare economics, and I made a point I'd like you to check and, if sound, help me extend. Since Samuelson's revealed preference program, I take it that utility has no independent psychological content: a utility function is just a convenient numerical representation of a consistent preference ordering, and saying someone maximizes utility is another way of saying their choices satisfy the standard consistency axioms. It follows, I argued, that the common behavioral-economics claim that people 'choose against their own utility' -- in present bias, framing effects, or failures of transitivity -- is literally self-contradictory, because whatever a person chooses is by definition what maximizes their utility. My colleague replied that this proves too much, but she couldn't say where the inference goes wrong. Can you identify precisely which step in my reasoning fails, and explain what, if anything, utility functions and revealed preferences actually entitle us to say about people's welfare when their choices are inconsistent?
