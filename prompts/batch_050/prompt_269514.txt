I am giving the after-dinner speech at the leaving party for the founder of our university’s AI and society centre: half the audience are philosophers of mind who still have strong views about the Chinese Room, and half are engineers, administrators and family who mainly know that the centre built systems that answer questions very convincingly. Write a six-minute speech in which the Chinese Room argument does the organising work: it must determine what may be toasted as achievement, what must not be casually described as understanding, and how to praise the founder’s intellectual honesty without either announcing that the argument is obviously sound or obviously refuted. Include one moment of lightness that both audiences can share, and end with a sentence that would still be true if the system-level reply turns out to be right.
