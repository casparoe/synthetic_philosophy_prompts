Here's something I've been chewing on for my freedom seminar. Berlin gave us the two concepts, Pettit and Skinner brought back the republican third, and every textbook example does the same boring trick: the contented slave of a kindly master (republican unfree, negative free), the addict (positive unfree, negative free), etc. Students can recite these in their sleep.

So: construct me a thought experiment with THREE characters in a single shared scenario -- a small town, a workplace, a hospital, whatever, but one connected setting, not three vignettes stapled together -- where each character is free on exactly ONE of the three conceptions (negative, positive, republican) and unfree on the other two, and each conception gets its own exemplar. So one character who's the negative-liberty case, one who's the positive-liberty case, one who's the republican case.

Constraints:
- Realistic. No hypnosis, no wireheading, no brain lesions, no cults. Addiction is allowed for at most one character and honestly I'd prefer zero, it's a crutch.
- The hard one: the republican exemplar has to be republicanly free without that just collapsing into being negatively free too. Most attempts fail here. Likewise the negative exemplar has to be negatively free without that accidentally making them republicanly free -- so you'll need a source of dominating power hanging over them that never actually gets exercised, and it has to be believable, not some cartoonish villain.
- No character's unfreedom should depend on them being obviously irrational or deluded. I want the cases to be genuinely contestable, not rigged.

After the scenario, do the bookkeeping: for each character, walk through why each of the three conceptions delivers the verdict it does, and name the specific feature of their situation that does the work for each verdict (the thing that, if you removed it, would flip the verdict). Then the honest part: for each character, give me the strongest objection a defender of one of the "wrong" conceptions would raise -- i.e., the move someone would make to argue your character is actually free (or unfree) on a conception you said they fail. If one of the three characters turns out to be impossible to build cleanly and you have to fudge something, tell me which one and why you think that is -- I have my own suspicians about which character will give you trouble and I'm curious whether we agree.
