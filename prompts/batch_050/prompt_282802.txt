I sit on the model-change review board of an independent AI auditing consortium, and I need the written opinion I will circulate before Thursday's vote. Last year the board certified a deployed model after a full mechanistic audit: our auditors localized the internal computation responsible for the model's refusals of dangerous requests, identified the components and features involved, and verified that a monitoring probe reading those internals flags the relevant reasoning with 97% accuracy. The lab now proposes a successor produced by continued fine-tuning, with no architectural change. On every behavioral evaluation we can run, the new model is indistinguishable from the old. But the internals have drifted: the monitor's accuracy on the successor's activations has fallen to 81%, causal-tracing experiments suggest the refusal behavior now depends partly on different attention heads, and the features our auditors originally named are still present but wired together differently. A full re-audit would take three months. The lab argues this would be absurd on principle: by that standard no mechanistic audit could ever survive a single gradient update, and behavioral equivalence plus training continuity should suffice for certification. Our charter, however, permits us to certify an audit as transferring to a successor only if the successor operates by 'the same mechanism,' a phrase the charter nowhere defines. Draft the board's opinion: recommend certification, full re-audit, or certification conditional on a specified equivalence test; supply a workable standard of mechanism-identity the board can reuse in future cases, stated at whatever level of abstraction you judge the safety claim to actually require; and engage the lab's continuity argument as seriously as it deserves rather than dismissing it.
