I'm auditing an old episode of a philosophy podcast I used to assign to my students, and I want a second opinion before I decide whether to keep recommending it. The episode covers personal identity, and the host summarizes Locke and Reid's famous exchange like this: "Locke held that personal identity consists in memory: you are simply whoever you remember being. Reid demolished this with a single case. Take an elderly general who remembers capturing a standard in battle as a young officer, and suppose that young officer remembered being flogged at school as a boy. The general, however, no longer remembers the flogging. By Locke's criterion the general is the officer, and the officer is the boy, but the general is not the boy -- and since identity is transitive, Locke's theory lands in a contradiction. Reid thereby showed that memory has nothing to do with who we are, and that personal identity must instead consist in the persistence of an immaterial soul. In the same stroke, Reid exposed the circularity at the heart of Locke's view: you can only count a memory as genuinely yours if you already know that you are the person who had the experience, so memory can't be used to define identity in the first place."

I have a decent grip on the brave officer case itself, and the host's setup of it sounds roughly like what I remember from the literature. But two things nag at me. First, the conclusion that memory has "nothing to do with" who we are feels stronger than what Reid's case could possibly deliver, and I have a vague sense Reid's own positive view was friendlier to memory than that. Second, I thought the circularity complaint was associated with a different critic of Locke entirely, and I'm not sure it belongs in a summary of Reid. On the other hand, this is a professionally produced show and I might be misremembering.

Please audit the summary piece by piece: which claims are accurate, which are defensible simplifications of views that are more hedged in the originals, and which are misrepresentations or misattributions?
