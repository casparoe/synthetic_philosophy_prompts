So I've been reading the literature on prioritarianism and the leveling-down objection and I honestly can't tell what the actual disagreement is anymore, and I want a diagnosis rather than another round of the arguments.

Quick setup so we're on the same page: prioritarians say benefiting people matters more the worse off those people are, full stop -- it's not about relations between people, just about weighting individual well-being by how much of it someone has. Egalitarians (the relational kind) say what matters is that people stand in relations of equality, that it's bad in itself if some are worse off than others through no fault of theirs. The leveling-down objection is then pressed against the egalitarians: take a world where everyone is at 100, compare it to a world where everyone is at 90, and the second world is perfectly equal while the first isn't (wait, that's a bad example, make the first world unequal: half at 100, half at 90). Bringing everyone down to 90 makes things better in one respect, on the egalitarian view, even though it's better for nobody and worse for some. Parfit and others take that to be absurd, or at least a serious cost; Temkin and others bite the bullet and say yes, there is something good about the leveled-down world, some respect in which it's better.

Here's what's bugging me. Everyone in this debate seems to agree on the structure of the cases. Nobody denies that leveling down helps nobody. The prioritarians say "there's no respect in which the equal-but-worse world is better," the egalitarians say "there is one respect in which it's better, just not one that benefits anyone," and then... nothing. It seems to me like both sides fully understand the other's position and just diverge on whether "good in some respect" has to mean "good for someone" or whether impersonal goodness without any beneficiary is even coherent. That looks less like a disagreement about equality and more like a disagreement about what goodness is -- the person-affecting restriction doing all the real work while everyone argues about equality as a proxy.

So my questions: Is my diagnosis right, that this is really a proxy war about whether all value has to be value-for-someone, with equality as the battleground? Or is it a genuinely substantive disagreement about equality itself that I'm flattening? Or is it partly verbal -- e.g., one side using "better in a respect" thinly (any pro tanto reason counts) and the other thickly? Use what each side actually commits to, not textbook summaries. And then: given your diagnosis, what would progress even look like here? Is there a distinction or a case that could actually force movement, or is this bedrock-intuition territory where we should just expect the stalemate to continue, and if so, say that plainly.
