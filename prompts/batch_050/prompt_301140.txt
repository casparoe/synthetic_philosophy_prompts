Write a short story engaging with dream skepticism in the tradition of Zhuangzi's butterfly dream -- the idea that upon waking from a vivid dream one may be unable to establish, even in principle, whether one was a person dreaming of being something else or that something else now dreaming of being a person. I'm a software engineer, so set it in that world: your protagonist is an engineer who builds "consistency tests" -- a suite of checks (clock behavior, text stability, physics probes) used to certify to clients that a given environment is base reality rather than a simulation. Midway through the story, have the tests pass in an environment the protagonist strongly suspects is a dream, forcing them to confront what the checks actually prove versus what they merely assume. End on the Zhuangzian turn: rather than resolving which side is the dream, the story should dramatize the "transformation of things" -- the possibility that the demand for a verdict between the two standpoints is itself the mistake. The story should have exactly five scenes, clearly separated, and no introduction or conclusion outside them.
