I'm a PhD student in political philosophy drafting the normative core of my dissertation on drug policy, and before I defend the prospectus I'd like a consistency audit of the position I've arrived at. My commitments are these:

(1) I accept a strong version of Mill's harm principle: the state may not use coercive law against competent adults for conduct that primarily harms only themselves. On this basis I hold that criminal prohibition of personal drug possession and use is unjust in principle, not merely counterproductive in practice.

(2) I endorse the Portuguese model as roughly the right framework for drugs other than cannabis: possession remains an administrative offense, users can be summoned before a dissuasion commission, noncompliance can attract fines or sanctions like being barred from certain venues, and the commissions' explicit purpose is to steer users toward treatment and away from use.

(3) For cannabis specifically, I support full legalization with a regulated commercial market, rather than mere decriminalization, largely on the grounds that criminal markets generate violence and corruption that legal markets don't.

(4) I oppose extending that legalization model to opioids and cocaine, and I want production and sale of those to remain criminal. My main reason is that commercial availability would predictably expand the population of addicted users and deepen the harms those users suffer.

(5) Methodologically, I hold that drug policy should be justified on deontological, rights-based grounds rather than by aggregating welfare outcomes — but I also routinely cite Portugal's post-2001 improvements in public-health indicators as evidence that the model I favor is vindicated.

Two questions. First, which pairs of these commitments are genuinely in tension, and which only appear to be? I'd like the tensions ranked by severity, and for each one the cheapest revision that resolves it, along with what that revision costs me elsewhere in the set. Second, if you had to pick a single revision that leaves the overall position most coherent, which would it be? I'd rather you not flag everything as equally problematic — part of what I want to learn is whether I'm helping myself to incompatible motivations rather than to incompatible theses.
