I teach a second-year political theory module, about 14 students, one 75-minute seminar. Next week is the week on the democratic boundary problem -- the fact that any democratic procedure presupposes an answer to who belongs to the demos, and that this answer can't itself be settled by a democratic vote without regress or circularity. On the page they find this a bit dry, so I want an activity where they crash into it themselves before anyone names it.

Here's the rough shape I have in mind, but restructure it if you can do better: the class plays some ongoing association (a hiking club, a tenants union, whatever works) that has to vote on a decision with real consequences for people outside the room -- merging with a rival group, say, or adopting a rule whose costs land on next year's members and on the neighbouring association that shares their meeting space. Before any vote can happen they have to fix the electorate, and the design should make it obvious that the candidate rules (current members only, everyone affected, everyone who'll be coerced by the outcome, everyone in the locality) each hand the win to a different side. Then I want the activity to iterate: each time they settle on an electorate and run the vote, I drop in new information showing the losing rule would have flipped the result, and eventually they have to vote on which electorate-rule to use -- which of course requires its own electorate, and I want them to notice that live.

Give me: concrete rules and materials, a timeline for the 75 minutes, the actual script for the moments where the new information drops, and a debrief that maps what happened onto the standard candidate principles (all-affected-interests, all-coerced, residence-based membership) -- but they shouldn't hear those names until after they've reinvented at least two of them. Also tell me what will predictably go wrong the first time I run it (I suspect someone will suggest handing the question to a neutral arbiter within five minutes and I need a good way to cash that out rather than just forbid it), and be honest about which feature of the real problem the game can't reproduce -- my guess is the path-dependent bit, that actual demoi were never chosen by anyone and just got inherited, but tell me if you think the unreproducible part is something else.
