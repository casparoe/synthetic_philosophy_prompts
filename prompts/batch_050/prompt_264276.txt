Okay so I spend way too much time arguing with my brother about voting (he sits out every election because "both parties are bought", classic) and I want to settle it by making a little quiz that tells you which actual stance you hold on voting, without ever using the fancy words. Like 7 or 8 questions, each one a concrete situation: a race where your favorite candidate has zero chance of winning, a ballot where the only realistic options are two people you dislike but one is clearly worse, an election you could skip because the outcome is basically certain already, a case where voting third party might "spoil" it, that kind of thing. Each answer option should be written as what a regular person would say ("I'd still vote, it's the principle", "not voting is my vote", etc.) not philosophy jargon. Then a scoring key that maps answer patterns onto the actual positions in this debate -- voting as a duty, voting as expressing who you are, lesser-evil consequentialist voting, abstention as protest, and the "one vote never matters so do whatever" view. One thing I want sorted out though: the duty people and the expression people agree on a lot of cases, so figure out which question actually splits them (maybe: would you vote for a candidate you think is terrible if not voting helped someone even worse?) and make sure the quiz has one. Also flag any two positions your scoring key can't actually tell apart, dont just pretend it can.
