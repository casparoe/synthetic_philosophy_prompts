Last night I got into a long, slightly exhausting argument with my freind about whether it’s ever legitimate to compare atrocities, or whether ranking historical evils is basically always obscene. I still don’t think either of us won, but now I have to turn the issue into something practical.

I’m an editor at a public-radio show. About twice a month, a producer or host wants to say that one historical atrocity was worse than another, equivalent to another, uniquely evil, or basically incomparable. Sometimes the comparison is central to the story; sometimes it’s just a punchy line. I need a reusable editorial procedure I can run in under 20 minutes, without a staff historian, for deciding whether a proposed comparison can air, needs reframing, or should be cut. If it raises a genuinely moral question rather than an editorial or factual one, I can send it to the managing editor and an outside adviser, but I need the procedure to identify exactly when that handoff happens.

Please write the procedure as ordered steps with clear pass/fail conditions. It should distinguish at least these purposes: explaining causes, recognizing victims, assigning blame, allocating present-day resources, and making an overall moral ranking. Build in checks for things like intent, scale, duration, methods, vulnerability of the victims, certainty of the historical record, and long-term consequences—but don’t just turn those into one fake-precise atrocity score. Also include stop conditions for comparisons that mainly humiliate one victim group, flatten morally important differences, or pretend that a contested value judgment is just arithmetic.

Then apply the procedure to three sample lines:

1. A host wants to say one genocide was worse than another because it killed more people.
2. A segment wants to compare two different atrocities in order to explain how governments learned to use propaganda and bureaucracy to organize violence.
3. A commentator wants to call a modern atrocity another Holocaust because that will make listeners take it seriously.

At least one application should produce an answer a ratings-conscious host probably won’t like. Finish by saying what the procedure cannot decide, what kinds of expertise would require escalation, and where simplification is most likely to make it misfire. Please answer in American English.
