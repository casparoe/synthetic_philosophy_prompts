The free will debate has been grinding on for centuries, and in our reading group we've gotten specifically stuck on Strawson's Freedom and Resentment. The incompatibilists in the group say Strawson just changed the subject: they asked whether determinism is compatible with genuinely deserved blame, and he answered a different question about whether we could or should ever give up our reactive attitudes like resentment and gratitude -- which is a point about human nature, not metaphysics. The Strawson fans say that's the whole point: the demand for an external, non-practical justification of our responsibility practices is itself confused, since those practices are too deep in what it is to live a human life to need or admit that kind of justification, and so the old debate dissolves rather then gets answered. My working hypothesis is that the two camps are really arguing about what kind of question the responsibility question is -- a theoretical one about what's true, or a practical one about what we have reason to feel and do -- and that the did-Strawson-dodge-or-dissolve dispute is just that disagreement in disguise. Is that the right diagnosis, or is there a real substantive question still open underneath?
