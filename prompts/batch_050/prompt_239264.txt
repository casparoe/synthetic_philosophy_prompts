Quick background so we're on the same page: Scheffler's idea is that most of what we care about would lose its point, or at least our confidence in it, if we knew humanity would end shortly after our own deaths. His two scenarios are roughly: an asteroid wipes out life on earth thirty days after you die, or humanity has gone infertile (Children of Men style) so the people now alive are the last generation. Got it. Now here's his argument:

(1) Suppose Scheffler is right: nearly all of my projects — finishing my thesis, voting, composting, teaching my kids to read, being a good friend — matter to me only on the assumption that human life continues well past my death.

(2) If something I care about matters only conditionally on X, then I have reason to secure X, and the more of my concerns that are conditional on X, the stronger my reason to secure X becomes.

(3) By (1), nearly everything I care about is conditional on humanity's survival after me, so my reason to secure humanity's survival swamps nearly all my other reasons.

(4) This holds even though humanity's long-term fate isn't very sensitive to what I personally do — the point about conditional reasons doesn't depend on my causal leverage being large, just on the dependence being real.

(5) Therefore I should reorient basically everything — career, savings, how I raise my kids, how I spend my weekends — around securing the long-run future of the species, and time spent on my own projects at the expense of that is irrational in the same structural way skipping your kid's doctor's appointment to watch TV is irrational.

(6) But that's absurd. My reasons to finish the novel or enjoy a Tuesday can't all be hostage to whether there are humans around in 10,000 years.

(7) So the afterlife conjecture must be wrong — our values don't actually depend on collective survival the way Scheffler claims.

The conclusion (7) seems way too strong, and honestly (5) already smells on its own. My friend insists every step is just "Scheffler plus common sense about conditional reasons." I keep suspecting the slide is somewhere around (2) or (3) but when I try to say exactly wich one, I go in circles — (2) does sound like something we accept in ordinary cases.
