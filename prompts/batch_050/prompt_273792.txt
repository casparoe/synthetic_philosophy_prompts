Write a deadpan policy white paper, roughly 700 words, issued by the fictional "Office of Evidence-Based Education" of a mid-sized local authority, recommending the abolition of all remedial and compensatory schooling on the grounds of the behavioural-genetics literature. The document should have the full apparatus of the genre it parodies: an executive summary, numbered recommendations, a cost-saving table, an FAQ for concerned parents, and a closing note on "anticipated objections from ideological quarters."

The white paper's reasoning should be built entirely out of real, recognisable mistakes about heritability, each one smuggled in as sober administrative logic and never flagged or corrected within the document itself. At minimum it should commit the following: treating a heritability estimate as a fixed property of a trait rather than a statistic of a particular population in a particular range of environments; inferring from high heritability that a trait is unresponsive to intervention, in a way that would equally rule out insulin for diabetes or spectacles for myopia; sliding from claims about variation within a group to claims about the causes of differences between groups; speaking of a trait as "80 per cent genetic" in an individual child, as though heritability partitioned one person's outcome; and concluding from equalised environments that further environmental change is pointless, thereby assuming the interventions already tried exhaust the ones that could exist. Where the document cites findings, keep them generic (twin studies, adoption designs) rather than attributing specific figures to named researchers. Every error should be one that someone who understands heritability will immediately be able to name, and the comedy should come from how faithfully the document reproduces the tone and argumentative habits of real policy writing while doing so.

The satire's target is the argumentative practice, not any named person or school of thought. Keep the irony completely straight-faced: no winks, no authorial signalling. A reader should be able to tell what is being ironised solely from the fact that the document's confidence survives each logical step it makes.

After the white paper, append a short out-of-character annotation of 150–200 words. In it, separate what your satire treats as settled among people who know this literature (for example, that heritability says nothing in itself about malleability, and the standard counter-examples) from what is genuinely contested (for example, the reliability of particular heritability estimates, the assumptions of twin methods, and what, if anything, the between-group inference licenses). Do not present a contested view as the consensus, and say plainly where the white paper's parody exaggerates or flattens a real finding for comic effect.

Write the white paper in the flat, confident register of British administrative prose.
