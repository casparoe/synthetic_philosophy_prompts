A guy I know from a founders forum summed up the “can companies be blamed?” debate after a panel like this: “Look, a corporation is just a nexus of contracts plus a legal badge. It can have policies, incentives, dashboards, escalation rules, even a kind of character, but no inner point of view, so ‘the company knew’ is only shorthand for what some people knew. When an optimization system routes trucks through a town and the asthma rates spike, responsibility has to land on the humans who wrote, bought, ignored, or failed to audit it; if none of them intended the harm, then nobody is blameworthy, only causally involved. Courts fining companies don’t prove moral personhood, they just prove the state needs a deep pocket. And blaming the firm is actually unfair because it launders punishment onto shareholders, junior staff, and customers while letting executives hide behind the logo. The grown-up conclusion is forward-looking only: fix incentives, disclose models, maybe jail a bad exec if you can find one, but retire corporate blame as a useful fiction.”

I definately feel parts of this are right and parts are slippery, but I can’t tell where. Can you audit it like someone sympathetic to corporate moral agency but not starry-eyed: which claims would a careful defender accept, which are conflations (legal personhood vs moral agency, lack of consciousness vs lack of reasons-responsiveness, causal involvement vs culpability, punishment vs blame), which smuggle in individualism as if it were obvious, and does his “retire corporate blame” conclusion follow from the accurate parts? Also say what changes if the routing model was explicitly flagged by an internal ethics review and overruled for margin, versus if nobody could reasonably have predicted the harm.
